1 · Qur'an

Chosen by the mushaf, never declared by hand

Gold ink for the verse and a darker gold for its English, with a mushaf mark and the chapter and verse set in white in the middle of a filled brown bar that heads the card, inside a 2px frame in the same brown a step lighter — the two gold rules that carried them until 2026-08-11 are gone. The one card with a frame — the other three keep the plate-not-outline rule. Chapter and verse always show — there is no card without them, so the audit has to record which ayah it matched.

The Prophet's nearness to the believer is stated by the Book itself:

Al-Ahzab · 6

ٱلنَّبِىُّ أَوْلَىٰ بِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ مِنْ أَنفُسِهِمْ ۖ وَأَزْوَجُهُۥٓ أُمَّهَتُهُمْ ۗ وَأُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَرْحَامِ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلَىٰ بِبَعْضٍۢ فِى كِتَبِ ٱللَّهِ مِنَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَٱلْمُهَجِرِينَ إِلَّآ أَن تَفْعَلُوٓا۟ إِلَىٰٓ أَوْلِيَآئِكُم مَّعْرُوفًۭا ۚ كَانَ ذَلِكَ فِى ٱلْكِتَبِ مَسْطُورًۭا

The Prophet has a higher claim on the believers than [they have on] their own selves, [seeing that he is as a father to them] and his wives are their mothers: and they who are [thus] closely related have, in accordance with God’s decree, a higher claim upon one another than [was even the case between] the believers [of Yathrib] and those who had migrated [there for the sake of God]. None the less, you are to act with utmost goodness towards your [other] close friends as well: this [too] is written down in God’s decree.

The Prophet is closer to the believers than they are to themselves, and his wives are their mothers. Blood relations are closer to one another in the Book of God than the believers and the emigrants, except that you do some kindness to your allies; that is written in the Book.

Prophetic tradition

عِشْ مَا شِئْتَ فَإِنَّكَ مَيِّتٌ، وَ أَحْبِبْ مَا شِئْتَ فَإِنَّكَ مُفَارِقُهُ؛ وَ اِعْمَلْ مَا شِئْتَ فَإِنَّكَ مَجْزِيٌّ بِهِ

"Live as you wish, for you shall surely die. Love what you wish, for you shall surely be parted from it. And do what you wish, for you shall surely be recompensed for it."

Among the counsels the Prophet gave to his community is this saying of his — the long form, wrapped over several lines:

Prophetic tradition

عَلَامَةُ إِعْرَاضِ اللَّهِ تَعَالَىٰ عَنِ الْعَبْدِ اشْتِغَالُهُ بِمَا لَا يَعْنِيهِ، وَ إِنَّ امْرَأً ذَهَبَتْ سَاعَةٌ مِنْ عُمْرِهِ فِي غَيْرِ مَا خُلِقَ لَهُ مِنَ الْعِبَادَةِ، جدير ان تطول عليه حسرته

"A servant's busying himself with matters that do not concern him is a sign that Allah has turned His special favor away from him. And if a single hour of a servant's life passes in the pursuit of anything other than that for which Allah created him, then it is fitting that his sorrow over it should be long."

And he also said — the longest Arabic run in the book, five lines of it:

Saying

إِذَا كَانَ أَوَّلُ اللَّيْلِ نَادَىٰ مُنَادٍ مِنْ تَحْتِ الْعَرْشِ: أَلَا لِيَقُمِ الْعَابِدُونَ. فَيَقُومُونَ وَيُصَلُّونَ مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ. ثُمَّ يُنَادِي مُنَادٍ فِي شَطْرِ اللَّيْلِ: أَلَا لِيَقُمِ الْقَانِتُونَ. فَيَقُومُونَ وَيُصَلُّونَ إِلَى السَّحَرِ. فَإِذَا كَانَ السَّحَرُ نَادَى مُنَادٍ: أَلَا لِيَقُمِ الْمُسْتَغْفِرُونَ. فَيَقُومُونَ وَيَسْتَغْفِرُونَ. فَإِذَا طَلَعَ الْفَجْرُ نَادَى مُنَادٍ: أَلَا لِيَقُمِ الْغَافِلُونَ. فَيَقُومُونَ مِنْ فُرُشِهِمْ كَالْمَوْتَى نُشِرُوا مِنْ قُبُورِهِمْ

"When the first part of the night comes, a caller cries out from beneath the Throne: Let the worshippers arise. So they arise and pray as long as Allah wills. Then, at the middle of the night, a caller cries out: Let the devout arise. So they arise and pray until the hour before dawn. When that hour arrives, a caller cries out: Let those who seek forgiveness arise. So they arise and seek forgiveness. And when dawn breaks, a caller cries out: Let the heedless arise. So they rise from their beds like the dead raised up from their graves."

— Sufyan al-Thawri

4 · Poem

Declared on the block — and it cannot be guessed

Classical Arabic verse is set in two equal columns: one line is two hemistichs, the first on the right and the second on the left, with a gutter between. Lines stack as rows and the gutter runs straight down the page — that vertical alignment is what makes a poem look like a poem to someone who cannot read the words. It is what printed diwans do and what the standard LaTeX package for Arabic verse produces. On a phone the two columns become two rows.

One line — two hemistichs, and the gutter falls between them:

Verse

سَهَرُ الْعُيُونِ لِغَيْرِ وَجْهِكَ ضَائِعٌ

وَ بُكَاؤُهُنَّ لِغَيْرِ فَقْدِكَ بَاطِلٌ

"The wakefulness of the eyes for anything but Your Countenance is wasted, and their weeping for the loss of anything but You is in vain."

Three lines. The gutter runs straight down the page, which is what makes the shape read as verse:

Verse

لَقَدْ هَتَفَتْ فِي جُنْحِ لَيْلٍ حَمَامَةٌ

على فننٍ وهناً، وإنّي لنائم

كذبتُ، وبيتِ الله، لو كنتُ عاشقاً

لَمَّا سَبَقَتْنِي بِالْبُكَاءِ الْحَمَائِمُ

وأزعم أنّي هائمٌ ذو صبابةٍ

لِرَبِّي، فَلَا أَبْكِي وَتَبْكِي الْبَهَائِمُ

"In the dark of night a dove cried out upon a swaying branch, while I lay fast asleep. I have lied, by the House of Allah! Had I truly been a lover, the doves would never have outstripped me in weeping. I claim to be one distracted, consumed with yearning for my Lord, yet I do not weep, while the very beasts weep on."

A Persian couplet takes the same grid:

Verse

كَرَمِي دُو هَزَارْ رَطْلْ هَمِي بِيْمَائِي

تَا مَيْ نَخُورِي نَبَاشَدَتْ شَيْدَائِي

"Even if you were to buy two thousand pounds of wine, it would not make you drunk until you drink it."

No book here contains a line with only one half. This is the line above with its second half removed, so you can see what the grid does when one arrives — it spans the full measure rather than sitting in one column:

Verse

كُلُّ الْعَدَاوَةِ قَدْ تُرْجَى إِزَالَتُهَا

"Every enmity may be hoped to be removed, save the enmity of one who bears you enmity out of envy."

7 · The circles

Not stops — a silent-letter mark

The mark says the letter beneath it is written but not pronounced. The shipped font declares it as a full-size letter rather than as a mark, so it takes its own width and breaks the word apart. The font may not be edited — its licence forbids it in so many words, and so does this repo's own vendoring note. Redirecting just that character in CSS was tried and does not work: the browser keeps a mark in the font of the letter it sits on. What is left is a different face. Amiri Quran is already in the book, is freely licensed, and draws the mark correctly — but it changes how every verse looks, which is your call.

One thing I checked in your books before designing the poem, because it decides the whole approach. The asterisk inside an Arabic quotation means two different things on the page today. In Ayyuhal Walad it splits a line of verse at lines 59, 167, 289–291 and 538 — but at lines 373, 498, 504, 510 and 516 it separates a Qur'anic verse from its own reference, as in الحجرات: ٥. A renderer that split on the asterisk would tear five citations in half and print the surah name as a second hemistich. So the two-column grid can only ever run on a block a person has marked as verse — the same rule the hadith and quote cards already follow. Across all seven books there is exactly one poem longer than a single line: the three lines above.
The literal-translation bug, traced to one line. Nothing stops a quotation's English from being improved: the articulation pass sends the whole chapter, blockquotes and all, and not one of its fidelity gates freezes what is inside a quote. The problem is the instruction. The ONLY thing the prompt says about a quotation's English sits in its YOU MAY NOT list — “Touch the artifacts … inside a quote, de-calque the wording only”. A permission written as an exception to a ban is read as a ban, so hadith, sayings and verse keep whatever literal English the translator first produced while the prose around them is rewritten. The fix is a positive instruction, not a new gate.
Three things I still need you to settle.
1. The gold: the verse ink is #7d6800, darkened from the #978002 in your other project so it stays readable at book size. Too dark, or right?
2. The Qur'an's English is Literata at 1rem. Your other project uses Charm at 2.5rem, which is a display face and would not hold up over a page of print.
3. The Qur'an's English above comes from the complete rendering already tracked in this repo — no network, no new dependency. Its bracketed asides are the translator's own and cannot be stripped without changing his sentences. Accept them, or fetch a plainer translation instead?
4. Books can currently pick their own Arabic ink. With all four cards fixed, that setting would govern nothing. Retire it, or keep it for the Quote card only?
