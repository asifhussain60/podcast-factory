This book is a series of talks, delivered aloud to an audience and then set down in prose. It is not a treatise composed at a desk, but a spoken teaching, transcribed from the sessions as they were given. The one who speaks in these pages is their author: he addressed listeners directly, and his voice, rather than a scholar's pen, is what carries the argument from first page to last.

Across five sessions the book makes a single journey. It begins by proposing that religion, at its root, is founded upon love rather than fear or mere obligation. From there it asks why messengers are needed at all, and what work they do that could be done no other way. It then turns to Islam not as a set of propositions to be argued, but as something to be lived and experienced. The fourth session dwells on the character of the Prophet himself, and the fifth draws the whole together, holding up his life as a model for a successful human life.

The reader who knows nothing of this subject will find here an accessible entry, one that moves from a first principle about the nature of faith toward a concrete example to follow. The tone throughout is that of a speaker among listeners: unhurried, direct, and concerned less with the machinery of scholarship than with what a person might take to heart and carry into his own days.
