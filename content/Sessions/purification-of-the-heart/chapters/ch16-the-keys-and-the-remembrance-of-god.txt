Now there was a question from the previous class about women and male slaves — specifically whether a woman may own a bondsman. Yes, that is permissible in the فِقْه. But they are not مَحْرَم, and all the normal rules regarding seclusion and mixing apply. There can be no sexual relationship between the two. That was the question.

Now before we close, let me do the مَفَاتِيح — the keys — because I did not cover that last week.

مَفَاتِيح is the plural of مِفْتَاح, which means key in Arabic. The root, if you look at the word, is the same root as the verb to open. And the وَزْن — the morphological pattern — it sits on is that of a tool: the thing you use to do the action. مِكْنَسَة is the thing you use to sweep. The مِفْتَاح is the thing you use to open.

<!-- page 209 -->

In Arabic, the word for key does not just mean an object that unlocks something — it means the thing that causes an opening. The metaphor is richer than the English word "key" carries. And it connects directly to one of the names of اللهُ: الْفَتَّاحُ — the One who opens. People who carry spiritual keys have the ability to open things that are otherwise closed. And there are many أَحَادِيث on this: "such and such is the key to such and such." صَبْر is the key to الْفَرَجُ — to the opening, to relief. If you want فَرَجٌ from اللهُ, the key is patience.

The شَرِيعَة, then, is what tells us what is good and what is bad or ugly — the intellect has no independent access to that. It can sometimes arrive at the right answer, but not always, which makes it a relative and limited source of guidance rather than an absolute one. The شَرِيعَة indicates what is حَسَنَة and what is سَيَّاحٌ. And when a person recognizes that his نَفْس will not save him or help him, he turns to what actually will help him — namely, doing what اللهُ has commanded — and that recognition leads him to act in accordance with اللهُ and His message.

The poem's point, the sheikh explains, is that the origin of both heart disease and spiritual well-being is the company one keeps, for the حَدِيث states that a man is upon the دِين of his companion. If you keep company with someone who delays his prayer, who neglects زَكَاةٌ, or who commits wrong actions, you will inevitably drift toward those same things. Conversely, good company will pull you in a good direction. This is precisely why people must examine who they spend time with, because one's companions are a reliable indicator of one's own spiritual state. The sheikh offers a vivid illustration: if you sit at the door of a bar, you will smell the foulness of alcohol; if you sit at the door of a perfumer, you will breathe in the fragrance of perfume. Whatever you are near, you are affected by. Proximity to goodness brings goodness; proximity to corruption brings corruption.

For this reason, لُقْمَانُ الْحَكِيمُ — mentioned in the قُرْآن — is recorded in the مُوَطَّأ of إِمَام Malik as having told his son: sit with the عُلَمَاء. Keep their company. The عُلَمَاء are people who possess knowledge and live by it, and sitting with them is like rain falling on dry earth and bringing it back to life. That is what their company does to the earth of your heart — it revives it, and it does so in a way you may not even notice. Simply being in the presence of righteous, knowledgeable people allows the heart to stir back to life, and إِيمَان begins to grow and increase within it.

He says that sitting with the عُلَمَاء — the people of true knowledge, the الْعَارِفِينَ — will take a person from six things to six things: from doubt to certainty; from doing things for other than اللهُ to doing them with sincerity, with إِخْلَاص, for اللهُ alone; from heedlessness to the remembrance of اللهُ; from desiring this world to desiring the next; from arrogance to repentance; and from corrupt inner character and bad intentions toward people to sincere, wholesome character and genuine goodwill. Ibn Ata'illah makes a related point in his حِكَم: do not keep company with someone whose state — his هَلْ, his condition — does not elevate you, and whose speech does not direct you toward اللهُ. If a person's presence neither lifts your هِمَّة nor points you toward اللهُ, that company is harmful.

Of all the diseases examined in the مَطْهَرَةٌ, the comprehensive cure — the one that addresses all of them — is the remembrance of اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. More than anything else, ذِكْرٌ is what heals the heart. ذِكْر includes recitation of قُرْآن, صَلَاح, and all the formulas of praise: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ, الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, سُبْحَانَ اللهِ الْعَظِيمِ, لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ, اللهُ أَكْبَرُ, لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ — as well as the supplications the Prophet ﷺ has transmitted to us. But the highest and best form of ذِكْرٌ is قِرَاءَةُ الْقُرْآنِ. A person who is in a state of ذِكْرٌ will have a طَهَارَة heart — a pure heart.

سَيِّدِي Ahmad ibn Hanbal is reported to have seen اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى ninety-nine times in his dreams. In one of them he was asked: of all the things that draw a slave near to اللهُ, which is the greatest? اللهُ answered him: recitation of قُرْآن. He then asked: يَا اللَّهُ, with understanding or without understanding? And اللهُ answered, according to the إِمَام, بِفَهْمٍ أَوْ بِغَيْرِ فَهْمٍ — with understanding or without. This means that even someone who does not understand the words he is reading is still drawing close to اللهُ through the قُرْآن.

In صَحِيحُ الْبُخَارِيِّ, the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ said that the difference between one who remembers اللهُ and one who does not is like the difference between the living and the dead. Whoever remembers اللهُ — قَلْبُهُ حَيٌّ — his heart is alive, and so he is alive. Whoever does not remember اللهُ — قَلْبُهُ مَيِّتٌ — his heart is dead, and so he is considered dead. A sound حَدِيث also says: make remembrance of اللهُ until people say you are mad. This is because one thing that marks people who are troubled in their minds is that they talk to themselves constantly. Someone who remembers اللهُ all the time may appear, outwardly, very similar — a non-Muslim who sees you walking down the street doing ذِكْرٌ may well think you are talking to yourself. That is what the حَدِيث points to.

There is also the sound حَدِيث, transmitted in various narrations, in which the Prophet ﷺ asks: sهَلْl I not tell you what the best of all your actions is — the purest in the sight of your Lord and King, the highest in rank among all the deeds that carry degrees, better for you than spending gold and silver, and better even than going out to meet your enemy in جِهَادٌ, striking them and being struck, killing them and being killed? They said: of course, tell us, يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ. He said: ذِكْرُ اللهِ تَعَالَى — the remembrance of اللهُ. Some people wonder how remembrance could be better than جِهَادٌ. The عُلَمَاء — may اللهُ have mercy on them — explain that ذِكْرٌ اللهُ is the غَايَة, the end itself, the very purpose for which we were created. Jihad and everything else are وَسَائِل — means. And no means, however noble, is greater than the end it serves. We were created to remember اللهُ, which is why ذِكْرٌ is the highest thing in the دُنْيَا.

اللهُ says: اذْكُرُونِي أَذْكُرْكُمْ — remember Me, and I will remember you. Whoever remembers his Lord, اللهُ remembers him. And whoever اللهُ remembers has been enriched in both this world and the next, and stands in need of nothing. Another distinguishing feature of ذِكْرٌ — unlike حَجٌّ, fasting, or even formal prayer — is that it is not bound to a specific time or place. It can accompany every human act. There are ذِكْرٌ related to going to the toilet, to eating, to marital relations — every moment of life has a ذِكْرٌ belonging to it. And it is said that whoever is in a state of remembrance of اللهُ is never struck by calamity in a way that harms him, because whatever befalls someone who was remembering اللهُ only raises him in rank. If he dies while remembering اللهُ, he dies as a martyr. This is the treatment for the diseased and foul soul — remembrance of اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى.

When you wish to engage in ذِكْرٌ, you begin with اِسْتِغْفَار — seeking forgiveness — because the purpose of ذِكْرٌ is purification, and the reason purification is needed is the wrong actions one has accumulated. So the process begins by asking اللهُ to remove those wrong actions. Once He removes them, the ذِكْرٌ that follows will then remove their lingering effects, for wrong actions leave traces and effects on the heart.

You then send prayers upon the Prophet ﷺ, because صَلَاح upon the Prophet acts as a مُرَبٍّ — a nurturer — for the heart and as a means of its purification. This is why most of the later عُلَمَاء specifically encouraged these two practices: اِسْتِغْفَار for the wrong actions most of us are engaged in, and صَلَاح upon the Prophet because by the إِجْمَاعٌ of the أُمَّة, it is the only ذِكْرٌ that is absolutely guaranteed to be accepted by اللهُ. Anyone who says أَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلِّمْ is مَقْبُولٌ — accepted. اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى commanded this with a command He Himself initiated, which is unique among all of His commands — He does not begin with the action Himself except in the case of prayer upon the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ. He said: إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ وَمَلَٓئِكَتَهُۥ يُصَلُّونَ عَلَى ٱلنَّبِىِّۚ — and then enjoined the believers: يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا صَلُّوا عَلَيْهِ وَسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا — pray upon the Messenger of اللهُ and greet him with peace. أَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلِّمْ. Whoever prays upon the Prophet ﷺ once, اللهُ prays upon him ten times — this is established in صَحِيح حَدِيث. And whoever اللهُ has prayed upon ten times, what else could he possibly need?

أَلصَّلَاةُ عَلَيْهِ، عَلَيْهِ نُورُهُ فِي قَدْرِهِ، وَنُورُهُ فِي قَدْرِهِ، وَنُورٌ عَلَى الصِّرَاطِ — the Prophet of اللهُ ﷺ said that prayer upon him is light in this world, light in the grave, and light on the صِرَاطٌ. It gives you guidance — and that guidance — when you need it most. It illuminates the grave so that it is light rather than dark. And it illuminates the صِرَاطٌ so that you can see your way across it. Light in the heart, as the Prophet ﷺ said.

Because we have been commanded to pray upon the Prophet ﷺ, it is generally good to begin by reciting the actual آيَةٌ, for in doing so you are fulfilling the command of اللهُ — this is called امْتِثَالُ أَمْرِ اللهِ. That command is obligatory at least once in a lifetime, but you can fulfill it anew every single time you send prayers upon the Prophet ﷺ. The one who prays upon the Prophet should observe the same أَدَب he would observe when remembering اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى: it is مَنْدُوب for him to be in وُضُوء, مَنْدُوب to face the قِبْلَة, and so on — the same courtesies that are مَنْدُوب for prayer apply here as well.

He also mentions اِسْتِحْضَار — that when you pray upon the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ, you should do so as if you were in his presence. In the same way that إِحْسَان in prayer means worshipping اللهُ as though you see Him, اِسْتِحْضَار in صَلَاح upon the Prophet ﷺ means bringing his presence before you as though he were there — calling to mind the guidance he gave us, all the things we owe him as our وَسِيلَة for teaching us لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ and for conveying to us the knowledge of اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. And you remember that اللهُ Himself and His angels send prayer upon this Messenger, so in doing so you are participating in something that اللهُ Himself declared He does.

Another thing to be mindful of in ذِكْرٌ is what is called لَحْن — incorrect pronunciation. You should avoid it to the best of your ability. Some people simply cannot pronounce every Arabic sound correctly, but the effort must still be made. He notes that some mispronunciations constitute a مَعْصِيَة or a خَطَأ, a sin or an error, while others can reach the level of رِدَّة — potentially taking a person out of Islam. As for those who cannot distinguish between sounds like دَادٌ and ذَاءٌ — the عَجَمِيَّة — if there is a genuine heaviness in the tongue, a physical impediment that persists despite every effort to correct it, then that person is مَعْذُور: excused by the شَرِيعَة. He should still engage in ذِكْرٌ even if the pronunciation falls short, because the incapacity is real and اللهُ does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear.

He receives the reward, and no wrongdoing is entailed by performing the ذِكْرٌ in that way. Among the greatest acts of worship is also meditating on اللهُ's creation. اللهُ mentions in the قُرْآن those who remember Him standing, sitting, and reclining on their sides, and who reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth. ذِكْر and تَفَكُّر — remembrance and reflection — are thus joined together.

It is said that the action of the heart, which is تَفَكُّر, is better than all the actions of the limbs. This is why one narration states that a single moment of reflection is better than a year of worship. The signs of genuine reflection fall into four directions.

The first is reflecting on the signs of اللهُ. The second is reflecting on اللهُ's creation and what those signs point to — and this leads to love of اللهُ, because one sees everything اللهُ has done for the human being. The third is reflecting on اللهُ's promised rewards and the signs connected to them; each sign kindles a desire for the آخِرَة, so that the mark of this reflection is a yearning, a رَغْبَة, for جَنَّة. The fourth is reflecting on اللهُ's promised punishment; the sign of this reflection is that fear takes root in the heart and the person turns away from disobedience.

There is also reflection on the weakness of the self — the harshness of the نَفْس toward اللهُ despite His unceasing إِحْسَان. When one contemplates all that اللهُ has given and then honestly weighs how little one has offered in return, this reflection produces حَيَاء, a deep sense of shame before اللهُ — shame that He has given so much, while one has given so little in return.

The heart is what distinguishes good from evil, and the source of all these matters lies in the inrushings that come to the heart. These inrushings arrive like a guest descending upon the heart, and they take different forms.

The question Qais raised concerned what people today call the مُتَصَوِّفَة. إِمَام المَقَارِي distinguished between the صُفِيَ and the مُتَصَوِّفَة. The مُتَصَوِّفَة are those who merely claim to be Sufis. A common sign of such people is that they engage in abundant ذِكْرٌ while neglecting the وَجِبَتْ — the obligatory duties.

The Shaykh's answer was direct: the first and foremost obligation on every human being is to acquire knowledge — before anything else. A person who has not learned the فَرَائِدُ الْعَيْنِ, the individually obligatory matters, has no مَقَام before اللهُ. It is as though he stands on the same footing as an animal, or worse, because an animal is not morally responsible, whereas this person is astray and is making no effort to correct himself.

Shaykh عَبْدُ الْخَادِمِ cited many scholars on this point — إِمَام الشَّعْرَانِيُّ, إِمَام السُّيُوطِي, and all the great imams who mastered both the outward sciences and تَصَوُّف — all of them affirm that فِقْه must precede تَصَوُّف. If a person pursues تَصَوُّف without first grounding himself in فِقْه, he will go astray. There is no تَصَوُّف without the knowledge of the فَرَائِض. تَصَوُّفٌ comes after فِقْه.

Even بِرُّ الْوَالِدَيْنِ — dutiful care of one's parents — though it is the highest virtue اللهُ mentions in the قُرْآن after تَوْحِيد, still comes after learning what اللهُ has made obligatory. If caring for one's parents is preventing one from acquiring that obligatory knowledge, then one is committing a wrong by abandoning what is more right.

Regarding the question about women: a woman in a state of menstrual impurity may perform all forms of ذِكْرٌ that men perform. She may not recite from the قُرْآن extensively, though there are nuances — if she has memorized the قُرْآن, she may recite what is needed to retain her memorization, or she may recite short phrases such as تَعَوُّذ or verses like سُورَةُ الْمُلْكِ in that capacity. She may not touch the physical قُرْآن during that period. Beyond these restrictions, she may freely recite the أَذْكَار, لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ, and the prayers upon the Prophet ﷺ.

Regarding the question about non-Muslims in America who are drawn to Islam through its spiritual teachings — those who hear about the inner meanings of the tradition and feel themselves drawn closer — the Shaykh's answer was that the best path for all people is submission to the will of اللهُ and acceptance of His commands as He has made them binding. When the Prophet ﷺ called people to Islam, he began with تَوْحِيد and then told them that اللهُ had made five prayers obligatory. He called them first to the unity of اللهُ and then to prayer. That is the methodology of the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ, and that is what we should be calling people to.

In contemporary culture there is a pervasive false or pseudo-spirituality built on the notion that one can access حَقِيقَة without شَرِيعَة. This is a دَجَّالِيّ phenomenon — it misleads people and draws them away from the truth. It is, in effect, anti-spirituality, because genuine spirituality is protected by the شَرِيعَة. The fruit is protected by its peel; if you strip the peel before the fruit has ripened, you ruin it. Likewise, if you reach for spirituality before you have shielded yourself with the شَرِيعَة, you will destroy your soul and become one of those false figures — the مُتَمَلِّئٍ — people who project an outward appearance of spiritual attainment while their hearts remain filled with every disease.

The Shaykh also addressed the importance of concealing one's faults. If a person has committed wrong, he should veil it and make تَوْبَةٌ; اللهُ will forgive him. But if he publicizes it openly, the harm multiplies, because the sin spreads as a tribulation beyond himself.

He cited the حَدِيث of the man who came to the mosque and told the Prophet ﷺ that he had committed زِينَة and asked to be given the prescribed punishment. The Prophet turned away from him. The man followed him outside and repeated himself. Finally the Prophet looked at him and said: "Did you not just perform وُضُوء and pray with us?" — meaning: that is what Islam is for, to purify one's wrongs. The man, however, persisted until the punishment was carried out. The Prophet ﷺ also said that whoever commits such offenses should cover himself with اللهُ's covering and make تَوْبَةٌ, but whoever comes forward and openly admits his sin, the prescribed punishment will be applied.

The Shaykh then turned to the question of companionship. For worldly matters, one should keep company with those who have less, not those who have more — because constantly comparing oneself to those who have bigger houses and better cars feeds love of the دُنْيَا. But for matters of the آخِرَة, one should always seek the company of those who are better. If one only keeps company with people whose دِين is weaker, one begins to think one's own دِين is sufficient and grows complacent.

The people of اللهُ, he noted, tend to see themselves as lesser than everyone around them, so the danger of pride does not really apply to them in the same way. Keeping their company benefits you precisely because their humility makes you feel how much further you still have to go.

This is also why one should never look down on anyone. A person who appears to be in a worse state today might make a تَوْبَةٌ tomorrow that اللهُ accepts and rise to become a وَلِيَ of اللهُ — while the one who despised him might later stumble. No one can know another person's standing with اللهُ. So one should never feel superior to anyone in this دُنْيَا, and should always seek out the company of those who are better in their آخِرَة.

Those are rightly guided, if they truly follow what those imams brought — because those four imams وَالْمُؤْمَى عَلَيْهِمْ أَرْضَعَهَا وَقَفُوا غَيْرِهُمْ جَمِعُوا مَنَعَهَا — it is مُؤْمَى عَلَيْهِمْ; those four imams are مُؤْمَى عَلَيْهِمْ, meaning it is an absolute consensus that they are rightly guided. So those who call to something other than that are on shaky ground. In matters of عَقِيدَة, take a simple, sound creed from a straightforward source — Ibn Ashir, إِمَام al-Tahawi, any of those — and do not wade into فُرُوعٌ, فَرْعِيَّات: where is اللهُ, what is the hand like, all of that. Someone came to me and asked what I say about the verse ٱلرَّحْمَنُ عَلَى ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱسْتَوَىٰ, and I said that the very fact they are asking that question is, by the testimony of the قُرْآن itself, a sign of deviation in the heart — because the قُرْآن states that anyone who pursues those verses has deviation in their heart. So whenever you hear people arguing over the verses of اللهُ that belong to the مُتَشَابِهَاتٌ, the ambiguous ones, just leave them alone. That is guidance.

The question had been: what do we do in a time when شُيُوخٌ have disappeared and people of تَرْبِيَة are scarce? What Shaykh Hafri — may اللهُ bless him — said was that the very asking of that question is a sign that the person cares about their soul, so it is a good question, and it reflects a real condition. He said that if you are in this country, then things like the classes you are attending now are good — this is the right kind of thing to do. He said to make sure the source of your knowledge is sound. The things we teach, like these مُتُونٌ — these are مُتُونٌ that are ثَابِتٌ, rooted in our tradition, and they come through a chain of شُيُوخٌ.

His shaykh and my shaykh is مُرَابِط al-Hajj, and whoever has seen مُرَابِط al-Hajj knows who he is. His knowledge is pure. The source of the knowledge is pure. No one who has lived with مُرَابِط al-Hajj for over seventy years can say they ever saw him do something even مَكْرُوه. He has never been heard to speak an ill word about anyone by name. His knowledge is pure Shariah, and he teaches people the remembrance of اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, because he is in a state that one of the people described this way: الْجُلُوسُ عِنْدَهُ كَالْجُلُوسِ عِنْدَ حَبْرِ الْغَيْبِ — sitting with him is like sitting with someone who is simultaneously present and absent. He is present in that he will teach you and answer your questions; he is absent in that his heart has moved away from this world — it is no longer in this world.

People like that are rare to find. There is a question here — اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, he is مُرَابِط; we love him. A woman asked: is it possible for a woman to come and learn from you, to study with you? He said that anyone who wants to go to Mauritania may go. He said the hidden aspiration of a مُؤْمِن is أَقْطَعُ مِنَ السَّيْفِ — more cutting than the sword. If the hidden intention is strong, people can do remarkable things. He gave the example of Yahya, an American man who became Muslim here. He was young — nineteen or twenty — when he took his شَهَادَة. He was, as the shaykh put it, a spoiled American, from a wealthy family, with older brothers. He became Muslim and wanted to seek knowledge. He had an expense-paid place at Berkeley, had already completed two years of college. He came to me, and I told him initially to just finish university for his parents' sake, and then go.

My sister Nabila — and I told Shaykh Haddameen this — said to me: you did him an injustice. I said: why? It is only two years; it will pass quickly, and then he can go do what he wants. She said: how do you know what will happen to him in those two years? Maybe he will encounter a فِتْنَة at Berkeley. Maybe he will lose the desire for knowledge right now, while the fuel is still burning in his heart. When she said that, I realized she was right. I went to him and said: that advice I gave you — I was wrong. He said: I am really glad you said that, because I was not going to take it anyway. That is what he told me.

When I told Shaykh Haddameen — who is the cousin of Shaykh Khatri and the cousin of مُرَابِط al-Hajj, having learned all his knowledge from him — when I told him what I had said, and what my sister had said, he replied: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ, I had wanted to tell you that you were wrong, but my respect for you held me back. I am glad your sister said it. And he said he loves her for the good counsel she gave. So Shaykh Haddameen said that Yahya — who is the son of مُرَابِط al-Hajj — now lives in a small hut made of tree branches with rags tied together. He said: learn for one year and you can learn four times over. It is not very much. And once you have learned those four times, go wherever you want, do whatever you want. Once you have your four times, practice it in your home, do your work, live your life. But if you have only four times, do not squander your time on other things, because it is all يض تجويد تجويد تجويد تجويد. Hadith should be transmitted with تَجْوِيد, and when you recite the أَذْكَار of the morning and evening, you should observe the proper مَخَارِجُ الْحُرُوفِ — where each letter originates — along with all the rules that apply to them.

The best of عِبَادَة is تَفَكُّر, and the highest station of تَفَكُّر is what is called الْفَنَاءُ. الْفَنَاءُ comes from إِمَام al-Junaid — إِمَام al-Junaid called it الْفَنَاءُ, which in Arabic literally means extinction. This concept arises from the fact that each of us experiences a sense of separation from other people. One of the things you notice about a newborn is that this sense of separation does not yet exist in them. There is no "I," no fixed focal point of self. You can see it in the unfocused way their eyes move — there is no centralization. As the child grows into its identity, into its sense of "I-ness," it begins to perceive itself as separate from everything else.

إِمَام al-Junaid said that in deep spiritual practice, certain experiences can arise. One type is called هَلْ, which occurs when someone is overcome by a spiritual state that is often beyond their control — it simply is uncontrollable. The scholars of this science distinguish between هَلْ and مَقَام, and مَقَام is actually higher than هَلْ. The مَقَام is where you are fixed in a condition. The مَقَام of تَوْبَةٌ, for instance, is where you cannot commit disobedience, because you are established in the station of تَوْبَةٌ — you are in a مَقَام, and so you cannot knowingly do wrong. The هَلْ of تَوْبَةٌ, by contrast, is when someone who has been doing wrong actions is suddenly seized by an intense state of remorse and turns to اللهُ for forgiveness. That is a هَلْ, not a مَقَام. The مَقَام is better because it endures. The مَقَام of زُهْد is when someone is settled in a station of detachment; the هَلْ of زُهْد is a state that comes upon them and does not last. A person may experience a هَلْ of زُهْد or a هَلْ of تَوْبَةٌ, but it passes. So the مَقَام is above the هَلْ.

Then there is what is called أَوَارِدُ — an inrush that enters the heart, very powerful, flooding it with light and bringing with it an extraordinary expansion. That too is a temporary state. What إِمَام al-Junaid is saying is that the highest is الْفَنَاءُ, and beyond فَنَاءٌ there is something called بَقَاءٌ. الْفَنَاءُ is when the self is no longer aware of itself — there is a غِيبَة, a disappearance, a loss of the experience of the self. This does not last permanently, because if it did, you would call it insanity. And this is precisely why spiritual psychosis is real. There are people who go into experiences they cannot control or understand, and they become ill. It happens regularly in this culture — many people who take up meditation, who go into Zen Buddhism or similar paths, and practice intensively, can literally lose themselves completely. This is why it is so dangerous to pursue these things without the protection of prayer, without وُضُوء, without the grounding that Islam provides.

Islam is all about grounding. We are not chasing spiritual experiences — we are meant to be grounded. That is what اللهُ has commanded. But if you do what اللهُ commands, and you do it properly, then certain things will often happen inwardly, in the interior self. And there is a science for understanding those things — part of which is the science of الْخَوَاطِر, knowing whether what arises comes from شَيْطَان, because شَيْطَان has many games he can play on those pursuing this path, or whether it is angelic, or whether it comes from the نَفْس.

This is one of the problems with New Age practitioners: most of what happens to them — and they do have genuine experiences, people who go into New Age religion, or Zen, or Kundalini with its fire yoga and breathing practices — they cannot distinguish between the satanic, the نَفْسًا, the angelic, and the رَحْمَانِيّ. They have no tools to tell them apart. And most of what is happening to them is from شَيْطَان and the نَفْس. Possession? Yes, absolutely — possession is very real. People open themselves up to a جِنٌّ to enter them. That is absolutely the case.

As for group ذِكْرٌ — I will address that in a moment. So this idea, what الْفَنَاءُ is, is simply that the person engaged in ذِكْرٌ is no longer aware of their own self. And that can genuinely happen. إِمَام al-Junaid says this is the highest form of تَفَكُّر. This is why when سَيِّدِنَا عَلِيٍّ's son — not الْحَسَنُ or الْحُسَيْن, but one of his other sons — was struck by an arrow, those who wanted to remove it waited until he entered prayer. When he went into prayer, they pulled it out, and he felt nothing. That is someone truly in that state where the self is absent — completely absent.

There are people in India, yogis, who can do very radical things through meditation — endure extraordinary pain and the like. So these experiences are not exclusive to Muslims in terms of the underlying psychology. The human psyche has remarkable capacities. What Muslims recognize, however, is that if a person is sincere and is genuinely remembering اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, then اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى can bring that person into a state of absolute witnessing with اللهُ, where they are no longer aware of their surroundings. Many of the صَحَابَة were in that state — clearly, many of them were.

This is one of the interpretations of the حَدِيث: "My slave does not draw near to Me by anything more beloved to Me than the فَرَائِض, and he will continue to draw near to Me through the نَوَافِل until I love him. And when I love him, I become the eye with which he sees, the ear with which he hears, the tongue with which he speaks, the hand with which he grasps. And if he seeks refuge in Me, I give him refuge. If he asks Me, I give him." That حَدِيث never means that God becomes man — which is called حُلُول. حُلُول is حَرَامٌ to believe, and whoever believes it is outside of Islam. حُلُول is the doctrine of divine incarnation, and that حَدِيث does not mean that. Anyone who interprets it that way is a زِنْدِيق — a heretic, outside of Islam. We do not believe in divine incarnation. We do not believe that God becomes creation. That is the position of Christianity, Hinduism, and similar traditions, not ours.

What we do believe is that a human being can reach such a state of awareness of the فِعَال of اللهُ — the acts of اللهُ — that it transforms their perception entirely. The whole world is the فِعَال of اللهُ; we do not say the world is اللهُ, but we say the world is His act. And there are people who enter a state of absolute witnessing in which they see everything as the فِعَال of اللهُ — they see اللهُ behind all of creation. They no longer perceive otherness the way other people do in this world of separation. They recognize that everything comes from اللهُ — the good and the bad — and so they see it all as good. For the moment, everything is good. That is what that experience means. Even though good and bad exist, it all comes from اللهُ, and because it all comes from اللهُ, it is all, for the moment, good — because even the bad holds wisdom: it causes growth, purification, and drawing closer to اللهُ. So even the bad is seen as good. This is why اللهُ says:

وَعَسَىٰ أَن تُحِبُّوا شَيْئًا وَهُوَ شَرٌّ لَّكُمْ

Maybe you love a thing and it is bad for you.

وَعَسَىٰ أَن تَكْرَهُوا شَيْئًا وَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ

And maybe you hate a thing, but it is good for you. And اللهُ knows, and you do not know. That is the reality of being in a state of مُشَاهَدَة or مُرَاقَبَة with اللهُ.
