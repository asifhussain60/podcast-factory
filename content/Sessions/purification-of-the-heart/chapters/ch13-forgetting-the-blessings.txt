The next disease is one of the most important in the entire book, because شُكَر — gratitude — is truly essential to the deen of Islam. One of the meanings of the Arabic word كَافِر is كُفْرَانُ النِّعْمَةِ: the denial or concealment of blessing. This is precisely why a Muslim can fall into a form of كَفَّرَ, for there is كُفْرٌ دُونَ الْكُفْرِ — a كَفَّرَ that falls short of leaving Islam altogether. The Prophet ﷺ warned women, for instance, against كُفْرَانُ الْمُعَاشَرَةِ — being ungrateful for the companionship of marriage, failing to recognize the نِعْمَةٌ of a husband who provides protection and care.

The next disease, then, is called oblivion to blessings. مِن عُيُوبِ النَّفْسِ نِسْيَانُ النِّعَم — and the root of it is غَفْلَةٌ, heedlessness toward the blessings one has been given. The Quran states: وَمَا بِكُم مِّن نِّعْمَةٍۢ فَمِنَ ٱللَّهِ — every blessing you possess is from Allah. People often say, count your blessings — but those who actually do so are rare. And Allah Himself declares: وَإِن تَعُدُّوا۟ نِعْمَةَ ٱللَّهِ لَا تُحْصُوهَآ — if you try to enumerate the blessings of Allah, you will never encompass them.

<!-- page 208 -->

In light of this, he says: وَبِدَوَامِ ذِكْرِهَا — keep this verse ever before you: وَمَا بِكُم مِّن نِّعْمَةٍۢ فَمِنَ ٱللَّهِ. The Quran opens with بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ, and some of the مُفَسِّرُونَ explain that الرَّحْمَٰن refers to the giver of جَلَائِلَ النِّعَمِ — the great, obvious blessings — المُنْعِمِ بِجَلَائِلِ النِّعَمِ — while الرَّحِيمِ refers to المُنْعِمِ بِدَقَائِقِ النِّعَمِ — the giver of subtle, hidden blessings.

The blessings of الرَّحْمَٰن are easy to recognize: health, for instance, is plain enough. Tell someone, "Thank God you're healthy," and they immediately agree — الحمد لله على الصحة والعافية الحمد لله على الإيمان. But then there are دَقَائِقُ النِّعَمِ — the subtle, intricate blessings. Consider the eye: it is constantly lubricated. Some people must use artificial drops because they suffer from dry eye, and when the eyes are dry, every blink — which normally cleans and protects the eye — becomes painful. Those eyelashes, that rapid blinking reflex that keeps even a stray hair from causing harm, the sockets that set the eye slightly back within the skull to shield it from blunt injury — all of these are hidden blessings most people never consider. People who cannot produce tears must use drops to relieve the pain. People who cannot close their eyelids, or who lose their eyelids entirely, exist — and only then does one grasp what had been taken for granted.

Or consider the eye's capacity for color — the full range of the visible world it opens to you. Then consider balance: people who sustain damage to the eighth cranial nerve in the ear begin to lose their balance and cannot remain upright. Balance itself is a نِعْمَةٌ from Allah. The way you eat — the digestive juices working silently within you, breaking down food and separating its components — these are hidden نَعَمْ you are entirely unaware of. The cerebellum, and the whole unconscious operation of the brain. Breathing: you do not have to remember to breathe. These are all نَعَمْ Allah has granted you, and most of the time you are not even conscious of them.

<!-- page 209 -->

Take even the thumb. Other animals lack the opposable thumb. Allah says قَدْ كَرَّمْنَا بَنِي آدَمَ — We have ennobled the children of Adam — and some scholars say one dimension of that ennobling was the thumb. Tellingly, the Arabic word for thumb is إِبْهَام, from the same root as مُبْهَم — something enigmatic, something strange — because no other creature has it. The thumb is a tremendous نِعْمَةٌ from Allah. Conquerors used to cut off their enemies' thumbs so they could never fight again — lose your thumb, and you lose most of your capability.

Then consider all the pleasures Allah has given you. Taste buds are a نِعْمَةٌ. Allah could have made your food like rocks and your water brackish. He says exactly that in the Quran — He could have made your water unpleasant to drink and you would still have needed it. But He made it sweet. He could have made all food taste the same, yet He gave lemons to those who love sour, and honey to those who love sweet, and then gave you the taste buds capable of savoring every one of these different things.

All of this points to the importance of reflecting on the نَعَمْ. Imam al-Ghazali says: look at your food. Allah says that in the Quran — فَلْيَنظُرِ ٱلْإِنسَنُ إِلَىٰ طَعَامِهِۦٓ — إِنَّا صَبَبْنَا الْمَاءَ صَبًّا ثُمَّ شَقَقْنَا الْأَرْضَ شَقًّا — We poured down rain, then split the earth open. You can see it happen: when the rain reaches the seed, اخْتَلَطَ بِهِ نَبَاتُ الْأَرْضِ — it mingles with what is in the earth, and then the earth breaks open. فَالِقُ ٱلْحَبِّ وَٱلنَّوَىٰ — Allah is the Splitter; He causes the earth to burst forth. So Allah says: فَلْيَنظُرِ ٱلْإِنسَنُ إِلَىٰ طَعَامِهِۦٓ — إِنَّا صَبَبْنَا الْمَاءَ صَبًّا شَقَقْنَا الْأَرْضَ شَقًّا فَأَنبَتْنَا فِيهَا حَبًّا وَعِنَبًا وَقَبًّا وَزَيْتُونًا — the grapes, the olives, the dates, the grain, the fodder for animals from which we benefit in turn. And there is إِبًّا in there too — the pasture that animals eat, from which we benefit indirectly: the animals consume it, and we eat the meat, receiving that nourishment at one remove. Allah is telling us: look at these things, reflect on them, and recognize that all of it is from Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. The more you do that, the less inclined you will be to forget the نِعْمَةٌ of Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, and the more you will honor it.

<!-- page 210 -->

The importance of this cannot be overstated. He says: وَذِكْرَ الْآيَاتِ الْمُرْجِفَةِ غَيْرِهَا — keep before you the other verses that should put fear in the heart. إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُغَيِّرُ مَا بِقَوْمٍ حَتَّىٰ يُغَيِّرُوا۟ مَا بِأَنفُسِهِمْ — Allah does not change the blessings of a people and turn them into their opposites until the people themselves change, by failing to show gratitude for them. That is how the scholars interpret this verse. Allah gives you a نِعْمَةٌ and will not take it away until you show ingratitude for it. Allah says: ذَلِكَ بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَمْ يَكُ مُغَيِّرًۭا نِّعْمَةً أَنْعَمَهَا عَلَىٰ قَوْمٍ حَتَّىٰ يُغَيِّرُوا۟ مَا بِأَنفُسِهِمْ — that is because Allah will not change the blessing He has bestowed upon a people until they change themselves through ingratitude.

A poet captured this perfectly: إِنَّ الْمَعَاصِيَ تُزِيلُ النِّعَمَ — if you are in a state of نِعْمَةٌ, guard it, for disobedience strips نِعْمَةٌ away. Disobedience to Allah costs you your blessings. This is why Waki', when he was old and still remarkably agile, was asked the secret of it, and he replied: ثِرْكَ الْجَوَارِحْ حَفِظْنَاهَا لِلَّهِ فِي صِغَرِنَا فَحَافَظَهَا لَنَا فِي كِبَرِنَا — these are limbs we guarded for Allah's sake when we were young, so Allah has guarded them for our sake now that we are old. If you protect the نِعْمَةٌ Allah has given you, that is how you maintain it. قَيْدُ النِّعَمِ الشُّكْرُ — the thing that binds نِعْمَةٌ to you is gratitude. And Allah has announced: لَىِٕن شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ — if you are grateful for My blessings, I will increase you in them. تَأَذَّنَ رَبُّكُمْ — your Lord has declared it.

So oblivion to نِعْمَةٌ is a disaster — a genuine disaster. And related to this is what is known as اِسْتِدْرَاج: Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى allows someone to continue in disobedience while continuing to increase their blessings, so that the person concludes that Allah must be pleased with them. This is the error the Quran describes: a man given abundance says رَبِّي أَكْرَمَنِي — my Lord has honored me. But when things become tight, he says رَبِّي أَهَانَنِي — my Lord has humiliated me, has demeaned me. People read ease as a sign that Allah loves them, and hardship as a sign that Allah hates them. This is a profound mistake.

<!-- page 211 -->

This brings us to an important distinction: what actually is a نِعْمَةٌ? How do we identify one? Consider the following categories. There are things that benefit you both in the short term and the long term — عِلْم and حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ, good character, are examples. Something that benefits you in your دُنْيَا and your akhirah is a pure, unadulterated نِعْمَةٌ. Then there are things that harm you in both the short term and the long term — ignorance and سُوءُ الخُلُقِ, bad character, are examples: ضَرَرٌ فِي الحَالِ وَالمَآلِ.. Then there are things beneficial in the short term but harmful in the long term — شَهَوَاتٌ often fall into this category. Overeating, for instance: enjoyable while it is happening, but harmful in the long run — plain and simple. Beneficial short term, harmful long term. And then there is the opposite: things that are difficult in the short term but beneficial in the long term, like restraining the شَهَوَاتٌ. Stopping before you are full is hard — يَضُرُّكَ فِي الحَالِ — you do not enjoy it in the moment, but the long-term benefit is real.

The ignorant person mistakes the third category — short-term benefit with long-term harm — for a genuine نِعْمَةٌ, and is drawn to it. This is, at its root, a failure of intelligence. The difference between an intelligent person and an ignorant one is that the intelligent person always looks at the عَاقِبَة — the long-term consequence. Children cannot do this naturally. There is a well-known study that illustrates this beautifully: a child is brought into a room with a cookie on the table. The researcher says, "You can have one cookie now, or if you wait, I will give you two when I return." Consistently, the more intelligent children chose to wait — willing to forgo the short-term reward for the greater long-term gain. That is intelligence. And that is precisely what Islam teaches: الْعَاقِبَةُ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ — the ultimate outcome belongs to the God-fearing. The disbelievers think in terms of the short term, the present quarter. The Muslims are meant to think long term.

There is a worldly example of this too. The Jews, for instance, have historically been skilled at long-term thinking — at planning for future benefit. This stands in painful contrast to where many Muslims find themselves today. Many Muslims are dangerously short-sighted, which is a sign of a loss of the intelligence we once had — intelligence that came from guidance. Because Allah does not grant us تَوْفِيق through anything other than Islam. سَيِّدِنَا Umar said: نَحْنُ قَوْمٌ أَعَزَّنَا اللَّهُ بِالإِسْلَامِ فَإِذَا طَلَبْنَا الْعِزَّةَ فِي غَيْرِهِ أَذَلَّنَا اللَّهُ — we are a people whom Allah gave dignity through Islam, and if we seek dignity from anything other than Islam, Allah will humiliate us. The rules are simply different for Muslims than for others. There are universal سُنَنٌ — patterns Allah has embedded in creation for everyone — but Allah will not give Muslims تَوْفِيق except through Islam. It is impossible otherwise. The disbelievers may enjoy temporary successes through their disbelief and disobedience, but in the long term they must ultimately fail. They have no other option.

Another point he raises — there are two categories of نِعْمَةٌ: أُصُولٌُ النِّعَمِ and فُرُوعُ النِّعَمِ. The أُصُولٌ are the roots — the foundational blessings — and they are the most important. They include things like health, إِيمَان, Islam, safety of body, security in one's home and community, and general well-being. These are the core نَعَمْ. The فُرُوع are the branches — the embellishments that Allah adds on top.

Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى says of Talut in the Quran: when the Bani Israel objected to his kingship on the grounds that he had no wealth, Allah replied that He had chosen him and given him بَسْطَةًۭ فِى ٱلْعِلْمِ وَٱلْجِسْمِ — an abundance in knowledge and physical stature. This is something Allah uses to embellish certain people with. This is why سَيِّدِنَا Umar used to say: إِذَا بَعَثْتُمْ رَسُولًا فَابْعَثُوا حَسَنَ الْوَجْهِ — if you send a messenger, send someone with a good face. The reason is practical wisdom: there is an innate human inclination toward people who are attractive and personable, and Allah has placed that inclination in hearts for a purpose. This is why the Prophets were all, without exception, striking to look at. Leadership has long been associated with height as well — tall stature tends to draw followers, as history repeatedly shows. Napoleon was a conspicuous exception to that pattern, compensating with cleverness where he lacked height. But even Napoleon, for all his brilliance in certain respects, ultimately proved to be deeply short-sighted in the end.

But there is wisdom in all of this — it continues to unfold. I think everyone understands the point, so let's move on.

A man once said, in the Prophet's presence, "اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ تَمَامَ النِّعْمَةِ" — "O Allah, I ask You for the completion of blessing." The Prophet ﷺ replied, "أَتَدْرِي مَا هُوَ تَمَامُ النِّعْمَةِ؟" — "Do you know what the completion of blessing is?" The man said he did not. The Prophet ﷺ said: "دُخُولُ الْجَنَّةِ" — to enter Paradise.

That is the completion of blessing. A good life in this world is a blessing from Allah, and إِيمَان is the greatest blessing of the worldly life. But the completion of that blessing is to enter Paradise with Allah, سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى — فَادْخُلِي فِي عِبَادِي، وَادْخُلِي جَنَّتِي: "Enter among My servants, enter My Paradise." That is the ultimate نِعْمَ.

Islam itself is the نِعْمَ Allah gave us in this world — وَأَتْمَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ نِعْمَتِى: "I have completed My blessing upon you" — the blessing of Islam.

<!-- page 197 -->

There is also something Sheikh Muhammad al-Hajjar shared with me. I met him in Medina, and as we walked together toward his car, right beside بَابُ السَّلَامِ, he said: "مَنْ أَصْبَحَ مُعَافًى فِي بَدَنِهِ، آمِنًا فِي سِرْبِهِ، عِنْدَهُ قُوتُ يَوْمِهِ، فَكَأَنَّمَا حِيزَتْ لَهُ الدُّنْيَا بِحَذَافِيرِهَا" — whoever wakes up in good health, safe in what he possesses, and with enough to see him through the day, it is as though the whole world has been handed to him. That is from the Tirmidhi. Reflect on it: if you woke up this morning with عَافِيَة — good health — free from fear, and with enough provision just for today, you effectively have everything in the دُنْيَا. That is a blessing every one of us should be grateful for.

Now, there are branches of blessing — what we might call ornamental نِعْمَ. A house, for example, is a need. But extra furnishings and comforts beyond what you need are فُرُوعُ النِّعْمَةِ — accessory blessings. You can live without them. And the more blessings you have, the greater the accounting you face. The Prophet ﷺ once visited a house where there was meat, dates, and cool water, and Allah said: ثُمَّ لَتُسْأَلُونَ يَوْمَئِذٍ عَنِ النَّعِيمِ — "Then, on that Day, you will surely be asked about the blessings." The Prophet ﷺ said, "هَذِهِ هُوَ النَّعِيمُ" — "This is the blessing we will be asked about."

When some of the Companions heard this, they came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: "يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ, we have no blessings to be asked about" — they were not eating meat and dates. Jibril, عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ, then came with a message from Allah: Does he wear sandals? Does he drink cool water? The point is that you can walk without shoes, so having them is a blessing. You can drink water that is not cool, so the coolness itself is a blessing you will be asked about. Follow that thread, and there is no end to it.

<!-- page 198 -->

سَيِّدِنَا Ali said: "أَعْقَلُ النَّاسِ الزُّهَّادُ" — the most intelligent people are those who practice زُهْد, doing without. "وَأَحْمَقُ النَّاسِ الْبَخِيلُ" — and the most foolish person is the miser. Why? Because, as he put it: "حُرِمَ مِنْهُ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَعُقِبَ عَلَيْهِ فِي الْآخِرَةِ" — the miser is denied enjoyment of his wealth in this life, and then punished for it in the next. He hoards without enjoying, and the hoard becomes a burden in the hereafter. That is the definition of foolishness.

The زَاهِد, by contrast, occupies a genuine spiritual station — a مَقَام. And the Prophet ﷺ himself embodied a kind of balance in this: by modern standards, he would be considered living well below the poverty line. What he had was so little it is almost unimaginable. Yet he was not impoverished in any meaningful sense — his needs were met, and he worried about nothing. Aisha, may Allah be pleased with her, said that two months would sometimes pass without smoke rising in the house of the Prophet ﷺ. Someone asked her what they were eating, and she said: "الْأَسْوَدَيْنِ" — dates and water.

Someone asked Marad al-Tarhaaj whether a person could be a زَاهِد while possessing great wealth. He replied: "كان عثمان أزهد الناس وكان عبد الرحمن بن عوف زاهدا" — Uthman was among the most ascetic of people, and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf was also a زَاهِد, yet both were enormously wealthy. So زُهْد is a condition of the heart, not the pocket. If you have wealth and could lose it all tomorrow and remain the same person — that is a sign of زُهْد. But if losing it breaks you, and people say, "He used to be wealthy and now look at him" — that means the wealth had taken root in his heart. The زَاهِد is the one who, if it is there, says "الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ," and if it is gone, says "الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ." That is why the Prophet ﷺ chose to go hungry one day and be full the next — to ensure he never grew attached to either state. It is all about non-attachment.

The next disease is الْحُزْء — derision, mockery, making fun of people. When Musa, عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ, commanded the Bani Israel to sacrifice a cow, they responded: "أَتَتَّخِذُنَا هُزُوًۭا" — "Are you mocking us?" He replied: "أَعُوذُ بِٱللَّهِ أَنْ أَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلْجَهِلِينَ" — "I seek refuge in Allah from being among the ignorant." From this exchange, the scholars derived that اِسْتِهْزَاءٌ — mockery — is rooted in جَهْل, ignorance. To ridicule people is an act of ignorance. This includes lampooning, caricature, comedic ridicule — any form of targeting someone to make others laugh at their expense.

What the author says about its cure is that it is the same as the cure for arrogance, because mockery is a form of arrogance. To mock someone, you must first look down on them. That is كِبْرٌ. This is why the Moroccans have a saying — though it traces back to سَيِّدِنَا Ali — "لا تحقر عبدنا عسى يكون لله وليا" — do not belittle anyone, for he may be a وَلِيٌّ of Allah.

Even if you see a man drunk and vomiting in the street, you do not know what his seal will be. Imam al-Qurtubi made a striking point on this: when Umar was still bowing to idols in Mecca, he was already beloved to Allah, سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, who knew what the seal would be. The Muslims watching Umar at that stage might have mocked him — "What a fool, a polytheist" — but they did not know Umar. They were seeing one moment in a story still unfolding. That is why it is profoundly dangerous to judge someone by where they stand right now, or to mock them for it.

This is why Allah, سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, says in the Quran:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا يَسْخَرْ قَوْمٌ مِّن قَوْمٍ عَسَىٰ أَن يَكُونُوا خَيْرًا مِّنْهُمْ

"O you who believe, let not one people mock another people — perhaps they are better than them."

وَلَا نِسَآءٌۭ مِّن نِّسَآءٍ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُنَّ خَيْرًۭا مِّنْهُنَّ

"And let not women mock other women — perhaps they are better than them."

It is worth recalling here that the Prophet ﷺ was commanded not to curse the idols of the polytheists:

لَا تَسُبُّوا الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ فَيَسُبُّوا اللَّهِ عَدْوًا بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ

"Do not revile those they call upon besides Allah, lest they revile Allah out of ignorance and hostility."

This is the principle of سَدُّ الذَّرَائِعِ — blocking the means to harm. Muslims burning Israeli flags or American flags in public — what does that provoke? They go and burn Qurans, or they stomp on a Saudi flag that bears لا إله إلا الله محمد رسول الله. You incite them into an act that is not only harmful to them but sacrilegious to us. We do not want people cursing Allah. There is no good reason to do it.

This is also why the Prophet ﷺ, even in his greatest victories, was never boastful. When he entered Mecca, his head was bowed low — not the posture of a conquering hero, but of a man in total humility before his Lord. That is the truly great leader. The other kind — the triumphant swagger, the chest-thumping — that is جَاهِلِيَّةٌ. That is أَبُو جَهْل. We are meant to be above it, not in a superficial way, but at a deep level.

You can, of course, make دُعَاء against the oppressors. The Quran contains supplications against the ظَالِمِينَ. But there is a difference between that and public spectacles of contempt. And making دُعَاء for people's guidance is مُسْتَحَبٌّ — recommended, though not obligatory.

Some scholars even cautioned against cursing "Israel" by name, because it is the name of a prophet — the Prophet Ya'qub, عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ. Beyond that, many Jews have embraced Islam throughout history and continue to do so. They are human beings, not demons. The Serbs who carried out atrocities are human beings too. Even Wahshi, who killed Hamza — even Hind, who mutilated his body — they both became Muslim. People can make تَوْبَةٌ. People change. And those who have committed terrible acts often carry those acts with them for the rest of their lives. More veterans of the Vietnam War have taken their own lives than were killed in the war itself — many people do not know that. They paid, and keep paying. They have hearts. As Allah says:

إِن يَمْسَسْكُمْ قَرْحٌۭ فَقَدْ مَسَّ ٱلْقَوْمَ قَرْحٌۭ مِّثْلُهُۥ

"If you are wounded, they are wounded just as you are." We all bleed red.

As for reading the Quran while being among the oppressors it condemns — the Prophet ﷺ said: "رُبَّ قَرِئَنَّ الْقُرَانَ وَالْقَرَانَ يَلْعَنَهُ" — perhaps a man recites the Quran while the Quran is cursing him. Because Allah says: أَلَا إِنَّ لَعْنَةَ اللَّهِ عَلَى الْكَاذِبِينَ — "Verily, the curse of Allah is upon those who lie." If you are reading the Quran and you are a liar, you are reading a curse upon yourself.

And that brings us to the final point on this disease. The author says its treatment follows the same path as the treatment of كِبْرٌ, which we have already covered. But he adds one more element of self-knowledge: recognize that the purpose of mockery is to humiliate someone else. That is what drives اِسْتِهْزَاءٌ. Yet in doing so, a person humiliates himself before Allah and earns in return only ignorance. Allah says: لَا تَنَابَزُوا بِالْأَلْقَابِ — "Do not insult one another with offensive labels" — and the consequence is that evil comes back upon you.

<!-- page 202 -->

Now we move to the final and comprehensive section: a treatment plan for the diseases of the heart as a whole. The author addresses the root causes of those diseases, the source of good and bad character, and treatment through the ذِكْرٌ of Allah. That will be the last section, and as I mentioned, we have essentially covered the full ground of the preceding material.

One more thing before we close — and I am glad I remembered it. Does anyone know the seven deadly sins? Any former Catholics in the room?

Pride. Anger. Envy. Sloth. Greed. Gluttony. Lust.

Now, of all those seven, which ones did the author cover? Pride — yes. Anger — yes. Envy — yes. Sloth — essentially, in its spiritual dimension. Greed — yes. That is five. But gluttony and lust — did he cover those?

He mentions شَهْوَة in relation to causes and contributing factors, but he does not treat it as a standalone disease. And that is deliberate. Both gluttony and lust are فِطْرَة inclinations — built into human nature by design. This is why even in the Catholic tradition they were placed in a different category; the other five were considered graver spiritual failings than these two.

Imam al-Ghazali addresses gluttony and lust in their own dedicated book — كَسْرُ الشَّهْوَتَيْنِ, "Breaking the Two Desires." Part of that work has been translated beautifully by Abd al-Hakim Murad — T. J. Winter — with excellent notes, and I would strongly recommend it as a companion to what we have been studying here.

<!-- page 203 -->

The connection between the two is important: gluttony tends to fuel lust. Feed the appetite excessively, and the other desire grows alongside it. The link is not absolute — people who fast rigorously can still struggle with lust — but the general pattern holds. This is why, when the Prophet ﷺ spoke to young men who could not afford to marry, he said: "فَعَلَيْكُمْ بِالصِّيَامِ" — "Then you must fast" — "فَإِنَّهُ لَكُمْ وِجَاءٌ" — "for it will act as a shield." The وِجَاءٌ was a small pouch placed on a goat to prevent it from impregnating others — a form of restraint. Fasting functions the same way: it dampens and subdues that desire.

Both hunger and sexual desire are part of human nature. They are not meant to be eliminated — you do not want to eradicate شَهْوَة any more than you want to eradicate شَهِيَّة. They share the same Arabic root: desire for food and desire for intimacy. The goal is not suppression but mastery — balance, not obliteration. And the book is worth reading for anyone who wants to work through these two more deeply.

I mention all this because, looking at the seven deadly sins, the author covers five of them in considerable depth. Those two he leaves largely untouched here, though they are not absent from his broader work. In any case — إِن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ — we are approaching closure on the diseases of the heart. What remains now is the comprehensive treatment plan: the general remedies, the root causes of corrupt character, the sources of virtue and vice, and the central role of ذِكْرٌ in healing the heart.

So he says, وَقَبُّوا أَمْرَادِ الْقُلُوبِ الْجَامِعُ فَهُنَّا نَهْيُ النَّفْسِ عَمَّا تَتْفَعُ — that the comprehensive and general treatment plan for the diseases of the heart is to prohibit the self from its own desires, coupled with — and then he gives us: وَالصَّغَبُ وَالسَّهْرُ اللَّيَالِ وَالصَّمْتُ وَالفِكْرَةُ وَهُوَ خَالِي — hunger, nighttime vigil, silence, and meditation in private.

He says, رَحِمَهُ اللَّهُ, that in order to treat the self — the heart itself — you must prohibit the self from what it desires. There is a hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ said: لا يؤمن أحدكم حتى يكون هواه تبعاً لما يأتوا به — that none of you truly believes until his هَوًى, his caprice or desire, conforms to what I have brought.

The way you purify the heart, then, is this: all those diseases he just enumerated are forbidden — they run contrary to the guidance Allah and His Messenger have given us. So the Messenger of Allah ﷺ tells us that faith is incomplete, that the heart is not sound, until your هَوًى accords with what he brought — which is guidance. And the way to achieve that is by withholding the self from precisely those things that conflict with guidance, until you no longer desire them.

This is the meaning of the verse: وَنَفْسُ كَطِفْرِي إِن تَهْمِلْهُ شَبَّعَ لَحُبَّ الرَّضَاعِ وَإِن تَفْتَمْهُ يَنْفَطِمِي — the nafs is like an infant: if you neglect it, it grows up craving to suckle; but if you wean it, the craving fades. Think about it — there are people in this culture, like followers of La Leche League, who breastfeed until the child is four or five years old. The child just keeps wanting it, and they keep going. But if you wean a child at two years, or one year, or six months — whatever age you choose — the desire passes. The Sharia actually prefers weaning at two years, and there is wisdom in that.

There was a study done in Israel — this is a digression — that performed a statistical analysis comparing breastfed children with children who were not breastfed. Correlations in studies like that are always the tricky part, but the finding was an increase in IQ of three points for every month of breastfeeding. There are obviously many variables in a study of that kind, but it is interesting, because the Prophet ﷺ said in a hadith: إِيَّاكُمْ وَالْحَمْقَىٰ فَإِنَّهَا تُغَذِّي — beware of the wet nurse who lacks intelligence, for she is nourishing the brain. Traditionally, people sought out women of sound mind and good character to serve as wet nurses. And Halima — Halima Sa'diyah, رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ — is a remarkable example of this. Read her hadith. If you do not know Arabic, read it in translation. In every hadith collection that includes it, the scholars have to annotate nearly every word because her Arabic is so elevated. She was extraordinarily eloquent. عَلَيْهَا السَّلَامُ — she is the nursing mother of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

This is what he is pointing us toward. And it is the same teaching found in the Quran: وَأَمَّا مَنْ خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ وَنَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَى فَإِنَّ الْجَنَّةَ هِيَ الْمَأْوَى — the one who fears standing before his Lord, and then actually restrains the self from what it desires — for such a person, Paradise is the abode.

So this is the work of مُجَاهَدَةُ قَمْعِ النَّفْسِ — literally suppressing the desires of the nafs. It is self-discipline. مَنْ دَانَ نَفْسَهُ — you must discipline the soul. Discipline the soul and you will find happiness. Fail to discipline it and you will be miserable. Look at all the self-help literature in this culture — it is overwhelmingly about people who cannot control themselves.

This is precisely why Islam gives us prayer: to train us in discipline over our time and our days. It gives us ritual washing: to discipline us in cleanliness. It gives us fasting — أَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلِّمْ — which is entirely about learning discipline: disciplining the tongue, the stomach, the genitals. Everything Islam brings relates, at its core, to learning how to discipline yourself.

Because if you have no discipline over time, you will not pray on time. Especially in this culture, where there is no adhan ringing out and no mosques on every corner, you truly have to stay vigilant. Whenever you travel to a Muslim-majority country from here, it is striking how effortless prayer becomes. The difference is enormous. Here, maintaining that discipline requires active effort — and that effort itself is part of the training.

So you discipline the soul, discipline the tongue. You learn to catch yourself when you feel the urge to backbite — stopping and saying inwardly, I don't want to do this. You have to learn to do that. It is a process. But if you do it enough, it reaches a point where you simply are not doing it anymore. And that is where the هَوًى finally comes into alignment with what the Messenger brought. That is what he is telling us.

Now he gives us the means. What are the core things the soul craves? صَغَب — hunger. The stomach. كَسْرَ شَهْوَتَيْنِ — control the stomach and you gain control over the genitals as well. The stomach is the primary driving impulse. If you can learn to master your eating, that is why one of the early masters, as quoted by Imam al-Qushayri, said: for me to lift my hand from the plate while I am still hungry is better than spending an entire night in prayer. Because if a person can discipline that one thing, everything else becomes easier.

People get very agitated when food is delayed. They blame it on hypoglycemia — and there are people who genuinely have that condition, I do not want to dismiss that — but there are also people who simply become angry if food is not on time. You see it in restaurants; waitstaff deal with it constantly. Where is my food? And that is America: fast food, drive-throughs, get it now. The infantile impulse — because the very first thing a newborn does is cry for food. The mother hears the cry and her milk flows. The baby gets the breast and immediately relaxes into a state of complete contentment. That is the infantile pattern, and it is what we must all work to discipline within ourselves: the ability to do without food, to not become grumpy over it, to recognize that missing a single meal will not kill you. People say, "I'm starving." You are not starving. There are people in the world who are genuinely starving — truly dropping from hunger — but missing a meal is not that.

This is why the early شُيُوخ focused on hunger above almost everything else in their teaching. This does not mean becoming anorexic. The point is simply: learn discipline. Two meals a day is enough — I can say that with confidence. Morning and late afternoon. If you want a third, make it something light in the evening. But two genuine meals are sufficient.

There is a man mentioned — he was from Yemen, from the tribe of Bani Hanifa, actually of the Hanafi persuasion, and I cannot recall his name at this moment — who came to the Prophet ﷺ, and the story is in al-Bukhari. The Prophet ﷺ gave him a bowl of milk. He drank it. The Prophet asked if he wanted more. He said yes. The Prophet kept giving, bowl after bowl, until the man had drunk seven bowls. The next day he embraced Islam, and that evening when he returned, he could only manage one bowl. The Companions who had watched him drink seven the night before were astonished. They asked the Prophet ﷺ about it. He said: وَالْمُؤْمِنُ يَأْكُلُ فِي مِعًى وَاحِدٍ وَالْكَافِرُ يَأْكُلُ فِي سَبْعَةِ أَمْعَاءٍ — the disbeliever eats from seven intestines, وَالْمُؤْمِنُ يَأْكُرُوا۟ مِنْ مَعِينٍ وَاحِدٍ — but the believer eats from one. So إِيمَان actually reduces the appetite.
