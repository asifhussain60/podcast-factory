The next disease is غِلّ. Ghil is a disease of the heart related to rancor — resentful malice. The root word is a ثُلَاثِي, a three-letter root that can go three ways: غِلٌّ, غِلٌّ, and غِلّ. The غِلٌّ or the غُلّ is a قَيْد, a shackle. The people of the Fire have a غِلٌّ that binds them. The Prophet ﷺ was sent to relieve humanity of their burdens and the things that were binding them — that is what the غِلٌّ is. And غِلّ is when the heart becomes bound, as though shackled, to treachery, malice, or the desire to betray or harm someone. There is a lock holding it in place, just as a handcuff has the mechanism that holds it closed.

What holds the غِلّ together is resentful malice — something that results from being angry at a person and keeping that anger inside the heart until it festers and eventually becomes a desire to harm that person. This is why among the things اللَّه mentions in the Qur'an is the supplication: do not place in our hearts غِلّ toward the believers. You do not want غِلّ in your heart toward any believer, because it will kill your heart. Literally kill it.

One thing اللَّه does in جَنَّة is remove غِلّ entirely. مُؤْمِنِيْن may die still carrying غِلّ, but you do not want to bring it with you. اللَّه says in the Qur'an: وَنَزَعۡنَا مَا فِی صُدُورِهِم مِّنۡ غِلٍّ — "And We will remove whatever غِلّ is in their hearts," and He makes them brothers, reclining together on couches.

In the دُنْيَا, سَيِّدِنَا عَلِيٍّ had an experience of this. A man who had fought against him — it may have been at the Battle of جَمَال — came to him and apologized. And that is the verse سَيِّدِنَا عَلِيٍّ recited: We removed from their hearts. He was saying, الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, اللَّه will take that غِلّ out of the heart. You can carry rancor toward a person and اللَّه can remove it. So ask اللَّه to remove the غِلّ from you.

He then tells us how to do it: أَحْسِنْ إِلَيْهِ — do good to that person. That is what the man did when he went to سَيِّدِنَا عَلِيٍّ. He humbled himself and apologized, and that was his إِحْسَان. If you have غِلّ toward a مُؤْمِن, do good to him. The nature of إِحْسَان is captured in a حَدِيثٌ: جُبِلَتِ الْقُلُوبُ عَلَى حُبِّ مَنْ أَحْسَنَ إِلَيْهَا — by their nature, hearts are inclined to love those who do good to them. اللَّه has built this inclination into the heart regardless of a person's will. If you do good to someone, اللَّه has made his heart naturally drawn toward you.

<!-- page 139 -->

Now some of the Arabs noted — and an Arab poet said — that when you do good to a noble person you win him over, but if you do good to a lowly person it can make him more rebellious, more agitated. So there are people like that. But you have done your part. If you do good to someone in order to cure your own heart, that is what اللَّه is asking of you. You do your best. If the other person does not respond, you have done what was in your power. You are then left to make دُعَاء for them and to remember that اللَّه can forgive them.

He also says this will cause your enemies — meaning شَيْطَان and his minions who love to sow غِلّ among the believers — to despair. شَيْطَان loves to do this. He loves to set the believers at each other's throats. I once asked a شَيْخٌ why it is that so many disbelievers seem to live peacefully together without much conflict, while even sincere Muslims often fight one another. He said: because شَيْطَان is not occupied with the disbelievers anymore. They are already in كُفْرٌ — he can leave them alone. But he is obsessed with the people of إِيمَان. He works on them constantly, trying to make them stray. That is why, as has been transmitted, شَيْطَان has despaired of being worshipped on the Arabian Peninsula, but he has not despaired of causing فِتْنَةٌ and dissension among the people. We have to be vigilant against him.

And تُقْنِتُهُ — the way you cause شَيْطَان to despair when غِلّ begins to arise in the heart is to reject it and to do good to that person at precisely the moment you want to harm them.

Then remember what came in the authentic حَدِيثٌ about Mondays and Thursdays. According to that حَدِيثٌ, the Prophet ﷺ said that اللَّه forgives people on those two days, and when the angels bring the records, they say: there are two people here who are in conflict, both believers. And اللَّه says: leave them — leave them until they reconcile with one another. What this means is that if you carry غِلّ toward a fellow believer, اللَّه is not forgiving you. If you are at war with another Muslim, that forgiveness is withheld. All you have to do is extend the greeting with sincerity. That is all. Do not turn your back. If you say السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ and they do not reply, you have fulfilled your obligation. It is then in their hands. Do what you can — that is all you are responsible for. But this is a frightening حَدِيثٌ. You do not want animosity toward another believer, because it will prevent you from receiving اللَّه's forgiveness.
