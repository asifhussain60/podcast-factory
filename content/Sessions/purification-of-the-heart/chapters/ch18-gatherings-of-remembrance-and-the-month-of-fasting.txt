وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ غَلَبَةِ الدَّيْنِ وَقَهْرِ الرِّجَالِ — "I seek refuge in You from overwhelming debt and from being overpowered by men." These two are paired deliberately. If you study history, you will find that one of the primary means by which the kufar gained control over the Muslims was through debt. They would lend Muslims money — on interest — and when the Muslims could not repay, the debt had compounded to a point where they could no longer meet their obligations. That is when the demands for concessions began. Debt is a mechanism of control. In this country, the entire economic system is designed to put people into debt. The tax laws are structured that way. The banking system was built that way — because those who hold your debt hold power over you. They make people into economic slaves. People will resist physical enslavement — they will rebel against it. But economic slavery is different: it comes with guilt. In German, the word for debt — Schuld — is the same word for guilt. When you are in debt, you feel you owe something; you feel diminished. And through that sense of obligation, people become enslaved. This is a deep wisdom in seeking refuge from debt and from being overpowered by others — because the two go together.

This is also why the scholars said: if there were anything better to ask for than عَافِيَة — and عَافِيَة in its full meaning encompasses Islam itself, as well as spiritual, physical, and economic well-being — the Prophet ﷺ would have told his uncle عَبَّاس to ask for it. So asking اللهُ for عَافِيَة is among the most comprehensive of supplications.

And this is why, when سَيِّدِنَا عُمَر went to شَمّ and found سَيِّدِنَا مُعَاوِيَة wearing a magnificent شَامِي robe, richly ornamented, عُمَر was troubled by it.

And سَيِّدِنَا عُثْمَان said to سَيِّدِنَا عُمَر: these are people — if you come to them dressed the way we dress in the desert, they will think nothing of you. That was the wisdom behind it. سَيِّدِنَا عُمَر did remove his robe and put on a finer one, then mounted a بَرْزُونٌ — but then he dismounted, put his own robes back on, and refused. He simply said: اللهُ gave us dignity through our Islam, not through outward trappings. And if we seek honor in anything other than that, اللهُ will humiliate us.

Remarkably, in Jerusalem, their own books had foretold that the man who would open the city would have — I believe — seventy-three patches on his robe. When they counted the patches on سَيِّدِنَا عُمَرُ robe, the number matched what was written in their books. Seventy-two? Seventeen? I am not entirely certain of the exact figure.

Now, regarding the last point someone raised — about group ذِكْرٌ: there is a scholarly disagreement on this. Some scholars considered it a blameworthy innovation — a بِدْعَةٌ مَكْرُوهَةٌ — meaning not forbidden outright, but disapproved. What troubled them was the absence of a clear narration showing that the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ or the Companions practiced it in that specific form. Imam الشَّافِعِيُّ, on the other hand, considered it permissible and saw nothing wrong with it. He drew on حَدِيثٌ indicating that there was a teacher with a group of learners, and that the Prophet ﷺ sat among those learning and said, "I was sent as a teacher" — though he did not rebuke the other group either. However, because of the weakness of that particular narration, it is difficult to derive a firm ruling from it alone.

There is, however, a completely sound حَدِيثٌ stating that اللهُ has traveling angels — مَلَائِكَةٌ سَيَّارَةٌ — who roam the earth searching for circles of remembrance, حِلَقَاتُ الذِّكْرِ. When they come upon one, they descend upon it. Then, when they return to اللهُ, He asks them — and this is to teach us; اللهُ, of course, knows — "How did you find My servants?" And they reply: "We found them saying..." The حَدِيثٌ indicates they were gathered in a حَلْقَة.

Some scholars derived from this that it is permissible to gather for ذِكْرٌ, but not necessarily to recite in unison, since the حَدِيثٌ does not specify a single voice. Other scholars held that there is no prohibition on reciting in unison, since ذِكْرٌ itself is permissible and no clear prohibition of that form exists. They noted that there are حَدِيثٌ showing the Companions did say certain things together — the تَكْبِير and the like — so collective vocalization is not without precedent.

These are خِلَافِي matters. The majority of scholars, particularly the later ones, permitted group ذِكْرٌ. The موحِّدٌ actively encouraged it in Morocco, including the communal recitation of the قُرْآن in a single voice. They established a practice in Morocco that continues to this day: completing the entire قُرْآن every month in the mosques. The موحِّدٌ initiated this because they observed that people had stopped reading the قُرْآن — and by setting a collective recitation in the mosque, people would join in simply because it was there and it was a good thing to do. Their reasoning was that it was better for people to read and complete the قُرْآن than not to read it at all.

Ibn al-Hajj disagreed with this in his مَدْخَل. On the question of poetry, he noted that the Prophet ﷺ loved to hear it — there are many sound حَدِيثٌ on this — and that he would listen and say, in effect, "Yes, let us hear more." The Arabs used to sing their poetry — the أَلْحَانُ الْعَرَبِ — in their gatherings. So in the tradition of نَشِيدٌ, such as one finds in Palestine, Syria, and Morocco, there are sung poems in praise of اللهُ and of the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ. That is permissible, and it is in fact a wholesome form of

<!-- page 304 -->

entertainment — beautiful to listen to when done well, with good voices. People would be far better served listening to that than to music and the like.

As for a translation — I do not have one written down, but I just gave you one, more or less, so it is on tape. It would need to be polished, since I rendered it on the spot. But even without the meanings, these things carry benefit.

On the question of what Abu Hanifa said about ذِكْرٌ — as far as I know, the Hanafi مَذْهَب accepts group ذِكْرٌ. And historically, Muslims did practice it. If you study the early books, you will find that group ذِكْرٌ was indeed practiced.

Murabit al-Hajj does not do it in that form — he is like Ibn al-Hajj in the مَذْهَب, cautious about anything that even carries a scent of بِدْعَة. But he does not make إِنْكَار of others, because he is a scholar and he knows the ruling. He himself simply exercises caution — that is all. And yet if you spend time with him, all he does is ذِكْرٌ. Being in his company is itself a form of group ذِكْرٌ, because everyone around him is in a state of remembrance. So, الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ.

The essential point is: remember اللهُ. Those who remember اللهُ standing, sitting, and reclining on their sides — that is the standard. And there is a حَدِيثٌ that Sheikh Khatri mentioned in which the Prophet ﷺ said: "Shall I not tell you what the best of your actions is, the purest in the sight of your Lord, the highest in your ranks, better for you than spending gold and silver, and better for you than that you should go out and strike the necks of the enemy and they strike yours?" They said: "Tell us, O Messenger of اللهُ." He said: "The remembrance of اللهُ — ذِكْرُ اللَّهِ."

<!-- page 305 -->

The scholars stipulate, of course, that if a city is under attack, جِهَادٌ takes precedence over everything. So these matters must always be understood in context. If defenders are needed at the walls and a group instead sits saying "ذِكْرٌ is better" — that is ignorance and foolishness. Everything has its context. But in its proper place, ذِكْرٌ is the highest act. This is why the scholars have said that performing voluntary حَجٌّ — when the communal obligation of جِهَادٌ is already being fulfilled — is superior to going out on voluntary جِهَادٌ. The reason is fundamental: جِهَادٌ is a means to an end, and an end always ranks above the means. We were not created to kill people. We were created to remember اللهُ. But if people are preventing us from remembering اللهُ, killing us for it, then we must go out and stop them — and that is the point.

So جِهَادٌ holds the immense rank that it holds. اللهُ says in the قُرْآن:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اسْتَجِيبُوا لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ إِذَا دَعَاكُمْ لِمَا يُحْيِيكُمْ

"O you who believe, answer اللهُ and His Messenger when they call you to what brings you to life." The scholars have said, nearly by consensus, that this أَيَّهْ is interpreted as referring to جِهَادٌ — for that is what brings this أُمَّة back to life. So let no one belittle جِهَادٌ or the station of the مُجَاهِدٌ. The مُجَاهِدٌ is honored in this أُمَّة, and all of us should carry within ourselves a longing for شَهَادَة. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever dies and never even entertained in his heart the thought "I wish I could go on جِهَادٌ" dies a form of spiritual death — a نِفَاق.

<!-- page 306 -->

So جِهَادٌ is immense. I am not belittling it in any way. But what people have forgotten is this: what will give Muslims the certainty — the يَقِين — they need when they go out to fight, and what will make them capable of fighting solely for اللهُ's sake, is ذِكْرٌ of اللهُ. Because there are many Muslims today who want to go out and fight simply because they are angry. Truly. They are angry at the world, angry at the Americans, angry at one thing or another. And there is much in the world that is legitimately angering. But anger alone is not spirituality. It is not a purified state of the نَفْس. Dhikr is what rectifies the heart and elevates its motivation from the self to اللهُ. That is the work.

As for the مَوْلِد — that is another خِلَافِي matter. Personally, I believe the scholars settled these questions centuries ago, and it is genuinely saddening that we are still debating them. The formal celebration of the مَوْلِد on a fixed date was not something the first community practiced in that organized form. But there is no doubt that they were celebrating him every single day of their lives. There is a sound حَدِيثٌ in which a man asked the Prophet ﷺ whether he could fast on Mondays, and the Prophet replied: "That is the day I was born" — indicating it was an excellent day to observe the fast. That is a sound حَدِيثٌ.

There is also a حَدِيثٌ in which the Prophet ﷺ mentioned that Abu Lahab — the bitter enemy of Islam — is given a small relief from punishment every Monday in جَهَنَّم, amounting to

<!-- page 307 -->

water between his thumb and forefinger, because he freed a slave girl on the day the Prophet was born. In other words, even Abu Lahab's spontaneous joy at the Prophet's birth earned him a weekly reprieve from torment. So one should never say "we do not celebrate the Prophet's birthday." And I have heard people say that. There is a حَدِيثٌ that a man will utter a word without thinking, and it will carry him further than he imagines. You should never say that. If you wish to frame it in فِقْه terms, you may say: "To celebrate his birthday on a specific fixed date is something the سَلَفٌ did not formalize." But to say "we do not celebrate his birthday" — no. Never. We celebrate his birth. We should be happy every day of our lives that he was born, because it is only through him that we came to know اللهُ, and it is only through his sacrifice that we have the دِينٌ today. We should celebrate every single day that he came into this world.

Holding a gathering on the twelfth of رَبِيعُ الأَوَّلِ is a later innovation, yes. Most scholars called it a بِدْعَةٌ حَسَنَةٌ — a praiseworthy innovation. Some scholars were against it; that is true. But the majority were not. Imam al-Suyuti wrote a فَتْوَى saying it was a good thing and that people should do it. You can find that فَتْوَى in his collected فَتْوَىs, الْحَاوِي لِلْفَتَاوِي.

<!-- page 308 -->

So if you choose not to observe it in that form, that is fine. But consider the person whose priorities are entirely disordered: he attends the مَوْلِد celebration once a year and does nothing the Prophet commanded for the rest of the year. That is truly shameful. If you want to celebrate the Prophet ﷺ, do what he told you to do — that is what will genuinely honor him. Coming together once a year for a celebration and then ignoring his guidance for the remaining three hundred and sixty-four days is a contradiction. What is genuinely good is to hear the سِيرَةٌ, and traditionally that is what these gatherings consisted of: reciting the account of his birth and his مَنَاقِب — the noble qualities and virtues of the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ. That is a sound and beneficial practice.

On the question of which date the Prophet was actually born — that is a separate dispute between Eastern and Western scholarship. The argument over the precise date is one of the classical objections raised against fixing the celebration to a single day. Time magazine even ran an article about this once, with a photograph of someone celebrating the Prophet's birthday as though it were literally a birthday party — candles and all.

There you go. We should pray عَصْر. اللَّهُمَّ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا مَا عَلَّمْتَنَا إِنَّكَ تَعَالَىٰ حَكِيمُ الْعَلِيمِ اللَّهُمَّ افْتَحْ عَلَيْنَا حِكْمَتَكَ وَانْشُرْ عَلَيْنَا رَحْمَتَكَ يَا ذَا الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ، اللَّهُمَّ افْتَحْ عَلَيْنَا حِكْمَتَكَ وَانْشُرْ عَلَيْنَا رَحْمَتَكَ يَا ذَا الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ، اللَّهُمَّ افْتَحْ عَلَيْنَا حِكْمَتَكَ وَانْشُرْ عَلَيْنَا رَحْمَتَكَ يَا ذَا الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ، وَصَلِّ اللَّهُمَّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَصَحْبِهِ وَسَلِّمْ تَسْلِيمًا، وَلَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ الْعَلِيِّ الْعَظِيمِ، وَلَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ الْعَلِيِّ الْعَظِيمِ، وَلَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ الْعَلِيِّ الْعَظِيمِ ‏إِنَّهُۥ مِن سُلَيْمَنَ وَإِنَّهُۥ بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ ‎

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ. I think it is a blessing that we have come to the end of the مُظَاهَرَاتٌ, and for most of us I believe this is the first day of رَمَضَان — which is truly the month of مُظَاهَرَةٌ. It feels entirely fitting that we should conclude the study of the مُطَهَّرَاتُ الْقُلُوبِ at the opening of this month, because رَمَضَان is the greatest practical school for implementing everything we have learned over the past twelve weeks about the heart. The purpose of رَمَضَان is to purify the heart with اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى — that is its purpose, that is its blessing, and that is its secret: it exists to purify the heart.

I want to say something about — I'm not sure how many of you were here last night — how many people saw the moon last night? How many went out to look for it? About the same number. For those who were here, I think we all had a truly wonderful experience. Were you here with us? Who was here? Wasn't that a remarkable experience?

I was also thinking this morning about yesterday and seeing the هِلَالٌ, and something struck me that I find to be a genuinely interesting perspective. When we saw the هِلَالٌ last night for the first time, my initial reaction was uncertainty — I didn't know whether it was really there. I actually wondered whether my eyes were playing tricks on me, because it seemed to come into existence and then vanish again, flickering in and out. I realized that when we first went out, there was nothing there but the light of the sun. We had to wait. Then Venus appeared, and then, after some time, we were finally able to see that small sliver.

What I came to understand is this: اللهُ says that they ask you about the new moons, and اللهُ directed the Messenger of اللهُ to tell them that these moons are simply markers of time. But the مُفَسِّرُونَ explain that the reason people asked about the هِلَالٌ was that they were trying to understand the mechanism — how does this new moon come into being? The reason for that question is that people in antiquity actually believed that every moon was literally a new moon: that it disappeared completely and then began again from nothing. They wanted to understand how that was happening.

What I realized is that اللهُ, in His response, did not negate their belief — He simply explained why the moon is there. It is their مَوَاقِيتُ, their appointed times: a way of marking time for people, for حَجٌّ, to determine the times of رَمَضَان and حَجٌّ. And one of the things that Qabiyyullah ibn Arabi said in his تَفْسِير is that the secret of this أُمَّة using a lunar calendar rather than a solar calendar is that the sun governs worldly benefits while the moon governs otherworldly benefits. So the orientation of time for this أُمَّة is toward the next world, not toward this one.

What I also realized is this: if you look at it from a purely direct, non-abstract perspective, what you witness when you watch the new moon is what philosophers call emergence — and this is the real problem for those who deny the Creator. How do things come into existence out of nothing? Why is there something rather than nothing? That is their problem. And there you are, sitting, looking at the sky, and suddenly this thing emerges out of nothing right before your eyes — because you cannot see it initially. The reason you cannot see it is the sun: the sun's light is too strong. The reason you are then able to see it is that the sun's light has dimmed. And what emerges is reflected light. What you are seeing is reflected light — and really, the entire creation is reflected light. اللهُ is the source; the entire creation is reflected light from اللهُ. The only reason we can see it is that اللهُ allows it to exist. He is the one who brings it into existence and the one who takes it out of existence. What you are witnessing is this extraordinary event: اللهُ is letting you see reflected light come into being. That is the new moon. That is why it is called اِسْتِهْلَال — the birth. Just as a child must be born, this is the birth of reflected light, which is creation itself, because all of it reflects اللهُ. The whole thing is a reflection of اللهُ.

كيف يجحد الاله يا عجب كيف يعصى الاله عن كيف يجحده الجاهد ولله في كل تحريكة تسكينة أبدا شاهد وفي كل شهر آية تدل على أنه واحد

How strange that we can disobey اللهُ — and stranger still that anyone can disbelieve in Him — when in every movement and every stillness there is a clear witness for اللهُ, and in everything itself there is a sign.

So the creation sets up these meanings in these images, and what we are doing is trying to decode what those meanings are. It is a genuine gift to witness the birth of the new moon — a real gift. I think people felt that last night, because instead of عِلْمُ الْيَقِينِ you had عَيْنُ الْيَقِينِ. The قُرْآن speaks of three levels: عِلْمُ الْيَقِينِ، وَعَيْنُ الْيَقِينِ، وَحَقُّ الْيَقِينِ. Imam الْغَزَالِي's example is that عِلْمُ الْيَقِينِ is like hearing a trustworthy person tell you there is a fire in the forest — you believe them, but that is not the same as seeing the fire yourself. When you go and actually see it, that is عَيْنُ الْيَقِينِ. And if you go and touch it, that is حَقُّ الْيَقِينِ. The new moon is an amazing thing in that regard. Imam al-Hakim recorded a حَدِيثٌ in which the Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, said: خَيْرُ عِبَادِ اللَّهِ الَّذِينَ يُرَاعُونَ الْأَذِنَةَ وَالْأَحِنَّةَ ذِكْرًا لِلَّهِ — the best of اللهُ's servants are those who are vigilant about new moons and prayer times as a means of remembering اللهُ. And I think for all of us last night, the natural impulse when we saw it was precisely to remember اللهُ — لِتُكَبِّرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَىٰكُمْ, اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ. And for me personally, there was a real نَشَبَ of رَمَضَان that entered my heart — a feeling, a surge of energy — because it was عَيْنُ الْيَقِينِ. That was the رَمَضَان moon. It is a real gift.

Anyway, I just wanted to share that with you. So let us review what we covered. We went through the diseases of the heart: we began by looking at the importance of أَدَب, and then we examined the حُكْم of knowing the أَمْرَاضُ الْقُلُوبِ وَأَذِيَّتِهَا and their treatments — which, as Imam الْغَزَالِي stated, constitutes a فَرْضُ عَيْنٍ — and then he went through the diseases of the heart alphabetically. He obviously gives the most attention to those that matter most. The scholars who have narrowed it down say that if you can control roughly four or five of these diseases, the rest become relatively easy to remove. But he began with بُخْل.

رَمَضَان is a month that is truly suited to curing بُخْل. They said that the Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, was the most generous of human beings and was even more generous during رَمَضَان — described as كَرِيءُ الْمُرْسَلَةِ, like a rain-bearing wind that brings gifts and causes things to flourish. So there is بُخْل. Then there is بَطَر — بَطِرَتْ مَعِيشَتَهَا... 

بَطَرَ مَعِيشَتَهُمْ: being delighted by the دُنْيَا, by one's livelihood. The believer's فَرَاءَة, his joy, is حِينَمَا يُفْطِرُ وَحِينَمَا يَلْقَى رَبَّهُ — when he breaks his fast and when he meets his Lord. So that stands in direct contrast to بَطَر.

Then there is بُغْضٌ. رَمَضَان is a time when you are supposed to address بُغْضٌ — enmity between hearts. The Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, said: أَفْضَلُ صَدَقَةٍ فِي رَمَضَانَ — the best صَدَقَةٌ is in رَمَضَان — and in another حَدِيثٌ: أَفْضَلُ صَدَقَةٍ يُصْلَحُ بَيْنَ اثْنَيْنِ — the best صَدَقَةٌ is rectifying relations between people. So the best صَدَقَةٌ of all would be reconciling between people during رَمَضَان. This is the time to remove بُغْضٌ from your heart toward others.

Then there is بَغْيٍ — oppression and transgression against others. You are fasting, and the Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, told us: if someone provokes you or fights with you — وَأَنْتَ صَائِمٌ، فَقُلْ: اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي صَائِمٌ — while you are fasting, say: I am fasting. In other words, لَا تَبْغِ: do not fight back. Tell them you don't want to go there. Say: I'm fasting.

Then there is حُبُّ الْجَاهِ — love of position and status. رَمَضَان is about gaining a position with اللهُ. The secret of fasting is that it is the only act of عِبَادَة that no one can tell you are performing while you are performing it. If you give charity, that is a visible act. If you pray, that is a visible act. If you make حَجٌّ, that is visible. All the acts of عِبَادَة that اللهُ has given us can be seen outwardly. Fasting alone is invisible — you can be standing next to someone who is not fasting and someone who is fasting, and only اللهُ knows which is which. You can be among all these non-Muslims; they see you and have no idea you are fasting. You look like everyone else. The point is that the مَنْزِلَة you are seeking is with اللهُ, not with people. That is the cure for حُبُّ الْجَاهِ.

Then there is حُبُّ الدُّنْيَا. رَمَضَان is also about weaning yourself from the دُنْيَا. The نَفْس is like a child: if you neglect to wean it, it grows up clinging to what it loves — love of دُنْيَا — but if you wean it, it becomes weaned. رَمَضَان is the time to wean the soul from the دُنْيَا and from the love of the دُنْيَا.

Then there is حُبُّ الْمَدْحِ بِلَا فِعْلٍ — loving to be praised for things you have not done. رَمَضَان is the time to witness the shortcomings of your own actions, to genuinely recognize that you have wasted the whole year, and that now it is time to make up for it. The قِيَامٌ you didn't do in the previous year — make it up now. The صَدَقَةٌ you neglected — give it now. What you are trying to do is perform actions that actually warrant praise. So this disease too has its cure in رَمَضَان.

Then there is حَسَد — envy. To purify the heart from envy. But if you can conquer desire — because envy arises from desire — then you can remove envy from your heart. And desire, according to Imam الْغَزَالِي, is rooted in the two appetites of the فَرْج and the بَطْن. If you can master those two — not destroy them, but bring them under control — then all the other desires become manageable.

Then he mentions that what we are really doing in this month is defeating the enmity of شَيْطَان by taking control of all the things شَيْطَان uses to wage war against the إِرَادَةٌ of اللهُ for human beings — His will for them, which is that they believe in Him and follow Him. That is why he mentions that اللهُ says: if you give victory to اللهُ through fasting and conquering شَيْطَان, اللهُ will give you victory and make you firm — and that is what you want. صَبْر is part of ثَبَاتٌ — steadfastness. We are learning both. And by doing that, اللهُ says He is with the مُحْسِنِينَ. He also mentions the أَيَّهْ that اللهُ will not take away His blessings from a people until they become preoccupied with their شَهَوَاتٌ. And you can see that in this society — it is the best example of a pleasure culture. Everything is oriented toward gratification. The idea of fasting is completely alien to most people now because they eat all day long. Not that long ago, people did not graze all day — there were no snack machines everywhere. This grazing culture is something new.

Now, regarding the شَيَاطِين being chained in رَمَضَان — it is only the شَيَاطِين, meaning the bad جِنٌّ, as the حَدِيثٌ indicates. And some of the عُلَمَاء, like Imam الْقَادِسِيُّ الْفَارِسِيُّ who was a Maliki scholar, was asked why people still experience bad thoughts in رَمَضَان. His explanation was that the حَدِيثٌ indicate only the كَافِرٌ جِنٌّ are locked up, while the فُسَّاق from among the believing جِنٌّ are not. He admitted, however, that he had no textual basis for that, and said that it seems a high probability — and اللهُ knows best. On a different note, it would genuinely be interesting to conduct serious statistical studies on whether crime rates go down in رَمَضَان. That would require the kind of resources and stability you have when there is a functioning خِلَافَة and peace in the land — but someone should attempt a PhD thesis on it and convince a university to fund the research.

Now, a couple of important points. Imam الْغَزَالِي, رَحِمَهُ اللَّهُ, goes into the فِقْهِي issues of fasting, which we covered last night. There are two levels of فِقْه: the outward فِقْه, which is the concern of the فُقَهَاء, and the inward فِقْه, which is the concern of the قَوْم — the صُوفِيَّة. The outward فِقْه ensures that your outward fasting is legally valid. The inward فِقْه ensures that your inward fasting is sound. A حَدِيثٌ makes this very clear, in which the Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, said that many people who fast gain nothing from their fast except hunger and thirst. Their outward fast may be correct, but inwardly they are breaking their fast constantly — because if you are backbiting, and doing all these things, you are breaking the fast of the heart.

He describes three types of fasting: صَوْمُ الْعُمُومِ — the fast of the general public; صَوْمُ الْخُصُوصِ — the fast of the elect; and صَوْمُ خُصُوصِ الْخُصُوصِ — the fast of the elect of the elect.

The fast of the common people is simply restraining the stomach and the genitals from pleasure during the daylight hours. That is the fast any Muslim is capable of observing.

The fast of the elect is كَفُّ السَّمْعِ وَالْبَصَرِ وَاللِّسَانِ وَالْيَدِ وَالرِّجْلِ — guarding the ears, the eyes, the tongue, the hands, and the feet. Remember that Ibn 'Ashir, رَحِمَهُ اللَّهُ, speaks of the seven جَوَارِح — the seven limbs — for which we are responsible: the eyes, the ears, the tongue, the hand, the stomach, the genitals, and the feet. Two of those seven are addressed by outward fasting — the stomach and the genitals — and so the person who abstains from food and sexual relations has guarded two of the seven. These are called the مَدَاخِلُ الشَّيْطَانِ, or the فُرُوقُ الشَّيْطَانِ: the entrances of شَيْطَان.

Ibn al-Qayyim, رَحِمَهُ اللَّهُ, noted that the human body is like a country: the capital is the heart, and the borders are the seven limbs. You must guard those seven limbs with vigilance. The heart is the one that sends out the command to guard the limbs — it dispatches the armies to the borders. Shaytan enters the heart through one of those seven things. So the point of fasting is to protect the borders during رَمَضَان and guard them so that the heart has a chance to draw near to اللهُ.

‏يَٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا۟ كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ ‎

Fasting is prescribed so that you may learn تَقْوَى. And تَقْوَى —

نحن التقوى ها هنا وأشار إلى قلبه

— the Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, said: نحن التقوى ها هنا وأشار إلى قلبه ثلاث مرات — تَقْوَى is here, pointing to his heart, three times. So تَقْوَى resides in the heart. What you are doing through fasting is protecting the heart — by guarding the entrances through which شَيْطَان would enter it. And اللهُ has helped you: He has placed constraints on the شَيَاطِين, giving you tremendous assistance this month. He has also helped by making it obligatory to abstain from food and relations — the two most fundamental sources of temptation. But He left the rest to us, because there must be some مُجَاهَدَة. It is up to us to guard the eyes, the tongue, the ears, the hands, and the feet. That is the fasting of the خَوَاصّ: they fast with all seven limbs. You guard your tongue, guard your eyes, guard your hands, guard your feet. Do not look at the حَرَمٌ. Do not speak the حَرَمٌ. Do not listen to the حَرَمٌ. If someone starts to backbite in your presence, protect yourself — don't let it reach your ears. Guard your fast.

The دُعَاء of the Prophet about رَمَضَان was: اللَّهُمَّ سَلِّمْنَا رَمَضَانَ وَسَلِّمْ رَمَضَانَ مِنَّا — O اللهُ, give us رَمَضَان safely, and protect رَمَضَان from our evil. You do not want رَمَضَان to receive any portion of your evil. You want to receive رَمَضَان's portion of good. This is why رَمَضَان carries what is known as حُرْمَةٌ زَمَانِيَّةٌ — the sanctity of time. Wrong action committed in رَمَضَان is graver than wrong action at other times, because اللهُ has given this month a sanctity that must be honored. And you honor it by refraining from what اللهُ has forbidden.

One of the things اللهُ has told the human being is that we are to deliver the أَمَانَةٌ to those who are entitled to it — that is one of the signs of the believer. The believer gives back what has been entrusted to him. The word مُؤْمِن comes from the same root as أَمَانَةٌ. A person who has no أَمَانَةٌ has no إِيمَان. اللهُ has given us a trust: our sight, our hearing, our tongue, and above all the heart itself, which generates all the actions of the other limbs. You are مَسْؤُول — responsible — for all of them. They are trusts that اللهُ has placed in your care, and you must return the أَمَانَةٌ on يَوْمُ الْقِيَامَةِ, returning it intact, not having corrupted it. Fasting is اللهُ teaching us how to honor the trust and return it to Him the way He wants it returned: the tongue free of slander, the ears free of what is حَرَمٌ, the eyes free of what is حَرَمٌ, the hands free of حَرَمٌ acts, the feet free of walking toward what is حَرَمٌ, the genitals guarded, and the stomach free of consuming what is حَرَمٌ. That is what اللهُ wants. And the مَدْرَسَة through which He is teaching us to achieve this — is fasting.

Then there is the highest level: the fast of the elect of the elect. He defines it as صَوْمُ الْقَلْبِ عَنِ الْهِمَمِ الدَّنِيَّةِ وَالْأَفْكَارِ الدَّنِيَّةِ — the fasting of the heart from base aspirations and lowly thoughts. In other words, the fast of the elect of the elect is that the heart's aspiration is not directed toward the دُنْيَا at all. That is why in رَمَضَان, giving out is the mode of the آخِرَة, whereas in other months we are often busy acquiring wealth. In رَمَضَان, you should be busy giving wealth away for the sake of اللهُ. You are not in a دُنْيَا mode — you are in an آخِرَة mode. You have to shift this month. That is the shift.

And regarding الْأَفْكَارُ الدَّنِيَّةُ: one of the scholars of Andalusia said that the first degree of وِلَايَة with اللهُ is مُحَاسَبَةُ الْخَوَاطِرِ — beginning to hold one's thoughts accountable. That is where it begins: the وَلِيٌّ measures each thought against the مِيزَان of the شَرِيعَةٌ. That is what makes him a وَلِيٌّ. رَمَضَان is the time to do مُحَاسَبَة of the heart.
