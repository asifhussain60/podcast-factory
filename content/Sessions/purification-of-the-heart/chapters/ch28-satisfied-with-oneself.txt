Now we come to the root causes of the diseases. And what I will do is cover the treatment of diseases through ذِكْر next week — إِن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ, شَيْخ خَتْرِي will be here, and I will ask him to teach that class. He knows this book deeply. He loves it. He has spent a great deal of time with it. I have read sections of it with him — and I read much of it with شَيْخٌ Abdullah as well. We actually completed a خَتْمٌ of it on حَجٌّ, on the day of عَرَفَات. We went through every disease, named them all, and made دُعَاء asking اللهُ to purify us of all of them. إِن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ, for all of us.

Now he says: the comprehensive root — in the view of both Imam al-هِلَالi and Imam Ibn Askar — of all these diseases is love of the temporal world, حُبُّ الدُّنْيَا. There is some difference of opinion on this, as we saw: Ibn Abbad considered the root to be طَمَعٌ — desire. But you can see how طَمَعٌ flows from حُبُّ الدُّنْيَا. And when Ibn Askar says the root is حُبُّ الرِّيَاسَةِ — love of status and power — even that is حُبُّ الدُّنْيَا, because why do you want status? You want power, you want the world. So in the end it really does come back to حُبُّ الدُّنْيَا. There is a حَدِيث on this — though it is classified as weak — رَأْسُ كُلِّ خَطِيئَةٍ حُبُّ الْعَاجِلَةِ: the love of this world is at the root of every wrong action.

ابْنُ عَطَاءِ اللهِ, on the other hand, considered the root to be a man's self-satisfaction with himself. That is a distinct and fascinating take. He was a مَالِكِي jurist who died in 709 AH, corresponding to 1309 CE, and he is considered one of the greatest masters of this science. He wrote the حِكَم. Imam al-Banna said that had it been permissible to recite something other than the قُرْآن in prayer, it would have been permissible to recite the حِكَم — a clear hyperbole, but an indication of the profound esteem in which the work was held.

<!-- page 202 -->

In the thirty-fifth aphorism, ابْنُ عَطَاءِ اللهِ says that the source of every disobedience, every indifference, and every passion is self-satisfaction with the self. And the source of every obedience, every vigilance, and every virtue is dissatisfaction with the self. He says: it is better for you to keep company with an ignorant man who is dissatisfied with himself than with a learned man who is satisfied with himself. And then: what knowledge is there in a self-satisfied scholar? And what ignorance is there in an unlearned man who is dissatisfied with himself — and who therefore keeps asking, keeps wanting to learn?

This connects directly to something strange about modern culture. Think of the whole industry of self-help books aimed at getting rid of shame, building confidence, feeling good about yourself. But why is the person standing in that self-help aisle in the first place? Because he is dissatisfied with himself. And if he were completely self-satisfied, he would never pick up a book. The author of the book, incidentally, has no real interest in your becoming self-satisfied — because then you would stop buying books. Most of that industry is built on keeping you just dissatisfied enough to keep purchasing. But the point stands: dissatisfaction with the self is what drives a person to want to change and improve.

If anyone here were truly satisfied with themselves, you would not be in this class studying the diseases of the heart. The very fact that people have these struggles is what moves them to seek help. If you feel you lack guidance, you seek guidance. The one who submits to اللهُ begins seeking because he knows he needs اللهِ direction. But if you are walking through life perfectly content — I am already guided, I have no need — you will never reach out.

Now, Sidi Ahmad Zarruq identifies three signs of contentment with the soul.

The first is seeing that the حَقٌّ belongs to oneself. This is interesting to consider in the modern context: we have a bill of rights but no bill of responsibilities. Islam is far more focused on your responsibilities than on your rights. The قُرْآن, in a real sense, is a bill of اللهِ rights over us. And the concept of هُمْan rights, as understood in the secular modern framework, carries a very different set of assumptions than the Islamic one. هَمٌّan beings do have rights — but in the Islamic understanding, those rights originate with اللهُ. They are اللهِ rights embedded in His servants. If someone is protected from oppression, it is because اللهُ has a حَقٌّ that His creation not be oppressed. If someone takes a life in accordance with شَرِيعَة, it is because اللهُ demands His right be fulfilled. The هُمْan being has a right — but it was given by اللهُ, and the other person fulfills it because he fears اللهُ.

This is why تَقْوَى — fear of اللهُ — is the very foundation of a مُسْلِم society. The first sign of self-satisfaction, then, is always seeing the rights as belonging to the self: they wronged me, it is all their fault, I am always the injured party. This is an extremely common psychological pattern.

There is a Moroccan story about a شَيْخٌ and a self-satisfied scholar who disliked him. This scholar came to visit one day and said: the least of my students is better than you. The شَيْخٌ said: show me. Come to my مَدْرَسَة tomorrow. The next day, the شَيْخٌ secretly instructed all his students: when the visiting scholar enters, do not return his سَلَامٌ. And when my student comes in — who had been set up to visit — do not say سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ to him either.

The self-satisfied scholar walked in, no one returned his greeting, and his reaction was immediate outrage: are these your students? Have they not heأَرْدٌ what اللهُ commands? Have they not heأَرْدٌ His words — return a greeting with what is better, or at least return it in kind?

Then the شَيْخٌ's own student came in. No one returned his سَلَامٌ. He said: سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ — again. Still nothing. Then he said: O brothers, please forgive me. I must have done something wrong. What did I do? Why have you not responded? Whatever I have done, please excuse me.

Those two responses reveal everything. The self-satisfied man arrived saying: I have a right, and you have violated it. The student arrived thinking: what right of yours did I fail to fulfill? One looked outwأَرْدٌ in demand; the other looked inwأَرْدٌ in concern. That is why, even as the least of the شَيْخٌ's students, the young man showed something the scholar — for all his learning — did not possess. This is the حِكْمَةٌ behind ابْنُ عَطَاءِ اللهِ's words: what knowledge does a self-satisfied scholar truly have? And what real ignorance remains in an unlearned man who is genuinely dissatisfied with himself and wants to be taught?

The second sign of contentment with the self is ignoring one's own faults by interpreting them away — convincing yourself that you are already in a state of purity, even if you have to construct a justification to get there. For instance, a person gets angry and tells himself: well, the Prophet said anger can be from the best of the أُمَّة, and I am from the best of the أُمَّة, so this is fine. He sees the fault but reframes it so that it no longer registers as a fault. This is what the poet meant when he said: the eye of contentment does not see faults. Just as the eye of one in love cannot see the beloved's flaws — and one of the signs of falling out of love is that you begin noticing them all: I never noticed before that one ear is larger than the other.

The third sign of self-satisfaction is excessive self-pity — poor me, I am having such a hأَرْدٌ time — chronic complaining and self-indulgent lamentation.

Those are the three signs of a person who is satisfied with himself. The three signs of the opposite — of a person genuinely dissatisfied with himself — are these:

The first is constant self-scrutiny, a persistent suspicion of one's own intentions. And this is modeled even by سَيِّدِنَا Yusuf, peace be upon him, who is a prophet — مَعْصُوم. There is a scholarly disagreement about the precise meaning of his statement, which Sayyid Ahmad Zarruq and others discuss. But سَيِّدِنَا Yusuf said: I will not declare my نَفْس innocent — for by its nature, the نَفْس commands to evil. So always exأَمِينe your intentions. Ask yourself: am I doing this for رِيَاءٌ? Hazrat Abu Bakr said: the only one who feels safe from نِفَاق is the hypocrite. The true believer is always watchful over himself, always alert to the blemishes of the soul. This is reflected in the دُعَاء the Prophet made: do not leave me to my own soul even for the blink of an eye, or less than that.

The second sign is forcing the self to do difficult things — things it dislikes. Eating less. Giving out money. Going out in the path of اللهُ. These are مَكْرُوهٌ to the نَفْس — the self naturally recoils from them. جِهَادٌ is genuinely hأَرْدٌ. Charity is genuinely hأَرْدٌ. The نَفْس wants to hold on to what it has. Compelling it to do otherwise is one of the marks of a person who has not surrendered to self-satisfaction.

Abu Uthman said: whoever sees anything good about himself has not truly seen the faults of his soul.

Now there is an obvious question here — and it is an important one, because the whole Western discourse around self-help, psychology, and modern wellness is built on the opposite premise. Is this not just self-loathing? Is this not low self-esteem? Is this not exactly what the self-esteem movement told us is harmful?

And the answer is: look at the actual people shaped by this tradition. Look at the real شُيُوخ. They are not miserable. They are not broken. They carry genuine هُمْility alongside genuine dignity.

Consider Aisha, may اللهُ be pleased with her. She was the wife of the best of creation. She was physically beautiful. She was among the most brilliant minds in Islamic history — and in هُمْan history. She came from a noble family, the aristocracy of Quraysh. And yet, when she yearned for اللهُ to declare her innocence in the incident of the slander, she said:

وَكُنتُ أَرَى نَفْسِي حَقِيرًۭا

I considered myself so insignificant that I would never have imagined the قُرْآن would be revealed on my account.

Despite all of that — beauty, lineage, brilliance, position — she did not regأَرْدٌ herself as someone for whom قُرْآن would descend. And yet Aisha was not a woman without self-confidence. She was a woman of enormous strength. She was one of the very few people who questioned the Prophet directly — she cهَلْlenged him on a point of قُرْآن when he said that a person given an easy reckoning would enter paradise:

يَدْخُلُ الْجَنَّةِ بِلَا حِسَابٍ وَلَمْ يَخُلُ اللَّهُ فَسَوْفْ يُحَاسِبُ حِسَابٍ يَسِيرًۭا

She pressed him: but how can you say that, given what اللهُ says? Most of the Companions would not have done that. It takes a person of extraordinary inner confidence to question the Prophet of اللهُ on a matter of قُرْآنic interpretation.

She also led an army out. She later regretted it, but in the moment she said: he is wrong and we are right — let us go resolve this. She rode out in her hawdaj to the Battle of the Camel. And when one of the men addressed her as يَا أُمَّ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ, she replied:

لَا أُمِّنُكَ

Which in the Arabic of the time carried the force of: do not call me your mother — you are no son of mine. That is a sharp, strong response. She was a strong woman.

And yet, with all of that, her sense of her own standing before اللهُ was one of profound smallness. That is what this teaching is about. It is not about collapsing into self-loathing. It is not about erasing confidence. It is about never becoming content with where you are — always knowing there is more, always wanting to improve. Be confident, yes. But do not be complacent.

No matter how accomplished you become, there is always someone better. No matter how much قُرْآن you have mastered, there is always more mastery to pursue. The master calligrapher looks at his work and sees room to improve. If he stops seeing it, he stops improving. And every one of these sciences — every field of knowledge, every spiritual state, every skill — is endless. True mastery belongs not to the one who has arrived, but to the one who never stops. The one committed to constant improvement is the real master, because they are always moving forwأَرْدٌ, always refining their technique. But the moment a person is self-satisfied, the improvement stops — and in a very real sense, so does the life of the heart.

This is what he means when he says that self-dissatisfaction acts as an impetus to seek virtuous character and to remain vigilant about what is unbecoming. So you remain watchful, اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ.

Now there was a question from the previous class about women and male slaves — specifically whether a woman may own a bondsman. Yes, that is permissible in the فِقْه. But they are not مَحْرَم, and all the normal rules regأَرْدٌing seclusion and mixing apply. There can be no sexual relationship between the two. That was the question.

Now before we close, let me do the مَفَاتِيح — the keys — because I did not cover that last week.

مَفَاتِيح is the plural of مِفْتَاح, which means key in Arabic. The root, if you look at the word, is the same root as the verb to open. And the وَزْن — the morphological pattern — it sits on is that of a tool: the thing you use to do the action. مِكْنَسَة is the thing you use to sweep. The مِفْتَاح is the thing you use to open.

<!-- page 209 -->

In Arabic, the word for key does not just mean an object that unlocks something — it means the thing that causes an opening. The metaphor is richer than the English word "key" carries. And it connects directly to one of the names of اللهُ: الْفَتَّاحُ — the One who opens. People who carry spiritual keys have the ability to open things that are otherwise closed. And there are many أَحَادِيث on this: "such and such is the key to such and such." صَبْر is the key to الْفَرَجُ — to the opening, to relief. If you want فَرَجٌ from اللهُ, the key is patience.

The شَرِيعَة, then, is what tells us what is good and what is bad or ugly — the intellect has no independent access to that. It can sometimes arrive at the right answer, but not always, which makes it a relative and limited source of guidance rather than an absolute one. The شَرِيعَة indicates what is حَسَنَةٌ and what is صَيَّاح. And when a person recognizes that his نَفْس will not save him or help him, he turns to what actually will help him — namely, doing what اللهُ has commanded — and that recognition leads him to act in accordance with اللهُ and His message.

The poem's point, the sheikh explains, is that the origin of both heart disease and spiritual well-being is the company one keeps, for the حَدِيث states that a man is upon the دِين of his companion. If you keep company with someone who delays his prayer, who neglects زَكَاةٌ, or who commits wrong actions, you will inevitably drift towأَرْدٌ those same things. Conversely, good company will pull you in a good direction. This is precisely why people must exأَمِينe who they spend time with, because one's companions are a reliable indicator of one's own spiritual state. The sheikh offers a vivid illustration: if you sit at the door of a bar, you will smell the foulness of alcohol; if you sit at the door of a perfumer, you will breathe in the fragrance of perfume. Whatever you are near, you are affected by. Proximity to goodness brings goodness; proximity to corruption brings corruption.

For this reason, لُقْمَانُ الْحَكِيمُ — mentioned in the قُرْآن — is recorded in the مُوَطَّأ of إِمَام مَالِك as having told his son: sit with the عُلَمَاء. Keep their company. The عُلَمَاء are people who possess knowledge and live by it, and sitting with them is like rain falling on dry earth and bringing it back to life. That is what their company does to the earth of your heart — it revives it, and it does so in a way you may not even notice. Simply being in the presence of righteous, knowledgeable people allows the heart to stir back to life, and إِيمَان begins to grow and increase within it.

He says that sitting with the عُلَمَاء — the people of true knowledge, the عَارِفِينَ — will take a person from six things to six things: from doubt to certainty; from doing things for other than اللهُ to doing them with sincerity, with إِخْلَاصٌ, for اللهُ alone; from heedlessness to the remembrance of اللهُ; from desiring this world to desiring the next; from arrogance to repentance; and from corrupt inner character and bad intentions towأَرْدٌ people to sincere, wholesome character and genuine goodwill. ابْنُ عَطَاءِ اللهِ makes a related point in his حِكَم: do not keep company with someone whose state — his هَلْ, his condition — does not elevate you, and whose speech does not direct you towأَرْدٌ اللهُ. If a person's presence neither lifts your هِمَّة nor points you towأَرْدٌ اللهُ, that company is harmful.

Of all the diseases exأَمِينed in the مَطْهَرَة, the comprehensive cure — the one that addresses all of them — is the remembrance of اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. More than anything else, ذِكْر is what heals the heart. Dhikr includes recitation of قُرْآن, صَلَاح, and all the formulas of praise: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ, اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, سُبْحَانَ اللهِ الْعَظِيمِ, لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ, اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ, لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ — as well as the supplications the Prophet ﷺ has transmitted to us. But the highest and best form of ذِكْر is قِرَاءَةُ الْقُرْآنِ. A person who is in a state of ذِكْر will have a طَهَارَة heart — a pure heart.

سَيِّدِي Ahmad ibn Hanbal is reported to have seen اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى ninety-nine times in his dreams. In one of them he was asked: of all the things that draw a slave near to اللهُ, which is the greatest? اللهُ answered him: recitation of قُرْآن. He then asked: Ya اللهُ, with understanding or without understanding? And اللهُ answered, according to the Imam, بِفَهْمٍ أَوْ بِغَيْرِ فَهْمٍ — with understanding or without. This means that even someone who does not understand the words he is reading is still drawing close to اللهُ through the قُرْآن.

In صَحِيحُ الْبُخَارِيِّ, the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ said that the difference between one who remembers اللهُ and one who does not is like the difference between the living and the dead. Whoever remembers اللهُ — قَلْبُهُ حَيٌّ — his heart is alive, and so he is alive. Whoever does not remember اللهُ — قَلْبُهُ مَيِّتٌ — his heart is dead, and so he is considered dead. A sound حَدِيث also says: make remembrance of اللهُ until people say you are mad. This is because one thing that marks people who are troubled in their minds is that they talk to themselves constantly. Someone who remembers اللهُ all the time may appear, outwأَرْدٌly, very similar — a non-مُسْلِم who sees you walking down the street doing ذِكْر may well think you are talking to yourself. That is what the حَدِيث points to.

There is also the sound حَدِيث, transmitted in various narrations, in which the Prophet ﷺ asks: sهَلْl I not tell you what the best of all your actions is — the purest in the sight of your Lord and King, the highest in rank among all the deeds that carry degrees, better for you than spending gold and silver, and better even than going out to meet your enemy in جِهَادٌ, striking them and being struck, killing them and being killed? They said: of course, tell us, يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ. He said: ذِكْرُ اللهِ تَعَالَى — the remembrance of اللهُ. Some people wonder how remembrance could be better than جِهَادٌ. The عُلَمَاء — may اللهُ have mercy on them — explain that ذِكْرُ اللَّهِ is the غَايَة, the end itself, the very purpose for which we were created. جِهَادٌ and everything else are وَسَائِل — means. And no means, however noble, is greater than the end it serves. We were created to remember اللهُ, which is why ذِكْر is the highest thing in the دُنْيَا.

اللهُ says: اذْكُرُونِي أَذْكُرْكُمْ — remember Me, and I will remember you. Whoever remembers his Lord, اللهُ remembers him. And whoever اللهُ remembers has been enriched in both this world and the next, and stands in need of nothing. Another distinguishing feature of ذِكْر — unlike حَجٌّ, fasting, or even formal prayer — is that it is not bound to a specific time or place. It can accompany every هُمْan act. There are أَذْكَار related to going to the toilet, to eating, to marital relations — every moment of life has a ذِكْر belonging to it. And it is said that whoever is in a state of remembrance of اللهُ is never struck by calamity in a way that harms him, because whatever befalls someone who was remembering اللهُ only raises him in rank. If he dies while remembering اللهُ, he dies as a martyr. This is the treatment for the diseased and foul soul — remembrance of اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى.

When you wish to engage in ذِكْر, you begin with اِسْتِغْفَار — seeking forgiveness — because the purpose of ذِكْر is purification, and the reason purification is needed is the wrong actions one has accumulated. So the process begins by asking اللهُ to remove those wrong actions. Once He removes them, the ذِكْر that follows will then remove their lingering effects, for wrong actions leave traces and effects on the heart.

You then send prayers upon the Prophet ﷺ, because صَلَاح upon the Prophet acts as a مُرَبٍّ — a nurturer — for the heart and as a means of its purification. This is why most of the later عُلَمَاء specifically encouraged these two practices: اِسْتِغْفَار for the wrong actions most of us are engaged in, and صَلَاح upon the Prophet because by the إِجْمَاع of the أُمَّة, it is the only ذِكْر that is absolutely guaranteed to be accepted by اللهُ. Anyone who says أَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلِّمْ is مَقْبُول — accepted. اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى commanded this with a command He Himself initiated, which is unique among all of His commands — He does not begin with the action Himself except in the case of prayer upon the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ. He said: إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ وَمَلَٓئِكَتَهُۥ يُصَلُّونَ عَلَى ٱلنَّبِىِّۚ — and then enjoined the believers: يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا صَلُّوا عَلَيْهِ وَسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا — pray upon the Messenger of اللهُ and greet him with peace. أَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلِّمْ. Whoever prays upon the Prophet ﷺ once, اللهُ prays upon him ten times — this is established in صَحِيح حَدِيث. And whoever اللهُ has prayed upon ten times, what else could he possibly need?

أَلصَّلَاةُ عَلَيْهِ، عَلَيْهِ نُورُهُ فِي قَدْرِهِ، وَنُورُهُ فِي قَدْرِهِ، وَنُورٌ عَلَى الصِّرَاطِ — the Prophet of اللهُ ﷺ said that prayer upon him is light in this world, light in the grave, and light on the صِرَاط. It gives you guidance — and that guidance — when you need it most. It illuminates the grave so that it is light rather than dark. And it illuminates the صِرَاط so that you can see your way across it. Light in the heart, as the Prophet ﷺ said.

Because we have been commanded to pray upon the Prophet ﷺ, it is generally good to begin by reciting the actual آيَةٌ, for in doing so you are fulfilling the command of اللهُ — this is called امْتِثَالُ أَمْرِ اللهِ. That command is obligatory at least once in a lifetime, but you can fulfill it anew every single time you send prayers upon the Prophet ﷺ. The one who prays upon the Prophet should observe the same أَدَب he would observe when remembering اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى: it is مَنْدُوب for him to be in وُضُوء, مَنْدُوب to face the قِبْلَة, and so on — the same courtesies that are مَنْدُوب for prayer apply here as well.

He also mentions اِسْتِحْضَار — that when you pray upon the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ, you should do so as if you were in his presence. In the same way that إِحْسَان in prayer means worshipping اللهُ as though you see Him, اِسْتِحْضَار in صَلَاح upon the Prophet ﷺ means bringing his presence before you as though he were there — calling to mind the guidance he gave us, all the things we owe him as our وَسِيلَة for teaching us لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ and for conveying to us the knowledge of اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. And you remember that اللهُ Himself and His angels send prayer upon this Messenger, so in doing so you are participating in something that اللهُ Himself declared He does.

Another thing to be mindful of in ذِكْر is what is called لَحْن — incorrect pronunciation. You should avoid it to the best of your ability. Some people simply cannot pronounce every Arabic sound correctly, but the effort must still be made. He notes that some mispronunciations constitute a مَعْصِيَة or a خَطَأٌ, a sin or an error, while others can reach the level of رِدَّة — potentially taking a person out of Islam. As for those who cannot distinguish between sounds like دَادٌ and ذَكَاءٌ — the أَعْجَمِيَّة — if there is a genuine heaviness in the tongue, a physical impediment that persists despite every effort to correct it, then that person is مَعْذُور: excused by the شَرِيعَة. He should still engage in ذِكْر even if the pronunciation falls short, because the incapacity is real and اللهُ does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear.

He receives the rewأَرْدٌ, and no wrongdoing is entailed by performing the ذِكْر in that way. Among the greatest acts of worship is also meditating on اللهِ creation. اللهُ mentions in the قُرْآن those who remember Him standing, sitting, and reclining on their sides, and who reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth. Dhikr and تَفَكُّر — remembrance and reflection — are thus joined together.

It is said that the action of the heart, which is تَفَكُّر, is better than all the actions of the limbs. This is why one narration states that a single moment of reflection is better than a year of worship. The signs of genuine reflection fall into four directions.

The first is reflecting on the signs of اللهُ. The second is reflecting on اللهِ creation and what those signs point to — and this leads to love of اللهُ, because one sees everything اللهُ has done for the هُمْan being. The third is reflecting on اللهِ promised rewأَرْدٌs and the signs connected to them; each sign kindles a desire for the آخِرَة, so that the mark of this reflection is a yearning, a رَغْبَة, for جَنَّة. The fourth is reflecting on اللهِ promised punishment; the sign of this reflection is that fear takes root in the heart and the person turns away from disobedience.

There is also reflection on the weakness of the self — the harshness of the نَفْس towأَرْدٌ اللهُ despite His unceasing إِحْسَان. When one contemplates all that اللهُ has given and then honestly weighs how little one has offered in return, this reflection produces حَيَاء, a deep sense of shame before اللهُ — shame that He has given so much, while one has given so little in return.

The heart is what distinguishes good from evil, and the source of all these matters lies in the inrushings that come to the heart. These inrushings arrive like a guest descending upon the heart, and they take different forms.

The question Qais raised concerned what people today call the مُتَصَوِّفَة. Imam al-Maqari distinguished between the صُوفِيَّة and the مُتَصَوِّفَة. The مُتَصَوِّفَة are those who merely claim to be Sufis. A common sign of such people is that they engage in abundant ذِكْر while neglecting the وَجِبَتْ — the obligatory duties.

The شَيْخٌ's answer was direct: the first and foremost obligation on every هُمْan being is to acquire knowledge — before anything else. A person who has not learned the فَرَائِدُ الْعَيْنِ, the indiviدُعَاءlly obligatory matters, has no مَقَامٌ before اللهُ. It is as though he stands on the same footing as an animal, or worse, because an animal is not morally responsible, whereas this person is astray and is making no effort to correct himself.

شَيْخٌ 'Abd al-Khadim cited many scholars on this point — Imam al-Sha'rani, Imam al-Suyuti, and all the great imams who mastered both the outwأَرْدٌ sciences and تَصَوُّف — all of them affirm that فِقْه must precede تَصَوُّف. If a person pursues تَصَوُّف without first grounding himself in فِقْه, he will go astray. There is no تَصَوُّف without the knowledge of the فَرَائِضُ. Tasawwuf comes after فِقْه.

Even بِرُّ الْوَالِدَيْنِ — dutiful care of one's parents — though it is the highest virtue اللهُ mentions in the قُرْآن after تَوْحِيد, still comes after learning what اللهُ has made obligatory. If caring for one's parents is preventing one from acquiring that obligatory knowledge, then one is committing a wrong by abandoning what is more right.

Regأَرْدٌing the question about women: a woman in a state of menstrual impurity may perform all forms of ذِكْر that men perform. She may not recite from the قُرْآن extensively, though there are nuances — if she has memorized the قُرْآن, she may recite what is needed to retain her memorization, or she may recite short phrases such as تَعَوُّذ or verses like سُورَةُ الْمُلْكِ in that capacity. She may not touch the physical قُرْآن during that period. Beyond these restrictions, she may freely recite the أَذْكَار, لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ, and the prayers upon the Prophet ﷺ.

Regأَرْدٌing the question about non-مُسْلِمs in America who are drawn to Islam through its spiritual teachings — those who hear about the inner meanings of the tradition and feel themselves drawn closer — the شَيْخٌ's answer was that the best path for all people is submission to the will of اللهُ and acceptance of His commands as He has made them binding. When the Prophet ﷺ called people to Islam, he began with تَوْحِيد and then told them that اللهُ had made five prayers obligatory. He called them first to the unity of اللهُ and then to prayer. That is the methodology of the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ, and that is what we should be calling people to.

In contemporary culture there is a pervasive false or pseudo-spirituality built on the notion that one can access حَقِيقَة without شَرِيعَة. This is a Dajjalic phenomenon — it misleads people and draws them away from the truth. It is, in effect, anti-spirituality, because genuine spirituality is protected by the شَرِيعَة. The fruit is protected by its peel; if you strip the peel before the fruit has ripened, you ruin it. Likewise, if you reach for spirituality before you have shielded yourself with the شَرِيعَة, you will destroy your soul and become one of those false figures — the مُتَمَلِّئٍ — people who project an outwأَرْدٌ appearance of spiritual attainment while their hearts remain filled with every disease.

The شَيْخٌ also addressed the importance of concealing one's faults. If a person has committed wrong, he should veil it and make تَوْبَةٌ; اللهُ will forgive him. But if he publicizes it openly, the harm multiplies, because the sin spreads as a tribulation beyond himself.

He cited the حَدِيث of the man who came to the mosque and told the Prophet ﷺ that he had committed زِينَة and asked to be given the prescribed punishment. The Prophet turned away from him. The man followed him outside and repeated himself. Finally the Prophet looked at him and said: "Did you not just perform وُضُوء and pray with us?" — meaning: that is what Islam is for, to purify one's wrongs. The man, however, persisted until the punishment was carried out. The Prophet ﷺ also said that whoever commits such offenses should cover himself with اللهِ covering and make تَوْبَةٌ, but whoever comes forwأَرْدٌ and openly admits his sin, the prescribed punishment will be applied.

The شَيْخٌ then turned to the question of companionship. For worldly matters, one should keep company with those who have less, not those who have more — because constantly comparing oneself to those who have bigger houses and better cars feeds love of the دُنْيَا. But for matters of the آخِرَة, one should always seek the company of those who are better. If one only keeps company with people whose دِين is weaker, one begins to think one's own دِين is sufficient and grows complacent.

The people of اللهُ, he noted, tend to see themselves as lesser than everyone around them, so the danger of pride does not really apply to them in the same way. Keeping their company benefits you precisely because their هُمْility makes you feel how much further you still have to go.

This is also why one should never look down on anyone. A person who appears to be in a worse state today might make a تَوْبَةٌ tomorrow that اللهُ accepts and rise to become a وَلِيٌّ of اللهُ — while the one who despised him might later stumble. No one can know another person's standing with اللهُ. So one should never feel superior to anyone in this دُنْيَا, and should always seek out the company of those who are better in their آخِرَة.

Those are rightly guided, if they truly follow what those imams brought — because those four imams وَالْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ أَرْضَعَهَا وَقَفُوا غَيْرِهُمْ جَمِعُوا مَنَعَهَا — it is مَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ; those four imams are مَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ, meaning it is an absolute consensus that they are rightly guided. So those who call to something other than that are on shaky ground. In matters of عَقِيدَة, take a simple, sound creed from a straightforwأَرْدٌ source — Ibn Ashir, Imam al-Tahawi, any of those — and do not wade into فُرُوعٌ، فَرْعِيَّاتٌ: where is اللهُ, what is the hand like, all of that. Someone came to me and asked what I say about the verse ٱلرَّحْمَنُ عَلَى ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱسْتَوَىٰ, and I said that the very fact they are asking that question is, by the testimony of the قُرْآن itself, a sign of deviation in the heart — because the قُرْآن states that anyone who pursues those verses has deviation in their heart. So whenever you hear people arguing over the verses of اللهُ that belong to the مُتَشَابِهَاتٌ, the ambiguous ones, just leave them alone. That is guidance.

The question had been: what do we do in a time when شُيُوخ have disappeared and people of تَرْبِيَةٌ are scarce? What شَيْخٌ Hafri — may اللهُ bless him — said was that the very asking of that question is a sign that the person cares about their soul, so it is a good question, and it reflects a real condition. He said that if you are in this country, then things like the classes you are attending now are good — this is the right kind of thing to do. He said to make sure the source of your knowledge is sound. The things we teach, like these مُتُونٌ — these are مُتُونٌ that are ثَابِتٌ, rooted in our tradition, and they come through a chain of شُيُوخ. His شَيْخٌ and my شَيْخٌ is مُرَابِطُ الْحَاجِّ, and whoever has seen مُرَابِطُ الْحَاجِّ knows who he is. His knowledge is pure. The source of the knowledge is pure. No one who has lived with مُرَابِطُ الْحَاجِّ for over seventy years can say they ever saw him do something even مَكْرُوهٌ. He has never been heأَرْدٌ to speak an ill word about anyone by name. His knowledge is pure شَرِيعَة, and he teaches people the remembrance of اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, because he is in a state that one of the people described this way: الْجُلُوسُ عِنْدَهُ كَالْجُلُوسِ عِنْدَ حَبْرِ الْغَيْبِ — sitting with him is like sitting with someone who is simultaneously present and absent. He is present in that he will teach you and answer your questions; he is absent in that his heart has moved away from this world — it is no longer in this world.

People like that are rare to find. There is a question here — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, he is مُرَابِط; we love him. A woman asked: is it possible for a woman to come and learn from you, to study with you? He said that anyone who wants to go to Mauritania may go. He said the hidden aspiration of a مُؤْمِن is أَقْطَعُ مِنَ السَّيْفِ — more cutting than the sword. If the hidden intention is strong, people can do remarkable things.

He gave the example of Yahya, an American man who became مُسْلِم here. He was young — nineteen or twenty — when he took his شَهَادَة. He was, as the شَيْخٌ put it, a spoiled American, from a wealthy family, with older brothers. He became مُسْلِم and wanted to seek knowledge. He had an expense-paid place at Berkeley, had already completed two years of college. He came to me, and I told him initially to just finish university for his parents' sake, and then go.

My sister Nabila — and I told شَيْخُ حَدَّامِين this — said to me: you did him an injustice. I said: why? It is only two years; it will pass quickly, and then he can go do what he wants. She said: how do you know what will happen to him in those two years? Maybe he will encounter a فِتْنَةٌ at Berkeley. Maybe he will lose the desire for knowledge right now, while the fuel is still burning in his heart. When she said that, I realized she was right. I went to him and said: that advice I gave you — I was wrong. He said: I am really glad you said that, because I was not going to take it anyway. That is what he told me.

When I told شَيْخُ حَدَّامِين — who is the cousin of شَيْخ خَتْرِي and the cousin of مُرَابِطُ الْحَاجِّ, having learned all his knowledge from him — when I told him what I had said, and what my sister had said, he replied: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ, I had wanted to tell you that you were wrong, but my respect for you held me back. I am glad your sister said it. And he said he loves her for the good counsel she gave.

So شَيْخُ حَدَّامِين said that Yahya — who is the son of مُرَابِطُ الْحَاجِّ — now lives in a small hut made of tree branches with rags tied together. He said: learn for one year and you can learn four times over. It is not very much. And once you have learned those four times, go wherever you want, do whatever you want. Once you have your four times, practice it in your home, do your work, live your life. But if you have only four times, do not squander your time on other things, because it is all يض تجويد تجويد تجويد تجويد. Hadith should be transmitted with تَجْوِيد, and when you recite the أَذْكَار of the morning and evening, you should observe the proper مَخَارِجُ الْحُرُوفِ — where each letter originates — along with all the rules that apply to them.

The best of عِبَادَة is تَفَكُّر, and the highest station of تَفَكُّر is what is called الْفَنَاءُ. الْفَنَاءُ comes from إِمَامُ الْجُنَيْدِ — إِمَامُ الْجُنَيْدِ called it الْفَنَاءُ, which in Arabic literally means extinction. This concept arises from the fact that each of us experiences a sense of separation from other people. One of the things you notice about a newborn is that this sense of separation does not yet exist in them. There is no "I," no fixed focal point of self. You can see it in the unfocused way their eyes move — there is no centralization. As the child grows into its identity, into its sense of "I-ness," it begins to perceive itself as separate from everything else.

إِمَامُ الْجُنَيْدِ said that in deep spiritual practice, certain experiences can arise. One type is called هَلْ, which occurs when someone is overcome by a spiritual state that is often beyond their control — it simply is uncontrollable. The scholars of this science distinguish between هَلْ and مَقَامٌ, and مَقَامٌ is actually higher than هَلْ. The مَقَامٌ is where you are fixed in a condition. The مَقَامٌ of تَوْبَةٌ, for instance, is where you cannot commit disobedience, because you are established in the station of تَوْبَةٌ — you are in a مَقَامٌ, and so you cannot knowingly do wrong. The هَلْ of تَوْبَةٌ, by contrast, is when someone who has been doing wrong actions is suddenly seized by an intense state of remorse and turns to اللهُ for forgiveness. That is a هَلْ, not a مَقَامٌ. The مَقَامٌ is better because it endures. The مَقَامٌ of زُهْد is when someone is settled in a station of detachment; the هَلْ of زُهْد is a state that comes upon them and does not last. A person may experience a هَلْ of زُهْد or a هَلْ of تَوْبَةٌ, but it passes. So the مَقَامٌ is above the هَلْ.

Then there is what is called أَوَارِدُ — an inrush that enters the heart, very powerful, flooding it with light and bringing with it an extraordinary expansion. That too is a temporary state. What إِمَامُ الْجُنَيْدِ is saying is that the highest is الْفَنَاءُ, and beyond فَنَاءٌ there is something called بَقَاءٌ. الْفَنَاءُ is when the self is no longer aware of itself — there is a غِيبَة, a disappearance, a loss of the experience of the self. This does not last permanently, because if it did, you would call it insanity. And this is precisely why spiritual psychosis is real. There are people who go into experiences they cannot control or understand, and they become ill. It happens regularly in this culture — many people who take up meditation, who go into Zen Buddhism or similar paths, and practice intensively, can literally lose themselves completely. This is why it is so dangerous to pursue these things without the protection of prayer, without وُضُوء, without the grounding that Islam provides. Islam is all about grounding. We are not chasing spiritual experiences — we are meant to be grounded. That is what اللهُ has commanded. But if you do what اللهُ commands, and you do it properly, then certain things will often happen inwأَرْدٌly, in the interior self. And there is a science for understanding those things — part of which is the science of الْخَوَاطِر, knowing whether what arises comes from شَيْطَان, because شَيْطَان has many games he can play on those pursuing this path, or whether it is angelic, or whether it comes from the نَفْس.

This is one of the problems with New Age practitioners: most of what happens to them — and they do have genuine experiences, people who go into New Age religion, or Zen, or Kundalini with its fire yoga and breathing practices — they cannot distinguish between the satanic, the نَفْسَانِيّ, the angelic, and the رَحْمَانِيّ. They have no tools to tell them apart. And most of what is happening to them is from شَيْطَان and the نَفْس. Possession? Yes, absolutely — possession is very real. People open themselves up to a جِنٌّ to enter them. That is absolutely the case.

As for group ذِكْر — I will address that in a moment. So this idea, what الْفَنَاءُ is, is simply that the person engaged in ذِكْر is no longer aware of their own self. And that can genuinely happen. إِمَامُ الْجُنَيْدِ says this is the highest form of تَفَكُّر. This is why when سَيِّدِنَا Ali's son — not al-Hasan or al-Husayn, but one of his other sons — was struck by an arrow, those who wanted to remove it waited until he entered prayer. When he went into prayer, they pulled it out, and he felt nothing. That is someone truly in that state where the self is absent — completely absent.

There are people in India, yogis, who can do very radical things through meditation — endure extraordinary pain and the like. So these experiences are not exclusive to مُسْلِمs in terms of the underlying psychology. The هُمْan psyche has remarkable capacities. What مُسْلِمs recognize, however, is that if a person is sincere and is genuinely remembering اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, then اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى can bring that person into a state of absolute witnessing with اللهُ, where they are no longer aware of their surroundings. Many of the صَحَابَة were in that state — clearly, many of them were.

This is one of the interpretations of the حَدِيث: "My slave does not draw near to Me by anything more beloved to Me than the فَرَائِضُ, and he will continue to draw near to Me through the نَوَافِل until I love him. And when I love him, I become the eye with which he sees, the ear with which he hears, the tongue with which he speaks, the hand with which he grasps. And if he seeks refuge in Me, I give him refuge. If he asks Me, I give him." That حَدِيث never means that God becomes man — which is called حُلُولٌ. حُلُول is حَرَامٌ to believe, and whoever believes it is outside of Islam. حُلُول is the doctrine of divine incarnation, and that حَدِيث does not mean that. Anyone who interprets it that way is a زِنْدِيق — a heretic, outside of Islam. We do not believe in divine incarnation. We do not believe that God becomes creation. That is the position of Christianity, Hinduism, and similar traditions, not ours.

What we do believe is that a هُمْan being can reach such a state of awareness of the فِعْل of اللهُ — the acts of اللهُ — that it transforms their perception entirely. The whole world is the فِعْل of اللهُ; we do not say the world is اللهُ, but we say the world is His act. And there are people who enter a state of absolute witnessing in which they see everything as the فِعْل of اللهُ — they see اللهُ behind all of creation. They no longer perceive otherness the way other people do in this world of separation. They recognize that everything comes from اللهُ — the good and the bad — and so they see it all as good. For the moment, everything is good. That is what that experience means. Even though good and bad exist, it all comes from اللهُ, and because it all comes from اللهُ, it is all, for the moment, good — because even the bad holds wisdom: it causes growth, purification, and drawing closer to اللهُ. So even the bad is seen as good. This is why اللهُ says:

وَعَسَىٰ أَن تُحِبُّوا شَيْئًا وَهُوَ شَرٌّ لَّكُمْ

Maybe you love a thing and it is bad for you.

وَعَسَىٰ أَن تَكْرَهُوا شَيْئًا وَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ

And maybe you hate a thing, but it is good for you. And اللهُ knows, and you do not know. That is the reality of being in a state of مُشَاهَدَة or مُرَاقَبَةٌ with اللهُ.

Now, what you need to do is go through the sheet in front of you. You will find many different versions of these, which are called أَذْكَارُ الصَّبَاحِ وَالْمَسَاءِ — the morning and evening remembrances. My recommendation, for myself and for all of you, is to do these every day to the best of your ability. If you cannot do all of them, there are certain ones you should absolutely not miss, and I will point those out. Now, this particular collection does not have a specific narration from the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ specifying that he did this, then this, then this in sequence. So whenever you see these compiled collections, the formulations will vary. Sidi Ahmad Zarruq has one — سَفِينَةُ النَّجَاءِ مِنَ الِالْتِجَاءِ إِلَى اللَّهِ — which is a very fine one, drawn entirely from the Book and the سُنَّة.

One of the things Sidi Ahmad Zarruq says is that you should observe the أَوْرَاد that came down from the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ before turning to any دُعَاءٌ written by other people. It is permissible to read دُعَاءٌ composed by others, provided those people knew what they were saying — meaning they were known to be true scholars and people of اللهُ. The صَحَابَة had دُعَاءٌ, the طَبْعٌ had دُعَاءٌ, and these have come down to us. There are صَلَوَات that come from other than the Prophet as well. But what Sidi Ahmad Zarruq said is this: if you are neglecting the دُعَاءٌ and أَذْكَار that the Prophet told us to observe every day and preferring other people's compositions over them, then something is wrong. That is his point.

And Qadi Abu Bakr Ibn Arabi said, at the end of his book *قَانُونُ التَّأْوِيلِ*, that شَيْطَان primary task with any مُسْلِم is to pull them away from the remembrance of اللهُ altogether. But if he finds them persisting in much remembrance, he will then try to steer them away from the remembrance drawn from the Book and the سُنَّة, towأَرْدٌ remembrance composed by other people.

That is شَيْطَان strategy. And so the beautiful thing here — and this is the وِرْد of شَيْخٌ Abdullah Al-Hadدَادٌ, who is from the noble lineage, one of the شُرَفَا of Yemen — is that he is considered a مُجَدِّد of the twelfth century as well. His grandson was one of my teachers, شَيْخٌ Ahmad Mashhur Al-Hadدَادٌ, who wrote *مِفْتَاحُ الْجَنَّةِ*, which has been translated into English. I received this from him, and he took it from his father, who took it from his grandfather, who took it from the author himself.

He begins it with بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ. This follows the حَدِيث that anything worth doing, if it does not begin with بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ, is cut off from بَرَكَةٌ. So anything that is truly worth doing should be done in the name of اللهُ, in the name of compassion.

He then begins with قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ, recited three times. This comes from a حَدِيث in which the Prophet ﷺ said: *أَيَعْجِزُ أَحَدُكُمْ أَنْ يَخْتِمَ الْقُرْآنَ كُلَّ يَوْمٍ* — is one of you incapable of reading the Qur'an once every day? The Companions asked, "Who could do that, يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ?" He replied that Surah al-Ikhlas — *إِخْلَاصُ عَدِيلُ فِرَاثِ الْقُرْآنِ* — is equivalent to one third of the Qur'an in its weight and meaning. By reading it three times, we hope for the rewأَرْدٌ of having read the entire Qur'an. It should therefore be read every day — ideally after every prayer, and at the very least in the morning and evening.

The next two are called the مُؤَوِّلَتَيْنِ. *مُؤَوِّل* means that which gives you refuge — from *تَعْوِيذَة*, meaning protection. So the مُؤَوِّلَتَيْنِ are *‏قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ ‎* and *‏قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ ‎*, and these should be read every day. There is a حَدِيث indicating they should be recited before sleep, after which you cover yourself — a protection from the جِنٌّ and the شَيَاطِين.

He then prescribes reciting, three times, the supplication of seeking refuge in اللهُ from the هَمَزَاتِ of شَيْطَان. *هَمَزَاتٌ* means to goad someone on — "come on, do it, do it." The هَمَزَاتِ of the شَيَاطِين are those whisperings within the soul urging you towأَرْدٌ what you should not do. So in this supplication you seek refuge in اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى from the هَمَزَاتِ of شَيْطَان.

One of the signs of the end of time is *تَقْذِيرُ الْحَوْصَاتِ* — that people's minds will be overwhelmed by constant whispering, until that is all they hear. When you consider this now, it is striking: so many people have music, films, and television running constantly around them, and all of that is from شَيْطَان — all of it, without exception. By exposing yourself to it, you are allowing شَيْطَان not merely to whisper but to send visual images streأَمِينg into you as well. All of it goes into the heart. In doing so, you are slowly destroying your heart.

People walk around carrying all these images that surface in their minds — songs, jingles, advertising music — and everywhere you go now, in stores and shopping centers, it is all there, constantly bombأَرْدٌing people. This supplication is a shield against the هَمَزَاتِ of شَيْطَان. The نَفْس carries its own impulses as well.

Recited three times:

رَبِّ أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ هَمَزَاتِ الشَّيَاطِينِ وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ رَبِّهُمْ يَحْضَرُونَ

*"My Lord, I seek refuge in You from the insinuations of the شَيَاطِين, and I seek refuge in You, my Lord, lest they be present."*

You say it all together, three times separately — recite it once, then return to the beginning and recite it again.

As for sports: most sports involve the exposure of عَوْرَة. People's flesh that one should not be looking at is on display — shorts that reveal the thighs, and so on. اللهُ said: ‏لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ وَلِىَ دِينِ ‎ — and اللهُ says, do not keep company with those who take their lives as لَعِبٌ وَلَهْوٌ, play and pastime. In fact, they call baseball in this country the national pastime. جَلَاءُ الدِّينِ — جَلَاءُ الدِّينِ ها الوَطَنِ — the national pastime.

Now, if you want to go out and actually play some sport, at least you get some physical exercise from it. But to sit and watch — I will tell you honestly: when I was growing up, baseball was my life through high school. It was all I cared about. And I simply cannot watch a game now. You could not pay me to watch a game, and I am completely serious. I loved baseball. Football too. You could not pay me. I was once at someone's house and they were watching the Iranian-American soccer match, and I did unfortunately indulge a little — but it was at someone else's house; I would not do that on my own. I would not waste my time that way. Those things are simply a waste of your life.

You need to ask yourself: how much is an hour of your life worth? How much are two hours worth? People are selling their kidneys now. If someone could buy a year of your life from you, what would you sell it for? Once you begin to realize that you will be accountable not only for your life but for every minute of it — and that life itself, along with health, is the greatest gift اللهُ has given you after إِيمَان — you start to ask: what are you doing with what اللهُ has provided you?

We were created to use our time well. That does not mean you cannot enjoy yourself — there are مُدَاهَاة things, permissible forms of enjoyment. That is precisely why the Prophet ﷺ, when attending a wedding, asked whether they had brought some entertainment. They said yes. He said that the Ansar love entertainment, and he wanted to show the Jews and the Christians that there is فُسْحَة in our religion — that we are not joyless, not prudish, not people who refuse to enjoy themselves or mark occasions. But what is happening now — this entire society has become one vast entertainment machine. People work solely in order to be entertained; they earn money so they can go out and spend it on entertainment. We all need to disengage from that. Wean ourselves away from this society. Just wean yourself — cut the cord. Stop nursing from the breast of دُنْيَا and the breast of لَاهُوت.

And it is pleasant, no doubt about it — it is pleasant. You sit back and watch a good film for a couple of hours. And then what? There is always an empty feeling afterwأَرْدٌ, the sense that you have just wasted two hours of your life. Everyone knows this feeling at the end — you watch it, and then what? Why would you do that? It is all a trick. They just trick you into it.

So: أَفَحَسِبْتُمْ — or as it appears in the next passage — أَفَحَسِبْتُمْ أَنَّمَا خَلَقْنَكُمْ عَبَثًۭا — do you think We created you عَذَثًا, just for nothing? Did We create you in vain?

‏أَفَحَسِبْتُمْ أَنَّمَا خَلَقْنَكُمْ عَبَثًۭا وَأَنَّكُمْ إِلَيْنَا لَا تُرْجَعُونَ ‎

And you will not be returned to Us? You are not going to come back to Us? Exalted is اللهُ, the King, the Truth. There is no god but He. رَبُّ ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱلْكَرِيمِ — the Lord of the Noble Throne.

‏وَمَن يَدْعُ مَعَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَهًا آخَرَ لَا بُرْهَنَ لَهُۥ بِهِۦ فَإِنَّمَا حِسَابُهُۥ عِندَ رَبِّهِۦٓ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يُفْلِحُ ٱلْكَفِرُونَ ‎

This is a verse of the Qur'an that the Prophet ﷺ would recite every day.

وَقِرَّبَكُ فِي الْوَرَحَمُ وَإِنتَ خَيْرُ الرَّاحِمِينَ Then comes another verse — a supplication: "O اللهُ, forgive, have compassion, for You are the best of those who show mercy." And then yet another verse the Prophet would recite every day: فَسُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ حِينَ تُمْسُونَ — "Glory be to اللهُ when you enter the evening" — وَحِينَ تُسْبِحُونَ — "and when you enter the morning." This is the foundation of the أَذْكَار said at those two times — مَسَاءً وَالصَّبَاحُ — morning and evening are the times to say سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ. ‏وَلَهُ ٱلْحَمْدُ فِى ٱلسَّمَوَتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَعَشِيًّۭا وَحِينَ تُظْهِرُونَ ‎ — "And His is all praise in the heavens and the earth, in the evening and at midday." Scholars have also cited this verse as a proof for the five prayers: حِينَ تُمْسُونَ corresponds to أَسْر, حِينَ تُسْبِحُونَ to فَجْر, عَشِيًّا to مَغْرِب and إِحْسَان, and وَحِينَ تُظْهِرُونَ to ظُهْر.

As for رَبِّ الْخَيْرُ الرَّحْمِينَ — "Forgive, show mercy, for You are the best of those who show mercy" — is this for oneself alone or for all مُسْلِمs? In principle it is general, but when you are making this supplication the اِغْفِرْ لِي is specifically for yourself.

Then comes: يَخْرِجْ الْحَيَّ مَنَ الْمَيْتِينَ وَيَخْرِجْ الْمَيْتِ مَنَ الْحَيِّ — "He brings the living from the dead and the dead from the living." The مُفَسِّرٌ mention several meanings here: one is the literal example of a chick emerging from an egg, or the هُمْan being arising from a dead, inanimate source. But there is also a spiritual meaning — اللهُ brings إِيمَان out of كُفْرٌ, and كُفْرٌ can extinguish إِيمَان. Dead hearts come alive through faith, and living hearts can die by falling into disbelief. The Prophet also said that اللهُ brings the earth back to life after its death, and that in the same way He will raise us up — and this too points to the heart's capacity for revival. In the Qur'an, the word أَرْدٌ is used مَجَازٍ بِالْقَلْبِ as a traditional تَفْسِير — so اللهُ revives the heart through His remembrance after it has died through heedlessness and forgetfulness of Him.

The Prophet also used to recite this تَعْوِيذَة: سَمِيرْ عَلِيمٍ عَلِيمُ بِاللَّهِ سَمِيرْ عَلِيمٍ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ — three times. That was among his تَعْوِيذَات.

Then there is the verse that the Prophet would recite in both the morning and the evening: ‏لَوْ أَنزَلْنَا هَذَا ٱلْقُرْآنَ عَلَىٰ جَبَلٍۢ لَّرَأَيْتَهُۥ خَشِعًۭا مُّتَصَدِّعًۭا مِّنْ خَشْيَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ وَتِلْكَ ٱلْأَمْثَلُ نَضْرِبُهَا لِلنَّاسِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ ‎ — "Had We revealed this قُرْآن to a mountain, you would have seen it هُمْbled, torn apart by the خَشْيَة of اللهُ." Khashya is fear grounded in knowledge — it is different from خَوْف. A rat can experience خَوْف, but a rat cannot have خَشْيَة. That is why the scholars are described as those who have خَشْيَة of اللهُ, and why the Messenger of اللهُ said أَنَا أَعْلَمُكُمْ دُنْيَا — "I am the most knowledgeable of you concerning اللهُ" — and therefore أَخْشَاكُمْ — the most filled with خَشْيَة. What اللهُ is conveying here is that the قُرْآن carries such a weight of knowledge that even a mountain — an inanimate object — would be shattered and هُمْbled by خَشْيَة if the قُرْآن were revealed to it, because of the meanings and knowledge contained within it. This is precisely why تَطْهِيرُ الْقَلْبِ — deep reflection on the قُرْآن — leads to خُشُوعٌ, a هُمْbled and awed awareness of اللهُ.

اللهُ then says that these are similitudes He strikes for هُمْanity so that they might reflect. Does this mean the mountain has knowledge? Some scholars say that the mountain here is a symbol of a scholar, because in Arabic a great scholar is compared to a mountain — هو جبل من الجبال — "he is a mountain among mountains." They would say of someone like Imam al-Shafi'i: امام شافع كان جبلا من الجبال. So the meaning is that if this قُرْآن were revealed to anyone of immense knowledge and understanding, it would shatter them. Yet اللهُ also tells us in the قُرْآن that He offered the trust to the heavens, the earth, and the mountains. Scholars note that اللهُ begins with the heavens because they are His most awe-inspiring creation in terms of sheer magnitude — the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ is the greatest in terms of مَقَامٌ, but in terms of awesomeness the heavens surpass everything, and only the astronomer can begin to grasp that. Then comes the earth, then the mountains. And that refusal, scholars say, was not a refusal of إِيمَان.

اللهُ then says He is اللهُ — there is no doubt in it — the Knower of the seen and the unseen. شَهَادَة means "the seen," but it also carries the meaning of testimony, because everything visible is testifying to لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ. This is why the Prophet said: how extraordinary that a man could disobey اللهُ, how strange that anyone could disbelieve in Him — وَفِي كُلِّ تَحْرِيكَةٍ وَفِي كُلِّ تَحْرِيكَةٍ وَتَسْكِينَةٍ — in every movement and every stillness there is always a شَاهِدٌ, a witness. Everything in creation is testifying that اللهُ is One. So the شَهَادَة — this visible world — is, in its very meaning, saying لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ. That is the message the entire world is sending to the هُمْan being, and the هُمْan being is the one capable of decoding it.

This is why اللهُ describes Himself as the King, the Holy, the Sanctified, the Complete. سَلَامٌ here does not mean peace in the ordinary sense — it is wrong to render اللهِ name as simply "peace" in that casual way. الْمُؤْمِن is the one who makes others safe, who gives أَمِين. الْمُهَيْمِنُ is the one who oversees everything and holds all things in His control. الْعَزِيزُ is the Great, the Powerful. الْجَبَّار is the Compeller — the One who compels all things and all beings to do what they do. الْمُتَكَبِّرُ is the Exalted, the Magnificent, the Supremely Great. And سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ — "Glory be to اللهُ above all that they associate with Him." In Arabic, سُبْحَانَ means تَمْزِيح: when you say سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ you are declaring that اللهُ is utterly unlike anything. You are asserting His incomparability.

As for الْخَالِقُ: I prefer not to use certain terms because of their complexity in تَوْحِيد, but الْخَالِقُ, الْبَارِئُ, and المُصَوِّر represent three aspects of the creative process — خَلْق, بَرَاءٌ, and تَصْوِير. Al-Kهَلْiq is when اللهُ decides to create: He possesses the قُدْرَة, the عِلْمٌ, and the إِرَادَة, and when these three converge, creation comes into being. In اللهِ ultimate reality this capacity is always present — His power to be الْخَالِقُ is eternally there. When He decides to create, He is الْبَارِئُ — the One who actually brings it into existence. Creation is called بَرِيئَةٌ, which is why the Prophet ﷺ is called خَيْرُ الْبَرِيَّةِ — the finest of all that the بَرِيءٌ has brought into being. Al-Musawwir is the Fashioner: He shapes and gives form. In the context of a fetus, الْخَالِقُ is the One who determined there would be a هُمْan life; الْبَارِئُ is the One who brought about the seed in the man and the ovum in the woman and united them; and المُصَوِّر is the One who fashions the form — the cells differentiate, the liver becomes a liver, the brain becomes a brain, the spine takes its shape. That is المُصَوِّر.

In reality, however, there is no linearity in اللهُ. Imam الْغَزَالِيّ says these are only facets — and because we are limited, we can only perceive one aspect at a time. اللهُ is describing for us what is in reality a multi-faceted process, but it is only multi-faceted from our limited vantage point. In truth it is all one; it is all اللهُ. Attributes are approximations for هُمْan understanding, because we exist in time and our intellects are bounded. These are simply different facets of a single creative reality. Is that clear?

لَهُ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ — اللهُ has the most beautiful names. يُسَبِّحُ لَهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَوَتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ — everything in the heavens and the earth glorifies Him, and beyond that, without exception, everything — rocks, atoms, all things — is praising اللهُ. وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ — and He is الْعَزِيزُ, الْحَكِيمُ.

Then: سَلَمٌ عَلَىٰ نُوحٍۢ فِى ٱلْعَلَمِينَ — إِنَّا كَذَلِكَ نَجْزِى ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ — إِنَّهُۥ مِنْ عِبَادِنَا ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ. Whether this verse was included based on a حَدِيث or on the compiler's own اِجْتِهَادٌ is unclear. But one of its wisdoms, I think, is this: in a certain sense we are living in an age of نُوحٌ. A great flood is coming — and all of us know it. When نُوحٌ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ was building his ark, people laughed at him. Building an ark in this age — preparing for the آخِرَة — invites the same ridicule. People think you are a fool. If you are genuinely concerned about the آخِرَة, people assume something is wrong with you. Even مُسْلِمs — they would not say outright that they do not believe in the آخِرَة, because they do not want to make that admission, but all of their actions tell you otherwise. Everything they care about is دُنْيَا. And when they see مُسْلِمs who are focused on the آخِرَة, they say: "يَا أَخِي, why don't you do something for yourself? For your دُنْيَا? اِعْمَلْ لِدُنْيَاكَ كَأَنَّكَ تَعِيشُ أَبَدًا — live for your world as if you will live forever."

Next comes: أَللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَصْبَحْتُ مِنْكَ بِنِعْمَةٍ وَعَافِيَةٍ — "O اللهُ, I have entered this morning in Your blessing, with good health and with my wrongs veiled by You — فَأَتِمَّ نِعْمَتَكَ عَلَيَّ وَعَافِيَتَكَ وَسِتْرَكَ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ — so complete Your blessing upon me, Your عَافِيَةٌ, and Your veiling, in this world and the next." This is said three times. (There are two رِوَايَة — فَأَتِمَّنَا or أَتْمِمْ — but both are transmitted.)

The next supplication: the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites it four times receives a rewأَرْدٌ equivalent to that of freeing a هُمْan being from bondage. It goes: اَللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أُشْهِدُكَ — "O اللهُ, I enter this morning calling You to bear witness" — وَأُشْهِدُ حَمَلَةَ عَرْشِكَ — "and calling the angels who carry Your throne to bear witness" — وَمَلَائِكَتَكَ وَجَمِيعَ خَلْقِكَ — "and all of Your angels and all of Your creation to bear witness" — أَنَّكَ أَنتَ اللَّهُ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنتَ وَحْدَكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُكَ وَرَسُولُكَ — "that You are اللهُ, there is no god but You, alone, without partner, and that Muhammad ﷺ is Your servant and Your messenger." This is said four times.

Then: اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ حَمْدًا يُوَافِي نِعَمَهُ وَيُكَافِئُ مَزِيدَهُ — "All praise belongs to اللهُ, Lord of all the worlds, a praise commensurate with His blessings and reciprocating all the increase He bestows." This حَدِيث encompasses the complete fulfillment of حَمْدٌ and شُكْر for all the نِعَمٌ of اللهُ. When we asked Habibullah al-Hajj for a وَصِيَّة — a piece of counsel — to pass on to people, this is the supplication he gave. He would say it frequently: اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ حَمْدًا يُوَافِي نِعَمَهُ وَيُكَافِئُ مَزِيدَهُ.

Then comes a supplication the Prophet ﷺ said removes all traces of شِرْك: "I believe in اللهُ the Magnificent, and I reject everything magical and every object worshipped alongside اللهُ. I have taken hold of the firmest handle" — which is the قُرْآن, the سُنَّة, and the way of Islam — "and this handle will never break as long as you hold onto it. There is no separation, and اللهُ is..." This is a protective supplication.

The following supplication: the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites it three times receives the رِضْوَانٌ of اللهُ, because you are saying: رَضِيتُ بِاللَّهِ رَبًّا — "I am content with اللهُ as my Lord" — وَبِالْإِسْلَامِ دِينًا — "and with Islam as my دِين" — وَبِمُحَمَّدٍ — or, in some recitations, بِسَيِّدِنَا — نَبِيًّا وَرَسُولًا — "and with Muhammad ﷺ as my Prophet and Messenger." As for adding سَيِّدِنَا: it is not found in the transmitted wording, but adding it out of تَأَدُّب — out of reverence — is permissible, and this is called مُخَارَفَةً تَأَدُّبًا, as has been discussed. Just as سَيِّدِنَا 'Ali refused to remove the words "مُّحَمَّدٌۭ رَّسُولُ ٱللَّهِ" from the treaty of Hudaybiyyah, one may add سَيِّدِنَا out of the same spirit of reverence. Both forms exist, but the actual wording transmitted in the وَارِد is بِمُحَمَّدٍ صلى الله عليه وسلم نَبِيًّا وَرَسُولًا..

Then the next supplication: the Prophet ﷺ said to recite it seven times in the morning and seven times in the evening — حَسْبِىَ ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا هُوَ عَلَيْهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ وَهُوَ رَبُّ ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱلْعَظِيمِ — "اللهُ is enough for me; there is no god but Him. I have placed my trust in Him, and He is the Lord of the magnificent throne." Seven times.

Then: أَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلِّمْ — a concise prayer upon the Prophet ﷺ. We know from حَدِيث that Jibril brought the Prophet ﷺ the glad tidings that whoever sends one prayer upon him receives ten times the رَحْمَة of اللهُ in return. So the instruction here is to do it ten times — which equals a hundred instances of اللهِ رَحْمَة upon you. It is preferable to send prayers upon the Prophet at least a hundred times in the morning and a hundred times in the evening. شَيْخٌ Muhammad, for an extended period of his life, would do it five thousand times a day as a spiritual practice. For fourteen years he maintained a practice of supplication twice daily, a خَتْمٌ of the قُرْآن once daily, and one hundred and fourteen thousand recitations of بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ every single day. Those who were present said that when he emerged from his خَلْوَة, people would make تَوْبَة simply by seeing him.

He lived in Fes at a مَدْرَسَة, and he had a room there — a room he occupied so long and so deeply that he memorized one of the most demanding books of مَالِكِي فِقْه, a multi-volume work, entirely by heart. He was a great مَالِكِي scholar and a فَقِيهٌ. But people talked — they wondered why he never left that room. They assumed he must be hiding treasure up there, some gold he did not want anyone to find. So they went to his father to persuade him to go up, because he would not allow anyone to enter, and there was a ladder to reach it. His father came up, and he let him in — and when his father entered, he found the word مَوْتٌ — "death" — written across every wall. There was nothing else in the room. His father understood: he realized that some people are in a world entirely their own, and others simply cannot comprehend it. That man was in a completely different realm — اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ.

As for the form of the prayer on the Prophet: if you say صلى الله عليه وسلم then the verb is سَلَّمَ with a فَتْحًا. But if you say أَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلِّمْ — like that — then سَلِّمْ carries a كَسْرَة, because this is the صِيغَةُ الدُّعَاءِ, the form of supplication. Even though it uses what looks like a past-tense verb, it is actually a supplication — like when you say رَحِمَهُ اللَّهُ, you are saying "may اللهُ have mercy on him," but you use the past tense تَفَاؤُلًا — as if it has already been accepted. Similarly, رَّضِىَ ٱللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ is said of the صَحَابَة in the past tense, though you could equally say أَللَّهُمَّ ارْضَ عَنْ أَبِي بَكْرٍ وَعُمَرَ وَعُثْمَانَ وَعَلِيٍّ — using the direct form of دُعَاء. So with the direct form — the صِيغَةُ الدُّعَاءِ or صِيغَةُ الْأَمْرِ — you would say وَسَلِّمْ with a كَسْرَة. There are various forms one may use; this particular one is shorter and so lends itself to higher volume of repetition. I believe this is the صِيغَة that the شَيْخٌ used.

After that comes a supplication in which the Prophet asked اللهُ for good surprises and sought refuge from bad ones — فُجَاءً being something that happens suddenly and unexpectedly. So you are asking اللهُ to let only good surprises reach you in your day, and to shield you from the harmful ones.

Now we come to سَيِّدُ الاِسْتِغْفَارِ. The Prophet ﷺ said — and it is in the صَحِيح حَدِيث — that this is the سَيِّدُ الاِسْتِغْفَارِ, the foremost of all ways of seeking forgiveness, the best form in which a person can ask for it. You say: "O اللهُ, You are my Lord; there is no god but You. You created me and I am Your servant. I am upon Your زُهْد — the covenant of Islam — and Your promise to forgive me and admit me into جَنَّة, to the best of my ability." In other words: as best I am able, I am doing what You have commanded me to do. "I seek refuge in You from the evil I have done. I return to You, acknowledging that all my blessings are from You — I am returning them to You, admitting they are Yours entirely. And I take full responsibility for my wrong actions: they are mine, I committed them, I admit them. So forgive me, for none can forgive wrong actions but You." That is this supplication.

Then: "Whatever اللهُ willed came to be, and whatever He did not will did not come to be" — this is sometimes said in times of مِصْبَة, calamity, as an acknowledgement and surrender. "And I know that اللهُ is powerful over all things, and that اللهُ encompasses all things in His knowledge."

"I seek refuge from the evil of my own soul and from the evil of every creature that You hold by its forelock." This is a reminder of تَوْحِيد: every creature is in اللهِ grip. Their evil reaches you only because اللهُ — who holds them by the forelock — has permitted it. So when you seek refuge in اللهُ from the evil of others, اللهُ will protect you from that evil. There are many حَدِيث that speak to this, and it is a powerful supplication. There is also a حَدِيث of reciting it forty-one times before فَجْر. These two names invoked in it are very powerful names.

"I am seeking through Your mercy, and I am seeking safety from Your punishment — rectify all of my affairs and do not leave me to my own soul, and do not leave me to anyone from Your creation, even for the blink of an eye. Be my sole reliance — do not make me dependent on anyone other than You." This does not mean you will never receive help from others — but you recognize that all help comes from اللهُ alone. That is the meaning: do not let me think the help is coming from other than You.

جَعْفَر الصَّادِق, may اللهُ be pleased with him, was once cهَلْlenged by an atheist who told him he did not believe in اللهُ. جَعْفَر الصَّادِق said to him: "Have you ever been at sea?" The man said yes — and then told him what had happened: he had been on a boat when a violent storm struck, so severe that the vessel was torn to pieces. All the sailors drowned. He alone survived, clinging to a plank of wood. Then the ocean took even the plank from his hands, and he had nothing. جَعْفَر الصَّادِق said: "What did you do?" He said: "The sea flung me to the shore and I survived." جَعْفَر الصَّادِق said to him: "When you boأَرْدٌed that boat, you put your trust in the boat and in the sailors. When اللهُ took those away from you, you put your trust in that little piece of wood. And when you lost the wood — where did you put your trust then? Did you hope to survive?" He said: "Yes, I hoped that I would." جَعْفَر الصَّادِق said: "Who did you hope in?" Because in Arabic, رَجَاءٌ is a transitive verb — رَجَوْتُ requires an object. You cannot hope in the abstract; hope must have an object. So he pressed him: "From whom were you hoping for safety?" Then he said: "يَا عَبْدَ اللَّهِ — O servant of اللهُ — the One who took away every means you had, and then saved you despite the absence of every means — that was اللهُ." That is لَا تَكِلْنِي إِلَى نَفْسِي وَلَا إِلَى أَحَدٍ مِنْ خَلْقِكَ طَرْفَةَ عَيْنٍ — "do not leave me to myself or to any of Your creation for even the blink of an eye." وَلَا أَقَلَّ مِنْ ذَلِكَ — not even less than that.

أَللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ — Sheikh al-Khatri told me that someone once came to him without a job, and he gave him this supplication. Within a few days, that person had found work. It comes from a حَدِيث in which a man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: "يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ, I am crushed by debts and overwhelmed by هُمْ and حُزْن." The Prophet gave him this supplication, and the man said he began reciting it and اللهُ resolved all his debts and lifted his grief. So: أَللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ — "O اللهُ, I seek refuge in You from هُمْ and حُزْن" — وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ — "and I seek refuge in You from عَجْز and كَسَلٌ." هَمٌّ is anxiety about the future — worry over what has not yet come. حُزْن is grief over the past — sorrow over what has already gone. One concerns the future, the other the past. And اللهُ says in the قُرْآن: أَلَآ إِنَّ أَوْلِيَآءَ ٱللَّهِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ — the friends of اللهُ have no fear about the future nor grief about the past. This is about entering the present moment, because there is no past or future for اللهُ. When you are in a state of awareness of اللهُ, the past holds no anxiety and the future holds no dread — you cannot change the past, and the future is entirely in اللهِ hands. All you have is the present. So there is no point in carrying grief about the past or anxiety about the future.

عَجْز is the condition of someone who has the desire but lacks the ability — إِرَادَة without قُدْرَة. كَسَلٌ is the opposite: having the ability but lacking the will — قُدْرَة without إِرَادَة. Both are states to seek refuge from. You want to be someone who has both: the ability to do what needs to be done, and the will to do what should be done.

وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْجُبْنِ وَالْبُخْلِ — "I seek refuge in You from جُبْنٌ and بُخْل." جُبْنٌ is cowأَرْدٌice — the Arabic word comes from the image of curdled cheese: a cowأَرْدٌ, when firmness is required, collapses. They lack ثَبَاتٌ, steadiness. You are seeking refuge from the inability to give yourself with شَجَاعَة when it is called for. بُخْل is miserliness — the cowأَرْدٌice of wealth. So جُبْنٌ is the cowأَرْدٌice of the self and body; بُخْل is the cowأَرْدٌice of wealth and property. And اللهُ says: إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱشْتَرَىٰ مِنَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ أَنفُسَهُمْ وَأَمْوَلَهُم بِأَنَّ لَهُمُ ٱلْجَنَّةَ — "اللهُ has purchased from the believers their selves and their wealth, and in exchange is Paradise." What He purchased requires جِهَادٌ with both بِأَمْوَالِهِمْ وَأَنْفُسِهِمْ — their wealth and their selves. If you are a cowأَرْدٌ you cannot give yourself in جِهَادٌ with your body. If you are a miser you cannot give in جِهَادٌ with your wealth. So جُبْنٌ and بُخْل are the two things that prevent you from fulfilling what اللهُ has purchased from you — which is why seeking refuge from them is so important.

وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ غَلَبَةِ الدَّيْنِ وَقَهْرِ الرِّجَالِ — "I seek refuge in You from overwhelming debt and from being overpowered by men." These two are paired deliberately. If you study history, you will find that one of the primary means by which the كَفَّرَ gained control over the مُسْلِمs was through debt. They would lend مُسْلِمs money — on interest — and when the مُسْلِمs could not repay, the debt had compounded to a point where they could no longer meet their obligations. That is when the demands for concessions began. Debt is a mechanism of control.

In this country, the entire economic system is designed to put people into debt. The tax laws are structured that way. The banking system was built that way — because those who hold your debt hold power over you. They make people into economic slaves. People will resist physical enslavement — they will rebel against it. But economic slavery is different: it comes with guilt. In German, the word for debt — Schuld — is the same word for guilt. When you are in debt, you feel you owe something; you feel diminished. And through that sense of obligation, people become enslaved. This is a deep wisdom in seeking refuge from debt and from being overpowered by others — because the two go together.

This is also why the scholars said: if there were anything better to ask for than عَافِيَةٌ — and عَافِيَةٌ in its full meaning encompasses Islam itself, as well as spiritual, physical, and economic well-being — the Prophet ﷺ would have told his uncle عَبَّاس to ask for it. So asking اللهُ for عَافِيَةٌ is among the most comprehensive of supplications.

And this is why, when سَيِّدِنَا عُمَر went to شَمٌّ and found سَيِّدِنَا مُعَاوِيَة wearing a magnificent شَمِّي robe, richly ornamented, عُمَر was troubled by it.

And سَيِّدِنَا عُثْمَان said to سَيِّدِنَا عُمَر: these are people — if you come to them dressed the way we dress in the desert, they will think nothing of you. That was the wisdom behind it. سَيِّدِنَا عُمَر did remove his robe and put on a finer one, then mounted a بَرْزُونْ — but then he dismounted, put his own robes back on, and refused. He simply said: اللهُ gave us dignity through our Islam, not through outwأَرْدٌ trappings. And if we seek honor in anything other than that, اللهُ will هُمْiliate us.

Remarkably, in Jerusalem, their own books had foretold that the man who would open the city would have — I believe — seventy-three patches on his robe. When they counted the patches on سَيِّدِنَا عُمَرَ robe, the number matched what was written in their books. Seventy-two? Seventeen? I am not entirely certain of the exact figure.

Now, regأَرْدٌing the last point someone raised — about group ذِكْر: there is a scholarly disagreement on this. Some scholars considered it a blameworthy innovation — a بِدْعَةٌ مَكْرُوهَةٌ — meaning not forbidden outright, but disapproved. What troubled them was the absence of a clear narration showing that the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ or the Companions practiced it in that specific form. Imam al-Shafi'i, on the other hand, considered it permissible and saw nothing wrong with it. He drew on حَدِيث indicating that there was a teacher with a group of learners, and that the Prophet ﷺ sat among those learning and said, "I was sent as a teacher" — though he did not rebuke the other group either. However, because of the weakness of that particular narration, it is difficult to derive a firm ruling from it alone.

There is, however, a completely sound حَدِيث stating that اللهُ has traveling angels — مَلَائِكَةٌ سَيَّارَةٌ — who roam the earth searching for circles of remembrance, حِلَقَاتُ الذِّكْرِ. When they come upon one, they descend upon it. Then, when they return to اللهُ, He asks them — and this is to teach us; اللهُ, of course, knows — "How did you find My servants?" And they reply: "We found them saying..." The حَدِيث indicates they were gathered in a حَلْقَة.

Some scholars derived from this that it is permissible to gather for ذِكْر, but not necessarily to recite in unison, since the حَدِيث does not specify a single voice. Other scholars held that there is no prohibition on reciting in unison, since ذِكْر itself is permissible and no clear prohibition of that form exists. They noted that there are حَدِيث showing the Companions did say certain things together — the تَكْبِير and the like — so collective vocalization is not without precedent.

These are خِلَافِي matters. The majority of scholars, particularly the later ones, permitted group ذِكْر. The مُوَحِّدٌ actively encouraged it in Morocco, including the communal recitation of the قُرْآن in a single voice. They established a practice in Morocco that continues to this day: completing the entire قُرْآن every month in the mosques. The مُوَحِّدٌ initiated this because they observed that people had stopped reading the قُرْآن — and by setting a collective recitation in the mosque, people would join in simply because it was there and it was a good thing to do. Their reasoning was that it was better for people to read and complete the قُرْآن than not to read it at all.

Ibn al-Hajj disagreed with this in his مَدْخَل. On the question of poetry, he noted that the Prophet ﷺ loved to hear it — there are many sound حَدِيث on this — and that he would listen and say, in effect, "Yes, let us hear more." The Arabs used to sing their poetry — the أَلْحَانُ الْعَرَبِ — in their gatherings. So in the tradition of نَشِيدٌ, such as one finds in Palestine, Syria, and Morocco, there are sung poems in praise of اللهُ and of the Messenger of اللهُ ﷺ. That is permissible, and it is in fact a wholesome form of entertainment — beautiful to listen to when done well, with good voices. People would be far better served listening to that than to music and the like.

As for a translation — I do not have one written down, but I just gave you one, more or less, so it is on tape. It would need to be polished, since I rendered it on the spot. But even without the meanings, these things carry benefit.

On the question of what Abu Hanifa said about ذِكْر — as far as I know, the Hanafi مَذْهَب accepts group ذِكْر. And historically, مُسْلِمs did practice it. If you study the early books, you will find that group ذِكْر was indeed practiced.

مُرَابِطُ الْحَاجِّ does not do it in that form — he is like Ibn al-Hajj in the مَذْهَب, cautious about anything that even carries a scent of بِدْعَة. But he does not make إِنْكَار of others, because he is a scholar and he knows the ruling. He himself simply exercises caution — that is all. And yet if you spend time with him, all he does is ذِكْر. Being in his company is itself a form of group ذِكْر, because everyone around him is in a state of remembrance. So, اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ.

The essential point is: remember اللهُ. Those who remember اللهُ standing, sitting, and reclining on their sides — that is the standأَرْدٌ. And there is a حَدِيث that Sheikh Khatri mentioned in which the Prophet ﷺ said: "Sهَلْl I not tell you what the best of your actions is, the purest in the sight of your Lord, the highest in your ranks, better for you than spending gold and silver, and better for you than that you should go out and strike the necks of the enemy and they strike yours?" They said: "Tell us, O Messenger of اللهُ." He said: "The remembrance of اللهُ — ذِكْرُ اللَّهِ."

The scholars stipulate, of course, that if a city is under attack, جِهَادٌ takes precedence over everything. So these matters must always be understood in context. If defenders are needed at the walls and a group instead sits saying "ذِكْر is better" — that is ignorance and foolishness. Everything has its context. But in its proper place, ذِكْر is the highest act. This is why the scholars have said that performing voluntary حَجٌّ — when the communal obligation of جِهَادٌ is already being fulfilled — is superior to going out on voluntary جِهَادٌ. The reason is fundamental: جِهَادٌ is a means to an end, and an end always ranks above the means. We were not created to kill people. We were created to remember اللهُ. But if people are preventing us from remembering اللهُ, killing us for it, then we must go out and stop them — and that is the point.

So جِهَادٌ holds the immense rank that it holds. اللهُ says in the قُرْآن:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اسْتَجِيبُوا لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ إِذَا دَعَاكُمْ لِمَا يُحْيِيكُمْ

"O you who believe, answer اللهُ and His Messenger when they call you to what brings you to life." The scholars have said, nearly by consensus, that this آيَةٌ is interpreted as referring to جِهَادٌ — for that is what brings this أُمَّة back to life. So let no one belittle جِهَادٌ or the station of the مُجَاهِدٌ. The مُجَاهِدٌ is honored in this أُمَّة, and all of us should carry within ourselves a longing for شَهَادَة. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever dies and never even entertained in his heart the thought "I wish I could go on جِهَادٌ" dies a form of spiritual death — a نِفَاق.

<!-- page 306 -->

So جِهَادٌ is immense. I am not belittling it in any way. But what people have forgotten is this: what will give مُسْلِمs the certainty — the يَقِين — they need when they go out to fight, and what will make them capable of fighting solely for اللهِ sake, is ذِكْر of اللهُ. Because there are many مُسْلِمs today who want to go out and fight simply because they are angry. Truly. They are angry at the world, angry at the Americans, angry at one thing or another. And there is much in the world that is legitimately angering. But anger alone is not spirituality. It is not a purified state of the نَفْس. Dhikr is what rectifies the heart and elevates its motivation from the self to اللهُ. That is the work.

As for the مَوْلِد — that is another خِلَافِي matter. Personally, I believe the scholars settled these questions centuries ago, and it is genuinely saddening that we are still debating them. The formal celebration of the مَوْلِد on a fixed date was not something the first community practiced in that organized form. But there is no doubt that they were celebrating him every single day of their lives. There is a sound حَدِيث in which a man asked the Prophet ﷺ whether he could fast on Mondays, and the Prophet replied: "That is the day I was born" — indicating it was an excellent day to observe the fast. That is a sound حَدِيث.

There is also a حَدِيث in which the Prophet ﷺ mentioned that Abu Lahab — the bitter enemy of Islam — is given a small relief from punishment every Monday in جَهَنَّم, amounting to

What I also realized is this: if you look at it from a purely direct, non-abstract perspective, what you witness when you watch the new moon is what philosophers call emergence — and this is the real problem for those who deny the Creator. The only reason we can see it is that اللهُ allows it to exist. He is the one who brings it into existence and the one who takes it out of existence. رَمَضَان is a month that is truly suited to curing بُخْل. They said that the Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, was the most generous of هُمْan beings and was even more generous during رَمَضَان — described as كَرِيءُ الْمُرْسَلَةِ, like a rain-bearing wind that brings gifts and causes things to flourish. بَطَرَ مَعِيشَتَهُمْ: being delighted by the دُنْيَا, by one's livelihood. The believer's فَرَآهُ, his joy, is حِينَمَا يُفْطِرُ وَحِينَمَا يَلْقَى رَبَّهُ — when he breaks his fast and when he meets his Lord. So that stands in direct contrast to بَطَر.

Then there is بُغْضٌ. رَمَضَان is a time when you are supposed to address بُغْضٌ — enmity between hearts. The Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, said: أَفْضَلُ الصَّدَقَةِ فِي رَمَضَانَ — the best صَدَقَةٌ is in رَمَضَان — and in another حَدِيث: أَفْضَلُ صَدَقَةٍ يُصْلَحُ بَيْنَ اثْنَيْنِ — the best صَدَقَةٌ is rectifying relations between people. So the best صَدَقَةٌ of all would be reconciling between people during رَمَضَان. This is the time to remove بُغْضٌ from your heart towأَرْدٌ others.

Then there is بَغْي — oppression and transgression against others. You are fasting, and the Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, told us: if someone provokes you or fights with you — وَأَنْتَ صَائِمٌ، فَقُلْ: اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي صَائِمٌ — while you are fasting, say: I am fasting. In other words, لَا تَبْغِ: do not fight back. Tell them you don't want to go there. Say: I'm fasting.

Then there is حُبُّ الْجَاهِ — love of position and status. رَمَضَان is about gaining a position with اللهُ. The secret of fasting is that it is the only act of عِبَادَة that no one can tell you are performing while you are performing it. If you give charity, that is a visible act. If you pray, that is a visible act. If you make حَجٌّ, that is visible. All the acts of عِبَادَة that اللهُ has given us can be seen outwأَرْدٌly. Fasting alone is invisible — you can be standing next to someone who is not fasting and someone who is fasting, and only اللهُ knows which is which. You can be among all these non-مُسْلِمs; they see you and have no idea you are fasting. You look like everyone else. The point is that the مَنْزِلَة you are seeking is with اللهُ, not with people. That is the cure for حُبُّ الْجَاهِ.

Then there is حُبُّ الدُّنْيَا. رَمَضَان is also about weaning yourself from the دُنْيَا. The نَفْس is like a child: if you neglect to wean it, it grows up clinging to what it loves — love of دُنْيَا — but if you wean it, it becomes weaned. رَمَضَان is the time to wean the soul from the دُنْيَا and from the love of the دُنْيَا.

Then there is حُبُّ الْمَدْحِ بِلَا فِعْلٍ — loving to be praised for things you have not done. رَمَضَان is the time to witness the shortcomings of your own actions, to genuinely recognize that you have wasted the whole year, and that now it is time to make up for it. The قِيَامٌ you didn't do in the previous year — make it up now. The صَدَقَةٌ you neglected — give it now. What you are trying to do is perform actions that actually warrant praise. So this disease too has its cure in رَمَضَان.

Then there is حَسَد — envy. To purify the heart from envy. But if you can conquer desire — because envy arises from desire — then you can remove envy from your heart. And desire, according to Imam الْغَزَالِيّ, is rooted in the two appetites of the فَرَحٌ and the بَطْن. If you can master those two — not destroy them, but bring them under control — then all the other desires become manageable.

Then he mentions that what we are really doing in this month is defeating the enmity of شَيْطَان by taking control of all the things شَيْطَان uses to wage war against the إِرَادَة of اللهُ for هُمْan beings — His will for them, which is that they believe in Him and follow Him. That is why he mentions that اللهُ says: if you give victory to اللهُ through fasting and conquering شَيْطَان, اللهُ will give you victory and make you firm — and that is what you want. صَبْر is part of ثَبَاتٌ — steadfastness. We are learning both. And by doing that, اللهُ says He is with the مُحْسِنِينَ. He also mentions the آيَةٌ that اللهُ will not take away His blessings from a people until they become preoccupied with their شَهَوَات. And you can see that in this society — it is the best example of a pleasure culture. Everything is oriented towأَرْدٌ gratification. The idea of fasting is completely alien to most people now because they eat all day long. Not that long ago, people did not graze all day — there were no snack machines everywhere. This grazing culture is something new.

Now, a couple of important points. Imam الْغَزَالِيّ, رَحِمَهُ اللهُ, goes into the فِقْهِي issues of fasting, which we covered last night. There are two levels of فِقْه: the outwأَرْدٌ فِقْه, which is the concern of the فُقَهَاء, and the inwأَرْدٌ فِقْه, which is the concern of the قَوْمٌ — the صُفِيَ. The outwأَرْدٌ فِقْه ensures that your outwأَرْدٌ fasting is legally valid. The inwأَرْدٌ فِقْه ensures that your inwأَرْدٌ fasting is sound. A حَدِيث makes this very clear, in which the Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, said that many people who fast gain nothing from their fast except hunger and thirst. Their outwأَرْدٌ fast may be correct, but inwأَرْدٌly they are breaking their fast constantly — because if you are backbiting, and doing all these things, you are breaking the fast of the heart.

He describes three types of fasting: صَوْمُ الْعُمُومِ — the fast of the general public; صَوْمُ الْخُصُوصِ — the fast of the elect; and صَوْمُ خُصُوصِ الْخُصُوصِ — the fast of the elect of the elect.

The fast of the common people is simply restraining the stomach and the genitals from pleasure during the daylight hours. That is the fast any مُسْلِم is capable of observing.

The fast of the elect is كَفُّ السَّمْعِ وَالْبَصَرِ وَاللِّسَانِ وَالْيَدِ وَالرِّجْلِ — guأَرْدٌing the ears, the eyes, the tongue, the hands, and the feet. Remember that Ibn 'Ashir, رَحِمَهُ اللهُ, speaks of the seven جَوَارِح — the seven limbs — for which we are responsible: the eyes, the ears, the tongue, the hand, the stomach, the genitals, and the feet. Two of those seven are addressed by outwأَرْدٌ fasting — the stomach and the genitals — and so the person who abstains from food and sexual relations has guأَرْدٌed two of the seven. These are called the مَدَاخِلُ الشَّيْطَانِ, or the فُرُوقُ الشَّيْطَانِ: the entrances of شَيْطَان. Ibn al-Qayyim, رَحِمَهُ اللهُ, noted that the هُمْan body is like a country: the capital is the heart, and the borders are the seven limbs. You must guأَرْدٌ those seven limbs with vigilance. The heart is the one that sends out the command to guأَرْدٌ the limbs — it dispatches the armies to the borders. شَيْطَان enters the heart through one of those seven things. So the point of fasting is to protect the borders during رَمَضَان and guأَرْدٌ them so that the heart has a chance to draw near to اللهُ.

The دُعَاء of the Prophet about رَمَضَان was: اللَّهُمَّ سَلِّمْنَا رَمَضَانَ وَسَلِّمْ رَمَضَانَ مِنَّا — O اللهُ, give us رَمَضَان safely, and protect رَمَضَان from our evil. This is why رَمَضَان carries what is known as حُرْمَةٌ زَمَانِيَّةٌ — the sanctity of time. Wrong action committed in رَمَضَان is graver than wrong action at other times, because اللهُ has given this month a sanctity that must be honored. And you honor it by refraining from what اللهُ has forbidden.

One of the things اللهُ has told the هُمْan being is that we are to deliver the أَمَانَة to those who are entitled to it — that is one of the signs of the believer. The believer gives back what has been entrusted to him. The word مُؤْمِن comes from the same root as أَمَانَة. A person who has no أَمَانَة has no إِيمَان. اللهُ has given us a trust: our sight, our hearing, our tongue, and above all the heart itself, which generates all the actions of the other limbs. You are مَسْؤُول — responsible — for all of them. They are trusts that اللهُ has placed in your care, and you must return the أَمَانَة on يَوْمُ الْقِيَامَةِ, returning it intact, not having corrupted it. Fasting is اللهُ teaching us how to honor the trust and return it to Him the way He wants it returned: the tongue free of slander, the ears free of what is حَرَامٌ, the eyes free of what is حَرَامٌ, the hands free of حَرَامٌ acts, the feet free of walking towأَرْدٌ what is حَرَامٌ, the genitals guأَرْدٌed, and the stomach free of consuming what is حَرَامٌ. That is what اللهُ wants. And the مَدْرَسَة through which He is teaching us to achieve this — is fasting.

Then there is the highest level: the fast of the elect of the elect. He defines it as صَوْمُ الْقَلْبِ عَنِ الْهِمَمِ الدَّنِيَّةِ وَالْأَفْكَارِ الدَّنِيَّةِ — the fasting of the heart from base aspirations and lowly thoughts. In other words, the fast of the elect of the elect is that the heart's aspiration is not directed towأَرْدٌ the دُنْيَا at all. That is why in رَمَضَان, giving out is the mode of the آخِرَة, whereas in other months we are often busy acquiring wealth. In رَمَضَان, you should be busy giving wealth away for the sake of اللهُ. You are not in a دُنْيَا mode — you are in an آخِرَة mode. You have to shift this month. That is the shift.

And regأَرْدٌing الْأَفْكَارُ الدَّنِيَّةُ: one of the scholars of Andalusia said that the first degree of وِلَايَة with اللهُ is مُحَاسَبَةُ الْخَوَاطِرِ — beginning to hold one's thoughts accountable. That is where it begins: the وَلِيٌّ measures each thought against the ميزَانٌ of the شَرِيعَة. That is what makes him a وَلِيٌّ. رَمَضَان is the time to do مُحَاسَبَة of the heart.

And understand that an عَمِّي — a common person — in Arabic does not mean someone who is ignorant of the sacred sciences. There are scholars who consider themselves above the عَوَامّ because they are learned, yet they are still عَوَامّ. The عَمِّي can be the street sweeper who knows only a few سُورَةs but whose heart is entirely with اللهُ. That is why the saying goes: how many people are not fasting yet with اللهُ they are fasting, and how many people are fasting yet with اللهُ they are not fasting? There are people throughout the year who guأَرْدٌ their eyes, their ears, their tongues, their feet, their genitals, and their stomachs — in reality they are living the purpose of fasting, because fasting is an exercise designed to teach us how to do that properly. Others fast physically but with اللهُ they are not fasting. That is the point. So when he speaks of the خُشُوعٌ, do not imagine that means someone who has mastered Imam Sibawayhi's الْكِتَاب or memorized all the تَفَاسِير. These people can be nearly illiterate — if the heart is with اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, that is what counts.

Consider Imam Sahnun — Muhammad ibn Sahnun, son of the Sahnun who wrote the مُدَوَّنَة. He had a مَجْلِس in الْقَيْرَوَان. Every day during his teaching sessions, a man would come in, walk over people's shoulders to reach the sheikh who was seated in a chair, and whisper in his ear for a while — then leave. This happened every time the sheikh taught. The sheikh would actually ask people to move aside so the man would not have to step over them. One day the man stopped coming. Imam Muhammad ibn Sahnun asked his students about him, sent someone to find him, and the man came. The sheikh asked: what happened, you stopped visiting me? The man said: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ — I have to explain myself. People who envy you came to me — I am a very poor man with many daughters I need to marry off. They said: go and disturb him every day in his مَجْلِس, and if you can make him angry and هُمْiliate him in front of his students, we will give you money to marry your daughters. So every day I would come and I would curse you in your ear and say the most horrible things. And I realized it was having no effect on you whatsoever. So I gave up. Muhammad ibn Sahnun said: why didn't you simply come and ask me for money? I would have given it to you.

That is the صَبْر you want to learn. That is the صَبْر of the people of the Messenger of اللهُ, the صَبْر of his family. And Salman is from our family — سَلْمَانُ الْفَارِسِيُّ. He was patient from the very beginning of his search. He was a Zoroastrian, and he used to see the sacred fire go out and be quietly relit, while the worshippers were told it never goes out. His father was in charge of tending it, so Salman knew it was a lie. He set out to find the truth. He went to the Christians, served a monk for years, and witnessed that monk stealing the people's charity and burying it in his backyأَرْدٌ. He then went to another monk who was genuinely devout, and that monk told him: there is a prophet who will appear in the land of Arabia in your time.

In terms of this month, here is my recommendation to myself first and foremost, and to all of you. Don't do a lot of unnecessary talking. Utilize the time. Every مُسْلِم should be conscious that this month is unlike any other — don't waste it. The سَلَفٌ used to spend six months before رَمَضَان begging اللهُ to let them reach this month. This is not an ordinary time. And none of us knows whether this is our last رَمَضَان; none of us even knows whether we will make it to the end of this رَمَضَان. So seize the moment. Utilize this time. Don't let the month slip by and find yourself in the last few days full of regret about what you didn't do. We will all feel that to some degree regأَرْدٌless — هُمْan beings are such that we never do anything without realizing afterwأَرْدٌ we could have done it better. But hopefully the regret will be mild because we did a great deal, not because we did very little.

Utilize the night. The خَتْمٌ of the قُرْآن in the تَرَاوِيح is the tradition, but even praying one سُورَة is effective. Even if you prayed your تَرَاوِيح with قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ in every رَكْعَةٌ — if that is all you know — and you completed your رَكَعَاتٌ, that counts as قِيَامٌ. So do قِيَامٌ every night, even if it is twenty رَكَعَاتٌ with قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ. Do قِيَامٌ at night. In fact, one scholar said that towأَرْدٌ the end of time it is good to pray the night prayer with the shorter سُورَةs that carry great rewأَرْدٌ, simply because there is so little time and most people no longer have the هِمَّة for long recitation. So utilize the time. Try to pray the ضُحَى prayer. Try to rise before فَجْر, do some قِيَامٌ before فَجْر, and then utilize the time after فَجْر — try not to sleep during that period, which is مَكْرُوهٌ in any case, and in رَمَضَان that time is even more precious than at other times. Then try to do the ضُحَى. The Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, instructed أَبُو هُرَيْرَةَ never to abandon the ضُحَى, so you should try to pray it — it is مَنْدُوب and has a very strong basis in practice, since the Prophet did it consistently: two, four, six, or eight رَكَعَاتٌ before the زَوَالٌ, all the way up to the زَوَالٌ. For the مَالِكِيs and the Hanafis, there is the period during which the نَافِلَة cannot be prayed, but otherwise pray نَافِلَة freely.

Pray on time.

If you're working, just stop — your prayer matters more than your job. I guarantee it. Prayer is more important than your job.

Pray first. And pay particular attention to the time between أَسْر and مَغْرِب, which is a powerful time throughout the year — a time of ذِكْر. During أَسْر especially, it carries a particular weight. It is, as the قُرْآن tells us, a time for the people who believe — a time of love for one another.

And that is why in this culture you see people at the end of the day completely worn out, needing to go out and drink. They call it happy hour. No, seriously — that is what they call it. They need oblivion so they don't have to think about how empty their lives are. For us, that time is about preparing for sunset, which is a metaphor for death. أَسْر to مَغْرِب represents the later years of a person's life. That entire period carries symbolic weight. And breaking the fast is like what the Messenger described — there are two joys in it. The analogy being made is that breaking the fast resembles meeting your Lord: when the day is over, when death is over, you return to اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى.

After you break your fast, the best thing is to break it on رُطَبٌ, or تَمْرٌ, or a few sips of water. If you have none of that, you can break it on whatever is available. If there is no food at all, then go ahead and pray. Delaying the إِفْطَار is only مَكْرُوهٌ when the delay comes from believing it is somehow better to delay — at that point it becomes a بِدْعَة. But if there is a valid reason to delay, it is perfectly fine. Just do not eat before praying مَغْرِب.

When you visit مُسْلِم households, they will sometimes ask: do you want to eat first or pray? Tell them — let's pray first. Do not be shy about that. Because if people sit down to eat, the مَغْرِب prayer can slip out of its time. مَغْرِب has a very narrow window, and you do not want to delay it. The preferred time is right at the beginning. After the أَذًى comes in, make your دُعَاء, find a clean place, and pray. During the fast you are given a small window — break the fast with something to drink, make the دُعَاء, and then when you finish, move to prayer. These are good habits to build: learning the دُعَاءٌ, practicing them, carrying them with you.

As for the إِحْسَان prayer — if you have a strong resolve, it is better to pray your night prayers at home. But if you know yourself well enough to know that your resolve is not strong, or if there is a مُقْرِئ doing a خَتْمٌ of the قُرْآن in the مَسْجِد and you want to be part of that خَتْمٌ, then going to the مَسْجِد for it is also a good thing. It is مَنْدُوب.

What you should avoid — and unfortunately many of our brothers have fallen into this — is the idea that تَرَاوِيح is only eight رَكَعَاتٌ, and so once the eight are done, they say, "الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, we're following the سُنَّة," and then spend the next hour standing outside the مَسْجِد talking. Just be honest with yourself: it is fine to say you find twenty رَكَعَاتٌ long. But do not dress your tiredness up as following the سُنَّة. Because the سُنَّة is certainly not to loiter at the door of the مَسْجِد and make idle conversation — I guarantee you that. The Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, did not waste his time. And empty talk at the door of the مَسْجِد is — that is صَقْل-level behavior. That is what the marketplaces are for. We do not want to be part of that.

And do not belittle people, do not look down on them. I am not trying to be judgmental — everyone is where they are for their own reasons. The point is simply this: do not see yourself as better than someone else. Instead, thank اللهُ that He has not afflicted you with that affliction — because it is an affliction. When the Prophet, صلى الله عليه وسلم, saw fifty people doing such things, he said what he said. Recognizing a نِعْمَةٌ that اللهُ has given you is not arrogance. Arrogance is thinking you deserve it more than someone else does. It is simply a favor اللهُ has extended to you, for reasons known only to Him — and He could take it from you tomorrow, give your state to that person, and leave you standing at the door of the مَسْجِد talking.

There is a story about one man who accused Mu'adh ibn Jabal of doing something حَرَامٌ, and Mu'adh — who was known as مُسْتَجَابُ الدُّعَاءِ, someone whose supplications were answered — made a دُعَاء against him. In his old age, that man was seen on the streets of Medina, pinching young women as he walked, because his eyelids had literally fallen over his eyes. He could not see. That was the ابْتِلَاء اللهُ gave him in his old age, for falsely condemning Mu'adh ibn Jabal.

So I am not asking anyone to be self-righteous — myself included. But the point stands: do not go down that road. If people are gathered and talking, greet them. It is a joyous time and there is a place for that. But keep it in its proper place.

And remember our brothers and sisters who are in great tribulation right now — the people of Palestine, the people of Iraq, the people of Algeria, the people of Bosnia, the people of India. I could go on and on, and I would rather not. Those people are genuinely suffering. So temper your joy with some gravity as well.

One thing I learned a long time ago about spiritual light is that sometimes, when a great deal of it descends on people, their reaction is to become giddy — to laugh, to get frivolous. You can see it happen. And it is a way of spilling the light, of wasting it. رَمَضَان can do that to people. Because there is so much light pouring in, and they have not learned how to contain it, they end up laughing through the whole month, socializing, having a good time — and by the end they have spilled it all out. In closing this text, I would like to share some counsel written by one of our great scholars, Sidi Ahmad Zarruq, who died in 1492. He was a great scholar from Morocco who died in Misrata in Libya. He said that the fragrance of his teaching would spread throughout the world long after his bones were laid to rest — and this has proven to be one of his كَرَامَات, his saintly miracles, because his teaching is still reaching us and shaping us today.

He said — رَحِمَهُ اللهُ:

Know — may اللهُ grant you and us success, rectify our worldly and otherworldly lives, and grant us adherence to the way of truth in our journeys and in our sojourns — that repentance is a key, and تَقْوَى is vast, and uprightness is the source of all rectification. A servant is never free of blunders, or shortcomings, or lassitude. Therefore, never be neglectful of تَوْبَةٌ. Never turn away from returning to اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. Never neglect the acts that bring you closer to Him. Every time one of these three occurs — repent and return. Every time you make a mistake — listen and obey. Every time you display shortcomings or lack of enthusiasm — do not give up. Let your primary concern be to remove from your outwأَرْدٌ state anything that is displeasing to اللهُ, and then to maintain that outwأَرْدٌ state through continuous self-counsel. Keep doing this until you find that you naturally flee from anything outwأَرْدٌly displeasing, and that your avoidance of the boundaries of the prohibited has become like a protective net placed before you.

At this point, the time has come to turn inwأَرْدٌ — towأَرْدٌ your heart's presence and its reality — through both reflection and remembrance. Do not hasten the end result before you have completed the beginning, but likewise, do not begin without keeping the end in view. The one who seeks what belongs to the outset at the end loses providential care, and the one who seeks what belongs to the end at the outset loses providential guidance.

Act in accordance with principles and the appropriate legal rulings, not in accordance with stories and fantasies. Do not consider accounts of how things went with others except as a tonic to strengthen your resolve — certainly not as a reference to imitate based on their outwأَرْدٌ forms or what they appear to be saying.

In all of this, rely on a clear path you can refer to and a foundation you can depend upon in every state. The best of these is the path of ابْنُ عَطَاءِ اللهِ, for it contains a clear direction towأَرْدٌ اللهُ. Do not take from others' words unless what they say accords with your own path, but submit to their implications if you desire realization.

Avoid all forms of vain and foul speech to your absolute utmost. Set aside anything whose benefit you cannot discern immediately. Beware of being excessively hأَرْدٌ on yourself before you have attained mastery over it, but equally beware of being too lax in anything that concerns sacred rulings.

This is because the self is constantly fleeing from moderation in everything, inclining towأَرْدٌ extremism in both deviance and guidance alike. To pause and reflect on this is extremely important, because what the شَيْخٌ is telling us is that the nature of the self is extremism — and he is warning us to be aware of this in both good deeds and foul ones, in both obedience to اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى and disobedience to Him.

So he is telling us: beware of being excessively hأَرْدٌ on yourself before you have attained mastery over it, but also beware of being too lax in anything that concerns sacred rulings. He continues: seek out a companion to help you in your affairs and take his counsel in matters that arise from both your inwأَرْدٌ states and your outwأَرْدٌ affairs. If you do take his companionship, then treat him in a manner commensurate with his state, and offer your counsel according to his limitations and capacities, for the perfected friend is no longer to be found.

Indeed, in these times even a suitable, agreeable companion rarely lasts. Beware of the majority of people in matters concerning your religious and worldly affairs, unless you have established that a person has a sound relationship with his Lord — grounded in knowledge free of caprice or love of leadership, and in a sound intellect free of hidden agendas. Do not be heedless of the machinations of others or their hidden states.

Consider people from the vantage point of both their origins and their actions. A person of character and distinguished lineage will rarely affect you with anything other than good, whereas a person of low origins typically lets those roots lead him to disregأَرْدٌ you when times grow difficult. Be extremely vigilant of the dominant qualities of the people in any given land, do not be heedless of the divine wisdom in creation, and take notice of both gatheredness and separation.

Some of this we have already covered in our book الْقَرَائِبُ, so refer to it there. Engage with any matter only in a manner appropriate to what that moment specifically calls for, using gentleness and toleration, and be very wary of both harshness and laxity. Too much laxity in permissible matters pulls the heart backwأَرْدٌ in its journey until even a person of firm resolve ends up resembling a foolish boy.

Work for this world as if you will live forever, but work for the next life as if you will die tomorrow. Do not neglect the externals of your worldly needs, and in the meantime do not be heedless of your end and final resting place.

Be extremely vigilant about avoiding positions of leadership, but should you be tried with such responsibilities, at least know your own limitations. Be utterly sincere — with the sincerity of one who knows full well who is placing demands upon him. Surrender completely to His decree with the submission of one who knows he can never overcome Him.

Have a firm foundation for all your affairs and you will be safe from their pitfalls. Organize your devotional practices and you will find your time extended through the بَرَكَةٌ — the blessings — within it. Never be fanatical about anything, whether it is truth or falsehood, and your heart will remain sound towأَرْدٌ others.

Never claim anything you are entitled to, let alone what you are not entitled to, and you will be safe from tricks and treachery. Anyone who claims a rank above his own will fall in هُمْiliation; those who claim a rank they do warrant will have it stripped from them; while those who claim a station less than their true rank will be elevated to levels even higher than they actually deserve. Never give your companion anything of your inner state beyond what his own state warrants. If you descend to his level, he will show you contempt, whereas if you attempt to raise him to your level, he will abandon you.

Never demand a right from anyone, whether an intimate or a stranger. A stranger in reality owes you nothing, and someone close to you is too precious to be the target of your blame. Never assume that anyone in this world can truly understand your circumstances except through the lens of their own. In reality, everyone sees things in accordance with their own frame of reference and personal path. Yet when aims, purposes, and aspirations are similar, people tend to work together towأَرْدٌ a common goal.

Never belittle any talk that involves people in their absence, even if it seems harmless, given the possibility that harm may enter into it. Guأَرْدٌ your secrets even when you feel safe with someone, for the person you confide in is not a safer vessel than your own heart from which the secret first arose. Never abandon even an atom's weight of your regular devotional practice. Never be lenient with yourself, whether in times of laxity or times of high resolve. Should you miss some of your practice at a given time, make it up at another. If you are unable to perform your usual practice, at least occupy yourself with something comparable.

Never obey your ego, even for a moment. Never believe any of its claims, no matter what it tells you. Be vigilant about your resolve in all your affairs to your utmost. Should you resolve to do something, do it immediately before the resolve wanes. Exأَمِينe your soul constantly regأَرْدٌing what you are obliged or need to do. Anything you have no need to do, leave it — even if it is recommended. That means not involving yourself in anything beyond what is absolutely necessary and what constitutes a real, discernible need.

Treat others exactly as you would wish to be treated, and fulfill what is due to them. All of this is beautifully captured in the words of the poet:

*If you desire to live such that your religion is safe, your portion full, and your honor sound — guأَرْدٌ your tongue, and never mention another's faults, remembering that you yourself have faults and others have tongues. Watch your eye; should it ever reveal to you the faults of others, say to it: O my eye, other people have eyes too. Live treating others well, and avoid aggression, and should others aggress against you, leave them — but in the best way.*

The source of these words is nothing other than the traditions of the Prophet, peace and blessings of اللهُ be upon him, when he said: *Be vigilant of اللهُ wherever you are, and follow a misdeed with a good deed — it will remove it — and treat others with the most excellent of character.* In another narration he said, peace and blessings of اللهُ be upon him: *Every child of Adam makes mistakes, and the best of those who make mistakes are those who seek to redress them.* And again: *The Holy Spirit inspired my heart's core that no soul will die until it fulfills its decreed portion of this world and its appointed time here. So be conscious of اللهُ and make your requests with dignity.*

In summation, repentance, awareness of اللهُ, and uprightness are the foundations of all that is beneficial. The truth is clear, and its details are weighty and significant. The affair belongs only to اللهُ; success is in His hands. Peace be upon you.
