He then moves to the next disease, which is displeasure with the divine decree — and this will sound very familiar to many people. This disease occurs when a person resists what Allah, Majestic and Exalted, has decreed — for instance, saying, "I did not warrant this happening to me," or "I do not deserve this." And that is a remarkable thing to hear, because you hear it from people all the time.

I don't deserve this. Why is this happening to me? Or, as he puts it next: what did I do to deserve this suffering? What wrong did I do to deserve this? The point is that everything comes from Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. This is why Imam Abu al-Hasan said that people possess a quality they are entirely unaware of, and it destroys all of their good deeds. Very few people actually recognize it: being displeased with the divine decree. They do not realize they are showing displeasure toward what Allah has ordained. Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى says that they were displeased with what Allah had given them, and so Allah removed all of their good deeds. By contrast, the people of تَقْوَى, when asked what their Lord has sent down, say: "Good — it is all good." That is their answer. Everything is good. Nothing is evil. There is a reason, a lesson, a wisdom in it. This is the difference between the people of Allah and everyone else.

The people of Allah recognize that this world is a place of purification — a مَبَرَّة in itself. We were sent here to purify ourselves. The very purpose of our existence is to purify ourselves, to draw near to Allah, and to worship Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. That is it. Once you recognize that, everything becomes an opportunity to learn, to grow, and to draw closer to Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى — everything.

Because there are only four possible states a human being can be in. Only four — no fifth. If you can name a fifth, that would be remarkable. According to Abu al-Abbas al-Mursi, you are either in نِعْمَةٌ, or in بَلِيَّة, or in طَاعَةٌ, or in مَعْصِيَةٌ. That is it. You are either in a blessing from Allah, or a tribulation from Allah, in obedience to Allah, or in disobedience. That is the full scope.

Each one of these states calls for a specific response. Allah wants something from you in each case. If Allah gives you a نِعْمَةٌ — what response does He desire? شُكْر. Gratitude. That is the response. And what is gratitude according to the شَرِيعَة? It is to use the نِعْمَةٌ in what is acceptable to Allah — whether مُبَاح, مَنْدُوبٌ, or وَاجِب. To use it in something مَكْرُوهٌ does not quite constitute gratitude, but neither is it ingratitude, since it is not حَرَامٌ. Look at those five categories: only one carries punishment. The other four either earn reward or nothing. So: شُكْر.

He says later in the مَتْن that شُكْر is to utilize what Allah has given you in what pleases Allah. That is شُكْر. And so you have شُكْرُ اللِّسَانِ, شُكْرُ الْجَنَانِ, شُكْرُ اللِّسَانِ, شُكْرُ الْبَنَانِ — gratitude of the tongue, the heart, and the limbs. Part of شُكْر is to thank people, to tell others what someone did for you. That is actually considered part of شُكْر — to say, so-and-so benefited me in this or that. إِظْهَارُ النِّعْمَةِ, showing the blessing openly, is part of شُكْر. Allah loves that. To feel the blessing in your heart, and to act with those blessings in a way that pleases Allah.

The next state is tribulation — بَلِيَّة. What does Allah expect from us? صَبْر. صَبْر. صَبْر. If He gives you a tribulation, He expects صَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ. Though in truth, you are not even required to have صَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ in its fullest sense. صَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ is when you do not complain to anyone at all. You can complain — and we will get into that in a moment — but complaining to others is not ideal; it just gives you a degree of release.

Then the next state: طَاعَةٌ — obedience. What is the response? شُهُودُ الْمِنَّةِ — recognizing the gift. The danger is not to become arrogant. If you find yourself in obedience, you must recognize that it is from the تَوْفِيق of Allah that you are there. Do not start thinking you are better than others because you are in طَاعَةٌ while they are in مَعْصِيَةٌ. That is the danger of obedience — and in a deeper sense, the inherent danger of religion itself. The religious person begins to see himself as better than the irreligious person. That is شَيْطَان's opening. Once he has you there, it is rope-a-dope — like Muhammad Ali used to do. He has you until the end of the fight, because كِبْرٌ is his game. That is شَيْطَان. Once he has you in تَكَبُّر, in تَكَبُّرٌ عَلَى النَّاسِ, he has you.

So that is the danger of obedience. What Allah expects when you are obedient is that you recognize it as a gift from Him. Recognize that just as He made you obedient, He could just as easily have given the situation of the person who is disobeying Him to you instead, and given them the نِعْمَةٌ. The Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم said: do not find fault in others, because if you seek out their faults, Allah will remove those faults from them and place them in you. So there should be a sense of fear — a fear when you see someone in بَلِيَّة. That person is in بَلِيَّة and he needs to change. And if someone is in مَعْصِيَةٌ, that too is a بَلِيَّة, because طَاعَةٌ is a نِعْمَةٌ, and they go together. He is in a بَلِيَّة.

This is why Imam Malik relates in the مُوَطَّأ that عِيسَى عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ said — and Imam Malik frames it as something he heard reported — "بَلَغَنِي أَنَّ عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ قَالَ," meaning: I have just heard, so it is a narration, though he evidently thought it sound enough to include in his مُوَطَّأ. He said that عِيسَى عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ was reported to have said that there are only two kinds of people: those in tribulation and those in نِعْمَةٌ. And he said: do not look upon the people of tribulation as though you are lords over them. Look upon them with compassion, as servants would look upon them.

That is the point. When you see people in tribulation, do not say to yourself, "I am better than these people." Say rather: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ, they are in tribulation, and اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي عَافَانِي. That is the دُعَاء of the Prophet. When he saw people in tribulation, he said: الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي عَافَانِي مِمَّا ابْتَلَاهُ بِهِ وَفَضَّلَنِي عَلَى كَثِيرٍ مِمَّنْ خَلَقَ تَفْضِيلًا — praise be to the One who granted me well-being in a way that spared me from the tribulation He gave to these people, and who favored me over so much of His creation. That is where you should stand when it comes to obedience.

Now in مَعْصِيَةٌ — what is expected of you? تَوْبَة. You must make تَوْبَة. And if you look at this and realize: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ, every single thing that happens to me will be one of these four things, and for each one there is a response that will please Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى — then if I please Allah, the thing that happened to me was in fact a نِعْمَةٌ, because it brought me closer to Allah, and that is the whole point. Perhaps you dislike a thing and there is much good in it for you. Perhaps you love a thing and there is much harm in it for you. Allah knows and you do not. So just admit you do not know — and then comes الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ, الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ. Ibn 'Abbas said: if a tribulation befalls you, it is itself a نِعْمَةٌ in that it could have been worse. So you must thank Allah. الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ — it is not worse than it could be.

And the best model is to look at the سِيرَة of the Messenger of Allah صلى الله عليه وسلم — to see how he lived, and to recognize that no one endured greater tribulation. He buried all of his children except Fatimah. How many people will ever face that? Out of six children, to bury five? He lost the people closest to him. His most beloved uncle was slaughtered on the battlefield. The beloved wife with whom he had shared years of a blissful marriage died in his most difficult year. Abu Talib, the uncle who had raised him — he had been raised an orphan. There came a time when he had no supporters. The very people who had honored him were calling him a madman, saying he was مَجْنُونٌ. How many people experience that — where suddenly your friends, your community, everyone around you calls you مَجْنُونٌ and spits on you? He endured sanctions that most people could not bear to even imagine, and that was just one stretch of his life. All of those tribulations — and yet we have no record, not a single word, of complaint. Twenty-three years. No complaints. الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ. That is all he ever said. الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ.

So that is the situation. To be angry at the قَدَرٌ is to be in a state of غَفْلَة — in heedlessness, pure and simple.

Now, here is another important point. Imam al-Qarafi, رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ, wrote a book dedicated entirely to distinguishing things in the شَرِيعَة that appear similar but are not. One of the distinctions he draws is between الرِّضَا بِالْقَضَاءِ وَالْمَقْضِيِّ بِهِ — being pleased with the divine decree itself versus being pleased with the thing that is decreed. When looking at قَضَاءٌ, you have two components. There is the قَضَاءٌ itself, and separately the قَدَرٌ — and there is some difference of opinion around these two terms. The Maturidis and the Ash'aris actually hold opposite positions on which is which, but the point stands regardless of which label you assign. In one reading, قَضَاءٌ means that Allah has decided to bring something about, and قَدَرٌ means that He has measured and enabled the means by which it comes to be. The maqdi' bihi is the thing that actually occurs.

Now — should we be pleased with everything that happens, the maqdi' bihi? Why not? Because bad things happen, and do not forget that within Allah's قَدَرٌ is both خَيْر and شَرٌّ. You should not be pleased with شَرٌّ, because Allah Himself is not pleased with it. Allah has decreed that there will be evil in the world He created, but He is not pleased with it — meaning He does not reward it; that is what His رِضْوَان is. He does not reward it. So when you see مَعْصِيَةٌ, you should not like it or be pleased with it. Allah does not want you to be pleased with it. But you should not be displeased with the fact that Allah created a world in which such things exist. Do you see the distinction? One thing is refusing to be displeased with Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى and the world He created — because it is His world and He made it. The other is recognizing that within this world, Allah has placed things as a test and challenge for believers, and those are the things you should not be pleased with, because He does not want you to be pleased with them. If you were pleased with them, you would do nothing to change them. Your displeasure with them is precisely what compels you to go out and transform what is wrong in society.

That is the distinction. Imam al-Qarafi offers an analogy — just for تَقْرِيبُ الْمَعَانِي, to bring the concept closer. He says: imagine you have a doctor, and you go to the doctor, and the doctor says you have gangrene — he has to cut your arm off. So he does. One day that doctor is walking past and overhears you talking to a friend, your arm gone, saying: "I went to this incompetent doctor. I should have gotten a second opinion. He had no business doing this to me." The doctor hearing that is going to be hurt and upset — because he did not want to cause you pain; he wanted to help you, and the amputation was how he helped you. Now, if instead the doctor walks past and hears you say to someone: "I went to this remarkable doctor. He cut off my arm, and it has caused me enormous pain — I cannot sleep at night. It has been the most severe treatment I have ever been through" — when the doctor hears that, he feels compassion, not offense. Because you said nothing against him. All you said is that the treatment was grueling.

And this is the analogy for how we speak about what Allah does to us in this world. If you say, "Allah is really testing me hard right now — it is difficult, but مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ, there is wisdom in it" — that will not make Allah angry. It is better not to broadcast your troubles to others, and doing so does diminish your patience. But there is a world of difference between saying "I don't deserve this — why is this happening to me?" and saying "this is so hard to bear." The first is a complaint against what Allah has done. The second is an expression of the difficulty you are having in enduring it. That distinction matters enormously.

He also makes this point: we were never commanded by Allah to enjoy hardship and tribulation. Nowhere in the Quran does Allah tell you to take pleasure in horrible things that happen to you. That is not how He made us, and there is no تَكْلِيف in that.

But Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى does censure certain people in the Quran when He says that He sent them a punishment, yet it did not humble them and did not make them call out to Him to lift it from them. You see this today — things worsen year after year, and people simply throw up their hands. Muslim countries — look at what happened to Kuwait. You would think they would all be rising every night for تَهَجُّدٌ, beseeching Allah to never let that happen again. But things return to how they were. There is no اِسْتِكَانَة, no genuine عُبُودِيَّة before Allah. Tribulations come, and if they do not change people, that is a deeply troubling state to be in.
