The next disease is الْحُزْء — derision, mockery, making fun of people. When Musa, عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ, commanded the Bani Israel to sacrifice a cow, they responded: "أَتَتَّخِذُنَا هُزُوًۭا" — "Are you mocking us?" He replied: "أَعُوذُ بِٱللَّهِ أَنْ أَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلْجَهِلِينَ" — "I seek refuge in Allah from being among the ignorant." From this exchange, the scholars derived that اِسْتِهْزَاءٌ — mockery — is rooted in جَهْل, ignorance. To ridicule people is an act of ignorance. This includes lampooning, caricature, comedic ridicule — any form of targeting someone to make others laugh at their expense.

What the author says about its cure is that it is the same as the cure for arrogance, because mockery is a form of arrogance. To mock someone, you must first look down on them. That is كِبْرٌ. This is why the Moroccans have a saying — though it traces back to Sayyidina Ali — "لا تحقر عبدنا عسى يكون لله وليا" — do not belittle anyone, for he may be a وَلِيٌّ of Allah.

Even if you see a man drunk and vomiting in the street, you do not know what his seal will be. Imam al-Qurtubi made a striking point on this: when Umar was still bowing to idols in Mecca, he was already beloved to Allah, سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, who knew what the seal would be. The Muslims watching Umar at that stage might have mocked him — "What a fool, a polytheist" — but they did not know Umar. They were seeing one moment in a story still unfolding. That is why it is profoundly dangerous to judge someone by where they stand right now, or to mock them for it.

This is why Allah, سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, says in the Quran:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا يَسْخَرْ قَوْمٌ مِّن قَوْمٍ عَسَىٰ أَن يَكُونُوا خَيْرًا مِّنْهُمْ

"O you who believe, let not one people mock another people — perhaps they are better than them."

وَلَا نِسَآءٌۭ مِّن نِّسَآءٍ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُنَّ خَيْرًۭا مِّنْهُنَّ

"And let not women mock other women — perhaps they are better than them."

It is worth recalling here that the Prophet ﷺ was commanded not to curse the idols of the polytheists:

لَا تَسُبُّوا الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ فَيَسُبُّوا اللَّهِ عَدْوًا بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ

"Do not revile those they call upon besides Allah, lest they revile Allah out of ignorance and hostility."

This is the principle of سَدُّ الذَّرَائِعِ — blocking the means to harm. Muslims burning Israeli flags or American flags in public — what does that provoke? They go and burn Qurans, or they stomp on a Saudi flag that bears لا إله إلا الله محمد رسول الله. You incite them into an act that is not only harmful to them but sacrilegious to us. We do not want people cursing Allah. There is no good reason to do it.

This is also why the Prophet ﷺ, even in his greatest victories, was never boastful. When he entered Mecca, his head was bowed low — not the posture of a conquering hero, but of a man in total humility before his Lord. That is the truly great leader. The other kind — the triumphant swagger, the chest-thumping — that is جَاهِلِيَّة. That is Abu Jahl. We are meant to be above it, not in a superficial way, but at a deep level.

You can, of course, make دُعَاء against the oppressors. The Quran contains supplications against the ظَالِمِينَ. But there is a difference between that and public spectacles of contempt. And making دُعَاء for people's guidance is مُسْتَحَبّ — recommended, though not obligatory.

Some scholars even cautioned against cursing "Israel" by name, because it is the name of a prophet — the Prophet Ya'qub, عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ. Beyond that, many Jews have embraced Islam throughout history and continue to do so. They are human beings, not demons. The Serbs who carried out atrocities are human beings too. Even Wahshi, who killed Hamza — even Hind, who mutilated his body — they both became Muslim. People can make تَوْبَةٌ. People change. And those who have committed terrible acts often carry those acts with them for the rest of their lives. More veterans of the Vietnam War have taken their own lives than were killed in the war itself — many people do not know that. They paid, and keep paying. They have hearts. As Allah says:

إِن يَمْسَسْكُمْ قَرْحٌۭ فَقَدْ مَسَّ ٱلْقَوْمَ قَرْحٌۭ مِّثْلُهُۥ

"If you are wounded, they are wounded just as you are." We all bleed red.

As for reading the Quran while being among the oppressors it condemns — the Prophet ﷺ said: "رُبَّ قَرِئَنَّ الْقُرَانَ وَالْقَرَانَ يَلْعَنَهُ" — perhaps a man recites the Quran while the Quran is cursing him. Because Allah says: أَلَا إِنَّ لَعْنَةَ اللَّهِ عَلَى الْكَاذِبِينَ — "Verily, the curse of Allah is upon those who lie." If you are reading the Quran and you are a liar, you are reading a curse upon yourself.

And that brings us to the final point on this disease. The author says its treatment follows the same path as the treatment of كِبْرٌ, which we have already covered. But he adds one more element of self-knowledge: recognize that the purpose of mockery is to humiliate someone else. That is what drives اِسْتِهْزَاءٌ. Yet in doing so, a person humiliates himself before Allah and earns in return only ignorance. Allah says: لَا تَنَابَزُوا بِالْأَلْقَابِ — "Do not insult one another with offensive labels" — and the consequence is that evil comes back upon you.
