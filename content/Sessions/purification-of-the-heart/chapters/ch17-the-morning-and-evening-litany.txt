Now, what you need to do is go through the sheet in front of you. You will find many different versions of these, which are called أَذْكَارُ الصَّبَاحِ وَالْمَسَاءِ — the morning and evening remembrances. My recommendation, for myself and for all of you, is to do these every day to the best of your ability. If you cannot do all of them, there are certain ones you should absolutely not miss, and I will point those out. Now this particular collection does not have a specific narration from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ specifying that he did this, then this, then this in sequence. So whenever you see these compiled collections, the formulations will vary. Sidi Ahmad Zarruq has one — سَفِينَةُ النَّجَاءِ مِنَ الِالْتِجَاءِ إِلَى اللَّهِ — which is a very fine one, drawn entirely from the Book and the Sunnah.

One of the things Sidi Ahmad Zarruq says is that you should observe the أَوْرَاد that came down from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ before turning to any دُعَاءٌ written by other people. It is permissible to read دُعَاءٌ composed by others, provided those people knew what they were saying — meaning they were known to be true scholars and people of Allah. The صَحَابَة had دُعَاءٌ, the Tabi'un had دُعَاءٌ, and these have come down to us. There are صَلَوَاتٌ that come from other than the Prophet as well. But what Sidi Ahmad Zarruq said is this: if you are neglecting the دُعَاءٌ and أَذْكَار that the Prophet told us to observe every day and preferring other people's compositions over them, then something is wrong. That is his point.

And Qadi Abu Bakr Ibn Arabi said, at the end of his book *قَانُونُ التَّأْوِيلِ*, that شَيْطَان's primary task with any Muslim is to pull them away from the remembrance of Allah altogether. But if he finds them persisting in much remembrance, he will then try to steer them away from the remembrance drawn from the Book and the Sunnah, toward remembrance composed by other people.

That is شَيْطَان's strategy. And so the beautiful thing here — and this is the world of Shaykh Abdullah Al-Haddad, who is from the noble lineage, one of the Shurafa of Yemen — is that he is considered a مُجَدِّد of the twelfth century as well. His grandson was one of my teachers, Shaykh Ahmad Mashhur Al-Haddad, who wrote *مِفْتَاحُ الْجَنَّةِ*, which has been translated into English. I received this from him, and he took it from his father, who took it from his grandfather, who took it from the author himself.

He begins it with بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ. This follows the hadith that anything worth doing, if it does not begin with بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ, is cut off from barakah. So anything that is truly worth doing should be done in the name of Allah, in the name of compassion.

He then begins with قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ, recited three times. This comes from a hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ said: *أَيَعْجِزُ أَحَدُكُمْ أَنْ يَخْتِمَ الْقُرْآنَ كُلَّ يَوْمٍ* — is one of you incapable of reading the Qur'an once every day? The Companions asked, "Who could do that, يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ?" He replied that Surah al-Ikhlas — *إِخْلَاصُ عَدِيلُ فِرَاثِ الْقُرْآنِ* — is equivalent to one third of the Qur'an in its weight and meaning. By reading it three times, we hope for the reward of having read the entire Qur'an. It should therefore be read every day — ideally after every prayer, and at the very least in the morning and evening.

The next two are called the مُؤَوِّلَتَيْنِ. *مُعَوِّل* means that which gives you refuge — from *تَعْوِيذَة*, meaning protection. So the مُؤَوِّلَتَيْنِ are *‏قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ ‎* and *‏قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ ‎*, and these should be read every day. There is a hadith indicating they should be recited before sleep, after which you cover yourself — a protection from the jinn and the شَيَاطِين.

He then prescribes reciting, three times, the supplication of seeking refuge in Allah from the هَمَزَاتٌ of شَيْطَان. *هَمَزَات* means to goad someone on — "come on, do it, do it." The هَمَزَاتٌ of the شَيَاطِين are those whisperings within the soul urging you toward what you should not do. So in this supplication you seek refuge in Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى from the هَمَزَاتٌ of شَيْطَان.

One of the signs of the end of time is *تَقْذِيرُ الْهَوْسَةِ* — that people's minds will be overwhelmed by constant whispering, until that is all they hear. When you consider this now, it is striking: so many people have music, films, and television running constantly around them, and all of that is from شَيْطَان — all of it, without exception. By exposing yourself to it, you are allowing شَيْطَان not merely to whisper but to send visual images streآمِينg into you as well. All of it goes into the heart. In doing so, you are slowly destroying your heart.

People walk around carrying all these images that surface in their minds — songs, jingles, advertising music — and everywhere you go now, in stores and shopping centers, it is all there, constantly bombarding people. This supplication is a shield against the هَمَزَاتٌ of شَيْطَان. The نَفْس carries its own impulses as well.

Recited three times:

رَبِّ أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ هَمَزَاتِ الشَّيَاطِينِ وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ رَبِّهُمْ يَحْضَرُونَ

*"My Lord, I seek refuge in You from the insinuations of the شَيَاطِين, and I seek refuge in You, my Lord, lest they be present."*

You say it all together, three times separately — recite it once, then return to the beginning and recite it again.

As for sports: most sports involve the exposure of عَوْرَة. People's flesh that one should not be looking at is on display — shorts that reveal the thighs, and so on. Allah said: ‏لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ وَلِىَ دِينِ ‎ — and Allah says, do not keep company with those who take their lives as لَعِبٌ وَلَهُ, play and pastime. In fact, they call baseball in this country the national pastime. اللَّهُ الدَّيْنِ — اللَّهُ الدَّيْنِ ها الوَطَنِ — the national pastime.

Now, if you want to go out and actually play some sport, at least you get some physical exercise from it. But to sit and watch — I will tell you honestly: when I was growing up, baseball was my life through high school. It was all I cared about. And I simply cannot watch a game now. You could not pay me to watch a game, and I am completely serious. I loved baseball. Football too. You could not pay me. I was once at someone's house and they were watching the Iranian-American soccer match, and I did unfortunately indulge a little — but it was at someone else's house; I would not do that on my own. I would not waste my time that way. Those things are simply a waste of your life.

You need to ask yourself: how much is an hour of your life worth? How much are two hours worth? People are selling their kidneys now. If someone could buy a year of your life from you, what would you sell it for? Once you begin to realize that you will be accountable not only for your life but for every minute of it — and that life itself, along with health, is the greatest gift Allah has given you after إِيمَان — you start to ask: what are you doing with what Allah has provided you?

We were created to use our time well. That does not mean you cannot enjoy yourself — there are *مُدَاهَاة* things, permissible forms of enjoyment. That is precisely why the Prophet ﷺ, when attending a wedding, asked whether they had brought some entertainment. They said yes. He said that the أَنْصَار love entertainment, and he wanted to show the Jews and the Christians that there is *فُسْحَة* in our religion — that we are not joyless, not prudish, not people who refuse to enjoy themselves or mark occasions. But what is happening now — this entire society has become one vast entertainment machine. People work solely in order to be entertained; they earn money so they can go out and spend it on entertainment. We all need to disengage from that. Wean ourselves away from this society. Just wean yourself — cut the cord. Stop nursing from the breast of دُنْيَا and the breast of لَاهُوت.

And it is pleasant, no doubt about it — it is pleasant. You sit back and watch a good film for a couple of hours. And then what? There is always an empty feeling afterward, the sense that you have just wasted two hours of your life. Everyone knows this feeling at the end — you watch it, and then what? Why would you do that? It is all a trick. They just trick you into it.

So: أَفَحَسِبْتُمْ — or as it appears in the next passage — أَفَحَسِبْتُمْ أَنَّمَا خَلَقْنَكُمْ عَبَثًۭا — do you think We created you عَذَثًا, just for nothing? Did We create you in vain?

أَفَحَسِبْتُمْ أَنَّمَا خَلَقْنَكُمْ عَبَثًۭا وَأَنَّكُمْ إِلَيْنَا لَا تُرْفِعُونَ

And you will not be returned to Us? You are not going to come back to Us? Exalted is Allah, the King, the Truth. There is no god but He. رَبِّ الْعَشِدْ كَرِيمِ — the Lord of the Noble Throne.

‏وَمَن يَدْعُ مَعَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَهًا آخَرَ لَا بُرْهَنَ لَهُۥ بِهِۦ فَإِنَّمَا حِسَابُهُۥ عِندَ رَبِّهِۦٓ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يُفْلِحُ ٱلْكَفِرُونَ ‎

This is a verse of the Qur'an that the Prophet ﷺ would recite every day.

وَقِرَّبَكُ فِي الْوَرَحَمُ وَإِنتَ خَيْرُ الرَّاحِمِينَ — then comes another verse, a supplication: "O Allah, forgive, have compassion, for You are the best of those who show mercy." And then yet another verse the Prophet would recite every day: فَسُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ حِينَ تُمْسُونَ — "Glory be to Allah when you enter the evening" — وَحِينَ تُسْبِحُونَ — "and when you enter the morning." This is the foundation of the أَذْكَار said at those two times — مَسَاءً وَالصَّبَاحُ — morning and evening are the times to say سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ. ‏وَلَهُ ٱلْحَمْدُ فِى ٱلسَّمَوَتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَعَشِيًّۭا وَحِينَ تُظْهِرُونَ ‎ — "And His is all praise in the heavens and the earth, in the evening and at midday." Scholars have also cited this verse as a proof for the five prayers: حِينَ تُمْسُونَ corresponds to Asr, حِينَ تُسْبِحُونَ to فَجْر, عَشِيًّا to Maghrib and Isha, and وَحِينَ تُظْهِرُونَ to Dhuhr.

As for رَبِّ الْخَيْرُ الرَّحْمِينَ — "Forgive, show mercy, for You are the best of those who show mercy" — is this for oneself alone or for all Muslims? In principle it is general, but when you are making this supplication the اِغْفِرْ لِي is specifically for yourself.

Then comes: يَخْرِجْ الْحَيَّ مَنَ الْمَيْتِينَ وَيَخْرِجْ الْمَيْتِ مَنَ الْحَيِّ — "He brings the living from the dead and the dead from the living." The mufassirun mention several meanings here: one is the literal example of a chick emerging from an egg, or the هَمٌّan being arising from a dead, inanimate source. But there is also a spiritual meaning — Allah brings إِيمَان out of kufr, and kufr can extinguish إِيمَان. Dead hearts come alive through faith, and living hearts can die by falling into disbelief. The Prophet also said that Allah brings the earth back to life after its death, and that in the same way He will raise us up — and this too points to the heart's capacity for revival. In the Quran, the word ard is used مَجَازٍ بِالْقَلْبِ as a traditional tafsir — so Allah revives the heart through His remembrance after it has died through heedlessness and forgetfulness of Him.

The Prophet also used to recite this ta'weezah: سَمِيرْ عَلِيمٍ عَلِيمُ بِاللَّهِ سَمِيرْ عَلِيمٍ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ — three times. That was among his ta'weezat.

Then there is the verse that the Prophet would recite in both the morning and the evening: ‏لَوْ أَنزَلْنَا هَذَا ٱلْقُرْآنَ عَلَىٰ جَبَلٍۢ لَّرَأَيْتَهُۥ خَشِعًۭا مُّتَصَدِّعًۭا مِّنْ خَشْيَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ وَتِلْكَ ٱلْأَمْثَلُ نَضْرِبُهَا لِلنَّاسِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ ‎ — "Had We revealed this Quran to a mountain, you would have seen it هَمٌّbled, torn apart by the khashya of Allah." Khashya is fear grounded in knowledge — it is different from khawf. A rat can experience khawf, but a rat cannot have khashya. That is why the scholars are described as those who have khashya of Allah, and why the Messenger of Allah said أَنَا أَعْلَمُكُمْ دُنْيَا — "I am the most knowledgeable of you concerning Allah" — and therefore أَخْشَاكُمْ — the most filled with khashya. What Allah is conveying here is that the Quran carries such a weight of knowledge that even a mountain — an inanimate object — would be shattered and هَمٌّbled by khashya if the Quran were revealed to it, because of the meanings and knowledge contained within it. This is precisely why تَبَدَّرَ الْقُرَآنِ — deep reflection on the Quran — leads to khushu', a هَمٌّbled and awed awareness of Allah.

Allah then says that these are similitudes He strikes for هَمٌّanity so that they might reflect. Does this mean the mountain has knowledge? Some scholars say that the mountain here is a symbol of a scholar, because in Arabic a great scholar is compared to a mountain — هو جبل من الجبال — "he is a mountain among mountains." They would say of someone like Imam al-Shafi'i: امام شافع كان جبلا من الجبال. So the meaning is that if this Quran were revealed to anyone of immense knowledge and understanding, it would shatter them. Yet Allah also tells us in the Quran that He offered the trust to the heavens, the earth, and the mountains. Scholars note that Allah begins with the heavens because they are His most awe-inspiring creation in terms of sheer magnitude — the Messenger of Allah ﷺ is the greatest in terms of maqam, but in terms of awesomeness the heavens surpass everything, and only the astronomer can begin to grasp that. Then comes the earth, then the mountains. And that refusal, scholars say, was not a refusal of إِيمَان.

Allah then says He is Allah — there is no doubt in it — the Knower of the seen and the unseen. Shahada means "the seen," but it also carries the meaning of testimony, because everything visible is testifying to لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ. This is why the Prophet said: how extraordinary that a man could disobey Allah, how strange that anyone could disbelieve in Him — وَفِي كُلِّ تَحْرِيكَةٍ وَتَسْكِينَةٍ — in every movement and every stillness there is always a شَاهِدٌ, a witness. Everything in creation is testifying that Allah is One. So the شَهَادَة — this visible world — is, in its very meaning, saying لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ. That is the message the entire world is sending to the هَمٌّan being, and the هَمٌّan being is the one capable of decoding it.

This is why Allah describes Himself as the King, the Holy, the Sanctified, the Complete. سَلَامٌ here does not mean peace in the ordinary sense — it is wrong to render Allah's name as simply "peace" in that casual way. الْمُؤْمِن is the one who makes others safe, who gives آمِين. الْمُهَيْمِنُ is the one who oversees everything and holds all things in His control. الْعَزِيزُ is the Great, the Powerful. الْجَبَّار is the Compeller — the One who compels all things and all beings to do what they do. الْمُتَكَبِّرُ is the Exalted, the Magnificent, the Supremely Great. And سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ — "Glory be to Allah above all that they associate with Him." In Arabic, سُبْحَانَ means تَمْزِيح: when you say سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ you are declaring that Allah is utterly unlike anything. You are asserting His incomparability.

As for الْخَالِقُ: I prefer not to use certain terms because of their complexity in تَوْحِيد, but الْخَالِقُ, البَارِئ, and الْمُصَوِّر represent three aspects of the creative process — خَلْق, بِرّ, and تَصْوِير. الْخَالِق is when Allah decides to create: He possesses the قُدْرَة, the عِلْم, and the إِرَادَة, and when these three converge, creation comes into being. In Allah's ultimate reality this capacity is always present — His power to be الْخَالِقُ is eternally there. When He decides to create, He is البَارِئ — the One who actually brings it into existence. Creation is called بَرِيئَةٌ, which is why the Prophet ﷺ is called خَيْرُ الْبَرِيَّةِ — the finest of all that the بَرِيء has brought into being. الْمُصَوِّر is the Fashioner: He shapes and gives form. In the context of a fetus, الْخَالِقُ is the One who determined there would be a هَمٌّan life; البَارِئ is the One who brought about the seed in the man and the ovum in the woman and united them; and الْمُصَوِّر is the One who fashions the form — the cells differentiate, the liver becomes a liver, the brain becomes a brain, the spine takes its shape. That is الْمُصَوِّر.

In reality, however, there is no linearity in Allah. Imam al-Ghazali says these are only facets — and because we are limited, we can only perceive one aspect at a time. Allah is describing for us what is in reality a multi-faceted process, but it is only multi-faceted from our limited vantage point. In truth it is all one; it is all Allah. Attributes are approximations for هَمٌّan understanding, because we exist in time and our intellects are bounded. These are simply different facets of a single creative reality. Is that clear?

لَهُ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ — Allah has the most beautiful names. يُسَبِّحُ لَهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَوَتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ — everything in the heavens and the earth glorifies Him, and beyond that, without exception, everything — rocks, atoms, all things — is praising Allah. وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ — and He is الْعَزِيزُ, الْحَكِيم.

Then: سَلَمٌ عَلَىٰ نُوحٍۢ فِى ٱلْعَلَمِينَ — إِنَّا كَذَلِكَ نَجْزِى ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ — إِنَّهُۥ مِنْ عِبَادِنَا ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ. Whether this verse was included based on a hadith or on the compiler's own اِجْتِهَادٌ is unclear. But one of its wisdoms, I think, is this: in a certain sense we are living in an age of نُوحٌ. A great flood is coming — and all of us know it. When نُوحٌ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ was building his ark, people laughed at him. Building an ark in this age — preparing for the آخِرَة — invites the same ridicule. People think you are a fool. If you are genuinely concerned about the آخِرَة, people assume something is wrong with you. Even Muslims — they would not say outright that they do not believe in the آخِرَة, because they do not want to make that admission, but all of their actions tell you otherwise. Everything they care about is دُنْيَا. And when they see Muslims who are focused on the آخِرَة, they say: "يَا أَخِي, why don't you do something for yourself? For your دُنْيَا? اِعْمَلْ لِدُنْيَاكَ كَأَنَّكَ تَعِيشُ أَبَدًا — live for your world as if you will live forever."

Next comes: أَللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَصْبَحْتُ مِنْكَ بِنِعْمَةٍ وَعَافِيَةٍ — "O Allah, I have entered this morning in Your blessing, with good health and with my wrongs veiled by You — فَأَتِمَّ نِعْمَتَكَ عَلَيَّ وَعَافِيَتَكَ وَسِتْرَكَ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ — so complete Your blessing upon me, Your 'afiyah, and Your veiling, in this world and the next." This is said three times. (There are two رِوَايَة — فَأَتِمَّنَا or أَتْمِمْ — but both are transmitted.)

The next supplication: the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites it four times receives a reward equivalent to that of freeing a هَمٌّan being from bondage. It goes: اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أُشْهِدُكَ — "O Allah, I enter this morning calling You to bear witness" — وَأُشْهِدُ حَمَلَةَ عَرْشِكَ — "and calling the angels who carry Your throne to bear witness" — وَمَلَائِكَتَكَ وَجَمِيعَ خَلْقِكَ — "and all of Your angels and all of Your creation to bear witness" — أَنَّكَ أَنتَ اللَّهُ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنتَ وَحْدَكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُكَ وَرَسُولُكَ — "that You are Allah, there is no god but You, alone, without partner, and that Muhammad ﷺ is Your servant and Your messenger." This is said four times.

Then: اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ حَمْدًا يُوَافِي نِعَمَهُ وَيُكَافِئُ مَزِيدَهُ — "All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all the worlds, a praise commensurate with His blessings and reciprocating all the increase He bestows." This hadith encompasses the complete fulfillment of حَمْدٌ and شُكْر for all the نِعَمٌ of Allah. When we asked Habibullah الْحَجُّ for a وَصِيَّةٌ — a piece of counsel — to pass on to people, this is the supplication he gave. He would say it frequently: اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ حَمْدًا يُوَافِي نِعَمَهُ وَيُكَافِئُ مَزِيدَهُ.

Then comes a supplication the Prophet ﷺ said removes all traces of شِرْك: "I believe in Allah the Magnificent, and I reject everything magical and every object worshipped alongside Allah. I have taken hold of the firmest handle" — which is the Quran, the Sunnah, and the way of Islam — "and this handle will never break as long as you hold onto it. There is no separation, and Allah is..." This is a protective supplication.

The following supplication: the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites it three times receives the رِضْوَان of Allah, because you are saying: رَضِيتُ بِاللَّهِ رَبًّا — "I am content with Allah as my Lord" — وَبِالْإِسْلَامِ دِينًا — "and with Islam as my deen" — وَبِمُحَمَّدٍ — or, in some recitations, بِسَيِّدِنَا — نَبِيًّا وَرَسُولًا — "and with Muhammad ﷺ as my Prophet and Messenger." As for adding Sayyidina: it is not found in the transmitted wording, but adding it out of تَأَدُّبٌ — out of reverence — is permissible, and this is called مُخَارَفَةً تَأَدُّبًا, as has been discussed. Just as Sayyidina 'Ali refused to remove the words "Muhammad Rasulullah" from the treaty of Hudaybiyyah, one may add Sayyidina out of the same spirit of reverence. Both forms exist, but the actual wording transmitted in the warid is بِمُحَمَّدٍ صلى الله عليه وسلم نَبِيًّا وَرَسُولًا..

Then the next supplication: the Prophet ﷺ said to recite it seven times in the morning and seven times in the evening — حَسْبِىَ ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَهَ إِلَّا هُوَ عَلَيْهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ وَهُوَ رَبُّ ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱلْعَظِيمِ — "Allah is enough for me; there is no god but Him. I have placed my trust in Him, and He is the Lord of the magnificent throne." Seven times.

Then: أَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلِّمْ — a concise prayer upon the Prophet ﷺ. We know from hadith that Jibril brought the Prophet ﷺ the glad tidings that whoever sends one prayer upon him receives ten times the رَحْمَة of Allah in return. So the instruction here is to do it ten times — which equals a hundred instances of Allah's رَحْمَة upon you. It is preferable to send prayers upon the Prophet at least a hundred times in the morning and a hundred times in the evening.

Shaykh Muhammad, for an extended period of his life, would do it five thousand times a day as a spiritual practice. For fourteen years he maintained a practice of supplication twice daily, a خَتْم of the Quran once daily, and one hundred and fourteen thousand recitations of بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ every single day. Those who were present said that when he emerged from his خَلْوَة, people would make تَوْبَة simply by seeing him. He lived in Fes at a madrasa, and he had a room there — a room he occupied so long and so deeply that he memorized one of the most demanding books of Maliki فِقْه, a multi-volume work, entirely by heart. He was a great Maliki scholar and a فَقِيهٌ. But people talked — they wondered why he never left that room. They assumed he must be hiding treasure up there, some gold he did not want anyone to find. So they went to his father to persuade him to go up, because he would not allow anyone to enter, and there was a ladder to reach it. His father came up, and he let him in — and when his father entered, he found the word مَوْتٌ — "death" — written across every wall. There was nothing else in the room. His father understood: he realized that some people are in a world entirely their own, and others simply cannot comprehend it. That man was in a completely different realm — اللهُ أَكْبَرُ.

As for the form of the prayer on the Prophet: if you say صلى الله عليه وسلم then the verb is sallama with a fatha. But if you say أَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلِّمْ — like that — then سَلِّمْ carries a kasra, because this is the صِيغَةُ الدُّعَاءِ, the form of supplication. Even though it uses what looks like a past-tense verb, it is actually a supplication — like when you say رَحِمَهُ اللَّهُ, you are saying "may Allah have mercy on him," but you use the past tense تَفَاؤُلًا — as if it has already been accepted. Similarly, رَّضِىَ ٱللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ is said of the صَحَابَة in the past tense, though you could equally say أَللَّهُمَّ ارْضَ عَنْ أَبِي بَكْرٍ وَعُمَرَ وَعُثْمَانَ وَعَلِيٍّ — using the direct form of دُعَاء. So with the direct form — the صِيغَةُ الدُّعَاءِ or صِيغَةُ الْأَمْرِ — you would say وَسَلِّمْ with a kasra. There are various forms one may use; this particular one is shorter and so lends itself to higher volume of repetition. I believe this is the صِيغَة that the Shaykh used.

After that comes a supplication in which the Prophet asked Allah for good surprises and sought refuge from bad ones — فُجَاءٌ being something that happens suddenly and unexpectedly. So you are asking Allah to let only good surprises reach you in your day, and to shield you from the harmful ones.

Now we come to سَيِّدُ الاسْتِغْفَارِ. The Prophet ﷺ said — and it is in the صَحِيح hadith — that this is the سَيِّدُ الاسْتِغْفَارِ, the foremost of all ways of seeking forgiveness, the best form in which a person can ask for it. You say: "O Allah, You are my Lord; there is no god but You. You created me and I am Your servant. I am upon Your طَهَارَةُ الْقَلْبِ — the covenant of Islam — and Your promise to forgive me and admit me into جَنَّة, to the best of my ability." In other words: as best I am able, I am doing what You have commanded me to do. "I seek refuge in You from the evil I have done. I return to You, acknowledging that all my blessings are from You — I am returning them to You, admitting they are Yours entirely. And I take full responsibility for my wrong actions: they are mine, I committed them, I admit them. So forgive me, for none can forgive wrong actions but You." That is this supplication.

Then: "Whatever Allah willed came to be, and whatever He did not will did not come to be" — this is sometimes said in times of مِصْيَبَة, calamity, as an acknowledgement and surrender. "And I know that Allah is powerful over all things, and that Allah encompasses all things in His knowledge."

"I seek refuge from the evil of my own soul and from the evil of every creature that You hold by its forelock." This is a reminder of تَوْحِيد: every creature is in Allah's grip. Their evil reaches you only because Allah — who holds them by the forelock — has permitted it. So when you seek refuge in Allah from the evil of others, Allah will protect you from that evil. There are many hadith that speak to this, and it is a powerful supplication. There is also a hadith of reciting it forty-one times before فَجْر. These two names invoked in it are very powerful names.

"I am seeking through Your mercy, and I am seeking safety from Your punishment — rectify all of my affairs and do not leave me to my own soul, and do not leave me to anyone from Your creation, even for the blink of an eye. Be my sole reliance — do not make me dependent on anyone other than You." This does not mean you will never receive help from others — but you recognize that all help comes from Allah alone. That is the meaning: do not let me think the help is coming from other than You.

Ja'far al-Sadiq, may Allah be pleased with him, was once challenged by an atheist who told him he did not believe in Allah. Ja'far al-Sadiq said to him: "Have you ever been at sea?" The man said yes — and then told him what had happened: he had been on a boat when a violent storm struck, so severe that the vessel was torn to pieces. All the sailors drowned. He alone survived, clinging to a plank of wood. Then the ocean took even the plank from his hands, and he had nothing. Ja'far al-Sadiq said: "What did you do?" He said: "The sea flung me to the shore and I survived." Ja'far al-Sadiq said to him: "When you boarded that boat, you put your trust in the boat and in the sailors. When Allah took those away from you, you put your trust in that little piece of wood. And when you lost the wood — where did you put your trust then? Did you hope to survive?" He said: "Yes, I hoped that I would." Ja'far al-Sadiq said: "Who did you hope in?" Because in Arabic, رَجَاء is a transitive verb — رَجَوْتُ requires an object. You cannot hope in the abstract; hope must have an object. So he pressed him: "From whom were you hoping for safety?" Then he said: "يَا عَبْدَ اللَّهِ — O servant of Allah — the One who took away every means you had, and then saved you despite the absence of every means — that was Allah." That is لَا تَكِلْنِي إِلَى نَفْسِي وَلَا إِلَى أَحَدٍ مِنْ خَلْقِكَ طَرْفَةَ عَيْنٍ — "do not leave me to myself or to any of Your creation for even the blink of an eye." وَلَا أَقَلَّ مِنْ ذَلِكَ — not even less than that.

أَللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ — Sheikh al-Khatri told me that someone once came to him without a job, and he gave him this supplication. Within a few days, that person had found work. It comes from a hadith in which a man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: "يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ, I am crushed by debts and overwhelmed by هَمٌّ and حُزْن." The Prophet gave him this supplication, and the man said he began reciting it and Allah resolved all his debts and lifted his grief. So: أَللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ — "O Allah, I seek refuge in You from هَمٌّ and حُزْن" — وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ — "and I seek refuge in You from عَجْز and كَسَلٌ." هَمّ is anxiety about the future — worry over what has not yet come. حُزْن is grief over the past — sorrow over what has already gone. One concerns the future, the other the past. And Allah says in the Quran: أَلَآ إِنَّ أَوْلِيَآءَ ٱللَّهِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ — the friends of Allah have no fear about the future nor grief about the past. This is about entering the present moment, because there is no past or future for Allah. When you are in a state of awareness of Allah, the past holds no anxiety and the future holds no dread — you cannot change the past, and the future is entirely in Allah's hands. All you have is the present. So there is no point in carrying grief about the past or anxiety about the future.

عَجْز is the condition of someone who has the desire but lacks the ability — إِرَادَة without قُدْرَة. كَسَلٌ is the opposite: having the ability but lacking the will — قُدْرَة without إِرَادَة. Both are states to seek refuge from. You want to be someone who has both: the ability to do what needs to be done, and the will to do what should be done.

وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْجُبْنِ وَالْبُخْلِ — "I seek refuge in You from جُبْنٌ and بُخْل." جُبُنٌ is cowardice — the Arabic word comes from the image of curdled cheese: a coward, when firmness is required, collapses. They lack ثَبَاتٌ, steadiness. You are seeking refuge from the inability to give yourself with شَجَاعَة when it is called for. بُخْلٌ is miserliness — the cowardice of wealth. So جُبْنٌ is the cowardice of the self and body; بُخْل is the cowardice of wealth and property.

And Allah says: إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱشْتَرَىٰ مِنَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ أَنفُسَهُمْ وَأَمْوَلَهُم بِأَنَّ لَهُمُ ٱلْجَنَّةَ — "Allah has purchased from the believers their selves and their wealth, and in exchange is paradise." What He purchased requires جِهَادٌ with both بِأَمْوَالِهِمْ وَأَنْفُسِهِمْ — their wealth and their selves. If you are a coward you cannot give yourself in جِهَادٌ with your body. If you are a miser you cannot give in جِهَادٌ with your wealth. So جُبْنٌ and بُخْل are the two things that prevent you from fulfilling what Allah has purchased from you — which is why seeking refuge from them is so important.
