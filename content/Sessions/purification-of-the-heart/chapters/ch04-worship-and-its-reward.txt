Yes, all right. So what the text says here is that the scholars hold two opinions concerning those who seek some worldly benefit through worship rather than a benefit in the next world. For instance, if a person recites سُورَةُ الْوَاقِعَةِ for the sole reason of protecting themselves from financial hardship — because there is a hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ is reported to have said that whoever recites سُورَةُ الْوَاقِعَةِ every day will not be afflicted with poverty or serious financial calamity in this life. This is well known. Even Ibn Mas'ud, رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ, when asked what inheritance he left his daughters, said: "I left them سُورَةُ الْوَاقِعَةِ." سُورَةُ الْوَاقِعَةِ is understood to protect from poverty when recited daily.

The question the scholars discuss is: what if that protection from poverty is the only reason a person recites it? The hadith indicates the benefit is there for whoever does it daily, but what is the intention behind it? Obviously, there is always some degree of إِيمَان involved when a person does anything the Messenger ﷺ has encouraged. Reading سُورَةُ الْوَاقِعَةِ every day is not like, say, investing in the stock market, where the benefit is tangible and worldly in a straightforward sense. The benefit here comes from the unseen world — it is grounded in إِيمَان.

He also raises the case of someone who worships in order to attain the next world — seeking paradise as the purpose of their عِبَادَة. Imam al-Ūbī al-Mālikī, رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ, said that worshipping for the sake of the next world is جَائِزٌ — permissible — but that it should not be the sole reason driving a person's worship. So it falls under the category of مُبَاح — permitted but not the ideal. That is one opinion.

The people of تَصَوُّف, however, tend to be extremely critical of doing عِبَادَة for any worldly or otherworldly benefit. Their position is that you should perform عِبَادَة solely because اللَّه طَهَارَةُ الْقَلْبِ is worthy of تَقْوَى and worthy of مَغْفِرَة — full stop. No reward expected, no paradise sought, nothing in return. That is the stance of the greatest Sufis. Qadi Abu Bakr ibn al-'Arabi, رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ — who was both a Sufi and a jurist —

He argues that if you read the Qur'an carefully, using your intellect, you will see that اللَّه is actively encouraging you to strive for His garden and to fear His fire. There is a famous saying attributed to Rabi'a al-'Adawiyya: "Go after the Gardener, not the garden." She also said, "O اللَّه, if I worship You for the garden, then put me in the fire." That is a remarkably strong statement — I would not say it myself — but it reflected her state, her مَقَام, her spiritual station. Qadi Abu Bakr ibn al-'Arabi, may اللَّه be pleased with him, along with Sidi Ahmed Zarruk, both maintain that the worship of اللَّه in hope of His garden should in no way be belittled, because the Qur'an itself does not belittle it.

Imam al-Ghazali identifies three types of worshippers. The first worships اللَّه as a free person — purely for His sake, desiring nothing else. There is a hadith — though I believe it is either weak or has been commented upon by scholars — whose meaning is that there are people who would not disobey اللَّه even if there were no fire, simply because اللَّه is not to be disobeyed. He is worthy of تَقْوَى in and of Himself. The second type, as Qadi Abu Bakr describes it, resembles the merchant — the تُجَرُ — asking, "What is in it for me?" This person tallies rewards: if I pray so many رَكْعَةٌ, I receive such-and-such; if I recite so much Qur'an, I earn such-and-such. He desires the garden, and that desire drives his worship. The third type worships like a slave fearing the whip — he acts out of terror of the fire. So you have three categories: those who worship purely for اللَّه's sake, those who worship desiring the garden, and those who worship fearing the fire.

If you look at the Qur'an, there is clearly both تَرْغِيب and تَرْهِيب — both encouragement and warning. اللَّه says: race toward forgiveness from your Lord and a garden — سَابِقُوٓا۟ إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍۢ مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ وَجَنَّةٍ — and elsewhere: فَفِرُّوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ — flee toward اللَّه. And: وَفِى ذَلِكَ فَلْيَتَنَافَسِ ٱلْمُتَنَفِسُونَ — let those who compete, compete for that. The Qur'an contains many verses warning about the fire and many more encouraging good deeds by promising reward. What Qadi Abu Bakr, may اللَّه be pleased with him, is saying is that having طَمَعٌ — a longing — for اللَّه's garden, and fear of His fire, is itself إِخْلَاص. It is sincerity, and there is no taint of impurity in it.

So all he is telling us in this passage is that there is a genuine difference of opinion here. If you perform worship for the sake of the next world, some will take issue with that — and those tend to be the صُوفِيَّة. The فُقَهَاء, scholars like Qadi Abu Bakr, Imam Ruyani, and others, consider that position extreme. They hold that worshipping اللَّه with hope for His garden is إِخْلَاص, because اللَّه Himself affirms it in the Qur'an. That is, إِن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ, the stronger opinion.

The next point he raises is equally important: some scholars also consider seeking the pleasure of worship itself to be a concern. And this is something that people who worship seriously over a sustained period genuinely discover — that there is a pleasure in عِبَادَة that surpasses other pleasures people ordinarily chase. One of the great Hanafi scholars said: "If kings knew the pleasure we are in, they would send their armies with swords to take it from us." He was saying that the pleasure of عِبَادَة, of عِلْمٌ, of worshipping اللَّه is so profound that a king, with all his palaces and all his worldly wealth, would fight to possess it if he knew what we had. So some scholars — especially the عَارِفٍ and the people of تَصَوُّف — warn that this pleasure can become so intense that a person begins performing عِبَادَة in order to obtain that feeling. He prays the night prayer because of the pleasure it brings.

Some scholars caution against this. But Ahmad ibn al-Hawari — or one of the great early scholars, I am working to recall exactly — said that were it not for these gifts, most people would struggle to sustain their practice consistently. اللَّه has granted that pleasure as a reward for regular, committed worship, and it is a blessing that should not be diminished. He is simply laying out the range of scholarly opinion on this question.

Then he says: as for the one who likes people to see him doing good deeds — meaning he performs the deed for اللَّه, but enjoys the fact that others happen to learn of it — that is considered رِيَاءٌ according to some scholars, and not رِيَاءٌ according to others. The basis for the second view is the Qur'anic reference to those who love to be praised for what they have not done. Some scholars drew from that verse the conclusion that it is natural — part of human nature itself — for a person to enjoy recognition for genuinely good deeds. The problem arises when that recognition becomes the reason for doing them. There is a distinction: if you did the deed for that reason, that is unambiguously رِيَاءٌ. But if that was not your reason, yet you find yourself pleased that people acknowledge what you did, some scholars say that is not رِيَاءٌ.

One scholar said: whoever loves to do something and to be known and remembered for it has committed شِرْك with اللَّه, Exalted and Glorified. Imam al-Jazuli — a great Maliki فَقِيهٌ from the Maghrib and the teacher of Sidi Ahmed Zarruk — added that رِيَاءٌ can occur even when no one is present. For example: you are reading and come across a rare, obscure point that few people know, and you think, "This would make a strong impression in a gathering" — so you write it down and memorize it to produce it later. He says that is رِيَاءٌ. Full stop. It is a serious problem.

If your intention was to benefit others, of course, that is not رِيَاءٌ. But what Imam al-Jazuli was describing is the person who thinks, "This will really get them," or "They will be impressed by this" — which is an obvious trap for people of scholarship in particular. When scholars gather, there is a kind of expectation of مُذَاكَرَة and مُشَارَكَة — mutual discussion and exchange — and that can quietly slide into رِيَاءٌ. Sakhr ibn 'Abdillah, a great early scholar and مُحَدِّث among other things, said: "Whoever desires people to know what is between him and his Lord is in غَفْلَة." In other words, it is no one's business but yours and your Lord's. You should not want others to know — unless he sees that people have abandoned good deeds and performs them publicly as نَصِيحَة, as sincere counsel and example.

So, for instance, Ibn 'Umar, may اللَّه be pleased with him, and Ibn Mas'ud were known to go to the masjid before Fajr to perform تَهَجُّد. They could have done this at home, but they did it there so that the تَابِعِين would see them and follow their example. Their intention was not self-promotion but نَصِيحَة to the Muslims.

Then he says — and النَّجْم here refers to Imam Malik, may اللَّه be pleased with him, who is called النَّجْم because his student, later the independent mujtahid Imam al-Shafi'i, may اللَّه be pleased with him, said: "When the scholars are mentioned, Malik is the star" — إِذَا ذُكِرَ الْعُلَمَاءُ فَمَالِكٌ النَّجْمُ.. From that, Imam Malik became known as النَّجْم. It is one of his titles, his أَلْقَابٌ. He also carries the title عَالِمُ الْمَدِينَةِ, just as Imam al-Shafi'i is called عَالِمُ قُرَيْشٍ and Abu Hanifa, may اللَّه be pleased with him, is often called الْإِمَامُ الْأَعْظَمُ — the greatest imam. These are titles bestowed upon them by later generations.

Imam Malik did not consider it harmful, as long as the original intention was grounded in sincerity. This position traces back to a question once put to Imam Malik about a man who set out for the masjid for the sake of اللَّه — his intention was clear: to pray in congregation. But on the way, a thought crept in: "I hope someone sees me going to the masjid." Imam Malik said: "لَا أَرَى بَأْسًا" — I see no harm in that — "إِذَا كَانَ السَّرَائِلُ إِخْلَاصٌ." He does not see it as harmful if the man began with إِخْلَاص and does not follow through on that inner whisper — if he repels it, says "أَعُوذُ بِاللهِ," and does not entertain it. That is an important distinction to hold.

Then he says: وَأَمَرٌ عَلَى الرِّيَاءِ أَنْ أَفْعَلَهُ مِنْ تَرْكِهِ لِخَوْفِهِ وَأَفْضَلُ ذِكْرُ اللِّسَانِ فَارِغُ الْجَنَانِ أَرَاقِبُ الْقَلْبِ وَاللِّسَانِ.. This is a crucial point. He is saying that to abandon an action out of fear of رِيَاءٌ is actually worse than performing it with رِيَاءٌ. Imagine you walk into the masjid and want to pray a few رَكْعَةٌ, but then you think: "Those people might think I am showing off," or "Maybe I am only doing this so they will think well of me." That inner concern is called خَوْفُ الرِّيَاءِ — fear of رِيَاءٌ. If that fear causes you to leave the action, it is itself رِيَاءٌ. Why? Because the reason you abandoned the act was the people. You were preoccupied with them, not with اللَّه. Leaving an act out of concern for what people think is as much a form of رِيَاءٌ as performing it for their eyes — in both cases, the people are at the centre, not اللَّه. That is why the scholars say it is better to perform the action even when you sense a trace of رِيَاءٌ in yourself.

This does not mean, however, that you should be careless. If the act of worship is something you can perform privately, it is always better to do so — precisely because of the danger of رِيَاءٌ. But abandoning it out of fear of رِيَاءٌ is worse than doing it despite that fear. The same principle applies to ذِكْرٌ. Scholars say it is better for your tongue to make ذِكْرٌ while your heart is heedless than for both your tongue and your heart to be heedless together. If you keep the tongue moving, it may reach the heart — because, as they say, a man's heart lies beneath his tongue. And interestingly, in Chinese medicine, their physiology affirms a direct connection between the tongue and the heart: what the tongue does actually affects the heart, and even the physical movement of the tongue has an impact on it.

One more thing worth knowing: the word رِيَاءٌ is derived from the root of رُؤْيَا — seeing. And it is not about others seeing you. It is about you seeing them. The fault lies in your gaze toward the creation rather than toward اللَّه, Glorified and Exalted. You are the one looking at them; that is where the رِيَاءٌ lives.

One of the many profound things about the Day of Resurrection is that 'A'isha, may اللَّه be pleased with her, when told by the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that everyone would be raised naked, asked whether people would be looking at one another. He replied: "It will be far greater than that, 'A'isha." People will be in such a state of awe that they will not even be aware of those around them. And this is the very point of worshipping for the sake of اللَّه alone. When worship is truly for Him, you carry a knowledge of اللَّه that renders everyone else insignificant by comparison. When you truly contemplate what you are worshipping — consider that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, once saw Jibril in his true form, and Jibril filled the entire horizon. One angel. The entire horizon. Imagine looking out across that vast expanse and seeing a single being filling all of it. And that is Jibril. When you begin to grasp that what you worship is infinite, the thought of other human beings — equally helpless, equally needy, in the same predicament as yourself — loses its grip. They can do nothing for you. No benefit, no harm, nothing. That is the bottom line.

That is why he says: fear of other than اللَّه and desire for other than اللَّه are incompatible with تَوَكُّل. اللَّه says: وَعَلَى ٱللَّهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلُونَ — upon اللَّه let those who trust, place their trust. If you are preoccupied with other people, you do not have تَوَكُّل. And if you lack تَوَكُّل, the reason is an absence of يَقِين — certainty. The يَقِين you are missing is this: that all good comes from اللَّه, and that no harm can befall you except by His will. That is what تَوْحِيد is teaching us — to be utterly certain that only اللَّه can harm you or benefit you, and for that reason, fear of human beings dissolves.

This does not mean becoming reckless or impetuous — driving like a madman and saying, "It is all written by اللَّه." That is not تَوَكُّل. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told the Bedouin: "Tie your camel, then put your trust in اللَّه."

Then he says: the root of every disease is a lack of certainty. What is prohibited of these two — fear and desire directed at the creation — is specifically that which prevents an obligation from being fulfilled. If it leads instead to neglecting something recommended, it is considered reprehensible. In either case, flee to your Lord from both. Do not desire the creation and do not fear the creation. And the cure for both, he reminds us, is to know with certainty that no one harms or benefits except اللَّه.

He then moves to the next disease, which is displeasure with the divine decree — and this will sound very familiar to many people. This disease occurs when a person resists what اللَّه, Majestic and Exalted, has decreed — for instance, saying, "I did not warrant this happening to me," or "I do not deserve this." And that is a remarkable thing to hear, because you hear it from people all the time.

I don't deserve this. Why is this happening to me? Or, as he puts it next: what did I do to deserve this suffering? What wrong did I do to deserve this? The point is that everything comes from اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. This is why Imam Abu al-Hasan said that people possess a quality they are entirely unaware of, and it destroys all of their good deeds. Very few people actually recognize it: being displeased with the divine decree. They do not realize they are showing displeasure toward what اللَّه has ordained. اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى says that they were displeased with what اللَّه had given them, and so اللَّه removed all of their good deeds. By contrast, the people of تَقْوَى, when asked what their Lord has sent down, say: "Good — it is all good." That is their answer. Everything is good. Nothing is evil. There is a reason, a lesson, a wisdom in it. This is the difference between the people of اللَّه and everyone else.

The people of اللَّه recognize that this world is a place of purification — a مَبَرَّة in itself. We were sent here to purify ourselves. The very purpose of our existence is to purify ourselves, to draw near to اللَّه, and to worship اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى. That is it. Once you recognize that, everything becomes an opportunity to learn, to grow, and to draw closer to اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى — everything.

Because there are only four possible states a human being can be in. Only four — no fifth. If you can name a fifth, that would be remarkable. According to أَبُو الْعَبَّاسِ الْمُرْسِيُّ, you are either in نِعْمَةٌ, or in بَلِيَّةٌ, or in طَاعَةٌ, or in مَعْصِيَة. That is it. You are either in a blessing from اللَّه, or a tribulation from اللَّه, in obedience to اللَّه, or in disobedience. That is the full scope.

Each one of these states calls for a specific response. اللَّه wants something from you in each case. If اللَّه gives you a نِعْمَةٌ — what response does He desire? شُكْر. Gratitude. That is the response. And what is gratitude according to the شَرِيعَة? It is to use the نِعْمَةٌ in what is acceptable to اللَّه — whether مُبَاح, مَنْدُوبٌ, or وَاجِب. To use it in something مَكْرُوه does not quite constitute gratitude, but neither is it ingratitude, since it is not حَرَامٌ. Look at those five categories: only one carries punishment. The other four either earn reward or nothing. So: شُكْر.

He says later in the مَتْن that شُكْر is to utilize what اللَّه has given you in what pleases اللَّه. That is شُكْر. And so you have شُكْرُ اللِّسَانِ, شُكْرُ الْجَنَانِ, شُكْرُ اللِّسَانِ, شُكْرُ الْبَنَانِ — gratitude of the tongue, the heart, and the limbs. Part of شُكْر is to thank people, to tell others what someone did for you. That is actually considered part of شُكْر — to say, so-and-so benefited me in this or that. إِظْهَارُ النِّعْمَةِ, showing the blessing openly, is part of شُكْر. اللَّه loves that. To feel the blessing in your heart, and to act with those blessings in a way that pleases اللَّه.

The next state is tribulation — بَلِيَّةٌ. What does اللَّه expect from us? صَبْر. صَبْر. صَبْر. If He gives you a tribulation, He expects صَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ. Though in truth, you are not even required to have صَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ in its fullest sense. صَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ is when you do not complain to anyone at all. You can complain — and we will get into that in a moment — but complaining to others is not ideal; it just gives you a degree of release.

Then the next state: طَاعَةٌ — obedience. What is the response? شُهُودُ الْمِنَّةِ — recognizing the gift. The danger is not to become arrogant. If you find yourself in obedience, you must recognize that it is from the تَوْفِيق of اللَّه that you are there. Do not start thinking you are better than others because you are in طَاعَةٌ while they are in مَعْصِيَة. That is the danger of obedience — and in a deeper sense, the inherent danger of religion itself. The religious person begins to see himself as better than the irreligious person. That is شَيْطَان opening. Once he has you there, it is rope-a-dope — like Muhammad Ali used to do. He has you until the end of the fight, because كِبْرٌ is his game. That is شَيْطَان. Once he has you in تَكَبُّر, in تَكَبُّرٌ عَلَى النَّاسِ, he has you.

So that is the danger of obedience. What اللَّه expects when you are obedient is that you recognize it as a gift from Him. Recognize that just as He made you obedient, He could just as easily have given the situation of the person who is disobeying Him to you instead, and given them the نِعْمَةٌ. The Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم said: do not find fault in others, because if you seek out their faults, اللَّه will remove those faults from them and place them in you. So there should be a sense of fear — a fear when you see someone in بَلِيَّةٌ. That person is in بَلِيَّةٌ and he needs to change. And if someone is in مَعْصِيَة, that too is a بَلِيَّةٌ, because طَاعَةٌ is a نِعْمَةٌ, and they go together. He is in a بَلِيَّةٌ. This is why Imam Malik relates in the مُوَطَّأ that عِيسَى عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ said — and Imam Malik frames it as something he heard reported — "بَلَغَنِي أَنَّ عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ قَالَ," meaning: I have just heard, so it is a narration, though he evidently thought it sound enough to include in his مُوَطَّأ. He said that عِيسَى عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ was reported to have said that there are only two kinds of people: those in tribulation and those in نِعْمَةٌ. And he said: do not look upon the people of tribulation as though you are lords over them. Look upon them with compassion, as servants would look upon them.

That is the point. When you see people in tribulation, do not say to yourself, "I am better than these people." Say rather: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ, they are in tribulation, and الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي عَافَانِي. That is the دُعَاء of the Prophet. When he saw people in tribulation, he said: الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي عَافَانِي مِمَّا ابْتَلَاهُ بِهِ وَفَضَّلَنِي عَلَى كَثِيرٍ مِمَّنْ خَلَقَ تَفْضِيلًا — praise be to the One who granted me well-being in a way that spared me from the tribulation He gave to these people, and who favored me over so much of His creation. That is where you should stand when it comes to obedience.

Now in مَعْصِيَة — what is expected of you? تَوْبَة. You must make تَوْبَةٌ. And if you look at this and realize: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ, every single thing that happens to me will be one of these four things, and for each one there is a response that will please اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى — then if I please اللَّه, the thing that happened to me was in fact a نِعْمَةٌ, because it brought me closer to اللَّه, and that is the whole point. Perhaps you dislike a thing and there is much good in it for you. Perhaps you love a thing and there is much harm in it for you. اللَّه knows and you do not. So just admit you do not know — and then comes الحمد لله على الصحة والعافية الحمد لله على الإيمان. Ibn 'Abbas said: if a tribulation befalls you, it is itself a نِعْمَةٌ in that it could have been worse. So you must thank اللَّه. اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ — it is not worse than it could be.

And the best model is to look at the سِيرَة of the Messenger of اللَّه صلى الله عليه وسلم — to see how he lived, and to recognize that no one endured greater tribulation. He buried all of his children except فَاطِمَة. How many people will ever face that? Out of six children, to bury five? He lost the people closest to him. His most beloved uncle was slaughtered on the battlefield. The beloved wife with whom he had shared years of a blissful marriage died in his most difficult year. Abu Talib, the uncle who had raised him — he had been raised an orphan. There came a time when he had no supporters. The very people who had honored him were calling him a madman, saying he was مَجْنُونٌ. How many people experience that — where suddenly your friends, your community, everyone around you calls you مَجْنُونٌ and spits on you? He endured sanctions that most people could not bear to even imagine, and that was just one stretch of his life. All of those tribulations — and yet we have no record, not a single word, of complaint. Twenty-three years. No complaints. اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ. That is all he ever said. اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ.

So that is the situation. To be angry at the قَدَرْ is to be in a state of غَفْلَة — in heedlessness, pure and simple.

Now, here is another important point. Imam al-Qarafi, رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ, wrote a book dedicated entirely to distinguishing things in the شَرِيعَة that appear similar but are not. One of the distinctions he draws is between الرِّضَا بِالْقَضَاءِ وَالْمَقْضِيِّ بِهِ — being pleased with the divine decree itself versus being pleased with the thing that is decreed. When looking at قَضَاءٌ, you have two components. There is the قَضَاءٌ itself, and separately the قَدَرْ — and there is some difference of opinion around these two terms. The Maturidis and the Ash'aris actually hold opposite positions on which is which, but the point stands regardless of which label you assign. In one reading, قَضَاءٌ means that اللَّه has decided to bring something about, and قَدَرْ means that He has measured and enabled the means by which it comes to be. The مَقْدِعٌ بِهِ is the thing that actually occurs.

Now — should we be pleased with everything that happens, the مَقْدِعٌ بِهِ? Why not? Because bad things happen, and do not forget that within قَدَرُ اللَّهِ is both خَيْر and شَرٌّ. You should not be pleased with شَرٌّ, because اللَّه Himself is not pleased with it. اللَّه has decreed that there will be evil in the world He created, but He is not pleased with it — meaning He does not reward it; that is what His رِضْوَان is. He does not reward it. So when you see مَعْصِيَة, you should not like it or be pleased with it. اللَّه does not want you to be pleased with it. But you should not be displeased with the fact that اللَّه created a world in which such things exist. Do you see the distinction? One thing is refusing to be displeased with اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى and the world He created — because it is His world and He made it. The other is recognizing that within this world, اللَّه has placed things as a test and challenge for believers, and those are the things you should not be pleased with, because He does not want you to be pleased with them. If you were pleased with them, you would do nothing to change them. Your displeasure with them is precisely what compels you to go out and transform what is wrong in society.

That is the distinction. Imam al-Qarafi offers an analogy — just for تَقْرِيبُ الْمَعَانِي, to bring the concept closer. He says: imagine you have a doctor, and you go to the doctor, and the doctor says you have gangrene — he has to cut your arm off. So he does. One day that doctor is walking past and overhears you talking to a friend, your arm gone, saying: "I went to this incompetent doctor. I should have gotten a second opinion. He had no business doing this to me." The doctor hearing that is going to be hurt and upset — because he did not want to cause you pain; he wanted to help you, and the amputation was how he helped you. Now, if instead the doctor walks past and hears you say to someone: "I went to this remarkable doctor. He cut off my arm, and it has caused me enormous pain — I cannot sleep at night. It has been the most severe treatment I have ever been through" — when the doctor hears that, he feels compassion, not offense. Because you said nothing against him. All you said is that the treatment was grueling.

And this is the analogy for how we speak about what اللَّه does to us in this world. If you say, "اللَّه is really testing me hard right now — it is difficult, but مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ, there is wisdom in it" — that will not make اللَّه angry. It is better not to broadcast your troubles to others, and doing so does diminish your patience. But there is a world of difference between saying "I don't deserve this — why is this happening to me?" and saying "this is so hard to bear." The first is a complaint against what اللَّه has done. The second is an expression of the difficulty you are having in enduring it. That distinction matters enormously.

He also makes this point: we were never commanded by اللَّه to enjoy hardship and tribulation. Nowhere in the Quran does اللَّه tell you to take pleasure in horrible things that happen to you. That is not how He made us, and there is no تَكْلِيف in that. But اللهُ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى does censure certain people in the Quran when He says that He sent them a punishment, yet it did not humble them and did not make them call out to Him to lift it from them. You see this today — things worsen year after year, and people simply throw up their hands. Muslim countries — look at what happened to Kuwait. You would think they would all be rising every night for تَهَجُّد, beseeching اللَّه to never let that happen again. But things return to how they were. There is no اِسْتِكَانَة, no genuine عُبُودِيَّة before اللَّه. Tribulations come, and if they do not change people, that is a deeply troubling state to be in.

Then he moves to the next disease: سُمْعَة. Sum'ah is one of the diseases of the heart. The clearest way to understand it is that سُمْعَة is identical to رِيَاءٌ, except that رِيَاءٌ pertains to what the eyes perceive, while سُمْعَة pertains to what the ears hear. In other words, سُمْعَة is wanting other people to hear about you — needing to tell them what you have done. "You won't believe it — I gave ten thousand dollars to such-and-such charity." You want to tell people about your good deeds. That is سُمْعَة. And the Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم said: مَنْ سَمَّعَ سَمَّعَ اللَّهُ بِهِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ — whoever does this, whoever broadcasts their deeds to people, اللَّه will broadcast to all people on the Day of Judgment what they really did. Because everyone has their skeletons. كُلُّكُمْ خَطَّاءٌ. All of you sin. وَخَيْرُ الْخَطَّائِينَ التَّوَّابُونَ. Everyone falls short. So if you go around advertising your good deeds, اللَّه says He will advertise your bad deeds on the Day of Judgment — and you do not want that. So say nothing.
