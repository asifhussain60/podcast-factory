This book is a series of spoken sessions, delivered aloud to an audience and afterward set down as prose. It takes the opening chapter of the Quran, Surah Al-Fateha, and reads it slowly, almost phrase by phrase, drawing out of each word far more than a first hearing would suggest is there.

The series belongs to a larger conversation about knowing one's master. It begins where the speaker says understanding must begin: not with theology but with friendship, and from friendship with love, because the name Allah reaches back to a root the sessions approach through the Quranic idea of love. From there the talks move outward — the linguistic meaning of Allah, of Rabb, of REHMA, and the striking image of the womb as the source of mercy. They ask what worship truly is, what it means that Allah is the Lord of all the worlds, and how being differs from attribute. They turn to the Day of Judgment, to the motives that move a person toward piety, and at last to the two petitions the surah teaches us to make: guidance to the straight path, and the steadfastness to remain upon it.

The speaker is the author of these sessions; he gave them, and they carry his voice throughout. What the reader receives is a patient, close reading meant to make a familiar prayer new again.
