# The Heavenly Stable and the Great Sage Equal to Heaven

*The Great White Planet of Venus brings Sun Wukong up out of the Water-Curtain Cave and through the Southern Gate of Heaven to the throne of the Jade Emperor. Wukong, who knows no court rites, answers the throne with "That'd be old Monkey here," and is pardoned for his ignorance. The civil and military offices being all full, he is given the only vacancy — the Imperial Stable — and is made a BanMaWen, a Keeper of the Horses. For half a month he tends the thousand heavenly horses sleek and fat, until at a stable-officers' banquet he learns that the post has no rank at all: it is the lowest of the low, an "unranked" office. He overturns the desk, fights his way out of the Southern Gate, and returns to the Mountain of Flowers and Fruit. There two One-Horned Demon Kings present him with a russet-yellow robe and prompt him to take the title GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN, which is raised on a banner at the cave-mouth. The Jade Emperor dispatches the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King Li Jing and the third Prince Nezha to subdue him; the vanguard Mighty Magic Spirit is beaten back with his ax-handle broken in two, and Nezha, transforming into three heads and six arms, is wounded in the left arm when Wukong matches the transformation and slips a hair-double behind him in the press. The Heavenly King retreats. The Great White Planet steps forward again with an elegant pivot — let the title stand, but make it an empty office: no affairs, no salary, only a name to settle his wayward heart. The Jade Emperor agrees, and Wukong is invested as Great Sage Equal to Heaven, installed in a mansion beside the Garden of Immortal Peaches with two bureaus and immortal clerks, given two bottles of imperial wine and ten golden flowers, and charged on no account to behave wildly.*

## Through the Southern Gate

The Great White Planet Star and the Handsome Monkey King came out together from the deepest recesses of the cave-heaven and rose into the air on a single cloud. Now Wukong's somersault cloud was unlike any other — wondrously swift — so that he left the Planet Star trailing far behind and arrived first outside the Southern Gate of Heaven. Just as he was about to gather in his cloud and press forward, the Heavenly King Virūḍhaka, at the head of his mighty heavenly soldiers — Pang, Liu, Gou, Bi, Deng, Xin, Zhang, and Tao — with spears, swords, sabers, and halberds, blocked the gate of Heaven and would not let him pass.

*This old Planet Star is a treacherous fellow!* cried the Monkey King. *He invited me, old Monkey, himself — so why has he set men to brandishing blade and spear and barring the road?*

While he was still bawling, the Planet Star arrived in a flash. Wukong rounded on him at once, fierce-faced. *You old fellow — how dare you cozen me? You told me you came at the Jade Emperor's command to summon me to amnesty and office. So why have these men blocked the gate of Heaven and refused to let old Monkey in?*

The Planet Star laughed. *Calm your anger, Great King. You have never before come to this celestial palace; your name is not yet registered, and these heavenly soldiers have never made your acquaintance. How could they let you barge in as you pleased? Once you have seen the Celestial Venerable, received your immortal scroll, and had your office recorded, you may come and go thereafter as you wish — and who would dare block you then?*

*In that case,* said Wukong, *never mind — I won't go in.*

But the Planet Star caught hold of him by the hand. *You shall go in together with me.*

As they neared the gate of Heaven, the Planet Star called out in a loud voice: *Gatekeepers and heavenly generals, officers and soldiers great and small — clear the way! This is an immortal from the lower world. I bear the Jade Emperor's sacred decree and have summoned him hither.* Only then did Heavenly King Virūḍhaka and his soldiers draw back their weapons and stand aside. Now at last the Monkey King believed the old man's words, and step by step he passed within alongside the Planet Star, taking in all he saw. And truly it was a sight to behold:

> First treading the upper realm,
> at last entering Heaven's hall.
> Ten thousand strands of golden light come rolling in red rainbow,
> a thousand wisps of auspicious vapor breathe forth a purple mist.
> See the Southern Gate of Heaven:
> deep-shining azure, wrought all of lapis glaze;

> bearing saber, leaning on sword.

## "That'd be old Monkey here"

The Great White Planet Star led the Handsome Monkey King to the outer reaches of the Numinous Empyrean Hall and, without waiting for the summons to be announced, went straight up before the throne and bowed in homage. Wukong stood stiffly to one side, offering no obeisance whatever, but cocked his ear to listen as the Planet Star made his report.

*Your servant, in obedience to the sacred decree,* the Planet Star announced, *has summoned the demon-immortal, and here he is.*

The Jade Emperor lowered the screen and asked, *Which is the demon-immortal?*

Only then did Wukong bow from the waist and answer, *That'd be old Monkey here.*

The noble immortals all went pale with astonishment. *This wild ape! Why does he not prostrate himself and pay homage, but dares to answer so — "That'd be old Monkey here"? He deserves to die! He deserves to die!*

But the Jade Emperor handed down his command. *This Sun Wukong is a demon-immortal of the lower world, only newly come into human form; he does not know the rites of court. Let his offense be pardoned for now.*

The noble immortals all cried, *We thank you for your grace.* And the Monkey King, at last, made a great long bow toward the throne.

The Jade Emperor summoned the immortal officers of civil and military appointments to find whatever post stood vacant, that Sun Wukong might be installed in it. From the ranks the Star Lord of Wuqu stepped forward and reported, *In all the palaces and halls of the heavenly court, in every quarter and every region, there is no shortage of officers — save that the Imperial Stable lacks a chief to manage its affairs.*

The Jade Emperor handed down his command. *Then let him be made a BanMaWen — a Keeper of the Horses.* The assembled ministers cried their thanks, and he too merely made a great long bow toward the throne. The Jade Emperor then dispatched the Star Officer of the Wood Virtue to escort him to the Imperial Stable to take up his post.

## A thousand heavenly horses

The Monkey King went off cheerfully with the Star Officer of the Wood Virtue and arrived directly at his new station; the matter settled, the Star Officer returned to his palace. There in the stable Wukong assembled the deputy directors, the assistant directors, the registrar, the grooms, and all the officers and men great and small, examined the affairs of the office, and found that there were just a thousand heavenly horses in his care. They were:

> Hualiu and Qiji, Lu'er and Xianli;
> Longmei and Ziyan, the wing-hung Sushuang;
> Jueti and Yinhua, Yaoniao and Feihuang;
> Taotu the feather-turner, Chitu that outraces light;
> Yuhui and Mijing, mist-leaping Shenghuang;
> Wind-chaser, Earth-render, Skyward-flying steeds;
> swift as the red lightning, the Bronze Sparrow that floats on cloud;
> the dappled, the dragon-marked, the dust-leaving Zilin —
> the great steeds of Dayuan from the four corners of the earth,
> the eight chargers and the nine fleet ones,
> coursers that leave the herd a thousand li behind.
> Such fine horses, each and every one,
> neigh at the wind and chase the lightning, strong of spirit;
> tread the mist and mount the clouds, long of strength.

The Monkey King examined the ledgers and checked the count of the horses. Within his office, the registrar managed the requisition and provision of fodder; the groom-officers managed the currying and washing of the horses, the cutting of grass, the watering, and the boiling of feed; the deputy and assistant directors helped urge the work along. The Keeper of the Horses neither slept by day nor by night, but tended his charges without rest. By daylight there was sport enough; but at night he watched over them with tireless diligence — rousing any horse that lay sleeping to feed it grass, catching back any that strayed and tying it fast to the trough. The heavenly horses, when they saw him, laid back their ears and drew in their hooves, so well-kept that every one grew sleek and fat. A good half-month slipped by without his marking it.

## An office without a grade

One day, at leisure, all the officers of the stable arranged a banquet — partly to welcome him, partly to congratulate him. In the midst of the merry drinking, the Monkey King suddenly set down his cup and asked, *This BanMaWen of mine — what rank of office is it?*

*The title is just that — Keeper of the Horses,* they said.

*And what grade does this office hold?* he asked again.

*It has no grade at all,* they said.

*No grade?* said the Monkey King. *I suppose that means it is exalted beyond all measure.*

*Not great, not great,* they said. *It is merely called "unranked."*

*What do you mean by "unranked"?* asked the Monkey King.

*The very bottom,* they said. *An office like this is the lowest and smallest of all — fit only for looking after horses. Even after Your Honor took up the post and tended so diligently that the horses grew fat, all you'll earn is a single word of praise. Should they fall the least bit thin and weak, you'll be called to account; and should they suffer any serious harm, you'll be fined, made to pay restitution, and charged with the crime.*

When the Monkey King heard this, fire flared up in his heart, and he ground his teeth in great fury. *So this is how they slight old Monkey! On my Flower-Fruit Mountain I am hailed as king and patriarch — and they've gulled me into coming here to tend their horses for them! Horse-tending is the base drudgery of low-born striplings — is that any way to treat the likes of me? I'll not do it, I'll not do it! I'm off!*

With a clatter he overturned the official desk, drew his treasure from his ear, gave it a shake until it was as thick as a rice-bowl, and with a flourish of fighting moves battled his way clear out of the Imperial Stable and straight to the Southern Gate of Heaven. The heavenly soldiers, knowing he had received an immortal scroll and held the post of Keeper of the Horses, dared not stop him, and let him fight his way out through the gate.

## Homecoming, and the dozen years that were half a month

In a moment he pressed down the head of his cloud and returned to Flower-Fruit Mountain. There he saw his Four Mighty Generals and the demon kings of the various caves drilling their troops. The Monkey King called out in a ringing voice, *Little ones — old Monkey is back!* The whole crowd of monkeys came rushing to kowtow before him and welcomed him into the deep recesses of the cave-heaven, urging him to ascend his treasure-seat, while on one side they laid out wine to celebrate his homecoming.

*Congratulations, Great King!* they all cried. *You went up to the upper realm for a dozen years or more — surely you've come home in triumph and high content?*

*I've been gone scarcely a half-month,* said the Monkey King. *How could it be a dozen years?*

*Great King,* said the monkeys, *up in Heaven one takes no notice of the passing hours. One day in Heaven is a whole year in the world below. May we ask what office the Great King held?*

The Monkey King waved his hand dismissively. *Don't speak of it, don't speak of it — it shames a fellow to death! That Jade Emperor doesn't know how to use a man. He looked old Monkey over and made me some sort of "BanMaWen" — which turns out to be a horse-keeper, the kind of post that doesn't even rank in the grades. When I first took it up I didn't know any better, and just amused myself about the Imperial Stable. But today I asked my colleagues, and only then learned how base and lowly it was. My heart was sorely vexed — I overturned the banquet table, refused the title, and so came back down here.*

*Welcome, welcome!* cried the monkeys. *Great King, being king in this blessed land and cave-heaven of ours is honor and pleasure beyond measure — why should you go and play groom for him?* And they ordered the little ones to fetch wine quickly, to dispel the Great King's gloom.

## The russet-yellow robe and the four great words

In the very midst of this merry drinking, someone came in to report: *Great King, outside the gate are two One-Horned Demon Kings who wish to see Your Majesty.*

*Show them in,* said the Monkey King.

The Demon Kings straightened their robes, ran into the cave, threw themselves down, and bowed. *What is your business with me?* the Handsome Monkey King asked them.

*Long have we heard that the Great King seeks out men of worth,* said the Demon Kings, *but we found no way to gain an audience. Now that we see the Great King has received the heavenly scroll and come home in triumph, we have brought a robe of russet-yellow as a gift to mark the occasion. If you will not scorn us as base and mean, but take us into your service, we too may render you the labor of dog and horse.*

The Monkey King was overjoyed and put on the russet-yellow robe at once. The others gladly fell into ranks to do him homage, and he appointed the Demon Kings then and there to be Vanguard Commanders of the Front Division. The Demon Kings gave their thanks, then spoke again: *The Great King was in Heaven a long while — what office were you granted?*

*The Jade Emperor makes light of the worthy,* said the Monkey King, *and named me some sort of "BanMaWen."*

When the Demon Kings heard this, they came forward once more. *With supernatural powers such as yours, Great King, why should you tend his horses? Why not proclaim yourself "Great Sage Equal to Heaven"? What is there to stop you?*

When the Monkey King heard this, his delight knew no bounds. *Good! Good! Good!* he cried, again and again. He ordered the Four Mighty Generals: *Quickly set up a banner for me, and on the banner write the four great words "GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN." Raise the pole and hang it out. From this day forward, call me only the Great Sage Equal to Heaven, and never again "Great King." Let word of this go out to the demon kings of all the caves as well, that all may know.* But of this no more for now.

## Two memorials reach the throne

Now the Jade Emperor held court the following day, and there came Heavenly Master Zhang, leading the deputy and assistant directors of the Imperial Stable, who bowed at the foot of the vermilion steps and reported: *Your Majesty, the newly appointed BanMaWen, Sun Wukong, disdaining the smallness of his office, fled the heavenly palace yesterday in revolt.* Even as they spoke, there came also from outside the Southern Gate the Heavenly King Virūḍhaka with his soldiers, who likewise reported: *The BanMaWen, for reasons unknown, has fled out through the gate of Heaven.*

When the Jade Emperor heard this, he straightway handed down his command: *Let the spirits of both reports return each to his proper duty. We shall dispatch heavenly soldiers to capture this monster.* From the ranks stepped forth the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King Li and his third son, the Prince Nezha, who came forward and memorialized: *Your Majesty, though your servants are without talent, we beg the decree to subdue this monster.* The Jade Emperor was greatly pleased, and at once made the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King Li Jing the Demon-Quelling Grand Marshal, and the third Prince Nezha the Great Spirit of the Three Altars and the Ocean Assembly, bidding them raise their host and descend to the lower world without delay.

Heavenly King Li and Nezha kowtowed their thanks, took their leave, and went straight to their own palace, where they mustered their three armies and their captains. They named the Mighty Magic Spirit as vanguard, the Fish-Belly General as rearguard, and the Yaksha General to urge the troops along. In an instant they passed out through the Southern Gate and came straight to Flower-Fruit Mountain. Choosing a level open place, they pitched their camp, and the order went forth for the Mighty Magic Spirit to issue the challenge.

## The Mighty Magic Spirit and the patterned ax

Receiving the command, the Mighty Magic Spirit girt himself in good order, swung up his patterned battle-ax, and came to the mouth of the Water-Curtain Cave. There outside the little cave-gate were many demons and monsters — all manner of wolves and serpents, tigers and leopards — in jagged ranks, brandishing spears and whirling swords, leaping and snarling in combat-play. The Mighty Magic Spirit shouted: *You cursed beasts! Hurry and report to your BanMaWen: I am a great general of the upper heavens, come by the Jade Emperor's decree to subdue him. Tell him to come out at once and submit, lest you all be hacked to pieces.*

The monsters went scrambling and stumbling to spread the word within the cave. *Disaster! Disaster!*

*What disaster?* asked the Monkey King.

*Outside the gate,* said the demons, *is a heavenly general who calls out your title of "BanMaWen," and says he comes by the Jade Emperor's decree to subdue you. He bids you come out quickly to surrender, and so spare our lives.*

When the Monkey King heard this, he called for his armor. He set the purple-gold cap on his head, donned the golden mail, slipped on his cloud-treading shoes, took the As-You-Will Golden-Banded Cudgel in hand, and led his host out through the gate, drawing up his battle-line. The Mighty Magic Spirit opened his eyes wide and gazed: a fine Monkey King indeed —

> His body clad in golden mail, gleaming and bright;
> on his head a golden cap, light flashing forth.
> In his hand he raises a golden-banded cudgel;
> on his feet, cloud-shoes that match it well.
> A pair of strange eyes like bright stars;
> two ears that pass his shoulders, keen and hard.
> Straight and tall, of many transformations;
> his voice rings clear as bell and chiming-stone.
> Sharp-mouthed, jutting-toothed BanMaWen —
> high-hearted, he would be the Great Sage Equal to Heaven.

The Mighty Magic Spirit cried out in a stern voice, *You insolent ape! Do you know who I am?*

The Great Sage turned sharp eyes upon him. *What hairy little spirit are you, and from what quarter? Old Monkey has never met you. Give me your name, and be quick about it.*

*I'll have you yet, you deceitful baboon!* said the Mighty Magic Spirit. *You don't know me? I am the Mighty Magic Heavenly General, vanguard of the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King Li of the High Numinous Empyrean, come by the Jade Emperor's decree to subdue you. Strip off your gear at once and submit to Heaven's grace, lest all the beasts of this mountain be put to the sword. Breathe half a word of refusal, and I'll grind you to powder in an instant.*

When the Monkey King heard this, great rage rose in his heart. *Foul hairy spirit! Don't boast so loudly or wag so long a tongue. I had a mind to strike you dead with a single blow of my cudgel — but then there'd be no one to carry the message. So I'll spare your life: hurry back to Heaven and tell the Jade Emperor that he sorely fails to use the worthy. Old Monkey has boundless powers — why does he set me to tending his horses? Look at the words on my banner there: if he'll promote me according to that title, I'll lay down my weapons and Heaven and earth will of themselves be at peace. But if he won't, I'll come straight up and beat upon the Numinous Empyrean Treasure-Hall, and he'll not sit easy upon his dragon-throne!*

When the Mighty Magic Spirit heard this, he squinted against the wind and looked — and there indeed, outside the gate, stood a tall pole, and on the pole a banner, and on the banner the four great words *GREAT SAGE EQUAL TO HEAVEN.* The Mighty Magic Spirit gave three cold laughs. *This insolent ape! So utterly ignorant of his place, so brazenly without manners — you would be a Great Sage Equal to Heaven? Then taste this ax of mine!* And he hacked straight at the Monkey King's head.

But the Monkey King, being a master of the art, was in no haste; he met the ax squarely with his Golden-Banded Cudgel, weapon answering weapon. And this was a fine fight:

> The cudgel named As-You-Will,
> the ax called Patterned-Blossom.
> The two of them, meeting for the first time,
> knew not each other's depth.
> Ax and cudgel crossed left and right.
> One concealed a marvelous craft;
> the other boasted with a great loud mouth.
> Wielding their arts, they breathed out cloud and mist;
> spreading their hands, they scattered earth and flung the sand.
> The heavenly general had his powers and his way,
> but the Monkey King's transformations were truly without bound.
> The cudgel raised was like a dragon at play in water;
> the ax that came was like a phoenix piercing flowers.
> The Mighty Magic Spirit's fame was known throughout Heaven —
> yet his skill, it turned out, was no match for the other:
> the Great Sage lightly swung his iron rod,
> and one blow to the head left him numb from crown to sole.

The Mighty Magic Spirit could hold his ground no longer. The Monkey King brought his cudgel crashing down upon his head; in a panic the spirit threw up his ax to fend off the blow — but with a sharp crack the ax-handle was beaten clean in two, and he hastily turned and fled the field to save his life.

The Monkey King laughed. *Soft sack! Soft sack! I've spared you — now hurry off and carry the message!*

The Mighty Magic Spirit returned to camp and went straight to the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King, dropping to his knees and stammering, *The BanMaWen truly has vast and far-reaching powers. Your servant could not fight him, and has returned defeated to beg punishment.*

Heavenly King Li flew into a rage. *This wretch has blunted my army's keen spirit! Drag him out and behead him!*

But from the side stepped Prince Nezha, who bowed and pleaded, *Calm your anger, Father King, and pardon the Mighty Magic Spirit's offense for now. Let your child go forth first and take the creature's measure.* The Heavenly King heeded the counsel, and bade the spirit return to camp to await judgment and manage affairs.

## Prince Nezha's six arms

Now Prince Nezha, with his armor all in order, sprang out of the encampment and rushed to the mouth of the Water-Curtain Cave. Wukong, just then drawing in his troops, saw how boldly Nezha came on. A fine prince —

> His forelocks scarce shading his brow,
> his soft hair not yet reaching his shoulders.
> Wondrously quick of wit and bright of mind,
> fine of bone and clear of feature.
> Truly a unicorn-child of Heaven,
> indeed an immortal phoenix of mist and sunset-cloud.
> Dragon-seed by nature, of no common cast;
> in his tender years, no creature of the dust.
> About him hung six kinds of divine weapons;
> his flying transformations boundless and wide.
> Now, by the golden decree from the Jade Emperor's lips,
> he is invested with the title of the Three Altars and the Ocean Assembly.

Wukong came forward to meet him and asked, *Whose little lad are you, that you press up to my gate? What is your business?*

Nezha shouted, *Insolent demon-ape! Do you not know me? I am Nezha, third prince of the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King, and I come by special commission of the Jade Emperor to seize you.*

Wukong laughed. *Little prince, your milk-teeth aren't out yet and your baby-down isn't dry — how dare you talk so big? I'll spare your life and not strike you. Only look at what title is written on my banner, and report it faithfully to the Jade Emperor: grant me that office, and there'll be no need to trouble the host — I'll submit of my own accord. But if it doesn't suit my heart, I'll surely beat my way up to the Numinous Empyrean Treasure-Hall.*

Nezha raised his head and looked — there were the four words *Great Sage Equal to Heaven.*

*What great powers can this demon-ape have,* said Nezha, *that he dares lay claim to such a title? Fear not — take this sword of mine!*

*I'll just stand here and not move,* said Wukong, *and you may hack at me with a few strokes, as you please.*

Nezha, roused to fury, gave a great shout — *Change!* — and at once changed into three heads and six arms, fierce and terrible, his six hands gripping his six weapons: the demon-slaying sword, the demon-hacking saber, the demon-binding rope, the demon-subduing pestle, the embroidered ball, and the fire-wheel; and in jagged array he came hurtling at Wukong's face.

Seeing this, Wukong was startled at heart. *This little lad knows a trick or two after all. No insolence, now — watch my powers!* A fine Great Sage: he too gave a great shout — *Change!* — and changed into three heads and six arms; with one shake of the Golden-Banded Cudgel he made it three cudgels, and his six hands took up the three rods to ward off the blows. This battle truly made the earth quake and the mountains shudder — a fine fight indeed:

> Six-armed Prince Nezha,
> the heaven-born fine stone Monkey King —
> meeting, they were true match for each other,
> coming to grips at the very source.
> The one, dispatched, had come to the lower world;
> the other, deceitful of heart, had stirred up Dipper and Bull.
> The demon-slaying treasure-sword, keen of edge;
> the demon-hacking saber, cruel enough to grieve gods and ghosts;
> the demon-binding rope, like a flying python;
> the great demon-subduing pestle, like a wolf's head;
> the fire-wheel flashing lightning, blazing red and hot;
> the embroidered ball rolling back and forth, again and again.
> The Great Sage's three As-You-Will cudgels
> warded front and guarded rear with cunning craft.
> They fought bitterly through many bouts, neither gaining the upper hand;
> and the prince's heart would not give over.
> He made his six weapons each transform
> into hundreds of thousands of millions, raining down upon the head.
> The Monkey King, unafraid, laughed aloud,
> and tumbled his iron rod about by his own design,
> turning one into a thousand and a thousand into ten thousand,
> till the whole sky swarmed with them, dancing like flying serpents.
> The demon kings of every cave shut fast their doors in fright;
> all over the mountain the ghosts and goblins hid their heads.
> The heavenly soldiers' rage made the clouds grow grim;
> the golden-banded iron rod went whistling through the air.
> On that side, the heavenly host raised a cry, and every man was afraid;
> on this side, the monkey-monsters shook their flags, and every one was anxious.
> Furiously the two sides matched their valor,
> and who could tell which was the harder, which the softer?

The third prince and Wukong each gave free rein to his divine might, and they fought a full thirty bouts. The prince's six weapons turned into thousands upon thousands; Sun Wukong's Golden-Banded Cudgel turned into ten thousands upon thousands. In the middle air they clashed like raindrops, like shooting stars — and there was no telling victory from defeat.

Now Wukong was quick of hand and sharp of eye. In the very thick of the confusion he plucked out a single hair, cried *Change!*, and changed it into his own likeness, holding a cudgel and sparring with Nezha; while his true body, with one bound, slipped around behind Nezha and struck him a blow on the left arm. Nezha, in the midst of his sorcery, heard the wind of the cudgel-head whistle down; he tried to dodge but could not bring his hands round in time, and took the blow square. Wounded and in pain, he gathered in his magic, drew his six weapons back into his body, and retreated in defeat.

From the field below, Heavenly King Li had already seen it, and was about to lead his troops up in support, when all at once the prince appeared before him, trembling, and reported, *Father King, the BanMaWen truly has skill! With all my powers, your child could not best him, and has been wounded in the arm.*

The Heavenly King went pale with shock. *If the creature's powers are such as this, how are we to win?*

*Outside his cave-gate stands a pole with a banner,* said the prince, *and on it the four words "Great Sage Equal to Heaven." With his own mouth he boasts: let the Jade Emperor invest him with the title of Great Sage Equal to Heaven, and all will be over; but should it not be that title, he will surely beat his way up to the Numinous Empyrean Treasure-Hall.*

*If that is so,* said the Heavenly King, *then for now let us not hold the field against him. Let us return to the upper realm, report these words in our memorial, and request more heavenly soldiers to encircle and seize the wretch — it will not be too late.* The prince, hurt and unable to fight on, accordingly returned to Heaven with the Heavenly King to make their report. But of this no more.

## Seven Great Sages

Now see the Monkey King returning to his mountain in triumph. The demon kings of the seventy-two caves and his six sworn brothers all came to offer congratulations, and in the blessed land of the cave-heaven they feasted and made merry beyond compare. Then he said to his six brothers, *Since I have styled myself Great Sage Equal to Heaven, you too may each take the title of Great Sage.*

From among them the Bull Demon King suddenly cried out, *Well said, worthy brother! I shall style myself the Great Sage Who Pacifies Heaven.* The Flood-Dragon Demon King said, *I shall be the Great Sage Who Overturns the Sea.* The Roc Demon King said, *I shall be the Great Sage Who Confounds Heaven.* The Lion-Camel Demon King said, *I shall be the Great Sage Who Moves Mountains.* The Macaque Demon King said, *I shall be the Great Sage Who Carries the Wind.* The Lemur-Monkey Demon King said, *I shall be the Great Sage Who Drives the Spirits.*

And so the Seven Great Sages, each as he pleased, claimed his own title and styled himself as he would, and made merry a whole day before they each dispersed.

## An empty title

Now Heavenly King Li and the third prince, leading their generals, came straight to the Numinous Empyrean Treasure-Hall and reported: *Your servants, in obedience to the sacred decree, marched forth to the lower world to subdue the demon-immortal Sun Wukong; but his powers proved so vast and far-reaching that we could not prevail. We beg Your Majesty to send more troops to root him out.*

*A mere demon-ape,* said the Jade Emperor. *How much skill can he have, that you ask for more soldiers?*

The prince came forward again to report: *May Your Majesty pardon your servant's mortal offense. That demon-ape wields an iron rod; he first defeated the Mighty Magic Spirit, then wounded your servant in the arm. Outside his cave-gate he has set up a pole with a banner inscribed with the four words "Great Sage Equal to Heaven." He says that if he is invested with this office he will lay down his arms and submit; but if it is not this office, he will yet beat his way up to the Numinous Empyrean Treasure-Hall.*

When the Jade Emperor heard this, he exclaimed in astonishment, *How dares this demon-ape be so wild and presumptuous? Let the generals put him to death at once!*

Even as he spoke, the Great White Planet Star stepped forth again from the ranks and said: *This demon-ape knows only how to throw out big words, with no sense of high or low. To send troops against him in battle — I fear he cannot be subdued so easily, and the host would only be worn out to no purpose. Better that Your Majesty extend your great mercy and grace, hand down once more a decree of amnesty, and simply let him be the Great Sage Equal to Heaven. Bestow on him only an empty title — an office without emolument — and there's an end to it.*

*What do you mean by "an office without emolument"?* asked the Jade Emperor.

*His name shall be Great Sage Equal to Heaven,* said the Planet Star, *but give him no affairs to manage and no salary to draw. Keep him quietly somewhere between Heaven and earth, so as to gather in his wayward heart and let no wild presumption arise. Then the cosmos will be at peace, and the seas and skies will gain their calm.*

When the Jade Emperor heard this, he said, *We will do as our minister advises.* And he commanded that the decree be drawn up, and again sent the Planet Star to carry it down.

## The second descent of the Planet

The Planet Star passed out through the Southern Gate of Heaven once more and came straight to the mouth of the Water-Curtain Cave on Flower-Fruit Mountain. This time the scene was unlike before: an awe-striking majesty, a grim and murderous air, and demon-sprites of every sort — there was no kind that was not there. Each one gripped sword or hefted spear, took up saber or brandished staff, snarling and leaping. At sight of the Planet Star, they all surged forward to lay hands on him.

*You captains there,* said the Planet Star, *trouble yourselves to go and report to your Great Sage: I am a heavenly envoy sent by the Lord on High, bearing a sacred decree to invite him.*

The demons ran in at once to report: *Outside is an old man who says he is an envoy from the upper realm, and has a decree to invite you.*

*Welcome, welcome!* said Wukong. *This must be that Great White Planet Star who came before. That time he invited me up to the upper realm — and though the office wasn't fit to hold, still I walked the heavens once and learned the roads within and without the gate. Now he comes again — surely with good intent.* And he ordered the captains to throw wide the banners and drums and form up the ranks to receive him. The Great Sage then led his troop of monkeys forth; with cap on head and mail upon his body, the russet-yellow robe over the mail, and cloud-shoes on his feet, he hurried out the cave-gate, bowed from the waist, and called out loudly: *Old Star, come in, and forgive me the offense of failing to welcome you properly.*

The Planet Star stepped briskly forward, went straight into the cave, and stood facing south. *Now I tell you, Great Sage,* he said. *Some while ago, because the Great Sage disliked the smallness of his office, he left the Imperial Stable. The officers of the stable, great and small, reported it to the Jade Emperor, who handed down his command: "In conferring office, all proceeds from low to high — why should he disdain the smallness?" Whereupon Heavenly King Li led Nezha down to the lower world to give battle. Not knowing the Great Sage's powers, they met with defeat, and returning to Heaven reported: "The Great Sage has set up a banner and would be the Great Sage Equal to Heaven." The military officers still wished to dispute it, but it was I, your old servant, who at the risk of offense made the report on the Great Sage's behalf, so as to spare the raising of the host, and begged that the Great King be granted the scroll. The Jade Emperor approved the memorial, and so I have come to invite you.*

Wukong laughed. *Last time you put yourself to trouble on my account; now again I am favored with your kindness. My thanks, my thanks! But I do not know — will the upper Heaven truly grant me the office and title of Great Sage Equal to Heaven?*

*It was with this very title that I obtained the Emperor's approval before I dared come bearing the decree,* said the Planet Star. *Should it not come to pass, let the blame rest on your old servant alone.*

Wukong was overjoyed and pressed him earnestly to stay for a banquet, which the Planet Star declined. So the two of them mounted an auspicious cloud and came outside the Southern Gate of Heaven, where the heavenly soldiers and generals all clasped their hands in welcome. They went straight in below the Numinous Empyrean Hall, and the Planet Star bowed and reported: *Your servant, in obedience to the decree, has summoned the BanMaWen Sun Wukong, who is now arrived.*

*Let Sun Wukong come forward,* said the Jade Emperor. *Today We proclaim you Great Sage Equal to Heaven. The office could not be higher — but on no account are you to behave wildly.* The Monkey, again, merely made a long bow toward the throne and said, *I thank you for your grace.*

The Jade Emperor then commanded the master-builders Zhang and Lu, the two craft-officers, to raise on the right-hand side of the Garden of Immortal Peaches a Mansion of the Great Sage Equal to Heaven; and within the mansion to establish two bureaus — one named the Bureau of Peace and Quiet, the other the Bureau of Tranquil Spirit. Each bureau had its immortal clerks to attend him left and right. He further dispatched the Star Lords of the Five Bushels to escort Wukong to take up his post, and bestowed in addition two bottles of imperial wine and ten golden flowers, charging him to settle his heart and steady his will, and never again to behave wildly.

The Monkey King received the orders and obeyed. That same day he went with the Star Lords of the Five Bushels to his mansion, opened the bottles of wine, and drank them down together with his company. Then he saw the Star Officers back to their own palaces, and at last, his heart content and his wishes fulfilled, gladdened in earth and joyful in heaven, he took his pleasure in the heavenly palace with never a care or a hindrance. Truly it is said:

> His immortal name forever set in the scroll of long life;
> no more to fall through the wheel of rebirth — handed down for all the ages.

And in the end, there is no knowing what came after; so listen to the telling in the next chapter.
