# The Great Sage Plunders the Peaches and Steals the Elixir

## Opening directive

Open: "Welcome. We're with Wu Cheng'en's *Journey to the West* — the sixteenth-century Chinese novel — and the chapter where Heaven's elegant 'empty title' for the Monkey King collapses on the orchard it was meant to settle him beside." Land the spine verbatim: *With nothing to do, he will one day breed mischief in his idleness.* Repeat that spine three times: open, Peach-Garden pivot, close. Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. Forbidden openers: faux-profundity, "deep dive", "today's episode", "buckle up".

## Host dynamic

Host A (male) = storyteller voicing narration and verse. Host B (female) = curious listener. Roles do not rotate. No chorus openers ("exactly", "right", "wow") as Host B's first word.

## Name discipline

- Sun Wukong → "Sun Wukong" / "the Great Sage Equal to Heaven" / "the Monkey King".
- Xu Jingyang → "the Perfected Man Xu Jingyang" first; then "Xu Jingyang".
- Maidens → "the seven robed maidens" (red, green, white, black, purple, yellow, blue, in order).
- Barefoot Immortal → "the Barefoot Great Immortal" first; then "the Barefoot Immortal".
- Lord Lao → "Lord Lao Tzu, the Supreme Patriarch of the Way" first; then "Lord Lao Tzu".
- Jade Emperor → "the Jade Emperor". Queen Mother → "the Queen Mother of the Jade Pool".
- Heavenly King Li → "the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King Li"; then "Heavenly King Li". Nezha → "the third Prince Nezha"; then "Prince Nezha".

## Pronunciation

Say each name ONCE using the phonetic. Never spell and phoneticize back-to-back.

- Pronounce Sun Wukong as soon woo-KONG.
- Pronounce Xu Jingyang as shoo jing-YAHNG.
- Pronounce Nezha as NUH-jah.
- Pronounce Lao Tzu as LAOW-dzuh.
- Pronounce Tushita as too-SHEE-tah.

## Three-part focus

1. **Idleness, warning, the orchard.** Brotherhood-swearing with every star. Xu Jingyang's warning: *with nothing to do, he will one day breed mischief in his idleness.* Appointment: *Take charge for the present of the Garden of Immortal Peaches.* Three thousand six hundred trees in three cohorts of twelve hundred — ripening in three thousand, six thousand, nine thousand years. Spine again. Secret peach-eating; the manikin two inches tall asleep at the top of a tree.

2. **Maidens, gulling, Jade Pool, elixir.** Seven robed maidens find cap and robes but not the warden; the southward bough's peach half red, half white; *Stay! Stay! Stay!* and the maidens rooted. Wukong gulls the Barefoot Great Immortal with a counterfeit summons to the Hall of Universal Brightness. Empty Jade Pool: sleep-insects on the wine-officers, hundred-flavored delicacies, jade-fluid and jasper-nectar — *This is bad — this is bad!* Drunken wrong turn into the Tushita Palace; *inner and outer are one and the same*; five gourds of Nine-Cycle Golden Elixir crunched *as one cracks roasted beans.* Second self-reproach: *This is a calamity bigger than the sky itself.*

3. **Reports, muster, night-watch.** Flight through the Western Heaven Gate by invisibility; *Sweet or not sweet, it's the water of home.* Reports cascade: maidens, wine-officers, Lord Lao, residence clerks, Barefoot Immortal, Inspecting Numinous Officer. The Jade Emperor musters a hundred thousand heavenly soldiers under the Four Great Heavenly Kings, Heavenly King Li, and Prince Nezha; the eighteen frames of heaven-and-earth net ring the Mountain of Flowers and Fruit. Nine Luminaries beaten back at the iron-plank bridge; *ten unpardonable crimes*; Wukong: *These several matters — they are true, all true. But what of it now?* Melee from the hour of the Dragon to sundown; seventy-two cave-kings taken; hair-pluck and *Change!* — *a thousand and a hundred Great Sages, each one wielding a gold-banded cudgel* — Nezha beaten back, the five heavenly kings routed. Three-sobs-three-laughs return; *Victory and defeat are the common lot of soldiers.* Spine a third time, then the couplet: *The monkey-monster's rising shook both heaven and earth; they spread their net and kept their watch by day and night.* End there; do not pre-announce.

## Tone constraints

Three governing analogies — use these and no others:

1. **The warden in the orchard he was set to guard** — the office turned into the perch.
2. **The two-inch manikin asleep at the top of a tree** — offense hidden in the leaves.
3. **Roasted beans cracked between the teeth** — the casualness of the transgression.

No model-invented analogies. If "Think of it like…" appears, the image MUST be one of the three above or one already in the source (southward bough, iron-plank bridge, heaven-and-earth net). Walk at the source's pace; let block-quoted verse (Peach Garden rhapsody, Barefoot Immortal's approach, muster verse, melee verse, closing couplet) slow it down. No biographical background on Wu Cheng'en.
