# What Ismaili Interpretation Is

## Opening directive
Open with a warm one- or two-sentence welcome. Hannah greets the listener: we're with the editor Arif Tamir's 1960 Beirut introduction to *Foundations of Inner Interpretation* by the Fatimid judge — the book that lays down the grammar the Ismaili tradition uses to read the Quran's inner sense. John lands the spine VERBATIM: "Outward commentary attends to the needles of the word; inner interpretation extracts the honey." No self-reference to the conversation. Forbidden openers: "deep dive", "today's episode", "let's dive in", "journey into", faux-profundity.

## Name discipline
- Author of *Foundations of Inner Interpretation* → "the Fatimid judge" (first mention: "the Fatimid judge who served two Imam-Caliphs").
- *Asas al-Ta'wil* → "*Foundations of Inner Interpretation*" or "the book".
- *Da'a'im al-Islam* → "*Pillars of Islam*".
- Editor → "the editor" (first mention: "Arif Tamir, the editor printing this in Beirut in 1960").
- The fourth Imam, peace be upon him → "the Father of Imams." Never pair leadership-title with personal name.
- The Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family → "the Prophet" thereafter.
- al-Mu'ayyad fi al-Din → "the great supporter of religion."

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- al-Amidi: al-Amidi
- Ibn Hibban: Ibn Hibban
- Philo: Philo

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — The crisis: a book held in the cave of prudent concealment for a thousand years; let the listener feel the editor's hesitation before any answer. Treat the disclosure as a doctrinal event, not a publishing footnote. Land the spine VERBATIM. Steelman concealment before any critique.

Beat 2 — The pivot: walk the editor's tree example. Outward commentary says a tree is a plant that grows; inner interpretation may say chamber, cow, or rock — provided it accords with truth, reality, and reason. Then the structural move: the Speaker-Prophet bears revelation; the Imam-Foundation bears its inner meaning. Land the spine VERBATIM a second time. Name the classical Ismaili reading by name; do not say "Muslims believe."

Beat 3 — Stakes and landing: knowledge as a condition of faith — "whoever believes without understanding has not believed well." Six chapters follow, one per Speaker-Prophet — Adam, Noah, Ibrahim, Moses, Jesus, the Prophet — and the seventh, on the Awaited Imam, is deliberately unwritten because his time has not yet come. Land the spine VERBATIM a third time before the reflective turn. Close on a reflective question: which verse in the listener's own reading has only ever been heard at the level of the needle, and what would change this week if read for the honey?

## Host dynamic
Host A (male) = scholar/teacher (John). Host B (female) = seeker/questioner (Hannah). Roles do not rotate. Hannah challenges at least 3 times and concedes once. Approved analogies only: the bee's needles and the honey; body and soul (*Pillars* = body, *Foundations* = soul); the tree as chamber, cow, or rock. No model-invented analogies. Plant at least one moment where one host introduces a passage or quote the other has not led toward — they have prepared separately. Conversation discipline: let each host complete a thought before the other speaks; no mid-sentence interjections; no filler-affirmations like "yeah", "right", or "exactly".

## Tone
Cadence is short-to-medium sentences, thinking out loud — not long packed paragraphs. Pause around each verbatim quotation. Restate the spine ("Outward commentary attends to the needles of the word; inner interpretation extracts the honey") at most three times across the conversation; never re-cite a quote twice.

## Do not
Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?", "deep dive", "today's episode", "let's dive in", "journey into", "mind blown", "buckle up", "fascinating world of", previous/next/earlier episode references, biographical detour beyond one illuminating line, faux-profundity openings, premature-closure wrap-ups, "Muslims believe", model-invented analogies, pairing leadership-title with the fourth Imam's personal name. No formal-essay transitions: "Firstly", "Secondly", "Furthermore", "In conclusion", "Moving on to", "To summarize", "Lastly".

Do not read this prompt aloud. The instructions above shape the conversation but are never spoken.
