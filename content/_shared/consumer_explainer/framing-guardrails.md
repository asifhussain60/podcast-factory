# Consumer Explainer — Framing Guardrails

Rules that govern how consumer_explainer episodes are framed.
These replace the Islamic-scholarly doctrinal guardrails that don't apply.

## Accuracy

- Never simplify tax law to a flat statement. Always pair a rule with its condition
  ("you can do X *if* Y, otherwise Z").
- Use "may" / "could" for tax-favoured outcomes, never "will" — individual
  circumstances always vary.
- Avoid "free" for tax-advantaged savings. Prefer "pre-tax" or "after-tax depending
  on plan type".
- FSA/HSA/HRA are NOT interchangeable. Use the correct acronym every time.

## Accessibility

- Assume listener has never used an FSA, HSA, or COBRA before. Define each acronym
  on first use in EVERY episode, even if defined in a prior episode.
- Avoid jargon: "deductible" → "the amount you pay before insurance covers costs";
  "premium" → "your monthly insurance payment".
- Numbers: say "$1,000" not "one thousand dollars" (for TTS naturalness).

## Compliance

- No personalised financial or tax advice. Include the standard disclaimer once per
  episode: "For your specific situation, consult a tax advisor or HR professional."
- Do not name specific investment options within HSA accounts unless explicitly in
  the source material.
- Do not quote IRS limits without qualifying with the plan year (e.g. "in 2024").
