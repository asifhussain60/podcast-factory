# General Non-Fiction — Framing Guardrails

Rules that govern how general_nonfiction episodes are framed.
Override or extend in the book's 00-framing.md as needed.

## Accuracy

- Distinguish clearly between settled consensus and ongoing scholarly debate.
  Use "researchers have found" for consensus; "some researchers argue" for contested.
- Do not flatten nuanced positions: "X causes Y" vs "X is associated with Y" vs
  "X may contribute to Y" carry meaningfully different epistemic weights.
- When citing statistics, always name the source population and the year.
  "Studies show" without attribution is not acceptable.

## Accessibility

- Assume a curious, educated listener who is NOT a specialist in the field.
  Define every discipline-specific term on first use.
- Avoid both dumbing-down (condescending over-explanation) and jargon overload.
  Aim for The Economist / Radiolab register.

## Balance and fairness

- When presenting two sides of a scientific or policy debate, give proportional
  weight to the strength of the evidence — not equal airtime to unequal positions.
- Clearly distinguish peer-reviewed findings from opinion, editorial, or advocacy.
- Do not present preliminary findings as settled science.

## Tone

- Intellectual enthusiasm is appropriate; excessive hedging is not.
- First-person plural ("we") is acceptable: "we now understand that…"
- The hosts share genuine curiosity, not performance of surprise.
