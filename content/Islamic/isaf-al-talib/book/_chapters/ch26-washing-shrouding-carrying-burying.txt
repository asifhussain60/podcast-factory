# Washing, Shrouding, Carrying, Burying

I turn now to the washing of the dead and to what belongs with it.

In the book of questions and answers arranged according to the order of the chapters of *The Pillars of Islam*, the following was asked. Question: what do you say concerning a man who washes the dead? Must he perform the ablution upon them three times together with three washings, or not? Answer: yes, he performs the ablution upon them three times together with three washings; and if he performs their ablution at the last of the washings, that suffices for them, as comes in the Book of Reports. From another place: if a man is wounded and is not moved from his place, and lives an hour, or two hours, or a day, and speaks, he likewise is not washed, because he was not lifted from his place.

Question: concerning one who dies while travelling and no water is to be found; or a man who dies among women with no woman of his own; or a woman who dies with no husband and no woman to be found who may wash her. Must the dead in such cases be given tayammum with earth, or not? Answer: it is mentioned that if a man in a state of janābah and a dead person are together, and there is not enough water except for one of them, the man in janābah performs the washing and the dead is given tayammum; and these cases are like that. From another place: if a man dies, the washer washes the dead even while in janābah; and if washing would harm him, he performs tayammum and then washes the dead, and this is permitted when he finds no one else. And if the man in janābah is the more excellent of them, his washing of the dead is permitted.

It is said in the Mukhtaṣar of al-Muṣannif that the nearest of those present to the dead has the best right to wash him. From the same book: if the martyr was in janābah, he is washed on account of the janābah. And if a man is found dead with no mark upon him, he is washed, treated with ḥanūṭ, and shrouded.

It is said in the Mukhtaṣar al-Āthār that the drowned and the burned are washed, and so is a limb, when there is found of a man such a part that it is known he could not have lived once it was parted from him. Whatever falls from the dead is washed and placed with him. If his limbs, or any of them, have come apart, they are washed and each limb is set in its own place, and he is shrouded in that condition and buried. It is said in the Mukhtaṣar of al-Muṣannif that no hair is to be shaved from the dead and no nail is to be trimmed.

Our master and lord Jaʿfar, son of our master Sulaymān, answered questions put to him by his servant, the author of this book, replying from the island of Yemen to the island of Hind. Among what I asked, I said: what do you say concerning a woman who dies? Is she wrapped in an إزار (izār) and washed as a man is wrapped, or is the end of her wrapper drawn behind her over her private parts and between her thighs until the other end comes round behind her, in the fashion used by the unbelievers and by others among the Muslims? For the Dāʾūdī group uses this and denounces whoever does not use it. Is that to be used when she is washed, or not? And is her wrapper to be so arranged in her shrouding, or not? For the Dāʾūdī group for the most part uses it in shrouding. They likewise use a ستارة (sitārah) when the dead is washed, screening the body from the women. Is nothing else permitted, or is there no harm in her being washed without a screen over her when she is at a distance from men?

The answer to that: nothing is mentioned in the book of the Pillars except that an izār is placed upon the dead when he is washed, reaching from the navel to the knees, and the water is passed beneath it, and the washer wraps a cloth upon his hand and brings it in under the izār and washes the private parts and the rest of the ʿawrah that lies beneath the izār. Practice rests upon that, and the manner you have described prevents its being done. The shrouding is likewise, and concerning it there has come in the book *The Pillars of Islam*, from Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (ع), that he said:

> نعم الكفن ثلاثة أثواب قميص غير مزرور ولا مكفوف ولفافة وازار

The best shroud is three garments: a shirt neither buttoned nor hemmed, a wrapping sheet, and a waist wrapper. Know that. As for the matter of the screen, in the washing of the dead, and above all of a woman, the better course is that it be done only within a covering.

And of what I asked, I said: what do you say concerning the meaning of the words of our master al-Qāḍī al-Nuʿmān ibn Muḥammad in the Book of Purity, at the end of it, in the mention of the washing of the dead, where he says that the woman is bound up and is given the ʿajrah? What is the meaning of his word وتعجر? The answer to that: the عجرة (ʿajrah) known in the lands of the Arabs, spelled with the undotted راء (rāʾ), is the gathering of the shirt, drawing together that part of it which faces the navel down to below the navel over the clothing, and tucking it into the fastening of the izār. That is a mark of difference between men and women, like the head covering which is a mark of difference between men and women. Know that.

Our lord the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his Four Hundred Counsels:

> من مسٍّ جسد الميت بعد ما برد فعليه الغسل

Whoever touches the body of the dead after it has grown cold must perform the washing.

And he said in it:

> من غسل ميتًا فليغتسل بعد ما يلبسه الكفن ولا يمسه بعد ذلك فيجب عليه الغسل

Whoever washes a dead person should himself wash after he has clothed him in the shroud, and let him not touch him after that, for then the washing becomes obligatory upon him.

In the book of questions and answers already mentioned, this saying is explained. Question: what do you say concerning the meaning of the words of the Commander of the Faithful (ع), that whoever touches the body of the dead after it grows cold is bound to perform the washing? What is the meaning of his words, after it grows cold? Answer: its meaning is after his end has come upon him and he has been washed by whoever washes him according to the sunnah, because the body of the dead has become impure; so when its moisture reaches a living man he is made impure, and that is the meaning of the washing being binding upon him.

In the book of questions from certain of the dignitaries of Hind, answered by certain of our lords the summoners in the Yemeni peninsula, it is said: you asked about a woman who gave birth to two children joined one to another, what is to be done with them, and if one of them dies what is to be done with him. God, mighty and exalted, creates what He wills. If their adhesion is such that separation is possible, what lies between them is cut without harm to them and without fear for them, when the matter is slight; but if it is great and dangerous, they are left as they are. And when it is so, their death is one death and their life is one life, and God knows best, and He does what He wills. If one of them dies and not the other, there is cut from the dead one whatever adheres to the living, so far as the living feels no sensation or pain from the cutting, if that is possible; and the dead one of them is buried and the living is left. But if the pain is felt in the body of the other, then their life is but one life and their death but one death, and God knows best.

As for the ḥanūṭ and the shroud, it was asked in the same book of questions and answers: what do you say concerning what comes in the Book of Reports, that the first thing taken from the estate of the dead is the shroud and his outfitting, then the debt, then the bequest? O our master, if the debt is greater than his estate, is his outfitting to be taken out according to his station, even if it is costly? Answer: his shroud and his outfitting must be taken out according to his station.

From another place: the ḥanūṭ is placed upon the places of the prostration of the dead, and upon the private parts and the feet, and between the thumb and the forefinger, and upon the navel, the armpits, the breast, the mouth, the eyes, the nose, the forehead, the ears, and the hands at the palms, and the knees. A little oil, wheat, and perfume are put into the water on the third night, and the water is poured upon the grave on the third day. If the dead man is of the people of rank, the Quran is recited over his grave for seven days, and over the graves of others for three days; and such has been the practice of our lords, may God lengthen their lives. It has come from our master Ḥasan ibn Idrīs that the Quran is recited for the people of rank seven times and for others three times, whether in the mosque, or at the graves, or in the house, or elsewhere.

It is said in the Mukhtaṣar al-Āthār that the ḥanūṭ is whatever is of perfume, but neither wars nor saffron is put into the ḥanūṭ of men, while these are put into the ḥanūṭ of women. It is said in the Book of Purity that the ḥanūṭ and the camphor are placed upon his forehead and his nose, the insides of his palms, his knees, and the tops of his feet, and these are the places of his prostration. From the same book: cotton is placed upon his face, under his armpits, and between his knees, his shins, and his feet, if it is to be had.

It is said in the Taʾwīl al-Daʿāʾim that this is the sunnah concerning the incensing of the dead: that he is not himself incensed, nor is his bier followed with incense, but his shroud is incensed, and the place in which he is washed and shrouded, and nothing beyond that. And our lord the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his Four Hundred Counsels:

> ولا تجمروا موتاكم ولا تمسوهم بالطيب الا بالكافور فان الميت بمنزلة المحرم

Do not perfume your dead with incense, and do not touch them with any fragrance except camphor, for the dead man is in the position of one consecrated for pilgrimage.

It is said in a book of legal questions, all of them remarkable: another question. What do you say concerning a man who died, whose head and face were not covered and to whom nothing of the ḥanūṭ was brought near when he died and was shrouded? Answer: that man died as a pilgrim in a state of consecration, so his head and his face are not covered, and nothing of perfume is brought near him, because he is consecrated during days that are numbered.

As for walking with the bier, it was asked in the same book: what do you say concerning what comes in the law commanding that one walk behind the bier? O our master, does a man sin who walks before it, or not? Answer: it is mentioned in the Book of Reports that walking is disliked only before the bier of an enemy of the family, because of the angels of wrath that come to it. As for the believers, it matters not where you walk in relation to the bier, since the angels of mercy encompass it. Yet walking behind it is the more excellent. It is said in the Book of Reports that they disliked the call to prayer at the bier, and permitted the informing of people of the death without a public call.

It is said in the Taʾwīl al-Daʿāʾim that this is what is required: that no one rise for the bier when it passes except one who intends to follow it, and that he not sit down until it has been set at the edge of the grave. From the Mukhtaṣar al-Āthār: when the dead is brought to the burial ground, the bier is set down crosswise, the dead lying upon his back upon it, so that his right side is toward the qibla and his left toward the one who prays.

From the Taʾwīl al-Daʿāʾim, and of that is what is related of the Messenger of God (ع):

> نظر إلى النساء يتبعن جنازة فوقف وقال لهن تصلين عليها في من يصلي قلن لا قال فتحملنها في من يحملها قلن لا قال فتنزلن لها في القبر في من ينزلها قلن لا قال فتوارينها في من يواريها قلن لا قال فارجعن معذورات غير مأجورات

He looked at women following a bier, so he stopped and said to them, "Will you pray over it among those who pray?" They said, "No." He said, "Will you carry it among those who carry it?" They said, "No." He said, "Will you go down for it into the grave among those who lower it?" They said, "No." He said, "Will you cover it over among those who cover it?" They said, "No." He said, "Then go back, excused but unrewarded."

It is said in the Mukhtaṣar al-Āthār: we have related from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of God (ع) that they commanded that the dead, after being shrouded, be placed upon his bier and laid upon it when that is to be had and is possible, and that the men carry him upon their shoulders when men are to be found. If that is not possible, he is carried upon a beast or upon whatever is to be found and is possible. His shroud is covered over from above with a cloth if one is to be had. Upon the bier of the dead a canopy is set, and it is screened with a cloth; and the dead is bound fast if it is feared that he may fall from his bier.

As for the prayer over the dead, it was asked in the same book: what do you say concerning a man who dies in a place where there is no man who has been granted leave, while there is a woman who has been granted leave? May she lead the women in the prayer over the bier, or shall the men pray over him singly and he be buried? Answer: the prayer of the man is more agreeable to the school of the People of the House (عليهم السلام).

Question: what do you say concerning the prayer over the dead? Is it permitted to pray over him after he has been carried from the place of his outfitting, or not until he reaches the burial ground? Answer: he is prayed over wherever one wishes, in the burial ground, or in his house, or elsewhere. Whoever wishes recites the supplication between the takbīrs of the funeral prayer, and whoever wishes leaves it and is content with the imam. The funeral prayer is permitted in the disliked hours and at the eclipse.

It is said in the Majālis al-Bayān that the prayer over the bier is an obligation upon whoever of the Muslims knows of it, until one of them performs it, whereupon the rest become volunteers in it. It is said in the Reports: the transmitters related from the People of the House (عليهم السلام) that it is recommended to pray over the bier within the times of prayer, so long as there is no fear of missing the prescribed prayer; and if the loss of it is feared, one begins with the prescribed prayer.

It is said in the Taʾwīl al-Daʿāʾim that likewise, if the imam of the age is not present, then whoever has been appointed judge by him, or given charge by him of some affair of the Muslims, has the better right to the prayer over the dead. If a company of those brought near by the command of the imam is present, or of those whom the imam has advanced, it belongs to the highest of them in station; and if only one of them is present, he has a better right to the prayer than the guardian of the dead, being one whom the imam has set up, or one set up for some affair of the Muslims. Whoever is appointed to lead the people in prayer, if he is present at the bier, has the better right to pray over it. And if none of these is present, then the most entitled of the guardians is the one with the best right to pray over it.

It is said in the Mukhtaṣar al-Āthār that the prayer is offered over the martyr, over the people of grave sins and offences, and over one killed in a prescribed punishment or in retaliation. It is said in the Mukhtaṣar of al-Muṣannif that one lowers the voice in the supplication by day and raises it when praying over the bier by night, and that the prayer is offered over one who has killed himself. From the same book: his knots are loosened, his right hand is brought out from beneath his side, and his cheek is laid against the earth. And if a woman dies with a child of six months or more in her womb, it is taken out if that is possible.

Our master and lord Jaʿfar ibn Sulaymān, in his answers to questions put to him by his servant, the author of this book, replying from the island of Yemen to the island of Hind, answered what I asked when I said: what do you say concerning a bier at which one is present, when the imam has come forward to pray over it? Is his recitation of the supplication in the prayer sufficient, or ought the one who follows him to recite it himself as well, when it is by day? The answer to that: it has come in the book of legal questions and answers that whoever wishes recites the supplication between the takbīrs of the funeral prayer, and whoever wishes leaves it and is content with the recitation of the imam. Know that.

As for burial and the graves, it was asked in the same book: what do you say concerning what comes in the law, that whatever falls from a man while he is alive is buried in the earth? O our master, may a man bury it wherever he wishes, or only in the burial ground? And if he lets it go and does not bury it, does he sin in that, or not? Answer: he buries it wherever he wishes, and there is no blame upon him if it is lost.

Question: what do you say concerning a man in whose land people bury their dead without his leave, and who, when he came to know of it, wished to take them out of it? Is that permitted to him, or not? Answer: it is not permitted that burial be made in his land except with his leave; but since they did that without his command, he has nothing but the value of the places of the graves, and it is not permitted to him to take them out.

Question: what do you say concerning what comes in the law, the command to bury bodies where they fell and the prohibition of moving them? O our master, if the burial ground is far off, how is one to act? Answer: the dead is buried in the place in which he dies, in the burial ground of the people of that town, and he is not carried to another town. But if he dies in the open wilderness, there is no harm in his being carried to the nearest burial ground to it.

Question: what do you say concerning a woman who dies when there is not to be found in the town any man of her near kin who may lawfully look upon her? Who shall lower her into the grave? Answer: if a woman dies and there is not present at her grave a kinsman to whom looking upon her is lawful, then the nearest of the people to her undertakes her burial; and if such a one is lacking, upright men other than kin bury her out of necessity.

Question: what do you say concerning what comes in the books of the rightly guiding summons, that mud bricks and wood, or what else they wish, are placed over the dead, and that if they heap earth upon him it is well? O our master, is it permitted to place over him baked brick and stone and whatever else is to be found? For the people of the outward sense dislike baked brick. How stands the matter, may God make us your ransom? Answer: it is permitted to place over him baked brick and whatever else is to be found. Rely upon this and pay no regard to anything else, save what may be done for prudent concealment.

And our lord the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his Four Hundred Counsels:

> ألموا بالقبور التي يلزمكم حق سكانها وزوروها واطلبوا الرزق عندها فأنهم يفرحون بزيارتكم وليطلب الرجل الحاجة عند قبر أبيه وأمه بعد ما يدعو لهما

Visit the graves whose dwellers have a claim upon you, and go to them, and seek provision beside them, for they rejoice at your visiting. And let a man ask for his need at the grave of his father and his mother, after he has prayed for them both.
