# Water and Intention: The Anatomy of Ablution

I mention now the circumstances that make ablution (الوضوء) necessary.

It is said in the book known as the Book of Question and Answer, which is arranged according to the order of the doors of Da'a'im al-Islam, the questions being those of certain teachers of the Indian lands and the answers those of the summoners of the Yemeni lands.

Question: What do you say, O best of those who have cast upon us the lights of his knowledge, in a matter over which our minds have labored: it has come in the Book of Purity that a man is forbidden to urinate beneath fruit-bearing trees. O our master, what is intended by these trees, those upon which fruit is actually present, or those whose property it is to bear fruit? Answer: The trees in which the fruit is present, or those whose property it is to bear it.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the books of the da'wah forbidding the greeting of one who is in a state of hadath, and forbidding speech in that state? O our master, if the one in that state rises from that place, having cleansed himself with a stone or with something else, is it permitted for him to speak, and is it permitted to greet him or not? Answer: Once he has risen, he may be greeted, and he must return the greeting and may speak.

Question: What does our master say concerning a stone or a clod of earth with which a man cleanses himself after urinating, which afterward dries, and which he then uses a second time; is that permitted or not? Answer: There is no harm in it, provided that no impurity and no moisture are evident upon it.

It is said in the book Mukhtasar al-Musannaf that what nullifies ablution is sleep; and whatever issues from the front or the rear passage, whether a stone or wind; and it is nullified by an enema, by fainting, by insanity, and by the disappearance of the glans into the front passage of a living person or a dead one.

It is said in the book Ta'wil al-Da'a'im that it has likewise come down that if one performing ablution interrupts his ablution, he builds upon what he has done, so long as the water has not dried upon the limbs whose washing has already been completed.

And it is said in Mukhtasar al-Musannaf: whoever has had both hands amputated, the amputation being above the elbows so that nothing remains of them, the obligation of washing the hands has fallen away from him.

Our master and lord, the Trusted of the Imam, the Banner of Islam (رض), said in his gatherings entitled Majalis al-Bayan and Majalis al-Hikma, known as the Councils of al-Mustansiriyya, in the fifth council of them:

You have heard what was recited to you, wherein we singled you out with the splendid glad tidings and joined you to the company of those of whom God Almighty said:

> لهم البشرى في الحياة الدنيا وفي الآخرة

Theirs are the glad tidings in the life of this world and in the hereafter.

And we set forth the honoring of the number nineteen by its conjunction with the clauses of the testimony and its seven letters and its twelve, and that Islam is built upon the seven obligations, to which twelve practices are attached, in a manner that is not hidden from one who possesses knowledge and discernment. Listen now, then, to what is recited to you: that to every obligation among the obligations already mentioned there belong seven obligations and twelve practices. Among them is the walaya (الولاية), which is the first of them and the most excellent of them. It has been said before that in every allusion made by these numbers, the ranking of the friends of God is the utmost aim and intention of it.

After the walaya comes purity, and in it are seven obligations and twelve practices. The seven obligations are those of ablution, which is the lesser purity (الطهارة الصغرى):

1. Pure water
2. Intention
3. Washing the face
4. Washing the hands
5. Wiping the head
6. Wiping the feet
7. Order, which is that one begins with what God Almighty has begun by mentioning

The twelve practices are:

1. Cleansing oneself after relieving oneself (الاستنجاء)
2. Washing the hands before entering them into the vessel
3. Pronouncing the name of God when sitting down for ablution
4. Rinsing the mouth
5. Drawing water into the nose
6. Moving the ring so that the water reaches what is beneath it
7. Combing the water through the beard
8. Wetting the eyes with water
9. Giving the right limbs precedence over the left
10. Wiping the ears, their inner and outer parts, with water
11. The second and third washings of the face, each one of them a practice, while the first is the obligation
12. The siwak (السواك)

And we say that the greater purity (الطهارة الكبرى), which is the washing of the whole body, is divided into seven obligations and twelve practices. The obligations of it are:

1. The washing from janabah (الجنابة)
2. The washing from menstruation
3. The washing of the unbeliever when he enters Islam
4. The washing of one who has fallen unconscious when he recovers
5. The washing from the blood of nifas (النفاس)
6. The washing after immersion in impurity
7. The washing of the dead, which is an obligation upon the living until one of them performs it

And the practices of it are:

1. The washing of Friday
2. The washing for the festival of al-Fitr
3. The washing for the festival of al-Adha
4. The washing for ihram
5. The washing for entering the Haram
6. The washing for entering the Ka'bah
7. The washing for entering Madinah
8. The washing on the day of Arafah
9. The washing on three nights of the month of Ramadan, the night of the nineteenth
10. The night of the twenty-first
11. The night of the twenty-third
12. The washing after washing the dead

Our lord Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) said in his four hundred maxims of conduct:

> تنظفوا بالماء من الريح المنتنة

Cleanse yourselves with water from the foul odor.

And he said therein:

> إذا أراد أحدكم الخلاء فليقل بسم الله اللهم أمط عني الأذى وأعذني من الشيطان الرجيم الحمد لله الذي أطعمنيه في عافية وأخرج مني أذاه في عافية

When one of you intends to go to the privy, let him say: In the name of God. O God, remove the filth from me and shelter me from the accursed devil. Praise be to God, who fed me in soundness of health and brought its harm out of me in soundness of health.

> إذا نظر أحدكم إلى حدثه بعد فراغه فليقل اللهم ارزقني الحلال وجنبني الحرام فان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله قال ما من عبد إلا وقد وكل الله به ملكا يلوي عنقه إذا أحدث حتى ينظر إليه وينبغي له أن يسأل الله الحلال فان الملك يقول يا ابن آدم هذا ما حرصت عليه فانظر من أين أخذته وإلى ما صار

When one of you looks at what he has passed after he has finished, let him say: O God, provide me with the lawful and keep me away from the unlawful. For the Messenger of God said: There is no servant but that God has appointed over him an angel who turns his neck, when he relieves himself, so that he looks at it; and it is fitting for him to ask God for the lawful, for the angel says: O son of Adam, this is the thing you were so eager for; consider, then, where you took it from and what it has come to.

And he said therein:

> إذا خالط النوم القلب وجب الوضوء

When sleep mingles with the heart, ablution becomes obligatory.
