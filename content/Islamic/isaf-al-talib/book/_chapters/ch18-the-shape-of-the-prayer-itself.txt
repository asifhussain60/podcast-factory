# The Shape of the Prayer Itself

I turn now to the characteristics of the prayer and its sunnahs.

In the question-and-answer book mentioned at the outset, which one of its arrangers set out according to the order of the chapters of the book *Da'a'im al-Islam*, the following is asked. Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the Book of Purity, that Ibn al-Kawwa repeatedly opposed Amir al-Mu'minin Ali (ع) by raising his voice during the prayer, and that Ali (ع) then recited:

> فاصبر أن وعد الله حق

So be patient; the promise of God is true.

O our master, did Ali (ع) begin the prayer with the surah of which this verse is a part, or not? Answer: No. Rather he recited this verse in order to silence him and to rebuke him.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the books of the Shari'ah regarding the long portions of al-mufassal, its middle portions, and its short portions? O our master, what are their limits in the madhhab of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام)? Answer: The long portions of al-mufassal run from al-Hujurat to al-Mujadila; its middle portions run from al-Mujadila to عمّ يتساءلون; and its short portions run from عمّ يتساءلون to al-Nas.

And from the same source: whoever abandons the prayer without excuse, or abandons any of the prescribed works, has broken the covenant. Whoever accuses a believer, and the accused then swears by God, and the accuser does not believe him, has broken the covenant. Whoever commits fornication or kills a believer has broken the covenant. Whoever reviles the summoner, or reviles the one whom the summoner has appointed, has broken the covenant. And if the summoner commands the believer to strive with his person and his wealth, and he refuses without excuse, he has broken the covenant. And whoever ties the intention for the prayer, and then his intention slips away from him before he enters into the prayer, whether through some preoccupation, or forgetfulness, or anything else, and he then prays after that, it does not suffice him.

It is not permissible for a man to recite within himself what he has not uttered with his tongue. Whoever performs the first tashahhud has not thereby been excused from the second, though the second does suffice in place of the first.

He said in the book *Mukhtasar al-Musannaf*: Whoever ties the intention for the prayer, and then his intention slips away from him before he enters into it, whether through preoccupation or forgetfulness or anything else, and then prays after that, it does not suffice him. And from the same book: the one praying makes the tawajjuh after the takbirat al-ihram, the imam and the follower being equal in this, so long as the imam has not preceded the follower in something; and he begins the recitation after the tawajjuh.

He said in *al-Ikhtisar*: He opens the prayer with a takbir in which he raises his hands, spreading his palms and joining his fingers in a straight extension. The least by which the prayer is completed, and its minimum, is this:

1. Opening with the takbir
2. Standing, for whoever is able
3. Bowing
4. Prostration
5. Sitting for the tashahhud

Whoever performs these five limits, and has forgotten or does not know what lies beyond them, his prayer is complete. But whoever knows their limits and then deliberately omits something from the obligations or the sunnahs of the prayer has no prayer, and he must repeat what he omitted of the prayer in this way. As for the one who is ignorant of this, it is upon him to learn.

He said in *al-Akhbar*: The woman places her hands above her knees upon her thighs, so that she does not stoop too far and thereby raise her hindquarters.

He said in the book *Ta'wil al-Da'a'im*: Those behind the imam, when he says:

> سمع الله لمن حمده

God has heard the one who praised Him.

say quietly:

> ربنا لك الحمد

Our Lord, to You belongs all praise.

The exception is the one who relays for the imam when those praying behind him are many and one among them has been appointed to make them hear on his behalf. That man raises his voice with it and with the takbir, but he does not raise his voice with the tasbih.

He said in the book *Mukhtasar al-Musannaf*: Whoever performs the first tashahhud has not thereby been excused from the second, though the second suffices in place of the first. And he said in the book *Ta'wil al-Da'a'im*, concerning the outward sense, that when the one praying sits in the prayer he folds his left leg beneath him and keeps his right leg upright.

He said in the brief discourse on jurisprudence, whose questions come from the most eminent Shaykh Hasan ibn Mawla Adam al-Hindi and whose answers come from the sayyids of Yemen, may God sanctify all their souls: the prayer of a woman who has not undergone khafd is valid, and intimacy with her is permissible, and likewise for the man; but the sunnah is to hasten to that.

Our master and our lord Ja'far ibn Sulayman, may God exalt his sanctity and grant us his intercession and his company, said in his noble epistle, which he entitled *al-Sayf al-Aqta' fi Ibtal al-Bida'*, with which he counselled his people and roused from heedlessness those among them whose sleep had grown long, the people of the island of Yemen, after he had settled among them and taken up residence there. Section: And of this also is what has been known and witnessed of many among you, men and women alike, who cling to the mountain of loyalty and who come to the rope of obedience to God, glorified be He, by way of undertaking the obligation of the prayer, and yet their foreheads do not prostrate to God, glorified be He, with a single rak'ah of the prayer, so that the prayer is diminished of the rak'ahs that belong around it. This is among the gravest of all matters in its consequence, and the most notorious of them all in repute. Has there never reached the ears of such people the word of the Messenger of God (ع):

> بين العبد وبين الكفر ترك الصلاة

Between the servant and unbelief stands the abandonment of the prayer.

and the word of Amir al-Mu'minin (ع):

> لا حظ في الإسلام لمن ترك الصلاة

There is no portion in Islam for the one who abandons the prayer.

Since this is so, that there is no portion in Islam for the one who abandons the prayer, and that the abandonment of the prayer is the very thing that separates the servant from unbelief, then let not the one who abandons the prayer, while counting himself among the loyal, reckon that he is in the number of the Muslims, or that he holds of Islam so much as the parings of a fingernail. God, glorified be He, has said concerning this in the verses of His clear Book:

> ومن يبتع غير الإسلام ديناً فلن يقبل منه وهو في الآخرة من الخاسرين

And whoever seeks a religion other than Islam, it will never be accepted from him, and in the Hereafter he will be among the losers.

Those who abandon the prayer, among the people of this description whom we have seen, are of two kinds. One kind are people of tribal temper who turn away from the affairs of religion, people occupied with themselves and away from all that, the animal nature being dominant over them, as though they had sprung from the well. The other kind are people of arrogance and coarse insolence, especially the women among them. But there is no insolence and no arrogance against God, glorified be He, nor against His worship. Whoever does that has destroyed his own soul and has wronged it. As for his Creator, glorified be He, He has no need of his worship. Its benefit returns only to the worshipper himself, and to those like him, the learned among them and the lowly. God did not create them and require their service in His worship except that He might benefit them by it, as God, glorified and exalted be He, said concerning this:

> وما خلقت الجن والأنس الا ليعبدون

And I did not create the jinn and mankind except that they should worship Me.

There is no doubt that whoever walks that path has entered into the warning of the word of God, exalted be He, in the verses of His clear Book, where He says:

> إن الذين يستكبرون عن عبادتي سيدخلون جهنم داخرين

Indeed, those who are too proud to worship Me will enter Hell abased.

It was only in that very path that Iblis died, and he is in truth the dead one, for when the servant prostrates to his Lord, glorified be He, in his prayer, Iblis cries out, Woe to me, this one obeyed and I refused. As for the unbeliever, even though he does not pray the prayer of Islam, nor worship his Lord, glorified be He, with the worship of Him that is accepted, he still has within his own religion a worship by which he worships, handed down to him from his fathers. Not even the unbelievers of the lands of India are so arrogant toward the worship of God, glorified be He, with such arrogance, nor do they turn away from Him with such turning away, the male of them and the female, the whole of them and the parts, except for those among them who fall under the two kinds already described and who incline with them in what they are upon of misguidance and deviation. Those two kinds are likewise found in all the madhhabs of Islam, and likewise in the creeds of all religions. But such conduct does not befit one who has attached himself to the walaya of the friends of God, glorified be He, by its firm mountain, and who traces himself in his affairs to the people of piety and faith. No one has any claim of walaya to them except by holding fast to the hem of the prayer. Otherwise he is like one who proclaims upon himself in the Hereafter destruction and woe, and like the opponents of the guiding call. Even if there are among them, among their men and their women, some who are formed in that way, leave them and what they are formed in, for they are not granted success in their affairs, while you in your affairs, by the praise of God, glorified be He, are granted success. As for the one who acknowledges the call of the friend of God (ع), and the Garden and the Fire, and the punishment and the reckoning and the balance and the path and the gathering and the raising, his reason rules upon him that he hold fast to the worship of his Lord, glorified be He, through the prayer, which is the pillar of the religion and the support of Islam, and that he comply through it with the command. He who warns has given excuse, and he who has cautioned against the punishment of his Lord has cleared himself. So let those who would set things right set their souls right by this, and whoever fears God and guards himself against Him, those are the ones who prosper.

He said in the book *Ta'wil al-Shari'ah*: The obligations of the prayer are seven:

1. The first takbir
2. The recitation
3. The bowing
4. The prostration
5. The tasbih
6. The greeting
7. The taslim

And he said in it: When you have made the takbir, it is not permissible for you to move your foot, or to advance it or draw it back; rather you remain fixed. Nor are you able to speak from the opening takbir until the taslim, nor to move from your place.

And he said in it: The Prophet (ع) said:

> أُمُرتُ أن أسجد على سبعة أدوات

I have been commanded to prostrate upon seven members.

And he said in it: The Prophet (ع) said:

> لا تقرأ وأنت راكع ولا تحركوا الحصى في الصلاة ولا تلتفتوا التّغات الثعلب ولا تقعوا اقعاء القرد ولا تنقروا نقر الغراب

Do not recite while you are bowing, and do not disturb the pebbles during the prayer, and do not turn about with the turning of the fox, and do not squat with the squatting of the monkey, and do not peck with the pecking of the crow.

And he said in it: He separates the two rak'ahs with a sitting. And he said in it: Do not pray the obligatory prayer when the adhan is called; pray it when the iqama is given. And he said in it: The Zuhr prayer is four rak'ahs. And he said in it: It is related from the Prophet (ع) that he said:

> لا تطرقوا في الركوع إطراق الحمار والكبابه

Do not droop the head in the bowing with the drooping of the donkey and of the lowered beast.

and he commanded that the head not be dropped below the level of the back. And he said in it: The head and the back are level and aligned in the prayer. And he said in it: The Prophet (ع) said:

> من كثرت صلاته بالليل حسن وجهه بالنهار

Whoever prays much by night, his face is made beautiful by day.

The author of the guarded epistle which contains the protected secrets of the religion of God, and he is the most eminent and most noble Da'i Zayd ibn Muhammad ibn al-Fadl, may God exalt his sanctity and grant us his intercession and his company, said: The obligations of the prayer are seven, and they are:

1. The intention
2. The first takbir
3. The standing
4. The recitation
5. The bowing
6. The prostration
7. The taslim

And he said in it: Five prayers have been appointed for every day. And he said in it: The prayer has four limits, and they are:

1. The place
2. The time
3. The purity
4. The intention

And he said in it: The five prayers are seventeen rak'ahs. And he said in it: The prayer is of two kinds, obligation and sunnah. And he said in it: For that reason Muhammad (ع) said:

> تارك سنتي ملعون

The one who abandons my sunnah is cursed.

meaning the one who abandons my legatee is one banished from the mercy of God. And he said in it: The Messenger of God (ع) said:

> لا صلاة لمن لا يؤدي الزكاة

There is no prayer for the one who does not pay the zakat.

And he said in it: The movements of the prayer, the bowing and the prostration, are submission and humility. Then he said: For that reason the Messenger of God (ع) said:

> من لم تنه صلاته عن الفحشاء والمنكر لم يزدد من الله الاّ بعداً

Whoever is not restrained by his prayer from indecency and wrongdoing increases in nothing but distance from God.

And for that reason the people of Shu'ayb said to him:

> يا شعيب أصلواتك تأمرك أن نترك ما يعبد آبائنا

O Shu'ayb, does your prayer command you that we abandon what our fathers worshipped?

Then he said: because the outward prayer neither forbids nor commands.

Our master, the author of the *Majalis al-Mustansiriyya*, the peace of God be upon its author, said in the sixth of these councils: You have heard what we recited to you of the divisions of the obligations of purity and its sunnahs, corresponding to the number of the clauses of the shahada and its letters, since purity is one of the rights of the shahada. And among its rights also is the prayer. So listen now to what we recite to you of its meaning, namely that the prayers are seven obligatory prayers and twelve sunnah prayers. The obligatory are:

1. The prayer of residence, which is seventeen rak'ahs in every day and night
2. The prayer of travel, which is eleven rak'ahs in the day and night
3. The Friday prayer, which is not complete until four gather with the imam
4. The prayer of fear, part of which is prayed with the imam and the remainder of which is completed alone
5. The prayer of the circumambulation of the obligatory Hajj
6. The prayer of the circumambulation of the obligatory Umrah
7. The prayer over the bier, which is an obligation upon those Muslims who know of it, until one of them undertakes it, whereupon the rest become volunteers in it

And the sunnah prayers are:

1. The prayer of the Feast of the Breaking of the Fast
2. The prayer of the Feast of the Sacrifice
3. The prayer of the eclipse of the sun
4. The prayer of the eclipse of the moon
5. The prayer for rain
6. The prayer of the earthquake
7. The prayer for events that occur as signs
8. The prayer of the shaf' and the witr
9. The prayer of the watira, which is two rak'ahs performed sitting
10. The prayer of the two rak'ahs of the dawn, for the Prophet (ع) made them up after the rising of the sun and made up the obligatory prayer after them
11. Twenty-eight rak'ahs in every day and night, among them the prayer of the oft-returning, which is six rak'ahs after the sun's decline; and among them eight rak'ahs between Zuhr and Asr; and among them ten rak'ahs between Maghrib and the later Isha; and among them four rak'ahs after the later Isha, which is the prayer of the night
12. The prayer of greeting the mosque upon entering it

Then he said, may the good pleasure of God be upon him, in the seventh of these councils: So listen now to what we recite to you, that in each prayer of the five prayers there are seven obligations and twelve sunnahs. The seven obligations are:

1. The intention
2. Turning toward the qibla
3. Then the takbirat al-ihram
4. The recitation of the Opening of the Book
5. The bowing
6. The prostration
7. The sitting for the salutation

And the twelve sunnahs are:

1. The adhan
2. The iqama
3. Saying, I have turned my face to Him who created the heavens and the earth, as one of pure faith, to the end of the words
4. Seeking refuge from the accursed Satan
5. Reciting a surah after the Opening of the Book in the first two rak'ahs of every obligatory prayer
6. Raising the hands at every rising from the bowing and every descent into it
7. The tasbih during the bowing
8. The imam's saying, God has heard the one who praised Him, and the follower's saying, Our Lord, to You belongs all praise
9. The tasbih during the prostration
10. The first sitting and the first tashahhud
11. The second tashahhud
12. Every prayer in which there is no first sitting has a qunut that stands in its place in the count, and every prayer in which there are two sittings for the tashahhud has no qunut in it

Our lord Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) said in his Four Hundred Etiquettes:

> لا يجمع المؤمن يديه في الصلاة وهو قائم يشبه بأهل الكتاب

The believer does not clasp his hands together in the prayer while standing, resembling thereby the People of the Book.

And he said in it:

> إذا أصاب أحدكم في الصلاة الدابة فليدفنها أو ليستر عليها أو ليضعها في ثوبه حتى ينصرف

If one of you comes upon a crawling creature during the prayer, let him bury it, or cover it over, or place it in his garment until he has finished.

And he said in it:

> الالتفات الفاحش يقطع الصلاة فمن فعله فعليه الابتداء . الصلاة بالأذان والإقامة والتكبير

Gross turning about cuts off the prayer, and whoever does it must begin again: the prayer with the adhan, the iqama, and the takbir.

And he said in it:

> أعطوا كل سورة حقها من الركوع والسجود إذا كنتم في الصلاة

Give every surah its due of bowing and prostration when you are in the prayer.

And he said in it:

> أجلسوا بين السجدتين حتى تسكن جوارحكم وقوموا فان ذلك من فعلنا

Sit between the two prostrations until your limbs are still, and then rise, for that is of our practice.

And he said in it:

> ليرفع الساجد مؤخره في الصلاة إذا سجد

Let the one prostrating raise his hinder part in the prayer when he prostrates.

And he said in it:

> إذا صليت وحدك فاسمع نفسك القراءة والتسبيح والتكبير وإذا انقّلت عن الصلاة فعن يمينك

When you pray alone, make yourself hear the recitation and the tasbih and the takbir; and when you turn away from the prayer, turn to your right.

A question was raised by the head of the island of India, Muhammad Ali ibn Salih al-Badri, may God preserve him, and answered by the lord and master, our master Hibat Allah, may God prolong his life. This is its text and its substance. The second question: What does our master, may God perpetuate his support, say concerning what a person recites inadvertently in the obligatory prayer of Surat al-Falaq and Surat al-Nas? Does he complete the prayer, or does he repeat it? And if it is permissible, what is the ruling concerning one who recited Surat al-Nas in the first rak'ah? Does he repeat it in the second rak'ah, or does he recite the one before it, beginning from its opening, or does he repeat the prayer? And if it is not permissible, what of one who recited Surat al-Ikhlas in the first rak'ah? Does he repeat it in the second, or does he recite the one above it, beginning from its opening? May our master, may God perpetuate his exaltation, favor us with a satisfying answer.

Answer: From God we seek help for success and right direction, and from His friend and his full moon we ask rain from the clouds of confirmation. No text has come from the friends of God forbidding the recitation of the two surahs of refuge in the obligatory prayer. So if the one praying recites them, his prayer is complete. If he recited Surat al-Nas in the first rak'ah, he repeats it in the last. Whoever recited Surat al-Ikhlas in the first recites al-Falaq in the second. And one should not deliberately recite in this manner in the obligatory prayer, placing it in the position of the sunnah.
