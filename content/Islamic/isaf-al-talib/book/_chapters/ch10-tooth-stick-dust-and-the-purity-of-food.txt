# Tooth-Stick, Dust, and the Purity of Food

I turn now to the mention of the tooth-stick (السواك).

I record what he stated in the book of questions and answers mentioned above, which one of its compilers arranged according to the order of the chapters of the book دعائم الإسلام. Question: What do you say, O you at whose words our souls find rest, concerning the custom of most of the people of India, that after finishing with the siwak they take a split piece of bamboo cane or something similar and scrape their tongues with it to remove what has gathered there? Is this permissible or not? Answer: The siwak is the mainstay, and there is nothing against them if they do that.

From another place: one should not clean between the teeth with a stalk that has a knot in it, taken from dried grass. From another: it is disliked for anyone to use the siwak while in a state of janaba, that is, while in the condition of major ritual impurity.

From the book شرح الأخبار: الريحان is every plant of fragrant scent, and they singled out the myrtle (الآس) for the name. From the book تأويل الدعائم: الريحان in the language means the tips of every sweet-scented plant when its blossom comes forth, and الريحان is a comprehensive name for all fragrant plants; but most often they single out the myrtle for it, since it is the most lasting of them and the strongest.

The Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his four hundred points of etiquette:

> السواك مرضاة للرب عز وجل ومطيبة للفم وهو من السّنّة

The tooth-stick is a means of pleasing the Lord, mighty and majestic, it sweetens the mouth, and it belongs to the established practice.

I turn now to the mention of dry purification (التيمم), again from the book of questions and answers mentioned above, arranged by one of its compilers according to the order of the chapters of the book دعائم الإسلام.

Question: What do you say, O best of those who have urged us to sit in the company of the learned, concerning smooth rock set firm in the ground: may one perform tayammum upon it even if there is no dust on it, when no soil is to be found? And if tayammum from it is not valid unless there is dust upon it, what then is the difference between it and the rest of the stones? Answer: If there is dust upon it, he performs tayammum; if there is no dust upon it, he does not.

Question: What does our master say concerning a man who goes out from a city to a place short of the distance that counts as travel, and the time of prayer comes upon him on the road, and he finds no water: may he perform tayammum? Answer: Tayammum is not permitted to him while he is settled, except from some cause. But if he is on a journey, far from water, and cannot perform the ablution before the time lapses, or if he fears for his person or his property should he go to the water, he performs tayammum.

Question: Concerning a man who needs the full washing (الغسل) during a night of Ramadan, and forgets it until he has set out on a journey: does tayammum suffice him for prayer and for fasting, or not? Answer: If he forgets the washing until he travels and finds no water, tayammum suffices him for the prayer and for the fast.

Question: Concerning a man who dies on a journey and water cannot be had? Answer: In that case he is given tayammum. From another: whoever is on a journey and the time comes while water is present, but fears illness for himself if he performs the ablution, performs tayammum and prays.

Question: Concerning a traveler who has with him only a little water, enough to wash the limbs or enough to cleanse himself after relieving himself: with which does he begin? And if he does not cleanse himself, does the washing suffice him in place of tayammum or not? Answer: Our master al-Nu'man said in the book of purity: if he cleanses himself with stones or with something else and removes what is there, he performs the ablution, since that is from a cause, and the absence of water is a cause, and there is no call for tayammum.

Question: Concerning a man who is settled in town and has an ailment in his private part that prevents him from cleansing himself: if he washes the limbs, is tayammum then obligatory upon him after that or not? Answer: This has already been covered in the answer to the first question.

Question: Concerning the traveler who prays the obligatory prayers with tayammum before their times lapse: if the times pass him by, must he make up the prayer with tayammum or not? Answer: When there is no water, prayer with tayammum is permitted, both the prayer performed in its time and the one made up.

From another: the traveler's wife, if she menstruates and then becomes pure and finds no water, performs tayammum and prays, and it becomes lawful for her husband to have intercourse with her. If a company are traveling and have an imam among them, and he becomes junub, and they have with them only water enough for one man's ablution, their imam performs tayammum and leads in prayer those of them who have performed tayammum. If a man is on a journey and has with him only water enough for his ablution, and there is impurity on his garment, let him wash his garment with that water, then perform tayammum and pray. If a man becomes junub on a journey and the noon prayer comes, and he has water enough for the ablution but not enough for the full washing, he performs tayammum and prays and does not perform the ablution; then when the afternoon prayer comes and that water is still with him, he performs the ablution with it and does not perform tayammum, because he is now pure from janaba. If a deceased person and one in janaba come together and there is only water enough for a single washing, the one in janaba washes himself and the deceased is given tayammum. Whoever travels with a Christian or Zoroastrian wife, and she menstruates on the road and then becomes pure and finds no water, performs tayammum, and the husband teaches her the tayammum, and then he may go in to her.

The same rulings are stated in the book مختصر المصنف, with these further points: if water is found after the tayammum, the tayammum is broken and the man returns to the state of janaba; so if he has not washed and the sunset prayer comes and he has with him only water enough for the ablution and no more, he performs tayammum again; then when the final evening prayer comes and that water is still with him, he performs the ablution with it, since he is by then pure. If an unbeliever accepts Islam and finds no water, he performs tayammum and prays. It is recommended for a man who sleeps in the mosque of the Prophet and is touched by impurity and awakens, that he perform tayammum, and that he not pass through the mosque except having performed tayammum, until he leaves it; and likewise within the sanctuary. If a deceased person and one in janaba come together and there is only water enough for a single washing, the one in janaba washes and the deceased is given tayammum. As for the invalid who cannot perform tayammum himself, a sound person performs it for him.

From the same book: a man in a state of minor impurity, while settled in the city, performs tayammum for the prayer over the dead if he fears that it will pass him by. And whoever prays with tayammum while there is water in his baggage that he has forgotten, if he remembers and there is time to spare, he performs the ablution and prays; and if the time has gone out, what he did suffices him and the prayer he offered with it stands.

He said in المنتخبة:

> وكل من لم يجد التراب
> فلينفض اللبود والثياب
> حتى إذا لم يرى شيئا طار
> وليتيمم ذلك الغبار

Whoever finds no earth, let him beat out the felts and the garments; and then, even if he sees nothing fly up, let him take that dust for his dry purification.

He said in the book تأويل الدعائم, concerning the case of a man seized in a crush who cannot get free of it, meaning cannot get to the water, and the prayer comes upon him: he performs tayammum and prays, and repeats that prayer, meaning when he is able to reach the water and purify himself with it.

He said in the book of questions and answers mentioned above: if a woman becomes pure from menstrual blood in the month of Ramadan before sunset and fears harm from the full washing, it is permitted to her to perform tayammum and to fast, and she does not make up that day. Likewise a man who has an emission in his sleep during the month of Ramadan: if he fears illness for himself from washing at night, he performs tayammum and fasts, and he does not make up that fast.

He said in a book containing legal questions, all of them marvels, another question: What do you say concerning two men who performed tayammum and prayed in one place, because they found no water, and the prayer of one of them was valid while the prayer of the other was not? Answer: The case is that one man said to his companion, teach me the tayammum, and the other set about teaching him and striking the earth for him, and they both prayed. The prayer of the one who was taught was valid, and the prayer of the teacher was not, because he did not form the intention for himself.

I turn now to the mention of the purity of foods and drinks, from the book of questions and answers mentioned above, arranged by one of its compilers according to the order of the chapters of the book دعائم الإسلام.

Question: What do you say, O you whom God has made a stronghold at whose door even the accursed may hope for refuge, concerning the oil of the neem (النيم)? People know that most of the fruit of the neem is eaten by nothing but crows, so that the stones pass out from them with their droppings and dry, and people gather them and sell them, and the pressers buy them and press them and sell the bitter oil that comes out, and no trace of impurity is seen in it. Is it permitted to sell it, to buy it, to burn it for light, and to touch it, or not? Answer: Whatever is not lawful to eat, its dung is impure; it is not lawful to eat it, nor to sell it, nor to buy it.

Question: What do you say, O best of those who have given us sight from the stored treasure of his knowledge, of what our eyes were veiled from, concerning what has come in the book of purity, that there is no harm in the leavings of a menstruating woman, a Jew, a Christian, the beasts of prey, and the riding animals, while it has also come therein that what a dog has lapped from is impure? How are the two reports to be reconciled? Answer: If there is moisture, it has been defiled and it is impure, and there is no harm in selling it nor in buying it, as with the hide of carrion, which it is not lawful to sell; and the primary ruling concerns water that is above the limit.

Question: What does our master, the possessor of all, say concerning honey, clarified butter, olive oil, and sesame oil in which a mouse is found? Is that to be fed to the beasts or buried in the ground? And if soap is made from it, may it be sold in the market and elsewhere, or is it to be given to people without price? Answer: Any liquid in which an animal having flowing blood has died is not to be eaten, and it is not fitting that an animal that is lawful to eat be fed what is forbidden. There is no harm in making soap from it, but it is not to be sold unless the buyer is informed of its corruption; and a garment washed with the impure soap is to be rinsed with pure water after it.

From the book of reports: it is fed to dogs and to beasts whose flesh is not eaten. And from it: if a dead mouse is found in a cooking pot, the broth is poured away and the meat is washed and eaten. From another: food from which a single grain of mouse droppings is taken out is eaten, and likewise if there are two; but if three grains of droppings come out of it, it is not permitted as food for a human being, nor for an animal whose milk is drunk.

Question: What do you say concerning betel that is chewed with a small amount of lime (النورة) put into it? Is that permitted, and may it be eaten or not? Answer: If the lime is a small thing that does no harm to a person, there is no objection to eating it along with the rest. So too there may be in drinking water a small amount of earth, and that is not forbidden unless it is gross and would harm the one who takes it. God and His friends know best.

Question: Concerning the clarified butter, sesame oil, sugar, honey, and other such foodstuffs held by the unbelievers, which they sell in the markets of the Muslims: is it lawful to eat them or not? Answer: And from God is the granting of success. The slaughtered animals of the unbelievers were forbidden only because they do not pronounce the name of God over them and do not turn them toward the qibla. As for the food and the moist provisions they possess: what they have touched, they have defiled; and what they have not touched with their hands and their impure implements, there is no harm in its being sold and bought and eaten and used as a relish. Yet their handiwork is hardly ever free of this, since they are impure and they handle their implements while these are wet. So the obligation is to avoid what they have prepared if other than it is to be found; and if there is need of it, then nothing is forbidden except their slaughtered animals, and their moisture is impure. And if something falls upon a garment from the relish prepared by the people of association, or from moist food, and it is known that they defiled it, then whatever of that is on the garment or on the body of a person is washed.

From another: if the leavings of a Jew, a Christian, a Magian, or a cat fall into a small vessel, purification with it remains valid so long as no saliva is apparent.

He said in the questions of Sheikh Hasan ibn Nuh to our master Husayn ibn Idris, and his answers to him: as for the slaughtered animals of the People of the Book, if a Muslim has observed that they turned them toward the qibla and pronounced the name of God over them, they are eaten. As for the rest of their food, it is lawful by the text of the Noble Book; but if they have touched it with their moisture, it becomes impure. And God knows best.

He said in the brief legal discourse whose questions are from Sheikh Hasan al-Hindi and whose answers are from the masters of Yemen: eating the cooking of a serving girl who is not careful about ritual purity is permitted.
