# The Eight Levels of Faith

I turn now to the mention of faith as it is set down in the Book of Guardianship (كتاب الولاية).

I find it stated in some of the noble books:

> اعلم يا أخي إن الإيمان ثمان مراتب

"Know, my brother, that faith has eight levels."

The first of those levels is named in these words:

> أولها الإحسان بالوالدين ، وبذي القربى ، واليتامى ، والمساكين ، والجار ذي القربى ، والجار الجنب ، والصاحب ذي الجنب ، وابن السبيل وما ملكت أيمانكم

"The first of them is charity toward parents, toward relatives, toward orphans and toward the needy, toward the neighbor who is a relative and the neighbor who is nearby, toward the companion at one's side, toward the traveler, and toward those whom your faith obligates you to care for."

This is established in the Book of God, the Exalted.

He then said:

> وليس من تقلد عبادة الله ودعوة أهل البيت عليهم السلام بغير علمٍ ولا عمل يقع عليه اسم الإيمان ولا له عند الله ذمام ولا عند وليه عليه السلام عهد ولا إكرام لأنه قد خرج منه

"Whoever takes upon himself the worship of God and the call of the People of the House (عليهم السلام) without knowledge and without action does not have the name of faith fall upon him. He has no bond with God, and with His guardian (ع) he has neither covenant nor honor, for he has gone out from it."

I turn now to the distinction between faith and Islam, to what falls under each of them, and to a mention of what lies beyond that.

Our lord and master, the trust of the Imam, the banner of Islam, may God exalt his sanctity and grant us his intercession and his company, said in his noble councils, which are designated the Councils of Exposition and the Councils of Wisdom, known as the Mustanṣiriyya Councils, may the peace of God be upon their author, in the fourth of those councils:

> وقد تقدم القول إن أئمة الدين عليهم السلام سبعة يترددون في العالم كأيام الجمعة إلى حين قيام القائم والحجج الاثنى عشر منبثة في العالم فهذه معرفة جملة حدودها

"It has already been said that the Imams of religion (عليهم السلام) are seven, recurring in the world as the days of the week recur, until the rising of the one who will arise, while the twelve proofs are spread throughout the world. This is the knowledge of the sum of its limits."

By this he meant, may God exalt his sanctity, the limits of the testimony. As for the rendering of the rights owed to those limits, it consists of standing by seven obligations of religion and twelve practices binding upon the God-fearing.

The seven obligations are these:

1. Guardianship (الولاية)
2. Purity (الطهارة)
3. Prayer (الصلاة)
4. Almsgiving (الزكاة)
5. Fasting (الصوم)
6. Pilgrimage (الحج)
7. Struggle (الجهاد)

The twelve practices are these:

1. Honoring parents
2. Maintaining the ties of kinship
3. Protecting the neighbor
4. Enjoining what is good
5. Forbidding what is wrong
6. Truthfulness in one's home
7. Good conduct with one's spouse
8. Kindness to those in one's care
9. Spreading the greeting of peace
10. Feeding others
11. Keeping the bond among the brethren
12. Visiting the sick

These twelve are not obligatory upon women.

Our master the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع), said in his four hundred precepts:

> افترق بنو إسرائيل على اثنتين وسبعين فرقة وستفترق هذه الأمة على ثلاث وسبعين فرقة واحدة في الجنة وسايرها في النار

"The Children of Israel divided into seventy-two sects, and this community will divide into seventy-three sects. One of them is in the Garden, and the rest of them are in the Fire."
