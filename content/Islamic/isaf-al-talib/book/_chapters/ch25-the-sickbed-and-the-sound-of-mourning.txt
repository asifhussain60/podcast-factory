# The Sickbed and the Sound of Mourning

I turn now to the book of funerals, to what is taught concerning the sick person, the visits paid to him, and the state of dying itself. I find it written in the book of the interpretation of the pillars that the dying state is the presence of death and the nearness of the transfer from this world to the hereafter.

In the book of questions and answers, whose questions were put by the eminent Shaykh Yūsuf ibn Ismāʿīl al-Sāmiṭī al-Hindī and whose answers were given by our master Idrīs ibn Ḥasan, may Allah sanctify him, I read the following exchange.

Question: What do you say concerning people who move from one place to another out of fear of epidemic and plague, believing that it will not reach them? Is that permitted or not?

Answer:

> يتوجهون حيث يشاءون والأمر بيد الله تعالى

They may go wherever they wish, and the matter is in the hand of Allah, exalted be He.

I turn next to what our master and mawlānā Jaʿfar ibn Sayyidinā Sulaymān, may Allah sanctify him and grant us his intercession and his nearness, set down in his noble epistle which he named The Sharpest Sword in the Nullifying of Innovations, written to counsel his people and to rouse those among them who had grown heedless and slept too long, among the people of the island of Yemen, after he had settled and dwelt among them. In one section of it he says that it has been reported of some of you that you are slow in visiting the sick, though the reward for it has its established place in the noble book of the pillars of Islam, where it is recorded on the authority of your mawlā the Commander of the Faithful (ع) that he heard the Messenger of Allah say:

> ما من عبد مسلم يعود مريضا إلا صلى عليه سبعون ألف ملك من ساعته التي يعود فيها إن كان نهارا حتى تغرب الشمس أو ليلاً حتى تطلع

There is no Muslim servant who visits a sick person but that seventy thousand angels pray for him from the hour in which he makes the visit, if it is by day until the sun sets, and if by night until it rises.

And it is recorded there also, from the Commander of the Faithful (ع), that he said:

> نهى رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله أن يأكل العائد عند العليل فيحبط الله عيادته

The Messenger of Allah forbade that the visitor eat in the presence of the sick person, for Allah then brings his visit to nothing.

In another narration the wording is that Allah brings his reward to nothing, and indeed casts it behind him. The reason is that the visitor's eating and drinking in the sick man's house presses upon the sick man in the very condition that already presses upon him, and harms his household as well. He may be of slender means, so that he and his family are inwardly galled by it. Whoever knows this must make it known to the one who does not, for not all people are learned, and the learned among them are few and select. The one who warns has given his excuse. The truth has come and falsehood has perished.

Our mawlānā the Commander of the Faithful ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع) said in his four hundred points of conduct:

> ولا ينامنّ أحدكم على الحجة ولا على وجهه فمن رأيتموه نام على وجهه فانتبهوه ولا تنام المرأة مستلقية على ظهرها

Let none of you sleep in that posture, nor upon his face. Whomever you see sleeping upon his face, wake him. And let a woman not sleep stretched out upon her back.

And he said in it:

> أسأل الله العافية من جهد البلاء فان جهد البلاء ذهاب للدين

I ask Allah for well-being from the severity of affliction, for the severity of affliction is the loss of religion.

I come now to the command to remember death, and to what is joined with it. In the same book of questions and answers, which its arranger ordered according to the chapters of the book of the pillars of Islam, I read the following.

Question: What do you say concerning the lifespans of people? Is it to be held that they are fixed with Allah, glorified be He, neither increasing nor decreasing? And if that is so, what is the meaning of what has come in the books, that a lifespan is increased and decreased?

Answer:

> أنها أعمار مؤجلة لا تزيد ولا تنقص وأما معنى الزيادة والنقصان فهو صحيح بمعنى أن الذي ينقص في الدور الماضي يزيد في المستقبل فيه

They are appointed lifespans that neither increase nor decrease. As for the meaning of increase and decrease, it is sound in the sense that what is diminished in the cycle now past is added in the cycle to come.

Question: What do you say concerning his saying (ع):

> الشقي كل الشقي من أدركته الساعة حياً لم يمت

The wretched, wholly wretched, is the one whom the Hour overtakes while he is alive and has not died.

Is what is meant that the most wretched of the wretched is the one whose death comes upon him while he is still in the condition of sensory, animal life and has not yet passed over into the angelic life, or is something else intended?

Answer:

> إن قصده إذا أتته الساعة وهو في حال البهيمية

What is intended is that the Hour comes upon him while he is in the animal condition.

And in the questions of the eminent Shaykh Ḥasan ibn Nūḥ to our master Ḥusayn ibn Idrīs, may Allah sanctify his soul, together with his answers to them, the tenth question is this: concerning a woman who dies while pregnant, with a fully formed living child in her womb, is the child to be left in her womb or taken out?

Answer:

> إن حضرت المرأة امرأة تحسن إخراجه فهو الواجب إذا قدر أو في ستة أشهر فما فوقها ويرجى له الحياة وان لم فلا تقبر أمه حتى يتحقق موته والله أعلم

If there is present with the woman another woman skilled in delivering the child, that is what must be done, if it is possible, or if the child is of six months or more and life is hoped for it. If not, then the mother is not to be buried until his death is confirmed. And Allah knows best.

Our mawlānā the Commander of the Faithful ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع) said in his four hundred points of conduct:

> تمسكوا بما أمركم الله تعالى فما بين أحدكم وبين أن يغتبط ويرى ما يحب إلا أن يحضره رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وما عند الله خير وأبقى وتأتيه البشارة والله لتقرنّ عينه وليحينّ لقاء الله

Hold fast to what Allah, exalted be He, has commanded you. There is nothing between one of you and his rejoicing and seeing what he loves except that the Messenger of Allah should be present with him, and what is with Allah is better and more lasting, and the glad tidings come to him. By Allah, his eye shall be gladdened, and the meeting with Allah shall surely draw near.

And he said:

> أكثروا ذكر الموت يوم خروجكم من عند القبور ويوم قيامكم بين يدي الله تهن عليكم المصايب

Remember death often, and the day of your coming forth from the graves, and the day of your standing before Allah, and calamities will sit lightly upon you.

And he said in it:

> الحمى رايد الموت وسجن الله في الأرض يحبس به من يشاء من عباده وهو يحت الذنوب كما يحت الوبر من مسام البعير

Fever is the forerunner of death and the prison of Allah in the earth, by which He confines whom He wills of His servants, and it strips away sins as hair is stripped from the pores of the camel.

And he said in it:

> ما من شيعتنا أحد يفارق أمراً نهينا عنه فيموت حتى يبتلى ببلية تمحص ذنوبه أما في ماله أو ولده أو في نفسه حتى يلقى الله بمحبّنا وما له من ذنب وأنه لا يبقى عليه شيء من ذنوبه فيشدّد عليه عند موته ويمحص من ذنوبه والميّت من شيعتنا صديق شهيد صدّق بأمرنا وأحب فينا وأبغض فينا

There is no one among our followers who departs from a matter we have forbidden and then dies until he is tried with a trial that purges his sins, whether in his wealth, or his child, or his own person, so that he meets Allah loving us with no sin upon him. Nothing of his sins remains upon him, so it is made hard upon him at his death and his sins are purged. And the dead among our followers is truthful and a martyr, who held our cause to be true, and loved for our sake, and hated for our sake.

I come now to condolences and patience, to the weeping that is permitted and the wailing that is forbidden, and to what belongs with this.

It is said in the abridged compendium that no hair is to be shaved over the dead, no lamp lit for him, and no nail cut for him. And in the book of questions and answers already mentioned, arranged according to the chapters of the book of the pillars of Islam, I read this.

Question: What do you say concerning some people who place weapons beside the dead if the body passes the night in the house with them, and others who hold that sitting beside the body is a binding obligation? How is that?

Answer:

> مثل هذا لا معول عليه

There is no reliance to be placed upon anything of this kind.

Our master and mawlānā Jaʿfar ibn Sayyidinā Sulaymān, may Allah sanctify their souls in the chambers of the gardens, treated this in his noble epistle named The Sharpest Sword in the Nullifying of Innovations, by which he counselled his people and roused those among them who had grown heedless and slept too long, among the people of the island of Yemen, after he had settled and dwelt among them. In one section of it he writes that among these innovations is what has become known of some of you, namely the wailing over the dead, with all the innovations it carries: a company of women gathering from their several places to lament the dead through nights and days on end, as though it were a prescription from your Lord, glorified be He, appointed at fixed times, together with singing and all that follows it of diversion and play carried past the limit within which it is permitted on the days of weddings and banquets, where its purpose is to distinguish fornication from lawful marriage, and carried on into the region of what is prohibited and forbidden and not permitted at all. Even if your adversaries who are outside this noble creed have a full share of such things, you are not free of it either, and it disgraces you before Allah, glorified be He, and before His friends (عليهم السلام), and it brings you, may Allah protect you, to destruction in your return to Him. And you are the cream of this whole world and its kernel. What then is the matter with you, that you do not restrain yourselves from this fault, nor guard yourselves against falling into the pit? May Allah shield you from it from this hour onward, and from returning to its like, if you are believers.

The one among you who reads has read in the noble book of the pillars of Islam the words of its author, may Allah sanctify his soul, in the narration whose soundness he intended and sought, from Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad, the blessings of Allah be upon him, who made this bequest when death came upon him:

> لا يلطمنّ عليّ خدّ ولا يشقنّ عليّ جيب فما من امرأة تشق جيبها إلا لها في جهنم صدع كلما زادت زيدت

Let no cheek be struck for me and no collar be torn for me. There is no woman who tears her collar but that she has a rent in Hell, and whenever she increases it is increased for her.

And he records in it also, from ʿAlī, the blessings of Allah be upon him, that he wrote to Rifāʿah ibn Shaddād, his judge over al-Ahwāz:

> وإياك والنوح على الميت ببلد يكون لك به سلطان

Beware of wailing over the dead in a land in which you hold authority.

And he records in it also, with a meaning that is plain, from the Messenger of Allah, that he said:

> صوتان ملعونان يبغضهما الله اعوال عند مصيبة وصوت عند نعمة

Two voices are accursed, hated by Allah: a howling at a calamity and a voice at a blessing.

That is to say, wailing and singing. Whoever wails over his dead brings upon him the causes of affliction by which his soul is punished, according to what has come from the Prophet in explanation of this. That is what the noble book already named has spoken of, in the account of ʿAbdullāh ibn Rawāḥah. The Prophet went to him in an illness of his in order to visit him, and found him weighed down by pain and fallen unconscious, so that he understood nothing, while the women were shrieking around him. The Messenger of Allah called him three times and he did not answer, so he prayed for him with the prayer he made to his Lord, glorified be He, and turned away from him. Afterward ʿAbdullāh found a lightness in himself and came out of his unconsciousness, and the Prophet was told of it. So he came and stood over him and said to him:

> يا عبد الله حدّث بما رأيت فانك رأيت عجباً

O ʿAbdullāh, tell what you saw, for you have seen something wondrous.

He said:

> يا رسول الله رأيت ملكاً من الملائكة بيده مقمعة من حديد تأجج ناراً وكلما صرخت صارخة يا جبلاه أهوى بها لهامتي وقال أنت جبلها فأقول لا بل الله فيكفّ بعد أهوائها فإذا صرخت صارخة يا عزّاه أهوى بها لهامتي وقال أنت عزها فأقول لا بل الله فيكف بعد أهوائها

O Messenger of Allah, I saw an angel with an iron mace in his hand blazing with fire, and whenever a woman shrieked, "O my mountain," he brought it down toward the crown of my head and said, "Are you her mountain?" and I would say, "No, rather Allah," and he would hold back after bringing it down. And when a woman shrieked, "O my strength," he brought it down toward the crown of my head and said, "Are you her strength?" and I would say, "No, rather Allah," and he would hold back after bringing it down.

The Messenger of Allah said:

> صدق عبد الله فما بال موتاكم يبتلون بقول أحيائكم

ʿAbdullāh has spoken the truth. What is the matter with your dead, that they are afflicted by the speech of your living?

Is there not in this, and in less than this, sufficiency enough for your living? The learned among you has come to know it, and its marks and landmarks have been made clear to him, and yet he walks in a forbidden matter along a path that earns displeasure, with his own ground left unguarded. And he who hides it from the one in authority over him, keeping it within his house among his household, has broken the covenant of Allah which he took upon himself and has gone back from his knowledge to his ignorance.

The noble book already named has made clear what is permitted in this and what is not, in its noble sayings, in which it says to the one who conforms to them that he has attained. From ʿAlī, the blessings of Allah be upon him, that he said:

> بكى رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله عند موت بعض ولده فقيل له يا رسول الله تبكي وتنهانا عن البكاء فقال لم أنهكم عن البكاء وانما نهيتكم عن النوح والعويل وانما هذه رقة ورحمة يجعلها الله تبارك وتعالى في قلب من يشاء من خلقه ويرحم الله من يشاء وإنما يرحم الله من عباده الرحماء

The Messenger of Allah wept at the death of one of his children, and it was said to him, "O Messenger of Allah, you weep, yet you forbid us to weep?" He said, "I did not forbid you weeping. I forbade you wailing and lamentation. This is only tenderness and mercy which Allah, blessed and exalted, places in the heart of whom He wills of His creation, and Allah shows mercy to whom He wills, and Allah shows mercy to the merciful among His servants."

And from him (ع) that he said:

> رخص رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله في البكاء عند المصيبة وقال النفس مصابة والعين دامعة والعهد قريب وقولوا ما أرضى الله ولا تقولوا الهجر

The Messenger of Allah allowed weeping at a calamity and said, "The soul is stricken, the eye sheds tears, and the parting is recent. Say what pleases Allah, and do not speak foul words."

Upon this, then, let your practice be, in what you keep hidden and in what you make open. As for the accursed voice at a blessing, which is singing, the noble book already named has likewise made clear what is allowed of it at banquets and weddings, so that by it fornication may be distinguished from lawful marriage, and what is permitted from what is not. These are the words of its author, whose pen has already dried upon them, may Allah sanctify his soul, narrating from the Messenger of Allah that he passed by the Banū Zurayq and heard music, and said, "What is this?" They said, "O Messenger of Allah, so and so has married." He said:

> كمل دينه هذا النكاح لا السفاح ولا يكون نكاح في السر حتّى يُرى دخان أو يسمع حسّ دفّ

His religion is complete. This is lawful marriage, not fornication. There is no marriage in secret, until smoke is seen or the sound of a tambourine is heard.

And he said:

> الفرق ما بين النكاح والسفاح ضرب الدف

What distinguishes lawful marriage from fornication is the beating of the tambourine.

This, then, is what the believer who acts should act upon, in this matter and in that. Whoever oversteps it into something else, his portion in all his affairs is ruin.

> وأن في ذلك لذكرى لمن كان له قلب أو ألقى السمع وهو شهيد ومن عمل صالحاً فلنفسه ومن أساء فعليها وما ربك بظلام للعبيد

In that is a reminder for whoever has a heart, or gives ear while he is present in mind. Whoever does righteousness, it is for himself, and whoever does evil, it is against himself, and your Lord is not unjust to the servants.

And our mawlānā the Commander of the Faithful ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع) said:

> وأمروا أهاليكم بالقول الحسن عند موتاكم فان فاطمة بنت محمد صلى الله عليها لما قبض أبوها صلى الله عليه وآله أشعرها بنات هاشم فقالت أكثروا التحداد وعليكم بالدعاء

Command your households to speak well over your dead, for when Fāṭimah, the daughter of Muḥammad, lost her father, the daughters of Hāshim raised the lament with her, and she said, "Keep much to restrained mourning, and hold fast to supplication."

And he said in it:

> من ضرب على فخذه عند المصيبة فقد حبط أجره

Whoever strikes his thigh at a calamity, his reward has come to nothing.
