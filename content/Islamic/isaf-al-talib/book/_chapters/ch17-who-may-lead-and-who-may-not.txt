# Who May Lead, and Who May Not

I come now to the mention of the imamate.

I begin with the book of questions and answers I named at the outset, which its compiler arranged according to the order of the chapters of دعائم الإسلام.

Question: What do you say concerning an imam who prays with the people in the arch of the qibla while they stand behind him in the public mosque, since the common people dislike the imam's prayer in the arch, though not his prostration in it? Answer: There is no harm in praying and prostrating in it.

Question: What do you say concerning a traveler who follows a resident in a four-cycle prayer and prays two cycles with him? Is it better for him to give the greeting of peace and depart, or to remain until he gives the greeting together with the imam when the imam gives it at the end of the cycles? Answer: It is better for him to remain until he gives the greeting together with the imam.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the Shariah forbidding prayer behind the محدود? O our master, what is meant here by the حد and by the one so described? Answer: The محدود is one upon whom the prescribed penalty has been carried out.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the Shariah, that the one in fetters does not lead those who are free of them? O our master, is what is meant here every kind of bond and shackle placed upon him, or only those upon the legs? Answer: What is meant is bonds and shackles upon the legs and elsewhere.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the Shariah, that there is no harm in praying behind a slave if he is learned in jurisprudence and there is none present more learned than he to lead his people? O our master, is what is meant by his people those like him among the slaves, or all who are below him in rank? Answer: He leads whoever is below him in rank, even if they are free men.

Question: What do you say concerning one granted licence who prays with the people while some of the common people are with him, and who therefore omits saying ربنا لك الحمد out of taqiyya? Does a deficiency enter his prayer or not? Answer: There is no harm in it for the sake of taqiyya, but he should not omit it for any cause other than taqiyya. Rather, let him use it as the pure friends of God used it.

Question: What do you say concerning a ship, broad and long and wide, in which an imam prays the noon prayer with the people in one part of it, and then another imam prays that same prayer in another part of it, and likewise in a spacious house? Is that permitted or not? Answer: If there is a partition between them, there is no harm. Otherwise two imams are not to be gathered in one place, unless the later of them is the more excellent. The meaning of gathering here is that one granted licence prays the noon prayer with the people in a house or elsewhere, and then one above him among those granted licence comes and prays that same prayer with another congregation in that same house with no partition between them. That is permitted to him.

Question: What do you say concerning an imam who prays with the people on a ship while seated, because of the movement of the ship, or the narrowness of the place, or some other cause, when standing is not possible? Is that permitted or not? Answer: There is no harm in it for necessity, whatever the necessity may be.

Question: What do you say concerning an imam who prays with the people and stands ahead of them by a span or so, while all those following him have taken their place on his right and left his left side empty, and so they prayed? Is their prayer valid or not? Answer: They may pray on his right or on his left, and there is no harm in his standing slightly ahead. Their prayer is sound.

Question: What do you say concerning a man who prays the obligatory sunset prayer with the people while the people of the outward have also gathered with him? How is he to recite بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم aloud, when among them that is not permitted at all? Answer: He must recite بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم aloud, but he may lower it so that he hears himself only, for the sake of taqiyya.

Question: What do you say concerning one granted licence who prays with the people, and certain letters do not come out of his mouth correctly, either because his teeth have fallen out or because he knows little of the points of articulation? Is it permitted to complete the prayer with him or not? Answer: If he alters anything of the meaning, the prayer is spoiled.

Question: What do you say concerning one granted licence who prays with the people and whose nose bleeds in the midst of his prayer? May he appoint in his place, to complete the prayer, one whom he sees as fit for it, though he is not among those to whom leave has been given to appoint, or is the prayer left void? Answer: If he is one granted unrestricted leave, there is no harm, and he may put forward whom he wishes.

Question: What does our master say concerning a mosque in which an imam prays the noon prayer with the people, and when they have departed another one granted licence comes and prays that same prayer there with the people, and they did not know of the first prayer, and then afterward they came to know? Are they ordered to repeat the prayer or not? Answer: The prayer has been completed and there is no repetition upon them, whether they knew of it or did not know.

> فخذ ما آتيته وكن من الشاكرين

So take what you have been given, and be among the grateful.

Question: What do you say? May a resident pray with travelers, and a traveler with residents? Answer: If the traveler is more excellent than the residents, he leads them and departs after two cycles, and they rise and complete the prayer. Likewise, if the resident is more excellent than the travelers, he leads them and they depart after two cycles.

Question: Concerning an imam who prays with a congregation and, when he has finished, recalls that there was an impurity upon him which he had known of before the prayer and had forgotten to wash away. Does the one who prayed with the imam repeat the obligatory prayer and the sunna, or is nothing required of him but the repetition of the obligatory prayer without the sunna? Answer: Nothing is required of the follower but the repetition of the obligatory prayer. As for the sunnas, no.

From another source: if a eunuch prays with women it is permitted, and his sitting with them is permitted.

From the book مختصر المصنف: if a man opens an obligatory prayer for himself, wherever he may be, and then an imam comes and steps forward, it is recommended that the man bow and prostrate until he completes that cycle, and count it as a supererogatory prayer for himself, and begin the prayer anew with the imam. If he does not do so and continues his prayer alone, that suffices him. If some of those following stand in front of the imam, the prayer of the imam is valid, and so is the prayer of whoever stands at his side, but the prayer of whoever stands in front of him is not valid.

If he prays with them in Mecca and the rows stand encircling the Kaaba, then no one of the rows should draw nearer to the walls of the Kaaba than the imam draws near, from any of the directions. If one who has not reached maturity holds authority, as the appointed governor over the Muslims, it is not for anyone to stand ahead of him, and the imamate of other boys is not permitted in the obligatory prayers. One whose recitation carries a distortion that alters the meanings of the Quran does not step forward, nor does one whose deafness is such that he does not hear when he is alerted. If the master of a slave is present in the dwelling of the slave, the master has the better right to the imamate than he.

It is said in the book of the interpretation of the Shariah of our master al-Muʿizz, narrated from the Prophet (ع), that he once rode a horse and it stumbled with him and he fell, and his right side was hurt. His companions came in to visit him, and the time of prayer arrived, and he prayed with them seated. They stood, and he signaled to them to sit, so they sat. When he had completed his prayer he said:

> إنما الإمام ليؤتم به فإذا كبر فكبروا فإذا قرأ فأنصتوا وإذا قال سمع الله لمن حمده فقولوا ربنا لك الحمد الله أكبر وإذا سلم فسلموا وإذا صلى جالساً فصلوا جلوساً أجمعين

The imam is appointed only to be followed. When he magnifies God, magnify Him; when he recites, listen in silence; when he says that God hears the one who praises Him, say: Our Lord, to You belongs the praise, God is greatest; when he gives the greeting of peace, give it; and when he prays seated, pray seated, all of you.

The author of the book of glosses said that it is stated in الاختصار, concerning the imamate of a blind man, that it is allowed when there is need of him, and that he is not to be taken as a standing imam. And it is said in المنتخبة that the blind man is not to lead unless he is set straight on some day, and that he is not to be a standing imam, and that the nāṣib is set aside if he has led them from the side. Our master al-Qāḍī al-Nuʿmān ibn Muḥammad, in an epistle of his answering questions put to him by some of the judges, wrote: You asked about the imamate of the blind man. There is no harm in his leading if he is set straight, but he is not to be taken as a standing imam.

It is said in the book مختصر الآثار: We have related from the People of the House (عليهم السلام) that they disliked the imamate of the one afflicted with white leprosy, the leper, the child of adultery, the castrated, the owned slave, and the blind man, unless he be set straight when there is need of him, and he is not taken as a standing imam. Likewise they did not hold that the bedouin should lead, nor the one upon whom the prescribed penalty has been carried out, nor the madman. And it is said there: they gave licence to these that they should lead one another, each one of them leading whoever is in a condition like his own.

It is said in the book مختصر المصنف: if a man prays with a people and then his ablution is broken and he goes out of the mosque without appointing anyone to complete the prayer with them, their prayer is spoiled. If they put a man forward after the imam has gone out of the mosque, it does not suffice them. It suffices only if they put someone forward at the moment the imam's ablution is broken, and before he goes out of the mosque. And it is not fitting for the follower to begin any of the acts of the prayer before the imam; rather he must begin after him. If he precedes him in anything by way of forgetfulness, he returns to his former state so that he remains a follower.

It is said in المجالس الحاتمية, from the Prophet (ع), that he said:

> إنما يخشى الذي يرفع رأسه قبل الإمام أن يمسخ الله رأسه رأس حمار

The one who raises his head before the imam has cause to fear that God will transform his head into the head of a donkey.

It is said in the book of purity: if a wall stands between the imam and those following him, it is not permitted for them to follow him until they can see his prayer, and that is by the wall being low enough that they see his prayer over it, or pierced with openings, as the walls of the women's enclosures in the mosques are made.

It is said in the book مختصر المصنف: if a man prays with a people and two men enter the prayer with him having missed part of it, and then, when the imam has given the greeting of peace, they rise to make up what remains upon them and one of them follows the other, the prayer of the leader is permitted and the prayer of the follower is spoiled.

It has come in the book الإيضاح that the Messenger of God (ع) joined the noon and afternoon prayers, and the sunset and the later evening prayers, with neither travel nor illness nor any excuse.

Our master, the author of the book المجالس المستنصرية, said in the tenth of its assemblies that the imamate in prayer belongs to seven, ranked one above another in precedence. Our master Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad al-Ṣādiq (ع) said:

> يؤم القوم أقدمهم هجرة فان استووا فأقرأهم فان استووا في حفظ القرآن فأفقههم وان استووا في علم الفقه فأكبرهم سناً وصاحب المسجد أحق بمسجده والإمام عليه السلام ومن أقامه أحق من جميعهم والمرأة تصلي بالنساء ولا تتقدمهن بل يقفن عن يمينها وشمالها

The one who leads the people is the earliest of them in emigration. If they are equal in that, then the best of them in recitation. If they are equal in the memorizing of the Quran, then the most learned of them in jurisprudence. If they are equal in the knowledge of jurisprudence, then the eldest of them in years. The owner of the mosque has the better right to his own mosque. The Imam, and whoever the Imam appoints, has a better right than all of them. A woman prays with the women and does not stand ahead of them; rather they stand at her right and at her left.

Twelve among the men do not lead in prayer:

1. The sick man does not lead the healthy.
2. Nor the leper.
3. Nor the one afflicted with white leprosy.
4. Nor the madman.
5. Nor the one upon whom the prescribed penalty has been carried out.
6. Nor the child of adultery.
7. The bedouin does not lead the emigrants.
8. Nor does the one in fetters lead those who are free of them.
9. Nor does the one who has performed the dry ablution lead those who have performed the ablution with water.
10. Nor does the mute lead those who speak.
11. Nor does the traveler lead the residents.
12. Nor does the one set apart by hostility to the household lead those who follow it.

Each one of these leads whoever is in a condition like his own.

It is said in the book of questions, which is from the most excellent Shaykh Shamʿūn ibn Muḥammad Ghūrī al-Hindī (رض), with the answers from our master Badr al-Dīn Ḥasan ibn Idrīs ibn al-Ḥasan.

Question: What do you say concerning two men, one of them granted licence in Rajpur and the other granted licence in Jalalpur, who met in Rajpur and did not know which of them was the superior and which the lesser, so the one granted licence of Rajpur led and the one granted licence of Jalalpur followed him; and then they met in the same way in Jalalpur, and the one granted licence of Jalalpur led and the one granted licence of Rajpur followed him? What is the condition of their prayer? Answer: If it is not known which is the more excellent, then the owner of the mosque has the better right to his own mosque.

Question: What do you say concerning one granted licence who stood to pray for himself, and a man came before his bowing or after he had bowed and followed him, while the one granted licence did not know that he was leading another? Is the prayer permitted or not? Answer: His prayer is permitted along with the leader, even though he did not intend to lead another.

Question: What do you say concerning an imam who prays with the people on a ship while seated, because of the movement of the ship or some other cause, standing being impossible for some of them while the rest are able? What is the condition of their prayer? Answer: Licence has been given in this on account of the ship, when they are unable. Whoever is able should hasten and pray alone, standing.

It is said among the questions put by certain of the shaykhs of India, with the answers to them from certain of our masters, the summoners of Yemen.

Question: If a man prays and with him is a woman of his unmarriageable kin, where does the man stand and where the woman? Answer: If he prays with a woman of his unmarriageable kin, he stands ahead of her and she prays behind him, or at his right but set back from him. If she is a stranger to him, her prayer with him is not permitted unless there is another with the two of them.

A further question, from among the questions of certain of the ranks of India, with its answer from certain of our masters, the summoners of Yemen: You asked about an imam who leads a people while he does not recite well, and brings into the recitation substitution and distortion, as when he says أن الله بريء من المشركين ورسوله with a kasra upon the lām (lām), or ومنهم من هدى الله with a fatḥa upon the hāʾ (hāʾ), and the like of this. Answer: Whoever does not recite well in such matters is not to be taken as an imam, and it is incumbent upon him to set his recitation straight, so that he does not exchange a verse of mercy for a verse of punishment, nor exchange truth for falsehood. It is not fitting that anyone pray in the prayer of such a man. And upon whoever recites well it is incumbent to pray with the people when they call him to it and he is acceptable to them.

It is said among the questions of the law whose answers are from certain of our masters: You asked about an imam who led a people and then did not know how much he had prayed nor how much remained upon him. He must build upon what is certain. If those behind him prompt him with the words of glorification, he returns to that to which they have prompted him until he arrives at what is correct, provided those behind him are certain of it. If they too do not know, and neither he nor they can tell whether he has added to the prayer or fallen short in it, he begins the prayer anew.

I turn now to the mention of the congregation and the rows, again from that book of questions and answers arranged according to the order of the chapters of دعائم الإسلام.

Question: What do you say concerning a man who prays alone while opposite him his wife, or a woman of his unmarriageable kin, prays by herself at his right; and concerning a man who prays while before him his wife, or a woman of his unmarriageable kin, prays standing ahead of him? Is his prayer permitted or not? Answer: It is not permitted that the woman stand ahead of the man and pray while he prays behind her.

Question: What do you say concerning a man of the people of the summons who has prayed the afternoon prayer and then prays that same prayer a second time outside its time for the sake of taqiyya, and some of the people of the summons follow him in taqiyya before those who are not upon the way of the People of the House (عليهم السلام), so that they follow him and pray behind him intending him as their imam? What is the condition of their prayer, and is anything upon the imam in that? Answer: He does that for the sake of taqiyya, and he need not concern himself with their following him, and there is no sin upon him in that.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the book of purity, that the Messenger of God (ع) came upon a man who prayed alone behind the rows with a gap before him, and ordered him to repeat the prayer, and that Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (ع) said the like? O our master, is one who prays in that manner ordered to repeat? Answer: He is indeed ordered to repeat, and upon this is the practice.

Question: What do you say concerning a man engaged in the prayer of glorification or another of the supererogatory prayers? May he cut it short if he fears that the congregation will pass him by? Answer: The obligatory prayer in congregation is more excellent and more binding than any other.

Question: What do you say concerning a man who wishes to pray in congregation and has entered upon a supererogatory prayer? May he cut it short if he fears that he will miss the performance of the sunna? Answer: If he fears that the obligatory prayer will pass him by, he cuts it short; otherwise he does not cut it short.

Question: What do you say concerning a man who entered the sanctified state for the obligatory prayer and recited, and then the imam came and stepped forward while the man was standing, and when the imam bowed he bowed with him without renewing the sanctified state? What is the condition of his prayer? Answer: He must repeat it.

Question: What do you say concerning one granted licence who stood to pray for himself, and a man came before his bowing or after he had bowed and followed him, while the one granted licence did not intend to lead another? Is the prayer of the follower permitted or not? Answer: His prayer is permitted along with the leader, even though he did not intend to lead another.

Question: If a man prays and with him is a woman of his unmarriageable kin, or he prays with a man and with such a woman, where does the man stand and where the woman? Answer: If he prays with a woman of his unmarriageable kin, he stands ahead of her and she prays behind him, or at his right but set back from him. If she is a stranger to him, her prayer is not permitted unless a third is with the two of them.

Question: May those granted licence read the benefits of religion to women of unmarriageable kin and to others in a single gathering? Answer: If there is a screen between the man and the woman, he reads to her in such a way that she hears his speech and he does not see her. And let no man be alone with a woman unless another woman is with her, or another man with him.

Question: Concerning a man who reaches the imam in the obligatory prayer at the final sitting of witness. Does he intend to complete the prayer with him or not? Answer: If he reaches him at the final sitting of witness, he intends the prayer as a follower, because the final sitting of witness is the last of the limits of the prayer. He sits and supplicates with the supplication of turning toward God, and when the imam gives the greeting of peace he rises and prays, and he has caught the congregation.

From another source, the saying of al-Ṣādiq Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (ع):

> ينبغي للصفوف أن تكون تامة متواصلة بعضها إلى بعض فيكون بين كل صفين قدر مسقط جسد الإنسان إذا سجد فان لم يصلوا مثل هذا لم تكن لهم صلاة

The rows should be complete and joined one to another, so that between every two rows there is the measure of the fall of a man's body when he prostrates. If they do not pray in this manner, they have no prayer.

The meaning of the fall of a man's body is that the one praying has room enough to prostrate.

If the imam prays with the congregation and all the rows are filled, and a man comes and finds no place in the rows, he prays alone alongside the imam and does not come into the imam's row. And if the ḥujja of the imam comes, who stands in his place after him, and finds no place in the rows, he steps forward and stands level with him and prays with the imam. This is not permitted to any other than he. This is the explicit text.

It is said in the book مختصر المصنف, concerning the saying:

> من صلى الصلاة في جماعة فظنوا به كل خير

Whoever prays the prayer in congregation, think every good of him.

And it is said: this is when nothing evil is known of him. And it is said in the reports that they differed concerning the forming of rows between the pillars. Some of them related that it is disliked, and some related that the licence for it is the better established. And it has come in the book الإيضاح that the Messenger of God (ع) joined the noon and afternoon prayers, and the sunset and the later evening prayers, with neither travel nor illness nor any excuse.

Our master Jaʿfar ibn Sayyidnā Sulaymān answered, from the island of his Yemen to the island of his India, questions put to him by the author of this book, Sayyidnā ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān: That which you asked about, may God preserve you, concerning the women, when they pray in congregation with the men from behind a screen, whether it is incumbent upon the one of them nearest to the men to look toward them or not, and whether, if the place is connected, there is any need for them to look toward the men or not. Answer: It is incumbent upon them to look toward them before entering the sanctified state, just as the Dāʾūdīs hold that the woman whose place is near the men prays in a place close to them and keeps her gaze upon the men during the bowing and the prostration, and that otherwise there is no prayer for any of them. The intent is that if the place is connected, as by an opening or a lattice, the prayer is thereby complete, and looking toward the men before the sanctified state and after it is not permitted. Indeed, the prohibition of it has come even outside the prayer, so how much more within the prayer, which is the greatest of acts of worship. And looking during the bowing or the prostration, or while standing or sitting, after the sanctified state has been entered, cuts off the prayer if it is directed toward any of these things, and this is a looking toward one who is not of unmarriageable kin, and in it is disobedience. If the place is not connected, then the woman nearest to the men glances at their garments, a glance only, before entering the sanctified state, and in that is sufficiency, and by it the prayer of all of them is completed.

It is said in the interpretation of the Shariah that the Prophet (ع) saw a man praying alone and ordered him to repeat the prayer, meaning behind the row without standing level with the imam, facing him from behind him. And it is said there: just as none may step forward to the prayer niche except one whom the imam puts forward when his ablution is broken, so too none of the respondents may step forward except one whom the summoner puts forward and holds fit for it.
