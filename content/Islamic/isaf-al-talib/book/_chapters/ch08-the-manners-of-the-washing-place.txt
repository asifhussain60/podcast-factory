# The Manners of the Washing Place

I turn now to the manners that belong to the place of washing and to the ablution itself.

In the book of questions and answers, which is arranged according to the chapters of Da'a'im al-Islam, the following is recorded.

Question: What does our master, may God illumine his inner being, say concerning one who forgot the istinja' from a major impurity until he had prayed and the time of the prayer had passed, and then remembered it? May he perform istinja' and wudhu' and repeat the prayer, or not? Answer: He repeats the prayer, for one does not pray while impurity is upon him.

Question: What does our master, may God perpetuate his support, say concerning a man who performs wudhu' and washes himself in the washing place while uncovered, the place being screened so that no one of the people sees him? Does he pray with that wudhu' or not? Answer: What was mentioned of the concealment of some of the Imams (عليهم السلام) in the water was mentioned for the excellence of good manners. So if a man is where no one of the people sees him and he uncovers himself, there is no harm in that, though concealment is better, and he prays with that purity which he performed while uncovered.

From another source: whoever urinates in a place should not perform the istinja' in that same place, but in a second place; and whoever has cleansed himself with a stone after relieving himself should not return to that stone.

From Kitab al-Idah, the Messenger of God said:

> أمرني جبرئيل عليه السلام عن ربي أن اغسل فنكي عند الوضوء

Gabriel (ع) commanded me on behalf of my Lord to wash the two hollows beside my chin when I perform the ablution.

And in the sound narration:

> إذا توضأت فلا تنسين الفنكين

When you perform the ablution, do not forget the two hollows beside the chin.

That is, the side of the tuft below the lower lip, on the right and on the left, and these two are the neglected place.

He said in Mukhtasar al-Musannaf: whoever forgets the wudhu' until he has performed the ghusl, let him perform wudhu' and nothing is upon him. And if a man performs wudhu', then begins his ghusl, and before completing the ghusl a state of impurity befalls him, he carries on with the ghusl and performs wudhu' after it for the prayer.

He said in Ta'wil al-Da'a'im: whoever intends the wudhu' in its outward sense goes to the vessel in which is the water with which he performs wudhu' and purifies himself, and places it on his right.

And in the book of questions and answers already mentioned, arranged according to the chapters of Da'a'im al-Islam, it is asked: What does our master, may God perpetuate his support, say concerning one who entered the prayer after performing wudhu' and then recalled that he had forgotten the istinja'? Does he complete his prayer or repeat it? Answer: The prayer is not permitted while a man carries an impurity of which he knows, until he washes it away.

He said in Ta'wil al-Shari'ah, in the section on the manners of the wudhu': the manners of the wudhu' are twelve.

1. Do not enter the privy except with sandals.
2. Advance the left foot.
3. Cover your head until you come out.
4. Do not face the qiblah, nor turn your back to it.
5. Conceal yourself.
6. Lean upon your left leg.
7. Do not sit long in the privy.
8. Do not cleanse yourself with dung, nor with a decayed bone, nor with animal droppings, nor with a crumbling bone, but let it be three fresh stones.
9. Cleanse yourself with your left hand.
10. Do not pour water upon the excrement.
11. Do not speak until you come out.
12. Then advance your right foot when you leave.

The dung mentioned is human excrement, and the two terms rendered as decayed and crumbling bone both mean a worn bone.

He said therein:

> ولا تجوز الصلاة بغير وضوء

Prayer is not valid without ablution.

And he said that the Prophet forbade urinating in burrows, saying:

> إنها مأوى الثعابين والحيات

They are the lairs of serpents and snakes.

And the Prophet said:

> لا تقبل الصلاة الا بطهور

Prayer is not accepted except with purification.

And our master the Commander of the Faithful Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) said in his Four Hundred Manners:

> المضمضة والاستنشاق بالماء عند الطهور طهور للفم والأنف وهو من السنة

Rinsing the mouth and drawing water into the nose at the time of purification is a purification for the mouth and the nose, and it is of the practice of the Prophet.

He said therein:

> الاستنجاء بالماء البارد يقطع البواسير

Cleansing with cold water cuts off piles.

He said therein:

> الوضوء بعد الطهر عشر حسنات فتطهروا

Ablution after purification is ten good deeds, so purify yourselves.

He said therein:

> لا يتوضأ الرجل حتّى يقول قبل أن يمس الماء بسم الله اللهم اجعلني من التوابين واجعلني من المتطهرين

Let no man perform the ablution until he says, before the water touches him: In the name of God. O God, make me among those who turn to You in repentance and make me among those who purify themselves.

He said therein:

> إذا تعرى الرجل نظر إليه الشيطان فطمع فيه فاستتروا

When a man uncovers himself, Satan looks at him and covets him, so conceal yourselves.

He said therein:

> ليس للرجل أن يكشف ثيابه عن فخذه ويجلس بين أيدي قوم

It is not for a man to draw his garment back from his thigh and sit before a company of people.

He said therein:

> اغسلوا أيدي صبيانكم من الغمر فأن الشيطان يشم الغمر فيفزع الصبي في رقاده ويؤذى به الكاتبان

Wash the hands of your children of the grease of food, for Satan smells the grease, and the child is startled in his sleep, and the two recording angels are harmed by it.

I turn now to the qualities of the wudhu'.

In the same book of questions and answers it is asked: What do you say, O best of those who have drawn us to the love of wisdom and of the wise, concerning what has come in the book of purity, that rinsing the mouth, drawing in water through the nose, and the istinja' are practices of the Prophet which should not be deliberately abandoned, and that whoever forgets them or is ignorant of them has not thereby spoiled his wudhu' and owes no repetition if he has prayed? What is intended by this istinja'? Answer: The istinja', the rinsing of the mouth, and the drawing in of water are practices which should not be deliberately abandoned, and whoever leaves them has not spoiled his prayer. The istinja' is obligatory for the cleansing of impurity, because the removal of impurity is obligatory.

Question: What do you say, O best of those whose blessed meeting has made us forget the love of family and friends, concerning what has come in Da'a'im al-Islam in the command to wash the face while letting the eyes drink of the water? Does any deficiency enter the wudhu' of one who closes his eyes while washing his face, or not? Answer: If he lets his eyes drink, that is better; and if he does not, his wudhu' is complete.

Question: What do you say, O one with whom God is pleased only as He is pleased with His righteous servants, concerning a man who has upon his face or his hands a wound or a boil which the water cannot touch? Must he leave it in the wudhu' or the ghusl, or not? Answer: Whoever has upon his legs or elsewhere an ailment, a wound or a boil, which the water cannot touch, may wipe over the bandages and whatever else is upon the wound; and if he fears that removing them will destroy him through cold or through severe harm, or if he is sick, he performs tayammum. This is the ruling in the wudhu'. As for the greater purification, whoever has an ailment such as smallpox or wounds or the like pours the water over it in a stream; and if he fears for himself, he must perform tayammum, whether he be travelling or at home.

Question: What does our master, may God grant us his pleasure, say concerning wiping over the two feet when most of the foot is exposed and the lesser part is covered beneath the strap of the sandal? Is wiping over that permitted, or not? Answer: This is an obligation, and the wiping must be carried out beneath it.

Question: What does our master, may God perpetuate his support, say concerning one who entered the prayer after performing wudhu' and then recalled that he had forgotten the istinja' with water? Does he complete his prayer or repeat it? Answer: The prayer is not permitted while a man carries an impurity of which he knows, until he washes it away.

From another source: whoever performs wudhu' and forgets the hand and the face and prays, and the time passes, and he remembers after that, does not repeat the prayer. If a man performs wudhu', then rises and walks about, and the limbs dry, he begins the wudhu' again from the start; and if they have not dried, he completes what remains. Whoever performs wudhu' for the prayer, then rises and afterward sees a place of the wudhu' dry, does not repeat the wudhu', but wipes the place that has dried. As for one whose hands are cut off and who cannot perform wudhu' for the prayer, the bondmaid who belongs to him performs it for him, or his wife; and if he finds neither of them, he performs tayammum and prays.

Question: What do you say, O one whom God Most High has made the trustee of His friends, concerning a pool or an empty place without water, in which people relieve themselves, and then water flows into it together with filth, and when it has gathered it becomes overpowering, above the measure, subduing the impurities, so that nothing of their qualities is found in it? Is purification with it permitted, or not? Answer: If the water overpowers the impurity, purification with it is permitted, provided it is abundant, above the measure stated in the Shari'ah.

Question: What do you say, O one whom God Most High has made the delight of the eye of the beloved of God, concerning a vessel filled with water until it is above the measure, and then an impurity appears in it or falls into it and one of its qualities appears, and then water is poured into it in a stream until the trace of the impurity that had appeared is gone? Is purification with it permitted, or not? Answer: The water has become impure, and purification with it is not permitted, unless it be above the measure after the vessel has been purified.

Question: What do you say, O one whom God Most High has made a spring of water by which the souls of the people of Islam are made to endure, concerning a small quantity of pure water with which one who has no impurity on his body purifies himself, and then another purifies himself with it, and then another, and likewise the dust of the tayammum, seeing that the Shari'ah has stated that used water is pure and purifying, while we have found in it no mention of used dust, and the common people do not permit the use of water and dust for purification twice? Answer: Purification with it is permitted, once and many times.

Question: What do you say concerning the meaning of his saying (ع):

> من لم يطهره البحر فلا طهره الله

Whoever the sea does not purify, may God not purify him.

Answer: Water purifies and is not itself purified; and likewise the knowledge of the Imam: whomever it does not purify, nothing else will purify him.

If the water in a pool is eight cubits in its length and breadth, though its depth be only a span or less, purification in it is permitted. If rain falls in the city and the water runs together with filth and refuse into a great pool in which a man and a camel might drown, and the pool is filled, washing and purification with it are permitted so long as its color or its taste or its smell has not changed. And if water is drawn from a well and the water is running, and an unbeliever washes a garment in it and its dirt is seen, purification with it is permitted, because it has the ruling of running water.

From Mukhtasar al-Musannaf: if a man finds a little water upon the ground, or in a hollow, or the like of that, and it is greatly changed, and it is not known whether impurity has mixed with it or not, he performs wudhu' with it, for water may change without impurity mixing with it. What is extracted from substances by pressing or by distillation, such as rosewater and the like, may not be used for purification. What has been used in purifying the limbs when no impurity was upon them, and has not gathered in an impure vessel, may be used for purification. And it is permitted to perform wudhu' with water that has already been used once for wudhu', so long as no impurity has mixed with it.

It is mentioned in the Mu'ayyadi Sessions: washing with rainwater at the time of the rain suffices. And it is said in Mukhtasar al-Musannaf: whoever has on his hand a ring bearing the name of God should not touch impurity while wearing it.

Our master the Qadi al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad, may God exalt his sanctity, said in Kitab al-Idah, in the section on the description of washing the hands, that he had mentioned what was related from Abu Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع), who said:

> ولا ينبغي أن تمسح ذراعيك الا من المرفق إلى فوق وامسحه مسحاً فإذا كان يلزم في اليدين على قلة شعرهما فهو في الرأس الزم

You should not wipe your forearms except from the elbow upward, and wipe it thoroughly; for if this is required in the case of the arms, despite the scantness of their hair, it is all the more required in the case of the head.

It has likewise come down that when one performing the wudhu' interrupts it, he builds upon what he has done, so long as the water has not dried from the limbs he has already washed.

He said in Ta'wil al-Shari'ah of our master al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah, may the blessings of God be upon him, that the Prophet said:

> إذا ولغ الكلب في إناء أحدكم فليغسله سبع مرات آخرهن بالتراب

When a dog laps from the vessel of one of you, let him wash it seven times, the last of them with dust.

And it is said in Mukhtasar al-Musannaf: when a vessel is made impure by the lapping of a dog, it is washed seven times, the first of them with dust; and the pig is like it. It is said therein also: if an impurity falls into one of two vessels and it is not known which of them it is, he does not perform wudhu' with either of them, but performs tayammum and prays, and nothing is upon him.

It is said in Majalis al-Bayan that the obligatory acts of the wudhu' are seven:

1. Pure water.
2. The intention.
3. Washing the face.
4. Washing the hands.
5. Wiping the head.
6. Wiping the feet.
7. The order, which is that one begins with what God began with in His mention.

And its Sunnah acts are twelve:

1. The istinja'.
2. Washing the hands before entering them into the vessel.
3. Pronouncing the name of God when sitting down for the wudhu'.
4. Rinsing the mouth.
5. Drawing water into the nose.
6. Moving the ring.
7. Running the fingers through the beard.
8. Letting the eyes drink of the water.
9. Giving precedence to the right sides over the left.
10. Wiping the two ears.
11. The first washing, which is obligatory, the second and the third being Sunnah.
12. The tooth stick.

The obligatory forms of ghusl are seven:

1. The ghusl from major ritual impurity.
2. The ghusl of menstruation.
3. The ghusl of the unbeliever when he enters Islam.
4. The ghusl of the madman and of one who has fainted, when they recover.
5. The ghusl from the blood of childbirth.
6. The ghusl from immersion in impurity.
7. The washing of the dead, which is an obligation upon the living until one of them undertakes it.

And the Sunnah forms of ghusl are twelve:

1. The ghusl of Friday.
2. and 3. The ghusl for the two festivals.
4. The ghusl for entering the state of consecration.
5. The ghusl for entering the sacred precinct.
6. The ghusl for entering the Ka'bah.
7. The ghusl for entering Madinah.
8. The ghusl on the day of Arafah.
9. The ghusl on three nights of the month of Ramadan: the night of the nineteenth.
10. The night of the twenty-first.
11. The night of the twenty-third.
12. The ghusl after washing the dead.

And the prayer is disliked in twelve places:

1. The graveyard.
2. The slaughterhouse.
3. The bathhouse.
4. The privy.
5. The resting places of camels.
6. Praying toward a sleeping person.
7. Praying without a screen.
8. Upon wet, impure ground.
9. Upon anything of food.
10. In the places of worship of the Jews.
11. Of the Christians.
12. Of the Magians, unless water be sprinkled in them.

He said in Ta'wil al-Shari'ah that there is no fixed limit for the istinja' other than purity, the settling of the heart and the soul, and the arrival of certainty that purity has been attained and impurity removed. He said therein also that the Prophet forbade wiping over the socks and forbade the abandoning of saying aloud بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم.

Our master Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman, may God exalt his sanctity, said at the beginning of Kitab al-Rida' al-Batin that the Sunnah in the wudhu' is three times over, three times over, in every place of the wudhu', except the private parts, meaning that these are washed until the impurity is gone from them. Then he said, after that, may God exalt his sanctity, that the wudhu' is threefold, threefold. And he said therein, after further words, that the Sunnah in the ghusl is that a man wash himself with water until it drips and flows; and if he does not find abundant water, then let him wipe with as much as will reach his whole body with water.

It is said in Mukhtasar al-Athar, in the mention of the qualities of the wudhu', that one then rinses the mouth and draws in water three times, and that this is of the Sunnah and should not be abandoned; and if one leaves it without intending to leave it, nothing is upon him. It is said therein: he washes it three times, and if he washes it twice or once in a complete washing it suffices him, and three is better, and there is no merit beyond three. And it is said therein: once suffices him, and if he wipes it three times, that is good.

Our master the most exalted summoner Zayd ibn Muhammad ibn al-Fadl, may God sanctify his soul, said in al-Risalah al-Maknunah: God, the most truthful of those who speak, said:

> إن الله يحب التوابين ويحب المتطهرين

Truly God loves those who turn to Him in repentance, and He loves those who purify themselves.

And the Messenger of God said:

> لا تقبل الصلاة الا بطهور

Prayer is not accepted except with purification.

Purity is of two kinds and filth is of two kinds. Outward purity is by the outward water, from the outward filth of urine and excrement; and inward purity is by the inward water, which is knowledge, from the inward filth, which is doubt and ignorance.

He said therein that the name of the wudhu' falls upon seven meanings, four of them obligatory and three of them Sunnah, and that the Messenger placed the three Sunnah acts before the four obligations. He mentioned the washing of the hands before entering them into the vessel. He said that the mouth is that by which a man takes his nourishment from the various kinds of food and drink, and that a man has no other sense by which he is nourished, and it is the one that conveys the nourishment of the five senses, and to it belongs the exposition and to no other. Then he said: it is like the mouth of the lesser world, and whoever seeks nourishment from other than the mouth is not nourished and does not live. And as for drawing in water through the nose, he said: it is that which takes the breath from the air, so that the body is thereby alive and at rest; and when the two nostrils are stopped, the task is transferred to the mouth so that it may stand in their place with effort; and when both senses together, the nose and the mouth, are stopped, the man perishes and never lives again. Then he said: rinsing the mouth, drawing in water, and the istinja' are three Sunnah acts; and washing the face and washing the hands are two obligations, and wiping the head and wiping the feet are two obligations. That is seven bounds. One of the two washings is bounded and the other is unbounded, and likewise one of the two wipings is bounded and the other is unbounded. The face bears the meaning of the Speaker, because in it are the seven openings, which are the two eyes, the two ears, the two nostrils, and the mouth, and because a man is known by his face; and likewise no one is known except by his prophet, and there is no deliverance except by turning toward him.

And our master the Commander of the Faithful Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) said in his Four Hundred Manners:

> ليس في شرب المسكر والمسح على الخفين تقية

There is no dissimulation in the drinking of intoxicants or in wiping over the socks.
