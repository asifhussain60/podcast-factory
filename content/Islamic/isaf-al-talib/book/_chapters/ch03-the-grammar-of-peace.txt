# The Grammar of Peace

I come now to the mention of the guardianship of the Imams, the family of the Messenger of Allah (ع), and to the mention of the obligation of praying for Muhammad and the family of Muhammad (عليهم السلام), together with what pertains to that prayer: the good proclaimers, the righteous borders, their stations, and the command to return peace and to reply to the greeting of the book.

He said in the twenty-third council of the Mustansiriya councils, may God's peace be upon the author of the book: he has finished mentioning the Sunnah that we stipulated discussing, namely the revelation of peace. It is divided above into seven sections, which are what came from God and from His angels; and below it is divided into twelve sections, which are what came from human beings.

The highest, the first, the most particular, the most exalted, and the most sincere is the word of God Almighty:

> ⟪سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ⟫

"Peace be upon you."

The second, which carries some figurative quality, is His saying:

> ⟪يَا نُوحُ اهْبِطْ بِسَلَامٍ مِّنَّا⟫

"O Noah, descend in peace from Us."

The third, which is more suggestive, is His saying:

> ⟪تحيتهم يوم يلقونه سلام⟫

"Their greeting on the day they meet Him is peace."

The fourth is His saying:

> ⟪ويلقّون فيها تحية وسلاماً⟫

"And they will be met therein with greeting and peace."

The fifth expresses that it is from the angels in particular:

> ⟪وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ يَدْخُلُونَ عَلَيْهِم مِّن كُلِّ بَابٍ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُم⟫

"And the angels enter upon them from every gate: peace be upon you."

The sixth is that God and His angels pray for the Prophet (ع):

> ⟪يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا صَلُّوا عَلَيْهِ وَسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا⟫

"O you who believe, pray for him and submit to him in full submission."

The seventh is the word of the Almighty:

> ⟪سَلَامٌ عَلَىٰ آلِ يَاسِينَ⟫

"Peace be upon the family of Yasin."

And Yasin is our Prophet Muhammad (ع), and the family of Yasin is the family of Muhammad (عليهم السلام).

He said in the twenty-fourth assembly of it:

Know that the revelation of peace is an obligatory Sunnah of Islam, one that speaks of affection and submission, and Salam is one of the names of the One possessed of majesty and honor. You have heard what we have read to you from what it contains regarding the seven sections, and this is followed by the twelve divisions, which concern the greeting exchanged between human beings.

The first of these is the greeting of the people of the right hand to the faithful prophet (ع) and his noble family. He says, and He is the most truthful of speakers:

> ⟪سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُم بِمَا صَبَرْتُمْ⟫

"Peace be upon you for your patience."

For if they are among the people of the right hand, then (عليهم السلام) from the people of the right hand.

The second is the greeting upon entering the mosque, with peace, because the Almighty says:

> ⟪فَإِذَا دَخَلْتُم بُيُوتًا فَسَلِّمُوا عَلَىٰ أَنفُسِكُمْ تَحِيَّةً مِّنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ مُبَارَكَةً طَيِّبَةً⟫

"When you enter houses, greet one another with a greeting from God, blessed and good."

The third is the peace of one who concludes the prayer.

The fourth is the peace of the believer in the face of the ignorant. He said:

> ⟪وَإِذَا خَاطَبَهُمُ الْجَاهِلُونَ قَالُوا سَلَامًا⟫

"And when the ignorant address them, they say: peace."

The fifth is what the Sunnah specifies: that the rider on a camel initiates the greeting to the rider on a horse. The sixth is that the rider on a horse initiates the greeting to the rider on a donkey. The seventh is that the rider on a donkey initiates the greeting to one walking. The eighth is that the one walking initiates the greeting to one standing. The ninth is that the one standing initiates the greeting and then returns it.

And the return of the greeting is divided into three kinds: the virtuous reply, the complete reply, and the just reply. As for the virtuous reply, it is to greet whoever initiates the greeting with something better than what they offered. The complete reply is to respond in kind, as God Almighty said:

> ⟪وَإِذَا حُيِّيتُم بِتَحِيَّةٍ فَحَيُّوا بِأَحْسَنَ مِنْهَا أَوْ رُدُّوهَا⟫

"And when you are greeted with a greeting, return it with something better, or return it as it is."

And the just reply is in responding to the dhimmis, for they do not initiate the greeting; and if they do initiate it, one responds, "And upon you." This is because it was reported that some of them used to deceive Muslims in their greetings, saying ⟪السَّامُ عَلَيْكُم⟫, which is "Death be upon you," in place of ⟪السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُم⟫. So the Sunnah came to say in response, "And upon you," which is a just reply, for whoever said something good received its like, and whoever said something bad received its like.

Our master Amir al-Mu'minin, the Imam al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah (ع), said in his prayer on Sunday, after praying for the higher borders: "O God, reach the lower borders, the five, which are the spokesman, the foundation, the culmination, the successor, and the wing." And he said (ع) in his prayer on Saturday: "O God, upon the seven days and the twelve months, send the purest, most honorable, and most noble of prayers."

Some of the saints of the religion, may God sanctify their souls, said in praying for the borders, in his prayer on Sunday: "O God, reach the proclaimers and their gates, the imams of their stations, their proofs, and their preachers." And he said in his supplication on Monday: "O Allah, pray for the seven proclaimers, the seven culminations, the seven caliphs, and the twelve successors, the purest prayer, the most growing, the most honorable, and the highest. And admit us into all the good into which You have admitted them, and spare us from every evil from which You have spared them." And he said in his supplication on Tuesday: "Reach Your manifest borders and Your righteous guardians, the spokesmen who call to Your light, the proven foundations of the truth of Your religion, the culmination, the successor, and the wing, through whom the gates of Your mercy were opened." And he said in his prayer on Wednesday: "O God, upon them and upon their gates, their proofs, and their preachers, send the best of Your holy prayers." And he said in his prayer on Thursday: "O God, bless the days and months, the hours of the day and the hours of the night, both the manifest and the hidden." And he said in his prayer on the Sabbath: "Dedicate to Muhammad, O God, and to the Imams of his station and the proofs of their ages, many times over what You have prayed for them all."

And our master Amir al-Mu'minin (ع) said in his Four Hundred Counsels: "When someone greets you, return the greeting with something better than it, or return it in kind. Shake hands with your enemy if he dislikes it, for God has commanded His servants to do so, as the Almighty says:

> ⟪ادْفَعْ بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ فَإِذَا الَّذِي بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُ عَدَاوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُ وَلِيٌّ حَمِيمٌ⟫

'Repel with that which is better, and then the one between whom and you there was enmity will become as though he were a close friend.' And he said therein: 'If your brother says, "May God greet you with peace," then say: You are the peace of God, and the finest of abodes and stations.'"

He said in the book of the question, which is from the late Sheikh Shamoun ibn Muhammad Ghori al-Hindi (رض), and the answer, which is from our master Badr al-Din, our master Hasan ibn our master Idris ibn Sayyidna al-Hasan ibn Sayyidna Abdullah al-Alaf, may God sanctify him and grant us his intercession and his companionship. The question: "What do you say, you who purify our souls of what clings to them of the whispering of Shaytan, regarding the offering of peace to sheikhs and teachers and those close to them? Most of the people of India do not proclaim peace aloud before them, but instead bow or lower their heads, intending by that to greet them. Some say this resembles the practice of unbelievers. O our master, favor us with an answer in this matter that leaves no room for the objectors to speak." The answer: "Let peace be proclaimed aloud, for that is better."

And he said therein, the question: "What do you say, O you who have illuminated the jewels of our souls with the light of knowledge, regarding the returning of a greeting to a Muslim: must it be in the measure that he hears it, or not? And is it obligatory upon the one returning it to reply when he hears the greeting of the Muslim, or not? And in the case of sneezing, is the blessing obligatory upon the one who blesses when he hears the sneezer say 'Praise be to God,' or merely upon hearing the sneeze itself? And must the blessing be in the measure that the sneezer hears it, or not?" The answer: "This is a courtesy and not an obligation. One may do it as one wishes, and there is no fault upon him if he does not do it, except in returning a greeting in general, whether aloud or silently."
