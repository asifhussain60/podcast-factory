# The Pillar of Religion and Its Appointed Hours

I now enter the Book of Prayer, and I begin with what has been handed down concerning its merits, what is forbidden within it, and the matters attached to these.

### On the Virtues of Prayer, What Is Forbidden in It, and Related Matters

I record first what the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his four hundred precepts of good conduct:

> قيام الليل مصلحة للبدن ورضى للرب وتعرض للرحمة وتمسّك بأخلاق النبيين

"Standing in prayer at night is a benefit for the body, a cause of the Lord's pleasure, an exposing of oneself to mercy, and a holding fast to the character of the prophets."

And he said in it:

> ولا يقومنّ الرجل إلى الصلاة متكاسلاً ولا متناعساً وليقلل العبد الفكر إذا قام بين يدي الله عز وجل فإنما له من صلاته ما أقبل عليه ولا تدعوا ذكر الله في كل مكان على كل حال ولا يلتفتنّ أحدكم في صلاته فإن العبد إذا التفت فيها قال الله عز وجل: أني خير لك مما التفت إليه

"Let no man rise to prayer in laziness or in drowsiness. Let the servant limit his wandering thoughts when he stands before God Almighty, for he receives of his prayer only that much of it to which he turned his attention. Do not abandon the remembrance of God in any place or in any condition. And let none of you look aside during his prayer, for when the servant looks aside in it, God Almighty says: I am better for you than that toward which you have turned."

And he said in it:

> إياكم والكسل فإن من كسل لم يؤد حق الله عز وجل

"Beware of laziness, for whoever grows lazy does not discharge the right of God Almighty."

And he said in it:

> لا يعبث أحدكم بلحيته في الصلاة ولا بما يشغله عنها

"Let none of you toy with his beard during the prayer, nor with anything that distracts him from it."

And he said in it:

> الصلاة قربان لكل تقي

"Prayer is the offering of every godfearing person."

And he said in it:

> ليس من عمل أحبّ إلى الله من الصلاة فلا تشغلكم عن أوقاتها أمور الدنيا فإن الله سبحانه ذمّ قوماً استهانوا بأوقاتها

"There is no deed more beloved to God than prayer, so let not the affairs of this world distract you from its appointed times, for God, glory be to Him, reproached a people who made light of its times."

And he cited the words of God Almighty:

> فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ الَّذِينَ هُمْ عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ

"So woe to those who pray, those who are neglectful of their prayer." That is to say, heedless of it.

I turn now to what is stated in a book of legal questions, each of them a marvel.

Another question: What do you say of a man for whom prayer is not permissible, and who is yet not excused for leaving it? The answer to this is that he is a man who drank wine until his reason was gone. Prayer was therefore not permissible for him because of his drinking, yet he is not excused for leaving it, in view of the words of God Almighty:

> لَا تَقْرَبُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَأَنتُمْ سُكَارَىٰ

"Do not approach prayer while you are intoxicated." Consider what you say.

Another question: What do you say of a man who prayed while in a state of purity, and yet his prayer was not valid? The answer to this is that he was a man who had emigrated and who prayed behind a bedouin (أعرابي), so his prayer was not valid, because the bedouins are harder in disbelief and hypocrisy, and it is not permissible to pray behind them.

Another question: What do you say of an obligatory prayer in which the one praying it has neither bowing nor prostration? The answer to this is the man swimming who is unable to come out of the water onto dry land; upon him there is neither bowing nor prostration.

### On the Mention of Prayer Times and Related Matters

What follows I take from the aforementioned book of questions and answers, which is arranged according to the order of the chapters of the Book of the Pillars of Islam.

Question: What do you say, O you before whose awe the necks of the great tyrants have bowed, concerning what has come in the Book of Purity, that when a man remembers the missed prayers (al-fawa'it) during the time of a current prayer, then if there is room in the time he prays the missed prayers first and afterward prays the one whose time he is in? So, our master, does he sin if there is room in the time and he leaves the missed prayers and performs the one whose time he is in, or not?

Answer: Yes, he sins if there is room in the time, nothing hinders him, and he neglects them.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the Book of the Pillars, that the Messenger of God, when he prayed the Fajr prayer after its time had passed, began with the sunnah before the obligatory prayer? So, our master, how does one act who has missed the sunnah of Fajr and the obligatory Dhuhr?

Answer: He gives the sunnah of Fajr precedence, in the making up, over the missed prayers that come after it.

Question: What does our master say concerning a man who prayed the sunnah, two rak'ahs or four rak'ahs, and then prayed the obligatory prayer with the imam? How does he pray the remainder of the sunnah, as a performance in its time (ada') or as a making up (qada')?

Answer: What remains upon him of the sunnah he makes up if its time has passed. The time of the sunnah is before the obligatory prayer, except in the case of Maghrib.

Question: What does our master say concerning one who performed the obligatory prayer with the imam and had missed the sunnah? Does he perform the sunnah before the optional prayer (nafilah) as a performance in its time, or as a making up?

Answer: There is no harm in his praying the optional prayer as a performance in its time, so long as the time has not passed. The better course is that he pray the optional prayer as a performance in its time and then make up the sunnah.

Question: What does our master say concerning a man who prayed the obligatory prayer at the end of the time, so that the time then passed? Does he pray the optional prayer as a performance in its time, or as a making up?

Answer: He prays the optional prayer as a performance in its time, because he prayed the obligatory prayer as a performance in its time.

Question: What does our master say concerning a man who prays the obligatory Maghrib prayer and then falls to talking while the people are praying the sunnah, and goes out to a place nearby and then returns, and the people are again in the sunnah prayer? Does he pray the sunnah as a performance in its time, or as a making up?

Answer: If he prays the sunnah at the time of drawing near and of glorification, then the sunnah prayer is a performance in its time; and if that has passed, it is a making up. And if he went out to a nearby place and returned while the people were in the sunnah prayer, it is a performance in its time.

Question: What does our master say concerning a man who comes while the imam is praying the Asr prayer with the people during the time of Dhuhr, and the one who arrives knows that he is praying Asr? Does he intend Dhuhr with the imam, or Asr? Or does he pray Asr and Dhuhr apart from his imam?

Answer: If the one entering enters while the imam is in the Asr prayer and the time of Dhuhr is still possible, he intends Dhuhr with him. And if the time of Dhuhr has passed, he prays Asr with the imam if he wishes and then makes up Dhuhr. And in praying with the imam there is a merit that is encouraged.

Question: Concerning the worshipper who prays the obligatory Dhuhr prayer and then departs while the optional prayer is still owed by him, and he becomes occupied until the end of the time of Dhuhr and then wishes to pray it: is it a performance in its time or a making up?

Answer: Our master Malik al-Jami' said: if there is a remainder left in the time, he prays the optional prayer as a performance in its time.

Question: Concerning one who enters the mosque at the declining of the sun and prays the prayer of the declining, six rak'ahs, and then remembers that there are sunnah prayers owed by him from before which he had missed: is it incumbent upon him to make up the sunnah prayers he missed between the prayer of the declining and the obligatory Dhuhr prayer, or not?

Answer: If he prayed the sunnah of the Dhuhr prayer at the first of its time, there is no harm in his making up the sunnah prayers between it and the obligatory prayer.

Question: If prayer within its appointed time is a performance in its time, and upon the passing of the times it is a making up, then how is the intention of the worshipper for the sunnah of Maghrib, seeing that it is not prayed except after the time of Maghrib has passed?

Answer: The sunnah of Maghrib is prayed as a performance in its time after the obligatory prayer, provided that nothing separates the two. And if it was some slight matter, and he returned while the congregation had not yet finished the sunnah of Maghrib, he prays it as a performance in its time.

Question: Concerning the worshipper who prays the prayer of the declining as a performance in its time, and when he has finished remembers that a prayer of the declining is owed by him as a making up: is it incumbent upon him to make what he has already prayed the making up and begin the performance afresh, or not?

Answer: That is not established. Rather, he makes up the one he missed.

Another matter: whoever prays what he wishes between the obligatory later Isha prayer and its optional prayer, and then prays the optional prayer, does not intend it as a making up, because it is called the night prayer.

Question: Concerning the making up of missed prayers at the rising of the sun and at its setting and at its standing at the meridian, and the offering of voluntary prayer at these times on the day of Friday: is that permissible or not?

Answer: That is disliked (makruh) on Friday and on other days as well.

Another matter: whoever prays the later Isha prayer with the imam while Maghrib is owed by him makes up Maghrib first, and makes up the sunnah of Maghrib and the later Isha as a making up, and prays the optional prayer of the later Isha as a performance in its time until the middle of the night, and it is called the night prayer. And whoever prays the Dhuhr prayer while Fajr is owed by him makes up Fajr first, then the sunnah of Dhuhr, then the optional prayer of Dhuhr as a making up.

Question: A man abandoned the prescribed prayer in the days of his youth and repented after a long period. How does he act? Does he perform the obligatory prayers of their times, or does he also pray the sunnah prayers and the optional prayers?

Answer: Everyone who abandons the prescribed prayers for a long period without excuse is not counted a Muslim during that period, because of the saying of the Prophet:

> ما بين العبد والكفر إلا ترك الصلاة

"There is nothing between the servant and disbelief except the abandonment of prayer."

And no deed of the legitimate deeds is accepted from him, because of the saying of our master, the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع):

> الصلاة عمود الدين وهي أول ما ينظر الله فيه من عمل ابن آدم فإن صحّت نظر في باقي عمله وإن لم تصح لم ينظر له في عمل

"Prayer is the pillar of religion. It is the first thing God examines of the deeds of the son of Adam. If it is sound, He looks into the rest of his deeds, and if it is not sound, He looks into no deed of his."

And:

> لا حظ في الإسلام لمن ترك الصلاة

"There is no share in Islam for one who abandons prayer."

His disbelief is in this respect, that nothing is accepted from him, that is, from the one who abandons prayer, because he is as a disbeliever. Is the poll tax then taken from him, or is he killed, or are his offspring taken captive, or is he reckoned impure like the disbeliever, or is what he slaughters not eaten? In all these matters his ruling is rather the ruling of the Muslim, because the two testimonies of faith have brought him within the ruling of Islam.

So whoever was in this condition and then repented and turned to the establishing of the prescribed prayer with an intention truthful toward God and in the seeking of His good pleasure, not for display and not out of hypocrisy, God turns to him in acceptance, for He says:

> يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا تُوبُوا إِلَى اللَّهِ تَوْبَةً نَّصُوحًا عَسَىٰ رَبُّكُمْ أَن يُكَفِّرَ عَنكُمْ سَيِّئَاتِكُمْ وَيُدْخِلَكُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِن تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ

"O you who believe, repent to God with a sincere repentance. It may be that your Lord will efface your evil deeds from you and admit you into gardens beneath which rivers flow."

He then becomes truly a Muslim, and all the legitimate deeds are accepted from him. He is not obliged to repeat the prayers that he missed in the period past, before his repentance; and he prays every prayer whose time comes upon him after the repentance, along with its sunnah and its optional prayers. And if he does make up what he missed of the prescribed prayers before the repentance, and asks God's forgiveness for those sins, he is rewarded. But if he broke his fast deliberately in the daytime of the month of Ramadan before the repentance, during the period of his abandoning the prescribed prayers, then upon him is both the making up and the expiation. As for one who has missed an obligatory prayer of the prescribed prayers, or several obligatory prayers, out of necessity, while he is otherwise constant in prayer, he does not pray the sunnah prayers of the prayer of the present time nor its optional prayers until after he has made up the missed obligatory prayer.

The meaning of the words, "O you who believe, repent to God with a sincere repentance," is: O you who have affirmed the two testimonies, namely that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is the Messenger of God. Faith in this verse is the affirming of the two testimonies.

Another matter: there is no harm in leaving the prayer when food is present, so long as the prayer is not missed, as the Prophet said:

> إذا حضر العَشاء ترك العشاء

"When the evening meal is present, the evening prayer is set aside."

This is from the text of the book of the interpretation of the Pillars: that a man prays in other than the time, meaning the latter time, and that what he has missed of the time, meaning the first, would have been better for him than his family and his wealth.

It is stated in the book Mukhtasar al-Musannaf: whoever wishes to join Dhuhr and Asr because of an excuse, and prays Dhuhr before the declining of the sun and prays Asr after the declining, repeats them both. If he prayed Asr first after the declining and then prayed Dhuhr after it, the Dhuhr prayer suffices for him, and he must repeat the Asr prayer. And if he prayed Dhuhr after the declining without ablution, and then performed ablution and prayed Asr, he repeats them both. And if the Dhuhr was rendered invalid by any invalidity whatever, the advancing of Asr before it does not suffice for him. Whoever is in doubt about the time because of cloud or otherwise does not pray until he is certain of the time; and prayer at the end of the time with certainty is better than prayer at the beginning of the time with doubt. Whoever prays part of his prayer, or all of it, before the time, and then becomes certain of this, must repeat it.

It is stated in the reports: whoever has missed a prayer prays it at whatever time he remembers it, except at the time of the rising of the sun, or the time of its standing at the meridian, or the time of its setting. So it is mentioned in the reports.

And it is stated therein: they agreed that there is no prayer after the Fajr prayer until the sun declines, unless one is making up a missed prayer or an obligatory prayer such as the prayer of the state of consecration, the two rak'ahs of the circumambulation, the two festival prayers, and what resembles these. They disliked voluntary prayer in that interval, except on the day of Friday, for concerning it they said:

> لا تسعر فيها النيران

"The fires are not stoked on it."

It is stated in the book Mukhtasar al-Musannaf: there is no harm in a people who have missed the prayer making it up outside its time, with the call to prayer and the summons to stand, in congregation.

Another matter, transmitted from a book of questions and answers found in the hand of our master Hasan ibn Abd al-Malik: as for what has come from him (ع), that on the day of Friday voluntary prayer is permissible from the beginning of the day until the Asr prayer, together with praying Dhuhr at the first of its time and after an hour has passed, it is nonetheless not permissible for him to offer voluntary prayer after he has prayed the Asr prayer. For whoever prays Dhuhr and offers voluntary prayer after it as he wishes until the end of the Asr prayer, then once he has prayed Asr its time has passed, and it is not permissible for him to offer voluntary prayer.

It has come in the book of Clarification that the Messenger of God joined Dhuhr and Asr, and Maghrib and Isha, without journey, without illness, and without any pressing cause.

And it is stated in the aforementioned book of questions and answers.

Question: What do you say, O you who are entrusted with the divine mysteries, concerning the prayer of the middle of the night, two rak'ahs? Are they prayed after the two halves of the night, when the one rising rises from his sleep, or only at the moment of its midpoint?

Answer: The time of the declining of the night is the more excellent.

And it is stated among the matters whose questions are from our master Sulayman ibn Hasan and whose answers are from our master Dawud ibn Ajab: "Your question concerns one who had cut himself off from prayer for a period of time, until God, glory be to Him, favored him with repentance and return, and who had not reckoned how much of it he had missed so as to make it up, and who perhaps prayed at some of the times and abandoned others without keeping guard over it. What is obligatory upon him? The answer is that if he repents with a sincere repentance and enters into the noble covenant, then he does not cut himself off from prayer thereafter, and there is no guilt upon him for what has passed; and whoever goes back to it, God will exact His retribution from him."

And our master and father Muhammad ibn al-Fahd ibn Salah wrote in his letter upon this subject.

I now set down what is contained in that noble epistle ascribed to him without reservation, which is a path of guidance for those who seek guidance and a disclosure of what is obscure in the questions of fiqh among the matters of religion.

The first question. You asked, and may God the Exalted grant you success, about a man who died and bequeathed three letters' weight of gold to whoever would make up on his behalf the prayer and the fast he had not performed, and you mentioned that this stated wage is small for the one who would undertake to make up that on his behalf. The answer to you in that, and God, glory be to Him, grants success in what is correct, is that it has come in the books of the friends of God that whoever has missed a prayer owes first the making up of the obligatory portions of it, then the shaf' and the witr, then the sunan. If the stated wage suffices to make up all of that together with the making up of the mentioned fast, then let it discharge this and this. If it does not, the prayer is advanced before the fast that is obligatory, and what remains of the wage goes to the making up of the mentioned fast to the measure of what remains of the stated wage. Nor is it hidden from you that if the deceased owes the making up of the prayer alone, then the obligatory prayers are made up first, then the shaf' and the witr, then the sunan, to the measure of the wage, whether that be half of it or less or more. And God, glory be to Him, is the Patron of pardon and of acceptance.

He, may God exalt his sanctity, said further in the epistle mentioned:

The second question. You asked also, and may God, glory be to Him and exalted is He, grant you success in what pleases Him, about a woman who had children from a first husband, and who then married another man after him and bore a son by him. Then this later son of hers killed her, not having reached puberty by his own account. When the matter of her killing was what it was, and she died, her first children performed the Hajj on her behalf out of her property, the Hajj of Islam only, out of all that is owed of the religious obligations. Then her later son, the one who killed her, regretted his killing of her, and he asked you what is obligatory upon him in his killing of her, of the matter that will deliver him before God, glory be to Him, from the consequence of that deed of his, and what he may do of good on her behalf whose reward will reach her, and likewise whether anything of the blood money is binding upon him toward those brothers of hers who have been mentioned, or not.

The answer to you in that also, and God, glory be to Him, grants success in what is correct in it, is that if his question is confined to what will deliver him before God, glory be to Him, from the consequence of that deed of his, then it is fitting that he undertake on her behalf her release and her discharge, which are known in the books of the friends of God (عليهم السلام). Then, as to what he wishes to convey to her of deeds of good, whether a charity out of some of his property continuing upon the believers, or other than that of the doors of goodness and righteousness, then above all the Hajj, concerning which our master al-Sadiq, Imam Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع), spoke when one of his companions asked him about it and said to him:

> يا بن رسول الله أنا رجل موسر وقد حججت حجة الإسلام وقد سمعت ما في التطوع بالحج من الرغايب فهل لي أن صدقتُ بمثل نفقة الحج أو أكثر منها ثواب الحج، فنظر عليه السلام إلى جبل أبي قبيس وقال له: لو صدقتَ بوزن هذا ما أدركت ثواب الحج.

"O son of the Messenger of God, I am a man of means, and I have performed the Hajj of Islam, and I have heard of the great desirable rewards there are in performing the Hajj voluntarily. Is it mine, if I give in charity the like of the expense of the Hajj or more than it, to have the reward of the Hajj?" Then he looked toward the mountain of Abu Qubays and said to him: "If you gave in charity the weight of this, you would not attain the reward of the Hajj."

So he is rewarded in what he does, and his reward returns upon him in that deed of his by what returns upon him of the mercy of God, glory be to Him, toward him. This is if she was a believing woman who prayed. If she was not one who prayed nor a believer, he makes up her prayer on her behalf, for prayer is the first thing God, glory be to Him, looks at of the deed of the son of Adam. If it is sound, He looks into the rest of his deed, and if it is not sound, He does not look at him in any deed. His making up of her prayer is according to what he is able of it, all of the obligatory prayers or some of them.

> وَمَنْ يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ وَمَنْ يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ شَرًّا يَرَهُ

Whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it.

He, may God exalt his sanctity, then spoke on the matter of the blood money, and I shall bring that forward in its own place, if God wills.

Our master the Judge al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad, may God exalt his sanctity and multiply his intimacy, said in a letter of his which he wrote to one of the judges in answer to questions the judge had asked him, among them: As for what you asked about concerning the time of Dhuhr and 'Asr, when the sun declines its time has entered, and one is not prevented from the 'Asr prayer in it in the presence; and that is the beginning of the seventh hour.

He said in the book of the interpretation of the Sharia: Whoever forgets a prayer, let him pray it when he remembers it, for its time.

He said in it: The times of prayer are five, hidden, not known except by an indication other than themselves. Such is the time of Dhuhr, which is that the shadow of everything becomes its own length; and the time of 'Asr, which is that the shadow of everything becomes twice its length; and the time of the Maghrib prayer, at the setting of the disc of the sun; and the time of 'Isha' al-Akhirah, at the vanishing of the white twilight; and then the time of the Fajr prayer, from the breaking of dawn until the sun is about to rise. These are five times for five prayers. The times are hidden and not perceived, and the prayers are manifest, because they are movements that are sensed and stillnesses that are felt. So these are ten, standing in every day and night: five outward and five inward.

He said in it that prayer has four boundaries, like the boundaries of a house, and they are:

1. the time
2. the qibla
3. the wudu'
4. the intention

He said in it: The obligatory elements of prayer are seven:

1. the takbir
2. the recitation
3. the bowing
4. the prostration
5. the glorification
6. the tashahhud
7. the sealing

He said in it: Prayer is not permitted except in its time. He said in it: The Fajr, its prayer is performed in the last darkness of the night. He said in it: The Maghrib prayer is at the falling of the disc. He said in it: The Prophet said:

> إذا اشتد الحر فأبردوا بالظهر فإن شدة الحر من لفح جهنم

"When the heat grows severe, wait for the cool for the noon prayer, for the severity of the heat is from the scorching breath of Hell."

He said in it: Imam al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah (ع) was asked about the Fajr prayer, whether it is of the prayer of the night or of the prayer of the day. He said:

> لا من صلاة الليل ولا من صلاة النهار، لأنها لو كانت من صلاة الليل لكان يجوز للصائم أن يأكل وقت الصلاة، ولو كانت من صلاة النهار لما جهر فيها بالقراءة، وذلك لأن الصلوات خمس: صلاتان من قسم النهار وصلاتان من قسم الليل وصلاة متوسطة بين الصلوات الأربع لا من قسمة الليل ولا من قسمة النهار، وهذه الحكمة البالغة. وصلاة الفجر فريضتها ركعتان بعد السنة، لأنها دعوة بعد دعوة متقدمة وليس بعدها دعوة، لذلك لا صلاة بعدها.

"It is neither of the prayer of the night nor of the prayer of the day, because if it were of the prayer of the night it would be permitted for the one fasting to eat at the time of the prayer, and if it were of the prayer of the day the recitation would not be made aloud in it. That is because the prayers are five: two from the portion of the day, two from the portion of the night, and one prayer set midway between the four prayers, neither from the division of the night nor from the division of the day. This is the far-reaching wisdom. The Fajr prayer, its obligatory part is two units after the sunnah, because it is a call after a call that preceded it, and there is no call after it, and for that reason there is no prayer after it."

Then he said: In the prayer of the night the recitation is made aloud. Then he said: Every prayer in which the recitation is made aloud is of the prayer of the night and of its portion. Then he said: The Prophet said:

> صلاة الغداة يحضرها ملائكة الليل وملائكة النهار

"The morning prayer is attended by the angels of the night and the angels of the day."

Our master Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman, may God exalt his sanctity, said in the Book of the Inward Nursing: The time of the first prayer is seven hours of the day. Then he said, after words he brought forward concerning the inward meaning: And likewise the time of 'Asr comes after the time of the first prayer, following it. Then he said: And there are no voluntary prayers after 'Asr. Then he said: Then after it is the 'Isha' prayer, meaning by that the Maghrib. Then he said: Then after it is the prayer of the darkness. Then he said: And the time of the prayer of the darkness is after the vanishing of the twilight. Then he said, after further words: Then after the prayer of the night is the witr prayer, and it is three units. In this unit, after the surah of al-Hamd, there is one surah in particular and not any other of the surahs of the Qur'an, and it is:

> سَبِّحِ اسْمَ رَبِّكَ الْأَعْلَى الَّذِي خَلَقَ فَسَوَّى

"Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High, who created and gave order."

Then he said: And he recites in this second unit, after al-Hamd:

> قُلْ يَا أَيُّهَا الْكَافِرُونَ

"Say: O you who disbelieve."

Then he said: And as for the third unit, he recites in this unit, after the surah of al-Hamd, the surah of sincerity in particular and not the other surahs, and it is:

> قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ

"Say: He is God, One."

Then he said: And in it is the qunut, the qunut of the witr. The one praying spreads out his hands after he has bowed and raised his head, and he supplicates while standing with the supplication of the qunut. Then he said: Then after these three of the witr there is a separation. Then two units which the one praying prays while seated in both of them. Then he said: And he recites in these two units of sitting these two surahs, the surah:

> قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِ

"Say: I take refuge with the Lord of the daybreak,"

in the first unit, and the surah:

> قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِ

"Say: I take refuge with the Lord of mankind,"

in the second unit, with these two surahs in particular and not the other surahs. Then he said: And then the dawn prayer. Then he said: And it is the middle one, of which God said:

> حَافِظُوا عَلَى الصَّلَوَاتِ وَالصَّلَاةِ الْوُسْطَى وَقُومُوا لِلَّهِ قَانِتِينَ

"Guard strictly the prayers and the middle prayer, and stand before God in devout obedience."

So the qunut is in the dawn prayer. Then he said: And it is four units, two units voluntary and two units obligatory.

The author of the Hidden Epistle, may God sanctify his spirit, said in it: God, mighty and exalted, imposed upon the servants five prayers in the day and the night, and they are seventeen units, and He commanded them to keep guard over them. So the Fajr prayer is from the first beginning of the morning until the rising of the sun; and the first prayer at seven hours of the day, at the declining of the sun, from the rising to the setting; and the 'Asr prayer at nine hours, and that is the time when the shadow of everything becomes twice its length; and the Maghrib prayer at the setting of the sun and the appearance of the darkness; and the 'Isha' al-Akhirah at the vanishing of the twilight and the settling of the darkness. These are its outward times, in which God the Exalted has commanded you to stand devoutly and obediently.

He said in it: And there is no prayer after 'Asr until the sun sets. He said in it: And there is no prayer between the adhan and the iqamah in the Maghrib prayer, as one prays between the adhan of the four prayers and their iqamah.

In the book of the question which is from the most eminent Shaykh Shimon ibn Muhammad Ghuri al-Hindi (رض), with the answer which is from our master Badr al-Din Hasan ibn Idris:

The question: What do you say, O you who are entrusted with the divine secrets, concerning the prayer of two units at the middle of the night? Is it prayed after the two halves of the night, when the one who rises rises from his sleep, or only at the moment of its midpoint?

The answer: The time of the declining of the night is more excellent.

Our master and lord Hibat Allah ibn Ibrahim said, in his answer to questions from his slave and the lowliest of his servants Muhammad 'Ali ibn Salih al-Badri, which he answered for him from the island of Yemen to the island of Hind in the year 1145 of the Hijra:

Another question. O our master, may God perpetuate your support, it has come in the books of the radiant Sharia that the fasts which men have missed are made up by men, but as for prayer, nothing has come concerning it. So, O our master, do women make it up on behalf of men, or not? May our master be gracious with a sufficient answer. And also, if a man becomes weak and is not able to make up what he has missed of prayer, does another make it up on his behalf, or not?

The answer to that, by the help of God the Exalted and the support of His friend and His full moon (عليهم السلام), is that the ruling in the making up of prayer is like the ruling of the fast: none makes it up on behalf of men except men. As for the man who is advanced in his years and has reached an age that weakens him from the making up of prayer, he prays what can be borne to the measure of his ability, and another makes up on his behalf, and expiation is made. And this is after he has become bent and it is known that his ability is slight.
