# Skins, Blood, and Waiting Periods

I turn now to the purity of skins, bones, hair, and wool, and I set down what I find in the book of questions and answers mentioned earlier, which its compiler arranged according to the order of the chapters of the book دعائم الإسلام.

Question: What do you say, O ray of the sun of guidance, concerning impure garments, or the skins of carrion, or the skins of beasts of prey, when they cling to the body, whether the skin or the body is wet? Must the body be washed or not? And likewise garments washed by the hands of the polytheists? Answer: If it clings to the body, the body is washed, and so also with whatever the polytheists have washed with their hands.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in تأويل الدعائم, that the goat is not shorn of its hair as the sheep is shorn of its wool, so that benefit is taken from it while the animals are alive, and that it is lawful to be clothed in it and to pray in it and upon it, and that the rest of the animal is lawful, its flesh, its skins, and its bones, and that these are purified if it is properly slaughtered? So, our master, may one pray upon the goat's hair and skins if they were taken from it after the slaughter? Answer: If its hair and its skins are taken after it has been properly slaughtered, prayer upon them is permitted. The goat is a kind of sheep, its private parts uncovered, its tail turned upward, and it has two horns; it is most common in India.

Question: Concerning a lawful animal, when some of its wool, its fleece, or its hair is shorn from it while it is alive: is that shorn material washed as an impure garment is washed, or not? And if there is moisture in it before it is washed, and it touches a garment or the body, does it render them impure or not? Answer: The text has come that prayer is not permitted in it until it is washed, yet it is not itself impure.

Question: What does our master say, may God perpetuate his guidance, concerning a vessel made from the skins of carrion in the district of Waghar, which is a large town whose people are mostly unbelievers, and the vessel is filled with ghee or with oil and brought to Ahmedabad, and then what is in it is sold? Is that permitted or not? And soap is made from it, mixed with lye and soda ash: is the sale and purchase of it permitted or not? Answer: If anything that is eaten is placed in this vessel, it has become impure, and one does not pray in a garment that such a thing has touched. Its sale and purchase are permitted, but the buyer is to be informed that the ghee or the soap is impure. It is not lawful, however, to sell or to buy the skins of carrion.

And from the book تأويل الدعائم:

> ولا يجز الشعر من المعز الا بعد الذبح فان جز وهو حيّ فهو ميّة

The hair of the goat is not to be shorn except after the slaughter; if it is shorn while the animal is alive, it is carrion. This text applies to the goat in particular. And from the same book: the goat is not shorn of its hair as the sheep is shorn of its wool, so that benefit is taken from it while the animals are alive, and it is lawful to be clothed in it and to pray in it and upon it, and the rest of the animal is lawful, its hair, its skins, its bones, and whatever else is of it, and these are purified if it is properly slaughtered.

And from مختصر المصنف: if a person's bone is broken, it is not permitted to patch it except with the bone of an animal whose flesh is eaten and which has been properly slaughtered. Whoever patches a bone of his bones with the bone of carrion, or with the bone of an animal whose flesh is not eaten, or with the bone of a dead human being, must remove it and repeat every prayer he has prayed, and the ruling authority compels him to that if his case is known. If that is not known of him until he dies and he himself did not know of it, it is not removed after his death. And one may not take benefit from the hair of human beings as one takes benefit from the hair of beasts.

And from تأويل الدعائم, concerning the saying that benefit may be taken from a covering made from carrion that does not adhere: the meaning is that it does not cling to anything pure while one of the two is wet, so that impurity reaches it. And from كتاب الأخبار:

> وكرهوا أن ينتفع بلحم الخنزير

And they held it reprehensible to take benefit from the flesh of swine.

I come now to menstruation and postpartum bleeding, drawing again upon that same book of questions and answers.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the Sharia, that the menstruating woman wraps herself with a wrapper from below the navel to the knees, and that her husband may have of her what is above the wrapper and what is below it? So, our master, may he touch her whole body short of intercourse? Answer: Yes, he may touch her body, but it is better that he avoid what is beneath the wrapper, for fear that the fluid reach her womb.

Another matter: if a woman menstruates and remains in that state for ten days, and after the ten days the blood continues for two days, or three, or four, or five, she does not pray and does not fast, and her husband does not approach her, and she does not make up the prayers after her purification, provided she is certain that menstrual blood is distinguishable from the blood of istihadah. If she is in doubt concerning the blood, then she is a mustahadah.

Another matter: whoever misses the prayer of the Feast of Ghadir and its fast makes them up after that day, on whatever day it may be, performing the make-up at the time of the sun's decline, just as it would have been.

Question: What do you say concerning a woman who becomes pure from menstruation or from postpartum bleeding within the time of a prayer, and when she has finished the washing the time of the prayer has gone out? Is she obliged to make up that prayer or not? Answer: If she was leisurely, she makes it up; but if she was diligent in her washing and did not finish it until the time went out, she is not obliged to make up that prayer.

And from the answers of our master al-Nu'man, may God sanctify his soul, to the governor of Zawatah, Khattab ibn Wasim: You asked me about a blind woman, and how she is to manage her menstruation. Command her to insert a piece of cotton or a white cloth, and to have it inspected by a woman whose religion she trusts. As long as the blood comes out upon it she is menstruating; and when it comes out white she has become pure, so she washes, prays, and fasts, and her husband comes to her. She does likewise at the beginning of her menstruation if she does not perceive it herself.

Another matter: if a woman becomes pure from menstrual blood in the month of Ramadan before sunset and fears harm from washing, it is permitted to her to perform tayammum and to fast, and she does not make up that fast. Likewise the man who has a nocturnal emission in the month of Ramadan: if he fears illness for himself from washing at night, he performs tayammum and fasts, and he does not make up that fast.

Question: Concerning the woman who becomes pure in the month of Ramadan from menstruation and fasts nine days, and menstruation comes to her on the tenth day, is her fasting of the nine days established or not? Answer: It has been mentioned that the least of menstruation is three days, and the least of purity is ten days.

Question: From the book of purification, that the most of the days of menstruation is ten days: if the bleeding exceeds this term, are her prayer and her fasting established, even if the blood is thick and foul, or not? Answer: Menstrual blood and the blood of istihadah have both been described, and the distinction between them made, so rely upon the text in that matter.

Question: It has come in the Sharia that the limit of postpartum bleeding is forty days, and in some books sixty days. Which of the two, our master, is more correct? Answer: This answer is from our master Hasan ibn Nuh in his own words, may God sanctify his soul. He said: the woman in her postpartum period reckons the nifas according to the duration to which she is accustomed, and the blood of nifas is distinguishable from the blood of istihadah. Whenever she becomes pure and is clean of blood, she washes, prays, and fasts, and her husband comes to her. And it is said in the book of purification: the woman in her postpartum period, when she has given birth, or has seen blood before delivery in a state of menstruation, continues with the blood until it ceases from her; and the menstruating woman washes when she becomes pure. If the blood is prolonged with her, she is a mustahadah, and the blood of nifas is distinguishable from the blood of istihadah, just as we mentioned the distinction of menstrual blood from the blood of istihadah. And it is said in مختصر الآثار: the woman in her postpartum period is like the menstruating woman, and in a state like hers; what is obligatory upon her and upon her husband in her affair is what is obligatory in menstruation, and she washes when the blood of nifas ceases from her. And it is said in مختصر المصنف: the woman in her postpartum period leaves the prayer for the number of the days of her nifas and according to what her custom has been, if she has a custom; and likewise in دعائم الإسلام, which sets her upon this same course.

I myself saw, in one of the books of the Sharia of the people of the House (عليهم السلام), the term of the woman in her postpartum period reckoned by days. It is mentioned there that the least of nifas is twenty days, the middle is forty days, and the most is sixty days; and that this is when the blood continues with her, so that it does not exceed sixty days; and that menstrual blood is other than the blood of istihadah; and that it never falls short of twenty days, and the woman in her postpartum period does not become pure before twenty days. This is the sense of the words of that book. As for its exact wording, I did not find it at the time of writing this page; if I live and come upon that book, I will set down its wording exactly, God willing.

As for the point intended, I have made clear to you that the woman in her postpartum period, when she is clean of blood, has become pure after twenty days. This is the ruling concerning her, decisively. Thereafter she relies upon what she is accustomed to. If the blood exceeds her custom, she looks to see whether the blood has changed or is as it was. If it has changed, then it is the blood of istihadah; and if it has not changed, then its ruling is as I described at first. And our master al-Nu'man, may God sanctify his soul, mentioned in المنتخبة:

> والنفساء تجلس قدر الحيض
> فان تمادى دمها بالفيض
> تريصت لمبلغ الشهـرین
> ثم قضت ما بينما الوقتين
> تطهرت حياطة لتسلمَ
> من الصلاة والصيام بعد ما
> إن استحاضت بعد هذا الانقضا
> وقد ذكرت حكمها في ما مضى

The woman in her postpartum period sits for the measure of menstruation. If her blood flows on at length, she waits until the term of two months, then makes up what falls between the two times. She purifies herself by way of precaution, that she may be safe, from prayer and fasting after that. If her bleeding continues beyond this expiry, I have already mentioned her ruling in what has passed.

This is when the blood of nifas extends beyond her custom and the blood is turbid, thick, and foul like the blood of nifas. In that case she holds back from prayer and fasting and keeps herself from her husband until two months are complete. After the two months, if the blood is still prolonged with her, she is a mustahadah without doubt. She washes and makes up the prayers and the fasts she abandoned after the completion of her customary term until the completion of the two months, by way of caution, because this is a place of doubt, and it is possible that she menstruated after the nifas. This is the ruling of nifas, and God and His Messenger know best. The answer of our master is completed, by the praise of God and His bounty.

And it is said in the book مختصر المصنف: if she begins the prayer in its time and then menstruates, she does not make up that prayer. If she menstruates for a day or two and the blood then ceases from her and she sees purity for ten days, that is not menstruation, for menstruation is not less than three days; and if she abandoned the prayer, she repeats it. If she sees blood for a day or two, then sees purity for less than ten days, then sees blood again, the first and the second are of menstruation.

And it is said in تأويل الدعائم that the word nifas was mentioned only in connection with childbirth, and that what is meant by it is the blood that occurs with it. This is made plain by what came from the Messenger of God, who said to one of his wives, when she was with him upon his bed and she started away:

> ما لك أنفست ؟ قالت نعم

What is the matter with you? Have you begun to bleed? She said: Yes. He meant that she had menstruated. And likewise the mustahadah, who is the woman from whom the blood does not cease, though it is distinguishable from menstrual blood. When her menstruation comes to her, the menstrual blood comes thick and turbid; and when the menstruation departs from her, the blood is thin. What is binding upon her is that, so long as the menstrual blood is with her, she is in a state of menstruation: she does not pray, does not enter the mosque, and her husband does not come near her. When the menstrual blood departs from her and the blood that comes to her thins, she purifies herself as she purifies herself from menstruation; then she is in a state of purity, praying, fasting, entering the mosque, and her husband comes to her. And whenever anything of that blood appears from her, she performs the ablution, for it is an ailment in her.

And from تأويل الدعائم, concerning the saying that whoever approaches a menstruating woman has approached what is not lawful to him and has done what he ought not to do: he must seek the forgiveness of God and repent to Him from his offense, and if he gives a charity along with that, it is good. And the blood of nifas is distinguishable from the blood of istihadah, just as we mentioned the distinction of menstrual blood from the blood of istihadah. And from تأويل الدعائم also: the like of that is obligatory upon the woman if she consented to him in it; but if he compelled her, there is nothing upon her. And if the man did not know of her menstruation and she concealed it from him until he had intercourse with her, the sin in that is upon her and there is nothing upon him, since he did not know of her menstruation. And the menstruating woman does not touch the mushaf.

And it is said in the questions that came from the most venerable Shaykh Shimon ibn Muhammad Ghuri al-Hindi to our master Hasan ibn Idris, may God exalt his sanctity. Question: What do you say, O support of the Da'wa, concerning the term of nifas? The common people claim that the limit, both its least and its most, is forty days. Answer: It may be less than that, except that forty days must be reckoned. And you asked, O magnificent master and forbearing scholar, about the menstruation that exceeds ten days: if it exceeds ten days, she is a mustahadah. And our master and mawla, the dear father Muhammad ibn al-Fahd, may God exalt his sanctity, used to say that if a woman sees blood after ten days of her menstruation she is a mustahadah, and she washes for each prayer time if she is able, and prays. If she is not able to wash, she performs tayammum and prays. If she is able to wash at one time and not at another, she does so; and if she is unable, she performs tayammum for the time in which she is unable to wash, and prays and fasts.

And it is said in the book of the question that came from the most venerable Shaykh Shimon ibn Muhammad Ghuri al-Hindi (رض), with the answer of our master, the moon of the world and of religion, Hasan ibn Idris, may God exalt his sanctity and grant us his intercession and his company. Question: What do you say concerning the one in a state of janaba, and the menstruating woman, who recites supplications or other texts in which there is something of the Quran, whether the reciter knows it to be so or does not know it? How is he to act: does he recite it or leave it? For it has come in the radiant Sharia that these two do not recite the Quran and do not touch it. And does the one in a state of janaba, or the menstruating woman, recite the tahlil, the takbir, the tahmid, and the tasbih, when all of these are in the Quran? And does such a one recite the basmala, when it has been said that it is a verse of the Quran? Answer: The menstruating woman does not recite the Quran, nor the one in a state of janaba, nor any portion of it, neither in supplication nor in books; nor does such a one recite the basmala with the intention of reciting the Quran.

> فتحقق ما ذكرته لك أيها الأخ وكن من الموقنين وأعبد ربك حتى يأتيك اليقين

So make certain of what I have mentioned to you, brother, and be among those who are certain, and worship your Lord until certainty comes to you.

I come finally to the mention of istibra, the clearing of the womb, and I say what is stated in the book of questions and answers already mentioned:

> من زنى بامرأة بكراً أو ثيباً فأراد أن يتزوجها فلا يجوز العقد الا بعد الاستبراء

Whoever commits fornication with a woman, whether virgin or previously married, and then wishes to marry her, the contract is not permitted except after the clearing of the womb.

And it is said in the book الاقتصار: if a man has a wife who has a child by another man, and that child dies leaving property, her husband withdraws from her until her womb is cleared. If she is pregnant, the child in the womb inherits from the deceased, provided his mother does not inherit him; otherwise nothing is cast into the womb that has no right in the inheritance. That is when the mother is a slave or a polytheist. If she is a free Muslim woman, then his inheritance belongs to her rather than to the brothers, and she does not withdraw.

And it is said in مختصر المصنف: the woman undergoing the clearing of the womb withdraws for two months. And it is said in كتاب الأخبار: they narrated that a woman who has reached maturity and has not menstruated is cleared by forty-five nights; and they narrated that a virgin who reaches maturity and has not menstruated, and is then deflowered by the seller, is cleared by nine months.

And it is said in كتاب الزينة, concerning the saying:

> الولد للفراش وللعاهر الحجر

The child belongs to the bed, and for the fornicator is the stone. He said: what is meant is only that he has no right in the lineage of the child.

And it is said in the book of questions and answers already mentioned: whoever commits fornication with a man's slave girl and then buys her from him while she is pregnant does not approach her until she gives birth. If she bears a child, its sale is permitted, even though the child came from him, because the child belongs to the bed, and for the fornicator is the stone.
