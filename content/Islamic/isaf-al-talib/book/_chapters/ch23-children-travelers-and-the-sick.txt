# Children, Travelers, and the Sick

I turn now to the time at which boys are commanded to the prayer, and to what belongs with it.

### The Time at Which Boys Are Commanded to Pray

Our master Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) said in his four hundred counsels of manners:

> علمّوا صبيانكم ما ينفعكم الله به

Teach your boys that by which God will benefit you.

And he said in the same place:

> علمّوا صبيانكم الصلاة وخذوهم بها إذا بلغوا ثمان سنين

Teach your boys the prayer, and hold them to it when they reach eight years.

Our master the noble da'i Ibrahim ibn Husayn al-Hamidi said in the book عبارة البشارة that Amir al-Mu'minin (ع) said:

> والصبي يتغر لسبع سنين ويبلغ لأربع عشر ويكمل طوله وعرضه وعمقه لإحدى وعشرين سنة ويكمل عقله لثمانية وعشرين سنة

The boy sheds his milk teeth at seven years, reaches maturity at fourteen, completes his height and breadth and depth at twenty-one years, and completes his intellect at twenty-eight years.

And it is said in مختصر الآثار: they are to be separated in their sleeping places when they reach this stage, as a precaution for them against the nearness of one to another, since they have drawn close to maturity and to the stirring of desire, lest something arise among them, when one comes near another, that is feared for them.

### The Prayer of the Traveler

It is said in the book of questions and answers mentioned above, which was arranged by one of its arrangers according to the order of the chapters of the book دعائم الإسلام:

Question: What do you say of a man who has two houses, one in Ahmadabad and the other in Nahrwala, and who lives some days in Ahmadabad and some days in Nahrwala? May he pray in full before he enters the boundaries of the town in which his house stands? Answer: If he stays for five prayer times with his wife or with his mahram relatives by lineage, he prays in full and fasts.

Question: What do you say of what has come in the Book of Purity, that one whose journey is a pleasure outing does not shorten the prayer? Our master, what is meant by a journey of pleasure? Answer: If he has land of the extent that would ordinarily require the shortening of the prayer, and he wishes to walk in it for pleasure, he does not shorten the prayer.

Question: What do you say of a man who prays the sunnah and the supererogatory prayer aboard a ship, and the ship turns? Must he turn with it or not? Answer: He ascertains the direction at the moment he enters into the prayer. If the ship turns, nothing is required of him. If he is able to turn with it and does so, that is better. If he is in an obligatory prayer, let him turn with it as much as he is able.

Question: What do you say of an owned man who is authorized to trade, or who is not authorized, and whose master sends him traveling in the month of Ramadan or at another time? Must he shorten the prayer and break the fast or not? Answer: Yes, he shortens and he breaks the fast.

Question: What do you say of the divorced women in India, when someone goes out with them on a journey for trade or for anything else, in the month of Ramadan or at another time? Does he pray in full because of their company, and does he fast the month of Ramadan, or not? And likewise if someone goes out with the collector? Answer: If their purpose was to go out with him, they pray in full. If they went out for something other than that, their ruling is the ruling of travelers.

Question: What do you say of this, that God has forbidden in the Quran thirteen degrees of kinship in marriage? Our master, with which of these degrees does one travel, and through which of them does one complete the prayer and the fast? Answer: Everyone who is of the blood kin is one through whom the traveler completes his prayer and his fast by his stopping for the five prayers in the place where that relative is. A man completes through his wife, but this does not hold through his wife's relatives, nor through those related by nursing.

Question: And one who travels from Nahrwala to Ahmadabad and meets one of his relatives outside the town, does he pray in full or not? Answer: He does not pray in full until after he has entered the boundaries of the town in which his relative is.

From another place: whoever leaves his house for a journey and spends the night in the city, or in the house of a relative, or sits a day or more, does not shorten the prayer so long as he is in the city; and if he goes outside the city and spends the night or sits a day or more, he does not shorten the prayer. The master of the ship and the merchants all pray in full before one of them sets out from Cambay to Aden, and nothing further than this is required of them, nor is anything imposed upon him after he reaches his family.

From another place: whoever travels, and the time of prayer comes while the clouds are heavy so that the qibla is not known, prays each obligatory prayer toward the four directions, and God, may He be exalted, is the more excusing toward His servant when He afflicts him. If he prayed and finished, and then the sun broke through and he knew the qibla and there still remained time for the prayer to be performed, he repeats the prayer. If a traveler alights in a place intending to reside there, and after two days or more a matter arises for him in which travel is unavoidable, he does not repeat.

Whoever enters Mecca prays in full and does not shorten, even if he sits an hour or a day, because of the word of God:

> سواء العاكف فيه والباد

Equal are the one who dwells there and the one who comes from outside.

Our master Isma'il ibn Sayyidina Hiba said that the prayer is completed in the sanctuary of the House of God only, and that in Mecca the Honored the prayer is shortened.

Question: What do you say of the shaykhs who have leave to travel about, such as Shaykh Abdullah ibn Nuh, who goes round among the villages to make his rounds among the believers, some villages being near one another and some being distant by the measure that requires shortening the prayer or more? Does he shorten and break the fast or not? Answer: This man is like the prince who goes round within his emirate, and it is not upon him to shorten the prayer or to break the fast. This answer is from our master Idris.

Question: Concerning the traveler who reaches his people before midday and remains with them until after midday of the second day, is that counted for him as a day and a night or not? Answer: If he intended residence until he prays on the second day, his prayer and his fast are complete.

Question: Concerning the relatives through whom the traveler completes the prayer and the fast, are they those whom God, mighty and majestic, mentioned in the Quran where He said:

> حُرْمت عليكم أمهاتكم وبناتكم وأخواتكم وعماتكم وخالاتكم وبنات الأخ وبنات الأخت

Forbidden to you are your mothers, your daughters, your sisters, your paternal aunts, your maternal aunts, the daughters of the brother, and the daughters of the sister.

And what corresponds to these verses among the men? And do the mothers of wives, and stepdaughters, and relatives by nursing complete the prayer and the fast for the traveler or not? Answer: The relatives who are of the lineage of the men and the women are those through whom the prayer and the fast are completed. As for the mothers of wives and those mentioned with them, no.

From another place: a man who enters the gate of the city or the village from a journey in the month of Ramadan before midday, and the sun passes the meridian before he enters his house, completes his prayer and his fast. Whoever travels and reaches the village in which his wife's kinswoman or his wife's kinsman lives shortens the prayer. But if the woman has come to her father's house yet does not dwell there, and she has settled as a resident, or she is in the house of the relative who is a mahram, and she is young and has not reached maturity, or she has given birth to that child, he prays in full.

Whoever travels less than two barids intending residence, and the time of prayer comes upon him on the road, prays in full and does not shorten. And whoever travels and covers one barid, his intention being to return, and he returns to his family, and the time of prayer comes upon him on the road, prays in full and does not shorten.

Question: It has come in the shari'ah that when a man arrives from a journey and enters the road of his quarter, he prays in full. Our master, are Kanbur and Sarangpur and the rest of the outskirts of Ahmadabad all one with it, each within its boundary? Answer: It is not permitted for the traveler returning from his journey to his house to pray in full until after he enters his house. But if he enters one of the gates of the city and has not entered his dwelling and fears that the prayer will pass, he prays shortened. Likewise, if he goes out from his house while he is in the city and has not gone out through any of its gates, and the prayer falls due while he is in the city, he prays shortened, according to what has come set out plainly and expressly in the book مختصر الآثار, the composition of our master al-Nu'man, where it is said: the traveler breaks the fast and shortens when he goes out from his dwelling and sets out upon the journey, until he returns and enters his dwelling, if he is pressing on in the journey. But if one of his blood kin meets him at a distance from his town short of the distance of travel, and that blood relative is resident in that town, that traveler completes his prayer.

Question: If a man intends residence in a lodging for ten days, and then goes out from it after four or five days have passed because a need has arisen from which he finds no escape, what is his state regarding what he has prayed and fasted? Answer: So long as his intention of residence is in his heart, he prays and fasts in full. And when he intends travel and departure from the place in which he resided, and he is setting out upon the journey, he shortens from the moment the journey occurs to his heart and he resolves upon it.

Question: If the traveler goes out from a lodging in which he was residing, before the ten days, to another lodging near it, with no more than the measure of a mile between them, does he intend in it a full ten days of residence, or does his intention stand complete by the fulfillment of the first ten? Answer: So long as he intends to return to the place in which he intended residence, he prays and fasts in full. But if he went out from it with the intention of travel, he does not pray in full in the other place unless he renews the intention and resides in it ten days.

Question: If the traveler has family in the village and he enters its edge or a lodging within it, but has not yet come face to face with his family and his relatives in it, does he pray in full or shortened? Answer: He does not pray in full until he meets his blood relative, if he is staying with his blood relative for five prayer times, unless it is in his intention to reside ten days; for when he reaches the village and intends residence, he prays and fasts in full.

These three answers are from our master Idris ibn Hasan.

It is said in the book مختصر المصنف: if the traveler prays in full, whether forgetfully or deliberately, and the time still remains, let him repeat; and if the time has passed, then no. Every prayer or prostration that becomes obligatory upon a person while he is on the ground, it does not suffice him to perform it while riding; and every prayer or prostration that becomes obligatory upon him while he is riding, if he performs it on the ground, that suffices him. As for the Sacred Mosque, there is no shortening in it, the one who dwells there and the one who comes from outside being alike.

It is said in مختصر الآثار: they said that the traveler prays the sunnah and the supererogatory prayer; and if he is journeying by day and the journey presses him, he prays the obligatory prayer as two rak'ahs and defers the sunnah until he alights at night, then performs it with the night prayer.

From the same book: the barid is four farsakhs, and the farsakh is three miles, and the mile is three thousand cubits, which is a thousand even paces of a single leg, from where the right foot is lifted to where it is set down. And if the place he intends is the distance of a single barid, and there is in it no family and no dwelling of his, and he intends to return from it in less than ten days, he shortens and breaks the fast until he departs. The traveler breaks the fast and shortens when he goes out from his dwelling and sets out upon the journey, until he returns and enters his dwelling, if he is pressing on in the journey. But if one of his blood kin meets him at a distance from his town short of the distance of travel, and that blood relative is resident in that town, that traveler completes his prayers.

It is said in مختصر الآثار that praying upon the beaten highway is forbidden. And it is said in تأويل الدعائم, concerning the supererogatory prayer of the Messenger of God upon his mount while he was heading toward Medina, that he would gesture with his head for the prostration, the qibla being behind him.

It is said in the questions that are from certain of the hudud of the Indian island, with their answers from certain of the du'at of the Yemeni island:

The thirty-fifth question: concerning the traveler who follows a resident imam, then gives the tashahhud after two rak'ahs and salutes while the imam is still completing the prayer, is the traveler's prayer sound or not? Answer: This is expressly stipulated: if he wishes, he withdraws after two rak'ahs, and if he waits for the imam until he salutes, there is no harm in that.

And it is said therein: Question: What do you say of the imam who leads the people in prayer aboard a ship seated, because of the movement of the ship or something else, standing being impossible for some of them while the rest are able? What is the state of their prayer? Answer: A dispensation has been granted for this on account of the ship, when they are unable. And whoever is able, let him hasten and pray alone standing.

And it is said therein: Question: What do you say of the copy of the Quran and of other books, may they be used as a pillow in travel and at home or not? For the common people narrate from the Prophet that he said:

> لا توسّدوا بالقرآن

Do not use the Quran as a pillow.

Answer: It ought to be raised upon the head, for the honor of the Quran and for good manners, but it is not obligatory.

Our master and mawlana Ja'far ibn Sayyidina Sulayman said in غرفات الجنان, in answer to questions from his servant, Sayyidina Ali ibn Sulayman, the author of this book, which he answered for him from the Yemeni island to the Indian island: And that which you asked about, saying, what do you say of one who shortens and prays behind a resident imam, and sits after his completion of the two rak'ahs while the imam rises to complete his prayer, and there is no one behind the imam except that one who shortens, does the imam say the takbir aloud in the remainder of his prayer or not? The answer concerning that is that there is nothing that obliges the imam to say the takbir aloud, and he lowers his voice with it, for his raising the voice was for the sake of the one following him; so when no follower remains with him in his bowing and his prostration and his rising, there is no sense in his raising the voice with it. Know that.

Our master Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman said in كتاب الرضاع الباطن: the sunnah of shortening is in three prayers, the first, the afternoon, and the late evening, and each of these has four rak'ahs as its obligation, which is twelve rak'ahs; and the shortening from each four rak'ahs is two rak'ahs, so six rak'ahs are shortened from them.

It is said in the questions of the most noble Shaykh Hasan ibn Nuh to our master Idris ibn Hasan, with his answers to him:

The eleventh question: may a man travel with his son's wife, or with his wife's mother, without a screen, and speak with them? And may he bury her and travel with her? And likewise, what is the state of kin by nursing? Answer: There is no harm in it under necessity, if none of her blood kin is to be found, and God knows best. Yet the better course is caution regarding these women in the matters mentioned, because of the saying of the Prophet:

> المتقي من ترك ما لا بأس فيه حذراً أن يقع في ما بأس فيه

The God-fearing man is the one who leaves what carries no harm, out of caution lest he fall into what does carry harm.

This is when one of their blood kin is to be found. But if none of their blood kin is to be found, then all of that is permitted, and God knows best what is right.

Our master Muhammad ibn Fahd said in his treatise entitled لاحبة سبيل الهدى للمهتدين:

The seventeenth question: You asked, my brother, about the traveler who travels the distance of a barid, then resides there eight days and returns on the ninth day from there. Does he shorten his prayer or pray it in full? For it has come in the books of the purified shari'ah that when a traveler travels the distance of a barid, going and returning within it, he shortens, because the going and the returning have become two barids for him; and it has also come in them that the limit of residence is ten days. The answer, and upon you likewise be the granting of success from God the Glorified toward what is right in it, and its truth is this: what is expressly stipulated in the books of the friends of God (عليهم السلام) concerning the residence mentioned is that its limit is ten days, just as you have said, and not less than that nor short of it. Yet the better course for one who travels the distance of a barid and wishes to return from it after some days is that he intend residence of ten days in it and pray in full. Then if it appears to him to return from there before the ten days mentioned have been completed for him, he returns, and it is not upon him to complete their residence, I mean the ten days mentioned, according to what the text has stated concerning this in the books of the friends of God (عليهم السلام); and his praying in full, as mentioned, is not invalidated for him.

And you asked about the prayer of a traveler leading residents. He said that this is permitted if the imam is among the men of excellence, and he salutes after two rak'ahs and those following him rise and complete their prayers; and this is particular to the people of the high ranks and not to others among all the hudud.

And you asked about the prayer of one who has performed tayammum leading those who have performed the ablution. That is permitted for the people of the high ranks, as has come down concerning our master and mawlana, the Da'i of the age, Ja'far ibn Sayyidina Sulayman: when he was in the Yemeni island and came down from the fortress of Tayba to the land of Haraz, he purified himself for the noon prayer of one of his days as others purify themselves, but that did not suit him, because of the scrupulous care he took in this and in other matters, and scrupulous care in purification with the complete purification was not available to him. So when he finished, he performed tayammum where no one perceived it, and then led the people in congregation. Then he said to certain of the most noble hudud of his da'wa: thus has the dispensation come for the prayer of one who has performed tayammum leading those who have performed the ablution, when some weighty matter makes it necessary for the good of the guiding da'wa and the raising of its standard, just as there is the dispensation for the prayer of the traveler leading residents.

It is said in the book أنس الأفئدة المنيبة that he, meaning Amir al-Mu'minin (ع), ruled that six do not shorten their prayers or their fasts:

1. the collectors who go round in their collecting;
2. the merchant who goes round in his trade from market to market;
3. the prince who goes round in his emirate;
4. the man who goes out in pursuit of game;
5. the shepherd who seeks the places where rain has fallen and the trees sprout;
6. the warrior who cuts the road.

It is said in the book of the question that is from the most noble Shaykh Shamun ibn Muhammad Ghuri al-Hindi, with the answer that is from our master Badr al-Din Hasan ibn Idris:

Question: What do you say of this, that there was a question from certain of the shaykhs concerning the women who are mahram, as to which of them one travels with, and with which of them one does not shorten the prayer by his stopping with her for the five prayers, and the answer given was that whoever is of the blood kin, the traveler completes the prayer and the fast by his stopping with that person for the five prayers, and a man completes through his wife, and this does not hold through the relatives of the wife nor through kinship by nursing? So make clear to us, our master, in detail, which of these women one travels with and touches with his hand or otherwise, and with which of them one does not shorten the prayer, and the detail of his saying that a man completes through his wife; for in this the feet of the deniers have slipped, and from you comes deliverance, O best of those who consider the affairs of religion and reflect upon them. Answer: He does not pray in full except when one of his mahrams is stopping in a place and the traveler stops for the five prayer times with that person; then he prays in full. Likewise he prays in full through his wife if she is resident, when he stops with her for the five prayer times.

It is said in the questions that are from certain of the shaykhs of India, with the answers to them from certain of our masters, the du'at of Yemen: Question: What do you say, does the resident lead the travelers in prayer, or does the traveler lead the residents? Answer: If the traveler is more excellent than the residents, he leads them and withdraws after two rak'ahs, and they rise and complete the prayer. Likewise, if the resident is more excellent than the travelers, he leads them, and they withdraw after two rak'ahs.

### The Prayer of the Sick

It is said in the book of questions and answers mentioned above, which was arranged by one of its arrangers according to the order of the chapters of the book دعائم الإسلام:

Question: What do you say of the sick man who is not able to pray standing, nor seated cross-legged with the bowing and the prostration? How shall he do? Answer: He prays seated, or cross-legged with the bowing and the prostration, for God is the more excusing toward His servant when He afflicts him.

It is said in the Book of Purity: and if he is not able to gesture with his head, he gestures with his eyes; and if he is not able to do that, he gestures with his heart.

It is said in مختصر الآثار: if the sick man is not able to pray anything but the obligatory prayer, he prays it; then when he recovers, he makes up, if he wishes, what he omitted of the sunnah or the supererogatory prayer, and in that there is merit and reward, though making it up is not obligatory as the making up of the obligatory prayer is obligatory.

It is said in مختصر المصنف: whoever drinks something by which he treats himself, and it brings about the loss of his reason, repeats what passed him by of the prayer while he was in that state. And likewise if he does to himself what brings about the loss of his reason, and it is the same whether in what he did he was obedient to God, the Glorified and Exalted, or disobedient.
