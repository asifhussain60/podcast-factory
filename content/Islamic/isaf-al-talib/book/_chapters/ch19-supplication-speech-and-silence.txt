# Supplication, Speech, and Silence

I turn now to the mention of supplication after the prayer.

In the question-and-answer book arranged according to the order of the chapters of Pillars of Islam, the question was put: what do you say concerning what is found in the books of the guiding call, that supplication is not recited after the afternoon prayer? Is what is meant the supplication of drawing near, or all supplications? The answer given was: drawing near and other than it, and if anyone does supplicate, no sin rests upon him.

It was asked further: does the worshipper supplicate after the obligatory afternoon prayer or not, and does he wipe his face at the place of prostration at the close of every prayer or not? The answer: it is not permitted except after the afternoon prayer and after dawn. Our masters, may God sanctify their souls, commanded wiping after the afternoon prayer and after dawn in particular, and this is among what they took from the pure Imams (عليهم السلام). It has been mentioned that one does not prolong supplication after the afternoon prayer, yet if he does prolong it, nothing rests upon him.

In the book of the interpretation of the Sharia it is said that if the prayer falls in the daytime the recitation is kept low, and if it falls at night, in the dominion of darkness, the voice is raised with it, and the dawn prayer, offered in the last darkness of the night, is announced aloud.

Our lord and master, the author of the Mustansiriyya Councils, peace of God be upon him, said in the Eleventh Council: having brought the discussion of the jurisprudence of prayer to the limit set out above, in which the outward has its exposition and its expoundings and the inward has its sign and its intimation, we follow it with what is now read to you, the mention of the supplication that comes at the close of the prayer, and what it contains of the seven and the twelve, according to the manner in which our explanation has proceeded in all else, for the benefit of those of understanding who take it to heart.

Of the seven, these are they:

1 – the supplication of asking (دعاء المسألة), in which the supplicant spreads out his hands and sets the inner surface of his palms facing his face;
2 – the supplication of seeking refuge (دعاء الاستعاذة) from what he fears, in which he turns his palms about and sets their backs facing his face;
3 – the supplication of humble submission (دعاء الاستكانة), in which he grasps his shoulders with his hands, and this is done only in solitude;
4 – the supplication of devotion (دعاء التبتل), in which he points with the index finger;
5 – the supplication of entreaty (دعاء التضرع), which is made by moving the finger;
6 – the supplication of fervent appeal (دعاء الابتهال), in which he spreads his palms and extends both hands together, raised toward the sky;
7 – the supplication of abasement in the dust (دعاء التعفير), in which he supplicates while turning his cheeks upon the ground.

For each of these seven supplications there is a mention in the Quran, which we shall bring forward when we return to the explanation of what we have read, for everything we have set down is of the nature of roots, like the roots of a tree awaiting the branching of its boughs and the bringing forth of its fruit.

As for the twelve, they are the foundations of supplication and the base of its building. Whoever desires that his supplication be answered should do as follows:

1 – send ahead of it repentance from what God has forbidden;
2 – or a charity that he gives;
3 – or a fast;
4 – or a prayer, for God, mighty and majestic, says:

> إليه يصعد الكلم الطيب والعمل الصالح يرفعه

To Him ascends the good word, and the righteous deed raises it;

5 – then purify his intention in the supplication and turn toward God with his whole heart;
6 – begin with the praise of God, exalted is He, and with extolling Him as He deserves;
7 – call down blessings upon the Prophet at the beginning of the supplication, in its middle, and at its end;
8 – draw near to God, exalted is He, through the imams of the religion, naming them one by one until he reaches the imam of his own age;
9 – seek access through him and through them to God in the answering of his supplication;
10 – then ask for the good of this world and the hereafter;
11 – seek refuge from the evil of this world and the hereafter;
12 – then supplicate after that for whatever he loves and wills.

Our master, the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his Four Hundred Etiquettes:

> واذكروا الله عز وجل يقبل دعاءكم عند ذكره وحفظكم ورعايتكم إياه

Remember God, mighty and majestic, and He will accept your supplication when you remember Him, guard His right, and keep watch over it.

And he said:

> تقدموا في الدعاء قبل نزول البلاء فإن أبواب السماء تفتّح في ستة مواقيت: ١- عند نزول الغيث ٢- وعند الزحف ٣- وعند الأذان ٤- وعند قراءة القرآن ٥- وعند زوال الشمس ٦- وعند طلوع الفجر

Come forward with supplication before affliction descends, for the gates of heaven are opened at six appointed times:

1 – at the falling of rain;
2 – at the advance of the ranks in battle;
3 – at the call to prayer;
4 – at the recitation of the Quran;
5 – at the declining of the sun;
6 – at the breaking of dawn.

And he said:

> الدعاء يرد القضاء المبرم فادعوه واستعجلوه

Supplication turns back the decree that has been firmly settled, so call upon Him and press your request with urgency.

And he said:

> ادفعوا داعي البلاء بالدعاء وعليكم به قبل نزول البلاء فوالذي فلق الحبة وبرأ النسمة للبلاء أسرع إلى المؤمن من السيل من أعلى الجبل إلى أسفلها ومن ركض البراذين

Drive back what summons affliction by supplication, and hold fast to it before affliction descends. By Him who split the seed and brought the soul into being, affliction reaches the believer more swiftly than a torrent from the height of the mountain to its base, and more swiftly than the galloping of horses.

And he said:

> الداعي بلا عمل كالرامي بلا وتر

The one who supplicates without deeds is like an archer without a bowstring.

And he said:

> من قرأ قل هو الله أحد إلى أن تطلع الشمس عشر مرات وإنا أنزلناه في ليلة القدر لم يصب ذنبا ولو اجتهد إبليس

Whoever recites, until the sun rises, the chapter beginning "Say: He is God, One" ten times, and the chapter beginning "Indeed We sent it down on the Night of Decree," no sin will strike him, however hard Iblis may strive.

I come now to the mention of speech and of acts within the prayer.

It was asked in that same question-and-answer book: what do you say concerning a man praying behind the imam, when the imam becomes forgetful and the man alerts him by clearing his throat or by something of that kind rather than by saying the words of glorification? Does anything rest upon him for that? The answer: clearing the throat cuts off the prayer when it is done deliberately and without excuse; he should alert the imam by the words of glorification or by a gesture.

It was asked: what do you say concerning a man who clears his throat often in his prayer merely from habit? Does anything rest upon him for that? The answer: if he does so deliberately, he repeats the prayer.

It was asked: what do you say concerning a woman who prays in congregation or alone and carries her child with her while praying, because his weeping cannot otherwise be quieted? Is that permitted to her or not? The answer: it is not permitted to her to occupy herself in prayer with such a thing.

It was asked: what do you say concerning one who prays with some food in his mouth, which he placed there because of a pain that had come upon him? Does deficiency enter his prayer on that account or not? The answer: if he placed it in his mouth out of necessity, nothing rests upon him.

It was asked: what do you say concerning a man who prays with his braid wound inside his turban, drawing it from the back of his neck around to the region of the forehead above the hairline, without tucking it into the middle of his head? Is that permitted or not? The answer: there is no harm in that. The binding up of the hair that is forbidden is that a man turn the hair back upon its root, gathering it to the middle of his head, and the like of that.

And further: as for the man who prays with something in his mouth resembling a gemstone or a dirham or whatever is like it, if it does not prevent him from forming the letters properly, his prayer is complete. If he has something in his hand that he is holding, and it does not prevent him from fulfilling the limits of his prayer, nothing rests upon him. Yet to be free of all that is more fitting for him.

It is said in the abridgment of the compiled book that whoever eats or drinks in the prayer, whether forgetfully or deliberately, has cut off his prayer.

In the book of the interpretation of the Sharia it is said that the recitation comes before the bowing. And it says:

> ولا ترفع صوتك فوق صوته

Do not raise your voice above his voice, meaning the voice of the imam.

And it says: the recitation in the noon prayer and in the afternoon prayer is not made aloud but is kept low, because these belong to the prayer of the daytime. And it says: in the prayer of the night the recitation is made aloud. And it says: the Prophet forbade wiping over the leather socks and forbade leaving off the audible utterance of the words "In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate." And it says:

> المصلي إذا كبر لا يتكلم حتى يسلم

Once the worshipper has pronounced the opening magnification, he does not speak until he gives the salutation.

Our master Jafar ibn Mansur al-Yaman, may God exalt his sanctity, said in his book on the inward nursing, concerning the meaning of prayer: the prayer has doors that are numbered, and their full number must be completed, and by them the prayer is made complete. They are:

1 – the ablution;
2 – the opening;
3 – the call to prayer;
4 – the standing call;
5 – the turning toward God;
6 – the recitation;
7 – the bowing;
8 – the praise;
9 – the glorification;
10 – the prostration;
11 – the testimony;
12 – the salutation;
13 – the supplication of the standing invocation;
14 – the supplication of the odd-numbered prayer.

These are fourteen doors from which the worshipper has no escape. They are the supporting pillars of the prayer, and without them his prayer is not complete.
