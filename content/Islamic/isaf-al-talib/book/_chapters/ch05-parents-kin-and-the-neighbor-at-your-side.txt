# Parents, Kin, and the Neighbor at Your Side

I turn now to the affection that believers owe one another, and to what follows from it.

Maulana Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) said in his four hundred manners:

> لا تحقروا ضعفاء إخوانكم فإنه من احتقر مؤمناً حقّره الله ومقته ولم يجمع بينهما يوم القيامة إلا أن يتوب، ولا يكلف أحدكم أخاه الطلب إليه إذا عرف حاجته، تزاوروا وتعاطفوا وتباذلوا، ولا تكونوا بمنزلة المنافق الذي يصف ما لا يفعل.

Do not despise the weak among your brothers, for whoever despises a believer is belittled and detested by God, and the two will not be brought together on the Day of Resurrection unless he repents. Let none of you force his brother to ask when he already knows his need. Visit one another, be tender with one another, and give freely to one another. Do not be like the hypocrite, who describes what he does not do.

He said in it:

> المسلم مرآة أخيه المسلم، فإن رأيتم من أخيكم هفوة فلا تكونوا عليه ألباً وأرشدوه وانصحوا له وترفقوا به. إياكم والخلاف فإنه مروق، وعليكم بالفضل، ترافقوا وتراحموا.

The Muslim is the mirror of his Muslim brother. If you see a slip in your brother, do not band together against him; guide him, counsel him, and deal gently with him. Beware of discord, for it is a straying from the path. Hold fast to generous conduct, keep company kindly, and show mercy to one another.

He said in it:

> إياكم والغيبة فإن المسلم لا يغتاب أخاه، وقد نهى الله عزّ وجل عن ذلك فقال: أيحب أحدكم أن يأكل لحم أخيه ميتاً فكرهتموه.

Beware of backbiting, for a Muslim does not speak ill of his brother behind his back. God, mighty and majestic, has forbidden it and said: Would any of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother? You would hate it.

He said in it:

> إذا لقيتم إخوانكم المؤمنين فصافحوهم وأظهروا لهم البشاشة والبشر، تفترقوا وما عليكم من الأوزار قد ذهبت.

When you meet your believing brothers, shake their hands and show them a bright and welcoming face; you will part from one another with the burdens that were upon you already gone.

There is also the book of questions, the question being from the venerable Shaykh Shimon bin Muhammad al-Ghuri al-Hindi (رض), and the answer from our master Badr al-Din, Sayyidina Hassan bin Sayyidina Idris bin Sayyidina al-Hassan bin Sayyidina Abdullah al-A'la. The question asked about the custom of the people of India, that some of them ask others for water, salt, food, and the like, treating it as permissible and giving it freely to one another, the askers not being unable to buy such things but simply not having them at the moment, and so they ask those who have them: is such asking permitted to them or not? The answer was:

> لا بأس به، هذا لصفوّ المودّة وحسن الأخوّة.

There is no harm in it. This belongs to the purity of affection and the excellence of brotherhood.

I come now to the righteousness owed to parents, both the spiritual and the bodily.

He said in the seventeenth council of the Mustansiriya councils, may God's peace be upon its author: the explanation of the seven obligatory rights we mentioned has now been completed. By that is meant the seven that correspond to the seven clauses of the testimony, matching the number of its clauses, which are not subject to dispute, and there remain its twelve practices, corresponding to the number of its letters, in the manner in which we already defined them. The first of these is righteousness toward parents. God Almighty said:

> ووصينا الإنسان بوالديه إحساناً، حملته أمه كرهاً ووضعته كرهاً

And We have enjoined upon man goodness toward his parents; his mother carried him with hardship and gave birth to him with hardship.

So He enjoined goodness toward both parents together, yet He counted upon the child the mother's deed and not the father's. And He said in another place:

> ووصينا الإنسان بوالديه حملته أمه وهناً على وهن

And We have enjoined upon man goodness toward his parents; his mother carried him in weakness upon weakness.

In both of these places it is the mother's deed that is counted upon the child, and whomever God has distinguished by mention, it is obligatory to distinguish with honor and righteousness.

He said in the eighteenth council of them: we had reached, in the matter of righteousness toward parents, what God counted upon the child from the deed of the mother, which earns her an added rank in righteousness and in dignity. So listen now to what is recited to you: in righteousness toward parents there are seven obligatory parts and twelve manners.

The believer must show righteousness to his father and mother, who are the cause of the appearance of his bodily form, who labored to nourish him with the most beneficial and pleasing of foods until he reached in it the utmost of hope. He must show righteousness to his two parents in religion, who are the cause of the appearance of his form in knowledge, and they are the Imam of the age (ع) and his proof. And he must show righteousness to the two fathers of this community, who convey to them the divine sciences, and they are the Prophet and the legatee, and the Imams from their progeny. It is related from the Messenger of God that he said:

> أنا وأنت يا علي أبوا هذه الأمة

I and you, O Ali, are the two fathers of this community.

The seal of this righteousness is a person's righteousness toward his own soul: that he make the truth plain to it and so establish it in it, that he magnify it for the soul and so awaken its longing, that he place fear in it, and deal gently with it, and deliver it from what would destroy it, and rescue it from what has bound it.

As for the twelve manners in righteousness toward parents, they are that he honor their dwelling place, that he rise for them when he sees them, that he answer their call, that he lower his voice in their presence, that he magnify them and not rebuke them, that he lower to them the wing of humility out of mercy, that he not walk ahead of them in light nor ahead of them in darkness, that he not show any opposition to their command, that he not say to either of them a word of complaint, that he desire life for them in their old age as they desired life for him in his childhood, and that he provide for the needs of each of them when they have need of him, just as they provided for him when he had need of them.

He said in the twenty-fourth council of them: among the obligatory rights there are three rights.

1. The right of God.
2. The right of parents.
3. The right of the child.

God has commanded that they be discharged, and He will not accept them unless they are discharged in full. The right of God is to come out from what God has forbidden and to enter into what God has commanded. The right of parents is thankfulness for what has passed and obedience in what remains. The right of the child is that he be raised with tenderness, nourished with food, and taught the Book and wisdom.

Maulana Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) said in his four hundred manners:

> من خالف والديه عقّهما

Whoever goes against his parents has been undutiful to them.

I mention next the affection owed to relatives, those of religion and those of the world.

He said in the nineteenth council of the Mustansiriya councils: as for a person's relatives, they are divided into twelve categories, the first of them being:

1. The children, male and female.
2. Then the children of the father by the same mother, who are the brothers of one stock, called الأشقاء (full brothers).
3. And the children of the father by another mother, who are بنو العلّات (paternal half-brothers).
4. And the children of the mother by another father, who are الأخياف (maternal half-brothers), called brothers through the mother.
5. and 6. The grandfathers and grandmothers on the father's side.
7. and 8. The grandfathers and grandmothers on the mother's side.
9. The paternal uncles.
10. The paternal aunts.
11. The maternal uncles.
12. The maternal aunts.

Whoever is related through one of these takes that one's ruling. As for the tie with them that has been commanded, it belongs to those who do good that they keep the tie with them even if the relatives cut them off, that they give to them even if the relatives withhold from them, that they draw near to them even if the relatives push them away, and that they love them even if the relatives abandon them.

Maulana Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) said in his four hundred manners:

> ولا يمين في قطيعة رحم

There is no binding oath in the severing of kinship.

I mention now the affection owed to neighbors, those of religion and those of the world, and what follows from it.

He said in the twentieth council of the Mustansiriya councils: following upon what we mentioned of righteousness toward parents and of the ties of kinship, among the practices of the religion that we set forth earlier, comes the safeguarding of the neighbor. God Almighty said:

> والجار ذي القربى والجار الجنب والصاحب بالجنب

And the neighbor who is kin, and the neighbor who is a stranger, and the companion at your side.

This is divided into seven parts and twelve manners. The first of them:

1. Safeguarding the believing neighbor between whom and you there is kinship; he has three rights, the right of neighborliness, the right of kinship, and the right of faith.
2. The second is the one between whom and you there is neighborliness and lineage, but between you there is no bond of faith; he has two rights, the right of neighborliness and the right of kinship.
3. The third is the neighbor who is a stranger, that is, the believing neighbor between whom and you there is no kinship; he has the right of neighborliness and the right of faith.
4. The fourth is the companion at your side, that is, the neighbor who differs in religion and creed; he has the right of neighborliness.
5. The fifth is the companion aboard the ship, the one who sits beside you for the length of your journey; he has the right of being near.
6. The sixth is the one who shares your litter.
7. The seventh is the one who sits with you listening to wisdom; his is the most binding sanctity and the most obligatory claim upon you.

As for the twelve manners in neighborliness, they are:

1. That the neighbor not peer into his neighbor's house.
2. That he not listen in on his speech within his dwelling.
3. That he not disclose any matter of his that he comes to know.
4. That he not let him hear from his house what he would hate to hear.
5. That he not show him what he would hate to see.
6. That he not withhold from him a household implement when he asks for it.
7. That he not act haughtily toward him when he is beneath him.
8. That he not envy him when he is above him.
9. That he help him when he seeks his help.
10. That he cover his 'awrah (that which should remain concealed) if it becomes exposed.
11. That he not injure him with any kind of injury.
12. That he bear his injury with patience.

> فهذه أفعال قوم قد امتحن الله قلوبهم التقوى

These are the deeds of a people whose hearts God has tested for piety.
