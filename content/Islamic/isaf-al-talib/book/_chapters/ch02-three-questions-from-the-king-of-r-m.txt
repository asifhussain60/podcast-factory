# Three Questions from the King of Rūm

I turn now to the guardianship of the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع), and to his love and his station.

Our master and Mawlānā, the long-serving preacher Jaʿfar ibn Manṣūr al-Yaman, may God exalt him and sanctify him and grant us his intercession and his blessing, said in the Book of the Foster-Suckling that Muḥammad is His prophet, and that God says of him:

> فان الله هو مولاه وجبريل وصالح المؤمنين

"Indeed God is his master, and Gabriel, and the righteous of the believers."

By this is meant the Imam of the believers, who is ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, the Imam of every believing man and every believing woman. And His words:

> وكان أبوهما صالحا

"And their father was righteous."

By this is meant an Imam. And His words:

> إن الأرض يرثها عبادي الصالحون

"The earth is inherited by My righteous servants."

By this are meant the Imams from among His servants.

And Mawlānā the Imam al-Muʿizz li-Dīn Allāh (ع) said in the Friday sermon, likening himself in the physical world to a pen in the spiritual world, and he set four ranks beneath it so that before each one a spiritual rank guides it toward the one beyond. He thus established the foundation facing the tablet, engraving in it what he derived from the spiritual world, just as a pen engraves in a tablet.

I now mention the answers given by the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع), to questions he was asked, and what followed from them.

It is stated in the book Uns al-Afʾidah al-Munība, in the account of some of the cases of the Commander of the Faithful ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع) and his remarkable rulings:

The king of Rūm sent a messenger to Medina with a sum of money and instructed him: "Go to Muḥammad. If he does not agree with you, then ask about his successor, and put to him proof." He was to ask three questions, and if he was answered, he was to hand over the money.

The man arrived in Medina and was brought before the Messenger of God (ع), and he asked about his successor. He was directed to Abū Bakr. They brought him forward, and the man asked him: "Tell me what is not God, what is not with God, and what God does not know." Abū Bakr grew angry and said to him: "Woe to you. By saying this you have added to your disbelief." The man left his presence. He was then directed to ʿUmar, who responded in much the same way.

Then ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās said: "What kind of men are these? They could not answer his questions, and yet they did not simply say, 'We do not know.' Instead they accused him of knowing falsehood. I know who knows the answer." He was asked, "Who is he?" He said, "ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib." They said, "Take us to him," and they all set out together with the man and knocked on the door.

The Commander of the Faithful (ع) came out to them with a pen behind his ear and his finger stained with ink. ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās told him the story, and he (ع) said to the man: "Ask what you wish." The man said: "Tell me what is not God's." He said: "God has no partner." He said: "Tell me what is not with God." He said: "Injustice." He said: "Tell me what God does not know." He said: "That the Messiah is the son of God. For God Almighty, glorified and exalted be His name, has said:

> اتنبؤن الله بما لا يعلم

'Do you inform God of what He does not know?'"

The meaning is that it does not exist, and God has no knowledge of what does not exist.

The man said: "I bear witness that there is no god but God, that Muḥammad is the Messenger of God, and that you are his guardian." He then handed over the money, and ʿAlī (ع) gave it to al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn (عليهم السلام) and told them: "Go and distribute it among the Muslims," and they did as he commanded.

It is also narrated from Abū al-Jārūd, from al-Ḥārith al-Aʿwar, that while the Commander of the Faithful (ع) was in al-Raḥba with the people pressing around him, some seeking a ruling and some seeking help, a man stood and addressed him: "Peace be upon you, O Commander of the Faithful, and the mercy and blessings of God." The Commander of the Faithful (ع) looked at him and said: "And upon you peace, and the mercy and blessings of God. Who are you?" He said: "A man from your flock and from the people of your country."

He said to him, "Are you from my flock or from the people of my country? If you had greeted me for a single day, you would not be hidden from me." Then he said to those around him: "Do you know this man?" No one knew him. So he said to the man, "These are the people of your country, and they do not know you. Had I seen you for a single day, you would not be hidden from me." The man said, "Safety, O Commander of the Faithful." He said, "Have you committed any transgression in this land of mine since you entered it?" He said, "No." He said, "Perhaps during the days of war?" He said, "Yes." He said, "When war lays down its burdens, there is no harm in it."

The man said: "Muʿāwiya sent me to you and commanded me to come to you unannounced, to ask you about things that Ibn al-Aṣfar sent to him to inquire about. He says to him: if you are the rightful guardian of this matter and the claimant of the caliphate after Muḥammad, then answer me these things, for if you answer me I will follow you and send you the tribute. And when the messenger saw that he had no answer to them, he sent me to you to ask you about them."

He said, "And what are they?" He said: "How great is the distance between truth and falsehood? How great is the distance between heaven and earth? How great is the distance between east and west? And what is this galaxy? And what is the rainbow? And what is the darkening in the moon? And what is the first thing that appeared on the face of the earth? And what is the first thing that stirred upon it? And what is the spring to which the souls of the polytheists flee? And what is the hermaphrodite? And what are the ten things, each harder than the last?"

The Commander of the Faithful (ع) said to him: "God has destroyed the son of the liver-eater. How astray he is, and how astray are those with him. By God, he freed his slave girl and did not do well to marry her. God judge between me and this nation, who severed my ties of kinship, wasted my days, denied me my right, diminished the greatness of my station, and joined together in contending against me."

Then he said, "Bring me al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn and Muḥammad." They came to him. He said to the man, "O brother of the people of Syria, these are the sons of the Messenger of God (ع)," and he gestured toward al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn (عليهم السلام), "and this is my son," and he gestured toward Muḥammad (رض), "so ask whichever of them you wish." The Syrian said he wished to ask al-Ḥasan (ع). The Commander of the Faithful (ع) said to him, "Ask him," and he asked him all of those questions.

Al-Ḥasan (ع) said: "O brother of the people of Syria, the distance between truth and falsehood is four fingers. What your eyes see is truth, and with your ears you may hear a great deal of falsehood." The Syrian said, "You have spoken truly, may God set you aright."

He said: "And between heaven and earth is the prayer of the oppressed, and the reach of sight. Whoever says otherwise has lied." He said, "You have spoken truly."

He said: "And between east and west is the sun's course in a single day. You look at it as it rises and you look at it as it sets. Whoever says otherwise has lied." He said, "You have spoken truly."

He said: "As for this galaxy, it is the lamps of the sky, and from it the pouring water descends. As for the rainbow, it is the bow of God and a safeguard from drowning. As for the darkening in the moon, the light of the moon was once like the light of the sun, but God blotted it out. God, mighty and majestic, said regarding that:

> وَجَعَلْنَا اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ آيَتَيْنِ فَمَحَوْنَا آيَةَ اللَّيْلِ وَجَعَلْنَا آيَةَ النَّهَارِ مُبْصِرَةً

'And We made the night and the day two signs, and We erased the sign of the night and made the sign of the day visible.'

As for the first thing that appeared on the face of the earth, it is the valley of Dāb. As for the first thing that stirred upon the face of the earth, it is the palm tree. As for the spring to which the souls of the polytheists flee, it is a spring called Salmā. As for the hermaphrodite, it is a human being of whom it is not known whether the person is a woman or a man, so the person is examined: if a man, he has the emission of a man; and if a woman, she menstruates or her breasts develop. Otherwise the person is told to urinate against a wall, and if the urine strikes the wall, it is a man, and if it falls back as the urine of a camel falls back, it is a woman. As for the ten things, each harder than the last: the hardest thing God created is stone; harder than stone is iron, by which stone is cut; stronger than iron is fire; stronger than fire is water; stronger than water is cloud; stronger than cloud is wind; stronger than wind is the angel; stronger than the angel is the Angel of Death; and more severe than the Angel of Death is death itself; and more severe than death is the command of God, Lord of all worlds."

Then the Syrian said, "I bear witness that you are the son of the Messenger of God (ع), and that ʿAlī (ع) is the guardian of Muḥammad (ع), and that he has more right to this affair than Muʿāwiya." He wrote these things down and carried them to Muʿāwiya, and Muʿāwiya sent them to Ibn al-Aṣfar. Ibn al-Aṣfar said: "I bear witness that these are not from you, and that they come only from the mine of prophecy and the seat of the message. As for you, were you to ask me for a single dirham, I would not give it to you."

It is also narrated from him that he said: ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb came to the Commander of the Faithful (ع) and said to him, "O Abū al-Ḥasan, there are matters I neglected to ask the Messenger of God (ع) about. Do you have anything concerning them?"

He said, "And what are they?" He said: "A man lies down and sees something in his sleep, and when he awakens it is as though he had taken it in his hand; yet he may see something that turns out to be nothing at all. And a man meets another man and loves him without knowing him. And a man meets another man and hates him without knowing him. And a man sees a thing with his eye or hears it and remembers it for a long age, then forgets it at the moment he needs it, then recalls it when he has no need of it."

The Commander of the Faithful (ع) said to him concerning that: "As for what you say about the thing a man sees in his sleep, God, blessed and exalted, said in His noble Book:

> اللَّهُ يَتَوَفَّى الْأَنفُسَ حِينَ مَوْتِهَا وَالَّتِي لَمْ تَمُتْ فِي مَنَامِهَا فَيُمْسِكُ الَّتِي قَضَىٰ عَلَيْهَا الْمَوْتَ وَيُرْسِلُ الْأُخْرَىٰ إِلَىٰ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى

'God takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that have not died, during their sleep. He keeps those for whom He has decreed death and releases the others until an appointed term.'

There is no servant who lies down to sleep except that there is in him a likeness of death. Whatever he sees in his resting place while his soul is loosed from his body is true, and he is in the kingdom; and whatever he sees upon the return of his soul is false, and it is the delusion of Shayṭān. As for your saying that a man sees another and loves him without knowing him, and hates him without knowing him, it is because God, mighty and majestic, created the souls two thousand years before the bodies, and settled them in the air, and they used to meet and scent one another as horses scent one another. Whatever among them came to know one another on that day is joined together today and loves; and whatever among them rejected one another on that day is divided today and hates. As for your saying that a man sees a thing with his eye or hears it and then forgets it, then recalls it, then forgets it again, there is no heart that does not have a film over it like the film of the moon, and when that film passes through the heart, the servant forgets what he has seen and heard."

So ʿUmar said, "You have spoken truly, O Abū al-Ḥasan. May God not keep me alive after you, and may I never be in a land in which you are not."
