# The Charity of the Head

I turn now to zakat al-fitr.

He stated in the first book of questions and answers, which is arranged according to the order of the chapters of the Book of the Pillars of Islam, the following exchange. Question: the custom in taking the fitra among the Indians is that they mix the four kinds of grain, rice, maize, wheat, and salt, and take their value in money, whereas in the radiant Shariah the zakat of the charity of breaking the fast is a sa' of dates, or raisins, or wheat, or barley. Is it sufficient for one who gives a sa' of barley, or not? Answer: the reliance is upon what is mentioned in the Pillars, and that is a sa' of wheat, or barley, or dates, or raisins.

> وَالفطرة من أطيب ما تأكلون وأزكى ما تدخرون

The fitra is to be from the best of what you eat and the purest of what you store away.

Whoever mixes the fitra from everything he ate during the month of Ramadan, that suffices him; and whoever ate nothing but barley, that suffices him. So verify this well.

Question: what do you say concerning a man who wishes to deliver his fitra before the entry of the month of Ramadan, fearing that poverty or travel may prevent him from delivering it at its proper time? What does the noble law say in this? Does it suffice him or not, since it has been forbidden that the obligatory duties be performed except in their times, or made up except after they have passed? And if that does not suffice on his behalf, it has come in the Book of the Pillars that there is no harm in taking the zakat from those who owe it before the time of its falling due when there is need of that. May the answer be graciously given in this, and what is the case of one who did it in ignorance, has it sufficed on his behalf? Answer: if he delivers his fitra to one who has the right to take it, it has sufficed on his behalf, because the prohibition in this matter is that a person should not break his fast until he has delivered his fitra. No fixed time was set in this for advancing or delaying it, and the obligation is its delivery.

He stated in the book Mukhtasar al-Musannaf: a dhimmi who accepts Islam before the break of dawn on the day of Fitr owes the zakat of Fitr, and if he accepts Islam after the break of dawn, nothing is due from him.

He stated in the reports: they narrated that the zakat of Fitr is paid on behalf of the mukatab. He stated in the Landmarks of Religion: if a child is born before the breaking of dawn on the day of Fitr, the zakat of Fitr suffices on his behalf. He stated in the Book of the Pillars: and likewise it has been said that it is paid on behalf of the one still carried in the womb, before he is born. He stated in the Selected Book: or a sa' of dried curd, or half a sa' of wheat. He stated in the Landmarks of Religion that the weight of the sa' is four and a half ratls by the ratl of Mecca, and he stated therein that its weight by the ratl of Medina is five ratls less a third of a ratl, and its weight by the Baghdadi ratl is nine ratls and a tenth of a ratl.

Our master and lord, the author of the Mustansiriyya sessions, peace of God be upon him, stated in the thirteenth session: since we mentioned in the session before this one the zakat that is obligatory upon those who possess wealth, we follow that with the mention of the zakat that is obligatory in every condition, and upon those of small means who give it in charity, and that is the zakat of Fitr. In it are seven obligations and twelve practices of the Sunnah. The obligations are:

1. that a man pay it out on behalf of himself;
2. and on behalf of the elder of his household;
3. and the younger of them;
4. and the free among them;
5. and the enslaved among them;
6. and the males among them;
7. and the females among them.

The practices of the Sunnah are that he bring it out from the middle sort of what he eats, and it is a sa' of wheat, or a sa' of barley, or a sa' of dates, or a sa' of raisins; half a sa' of wheat suffices him, and from anything other than wheat nothing suffices but a full sa'; and the sa' is four mudds. And that he hand it to the Imam (ع), or to whomever the Imam has appointed; and that its bringing out be before the breaking of the fast; and that whoever delays bringing it out make it up; and whoever lacks the food brings out its value in silver or in gold. As for the woman who has no husband with her, she brings it out on behalf of herself and on behalf of those she supports.

He stated in the book of the Inner Nursing, regarding the saying of the Messenger of God:

> الفطرة لا تحل لي ولا لأهل بيتي

The fitra is not lawful for me, nor for the people of my house.

Our master Zayd ibn Muhammad, may God sanctify his spirit, stated in the Concealed Epistle: as for the charity of Fitr, the Messenger of God made it obligatory upon every head among the Muslims that he pay his fitra, upon the males and the females, the small and the great, even the infant born on that very day; and it is paid on behalf of the child in his mother's womb. And he made obligatory upon every head a sa' of wheat, or dates, or raisins, or barley, or other grains. He stated therein:

> وسميت صدقة الفطر صدقة الرأس

The charity of Fitr has been named the charity of the head.

He stated therein: and it is paid before the prayer, and if it is paid before the prayer it is charity. He stated therein: and if the child is born after the Fitr, the fitra is paid before the Fitr.

He stated in the book Mafatih al-Ni'ma, which belongs to our master the Judge al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad, may God exalt his sanctity, concerning the fitra:

> فان أخرجت قبل طلوع الشمس كانت فطرة وإذا أخرجت بعد طلوع الشمس كانت صدقة

If it is brought out before the rising of the sun it is fitra, and if it is brought out after the rising of the sun it is charity.

And he stated, up to his words:

> ومعنى الفطرة إخراج الواجب لله ولوليه من الفكاك والقربان

The meaning of the fitra is the bringing out of what is owed to God and to His appointed guardian, as ransom and as offering.

I mention now various rulings concerning the zakat, and concerning the one upon whom there is no zakat, and other matters. He stated in a book containing legal questions, all of them marvels, another question: what do you say concerning a man for whom the zakat of his wealth has been made lawful? Answer: in that case he is a man who owns a thousand dinars and owes a thousand dinars, so there is no zakat upon him in it. He stated therein another question: what do you say concerning a man for whom the zakat of his wealth is lawful while he is himself well off? Answer: in that case he is a man among the polytheists who has found no Muslim to whom to hand the zakat of his wealth, so he has more right to it than anyone else.
