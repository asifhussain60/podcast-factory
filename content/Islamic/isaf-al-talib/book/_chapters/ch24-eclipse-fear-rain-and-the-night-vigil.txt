# Eclipse, Fear, Rain, and the Night Vigil

I turn now to the prayer of the eclipse, and I set down what is found in the book of questions and answers already mentioned, which one of its arrangers ordered according to the chapters of كتاب دعائم الإسلام (The Pillars of Islam).

Question: concerning the eclipse that occurred in the year eight hundred and thirty-seven, in the month of Dhū al-Qaʿdah. It began on Monday, two hours after the sun had passed its height, and the solar eclipse fell out in accordance with the reckoning of the table. Yet it is stated in the letters of the Brethren of Purity, in the treatise on astronomy, that a solar eclipse occurs only at the end of the month, and the cause of this is given there. This has happened repeatedly in years before this one, and we were fasting in the first days of the blessed month of Ramadan when an eclipse occurred, so that the believers were perplexed as to how the matter is to be answered.

Answer: a statement has come from Mawlānā, the author of the letters (ع), in summary, and the pure Imams and the ranks that follow them have explained it. They said that the eclipse of the moon occurs at the dawn of the fifteenth and at the dawn of the sixteenth, while the eclipse of the sun may occur at the beginning of the month or at its end. The matter is in the hand of God.

> ولا حول ولا قوة إلا بالله العلي العظيم

There is no might and no power except with God, the Most High, the Great.

Question: one who joins the imam at the third rakʿah counts it as the beginning of his own prayer. He bows with the bowing of the imam and prostrates with his prostration. When the imam rises from the prostration, he rises with him and prays the five bowings with him and prostrates with him. When the imam makes the testimony, the latecomer sits poised and ready. When the imam gives the salutation, the latecomer rises and completes what remains upon him of the bowings, and he recites the qunūt, bows, prostrates, makes the testimony, and gives the salutation. He does not count the first two prostrations which he performed with the imam, just as is done by one who catches the prostration at the end of the obligatory prayers with the imam.

Another case: whoever misses the eclipse prayer with the imam counts his joining as the beginning of his own prayer. If he comes before the first two prostrations, he prays what remains with the imam, and whenever the imam prostrates in the first two prostrations he prostrates with him, for it has been mentioned that one who leaves these is counted as unable. When the imam rises, the latecomer prays the five bowings with him and prostrates and rises with the imam; when the imam prostrates he prostrates with him; when the imam sits, the latecomer sits poised and ready; and when the imam gives the salutation, the latecomer rises and prays what remains upon him of the bowings, then prostrates, makes the testimony, and gives the salutation. He does not count the first two prostrations in which he prostrated with the imam, just as this is done by one who has been outstripped in the prayer and catches only the prostration and the obligatory portions.

It is said in the book of purity: the eclipse prayer is not to be prayed until the eclipse appears and is seen. It is not prayed on the word of an astrologer or of anyone else that it is occurring, and if a veil of cloud or anything else intervenes, it is not prayed until the eclipse appears and is seen with the eye.

I turn now to the prayer of fear, and again from that same book of questions and answers.

Question: what do you say concerning what has come in the book of purity, that when there is the crossing of swords, close fighting, and the thrusting of spears, they make their prayers into takbīr, so that they say one takbīr in the place of each bowing. O our master, do they shorten the takbīr as they shorten in the prayer of fear? Answer: he shortens in the takbīr as shortening has been made obligatory in it.

It is said in the book مختصر المصنف: if the imam prays the prayer of fear with three men while three men stand guard for him, that is permitted. If their number is fewer than three, it is not recommended for them to pray the prayer of fear, since the word "party" does not apply to fewer than three. And if the eclipse prayer and a funeral prayer come together for the imam along with fear, he prays over the funeral, and prays the eclipse prayer and the prayer of fear.

I turn now to the prayer for rain. It is said in كتاب الاقتصار, and in الأخبار, and in المنتخبة: then he faced the people and said one hundred takbīrs; then he turned to his right and glorified God one hundred times; then he turned to his left and declared the oneness of God one hundred times, raising his voice in all of this. Then he faced the people and praised God with one hundred utterances of praise and magnification, and extolled Him. It is said in the book of purity: he delivered two sermons in the same manner as is done for the two feasts; then he descended and departed, and the people departed. He delivers the sermon leaning upon an Arabian bow, if he finds one, as the Messenger of God (ع) did. And from the same source: if they go out on a day other than Monday, there is no harm in it. It is said in مختصر المصنف: whoever delivers the sermon before the prayer must pray and then deliver the sermon.

I turn now to the witr prayer, the two bowings of dawn, and the qunūt, and again from that book of questions and answers.

Question: concerning a man who is heedless of the qunūt. Answer: in the witr he must repeat, and in the dawn prayer he performs the two prostrations of forgetfulness.

Question: what do you say concerning a man who prays the witr and forgets the qunūt, then remembers it after he has bowed or prostrated or sat? Does he repeat the witr, or does he perform the two prostrations of forgetfulness? Answer: the qunūt is a supplication that is not to be abandoned deliberately. Whoever forgets it need not repeat, and there are no two prostrations of forgetfulness in the sunnah prayers. Whoever abandons it in the witr deliberately must repeat. The least of the qunūt is three glorifications or three takbīrs.

Question: when is the qunūt recited in the witr? Answer: the qunūt is recited after the worshipper says:

> ربنا لك الحمد

Our Lord, to You belongs all praise.

Let that be verified. And whoever prays the shafʿ prayer of two bowings and then occupies himself with something until dawn breaks repeats the shafʿ as a make-up. If he has prayed three bowings and only the sitting remains until dawn breaks, he does not repeat the three, but makes up the sitting with the intention of a make-up.

Question: concerning the worshipper who gives the salutation after the first bowing of the witr, is speech permitted to him, or does he join the consecration to the consecration? For it has been said that the prayer of one seated is half the prayer of one standing, and it has been said in another place:

> أن الصلاة تحريمها تكبيرها وتحليلها تسليمها

The prayer: its consecration is its takbir, and its release is its salutation.

By which of the two does one act? Answer: our master the Judge al-Nuʿmān, may God raise his sanctity, has mentioned in المنتخبة:

> والوتر قد يفصل بالسلام
> من غير أعمال ولا كلام

The witr may be divided by the salutation, without actions and without speech.

And if one is compelled to speak, there is no harm in it so long as the talk is not prolonged.

Question: is it permissible to perform the shafʿ and the witr as prayers in their time during the first dawn, which the Arabs call ذنب السرحان, before the second dawn spreads across the sky, or not? And is the dispensation in it that one prays the two bowings of the sunnah of dawn? Answer: the shafʿ prayer and the witr are performed in their time so long as the dawn has not spread; this is established.

Whoever prays the dawn prayer with the imam and bows before the imam, then raises his head and bows a second time with the imam, his prayer is valid, because his prayer follows the prayer of the imam. But if he prayed alone, he repeats the prayer. Whoever prays the witr and forgets the qunūt performs the two prostrations of forgetfulness after the salutation.

Question: if a man prays the shafʿ, then enters the mosque and prays the greeting of the mosque during the time of the shafʿ, does he repeat the shafʿ or not? Answer: he repeats the shafʿ, because it is not for him to pray after it until dawn breaks. When dawn has broken he prays the greeting, then prays the dawn prayer.

It is said in معالم الدين: the witr prayer is obligatory, and there is no dispensation in abandoning it; it is among the appendages of the obligatory prayers, even though it is a sunnah.

It is said in the book of purity: he recites in the first bowing the opening of the Book and the surah:

> قل أعوذ برب الفلق

Say: I seek refuge with the Lord of the daybreak.

And in the second, the opening of the Book and the surah:

> قل أعوذ برب الناس

Say: I seek refuge with the Lord of mankind.

Then he makes the testimony and gives the salutation. These two bowings are part of the witr. He sits cross-legged in them as we have mentioned, spreading his palms upon his thighs with the tips of his fingers upon his knees, and he recites. When he has finished the recitation he bows and places the inner surfaces of his palms upon his knees, still seated cross-legged. When he raises his head from the bowing, he draws his right foot back behind him, sets his feet upright, prostrates, and then sits for the testimony as one sits in prayer.

Our master Muḥammad ibn Fahd, may God raise his sanctity, said in his noble treatise, which he named لاحبة سبيل الهدى للمهتدين, in the twenty-second question: you also asked about one who prays the shafʿ and the witr, then sits afterward for as long as is easy for him, then rises and prays the dawn, its sunnah and its obligation, and afterward it becomes clear to him that he was still in the night and its dawn had not yet come. What is his condition in that prayer of his? The answer in this is that his shafʿ and his witr stand confirmed, and he must repeat the dawn, its sunnah and its obligation. Unless the dawn was close upon him, in which case the sunnah prayer also stands confirmed for him, because it has come in the books of the friends of God (عليهم السلام) that it is permissible to pray it before dawn, on account of the noble meaning beneath this that permits it. So know this.

The Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع), said in his four hundred points of conduct:

> توكلوا على الله عند ركعتي الفجر بعد فراغكم منها ففيها تعطى الرغايب

Place your trust in God at the two bowings of dawn after you have finished them, for in them the things most desired are granted.

Our master and Mawlānā Sharaf al-Dīn Jaʿfar, son of our master Sulaymān, may God raise their sanctity in the chambers of the Garden, said in his book named الحدائق الأنيقة النفسانية المشتملة على الجوابات البرهانية, drawn from the answers of our master Dāʾūd ibn ʿAjab to his son in religion, Sulaymān ibn Ḥasan, what is here recounted of his chosen words: as for what you asked concerning one who owes a make-up of the witr, is it permissible for him to pray voluntary prayers or not, and concerning one who owes a make-up of the sunnah prayers? Answer: that is permissible, although it is better that he give precedence to the witr as a make-up before it.

I turn now to the sunnah and the voluntary prayers, and again from that book of questions and answers.

Question: what do you say concerning the prayer of opening, the prayer of glorification, and the prayer of night vigil? Has anything fixed come regarding their recitation, and what ought to be recited in them of the surahs? Answer: nothing fixed has come. They are like the sunnah and voluntary prayers: one recites in them whatever one wishes.

Question: what do you say concerning the witr prayer and the prayer of the two feasts? Is it permissible for one who has not memorized the appointed portion of the Quran to recite something else in them? Answer: if he has not memorized it, he recites what is easy for him and does not abandon the prayer.

Question: what do you say concerning the supplications of the night vigil? Is it permissible for one who has not memorized them to intend a voluntary prayer? Answer: he intends the prayer of the night vigil and supplicates with the supplication if he has memorized it; otherwise he supplicates with what is easy for him, and he intends the prayer of the night vigil. It is twelve bowings, and it has two times: one time is between the sunset prayer and the evening prayer, which a person prays with the short surahs of the detailed portion.

Question: what do you say concerning the tarāwīḥ prayer, when the believers pray with the imam for the sake of protective concealment with the intention of a voluntary prayer, while the imam recites the Quran from its top to its bottom, and this is not the practice in the obligatory prayers, and particularly not in the witr, the sunnah of dawn, and the greeting of the mosque? Answer: there is no harm in it for the sake of protective concealment, but they do not follow him as a leader.

Question: it is mentioned in كتاب الشواهد والبيان that the worshipper prays the noon prayer as ten bowings, the afternoon prayer as eight bowings, the sunset prayer as five bowings, and the evening prayer as ten bowings. Answer: the reliance is upon الدعائم, which is the gathering place of the questions. And if a person leaves anything of the sunnah for protective concealment, he makes it up.

Question: it has come concerning the prayer of glorification that when he rises from the second prostration he glorifies ten times. O our master, how does he glorify: does he glorify seated upon his feet, or standing? Answer: when one who prays the prayer of glorification wishes to do this, he sits poised and ready after he raises his head from the second prostration and glorifies ten times, then rises for the second bowing. Likewise after the second bowing he sits poised and ready after the second prostration and glorifies ten times, then sits settled and makes the testimony and gives the salutation. He does the same in the last two bowings. But this practice is irregular. As for that upon which the practice rests in the prayer of glorification, it is that he sits settled after raising his head from the second prostration in each bowing and glorifies ten times, then rises for the second, or makes the testimony after the glorification following the second, remaining settled in his sitting during both the glorification and the testimony.

Question: it has come concerning the prayer of sufficiency that in the first bowing one recites the opening of the Book, and the chapter of sincerity, and the chapter of the disbelievers, and the verse of the Throne, once each; and in the second, twice each; and in the third, three times each; and in the fourth, four times each. O our master, does he repeat the opening of the Book in each bowing, or is it once in each bowing? Kindly give the answer. Answer: he recites the opening of the Book once in each bowing, and God knows best.

It is said in مختصر الآثار: whoever has not learned the Quran, let him recite what is easy of it. God, Exalted is He, said:

> فأقرءوا ما تيسر من القرآن

So recite what is easy of the Quran.

And whoever wishes may stand the whole night with a single surah, repeating it, or with part of one, or with two surahs or more than that.

It is said in تأويل الدعائم, concerning the voluntary prayer of the Messenger of God (ع) upon his mount while he was making his way toward Medina: he would gesture with his head in indication of the prostration, with the direction of prayer behind him.

It is said in the abridged discourse on jurisprudence, whose questions are from the most honorable Shaykh Ḥasan, son of the Mawlā Ādam al-Hindī, and whose answers are from the masters of Yemen, may God sanctify all their spirits: whoever prays between the obligation of the later evening prayer and its voluntary prayer whatever he wishes, then prays the voluntary prayer, does not intend it as a make-up, for it is called the prayer of the night.

Our master and Mawlānā Jaʿfar, son of our master Sulaymān, may God raise their sanctity in the Gardens, wrote in like manner to his servant, the compiler of this book, from the island of Yemen to the island of Hind, concerning the matter of the prayer of Umm Dāʾūd, which is known among all who are of the ranks, since he had asked him about it so that the sources and the paths might be made clear by his sufficient answer. He mentioned what concerns the prayer of Umm Dāʾūd and the supplication of opening, and that Qāsim ibn Fīr, the summoner of the maimed and blinded Dāʾūdī faction, commanded his companions to pray it, to fast it, and to supplicate with it in the month of Rabīʿ al-Awwal of the year 1042, when the fifteenth of it fell on a Friday and they were in prison, and likewise in the honored month of Ramadan of that year. You asked about that, and about its permissibility and its practice, and I answered the one who asked you about it that Mawlānā al-Ṣādiq (ع) gave no text on the aforementioned prayer of Umm Dāʾūd except for the month of Rajab, and he commanded her to purify herself. And when I asked him about it, and whether that supplication may be recited in a month other than Rajab, he said:

> في يوم عرفة وإذا كان يوم جمعة لم يفرغ صاحبه الا وقد غفر له

On the day of ʿArafah; and if it falls on a Friday, the one who performs it does not finish until he has been forgiven.

So the matter in this is as stated, for there is nothing outward that does not have a sign by which it is signified, and there is no practice except upon the text in this. In the command to Umm Dāʾūd to seek forgiveness until the month of Rajab, for the supplication of opening already mentioned, there is sufficiency and satisfaction; and in the text after that upon the day of ʿArafah when it is not a Friday, and, if it is a Friday, as he mentioned, there is likewise satisfaction and sufficiency, meaning that it is only upon that mentioned day. As for anything other than that, no one should proceed upon it except by a text. If they possess a text in the matter, let it be followed; and if not, then little practice, as the Prophet (ع) said:

> عمل قليل في سنّة خير من عمل كثير في بدعة

A little practice within the sunnah is better than much practice within innovation.

You mentioned whether on the day of ʿArafah one supplicates with that mentioned supplication or prays that mentioned prayer in it. The root of this is that the mentioned supplication has as its condition the mentioned prayer, for he said:

> فأين أنت من دعاء الاستفتاح

Where are you with regard to the supplication of opening?

And when I asked him about it, he made it known to her by that mentioned prayer, then by the mentioned supplication. So if you pray in it, its prayer is upon that; and if one supplicates with it in it, it is accepted for its excellence and its nobility. God knows best and judges best, and from His friends we learn in order to know, and God grants success to what is right and guides to the best of means.

It is said in the questions that are from some of the shaykhs of Hind (رض), with the answers that are from some of our masters, the summoners of Yemen, may God raise their sanctity. Question: most of the believers do not keep the sunnah fasts as they pray the sunnah prayers. Answer: it is permissible for them to omit the sunnah fast, and there is no make-up upon them. As for the sunnah prayer, whoever has missed it ought to make it up. So the Imams (عليهم السلام) and their followers did, and they have reward from God if they fast.

Our master and Mawlānā Hibat Allāh, son of our master Ibrāhīm, said in his answer to questions from his servant and the humblest of his slaves, Ṣāliḥ ibn Aḥmad, answering him from the island of Yemen to the island of Hind in the year 1135: you asked concerning what has come in the books, that the prophets, such as Jesus and others, and the Imams, such as Mawlānā ʿAlī and Mawlānā Zayn al-ʿĀbidīn and others among the Imams, the blessings of God be upon them all, at the time of a great event would call for water, perform the ablution, and pray two bowings, and in some instances four bowings, for the relief of distress. It has not been made clear at what time they prayed. Is that prayer to be prayed at all times of trial, affliction, and calamity, or only at a time in which the sunnah and the voluntary prayers are permitted? May the master and owner graciously give the answer.

Answer: by the help of God, Exalted is He, and the favor of His friend upon His earth (ع), and by the instruction of my master Badr al-Dīn, the inspired and the helper: the prayer of the prophets and the prayer of the Imams, the blessings and peace and benedictions of God be upon them all, flows down to us from them at times of grave matters and events, and at the descent of calamities and disasters. Their prayer is praise of God and thanksgiving for what He disclosed to them of the secret of affairs, and for their knowledge that those things do not come to pass except by a measure decreed. You mentioned that they, the blessings of God be upon them, did not inform us at what times it is to be prayed, but only reported it in a summary way. Yes, it is permissible to pray it at all the times in which the sunnah and voluntary prayers are permitted, and prohibition has come only concerning the forbidden times, which are not hidden from one of clear mind and illumined form. And when their servants and their followers wish to pray that mentioned prayer, and by it to repel all calamities and to be shielded by it from all evils and ruinous things, it is for them to be guided by their guidance and to follow their practice. May God, Mighty and Majestic, keep us firm upon obedience to them, grant us their accepted intercession, and supply us from their secret and their blessings. He is the Guardian of that and the One able to do it, by the right that is His and the secret that is with Him.

I turn now to the prostration of the Quran, and again from that book of questions and answers.

Question: what do you say concerning one who recites a supplication in which there is a verse of prostration? Is it obligatory upon him to prostrate or not? Answer: if it is among the binding ones, he must prostrate.

Question: what do you say concerning the teacher of boys, when he is teaching them the Quran and passes over the places of prostration while instructing them? Does he prostrate every time he passes over them, and do those who hear him prostrate as well, or not? Answer: he has a choice in that: if he wishes, he prostrates, and if he wishes, he leaves it.

Another case: a man says in the prostration of the Quran what he says in the prostration of prayer.

It is said in مختصر المصنف: when the reciter recites one of the binding prostrations, whoever is present prostrates. If that same prostration is recited again, none of them is required to prostrate a second time so long as he remains in that place. As for the remaining prostrations, whoever wishes to prostrate may prostrate, and whoever wishes may leave it, and prostrating is better.

Mawlānā, the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع), said in his four hundred points of conduct:

> أطيلوا السجود فما من عمل أشد على إبليس من أن يرى ابن آدم ساجداً فأنه أمر بالسجود فعصى وأمر ابن آدم بالسجود فأطاع ونجا

Prolong the prostration, for there is no act harder upon Iblis than to see the son of Adam prostrating. He himself was commanded to prostrate and disobeyed, while the son of Adam was commanded to prostrate, and he obeyed and was saved.
