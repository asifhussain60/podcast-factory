# The Quarrel Over the Crescent

I turn now to the sighting of the crescent moon: what has been said about it, and what runs along the same course.

I find in the Book of Clarification that what has come down concerning fasting by the sighting of the crescent, together with what is mentioned in this chapter and in the two chapters before it, is directed to whoever is absent from the presence of the Imam and does not know the measures of the crescent moons. Our master and mawlana Sulayman ibn Hasan, may God exalt his sanctity in the chambers of the Gardens of Eden, treated this matter at the opening of his noble epistle known as the Ijadiyya, containing the Ahmadi secrets and the golden jewels drawn from the most glorious benefits. After he had spoken there of what is prior and more fitting, and after the restatement of both, he explained that when the moon has traveled twenty-eight stations after parting from the sun, it requires the traversal of the first two stations in order to overtake the sun once more, because during that period the sun has itself advanced two complete stations and a degree of the third. The moon therefore meets the sun at thirty stations, and the reckoners count this as one month.

Our master and mawlana, the most exalted summoner al-Mu'ayyad fi'l-Din, may God exalt his sanctity, spoke on this in the forty-second session of the first hundred of his noble Mu'ayyadi sessions, the ones known as the Wisdom Sessions, in refutation of those among the people who make fasting depend upon the sighting of the crescent.

The claimants have claimed that the month of Ramadan is sometimes complete and sometimes deficient, and that its fast is founded upon the sighting of the crescent. They have argued for this by the saying of the Prophet (ع):

> صوموا لرؤيته وأفطروا لرؤيته فإن غم عليكم فأكملوا ثلاثين

Fast when it is seen and break your fast when it is seen; and if it is hidden from you, then complete thirty.

This statement of theirs is unsound in several respects, which we shall set out, and we shall establish the proofs that the month of Ramadan is always complete and that no deficiency befalls it in any circumstance whatever.

We begin with the reply to whoever argues from the report "fast when it is seen and break your fast when it is seen." We say to them: you yourselves acknowledge that the sense of this report is that the Messenger of Allah (ع), when he wished to set out on one of the campaigns near the month of Ramadan, was approached by his companions, who gathered to him and said:

> يا رسول الله كنا نصوم بصومك ونفطر بإفطارك فكيف يجري حالنا في غيبتك

O Messenger of Allah, we used to fast by your fasting and break our fast by your breaking of the fast; how then shall our case run in your absence?

The Messenger of Allah (ع) said: fast when it is seen and break your fast when it is seen. The report thus indicates that fasting is obligatory by the fasting of the Messenger when he is present, or by the fasting of the one who stands in his place when he is absent, and that breaking the fast is obligatory by his breaking of the fast, and that fasting upon the sighting of the crescent belongs to the matters of necessity at the time when the Messenger and the Imam who stands in his place are not present. When the Messenger is present, or the Imam is present, the rule of the obligation is that one fasts by his fasting and breaks the fast by his breaking of it, just as the people said to the Prophet (ع): we used to fast by your fasting and break our fast by your breaking of the fast.

As for the saying of the one who claims that the Prophet (ع) used to fast by the sighting of the crescent, it is unsound in three respects, two of them legal and one rational.

As for the first of the two legal respects, it is well known that the Prophet (ع) used to say, and he is truthful, that the Trustworthy Spirit, Gabriel (ع), came upon him morning and evening with revelation and with the noble Quran. And one to whom Gabriel (ع) comes morning and evening with the tidings of heaven has no need to turn his face about seeking the crescent, when he has with him one who brings him certain news. Were he to go out toward the sky seeking a crescent, it would nullify his own saying that Gabriel descends upon him, and it would be said to him: why did you not ask him about it? So his independence of any searching is established.

The other respect is that it is handed down from him (ع) in the reports that he said:

> أنا بطرقات السماء أعرف منكم بطرقات الأرض

I know the roads of the sky better than you know the roads of the earth.

If, after this saying, he had been observed seeking a crescent, it would have been said to him: where then is your saying of yesterday, that you know the roads of the sky better than we know the roads of the earth?

As for the rational respect, it is well known that the Prophet (ع) is far above having hidden from him the differences in the risings of the crescents and their degrees, a thing that is scarcely hidden from an astronomer, and that the places of the earth likewise differ. Some of them are elevated, such that the sighting of the crescent there is swifter, like the summits of mountains and what runs in their course; and some are low-lying, such that the sighting there is slower. If it is known of his condition that this was not hidden from him, and had it been hidden it would have been the greatest deficiency, and he is far removed from deficiencies, then how can reason require, along with his knowledge of the difference in what is seen, that he should impose the obligation of fasting, which is bound up with the obligation of the pilgrimage, upon all people upon a single foundation, while he knows that it cannot be sound? For one people see it on a given night and another people do not see it. Nor can it be sound that one and the same day belongs to Sha'ban where it is not seen and to the month of Ramadan where it is seen. This is a thing that no man of reason doubts, and none rejects it but an ignorant man.

Besides this, God, glory be to Him, has said in the firm text of His Book:

> كُتِب عليكم الصيام كما كُتِب على الذين من قبلكم لعلكم تتقون

Fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may be mindful of God.

Those before you points to the Christians, and their fasting is not bound to sighting but to calculation. Then He said, in confirmation of that:

> أياماً معدودات

for a counted number of days.

The counted days are those that remain always counted, requiring neither sighting nor observation. Were it possible for the month of Ramadan to be thirty at one time and twenty-nine at another, He would certainly not have said "a counted number of days." It is like the saying of the speaker, this is a reckoned reckoning, and this is a counted dirham.

Another argument is that, since the ordering of the year is that six of its months are complete and six deficient, its root and its structure must rest upon completeness rather than deficiency. So the first month, which is Muharram, is complete; Safar is deficient; Rabi' al-Awwal is complete; Rabi' al-Akhir is deficient; Jumada al-Ula is complete; Jumada al-Akhira is deficient; Rajab is complete; Sha'ban is deficient; and the month of Ramadan is complete. The Prophet (ع) said:

> ما تمّ شعبان ولا نقص رمضان

Sha'ban is never complete, and Ramadan is never deficient.

The proof of the deficiency of Sha'ban is the night of its middle, while there is no middle night for Rajab nor for the month of Ramadan. For the middle night of Sha'ban is its fifteenth night, and this night is the middle in truth, since fourteen lie before it and fourteen behind it, and it stands in the middle. Nothing like this can hold for the month of Ramadan, because if you make its fifteenth night the middle it does not hold, for fifteen days of the month still remain; and if you make its sixteenth night the middle it does not hold, for the sixteenth is not the half of thirty.

What further indicates the completeness of the month of Ramadan is the ordering of the expiations laid upon whoever deliberately breaks his fast on a day of it, namely that he fast two consecutive months in repentance to God, and that is, like the month of Ramadan, sixty days; and if he is not able, then the feeding of sixty poor persons. Were it possible for the month of Ramadan to be twenty-nine days, it would be possible for the expiation to be the feeding of sixty poor persons or of fifty-eight poor persons. So praise God, O believers, for what He has preserved you from of deficiency in your religion, and for the success He has granted you in fulfilling the conditions of faith by following your masters, the friends of the Merciful.

He, may God exalt his sanctity, spoke of the like of this in the book of his noble life. He relates that he attended upon the king Abu Kalijar al-Daylami, lord of Shiraz and of Iraq al-Ajam and its king, and that there was brought before him a sharif from the notables of the Zaydi school. Speech passed between the two of them on the matter of fasting and its obligation, and whether it rests upon sighting or upon something other than the known sighting. He says that the aforementioned sharif said to him in this connection that it is related of the Messenger of Allah (ع) that he gathered his five fingers and said:

> نحن أمّيون لا نحسب الحساب الصوم مرة هكذا

We are unlettered; we do not reckon the reckoning. The fast is once like this.

He continued until he had completed the count of thirty in six turns, and then he gathered the fingers a second time, and when he came to the last he withheld one of the fingers and said: and once like this.

Our master al-Mu'ayyad fi'l-Din, may God sanctify his soul, said to him: God forbid that the Prophet (ع), whom God honored with the ascension and to whom He showed what lies beyond the veil, should be charged with such halting of speech and such inarticulacy, and should be so unlettered that he could not distinguish thirty from twenty-nine with his tongue, so as to be spared the gathering of the fingers and the moving of the hand these many times, in the manner of one who herds cattle and sheep. Moreover, the Prophet (ع) said: I know the roads of the sky better than you know the roads of the earth. So had he, upon this mighty saying being taken as settled and confined to him, risen to scan the sky in search of the sighting of the crescent, the people would have risen to confront him, saying: where is this beside your claim of yesterday, that you know the roads of the sky better than we know the roads of the earth? And besides this, were the seeking of the crescent a thing whose obligation and necessity were established, it would be so for us in particular, while he (ع) would have no need of it, since Gabriel (ع) informs him that the crescent has appeared. His asking Gabriel about that would be more fitting for him than his scanning the sky on his own account and thereby making the descent of revelation upon him of no effect. Indeed, if one of us found a way to an angel among the angels, to ask him for a ruling and for tidings of the hidden matters, he would not rely upon himself in searching and striving. This is a thing upon which no proof and no demonstration can stand.

The Alid was so shamed by this answer that he rose stumbling in his own robes, until the people standing in the outer ranks and the masters began to laugh at him and mock him; and each of us withdrew to his house.

Some of the summoners, may God sanctify their souls, spoke of the like of this in the argument against the opponents, for the guidance of the paths, in the form of a question:

What do the eminent scholars among all the people of Islam say concerning the saying of the Lord of Majesty and Honor:

> يا أيها الذين آمنوا كُتب عليكم الصيام كما كُتب على الذين من قبلكم لعلكم تتقون أياماً معدودات فمن كان منكم مريضاً أو على سفر فعدة من أيام أخر

O you who believe, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may be mindful of God, for a counted number of days; and whoever among you is sick or on a journey, then a number of other days.

up to His saying, mighty and majestic:

> فمن شهد منكم الشهر فليصمه ومن كان مريضاً أو على سفر فعدة من أيام أخر يريد الله بكم اليسر ولا يريد بكم العسر ولتكملوا العدة ولتكبروا الله على ما هداكم ولعلكم تشكرون

So whoever among you witnesses the month, let him fast it; and whoever is sick or on a journey, then a number of other days. God desires ease for you and does not desire hardship for you, and that you complete the count and magnify God for having guided you, and that you may be thankful.

Whom did God intend by His words "those before you, so that you may be mindful of God"? And who are "those before"? And how many are the counted days? And what is the witnessing of the month?

The people of the language have said that الشهر (al-shahr) is the crescent. Others have said that the month is a counted number of days in its fullness, which is completed, whereupon the second month enters. And in the saying of God the Exalted:

> شهر رمضان الذي أنزل فيه القرآن

The month of Ramadan in which the Quran was sent down.

there is an indication that it was sent down in counted days, not in the crescent. God has said that the number of the months with Him is twelve:

> إن عدة الشهور عند الله اثنا عشر شهراً في كتاب الله يوم خلق السموات والأرض منها أربعة حرم

The number of months with God is twelve months in the Book of God, from the day He created the heavens and the earth; four of them are sacred.

Yet only one crescent is known, which increases and becomes full, becoming a full moon, and then decreases through the days of the month; while the months are known from Muharram to Dhu al-Hijja.

A people have said concerning His saying "so whoever among you witnesses the month, let him fast it" that it means: when he looks upon the crescent. And His saying "and whoever among you is sick or on a journey" indicates that the witness is the one who is resident, as against the absent one who is traveling; and it is the resident who fasts and is granted no dispensation to break the fast.

And His saying "and that you complete the count and magnify God for having guided you": what is that count, and how much is it? The magnification is at the festival of the breaking of the fast, after the completion of the fasting. Is the completion of the count of the month thirty days, or twenty-nine days, or twenty-eight days? And which of these is the more complete count and the longer span?

The Prophet (ع) said:

> ما أتاكم عني فاعرضوه على كتاب الله فما وافق كتاب الله فأنا قلته وما خالفه فلم أقله وكيف أخالف كتاب الله وعليّ نزل

Whatever comes to you from me, set it against the Book of God. Whatever agrees with the Book of God, I said it; and whatever contradicts it, I did not say it. How should I contradict the Book of God when it was sent down upon me?

Now those who fast by the rising of the crescent may fall short of fulfilling the count of thirty. They have related from the Prophet (ع) that he said: fast when it is seen and break your fast when it is seen. Yet this, according to what they say about it, does not agree with the completeness of the count as the noble Book has declared it. Surely in that is a reminder for those possessed of understanding.

Then it has come down from the Prophet (ع), in what al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) related, that he said:

> ما تمّ شعبان ولا نقص شهر رمضان

Sha'ban is never complete, and the month of Ramadan is never deficient.

This agrees with the clear Book, and the completeness of the count of the month at thirty days is established.

We found the saying of the Prophet (ع), "fast when it is seen and break your fast when it is seen," to indicate the anticipation of its sighting. Looking into what the people of the language have set down concerning the uses of the letter ل (lam), we found this lam to be the lam of anticipation, as one of them has set down in verse:

> فللموت تغذوا الوالدات عيالها
> كما لخراب الدار تبنى المساكنا

Mothers nourish their children for death, just as dwellings are built for the ruin of the house.

And another said: they were born for death, and they built for ruin. Of this same kind is the saying of God the Exalted in the Book:

> هذا ما توعدون ليوم الحساب

This is what you are promised for the Day of Reckoning.

God set the promise first and then said "for the Day of Reckoning," making the lam the lam of outcome (لام العاقبة); and that agrees with what we have mentioned, and it is the most fitting and the correct view. So the saying of the Prophet (ع), "fast when it is seen," means: let it rise while you are fasting; and "break your fast when it is seen" means: let it rise while you are breaking the fast. And God knows best.

If you say otherwise, then bring the proof concerning the completeness of the count of the month mentioned in the Quran. And why did the Messenger of Allah (ع) not say, fast after seeing it and break your fast after seeing it, so that the matter would be clearer to his community? For it is said: work for a day on which you shall be raised:

> اعملوا ليوم فيه تبعثون ويكون للآخرة ما تعملون

Work for a day on which you will be raised up, and let what you do be for the hereafter.

So the revealed Book agrees with the saying of the Prophet who was sent. Bring us, then, the satisfying answer, for we do not tread the road of obstinacy and of rejecting what is plain to the eye. Praise be to God, the High, the Bestower, Lord of bounty, of favor and of kindness. May God bless His Messenger to all mankind and jinn, and his family and his companions, who made clear the meanings of the Quran, so long as night and day alternate and the two ages follow one another; and may He grant them peace, mercy and honor. God suffices us, and He is the best guardian.

Our master Zayd ibn Muhammad, may God sanctify his soul, said in his epistle known as the Maknuna, concerning the interpretation of the prayer of the eclipse, that God, blessed and exalted, when He created the creation, ordained among them a wondrous ordinance whose beginning confirms its end, and made all that He created in the first age a proof for the people of the last age. He made the eclipse of the moon a veiling for it, and ordained that its veiling should fall when fourteen nights of the month have passed, though it may occur at thirteen nights; but what is relied upon, and what happens most often, is at fourteen nights. In all of this there are causes and proofs of God, which stand as witnesses. And He ordained that the moon's absence and concealment from the world should be at twenty-eight nights in every month, so that in its concealment it is absent for two days and two nights, which are forty-eight hours, and then it rises after forty-eight hours.

Here follows the blessed epistle on the matter of the month of Ramadan, known as al-Lazima, on the fast of the month of Ramadan and its time, by our master and mawlana, the most exalted, unique and brilliant summoner Ahmad Hamid al-Din ibn Abdallah al-Kirmani, may God exalt his sanctity and grant us his intercession and his companionship, the proof of our mawlana al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah, Commander of the Faithful, may God bless him.
