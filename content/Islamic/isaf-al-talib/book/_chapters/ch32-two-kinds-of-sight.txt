# Two Kinds of Sight

In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate, and in Him I place my trust.

Praise be to Allah, by whose grace good deeds are brought to completion, and through obedience to His guardians blessings become general. May Allah bless His messenger and His servant Muhammad, the best of mankind and the pride of those noble messengers who came before him, and bless the righteous ones of his pure progeny, the beacons of guidance and the moons of the dark night, and grant them abundant peace.

And thereafter: my brother, you wrote to me recalling that the people of division and disagreement, in keeping with the habits they have long adopted, have set themselves against the people of truth and of concord, and that their sayings have multiplied in challenge, faulting them concerning the community and reproaching them for beginning the fast before the general public. You asked me to clarify for you the matter of entering the fast among the Shi'a of the Commander of the Faithful (ع), and in particular the fast of the year four hundred, in which the commands issued from the Pure Presence made it obligatory to enter the fast two days before the crescent was seen with the eye, and to set out the reason for it with statement, clarification, and proof.

I therefore resolved to place this in an epistle in which I would bring forward such healing proofs as would let the eyes of the discerning graze at ease upon the order of its foundations, and would leave the eyes of the opponents smarting at any attempt to take up its meanings. So I say, seeking help from Allah and from His guardians upon His earth, (عليهم السلام), and I have named it the necessary epistle on fasting the month of Ramadan and its time. For what I have placed within it are proofs binding upon the whole body of Muslims, from which they have no escape in the first principle of reason or of sense. Allah is the guardian of right success.

Know, may Allah preserve you from the disgrace of ignorance and guard you from the affliction of misguidance, that had the opposing party possessed discernment and insight, or had they any share or portion in reason and understanding, the cunning of Satan would have been turned away from them and their aspirations would have been directed toward the truth, and they would have been among those whom Allah excepts in His Book when He says:

> ولا يزالون مختلفين الا من رحم ربك

"They will not cease to differ, except those upon whom your Lord has mercy."

But when they mounted the steed of caprice, and the causes of mere personal opinion carried them off, Satan made their conduct seem fair to them and they followed him, all but a company of the believers. So the word of Allah came true of them:

> أفلم يسيروا في الأرض فتكون لهم قلوب يعملون بها أو آذان يسمعون بها فأنها لا تعمى الأبصار ولكن تعمى القلوب التي في الصدور

"Have they not traveled through the land so that they might have hearts with which to understand, or ears with which to hear? For it is not the eyes that go blind, but the hearts within the breasts that go blind."

In the state they are in they are like cattle, indeed like the dead, and they do not perceive, as Allah Almighty said:

> أموات غير أحياء وما يشعرون

"Dead, not living, and they do not perceive."

They have died to the knowledge of realities and have gone blind to them through their arrogance toward the people of truth, through their insolence and their disagreement. They have become like beasts in their disputes, wavering to and fro, running about in their blindnesses without discernment or insight. They fell into this disagreement and separation, which Allah has forbidden in His words:

> ولا تكونوا كالذين تفرقوا واختلفوا

"Do not be like those who separated and fell into disagreement."

They fell into it only through their insolence, their arrogance, their envy, and their disdain for obeying the one whose obedience Allah has made obligatory among those charged with the Command, the ones to whom Allah Almighty in His majesty directed that all disputed matters be referred, saying:

> فان تنازعتم في شيء فردوه إلى الله والرسول

"If you dispute about anything, refer it to Allah and to the Messenger."

And He said:

> ولو ردوه إلى الرسول والى أولي الأمر منهم لعلمه الذين يستنبطونه منهم

"Had they referred it to the Messenger and to those charged with authority among them, those among them who draw out conclusions would have known it."

Whoever sets aside arrogance, insolence, and envy, and obeys the people of truth, comes to stand upon the realities and departs from the company of the people of ignorance. Whoever opposes the people of truth and persists in the state he is in, running about in blindness and misguidance, is brought by that to ruin and to an evil saying, and the word of punishment comes true against him, and he earns the sign of chastisement. We seek refuge in Allah from that, and from division after concord, and from tyranny after faith, and from disobedience toward His guardians, may (عليهم السلام), after submission and certainty. And we ask Him to make us among those who win through His mercy and who stand firm in obeying Him and obeying His guardians, for He is the best of those who are asked.

He then made fasting obligatory upon His servants in the explicit text of His Book, saying:

> كتب عليكم الصيام كما كتب على الذين من قبلكم لعلكم تتقون

"Fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may guard yourselves."

And the Prophet (ع) said:

> شعبان شهري ورمضان شهر الله وصومه فريضة

"Shaban is my month, and Ramadan is the month of Allah, and fasting it is an obligation."

And the Imams (عليهم السلام) said:

> صوم شهر رمضان فريضة على كل مسلم

"The fast of the month of Ramadan is an obligation upon every Muslim."

When Allah Almighty imposed the fast of the month of Ramadan upon His servants, the Prophet (ع) did not leave them blind to the knowledge of its time and its season, just as he did not leave them in ignorance of the other obligations, such as the numbers of the prayers and their times when they were imposed, and the amount of the alms due upon the various kinds of property when that was made obligatory. So he said:

> صوموا لرؤيته وأفطروا لرؤيته

"Fast at its sighting and break the fast at its sighting."

Scarcely any disagreement occurs over this narration among the sects of the Muslims, and it is the pillar upon which the people of the outward rest their argument.

I say, then, that Allah Almighty created all things in pairs, so that the One alone in oneness should be He, and nothing should share with Him in it. Allah said:

> سبحان الذي خلق الأزواج كلها

"Glory be to the One who created all the pairs."

And He said:

> ومن كل شيء خلقنا زوجين لعلكم تذكرون

"Of every thing We created two kinds, so that you might remember."

Since this is so, and since we see all things in pairs and counterparts, such as substance and accident, sky and earth, land and sea, plain and mountain, night and day, good and evil, body and spirit, outward and inward, this world and the next, male and female, and other things too long to enumerate, and since we see that the Prophet (ع) made the foundation of his law and its governing principle to be pairing, when he joined the commands and obligations to their counterparts as an indication of what I have said concerning the pure affirmation of the oneness of Allah Almighty, as with the prayer which he joined to the alms, and the call to prayer which he joined to the iqama, and the Hajj which he joined to the Umrah, and al-Safa which he joined to al-Marwa, and the obligatory act which he joined to the practice of the Sunnah, and the bowing which he joined to the prostration, and the testimony that there is no god but Allah which he joined to the testimony that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, and the like of that which is too long to enumerate, I say that sight too is of two kinds, the one joined to the other: natural vision and vision of the soul.

Natural vision is that which occurs by means of the eye. It is the vision that apprehends colors, such as white, black, red, yellow, and others, and shapes, such as the round, the triangular, the square, the five sided, and so on. There is no need to argue for it, since it falls under the senses. Vision of the soul is that which occurs by way of knowledge, through the heart and the soul, and it apprehends what natural vision does not apprehend among those causes that are hidden from its perception. Allah said:

> ما كذب الفؤاد ما رأى

"The heart did not lie about what it saw."

And He said:

> ألم ترى ربك كيف مد الظل

"Have you not seen how your Lord extended the shadow?"

And He said:

> ألم ترى كيف فعل ربك بأصحاب الفيل

"Have you not seen what your Lord did with the companions of the elephant?"

We know that the Prophet (ع) was not living in the time of the companions of the elephant, so that he might have seen them; what is meant is rather the vision of the soul, which comes by way of the heart and of knowledge. It is likewise said in a proverb that so and so is blind of heart, meaning that he is dull and backward in knowing things and in seeing them when they are absent.

Since what I have set out is sound, that sight is of two kinds, as all things are divided into two states and two aspects so that the oneness of Allah may stand pure and nothing may share with Him in that oneness, I say: natural vision, which comes by way of the eye, apprehends of things only what is corporeal and falls under limits and time, such as colors and shapes. Vision of the soul, which comes by way of the heart and of knowledge, apprehends what natural vision does not apprehend and what it is too weak to grasp of all that is hidden from the eyes. From this follows the judgment that vision of the soul, which comes by way of the heart and of knowledge, is more exalted than natural vision, which comes by way of the eye and of sight, and is finer and subtler in its apprehension, since its act in knowing things is to apprehend them as they truly are, unlike the eye. For if a man were to take a single drop from a pool of water, vision of the soul would judge that the quantity of that water had decreased by the measure of the drop, and would see this with a sound seeing, while that decrease would not register for the sense that comes by way of the eye and of sight.

Since vision of the soul is the prior and the subtler in apprehension, taking hold of it in the performance of the obligations of Allah Almighty is more binding and more fitting than taking hold of anything else. For to confine oneself to what is not the more excellent while the more excellent is present belongs to the judgments of ignorance, not to the judgment of reason; and what truly binds people is to take hold of the more precise and the more excellent, by which I mean the vision of knowledge and of the soul.

Furthermore, if a man were to know in his heart and to see that the time of one of the prayers had come, and he had not heard the sound of the call to prayer, the prayer would still be binding upon him. In the same way, if one taking the meal before dawn were to see with his heart and know that the dawn had broken, while his eyesight was barred from seeing it by some barrier that stood in the way, abstaining from food would be binding upon him even though his eye had not seen it. Since this is so, the fast and the abstaining from food are binding upon the one taking that meal because of his knowledge that the dawn has broken, even though his eyesight is barred from seeing it by whatever bars it.

In the same way I say that the entry into the fast is binding once knowledge is obtained that the cause of the fast, which is the moon's separation from the greatest luminary after their conjunction, has come to pass, even if sight is barred from seeing it by any condition whatever, whether light, cloud, dust, or a mountain. For it is agreed that when the cause is realized, its effect becomes necessary by its necessity, so that neither precedes the other except in rank. The people of the call, therefore, in their entry into the fast, act by the judgment of vision of the soul, in which error scarcely occurs, upon clear evidence and knowledge.

Then, since the Messenger (ع) made the fast obligatory in certain months of the year, and informed us that Allah Almighty has made the sun and the moon a means to the knowledge of years and months, their times and their crescents, Allah said:

> هو الذي جعل الشمس ضياءً والقمر نورا وقدره منازل لتعلموا عدد السنين والحساب

"He is the One who made the sun a radiance and the moon a light, and determined phases for it so that you might know the number of the years and the reckoning."

And He said:

> ويسألونك عن الأهلة قل هي مواقيت للناس

"They ask you about the crescents. Say: they are appointed times for the people."

And the year is twelve months, as Allah said:

> إن عدة الشهور عند الله اثنا عشر شهراً في كتاب الله يوم خلق السموات والأرض

"The number of the months with Allah is twelve months in the Book of Allah, from the day He created the heavens and the earth."

The year is not complete except by the passing of that full count, and the month does not come to be, nor are its days completed, except by the moon's travel from the place at which it and the sun stand in one degree and one minute, which is what the people of the stars call the conjunction, until it traverses the whole sphere and returns to that same alignment and conjunction. From this comes the knowledge that when its degree and minute in the sign are like the degree and minute of the sun in it, its course for the past month is complete; and when it parts from the sun, its course belongs to the coming month. In its travel toward conjunction and separation it passes through states in which the light of half its body on the side facing the center, which is the earth, decreases, until it is in alignment, and at that moment the upper half of its body, on the side facing the encompassing sphere, is illuminated, just as the whole of the half facing the center is illuminated on the night of its opposition. Then the light appears in half its body at the separation and increases in proportion to its distance, through causes that the eye is too weak to apprehend in full. I say that the eye's inability to apprehend all these states of it, and to see the illumined portion of the moon's body as it travels toward conjunction at the end of the month and at its separation at the beginning of the month, comes only from the condition of the sun's light, which bars the eye from apprehension. For the light, in barring the eye from seeing it at that time, is like the other causes that intervene and prevent the eye from seeing it and knowing it, such as cloud, dust, a wall, or a mountain.

Do you not see that the stars are not seen by day because of the sun's radiance and its overpowering brightness, and that when the sun is eclipsed the eye apprehends the sight of them, because of the decrease of light through the eclipse? Since it is established that the illumined measure of the moon's body at its separation from the sun would be seen by the eye were it not for the sun's light and its rays, then the light is the barrier and the preventer, like the rest of them, such as cloud and the turbidity of the air. And since the eye is barred by the sun's light from seeing the crescent after its separation from it, the soul, by way of knowledge, is not barred from seeing it as it truly is. So the fast becomes binding by the vision of knowledge, which is the more exalted according to the proofs I have established. By these testimonies the people of the guiding call, in their entry into the fast by vision of the soul, are upon truth and right. And if they are upon the right, then whoever opposes them is in error.

Then I say to the hostile party and the rest of the opponents: from where have you made it obligatory that seeing is the seeing of the eye alone, apart from the seeing of the heart and of knowledge, when the proofs stand that seeing is of two aspects, which none denies except one who is openly obstinate? If you say that a man knows nothing until his sight falls upon it, and that when he has beheld it the judgment of the vision of knowledge concerning it is established for him, I say that this argument of yours is corrupt and does not hold, from several sides.

The first: if I ask you, have you seen Allah Almighty, then upon this principle of yours you are bound to say no. So I say: then why have you affirmed Him, when you have laid it down that a man knows nothing until his sight falls upon it, and your sight has not fallen upon Him, exalted and magnified as He is? You are therefore bound to retreat from the doctrine of the eye to the matter of knowledge, and to say: we knew, and it was established for us by proofs, and so we affirmed it. And that is precisely my position and my foundation.

The second: the matter of the world and its origination in time. If I were to question you about it, you would be bound to rule upon its origination by the judgment of the vision of knowledge and proof, not by the judgment of the vision of the eye. And that is my position.

The third: I say, what do you say concerning a man who broke his fast without having seen the crescent with his eye, because a barrier intervened, when thirty days of the month of Ramadan had passed? Was his breaking of the fast valid or not? If you say it was valid, I say: how so, when he did not behold the crescent with the eye, and the foundation of your school is that fasting and breaking the fast are not permitted except after seeing the crescent with the eye? You are then bound to say: we knew that after thirty days the crescent is seen, and that a barrier merely stood in the way of seeing it. And I say to you: you have abandoned the judgment of the vision of the eye and returned of necessity to the matter of the vision of knowledge, and that is my position. And if you say it was not valid, then what binds you binds you still.

Beyond this, whatever is known of things only by way of the senses, which include sight and the rest, is no more than the substance of corporeal things in their form and their quality alone. The soul, once it has drawn upon the senses in coming to know a thing a first time, has no need to draw upon them a second time. The outcome of the senses and their yield is that they become dispensable, since the soul stands by its own essence in their place. For the soul in man is one of the instruments by which he apprehends.

Within man the soul is one of the instruments by which he perceives things, as hearing, sight, smell, taste, and touch are instruments, and it possesses a capacity that none of the others possesses: it rises above them all, after the images of their objects have come to it, and perceives what hearing, sight, smell, taste, and touch do not perceive. Just as hearing perceives what sight, smell, taste, and touch do not perceive, and smell perceives what hearing, sight, taste, and touch do not perceive, so the soul perceives what lies beyond all of them. In the senses there is a wisdom from Allah Almighty, that man be not deprived of anything he cannot otherwise apprehend of what Allah has brought into being in the worlds, and that what one instrument apprehends not be lost to him because another cannot reach it.

The soul's perception is therefore of two kinds. The first is perception by means of the five senses, as its perception of colors and shapes by way of sight, of sounds that signify and sounds that do not signify by way of hearing, and whatever else belongs to the remaining senses; and it is from this that the soul draws its inference toward what the senses do not perceive. The second is perception by the soul's own essence along the path of inference, which stands in place of all that it apprehends by way of the senses, as its perception that the world is originated after it had first apprehended the world by way of the senses. To what the soul perceives by its own essence along the path of inference the senses have no road at all. And what it perceives by way of the senses it needs them for only in the first instance; it does not need a second perception from their side when it has occasion to know that thing again, for it has received the image of the sensed thing upon its own form at the first apprehension, and that image is present to it within itself whenever it returns to it, without any need to call upon the senses once more. So it is with the image of the planets in themselves and in their quality: once the soul has apprehended them by way of sight, that seeing makes a second seeing unnecessary, since the image is held in the soul and preserved by its having received it. Thereafter the soul stands in the place of the senses by its own essence, as the soul of one whose eyes have gathered the forms of visible things has no need of a second apprehension by the eyes, the image remaining in what belongs properly to the soul, standing upon its form without alteration.

What is meant in seeking the crescent and sighting it with the eye every month and every year is not that one should learn its figure in its form, its thinness and its thickness, for the knowledge of that would indeed require sight, which is the instrument for apprehending such things. What is meant in seeking it is to know the fact of its being in the west before the sun after it had been in the east behind her. And the apprehension of this fact of its being in the west belongs to the soul, not to sight, for sight, even when every obstruction is lifted, apprehends nothing but shapes and colors.

Since sight apprehends nothing but shapes and colors, and the seeking of the crescent is not for knowledge of its color and its shape, the matter is settled. For if it were sought in order to know its color and its shape, it would be absurd to seek it at all, since knowledge of the quality of its form, its figure, and its color is already possessed from earlier seeing by the eye, together with the certainty that it will not fail so long as the heavens and the earth endure, and that its image as apprehended by sight will not change from roundness of shape and the lighting of the edge of its body into squareness of shape and the lighting of the whole of its body. Nor is it sought because the obligation of fasting depends upon a condition of thinness or thickness in the luminous body. For if it were sought in order to know its thinness and its thickness, it would be absurd that the one who sees it should fast when it is thin while the condition is thickness, or fast when it is thick while the condition is thinness. No such condition of shape, color, thinness, or thickness exists in the obligation of fasting. It is therefore void that the obligation of fasting should be attached to apprehending the crescent by sight; and once that is void, it is established that it is attached to the soul's apprehension of it as being before the sun. For the proof has been given that man knows by way of inference, from what he has apprehended of bodily substances through his senses, what he does not apprehend through them. So the knowledge does not come about by sight, and fasting is attached to the vision of the soul and to nothing else; and the people of the da'wah, in taking what the vision of the soul requires when they enter the fast, are upon a sound way and a straight path.

Further, the seeing of the eye sometimes falls into error concerning things as they truly are, by reason of what befalls it, so that it sees the large as small and the small as large, the thin as thick and the thick as thin; and sometimes a thing is seen only from near, or only from far. But the seeing of knowledge scarcely errs in what it encompasses. To take, in the discharge of the obligatory duties, that which does not err is worthier in the judgment of those endowed with understanding than to take that which errs. The people of the true call, then, in entering the fast, have taken hold of that which does not err, upon clarity and upon truth.

As for the saying of the Prophet,

"صوموا لرؤيته وأفطروا لرؤيته"

"Fast for its sighting and break the fast for its sighting,"

outwardly it does not escape two faces. Either he intended by it that the fasting of the community should begin from the moment the crescent is seen with the eye, as the opponents say, or that the crescent is seen with the eye while the community is already fasting, as we say. If he intended that the fasting of the community begin from the moment of the crescent's being seen by the eye, then what is the meaning of his saying لرؤيته, and what is the reason for his restricting himself, out of all the modes of expression, to the letter اللام (al-lam) rather than to another of the letters used in expression, he who took pride in his speech and said,

"أنا أفصح العرب ولا فخر"

"I am the most eloquent of the Arabs, and this is no boast"?

Was his use of this lam in this place, saying لرؤيته rather than leaving it out, for any purpose other than that the community should already be fasting when the crescent is seen by sight? For the sense of the lam as it is used, and what is intended by it, is that the act precede that for which it is done. It is as when it is said to a company, "Set such a thing in order for the coming of so-and-so," meaning that the thing is to be already in order at the time of his coming. And as when it is said, "Compose for the meeting of so-and-so a poem that you may recite to him," meaning that they meet him with the poem already composed and spoken, not that they begin composing it from the moment of meeting him. And as when it is said, "Work for the Hereafter," meaning that they are to put the work forward before the time of the Hereafter, for it is known that in the Hereafter there is no work. By these witnesses it is sound that the letter lam was used for nothing other than that the people should already be fasting when the crescent is seen with the eye. And if the intention of the Prophet was that the crescent be seen while the community is fasting, that is what we have said and for which we have established the proof.

If an opponent objects and says, "What do you say against the lam in the saying of the Prophet لرؤيته being like the lam in the saying of Allah Almighty,

'أقم الصلاة لدلوك الشمس'

'Establish the prayer at the declining of the sun,'

so that the fast need not precede the sighting, just as the prayer need not precede the declining of the sun?" we say: the account of the lam in the saying of Allah Almighty is the same as the account of the lam in the saying of the Prophet. For the declining of the sun is its departure from the dome of the sky, as it has been explained, and the apprehension of that departure is of two kinds: an apprehension belonging to the soul and an apprehension belonging to nature. The apprehension belonging to the soul precedes the apprehension belonging to nature, because the sun's departure in reality from the dome of the sky is perceived only by the vision of the soul, by way of knowledge, since natural vision is too narrow to apprehend it until the sun has parted from the point of the zenith in descent and has come clear of that region in its descending, at the intersection of the line of the circle with the straight line, as we have set forth in the figure: the earth at the center, the east and the west, the meridian line, the circle of elevation, the straight line that cuts across, and the tangent of the circle upon the straight line, with the place of the vision of the soul of the declining marked at the point ب and the place of the sensible vision of the declining marked at the point ج.

Only then does sense apprehend it. For the movement of the sun and of its shadow falls under sense by its rising in the circle when it comes up from the east and by its descending in the circle toward its setting; where there is rising and descending, sense apprehends the shortening and lengthening of the shadow. But when the sun has come to where it has no elevation, while it is at the point of declining and descending, sense grows weary of apprehending its course and its movement, because it has reached the zenith in such a way that its motion is concealed from sense and hidden from it, and one who has no knowledge supposes that it has halted. It is hidden from sense only because the sun has come to stand over the place from which it is at its farthest remove in height, and its motion in its passage through the zenith has become, from the line of the circle, a motion upon a level line, where the plane touches the straight line of the circle. That is from the place of the line أ, the point where the line of the circle and the level line intersect, from the eastern side to the line on the western side; and sense does not discern the shortening and lengthening of the shadow, since that comes about only by elevation in height and by descent, and there the sun has neither elevation nor descent but is upon a level line.

This being so, the true declining by which the prayer becomes obligatory is at the arrival of the sun's disk from the line of the circle at the line ب, which is seen by the vision of the soul and is the sign of the line of the half of what is cut off by the day; not at its arrival at the line ج, which is seen by natural vision and is more than the half. For at the time it reaches the line ب it has begun to descend, even if sense does not apprehend its shadow, nor its movement in its place, since it is upon a level line, and this is not made plain to sense until after it has reached the intersection of the line of the circle and the level line. The account of the lam in "at the declining of the sun," then, is the same as the account of the lam in "for its sighting." It follows that when the shadow turns, the one who prays is already within his prayer, just as when the crescent appears and sense apprehends it, the one who fasts is already within his fast, this being the more careful course in the performance of the obligatory duties. By these witnesses the objection is void.

If he then says, "If the matter is as you have described, and fasting is binding by the vision of the soul according to what you have brought forward, what is the meaning of the saying of the Prophet,

'فإن غمّ عليكم فعدوا ثلاثين يوماً'

'If it is obscured from you, then count thirty days,'

and what is that but a requiring of the seeing of the eye?" we say: we have established the vision of the soul by the witnesses we have brought forward, and that it is the nobler of the two.
