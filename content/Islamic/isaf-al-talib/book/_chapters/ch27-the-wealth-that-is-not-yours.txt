# The Wealth That Is Not Yours

I come now to the Book of Zakāt, and to what has been handed down concerning the merit of giving zakāt and the matters bound up with it.

Our master Jaʿfar ibn Manṣūr al-Yaman, may God sanctify him and grant us his intercession and his company, spoke in the book of inward nursing, citing the word of the Messenger of Allah:

> لا تحل الصدقة لي ولا لأهل بيتي

Charity is not lawful for me, nor for the people of my house.

And he said in it: As for the one whose scope in the call, after this which is incumbent upon him, is that his excellence appear outwardly, in that he has abundance of wealth, he pays from his wealth what he wills, apart from the fiṭra, of the obligatory rights and of voluntary giving. And he said in it: Whoever pays the khums is thereby excused from paying the tithe, except as a voluntary act; but whoever pays the tithe alone is not thereby excused from paying the khums that is due from him, because the tithe does not encompass the khums as the khums encompasses the tithe. And the Prophet said:

> لا صلاة لمن لا يؤدي الزكاة

There is no prayer for one who does not pay zakāt.

Our master Zayd ibn Muḥammad, may God sanctify him, said in the Hidden Epistle: God Almighty has imposed zakāt upon ten things, and they are:

1. gold
2. silver
3. camels
4. cows
5. sheep
6. dates
7. raisins
8. wheat
9. barley
10. grains

And Mawlānā Amīr al-Muʾminīn Alī ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع) said in his Four Hundred Counsels:

> داووا مرضاكم بالصدقة وحصّنوا أموالكم بالزكاة

Treat your sick with charity, and fortify your wealth with zakāt.

Our master and Mawlānā, the author of the Mustanṣiriyya Councils, (ع), said in the twelfth council of them: You have heard what we recited to you in what came before, of the jurisprudence of guardianship and purity and prayer, by which the sincerity of the testimony is completed, that testimony which is the root of religion and worship and the first of the causes of mercy and felicity. So listen now to what we recite to you of the mention of zakāt, which is among its rights according to what has already been said. Its sincerity is the knowledge of its noble boundaries, and the fulfilling of its rights is the performance of the seven obligations that are the pillars of Islam, according to the order we have already set out. In zakāt likewise there are seven obligations and twelve sunnahs, like the number of the clauses of the testimony and its letters. Zakāt must be received by the Imam, and it is taken from seven things:

1. gold
2. silver
3. camels
4. cows
5. sheep
6. crops
7. minerals

And it is disbursed to eight who are named, who fall into seven divisions:

1. the poor and the needy, who are given charity from it
2. those who labor in its collection, who are given from it the wage of collection
3. those whose hearts are to be reconciled, who are paid from it as a bond of protection
4. and in the freeing of necks, by which those under bondage of service are set free from it
5. the debt burdened, whose debts are discharged from it among the people of allegiance
6. and in the way of God, meaning the struggle that is an obligation of sufficiency
7. and the wayfarer, those cut off from the road of guidance

As for its twelve sunnahs:

1. that it not be taken until the year has run its course
2. that the niṣāb of gold is twenty dinars
3. that the niṣāb of silver is two hundred dirhams
4. that the niṣāb of camels is five grazing head
5. that the niṣāb of cows is thirty grazing head
6. that the niṣāb of sheep is forty head
8. that the zakāt is taken from the middling of the livestock, which is what is below the best of them and above the poorest of them
9. that what is separate among them is not joined together, nor is what is joined together divided
10. that the stud animal is not taken from them
11. that the owner of crops gives charity from what has not reached the niṣāb, which is five awsāq, by the bundle and by the handful, at the time of his harvest, for the saying of God Almighty:

> ﴿وَآتُوا حَقَّهُ يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ﴾

And give its due on the day of its harvest.

12. that there is no zakāt upon dwellings, nor upon ornaments, nor upon anything that is for use.

And every word of this speech carries abundant meanings and traces of wisdom.

I set down next what has been said concerning the severity of the matter in the giving of zakāt. It is stated in the book of questions and answers already mentioned, which one of its arrangers ordered according to the arrangement of the chapters of the book Daʿāʾim al-Islām.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the Sharīʿa forbidding that a man conceal the zakāt of his wealth from his imam? Our master, who is meant by it, the one who withholds zakāt or another? And how does a man conceal zakāt from his imam if he is giving it to him openly? Answer: This report concerns one who brings out a part of the zakāt. As for the one who withholds it, he has abandoned the obligation altogether.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the book of Daʿāʾim, that the Messenger of Allah said:

> إنا أهل البيت لا تحل لنا الصدقة

We, the people of the House, charity is not lawful for us.

Our master, is what is meant here the Imams, or the rest of his descendants through Fāṭima (ع)? Answer: What is meant by it is the Imams (عليهم السلام).

Question: Concerning what has come in the book Daʿāʾim al-Islām, that charity is not lawful except for five, one of whom is a man who has undertaken a ḥamāla. Our master, what is the meaning of ḥamāla here? Answer: It is either a penalty imposed by a tyrannical ruler, or the bearing of the blood money of the ʿāqila.

Question: From the book of Daʿāʾim, in the covenant that Amīr al-Muʾminīn wrote to Muḥnaf ibn Sulaym: whoever is of the people of our obedience in the region between the Jazīra and Kufa and the land of Syria, and claims that he has paid his charity to the officials of Syria while he is within our domain, held back, encompassed by our horsemen and our foot soldiers, that is not permitted to him. Even if the matter is as he claims, it is not for him to dwell in our lands and pay the charity of his wealth to our enemy. What is the ruling in this, and does the withholding of the land of India by a ruler with his horsemen and his men make the zakāt due to him or not? Answer: The covenant that Amīr al-Muʾminīn (ع) wrote to Muḥnaf ibn Sulaym, and his saying that whatever his horsemen and his foot soldiers have not encompassed, the zakāt is not sought from it, means that it is not sought from one whom the enemy has seized who is not of the Shīʿa who hold to the allegiance. For it has come from the people of the House, God's blessings upon them, in the book of Daʿāʾim and elsewhere, that whoever hands over the zakāt to other than the one whom God, mighty and majestic, commanded that it be handed to, it does not suffice him, even if it is plundered or stolen, until he hands it to the guardian of the affair or to one who receives it by his command.

And it is said in the Mustanṣiriyya Councils: Likewise the one who upholds the outward of the prayer and believes in the inward, yet does not spend of what God has provided him that which He has made obligatory upon him, of the zakāt of his wealth and the khums of his earnings and the purchase of his own self and the redemption of his pledge, falls short in merit of the one whom God described in this verse with His saying:

> ﴿وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ﴾

And out of what We have provided them, they spend.

And the Prophet said that there is no prayer for one who does not pay zakāt, and he said:

> مانع الزكاة في النار

The one who withholds zakāt is in the Fire.
