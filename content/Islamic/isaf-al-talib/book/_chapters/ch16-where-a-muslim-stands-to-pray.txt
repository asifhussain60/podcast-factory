# Where a Muslim Stands to Pray

In the book of questions and answers that I mentioned at the outset, which one of its compilers arranged according to the order of the chapters of *Da'ā'im al-Islām*, I find the matter of mosques treated as follows.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the Sharia forbidding prayer between graves, and the dislike of building a mosque beside them, and concerning the saying of the Messenger of God (ع):

> جُعلت لي الأرض مسجداً وطهوراً إلا الحمام والمقبرة والحش

The earth has been made for me a place of prayer and a means of purification, except for the bathhouse, the cemetery, and the latrine.

Explain its detail to us. Answer: This is according to what has been stated in the outward sense of the Sharia. One does not pray in any of these places at all, and no mosque is built beside graves.

Question: What do you say concerning a mosque given over to public use that stands above a house or a shop: may its owners remove it and pull it down, or not? Answer: If it belongs to him alone and he has not given it over to public use, he may pull it down. If it has been given over to public use, it is not lawful for him to pull it down; and if it collapses of itself, nothing is owed by him.

Question: What do you say concerning a man who wishes to build the congregational mosque or another on his own property: must he seek the permission of the governor for that, or not? Answer: He does what he wishes and does not seek permission.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the Book of Purity, that there is no prayer for the neighbor of the mosque except in the mosque, meaning that no prayer of his has the merit of the prayer of one who prays in the mosque; and it also comes there that the prayer of a man alone in his house is a single prayer. So, our master, what is the difference between the neighbor of the mosque and another, if the prayer of a man in his house is a single prayer? Answer: The neighbor of the mosque is the one who hears the call to prayer, and the prayer of a man without congregation is a single prayer. As for the neighbor of the mosque, it is disliked for him to pray alone in his house without an excuse.

Question: What do you say concerning what is said in the Book of Purity, that when the mosque of the Prophet (ع) leaked upon them they said, "O Messenger of God, if only we plastered it," and he said:

> لا، عريش كعريش موسى

No; a shelter like the shelter of Moses.

What is the meaning of this saying? Answer: It means that this is not like the plastered shelter of Moses.

Question: What do you say concerning a man who hears the call to prayer of a mosque in which one of the licensed men of the mission leads the prayer, while the hearer prays in his house with another licensed man: have the two of them forfeited a prayer of merit, or not? Answer: The prayer in the mosque is more excellent, as the Prophet (ع) said:

> لا صلاة لجار المسجد إلا في المسجد

There is no prayer for the neighbor of the mosque except in the mosque.

Question: What do you say concerning a mosque in which the imam prays the noon prayer with the people, while another imam prays that same prayer with the people upon its roof: is that permissible, and is the prayer of greeting prayed both below it and above it, or not? Answer: There is no harm in it, and there is no prayer of greeting above it for one who has already prayed it below.

Question: What do you say concerning a quarter in which there are several mosques, and a man goes out from his house at the time of prayer: may he pass by the nearer mosque and go to the farther one because the licensed man in the farther one is of greater rank than the licensed man of the nearer? Answer: Yes, that is established, and there is no blame upon one who does it.

Question: What does our master say concerning a mosque that a man sets up within his house and does not make over to God, exalted be He, as a place open to the people: may he move it from its place, or take it into the enlargement of his house, and is the prayer of greeting prayed in it, or not? Answer: If the mosque is open to the people, the prayer of greeting is prayed in it. If it is confined within the house and has not been made open, then it is a place of prayer only, and there is no prayer of greeting in it, and he may take it into whatever part of the house he wishes so long as he has not made it open.

Question: What does our master say concerning a man who comes to the mosque at the time of dawn while the imam is praying the obligatory prayer in congregation, and the one arriving is able to pray the Sunnah and still join the imam: does he pray the greeting and the Sunnah, or does he enter with the imam? Answer: If it is possible for him to reach the prayer before the imam bows, let him pray whatever he wishes of the Sunnah prayers before the imam raises his head from bowing.

Question: What does our master say concerning a man who comes to the mosque before midday in order to make up an obligatory prayer he has missed: does he pray the greeting, or not? Answer: He does not pray a Sunnah prayer while an obligatory prayer is owed by him, except the Sunnah of the dawn, for that has been joined to the obligatory prayers; and the greeting is not prayed except at the times in which the Sunnah prayer is permitted.

Question: What does our master say concerning one who comes while the imam is in the obligatory dawn prayer and enters into it with him: does he make up the greeting or the Sunnah after finishing it, or after midday? Answer: If he prays anything before the greeting, its time has gone. There is no harm in his making up the Sunnah after the obligatory dawn prayer so long as the sun has not risen, or after midday, or at night.

Question: What does our master say concerning an imam who has prayed with the congregation in a mosque and then departed, after which another licensed man comes: does he pray that same prayer in congregation in that mosque, or not? Answer: Two imams do not lead prayer at one time in one assembly, unless the imam is overtaken by something that breaks his state and he appoints another imam in his place.

Question: If the imam prays the funeral prayer with the people in the mosque, how is it with the greeting? Answer: If they entered the mosque at a time in which the supererogatory prayers are permitted, they pray the greeting first.

Other rulings follow. Whoever prays in open country and finds nothing to set up as a screen draws a line and prays, and he does not leave his sandal facing the qibla as a screen. Whoever prays the doubled prayer or the Sunnah of noon in his house and then comes to the mosque begins with the greeting of the mosque, if his entry is at the time of noon or when dawn has broken. Whoever builds a mosque and the people are buried around it afterward, prayer in it is permitted; but if it was built after the grave, prayer in it is not permitted. It is disliked that the qibla of the mosque face toward a bathhouse, or toward graves, or toward a latrine. Whoever does not know the qibla because of something preventing him, and so exerts his judgment and prays, and then errs and learns of the error while he is in the prayer and the time is short, turns toward the direction of the House and completes his prayer, provided he was between the east and the west; but if he had his back to the Kaaba, nothing suffices him but repetition. If two directions are confused for a man, so that he sees the qibla as lying in each of them and neither of the two prevails in his judgment, he prays toward both directions together; and the like of this has been mentioned concerning the four directions. Whoever travels and the time of prayer comes upon him on the road while he is in a state of major impurity and finds no water, and there is a mosque on the way, performs the dry ablution and prays in the mosque. If prayer has been performed in congregation in the mosque and the imam departs and they depart, and a second imam comes with a congregation, then that imam prays by himself and they pray by themselves. The leading of prayer is not held in one mosque more than once. Whoever enters the mosque and prays the greeting of the mosque, then goes out for a need and returns to the mosque, prays the greeting of the mosque again. A man does not pray with camels facing the qibla; but if they are cattle or sheep or the like of them, the prayer is permitted. Whoever prays the doubled prayer in his house in its proper time and then enters the mosque may not pray the greeting of the mosque until dawn breaks; then he begins with the greeting of the mosque and prays the dawn prayer. If he enters the mosque and prays the greeting of the mosque and the time of the doubled prayer returns, he repeats the doubled prayer, for there is no prayer between the doubled prayer and the dawn prayer. Whoever prays the obligatory prayer in the mosque, and another comes and leaves a sword facing the qibla, and he is under a restriction and unable to move away, completes the prayer and his prayer is permitted. It is disliked to pray between the two pillars so long as the worshipper finds another place in the mosque; and if he finds none, the prayer is permitted. If land lies between partners and one of them builds a mosque upon it, prayer in it is permitted, because he built on his own property; and if the other builds a mosque, prayer in it is permitted also.

From the book *Mukhtaṣar al-Muṣannaf*: it is disliked that the qibla of the mosque face toward a bathhouse, or toward graves, or toward a latrine. Whoever does not know the qibla because of something preventing him exerts his judgment and prays; and if he errs and learns of the error while he is in the prayer and the time is short, he turns toward the direction of the House and completes his prayer, if it was between the east and the west; but if he had his back to the Kaaba, nothing suffices him but repetition. And if two directions are confused for a man, so that he sees the qibla as lying in each of them and neither of the two prevails in his judgment, he prays toward both directions together; and the like of this has been mentioned concerning the four directions.

From the book *Ta'wīl al-Da'ā'im*: as for His saying, وسلاحكم, the laying down of weapons and the bringing of them into the mosques is not permitted in the outward sense except for the imam of the mosque on the day of Friday.

It is said in the book *al-Īḍāḥ* that Abu Abd Allah (ع) disliked that a mosque be inside a house with a door locked upon it.

It is said in *Majālis al-Bayān* that prayer is disliked in twelve places, among them:

1. the cemetery;
2. the slaughterhouse;
3. the bathhouse;
4. the latrine;
5. the resting places of camels;
6. prayer facing a sleeping person;
7. prayer without a screen;
8. upon wet, impure ground;
9. upon anything of food;
10. prayer in places of worship;
11. churches;
12. the houses of the Magians, unless they have been sprinkled with water.

Our master and our lord and our father Muhammad ibn al-Fahd, may God exalt his sanctity and grant us his intercession and his company, said in his noble epistle which he named *The Clear Highway of the Path of Guidance for Those Who Seek Guidance, and the Uncoverer of Obscurities in the Juristic Questions of Religion*:

Question Nineteen: You asked also, may God, glorified be He, grant you success in what pleases Him, about prayer in a domed structure or a mosque in which there is a grave on any of its sides. Answer: You have, in this too, by the granting of success from God, glorified be He, the sound word concerning it, and its truth is this: that as for the said domed structure and the said mosque, if either of them was built for prayer to be performed in it, and afterward the making of a grave in it occurred to one who made it there and buried a dead person in it after that, then prayer in the two of them is permitted, in its obligatory prayers and its Sunnah prayers. But if either of them was built on account of a grave that required it, coming after the grave, then their ruling is the ruling of the cemetery, and prayer is never permitted in either of them for one who would pray.

He said also, may God exalt his sanctity, in the noble epistle mentioned, concerning the matter of the greeting of the mosque built for the seeking of the face of its Owner:

Question Twenty-one: You asked also, may God, glorified be He, grant you success in what pleases Him, about the prayer of greeting the mosque: is it binding only within the mosque and never binding in its courtyard and its forecourt? And if one who enters the mosque performs it there, then goes out to its forecourt and returns to it, is he bound to perform it again on account of that going out, or not? Answer: You have, in this too, by the granting of success from God, glorified be He, the sound word concerning it, and its truth is this: that it is not binding except within the mosque in particular, not in its courtyard and its forecourt; and if it is performed in them, that is good, because they have become part of it and of its bounds. So if one who is in the mosque goes out to them and returns to it, as I have explained, it is not obligatory upon him to repeat it, because he has not yet gone out from its bounds.

It is said in the book *Ta'wīl al-Sharīʿa*, and it is related from the Prophet (ع) that he said:

> لا تقام الحدود في المساجد، ومن سمعتموه ينشد ضالته في مسجد فقولوا: لا وجدت

The prescribed penalties are not to be carried out in the mosques; and whoever you hear calling out for his lost property in a mosque, say to him: May you not find it.

And it is said in it that the Prophet (ع) said:

> لا بأس بالصلاة في مرابض الغنم

There is no harm in praying in the folds of sheep.

The author of *al-Majālis al-Mustanṣiriyya*, the peace of God be upon its author, said in the eighth assembly of them: Hear now what we set before you concerning its meaning, and that is that prayer has seven ranks, in which the prayers of those who pray are graded one above another. Among them:

1. the prayer of the worshipper in his house, as a single prayer;
2. the prayer of the worshipper in the mosque of the market, as twelve prayers;
3. the prayer of the worshipper in the mosque of the tribe, as twenty-five prayers;
4. the prayer of the worshipper in the congregational mosque, as one hundred prayers;
5. the prayer of the worshipper in the Farthest Mosque, as one thousand prayers;
6. the prayer of the worshipper in the mosque of the Messenger of God, as ten thousand prayers;
7. the prayer of the worshipper in the Sacred House of God, as one hundred thousand prayers.

And for every kind of that there are abundant meanings and likenesses of wisdom.

Our lord, the Commander of the Believers, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his four hundred rules of conduct:

> الجلوس في المسجد بعد طلوع الفجر إلى طلوع الشمس أسرع لطلب الرزق من الضرب في الأرض

Sitting in the mosque from the breaking of dawn until the rising of the sun is swifter for the seeking of provision than traveling through the land.

And he said in it: The Muslim is not to spit toward the qibla; and if he does so forgetfully, let him ask forgiveness. And he said in it: A man is not to blow upon the place of his prostration, nor upon his food, nor upon his incense. And he said in it:

> ولا يصلي أحدكم وبين يديه سيف، فإن القبلة آمن

None of you is to pray with a sword before him, for the qibla is a place of safety.

And he said in it: Whoever eats anything of the offensive foods, let him not come near the mosque.
