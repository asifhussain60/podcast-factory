# Bodies, Hair, and the Reform of Custom

**Bodies, Hair, and the Reform of Custom**

I turn now to the matters of cleanliness and the purifications of the natural disposition, and I quote first from the book of questions and answers already mentioned, which its compiler arranged according to the order of the chapters of the book *Da'a'im al-Islam*.

Question: What do you say, O diver into the current of the sciences, concerning what has come in the Shari'ah, that the uncircumcised man is not left so in Islam until he is circumcised, even if he reaches eighty years of age? So, our master, if a woman grows old and has still not undergone the circumcision of girls (الخفض), is she to be left without it or not?

Answer: She undergoes it and is not left without it.

Question: What do you say, O our master, our support, and our pillar, concerning the custom of the lands of India, that men and women part the hair of the head toward the middle and not toward the nape? Does this suffice them as a parting or not?

Answer: Let the hair be parted in two halves, from the front of the head to its back, and let no one deliberately neglect this.

Question: What do you say, O best of those from whose direction we hope the gates of good will be opened to us, concerning the hair of the beard, when some of it is long and some short, and the bearded man shortens part of it so that it is made even, and this adds to his comeliness? Is there anything against him in that or not?

Answer: He has been permitted to trim from the two sides of the beard (العارضين). Other than that, it is not permitted to trim from it.

Question: What do you say, O one whom God has made a fountainhead of honor for the people of obedience to God, concerning the hair of the human body apart from that whose removal has been commanded? Is there anything against a man if he shaves it, shortens it, or plucks it, or not?

Answer: There is nothing against him in that, except the beard, for he removes nothing from it.

Question: What do you say, O one whom God has made a physician who heals the souls of His friends of every ailment, concerning a man and his wife combing their hair with a single comb, for some of the people of India dislike this?

Answer: There is no reliance to be placed upon that.

Question: What do you say, O best of those who have driven from the eyes of our insight, by the light of his knowledge, every blindness, concerning what has come in the book of purity, that the first to be circumcised was Ibrahim (ع) at the head of eighty years of his life? Our master, is the intent in the outward sense that this ruling and what follows it had not been sent down before that, or not? May I be your ransom.

Answer: This is established in the outward sense, for Ibrahim (ع) was not commanded with purification until the head of eighty years.

Question: What do you say concerning what has come in the book of purity, that Ali (ع) said:

> يا معشر النساء إذا خفضتن بناتكن فلا تستقصيه ولتترك به شيئًا فهو أحسن للوجه وأحظى للزوج

"O company of women, when you circumcise your daughters, do not take it to the utmost, and let something of it be left, for that is better for the face and more favored by the husband."

Our master, is there sin upon them if they take it to the utmost, and what is the meaning of his words, that it is better for the face?

Answer: One acts upon what has been mentioned, and it is not taken to the utmost, for that is better for the husband's inclination toward her.

Question: What do you say, O best of those whom God has made a storehouse for the secrets of His friends, concerning the saying of the Prophet:

> ليس لامرأة حاضت أن تتخذ قصة ولا جَمّة

"It is not for a woman who has begun to menstruate to take up a qassa or a jumma."

Our master, may I be your ransom, what is its meaning?

Answer: The jumma (الجمّة) is the plaiting of the hair from the side locks to the nape. The qassa (القصّة) is that the hair of the head be cut and about a quarter of it be left at the front. As for the qunzu'a (القنزعة), it is that the hair be taken and places of it left standing, and that too is forbidden.

Question: What do you say, O sea of the sciences, concerning this, that most of the women of the people of India engrave their palms and their feet with henna for adornment, and some of them dye their teeth with a red preparation or something else, likewise for adornment, while the Shari'ah has forbidden the engraving of dye? What is their condition?

Answer: The engraving of dye has indeed been forbidden. As for the teeth, there is no harm in dyeing them.

Question: What does our master say concerning a man who applies henna to his hands and feet for the sake of adornment and not for the sake of any benefit? Is that permitted or not?

Answer: It is not fitting for a man to apply henna for the sake of adornment. Women are commanded with it, and for men to leave it, where there is no cause, is better; yet there is no sin upon the one who does it.

Question: What do you say, O treasury of the jewels of wisdom, concerning a man or a woman whose teeth are uneven in their alignment, some long and some short, and who levels them by filing with a stone or with iron or with something else, as an increase in comeliness? Is there sin in that or not? Favor us with a satisfying answer; may God make us your ransom.

Answer: There is no harm in that for women.

Question: What do you say, O sea of hopes, concerning this, that the women of India pierce their own ears and the ears of their infant daughters, and keep the piercings open as far as they are able, so as to wear ornaments? Is there anything against them in that or not?

Answer: There is no harm in that.

Question: What do you say, O one by whom our souls have life, concerning a man who lets the hair of his head be, and neither combs it nor parts it each day? Does deficiency enter into his prayer or not? And how often should he comb it and part it?

Answer: If it is in two halves, there is no deficiency upon him. He should not leave it without a parting, and if he does so deliberately in defiance of the ruling, his prayer is corrupted.

Question: What do you say, O dwelling place of all who come to him out of perplexity, concerning combing the hair on the night of Friday, for most of the people of India hold that leaving it is a binding necessity?

Answer: No reliance is to be placed upon that.

Question: What does our master say, may God perpetuate his elevation, concerning what has been mentioned in the Shari'ah, that the uncircumcised is not left so in Islam until he is circumcised? Is he circumcised after his death if he was a child, or not?

Answer: The preferred practice of the sunna is that he be circumcised after his death.

Another has said: the prayer of a woman who has not undergone the circumcision of girls is valid, as is intercourse with her, and likewise for the man; but the sunna is that one hasten to it.

From the answer of our master Qadi al-Nu'man, may God sanctify his soul, to one who questioned him, namely the governor of his districts, al-Khattab ibn Wasim: You asked about a man found to be uncircumcised, who had married a woman and fathered children by her and had not been circumcised. It is upon him to be circumcised, for the uncircumcised is not left so in Islam, even if he reaches eighty years of age, unless he was born circumcised, for it has come down that some of the prophets (عليهم السلام) were born in that state. If the glans, or most of it, is exposed, he is not compelled to circumcision; and it is recommended, if he is young, that the skin be drawn back and whatever remains of it over the glans be cut away.

Our master and mawla Ja'far ibn Sulayman, may God exalt his sanctity and grant us his intercession, wrote in his noble letter which he named السيف الأقطع في إبطال البدع, by which he counseled his people and roused those among them who had grown heedless and long asleep, among the people of the Island of Yemen while he dwelt and settled there:

Section: Among these matters is what we have come to learn, that some of the womenfolk among your women, especially those bound by covenant among them, crop the hair of their heads by shaving, as men shave their heads. This, together with what we mentioned first, belongs to the contrary and inverted order of things: for that was something with which faces and heads are adorned, and this is something by which heads and faces are disfigured. When one of you reflects upon this, the aspects of the matter become plain to him. God, glorified and exalted, has placed the adornment of men in the hair of their beards, in its combing and its grooming, and the adornment of women in the hair of their heads, in its combing and its lengthening. As for hair, it has come down concerning it, in what has reached us of the prophetic reports, that it is one of the two beauties. So a woman's shaving of it and the cropping of her head is nothing but a disfigurement of her pleasing form. The Prophet has said concerning this that which removes the rust from hearts, where he said:

> إذا أراد أحدكم أن يتزوج امرأة فيسأل عن شعرها كما يسأل عن وجهها فان الشعر أحد الجمالين

"When one of you wishes to marry a woman, let him ask about her hair as he asks about her face, for the hair is one of the two beauties."

And from him:

> الشعر الحسن من كسوة الله فمن عرّى جسمه من كسوته لا سيما كسوة ربه سبحانه التي كساه إياها فقد شوّه بصورته ومن شوّه بصورته شاه في دنياه وآخرته

"Beautiful hair is part of the clothing of God. Whoever strips his body of its clothing, and above all of the clothing of his Lord with which He clothed him, has disfigured his own form; and whoever disfigures his form is made ugly in this world and the next."

The wonder is at those among them who have deemed this permissible and made a law of it for themselves, and at those who have not turned them away from it by prohibition and restraint, when all of them have learned what has come in the noble book *Da'a'im al-Islam* concerning the hair of women's heads, which indicates the prohibition of shaving it, both for the people of the guiding da'wa and for others. Its author, may God sanctify his soul, says:

> وقالوا يعني الأئمة من أهل بيت النبوة عليهم السلام في المرأة إذا تطهرت إنها تنقض شعرها الا أن تكون تعلم أن الماء يصل إلى بشرة رأسها ويبل شعرها كله وذلك أن تكون أظفار شعرها رخوة

"They said, meaning the imams of the household of prophethood, concerning the woman when she purifies herself, that she undoes her hair, unless she knows that the water reaches the skin of her head and wets all of her hair, and that is when the plaits of her hair are loose."

He said also, in mentioning the shaving and the shortening of the hair on release from ihram for the man and for the woman, and the distinction between them in that, and the running of the laws of the pure Shari'ah upon their proper foundation, reporting from Ali (ع) that he said concerning the bald man:

> يمر الموسي على رأسه

"The razor is passed over his head."

And from him (ع):

> إذا حلت المرأة من إحرامها أخذت من أطراف قرون رأسها

"When the woman is released from her ihram, she takes from the ends of the plaits of her head."

And he said also, may God sanctify his soul:

> وقد ذكرنا أن الأقرع والأصلع يمرّانِ الموسي على رأسيهما والمرأة تقصّر إذا حلت من الإحرام تقطع شيئاً من أطراف شعرها ولا تحلقه ولا شيئاً منه

"We have already mentioned that the man with scalp disease and the bald man pass the razor over their heads, and that the woman shortens her hair when she is released from ihram, cutting something from the ends of her hair; she does not shave it, nor anything of it."

This saying of his is among the most indicative proofs of the prohibition against women shaving their hair, whether little of it or much, since he forbade them that in the very matter of their release from ihram, with an emphatic prohibition into which no obscurity of any obscuring thing, small or great, can enter. Whoever walks this path in this matter has walked the way of his own right guidance and has removed misfortune from himself.

> ومن يرغب عن ملة إبراهيم الا مِن سفه نفسه

"And who turns away from the creed of Ibrahim except one who has made a fool of himself?"

Then he said, concerning a matter of the same kind:

Section: Among these matters is what we have come to learn, that the womenfolk among you altogether, including those who do not shave their hair, hold that they must shave the edges of their heads around the crown of the head, and shave the temples likewise. This is something with which God is not pleased in the woman who does it; indeed it is among the things that cast them into destruction, because of what has already been cited from the saying of our master Qadi al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad, already set down above, that the man with scalp disease and the bald man pass the razor over their heads, and that the woman shortens her hair when she is released from ihram, cutting something from the ends of her hair and shaving neither it nor anything of it. For if it is not permissible for women, on their release from ihram, to shave the ends of the hair of their heads, nor anything of it ever, but rather they cut from it only what they cut, then such shaving is never permissible as a standing custom, and its practice never calls to guidance. Whoever among them contravenes that has contravened the marks of the upright religion and has pulled down its foundation; and she has turned away from the creed of Ibrahim, and none turns away from it except one who has made a fool of himself. Nothing is incumbent upon us but this kind of instruction for the people of religion, because of the word of God, glorified and exalted, concerning that:

> وما على الرسول الا البلاغ المبين

"The Messenger has no duty but clear delivery of the message."

Then he said, concerning the circumcision of girls:

Section: Among these matters also is what we have come to learn, that you hasten the circumcision of your girls in the days that fall short of ten days, or beyond that up to a limit of forty days, which is the outermost term among the elite of you and the common. That is contrary to the Shari'ah of the Prophet and to the religion of Islam, and contrary to what our master Qadi al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad set down concerning it in the noble book *Da'a'im al-Islam*, reporting it from your mawla the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), where he stated it with the clearest exposition, and from him (ع) that he said:

> أسرعوا بختان أولادكم فأنه أطهر لهم

"Hasten the circumcision of your children, for it is purer for them."

And he said:

> لا تخفض الجارية دون أن تبلغ سبع سنين

"The girl is not circumcised before she reaches seven years."

This clear prohibition from the one who summons to guidance may never be transgressed. It belongs to the firm limits of God, blessed be His names.

> ومن يتعد حدود فقد ظلم نفسه

"Whoever transgresses the limits has wronged himself."

We have further learned in this matter that, at the time of the circumcision of the girls, those of the household and the neighbors who are to be gathered are gathered, hospitality is prepared, and there is performed for it the forbidden prayer with the call to prayer and the summons; and people spread word of their circumcision, so that near place and far, close neighbor and distant, hear of it. Nothing of this belongs to the matter except in the purification of male children. The circumcision of girls is only to be done in a concealed state, of which none is aware but the girl herself, the woman who performs it, and a few of the women of the household. Whoever makes that affair of theirs public has offended against the canons of his religion, and offended again. This belongs to the chapter of modesty, of which the Prophet said:

> الحياء من الإيمان ومن لا حياء له لا دين له

"Modesty is part of faith, and whoever has no modesty has no religion."

By this same measure is the deliberate deferral of the circumcision of girls, for it carries a meaning among the meanings intended by the Mighty Creator in the affairs of His precious religion. It is not permissible that His will be contravened in it, nor that His command be transgressed in what He has forbidden, glorified be He, in any of the forbidden matters. So it is in all matters whatever, the branches of them and the roots. Every sensible matter has an intelligible matter set opposite it, and every outward likeness has within it that which is likened. Whoever contravenes the established practice in this has nullified it and has damaged the road and its passages. It is never permissible to do that, in what the friends of God (عليهم السلام), the friends of their ages, have indicated and by which they have given warning. God, glorified and exalted, says concerning this:

> ما أتاكم الرسول فخذوه ما نهاكم عنه فانتهوا

"Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; and whatever he forbids you, abstain from it."

Then our master Ja'far said, concerning the purification of children:

Section: Among these matters also is what has become known of the delay by most of you in the purification of your male children, whose hastening is commanded in the transmitted prophetic report, in which the speaker among the imams (عليهم السلام) said: hasten the circumcision of your children, for it is purer for them. This is the reverse of your practice with the girls, so you have inverted the matter in both, contrary to the command of the Mighty Creator. It has been learned of people who are not of these regions of yours that they leave their children uncircumcised until close upon twenty years of age, and that is a manifest contravention of the Shari'ah of Islam.

The wonder of wonders in this, for whoever reflects upon it with the reflection it deserves, in what has been of affairs and what shall be, and surely in that are signs for a people who reflect, is that all who are before you of the people of the Book among the protected communities hasten the circumcision of their children with the utmost haste, on the seventh of their days, in compliance with what Moses, the one addressed by God (ع), prescribed for them in his law from his Lord. They have carried on with that practice through these successive thousands of years, enduring the pain of it for their children at that tender age; while you are nearer than they to the covenant of your Prophet, and yet you cast aside his command in this, when he commanded you with haste, and you defer it to what lies beyond and above those days, with this deliberate delay contrary to the canons of the Shari'ah of Islam. Glory be to God the Mighty, and again glory be to God the Mighty at what you do in this, for its matter is grave.

> يعظكم الله أن تعودوا لمثله أبداً إن كنتم مؤمنين وبين الله لكم الآيات والله عليم حكيم

"God admonishes you never to return to the like of it, if you are believers. God makes clear to you the signs, and God is All-Knowing, All-Wise."

And our master Ja'far said, concerning the practice of men applying henna, in the same noble letter:

Section: Among these matters also is what has become known and reported of some among you who are bound by the bond of allegiance to the friends of God (عليهم السلام), that they imitate the women of their households in dyeing their hands with henna, and some of them their hands and their feet. That is something neither the law of the Prophet nor the religion of Islam permits. It was appointed in both cases for women and not for men, like jewelry, which belongs to them and not to men in every circumstance. The man who does this and takes it from the women, adorning himself with it and resembling them in it, is like one who adorns himself as they adorn themselves with ornaments of silver and gold. None chooses this for himself but one whose reason has been lost and gone. So beware of committing it, for it is among the acts of disobedience to God, the Mighty and Exalted, and adorn yourselves in its place with the fear of God, for you will not attain your hope in any of your affairs except by the fear of God. You have heard the report current in the sunna among non-Arabs and Arabs alike:

> حلي الرجال الأدب وحلي النساء الذهب

"The ornament of men is good conduct, and the ornament of women is gold."

Leave the opponents of the guiding da'wa and what they are upon in this, for the one who by their claim is the master and owner of their affair had gone before them in that practice, and that detestable deed is reckoning enough for him and for them. God, God, in keeping yourselves clean of it, so that your faces may be made white before Him on the day when faces are made white and faces are made black.

And our master Ja'far, may God exalt his sanctity, said in answering questions put to him by the author of this book, his servant, which he answered from his Island of Yemen to the Island of Hind: As for what you asked, that it has come in the books of the pure Shari'ah that girls are not circumcised before seven years, so that if one of them passes on while below that limit, is she circumcised as male children are circumcised when they have not been circumcised in their lifetimes, or is she buried in her state without it? The point is that, since that stated limit has been set for their circumcision, it is not permissible that it be transgressed in their case.

Our master Muhammad ibn Fahd al-Mukarrami, may God exalt his sanctity, was asked about a daughter who dies before reaching seven years: is she circumcised or not? He answered his questioner: she is not circumcised, because she has not reached the age of circumcision.

And our mawla the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his four hundred counsels:

> اختنوا أولادكم يوم السابع ولا يمنعكم حرٌّ ولا بردٌ فأنه طهور للجسد وان الأرض تضجّ إلى الله من بول الأقلف عليها

"Circumcise your children on the seventh day, and let neither heat nor cold prevent you, for it is a purification of the body; and the earth cries out to God because of the urine of the uncircumcised upon it."
