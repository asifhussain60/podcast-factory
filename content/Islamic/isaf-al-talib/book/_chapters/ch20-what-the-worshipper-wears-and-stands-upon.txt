# What the Worshipper Wears and Stands Upon

I turn now to the dress of the worshipper and to what he prostrates upon.

I find in the book of questions and answers mentioned at the outset, which one of its arrangers ordered according to the chapters of the book دعائم الإسلام (Pillars of Islam), the following exchanges.

Question: What do you say about the prayer mat, the rug, and other cloths in which the qibla or something else is figured, yet in such a way that it does not occupy the worshipper when he looks at it? Is his prayer upon them permitted or not, whether the figuring lies in the place of prostration or elsewhere? Answer: If it is of a kind that occupies the worshipper when he looks at it, his prayer is not permitted. If it does not occupy him, his prayer is permitted, except in the case of images, toward which he does not pray.

Question: What do you say about a man who prays in a garment dyed with safflower that is not deeply red? Is his prayer permitted or not? And what of a woman who prays in pure silk, or in a garment of a very deep red, whether dyed with safflower or not? Is her prayer permitted or not? Answer: This belongs to the domain of etiquette. If anyone wears such a garment it does not spoil the prayer performed in it, whether the wearer is a man or a woman, except for pure silk, which is disliked for men.

Question: What do you say about what has come in the books of the Sharia, that if anything of a woman's عورة (ʿawra) becomes exposed during the prayer her prayer is spoiled, whether she knew of it or not? It is not the custom of the women of the people of the daʿwa in India to wear the long shift and the head-veil, and their legs are uncovered in the prayer once or twice. How then stands their prayer? Answer: The covering is indispensable, for the whole of a woman is عورة. If anything of her ʿawra is exposed, prayer is not permitted to her, whether she knew this ruling or was ignorant of it. This is its meaning.

Question: Concerning the man wading through water, the man in fear, and the traveler pressing hard upon his journey, who carry with them weaponry that bars them from the prayer, such as the feathering of arrows, leather straps, the trimmings of daggers, and garments of pure silk: is their prayer completed for them without repetition, or not? Answer: The prayer is valid only in pure garments, and whatever is impure the worshipper strips from himself.

Question: What do you say about a man who prays with weights of gold and silver upon him, wrought or unwrought, tucked into his waistband? Is his prayer established or not? Answer: His prayer is complete and established.

Question: Since it is said in the abridgment of the Musannaf that there is no harm in praying upon a rug in which there are figures, so long as they are not in the place of prostration and do not divert him from the prayer, is prayer upon such a rug permitted or not? And likewise, if garments bearing figures are worn, is prayer in them permitted or not? Answer: As for the rug and the garment in which there are figures, prayer is not permitted when they are seen; when they are not seen, prayer in them and upon them is permitted.

Question: If the worshipper prays upon stone, upon unbaked brick, or upon the like of these, is prayer upon them permitted or not? Answer: The obligatory prayer upon them is not permitted, only the supererogatory and the sunna prayer.

Question: What do you say about a man who applies henna to his hand or foot for adornment and not for any benefit? Is that permitted or not? Answer: It is not fitting that men use henna for adornment. Women are commanded to use it. For men, to leave it aside without cause is better, and yet there is no sin upon one who does it.

From another place: prayer is permitted in the garments of the children of the Muslims and the slaves of the Muslims, so long as no impurity appears in them and none is seen, when they themselves do not pray. Whoever prays and finishes his prayer and then sees that his garment is impure, if he knew of it beforehand his prayer is void and he repeats it, whether its time has passed or not. A man is not to pray bareheaded, without turban and without mantle; but if he finds neither, he prays. Whoever prays leaving upon his shoulders only a small kerchief, that is, his handcloth, while he has another garment besides it, his prayer is not permitted; but if he finds no other, his prayer in it is permitted.

It is said in the book of the abridgment of the Musannaf: if a man prays with his ʿawra uncovered and does not know of it until he has finished the prayer, and then comes to know, he must repeat it. And if a free woman or a slave woman prays with her ʿawra uncovered, whether she knew of it or not, her prayer is spoiled.

Our master Jaʿfar ibn Mansur al-Yaman, may God exalt his sanctity, says in the book of the obligations and the limits of religion: it is lawful for a man to uncover his head openly, to go out among the congregation, and to pray with his head uncovered. If a man prays with something in his mouth, such as a gemstone or a dirham or the like, and it does not prevent him from forming the letters properly, his prayer is complete. And if there is something in his hand that he is holding, and it does not prevent him from fulfilling the limits of his prayer, there is no harm upon him; yet to be free of it is better for him.

From the same: he who has nothing but an impure garment and can obtain no other, and has no water with which to purify it, prays in it and repeats when he finds another garment or water to purify it. And if he has two garments, one of them impure, and he does not know which of the two it is, it is incumbent upon him to pray that prayer in each one of them, intending by it the performance of the obligation. There is no harm in praying upon a rug in which there are figures, so long as they are not in the place of prostration and do not divert him from the prayer. As for plaster and the like, over which carrion is burned and with which the mosque and the house are plastered, there is no harm in prostrating upon it, for the fire has purified it.

In the book of the interpretation of the Daʿaʾim, upon the saying:

> ولا بأس في الصلاة في القميص الواحد الكثيف إذا أزره

There is no harm in praying in a single thick shirt if he belts it, meaning the worshipper. The sense of this is so that, when he bows, his private part should not appear from its edge.

From the same, upon the saying:

> مثل جناحي الخطاف

Like the two wings of the swallow. This means a garment that he wraps over his back and whose two ends he brings back upon his shoulders, when he finds nothing other than that.

From the same, upon the saying:

> نهى عن اشتمال الصمّاء

He forbade the wrapping called اشتمال الصمّاء. That is when a man wraps himself in a single garment, setting its middle upon his head or upon his shoulders, drawing one end with his left hand against his body, and turning the end that is on his right toward his left and behind him, gathering it with his right hand, so that he becomes as though sewn into it with both his hands beneath it. If he prays in this condition he is not able to perform the prayer, he does not set his palms upon the ground as is required, he is not able to bow nor to prostrate, and he cannot manage the raising of his hands. Such a prayer is therefore incomplete and does not suffice him.

It is said in the book of the abridgment of the Musannaf: as for the slave woman, the woman under a contract of manumission, the woman freed at her master's death, and the girl who has not yet reached menstruation, there is no harm in their praying without a head covering; and whichever of them is set free while she does not know of it repeats every prayer she prayed after her freedom without a head covering.

It is said in the book of questions and answers whose questions are from the most venerable Shaykh Yusuf ibn Ismaʿil ibn Ibrahim al-Samati al-Hindi, and whose answers are from our master Idris ibn al-Hasan, may God exalt his sanctity. Question: Is prayer permitted upon stones and plaster? Favor us with a clear and sufficient answer. Answer: The Prophet said:

> جُعلت لي الأرض مسجداً وطهوراً

The earth has been made for me a place of prostration and a means of purification.

And everything of the earth with which dry ablution is not permitted, prayer upon it is not permitted.

It is said in the book of the questions of the Sharia and their answers, whose number is one hundred and three questions, put by certain of the excellent shaykhs of India and answered by certain of the summoners of Yemen, may God sanctify their souls.

The seventeenth question: concerning the free woman who prays with a net upon her head, does the net suffice her in place of the mantle or not? Answer: It is not recorded that the prayer suffices her in the shift and the head-veil unless the two are thick, and the net does not cover.

The fourth question: concerning the camels, cattle, and sheep that feed upon filth, when their milk and their clarified butter, or the white of the eggs of hens, reaches the garments or the bodies of those praying, does it render them impure or not? Answer: A small amount of it is permitted.

It is said in the book of the interpretation of the Sharia: there is no prayer except upon the earth or upon what grows from it, such as green plants. And it is said therein: the Prophet said:

> حُبّب إليّ من دنياكم ثلاثة النساء والطيب وجعلت قرة عيني في الصلاة

Three things of your world have been made beloved to me: women and perfume, and the delight of my eye has been placed in the prayer.

And it is said therein: the Prophet (ع) said:

> كل صلاة لا يقرأ فيها بفاتحة الكتاب فهي خداج غير تامة

Every prayer in which the Opening of the Book is not recited is defective and incomplete.

And it is said therein:

> لا صلاة في أعطان الإبل ولا بأس بالصلاة في مرابض الغنم بعد أن تكنس وتُرش

There is no prayer in the resting places of camels, and there is no harm in praying in the folds of sheep after they have been swept and sprinkled with water.

And our master the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his four hundred counsels of etiquette:

> لا يقومنّ أحدكم بين يدي الله عز وجل الا وعليه ثوب نظيف

Let none of you stand before God, the Mighty and Exalted, except in a clean garment.

And he said therein:

> لا يصلي الرجل في قميص متوشحاً فأنه فعل قوم لوط

A man is not to pray in a shirt draped loosely across him, for that is the practice of the people of Lot.

And he said therein:

> لا تجوز الصلاة في ثوب يعسطل فيه على عنقه وفي القميّص الصفيق يردّه عليه

Prayer is not permitted in a garment that hangs slack about his neck, nor in a close-woven shirt unless he draws it over himself.

And he said therein:

> لا يسجد الرجل على صورة ولا على بساط هي فيه

A man is not to prostrate upon an image, nor upon a rug in which there is one.

And he said therein:

> ولا يعقد الرجل الدرهم الذي فيه الصورة في ثوبه وهو يصلي وتجوز أن تكون الدراهم في هميان أو ثوب إذا كان طاهراً ولا يسجد الرجل على كدس الحنطة ولا على شعير ولا على شيء مما يؤكل ولا على الخبز

A man is not to knot into his garment, while he prays, a dirham bearing an image; and it is permitted for the coins to be in a money belt or in a cloth, provided it is pure. And a man is not to prostrate upon a heap of wheat, nor upon barley, nor upon anything that is eaten, nor upon bread.
