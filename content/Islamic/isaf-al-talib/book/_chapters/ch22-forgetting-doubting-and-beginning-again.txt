# Forgetting, Doubting, and Beginning Again

I turn now to the mention of omission in the prayer.

In the question and answer book I named at the outset, which one of its arrangers set out following the order of the chapters of the Book of the Pillars of Islam, this is asked: What do you say if someone makes room for another to pray, standing in for a man who does not know all the places in prayer that require the prostration of omission? Must that man prostrate the two prostrations of omission after completing every obligatory act in which something appears uncertain to him, or not? The answer is: if it is established that he has indeed committed an omission, he prostrates for it; otherwise he does not.

Question: What do you say regarding the statement in the Book of Purity that whoever errs by not pronouncing the opening takbir, or leaves it deliberately or out of ignorance, must repeat the prayer? O our master, if a man forgets to open the prayer after every two rak'ahs in the four rak'ah and six rak'ah prayers, does that invalidate his prayer or not? The answer is: if he did not sit after each pair of rak'ahs, his prayer is invalidated.

Question: What do you say regarding the statement in the Book of Purity that whoever inadvertently adds to his prayer, even without sitting for the tashahhud, must repeat the prayer; and that if he adds an extra bowing he repeats the prayer, but if he adds an extra prostration he completes the prayer and performs the two prostrations of omission? What does this mean? The answer is: if he adds a bowing and has not sat for the tashahhud, he must repeat the prayer. But if he has sat for the tashahhud and completed two rak'ahs, whether with an additional bowing or an additional prostration, he has done so in order.

Question: What do you say about a man who is praying behind an Imam and neglects one of the pillars of the prayer while following the Imam? How is his prayer? The answer is: the Imam bears the responsibility for the omission, except in the case of the bowing and the opening takbir, and the first thing is that he should not neglect those.

Question: What do you say regarding the statement in religious law that whoever forgets a prostration should prostrate after delivering the salaam, when he remembers? O our master, how does he prostrate after having left the prayer? The answer is: he prostrates if he remembers before delivering the salaam; if he has already left the prayer, he has no recourse.

Question: What does our master say about a man who prayed the obligatory prayer and recited it in reverse order, from bottom to top? Must he perform the two prostrations of omission or not? The answer is: there is nothing wrong with that, and it is not obligatory upon him to perform the two prostrations of omission.

Question: A man prayed some of the obligatory Zuhr prayer and sat down and recited the first tashahhud, then prayed two more rak'ahs and recited the tashahhud again, then recalled that he had prayed only three rak'ahs. What should he do? The answer is: he prays the rak'ah he missed after the salaam, reciting in it the Opening and a surah, because it is one of the first two rak'ahs; he then recites the tashahhud, delivers the salaam, and performs the two prostrations of omission.

Question: Whoever neglects the first tashahhud in the obligatory prayer but recites the final tashahhud and then remembers before delivering the salaam, must he perform the prostration of omission or not? The answer is: there is no explicit text on this, and where there is no text it has not been stipulated that the worshipper must prostrate.

Question: Regarding the man who repeats a single surah in the obligatory prayer after the Opening, must he perform the prostration of omission or not? The answer is: this too has not been stipulated.

Question: Regarding a man who omits one obligation, should he prostrate two prostrations of omission; and if he omits three obligations, does he prostrate separately for each omission with two prostrations, or do two prostrations suffice for all of them? The answer is: the prostration of omission is not multiplied.

Question: Regarding a man who prays behind an Imam and makes it clear that he intends in his prayer to be a follower, if the Imam delivers the salaam of the dhikr yet he did not intend the prayer, is his prayer valid or not? The answer is: if the Imam intends the prayer on his behalf, then it is so; if he does not, the prayer lapses.

Question: What is transmitted from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع), who said:

> من سهى عن القراءة في بعض الصلاة فقرأ في ما بقي منها أجزاه ذلك فإن نسي القراءة كلها وأتم الركوع والسجود والتكبير لم يكن عليه الإعادة

Whoever forgets the recitation in part of the prayer and recites in what remains of it, that suffices him; and if he forgets the recitation altogether but completes the bowing, the prostration, and the magnification, he is under no obligation to repeat. The answer is: this statement presents no difficulty.

Question: If he forgets to recite some of the Quran or all of it, must he perform the two prostrations of omission or not? The answer is: the two prostrations of omission are required, and he performs them.

Question: What do you say about a man whom the Imam has preceded in some part of the prayer? Must the one who came late perform the two prostrations of omission that the Imam himself owed before the latecomer arrived? The answer is: whoever enters the prayer with the Imam prostrates the two prostrations for his Imam's omission, even if the only part he caught with him before the salaam was the opening takbir.

Question: In the case of one who was preceded and prays part of the prayer with the Imam, then makes an error in what remains upon him, must he prostrate for the omission or not? The answer is: if he omits something while praying on his own, he prostrates.

Question: It is stated in the text that

> الأعمال لا تقبل الا بالنيات

Deeds are accepted only with intentions. So if a person neglects, when purifying himself, or when beginning his prayer, or when fasting, or when performing any of the duties of God, to contract the intention with the tongue while he intends the act in his heart, is his action valid or not? The answer is: the intention resides in the heart, and what is said with the tongue is merely a verbal expression, as mentioned in the Pillars; if he has contracted the intention in his heart, his act is complete.

He said in the councils of instruction that doubt in prayer has its root in omission, which is inattention. It is a word that troubles many of those who occupy themselves with jurisprudence, so that they suppose doubt to be like omission, and it is not so, because the one who commits an omission is certain of what he has omitted, while the doubter is not certain of what he doubts.

Concerning the prostration of omission, it is said in the reports that it is performed after the salaam, and that after it one recites a brief testimony:

> بسم الله وبالله وأشهد أن لا اله إلا الله وصلى الله على محمد وآله

In the name of God, and by God, and I bear witness that there is no god but God, and may God bless Muhammad and his family.

It is also said: whoever falls into doubt while praying and does not know whether he is in an obligatory prayer or a voluntary one, if he stood up for the prescribed prayer and then became inattentive afterward and supposed it to be voluntary, that prayer suffices him for the prescribed one and his inattention does no harm; but if he stood up for a voluntary prayer, then it is voluntary. It is said: whoever doubts and does not know whether he prayed Zuhr or Asr, if some of the time of Asr still remains he prays it; and if the sun has set and he then doubts, there is no making up due from him. It is said: if you doubt about the dawn prayer, pray it again; and if you doubt about the sunset prayer, pray it again. It is said: whoever commits omissions repeatedly in a single prayer owes nothing but the two prostrations of omission.

It is mentioned in the book Mukhtasar al-Musannaf that if a man prays a four rak'ah prayer and then, through inattention, rises for a fifth, then if he had already recited the tashahhud in the fourth he delivers the salaam while standing and departs; and if he had not recited it, he sits, recites the tashahhud, and performs the two prostrations of omission. If he remembers that he rose to a fifth rak'ah only after he has bowed, and he had recited the tashahhud in the fourth, his prayer is complete, and he joins to that rak'ah another as a voluntary prayer for himself; but if he had not recited the tashahhud in the fourth and did not sit for the length of the tashahhud, he repeats the prayer. And whoever forgets a prayer and does not know whether he prayed two, three, or four rak'ahs, and the Imam who prays aloud in what is prayed silently, has done ill, though his prayer is complete; and if he did that inattentively, the two prostrations of omission are upon him. If he is not an Imam but prays for himself, and recites silently in what is recited aloud, or aloud in what is recited silently, nothing is due from him. Whatever comes concerning omission in the prayer of residence holds the same rank in the prayer of travel.

It is mentioned in the questions put by certain of the ranks of the island of India, with their answers from certain of the summoners of the island of Yemen, may God sanctify all their souls, in the twenty sixth issue: concerning the man who is inattentive in the recitation in the obligatory prayer and recites in the second rak'ah, after the first, a surah taken from above, which is not what is required in the obligatory prayer, are the two prostrations of omission upon him or not? The answer is: this has not been stipulated, and he should not do it deliberately. And in the first issue of that collection: it is in the text that deeds are accepted only with intentions, so if a person neglects, when purifying himself, or in his prayer, or in his fasting, or when performing any of the duties of God, to contract the intention with the tongue while he intends the act in his heart, is his action valid or not? And if he goes beyond that and abandons the intention of the tongue, is his action valid or not? The answer is: the intention resides in the heart, and what is uttered on the tongue is merely a statement, as mentioned in the Pillars; if he has contracted the intention in his heart, his act is complete.

It is mentioned in the book on the interpretation of the Sharia that whoever misses a prayer or forgets it should pray it when he remembers it.

Our master, the owner of the Mustansiriyya councils, God's (ع), said in the ninth of them: Listen now to what we recite to you of the meaning of doubt and omission in the prayer. The places of omission in prayer are seven, in six of which the two prostrations of omission are proper. They are the two that rub down the nose (المرغمتان), and they are so named because they rub down the nose of the devil when the corruption of forgetfulness is repaired by them. Of these:

1. Whoever is inattentive to a prostration prostrates when he remembers it, and the two prostrations of omission are upon him.
2. Whoever forgets to sit for the first tashahhud and stands for the third rak'ah, and remembers before he bows that he did not sit, sits and recites the tashahhud, and when he delivers the salaam he performs the two prostrations of omission.
3. If his inattention to the first tashahhud carries him on until he has bowed and prostrated, he completes his prayer, and the two prostrations of omission are upon him.
4. Whoever is inattentive to the second tashahhud and delivers the salaam owes the two prostrations of omission.
5. Whoever, through inattention, delivers the salaam after two rak'ahs when he is in a four rak'ah prayer, prays two rak'ahs after them and performs the two prostrations of omission.
6. Whoever, through inattention, rises from the final tashahhud after completing it to a fifth rak'ah owes the two prostrations of omission.
7. Whoever is inattentive behind the Imam owes no prostration of omission, for the Imam bears that for him.

These are the seven places of omission in which the two prostrations are proper. As for doubt in prayer, it is a word that troubles many of those who occupy themselves with jurisprudence, so that they suppose it to be like omission, and it is not so, because the one who commits an omission is certain of what he omitted, while the doubter is not certain of what he doubts. It falls into twelve cases; in some of them there is a prostration of omission, and in others nothing at all. Those in which there is a prostration of omission are:

1. If the worshipper doubts about the bowing while he is still in the prayer, he bows, and the two prostrations of omission are upon him.
2. If he doubts and does not know whether he is in one rak'ah or two, he builds upon what is certain, and that is the lesser.
3. Likewise, if he doubts and does not know whether he has prayed two rak'ahs or three, he builds upon what is certain.
4. If he doubts and does not know whether he has prayed three or four, he delivers the salaam, prays two rak'ahs seated, and performs the two prostrations of omission.
5. If he does not know whether he has prayed two or four, he delivers the salaam, prays two rak'ahs standing, and performs the two prostrations of omission.

As for those in which nothing is due:

6. If the worshipper doubts about the opening takbir after he has bowed, he continues in his prayer and nothing is due from him.
7. Likewise if he doubts about the recitation after his bowing.
8. Likewise if he doubts about the bowing after his prostration.
9. Likewise if he doubts about the first prostration after his second prostration.
10. Likewise if he doubts about the prostration after the tashahhud.
11. Likewise if he doubts about anything of the prayer after the salaam.

In all of this nothing is due from him.

Our master, the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his four hundred manners:

> من كان على يقينه فأصابه شك فليمض على يقينه فان الشك لا يدفع اليقين ولا ينقصه

Whoever is upon his certainty and is then visited by doubt should proceed upon his certainty, for doubt does not repel certainty nor diminish it.

It is mentioned in the questions that come from certain of the shaykhs of India, may God be pleased with them, with the answers from our masters the summoners of Yemen, may God exalt their sanctity and grant us their intercession and their nearness. Question: What do you say about a man who is inattentive in his intention and does not say that he is a follower? Is his prayer valid or not? The answer is: if his intention was the Imam's, his prayer is complete, even though he did not utter it.

It is mentioned in the book of questions: I was asked about one who doubts in his prayer and does not know how much of it he has prayed. He builds upon what is certain and prostrates the two prostrations of omission after he delivers the salaam. But if he is certain of nothing and does not know whether he added or fell short in his prayer, he begins the prayer again from its beginning, and so likewise does the Imam.

I come now to the mention of what interrupts the prayer.

It is mentioned in the question and answer book named at the outset, arranged according to the chapters of the book of the Pillars of Islam: whoever prays and vomits while in the prayer, and it reaches his garment, washes it if he finds water near at hand; otherwise his prayer is void, and he then washes his garment and prays. Whoever breaks his purity before the tashahhud repeats the prayer; and if he breaks it after he has said,

> السلام عليك أيها النبي ورحمة الله وبركاته السلام علينا وعلى عباد الله الصالحين

Peace be upon you, O Prophet, and the mercy of God and His blessings; peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of God, his prayer is permitted and he does not repeat it.

It is mentioned in the Book of Purity that whoever is afflicted with a nosebleed or with vomiting while in the prayer, and is able to wash the blood of the nosebleed and stop it, and to rinse away the vomit, without turning his face from the qibla, does that and builds upon his prayer; and if he turns his face from the qibla, he repeats the prayer from its beginning. It is said in the reports: unless he finds its smell, or hears its sound, or another hears it from him, meaning the breaking of purity.

It is mentioned in the book on the interpretation of the prayer that the Prophet (ع) said:

> ثلاث تقطع الصلاة المرأة والحمار والكلب الأسود

Three things interrupt the prayer: a woman, a donkey, and a black dog. And it is said that the Messenger of God (ع) stood in prayer, and a dog passed before him, then a donkey passed, then a woman passed while he was praying; and when he had finished he said:

> رأيت الذي رأيتم وليس يقطع صلاة المؤمن شيء ولكن ادرءوا ما استطعتم

I saw what you saw, and nothing interrupts the prayer of a believer; but ward off what you are able to ward off.

Our master, the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said in his four hundred manners:

> لا يقطع الصلاة التبسم ويقطعها القهقهة

Smiling does not interrupt the prayer, but loud laughter interrupts it.

It is mentioned in the book of questions, whose question is from the most honorable Shaykh Shim'un ibn Muhammad Ghuri al-Hindi and whose answer is from our master Badr al-Din Hasan ibn Idris, may God exalt their sanctity and grant us their intercession and their nearness. Question: What do you say about a man who entered the obligatory prayer with the opening takbir and recited, and then the Imam arrived and went forward while the man was standing, and when the Imam bowed he bowed with him without renewing the opening takbir? What is the state of his prayer? The answer is: he must repeat it.

I come now to the mention of the prayer of one who has been preceded in part of the prayer.

It is mentioned in the question and answer book named at the outset, arranged according to the chapters of the book of the Pillars of Islam. Question: What do you say about a worshipper who reaches the Imam while he is in the final tashahhud of any of the five prayers, and the latecomer intends in his prayer that he is a follower, and sits while the Imam sits, until when the Imam delivers the salaam of his prayer the latecomer rises and prays? Has the latecomer attained the congregation, and is his prayer valid or not? The answer is: if he reached him in the tashahhud, then the tashahhud is the last of the limits of the prayer; he intends the prayer as a follower, sits, and makes the supplication of turning toward God; and when the Imam delivers the salaam he rises and prays his prayer, and he has attained the prayer of the congregation.

It is mentioned in the book Mukhtasar al-Musannaf: if he reaches the Imam in the bowing, he says the takbir for the opening and then the takbir for the bowing, and that rak'ah is counted for him; and if he fears to miss it, a single takbir suffices him for the opening and the bowing. Whoever reaches the Imam having been preceded by him enters into whatever the Imam is in; if it is something that is counted he builds upon it, and if not he is one performing a voluntary act, and he prays what he has caught and makes up what escaped him.

It is said in the reports concerning the Imam whose purity is broken, so that he puts forward a man who has been preceded: if that man does not know what the Imam preceded him with, he proceeds upon his certainty until those behind him say the glorification to him, and then he returns to what is otherwise.

Our master and lord Muhammad ibn Fahd, may God exalt his sanctity, said in his noble epistle which he titled The Broad Road of the Path of Guidance for the Rightly Guided, in the twentieth question: You asked also, may God grant you success in what pleases Him, about the latecomer in the prayer who is not able to make the intention by uttering it with his tongue, and who enters with the Imam into whatever part of his prayer and its rak'ahs the Imam is in. The answer to you in this, by the granting of God, be He glorified, of what is correct in speech upon it, and its truth is thus: it has come in the books of the friends of God (عليهم السلام) that if the latecomer reaches the Imam in any rak'ah of his prayer and the Imam has not yet raised his head from the bowing in it, and he says a single takbir and enters with him into his prayer by it, then he has attained that rak'ah with him, and that is so together with the contracting of the intention in his heart upon it.
