# The Sun, the Moon, and the Order of Obedience

It is the nobler of the two and the more fitting to be relied upon in the performance of the obligatory duties. Yet in saying this we have not set aside the ruling of the sighting of the eye (رؤية العين), for these two sightings are two foundations in the performance of the obligatory duties, and one of them is more excellent than the other. The more excellent belongs to the select, who go before others by their knowledge, and they are the possessors of knowledge (أولوا العلم). The other belongs to the generality, whose ignorance obliges them to follow the people of knowledge, and they are the ones bare of understanding. The relation between the two sightings is like the relation between the two times of a prayer, the first of which is more excellent than the second.

So it is with all matters: they are two, hidden and apparent. The hidden is known only by the select among the excellent, while the apparent is known by the select and the generality alike. The select are obliged to carry out what follows from what they have come to know, just as the common people are obliged to carry out what follows from what they have come to know. This being so, what is there to deny in this: that the learned man, by the excellence of his knowledge, is bound to fast by the hidden vision of the soul (الرؤية النفسانية), and that the ignorant man is bound to follow the learned man in that? It is just as the one taking the pre-dawn meal who knows, by his hidden knowledge, that dawn has broken, so that food, drink, and marital relations become forbidden to him and fasting becomes binding upon him, while the one taking the pre-dawn meal who is ignorant of the dawn must follow the learned man in that.

As for the saying of the Prophet (ع),

> فإن غمّ عليكم فعدوا ثلاثين يوماً

"If it is obscured from you, then count thirty days," it can scarcely be held, upon consideration, that what he intended by it was the sighting of the eye. For fasting by the sight of the eye, when the impediments of cloud and the like are lifted, can hardly ever yield months of thirty days; a month may well be twenty-nine days. And if that is so, it is not permissible that the Prophet (ع) should bind his community, in one of the commands of Allah Almighty, to a rule in which doubt may arise, so that this becomes the cause of an addition to an obligation or a subtraction from it, leaving his community to perform it as though walking in the dark. Nor is it permissible that he should command his community to fast an added day in one of the deficient months on account of the occurrence of cloud, when, had the cloud not been there, it would have been known that fasting on that day is forbidden and that breaking the fast is required. For fasting is worship of Allah Almighty and belongs to the religion by which Allah is served, and the servants are commanded to sincerity in it, by the saying of Allah Almighty:

> وما أُمِروا إلا ليعبدوا الله مخلصين له الدين

"They were commanded only to worship Allah, sincere to Him in religion."

Sincerity is other than doubt and confusion. And since sincerity is other than doubt and confusion, and since doubt and confusion have entered into this rule and disturbed it, we say that the Prophet (ع) did not call people to doubt and confusion in the worship of Allah Almighty. And since the Prophet (ع) did not call to doubt and confusion, his restricting the days of the month to the fullest and most complete number, without deficiency and without the lesser count, while by the requirement of the sight of the eye the days of a month may be deficient and doubt may fall upon it, can only have been an indication pointing toward the vision of the soul, whose numbers neither increase nor decrease, and an urging to take hold of what Allah Almighty called for in the completing of the count, in His saying:

> ولتكملوا العدة

"And that you may complete the count."

Then we say: from where comes the assurance of a people whose fast, when cloud and impediment occur, rests upon that rule, that the day they counted as belonging to Ramadan or to Sha'ban, under a clouded sky, is indeed as they counted and reckoned it, when that very same day, for another people of their own school in taking the sight of the eye, is otherwise, because the impediments of cloud and the like have been lifted for them? So, for example, there is cloud in Kirman, and their fast differs from the fast of Shiraz, where, say, there is no cloud. And if it were permissible for the time of the fast to differ among the people of a single region, so that fasting becomes obligatory upon one people on a given day and upon another people on the morrow of that day, then it would be permissible for the time of prayer also to differ among the people of a single town, so that one and the same prayer becomes binding upon one man at one time and that same prayer becomes binding upon another man at another time. Since that is not permissible, and since, when the time of a prayer has come, the community is bound to establish it, whether a man establishes it or abandons it, so likewise, when the time of the fast has come, the fast is binding upon the community, whether a man fasts it or breaks it.

This being so, the saying of the Prophet (ع) is an indication given by him, when he knew that error may fall in the sighting of the eye, pointing to that in which error does not fall, namely the vision of the soul, which is knowledge, and its count, which belongs among the matters he commended, that is, the completing of the count. Further, his saying, "If it is obscured from you, then count thirty," requires, for entering the fast, that the counting be from the days of Sha'ban, and, for leaving it by breaking the fast, that the counting be from the days of Ramadan. And it is known that counting is not possible except by having something to count and knowing its first day. It is also known that it happens in most cases that the crescent is not seen at the beginnings of the months, on account of impediments that prevent the sight from seeing it, especially in places whose air is turbid, such as Tabaristan, the mountain regions, the coasts, and the islands of the sea. And if it is possible that the crescent is not seen by the eye at the beginning of Sha'ban, and is not seen because of some obstacle that occurs, and its first day is not known, then how is one to count from it? And how is one to count from Ramadan when its first day has not been verified? To carry this report upon the meaning of the sighting of the eye requires that the Prophet (ع) cast his community into disagreement and called them to what lies beyond their capacity, namely the counting of the days of a month whose beginning is not known and in which the crescent is not seen. Yet Allah Almighty says:

> لا يكلف الله نفساً إلا وسعها

"Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity."

And since such a thing cannot be imagined of the Prophet (ع), who is far removed and sanctified above burdening the servants of Allah with what they cannot do or casting them into disagreement, it must follow either that the report "If it is obscured from you, then count thirty days" is not sound, or that, if the Prophet (ع) did say it, it is to be taken in the sense that is sound and that cuts away the ground of what is objectionable, and in no other sense. And granting that this is necessary, and that the testimonies have stood forth to show that what is sound, and what creation is commended to, is the vision of the soul, which is knowledge, we say: the discussion has now made plain that what is intended is other than the sighting of the eye, and that the common people, who are scant in knowledge, are bound to follow, and the objection raised in this matter has been invalidated.

Then we say: which thing of the Law that Muhammad (ع) brought, among those that are the pillars of Islam, has had its affair entrusted to the community, which is not preserved from error and falls under slip and mistake? Prayer? Fasting? Zakat? Hajj? Jihad? That fasting alone should forever be committed to them in the seeking of the crescent and the knowing of its times? Is not prayer the pillar of religion and the face of Islam, and are not all its acts, the opening takbir, the recitation, the bowing, the prostration, and the salutation, committed to the Imam who stands in the place of the Messenger (ع) in leading the people in prayer, and not to those who follow him? Upon those who follow lies the following of the Imam in these acts, without their own preference being followed in advancing any one of its acts or in delaying any of its limits; and whoever among them does this, advancing or delaying, his prayer is deficient (خداج). Is not the collecting of zakat committed to the Imam who stands in the place of the Messenger (ع), so that to leave it aside or to turn it away from him is not permissible, and the distributing of what comes of it belongs to him among those who respond to him, and not to the community, as it was in the days of the Prophet (ع)? Whoever gives his zakat to one other than him who stands in the place of the Messenger (ع) must render it a second time, just as one who, in the days of the Prophet (ع), gave his zakat to another than him would have been bound to render it to him a second time, because he placed it where it did not belong. Is not the Hajj incomplete except with the Imam who goes before the assembly, so that they are bound to follow him in the rites he performs, and whoever sets himself apart by advancing one of the rites before another or delaying it, his Hajj is deficient? Is not the ordering of jihad in the way of Allah committed to the one entrusted with the affair of the Muslims who fight, in preserving their order and all their condition, in leading them forward in battle and in withdrawal, and in seeking what is best in truce and in fighting? Upon the community that fights lies the following of the one who commands among them by the command of Allah, and not the following of their own desires in peace and in war; and whoever does anything of that fighting without his command, and sheds blood without his permission, is held answerable for it.

This being so, and the foundations of Islam being committed to one who preserves them, the community being obliged to follow him in them, from where has it become permissible for these people to follow their own opinions in the entering of the fast, which is one of those foundations, without following in it the one who stands in the place of the Messenger (ع)? And is this severance of theirs, and their entering the fast by their own ijtihad, which brings them to make obligatory what is not an obligation and to abandon an obligation or to count it a Sunnah, anything but presumption against Allah, or a going out from the safeguarding of the command? We seek refuge in Allah from that.

If he says: "Upon the principle you have laid down for the entering of the fast, namely conjunction and separation, the day in which conjunction and separation fall must be partly of Sha'ban and partly of Ramadan, and the fasting of that part becomes binding; and this is a contradiction, which the Prophet (ع) did not command," we say: the Prophet (ع) imposed the obligations on behalf of Allah, and appointed for each obligation among them two causes by which it becomes due and binding. The first is a preceding cause, by which the second cause is made valid, the second being the cause by which one obligation is distinguished from another. The first is general, in the sense that it is a cause for this obligation and for others besides it, so that in performing the obligation one does not take hold of it alone but of the second; and the second follows upon the general one and is specific, in the sense that it belongs to that obligation alone and by it the obligation is set apart from all else.

This is like the first prayer, which Allah imposed in the day and not in the night. We say that since this prayer is not obligatory except by day, the day is its preceding general cause, in the sense that it is a cause for this prayer and for the other prayers performed by day; and then, since this prayer is not obligatory with the occurrence of this preceding cause, which is the day, except when the sun declines from the zenith, we say that this is a second cause, by which the prayer becomes obligatory and by which one takes hold in performing it, and it is specific. It is like the zakat that He imposed: we say that since zakat is not obligatory except upon wealth, wealth is the cause of the obligation of zakat, and it is a preceding general cause; and then, since zakat is not obligatory with the occurrence of the preceding cause, which is wealth, except when a year has passed over it, the year is a second cause by which it becomes due and by which one takes hold in performing it, and it is specific. It is like the Hajj that He imposed: we say that since the Hajj is not obligatory except with ability, ability is its cause, and it is preceding and general; and then, since it does not become obligatory that a man perform the Hajj with the occurrence of ability, which is the preceding cause, except on the greatest day at Mecca, we say that this is a second cause by which one takes hold in fulfilling it, and it is specific. It is like the jihad that Allah Almighty imposed upon the community: we say that jihad is not obligatory except with the occurrence of opposition and the raising of the standard of disobedience and the refusal of obedience, and opposition is the preceding general cause; and then, since jihad is not obligatory with the existence of opposition and the refusal of obedience except through the standing of the one who holds the place of the Messenger (ع) and a head who leads the community into battle, we say that this is a second cause, and it is specific.

Since that is so, we say that fasting too has two causes. The first is preceding and general, and it is the separation of the moon from the sun; the second is specific, and it is the time of dawn at which entering into the fast becomes obligatory. And just as prayer is not obligatory, though it be day, except at the declining of the sun, and that is its time; nor zakat, though there be wealth, except when a year has passed over it, and that is its time; nor Hajj, though there be ability, except at Mecca on the greatest day, and that is its time; nor jihad, though there be opposition, except by the ordering head who stands in the place of the Lord of the worlds, and he is its support; so likewise fasting is not obligatory, though the moon has separated from the sun, except at the dawn that follows upon that separation, and that is its time. Thus his saying in objection, that part of the day is of Sha'ban and part of it is of Ramadan and that the fasting of that part is binding, is invalidated.

What adds to the certainty of this, along with the proofs already standing upon it from the apparent, is the greatest testimony, which speaks the truthfulness of the Messenger of Allah (ع) in what he brought from Allah Almighty: the balance between what he laid down of the Law and the religious ordinances and what Allah Almighty created and formed in the horizons and in the souls, as stated in the saying of Allah:

> سنريهم آياتنا في الآفاق وفي أنفسهم حتى يتبين لهم أنه الحق

"We shall show them Our signs in the horizons and in their own souls, until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth."

No human being has the power to imitate him in such a work, save one whose lineage is in the holy precinct (حظيرة القدس) and whose soul has been given readiness and endowment for the receiving of the revelation of Allah Almighty, when one returns to the interpretation, which is one of the two divisions to which Allah commanded His Prophet (ع) to call His servants in His worship, in His saying:

> ادع إلى سبيل ربك بالحكمة

"Call to the way of your Lord with wisdom."

This wisdom divides into the work, which is the outward natural worship proper to the natural bodies by way of performing the obligations and the practices that the Prophet (ع) brought, and which requires the interpretation that is knowledge; and into the knowledge, which is the greatest worship, the hidden worship of the soul, proper to particular souls, taken by way of holding the truths concerning the manner of purifying the affirmation of the oneness of Allah, and confirming the outward natural worship, which is the work.

For the sun, in the interpretive call, has been made, in that it is the noblest of bodies and the most luminous of them, the source of light for others, the cause of the growth of the offspring in the world of nature, and a cause to which all the causes of the world are attached, an indication pointing to the knowledge of the rank of the Prophet (ع). As the sun is the noblest of bodies and the most luminous of them, so the soul of the Speaker is the noblest of souls and the most knowing of them. As the sun is the source of light for the other stars, so the soul of the Speaker (ع) is the source for the souls of men in the divine sciences that lead to the pure affirmation of oneness. As the sun is the cause of the growth of the offspring in the world of nature, so the Prophet (ع) is the cause of the growth of the spiritual offspring in the world of the soul. And as the sun is a cause to which the causes of the bodily world are attached, so the Prophet (ع) is a cause to which the causes of the world of religion are attached, which is the worship of Allah Almighty. This is in the body, and that is in the soul.

As for the moon, in that its rank is below the sun, and that it receives of the light of the sun more than the other stars receive, and that it gives light in the darkness of the night, and that it stands as an intermediary in the growth of the offspring between the earth and the sun by receiving and by bestowing, it has been made an indication pointing to the rank of the Foundation (الأساس). As the rank of the moon in light is below the sun, so the rank of the Foundation in receiving the radiance of the world of origination is below the Prophet (ع).

I have said that the moon receives more of the sun's light than any other of the wandering bodies receives; and just so, the Foundation (الأساس) receives from the Speaker (الناطق) more of the blessings of the affirmation of Allah's oneness that reach him than any other recipient receives. And just as the moon gives light in the darkness of the night, so the Foundation makes plain what the Prophet (ع) brought of the sealed symbols, the law set out in its breadth, and the parables that were struck. And just as the moon is the intermediary between the sun and the other bodies in the growth of the offspring, so the Foundation is the intermediary between the Speaker and the community in the growth of the offspring in the world of the soul, by conferring benefit and by receiving it.

Now that the states of the bodily sun and moon in the world of bodies have been set beside the states of the spiritual sun and moon in the world of religion, I say that the moon has several states.

Among them is that when it comes into conjunction with the sun, the upper half of its body, the half that faces the sun, is illumined. This is what the astronomers mean when they say that a power extends to it, by which everything that is to come into being of events and existing things is brought into being until the second conjunction, by a measure ordained from the Mighty, the All-Knowing. It is like a minister who comes into the presence of the king: the king charges him concerning the affairs of the kingdom with what he is to carry out once he has left the royal presence. So it is with the Prophet (ع) teaching the Foundation (ع) what he wished to teach him, that he might stand in his place in guidance and instruction, and with the Foundation's receiving from him in his days, without his conferring anything before that designation. It is for this reason that the Prophet (ع) pointed to Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) and said:

> "أنا مدينة العلم وعلي بابها فمن أراد العلم فليأت الباب"

"I am the city of knowledge and Ali is its gate. Whoever desires knowledge, let him come to the gate."

Among them is its hiddenness after the parting, when it lies beneath the light of the sun, so that it is concealed from the sight of the generality of people and seen only by those possessed of knowledge. This is like the hiddenness of the affair of the Foundation, in what is settled for him of his rank and of the obligation of obedience to him by means of the indications made toward him: hidden from the people of ignorance and from the common people, and disclosed only to those of merit and special standing.

Among them is its appearance as a crescent when the sun has reached its setting place, so that the great and the small alike behold it. This is like the manifestation of the affair of the Foundation through what came from the Speaker (ع) in the explicit text concerning him and the imposing of obedience to him, when the time of his own departure from the world drew near, so that the small and the great came to know it.

Among them is that the moon has no light during the day while the disc of the sun stands above the earth, and has light only after the sun has set. This is like the Foundation, who has no affair during the lifetime of the Speaker (ع), and only after his departure from the world.

Among them is that at the beginning of its appearance it is faint, seen only by one whose sight is strong, and people fall into disagreement over the sighting of it. This is like the weakness of the affair of the Foundation at the beginning of its season, and the arising of disagreement concerning his Imamate, and the shrinking back of the community from binding themselves to obedience to him, except for one who possessed sincerity and knowledge.

Among them is the increase of its light with the passing of the days. So too does the affair of his summons rise, and his Imamate spread among the people, with the passing of the years.

Among them is the completion of its light when fourteen days of it have passed, so that the small and the great see it and no one doubts the sighting of it. This is like the arriving of the light of his Imamate to all the nations at the passing of the seven repetitions (المثاني السبعة) at the end of the cycle, so that at that time no one doubts his Imamate and his precedence.

Then there is the fact that the people of knowledge and faith assign the day that falls within the concealment after the parting to the coming month, just as one of sound faith held himself bound, by what he knew of the indications of the Prophet (ع) given to a few in particular, to obedience to the Commander of the Faithful before the explicit text was given outwardly for all. The people of ignorance and the common people, by contrast, assign that day, whose fast is obligatory, to the month now past, whose fast is not obligatory, through the scantness of their knowledge, and so they fail in fasting it until they see the crescent with the eye. In the same way they assign to the Foundation, obedience to whom is obligatory, the same place as any other to whom obedience is not made obligatory, neither by indication nor by explicit text, and they fail in obedience to him out of ignorance of its being binding upon them, until the truth of his Imamate becomes plain to them.

The month of Ramadan is, among the months of the year, the noblest of them and the greatest in weight; and it stands for the Foundation, who is placed ahead of the rest of the companions, whose rank was ennobled by exposition and interpretation, which is knowledge, and who was pointed to by the indications, and who is the gathering place of the knowledge of all that the Prophet (ع) brought from Allah the Exalted. It is for this reason that Allah the Exalted said:

> "شهر رمضان الذي أنزل فيه القرآن هدىً للناس والبينات من الهدى والفرقان"

"The month of Ramadan, in which the Quran was sent down as guidance for the people, and as clear proofs of guidance and of the criterion."

This was so that it might serve as an indication of him. For we know that the Quran came down over twenty-three years, not all at once in the month of Ramadan, but rather day by day, month by month, and year by year; and to carry the verse upon the outward sense of the words leads to contradiction. For the like of this, interpretation is needed, so that the word of Allah the Exalted and the word of the Messenger (ع) may both stand, together with the binding character of the fast and its being made obligatory upon the sighting of its crescent: from the side of the soul, for the people of knowledge, first, at the moment of their sighting; and from the side of the senses, for the people of ignorance, last. So too with the obligation of obedience to the Foundation (ع) when the Prophet (ع) raised him to this station and pointed to him by the indications, to those possessed of knowledge and discernment first, and by the explicit text and formal declaration, to the common people last. So too with the obligation upon whoever is present to hold back from opening discourse and from teaching, even while the Foundation stands ahead of the assembly. It is for this that Allah the Exalted said:

> "لا ترفعوا أصواتكم فوق صوت النبي"

"Do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet."

This forbids the community to overstep in a way that drives out the ruling of Allah the Exalted and narrows it rather than widening it, by going ahead of the Messenger in his time, and of the Foundation and of the one who stands in their place in their own time, so that the Imam may be distinguished from the one who follows him, and the head from those under him. Then He said:

> "من شهد منكم الشهر فليصمه"

"Whoever among you witnesses the month, let him fast it."

This is an indication from the Speaker to the community that follows him, that they are to follow the Foundation and to listen in silence to his speech. The meaning is that whoever attends the assembly of the Foundation in the knowledge of the outward and the inward, let him keep silent, for it is he who is granted the honor, who is placed ahead, and to whom the affair of religion is entrusted, and no one else. This is evidence that before obedience to him was made obligatory the people used to discourse with one another in the matter of religion and knowledge however they wished, and were then forbidden from it, just as before the fast and its obligation they used to eat however they liked, and were then forbidden from it by the imposing of the fast. It is like the command of the Prophet (ع) in the matter of marriage, when he said:

> "لا نكاح إلا بولي وشاهدين"

"There is no marriage except with a guardian and two witnesses."

This indicates that marriage had been contracted without a guardian and without two witnesses, which is temporary marriage (المتعة), however they liked, in the first days of Islam, and that they were then forbidden from it by this.

Then His word:

> "ومن كان مريضاً أو على سفر فعدة من أيام أُخْر"

"And whoever is sick or on a journey, then a number of other days."

This is an exception from the whole of those who witness the month. The meaning is that silence has been made obligatory upon all people when they attend the assembly of interpretation ascribed to the Foundation, except for one whose speech is for the sake of receiving benefit, a man in doubt or a learner; and then silence becomes binding upon him afterward according to the rule. For attendance at that assembly is the prayer of the soul and of knowledge, and it is required that there be employed in it what is required in the prayer of works: the etiquettes, the customary practices, and the upholding of the rulings. I have also set down in the comprehensive treatise a portion concerning the fast, with explanation and clarification, to the measure of the benefits that have reached me from the side of the guardian of Allah (ع).

As for the month of Ramadan of the year four hundred, and the agreement of the people of truth to enter its fast two days before the sighting of the crescent with the eye, when it is well known that no more than a single day of difference used to pass between the fast of the people of truth and the fast of the people of disagreement, the matter, upon my life, is confusing to all but the people of understanding. The reason is this: the conjunction that occurred in the month of Ramadan fell on the night of Monday, so Monday was its first day. The crescent was not seen on Tuesday night by the sighting of the eye, because of the overpowering of the light over its body; for at the time of the sun's setting it stood at six degrees and some minutes from it, and at such a distance, which is the measure of half its body, the sight cannot reach it, since it falls beneath the ray that prevents the sight from doing its work. So it was not permissible, out of propriety, to break the fast on that day, since the moon stood from the sun at the end of the month at the like of those degrees. Had one broken the fast, then what could have been fasted of the month of Ramadan would have been twenty-nine days, because the conjunction of the sun and the moon for Shawwal fell on the night of Wednesday, at two hours and some minutes of it, so that Wednesday was the day of the festival, on which fasting is forbidden. Thus a failure in one of the obligatory duties would have occurred, since the month of Ramadan is thirty days without deficiency, by the word of our masters (عليهم السلام):

> "ما تم شعبان ولا نقص رمضان"

"Sha'ban was never complete, and Ramadan was never deficient."

The strongest proof of the soundness of our entering the fast from Monday is the word of the Prophet (ع):

> "يوم صومكم يوم نحركم"

"The day of your fasting is the day of your sacrifice."

The sacrifice in the Sacred Precinct fell on Monday, the tenth of Dhu al-Hijjah of the year four hundred, according to what the sighting by knowledge requires, which is the ninth of it according to what the sighting by the eye requires. And the matter holds in this way every year, as he said (ع), without any disagreement arising.

If someone should say: what you have brought forward about the sun and the moon, the degrees, the distance, and the hours, and have made your argument and your proof, belongs to the science of the stars, and the Prophet (ع) forbade taking it up and looking into it; I say: how is it that inference concerning the establishing of the time of the fast and the knowledge of it by way of the sun and the moon, which are the cause of the months and the years, and which cannot be had except through their motion and their course and their conjunction and their separation, is forbidden to us, while inference concerning the establishing of the direction of prayer and the times of prayer and the knowledge of them, which is like the fast in being among the pillars of Islam, by way of the mansions of the moon and the elevation of the sun with the measures we have set up for you, is commanded? Is this anything other than an unjust division (قسمة ضيزى)?

The Prophet (ع) forbade that only in a particular respect. It is that whoever begins in the seeking of the sciences by learning the science of the stars, without the divine and revealed sciences preceding it for him, is led by that to what the followers of Ptolemy hold, and those who ascribe divinity to the heavenly bodies. But when the religious and revealed sciences have preceded it, then knowledge of the manner of the spheres and the stars, and of that for which they were composed, increases the soul in certainty concerning the oneness of Allah the Exalted, and gains for its conception of His unity a firmer faith. So too Allah the Exalted said:

> "لخلق السموات والأرض أكبر من خلق الناس ولكن أكثر الناس لا يعلمون"

"The creation of the heavens and the earth is greater than the creation of mankind, but most people do not know."

And He said:

> "سنريهم آياتنا في الآفاق وفي أنفسهم حتى يتبين لهم أنه الحق"

"We shall show them Our signs on the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth."

And the horizons are nothing other than the heavens and what is in them. Moreover, were the matter as that objector said, Allah the Exalted would not have sworn by the stars, nor would He have described them as indicating His unity in His Book, where He says:

> "تبارك الذي جعل في السماء بروجا وجعل فيها سراجا وقمراً منيراً"

"Blessed is He who placed constellations in the sky and placed in it a lamp and an illuminating moon."

Nor would He have sworn, saying:

> "فلا أقسم بمواقع النجوم وأنه قسم لو تعلمون عظيم"

"So I swear by the settings of the stars, and it is indeed a great oath, if you only knew."

And He said:

> "فلا أقسم بالخنس الجوار الكنس"

"So I swear by the receding stars, that run their course and hide."

And He said:

> "فالمدبرات أمرا"

"And those that arrange the affair."

And He said:

> "والسماء ذات البروج"

"By the sky containing the constellations."

And He said:

> "والشمس وضحاها والقمر إذا تلاها"

"By the sun and its morning brightness, and by the moon when it follows it."

And He said:

> "هو الذي جعل الشمس ضياءً والقمر نورا وقدره منازل لتعلموا عدد السنين والحساب"

"It is He who made the sun a radiance and the moon a light, and determined for it stages, that you might know the number of the years and the reckoning."

What merit, then, is nobler than knowledge of the luminous stars in their circles, and of their places in their heights and their depths, which speak for the soundness of what Allah the Exalted has founded of His religion, and which bear witness to the truth of the word of the Prophet (ع) in what he brought from Allah? And what knowledge is nobler, after the affirmation of oneness and the ranks of the ḥudūd (الحدود), than knowledge of what comes to be through the passages of the stars through their constellations, southward and northward, and through the meetings of the lights of some of them with others, in the world of generation and corruption, in the appearing of the offspring and of other events that indicate the ranks of the friends of Allah?

> "فهل يستوي الظلمات والنور أم هل يستوي الأعمى والبصير أم هل يستوي الذين يعلمون والذين لا يعلمون إنما يتذكر أولوا الألباب"

Are darkness and light equal? Or are the blind and the seeing equal? Or are those who know and those who do not know equal? Only those of understanding take heed.

It is therefore established, by the testimonies I have brought forward, outward and inward alike, that the sighting is of two kinds; that to take the more excellent of the two in the performance of the obligatory duties is the better course; and that the intention of the Prophet (ع) in his use of the letter ل (lām) in his words لرؤيته is that it indicates the sighting of knowledge, which requires that the people be already fasting when the crescent is sighted with the eye, and not otherwise.

Know, then, and may Allah help you to obey Him and to obey His friends, the meanings of what I have set forth. Seek help from Allah in all your affairs, and draw near to Him by sincerity of intention and of inward purpose in worshipping Him and in obeying His friends; for He is the best helper toward what one seeks to know of the realities. And give thanks to Allah the Exalted for the blessings that reach you from the side of His trustees.

Here I bring the treatise to its close, and I close it as it began, with praise belonging to Allah and with prayers upon those through whom His guidance reaches us. What I have set down was never mine to originate; it was only an ordering of what has been received, and the fitting seal upon such a work is thanks, not claim.

> وَبَعْدُ فَقَدْ خَتَمْتُ الرِّسَالَةَ بِالْحَمْدِ لِلَّهِ الْعَلِيِّ الْأَعْلَى وَبِالصَّلَاةِ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ مُحَمَّدٍ الْمُصْطَفَى وَالْوَلِيِّ عَلِيٍّ الْمُرْتَضَى وَالْأَئِمَّةِ الطَّاهِرِينَ أَقْمَارِ الدُّجَى

And now: I have sealed this treatise with praise of Allah, the High, the Most High, and with prayer upon the Prophet Muhammad the Chosen, and upon the guardian Ali the Well-Pleasing, and upon the pure Imams, the moons amid the darkness.

The likeness is not an ornament. Just as the moon in the night sky carries a light it has received and gives it to those who travel while others sleep, so the pure Imams stand in the darkness of the age, luminous by what has been entrusted to them and not by what they claim for themselves. And the same order continues unbroken in the Imam of our own time.

> وَإِمَامِ الزَّمَانِ مَوْلَانَا وَهَادِينَا وَوَلِيِّ نِعَمِنَا الْإِمَامِ الْحَاكِمِ بِأَمْرِ اللَّهِ أَمِيرِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ صَلَوَاتُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ وَعَلَى آبَائِهِ الطَّاهِرِينَ وَأَبْنَائِهِ الْأَكْرَمِينَ الْمُنْتَظَرِينَ إِلَى يَوْمِ الدِّينِ أَفْضَلُ التَّحِيَّاتِ وَالتَّسْلِيمِ

And upon the Imam of the age, our master, our guide, and the guardian of our blessings, the Imam who rules by the command of Allah, the Commander of the Faithful. May the prayers of Allah be upon him, and upon his pure forefathers and upon his noble sons who are awaited until the Day of Judgment, the finest of greetings and salutations.

This is the order I have been describing throughout: knowledge descends, obedience ascends, and the two meet in the one whom Allah has appointed. Whoever holds to that order is not left to grope in the dark, for the light he walks by is not his own invention. And so the only request that remains to be made is the request for steadfastness and for illumination, since neither is within our power to produce.

> وَنَسْأَلُ اللَّهَ أَنْ يُثَبِّتَنَا عَلَى طَاعَتِهِ وَطَاعَةِ أَوْلِيَائِهِ وَيُنَوِّرَ عُقُولَنَا وَبَصَائِرَنَا بِمَنِّهِ وَكَرَمِهِ وَقُدْرَتِهِ إِنَّهُ خَيْرُ مَسْئُولٍ وَأَكْرَمُ مَأْمُولٍ وَحَسْبُنَا اللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ الْوَكِيلُ وَنِعْمَ الْمَوْلَى وَنِعْمَ النَّصِيرُ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَسَلَّمَ

We ask Allah to make us firm in obeying Him and in obeying His friends, and to give light to our minds and to our inner sight, by His favor, His generosity, and His power. He is the best of those who are asked and the most generous of those in whom hope is placed. Allah is our sufficiency and the best of guardians, the best of masters and the best of helpers. May Allah bless our master Muhammad and his family and grant them peace.
