# Gold, Herds, and Harvest

I turn now to the zakāt of gold, silver, and jewels.

### Mentioning Zakāt on Gold, Silver, and Jewels

He said in the book of questions and answers, arranged according to the order of the chapters of the book of the Pillars of Islam:

Question: What do you say about a man who died and appointed two guardians, and one of them, or another of those close to him, feared that the second would obstruct the payment of what was due from the estate of the deceased in the way of khums, zakāt, Ḥajj, and the rest, so he paid it secretly and did not inform him, out of fear of him? Is anything held against him in that? And what is it that must be paid, given that the deceased fixed nothing and left the matter unspecified, and the state of the kinsfolk is as it is, and it was known of him that he had withheld zakāt, had not paid the khums of all his wealth, and had not performed the Ḥajj?

Answer: If the guardian discharges what lies upon the deceased, there is nothing wrong in it, because zakāt is a debt from which release must be obtained first, then the Ḥajj, then the khums. If the next of kin do not obtain that release, nothing falls upon anyone else, so long as they clear his neck, whether in secret or openly.

Question: What do you say about a man who does not bring out the zakāt of his wealth and does not deliver it to the one commanded to receive it? Is it permissible to eat food in his house?

Answer: There is nothing wrong with that.

Question: What do you say about a man who possessed wealth, did not pay its khums, and then died? Must the khums be taken out of his whole estate, including the houses, the servants, and the undivided holdings, or not?

Answer: Yes, the khums is taken out of his entire estate.

Question: What do you say about a man who owned the niṣāb and did not pay the zakāt, or did not own it until he died, and then what he left of goods and houses was reckoned and it was seen that the estate had reached the limit of the niṣāb? Must zakāt be taken out of it or not? And likewise the Ḥajj and the rest of what was due upon his wealth?

Answer: If what he left behind reaches the niṣāb from what was held for selling, buying, and commerce, then bringing out its zakāt is obligatory, and the Ḥajj is discharged after the zakāt.

Question: What do you say about a man who died, and his whole estate was examined and found sufficient for the Ḥajj and the other obligations, such as expiation, al-Shurwa, and khums, though in his lifetime the deceased had owned little beyond the house and its furnishings? Are the Ḥajj and the other obligations to be discharged while the heirs take what remains?

Answer: If he leaves wealth, the Ḥajj takes precedence over the inheritance, because it is a debt, and debt comes before inheritance. The rest of the obligatory duties are likewise binding if there is in the wealth what suffices for them and something remains for the heir.

Question: The collector of the customs levy takes ten dirhams from the polytheists for every two hundred dirhams, and takes five dirhams from the Muslims for every two hundred dirhams. Now the collector remitted a dirham or two dirhams for some of the Muslims, and the owner of cloth for whom the collector remitted nothing comes to the man for whom he did remit, so that the goods may be registered in that man's name, on the condition that what remains is divided equally between the two of them. Is it permissible to take that or not?

Answer: Whatever the partners have agreed upon and know of is permissible.

Question: A man died and left two hundred dinars, or less, or more, and zakāt, Ḥajj, khums, al-Shurwa, and expiation were all due from him. With which of these claims does one begin, out of the capital?

Answer: He begins with the zakāt, for it is a debt upon him and is not part of his wealth; then the Ḥajj, then the khums, then the expiation, then al-Shurwa.

Question: A man died and left wealth and an heir, and made no bequest at all. Are the khums, al-Shurwa, and the expiation taken out of the capital or not?

Answer: The khums must be brought out of the capital. If the heirs are God-fearing, it is their duty to discharge it from the responsibility of the one who has passed and so to purify the wealth. If they refuse that, then:

> لا إكراه في الدين

There is no compulsion in religion.

From another source: whoever pays the zakāt of two years to the one authorized to receive it before the second year falls due, it is discharged from him.

Question: Concerning a man who has two hundred mithqāls and brings out the zakāt at the head of the ḥawl, and then leaves one hundred in his house, neither selling nor buying with it, while he trades with the remaining hundred, and then the second ḥawl comes round upon him: does he pay zakāt on the two hundred or on the one hundred? And likewise, if he buys a commodity and the ḥawl passes over it while he has not sold it, and a loss has fallen upon its price, is there zakāt upon him in it or not?

Answer: Zakāt remains obligatory in that upon which it fell due for as long as it remains with its owner, whether he trades with it or hoards it, until what he owns falls below the niṣāb.

Question: It has come in al-Masāʾil that al-Shurwa is one hundred and nineteen dirhams, and that upon the people of means there is a dinar. O our master, what is the limit of means, and what is the weight of a dinar, and what is the weight of a dirham?

Answer: Our master said: the limit of means for all is the ownership of wealth in which zakāt falls due after the ḥawl, and that is either two hundred dirhams or twenty gold dinars. Each dirham is fourteen carats of silver and two fifths of a carat, and by this reckoning the weight of two hundred dirhams is eighteen ounces by the weights of the Arabs, which is ninety-five kadiyāna and a quarter kadiyāna, and a wāl and a quarter wāl of silver by the weights of Gujarat. One then looks at what this silver is worth in the coinage current at the time. Whoever owns this amount or more than it is of the people of means. As for the reckoning of gold, each dinar is twenty-four carats, and by this reckoning the weight of twenty dinars is three ounces of gold by the weights of the Arabs, which is sixteen kadiyāna less a wāl and half a wāl of gold. Yet in this there is a matter to consider, because the price of gold and silver differs in every age according to people's desire for the one and their lack of desire for the other, and the reverse, so that at times the price of twenty dinars is more than two hundred dirhams, and at other times the opposite. It is therefore fitting for the believer who holds to his religion to reckon by whichever of the two coinages named, gold and silver, is the lesser in price.

Question: What do you say about a man who has five hundred tankas and brought out its fifth, one hundred tankas, in the first year, so that four hundred tankas remained to him? He traded with them and gained three hundred tankas in profit, and of that the household expenses took two hundred tankas, leaving him one hundred of the profit. Does he bring the khums out of three hundred tankas or out of one hundred?

Answer: If he has already brought out the khums from the capital, then there is no khums in the gain except upon what remains in his hand. If he brings out the khums on the whole, that is a merit and not a binding obligation.

This is the answer of our master Idrīs.

He said in Mukhtaṣar al-Muṣannaf: zakāt is obligatory when what is liable to it reaches its niṣāb unencumbered and the ḥawl has passed over it. He said in it: as for a deposit, if the one holding it alters it and introduces into it what takes it out of the ruling of a deposit, or consumes it and puts in its place wealth of his own, then zakāt in all of that is obligatory upon him. And if the ḥawl passes over the estate of a dead man after he has died and his heirs have not divided it, zakāt is not obligatory in that upon the whole of the wealth, but is obligatory upon each of the shares. Whoever's share reaches a niṣāb must pay zakāt on it, and whoever's share does not reach a niṣāb, and who has no other wealth besides it, owes no zakāt on it. If a man has wealth and also owes wealth, and the ḥawl passes over it, one looks at what he owes and what he has; if his wealth exceeds what he owes by a niṣāb or more, the zakāt of the excess over his debt is binding upon him. If a man sells trade goods of his for the goods of another which he also buys for trade, the second goods are valued at the head of the ḥawl reckoned from the time he owned the first goods, and whether he suffered loss in that or did not, he pays what is due from him in zakāt.

He said in it: likewise, if he buys trade goods with coin of the kind in which zakāt is due, the reckoning follows how long the money and the goods stood in his hands; and when the ḥawl passes over it, he pays the zakāt of his wealth.

He said in al-Akhbār: they narrated that whoever sets aside wealth that is surplus to him and then finds it again pays zakāt on it. And from it: the apostate begins his zakāt anew and does not pay zakāt for what was his during his apostasy. He said: there is no zakāt in the wealth of an orphan, nor in the wealth of one who lacks his reason.

Taʾwīl al-Daʿāʾim said: this means that there is no zakāt in that upon the one who owns it and keeps it for himself; but whatever of it is held for trade is valued at a price, and zakāt is due in it.

He said in Mukhtaṣar al-Muṣannaf: in what comes out of a mine there is the khums, and the remainder is for the one who found it; in buried treasure there is the khums; and dinars that are found bearing the coinage of Islam fall under the ruling of found property.

Some of the friends of God (عليهم السلام) said: al-Shurwa due from the rich man who has the means is one hundred and nineteen mithqāls of gold, and from the one who has not the means, one hundred and nineteen pieces of silver.

He said in the questions put by our master Sulaymān ibn Ḥasan in his early days, with the answers of our master Dāʾūd ibn ʿAjab: I also asked about one who has paid the khums of the lands of his estate, whether the khums is due upon their seed or not.

Answer: Yes, may God prolong you, the khums is obligatory upon him in the seed of the lands, as God Almighty said:

> ⟪ar:وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّمَا غَنِمْتُم مِّن شَيْءٍ فَأَنَّ لِلَّهِ خُمُسَهُ⟫

Know that whatever you take as spoil, a fifth of it belongs to God.

These seeds enter under the word "thing," as our master the Qāḍī al-Nuʿmān ibn Muḥammad mentioned and made clear and plain in Kitāb al-Himma.

He said in Taʾwīl al-Sharīʿa: Imam al-Muʿizz li-Dīn Allāh (ع) was asked about zakāt, and he said that it is five dirhams, and that the poor are of five classes, for each class a dirham; for zakāt is mutual consolation, since all people and the whole of creation are as a single soul, and when one limb of the body suffers pain, all the limbs turn to that limb to treat it until its pain is gone. When God tests one of the children of the human kind, it is the duty of the kind to occupy itself with his healing. And a man was mentioned in his presence, and it was said, "There is no defect in him." He (ع) said:

> لو كان أحدٌ لم يمسسه عيب لما أدركه الموت

If there were anyone whom no defect had touched, death would never have reached him.

Our master, the most exalted summoner, Jaʿfar son of our master Manṣūr al-Yaman, said at the beginning of the Book of the Inner Nursing: the five prayers are known at five times, and the zakāt likewise is five.

1. The khums of every wealth a man earns through trade, sales, and gain.
2. Then the zakāt of the wealth whose khums has already been brought out, which is one dinar from every forty dinars; it is an obligation due to the Ḥujja, and the khums is for the Imam.
3. The zakāt of crops, which is likewise its tenth.
4. The zakāt of livestock, which is from every forty head, one head.
5. The zakāt of the soul, which is what the believer pays for himself from his shurwa for the redemption of his neck, and from the fiṭra of his fast, after these four obligatory zakāts.

Our master Zayd ibn Muḥammad said in al-Risāla al-Maknūna: whoever owns twenty mithqāls of gold owes half a mithqāl. Then he said: and upon whoever owns two hundred dirhams there are five dirhams, and the two hundred are two covenants. He said in it: there is no zakāt upon one who does not own twenty mithqāls, nor upon one who does not own two hundred dirhams. He said in it: whoever owns two hundred dirhams is forbidden to take zakāt and charity. He said in it: the owner of gold and silver owes no zakāt of wealth so long as the ḥawl has not passed over it.

### Mentioning Zakāt on Livestock

He said in Mukhtaṣar al-Muṣannaf: there is no zakāt in livestock until they are free-grazing and the ḥawl passes over them. If they graze during part of the ḥawl and are fed with fodder during another part, the ruling follows whichever is the greater part.

He said in it: whoever has five camels and does not pay their zakāt for two ḥawls owes a sheep for the first and owes nothing for the second, because the sheep of the second stands upon him as a debt; and the case is the same in what is other than camels. If a man has camels whose due is camels, and he also has sheep, then it is taken from his sheep, from what would be fit to serve as a sacrificial animal, and likewise it is taken from the zakāt of sheep. Buffaloes and cows are alike in zakāt, and so are sheep and goats.

He said in Taʾwīl al-Daʿāʾim: this holds particularly where something of the charity has fallen due; and if nothing has fallen due in them, then nothing is upon him.

Our master Jaʿfar ibn Manṣūr al-Yaman said at the beginning of the Book of the Inner Nursing: the zakāt of livestock is from every forty head, one head from among them.

Our master Zayd ibn Muḥammad said in al-Risāla al-Maknūna: camels are slaughtered by the thrust to the throat and then by the cut. He said in it: there is no charity upon one who does not own five camels. He said in it: until the five are complete; and when he owns them, a sheep of the flock is obligatory upon him, a sheep for every five, until they reach twenty, when he pays four sheep. When they reach twenty-five, a bint makhāḍ is due in them. He said in it: the bint makhāḍ is the one whose mother has carried a young one in her belly. He said in it: then with an increase of ten, when they reach thirty-five, a bint labūn. He said in it: and when they reach forty-five, he pays a ḥiqqa fit to be covered by the stallion. He said in it: then with an increase of fifteen; and when one is added above sixty, a jadhaʿ is due in them. He said in it: above the jadhaʿ there is nothing that is taken in charity. He said in it: a ḥiqqa was appointed at sixty camels only because she resembles the stallion and is able to bear what he bears. He said in it: when the camels reach one hundred and twenty, two ḥiqqas are due in them, because one hundred and twenty is twice sixty.

He said in it: when the number of cattle reaches thirty, a tabīʿ is due in them. He said in it: when they reach forty, a musinna is due. He said in it: and in sixty, two tabīʿs. He said in it: and in ninety, three yearlings. He said in it: when they reach one hundred and twenty, then in every forty a musinna and in every thirty a tabīʿ.

He said in it, concerning sheep: when they reach forty, one sheep is due in them as charity. He said in it: up to one hundred and twenty, one sheep is due in them. He said in it: when they reach two hundred sheep, two sheep are due in them. He said in it: then after that, in every hundred a sheep. He said in it: nothing defective is taken in charity. He said in it: the ram of the flock is not taken in charity unless the collector wishes it.

### Mentioning Zakāt on Grains, Fruits, and Plants

He said in the book of questions and answers mentioned above:

Question: The niṣāb of gold is twenty dinars and of silver two hundred dirhams. How then is the niṣāb of grains reckoned?

Answer: It has come in the Pillars that zakāt is due from everything the earth brings forth, and upon this rests the practice of our masters, so it is a duty to follow them. They brought out the tenth from little and from much, and in that is merit. And it has come in the Sharīʿa that the niṣāb in grains is five awsāq, and the wasq is sixty ṣāʿ.

He said in al-Akhbār: what is sold as a standing crop, that is, grain, zakāt is due in it; and what is sold before it forms its kernel and is then harvested, no zakāt is due in it. The same holds for fruits. And they narrated that the owner of the land does not set off against it what he spent upon it.

He said in Mukhtaṣar al-Muṣannaf: this is obligatory upon the young and the old, the rich and the poor, and upon one who is in debt.

From al-Akhbār: they narrated that the land of the tenth is every land of the lands of the Arabs against which the Muslims did not charge with horse or riding beast, and every land of the mountain country which its own people brought into cultivation and which no water from the great rivers overflowed. This is the land of the tenth. And they narrated that the land of kharāj is the land of the Sudan and the mountain lands against which the Muslims charged with horse and riding beast and which they brought under cultivation by force. When the people of war submit while still upon their own territory, it is land of the tenth. They further narrated that if a Muslim buys land of kharāj from a protected non-Muslim and takes possession of it, its ruling is not changed by its passing to the Muslim, and it remains land of kharāj as it was; and if a protected non-Muslim buys land of the tenth from a Muslim and takes possession of it, the tenth is due upon him in what it brings forth, because that obligation attaches to what the land brings forth, and no regard is had to its owner.

He said in Maʿālim al-Dīn: zakāt is obligatory in all grains that run in the course of wheat and dates, and nothing of that reaches the measure if it does not reach five awsāq.

He said in al-Muntakhaba: the niṣāb of the grains we have mentioned is when they reach five awsāq, and the wasq is sixty ṣāʿ.

He said in al-Akhbār: if part of a crop is watered by one means and part of it by another, the ruling follows whichever is the greater; and if the two are equal, three quarters of the tenth are taken from him. He said in it: one half is not joined to another half among grains and fruits, and there is no zakāt in any of them until the half reaches the measure in which zakāt is obligatory.

He said in Mukhtaṣar al-Āthār: the tenth becomes obligatory in grains only when they form their kernels, and at that point an estimate is made, which is when their soundness appears; and a just estimate is made upon their owners.

He said in the Book of the Inner Nursing: as for the tenth, it is an outward ruling by which the Messenger of God judged, taken from the believers and from the people of the outward. He said in it: likewise, in the outward, whoever pays the tenth of his earnings in truth has rightly discharged his outward obligation, if he has kept back only nine tenths; he has done what he was told to do, even if the ruler over his outward affairs had made no demand of him. And whoever holds back something of his earnings and keeps with himself more than nine tenths has not rightly accomplished what he intended in paying the tenth that was told and commanded to him, and has not in truth rendered obedience to the ruler over his outward affairs.

Our master Zayd ibn Muḥammad said in al-Risāla al-Maknūna: these five, meaning dates, raisins, wheat, barley, and grain, when they reach five awsāq, then half a wasq of charity is due in them. He said in it: the tenth is obligatory in what the sky waters and what the rivers water; and what is watered by wheels and by water-carrying beasts, upon it is half the tenth. He said in it: God imposed the khums in the spoils. God Almighty said:

> ⟪ar:وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّمَا غَنِمْتُم مِّن شَيْءٍ فَأَنَّ لِلَّهِ خُمُسَهُ وَلِلرَّسُولِ وَلِذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْيَتَامَىٰ وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ⟫

Know that whatever you take as spoil, a fifth of it belongs to God, and to the Messenger, and to the near of kin, and the orphans, and the needy, and the wayfarer.

These are five stations to which the khums is directed. He said in it: the khums is taken from mines, from treasures, and from spoils.

And from Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad al-Ṣādiq (ع) it is transmitted that he said:

> كان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله إذا أتاه المغنم أخذ نصيبه ثم يقسم ما بقي بين القربى واليتامى والمساكين وابن السبيل

When spoils came to the Messenger of God, he would take his share and then divide what remained among the near of kin, the orphans, the needy, and the wayfarer.
