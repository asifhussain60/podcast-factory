**M22 — Hierarchy tree, the seven obligatory elements**

- **File:** `content/Islamic/isaf-al-talib/slide-decks/ch07-deck-prayer-virtue-and-sacred-times.txt:308`
- **Content (excerpt):**
  > Hierarchy tree. What the prayer itself is made of, as the source lists it.
  > Level 1: The obligatory elements of prayer, seven in number
  >   Level 2: Takbir / Recitation / Bowing / Prostration / Praise / Tashahhud / The sealing
  > Governing condition: it is not permissible to pray except within the prayer's time.
- **The audio sentence that replaces it:** `chapters/ch07-prayer-virtue-and-sacred-times.txt:115` — "The obligatory elements of prayer are listed as seven: takbir, recitation, bowing, prostration, praise, tashahhud, and the sealing. It is not permissible to pray except within the prayer's time."
- **What's wrong:** the moment is a flat seven-item enumeration wearing a hierarchy label. There is no second branch, no kind-of or part-of split among the seven, no ordering claim, no relation between any two nodes. It is `slide-deck-patterns.md` anti-pattern 2 verbatim ("Bullet-list-as-diagram — Calling a hierarchy tree what is actually a flat bulleted list"), and the framing itself prohibits it ("No bullet list masquerading as a diagram"). Nothing is lost if the host simply says the sentence.
- **Suggested Worker re-authoring (pick one):**
  1. **Give the tree a real second level.** The chapter supplies the material: split the seven into the elements that are spoken (takbir, recitation, praise, tashahhud, the sealing) against the elements that are postural (bowing, prostration), and put the governing time-condition as the root constraint that binds both branches. That produces an actual parent-child structure and connects the element list to the chapter's argument that time encloses the act.
  2. **Fold M22 into M21.** The house-walls annotated structure at deck:298 already carries the enclosure argument; the seven elements are what stands inside the walls. Merging gives one memorable moment instead of one memorable moment plus one list.
  3. **Delete M22.** Coverage does not depend on it — the seven elements would still reach the listener through the audio chapter, which is the deliverable that carries them well.
- **Verified | Inferred:** VERIFIED — the replacing audio sentence exists at a cited line and the deck adds no structural commitment beyond enumeration.

**M33 — Process flow, the chapter's last practical answer**

- **File:** `content/Islamic/isaf-al-talib/slide-decks/ch07-deck-prayer-virtue-and-sacred-times.txt:458`
- **Content (excerpt):**

  > Start: may women make up prayers for men, and may others make up prayers for a weak man who cannot fulfill what he missed ↓ Answer: no ↓ Reason: prayer is like fasting here, and no one makes it up on behalf of a man except the man himself ↓ Branch for the weak man: he prays to the extent of his ability, after bowing and acknowledging his incapacity ↓ End: sacred times, inherited forms, sequences, forbidden moments, make-up rules, exceptions for uncertainty, and symbolic correspondences all remain, and none removes the personal address
- **Three separate problems, each independently sufficient:**
  1. **It is a single sentence.** "No one may make up prayers on another's behalf; the weak man prays to the extent of his ability" carries the whole of the Start→Answer→Reason→Branch span.

Yet none of that removes the personal address." Under SL-A1 a summary is a memory-test failure, and the `Branch:` label names a branch that has only one arm, so the declared Process-flow type is not honoured either.

The chapter's closing argument is already carried twice and better — analytically by M20 (which sets the principle beside the bequest case and the transferability limit) and rhetorically by M35 at deck:484, whose Column B ("Stand / Remember / Repent / Perform — what no one else can do") is the same personal-address claim rendered as an actual contrast.
