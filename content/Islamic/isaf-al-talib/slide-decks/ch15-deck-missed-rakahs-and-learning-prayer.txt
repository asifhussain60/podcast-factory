# Missed Rak'ahs and Learning Prayer

## Where this episode picks up

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The previous chapter's question against this chapter's question.

Column A: The previous chapter
- What was assumed: the prayer is standing

- What it refused: letting an unsettled mind manufacture a defect

Column B: This chapter
- What is assumed: nothing

- What it refuses: treating discomfort, embarrassment, or lateness as grounds to tear the prayer down

## Prayer That Does Not Break

Diagram type: Process flow. Impurity reaching the garment of a man praying in purity.

Start: a man is praying in a state of purity, and impurity reaches his garment while he is praying.
  Branch on one fact about the room, is water nearby:
    Path A, water is nearby: he washes it, and the prayer goes on.
    Path B, water is not nearby: his prayer is invalidated, so he washes his garment, and then he prays.
  Down to, a second branch on place in the prayer:
    Path A, he encounters this before the tashahhud: he must repeat the prayer.
    Path B, he encounters it after the tashahhud has been spoken: his prayer is permissible and he does not repeat it.
End states, two only: the prayer continues, or the obligation survives the prayer and is paid in proper order.
Blocked transition: the arrival of impurity to the end of the prayer. The ruling never takes that step.
Attribution: the first question-and-answer book arranged according to the chapters of the Pillars of Islam.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. Two men, the same misfortune, two verdicts.

Column A: The man praying near water
- The defect: impurity on the garment, mid-prayer
- The garment: the same
- The prayer: the same
- What differs: a fact about the room he is standing in
- Verdict: wash it, continue, no repetition

Column B: The man praying far from water
- The defect: impurity on the garment, mid-prayer
- The garment: the same
- The prayer: the same
- What differs: the remedy is out of reach
- Verdict: the prayer is invalidated, he washes, then he prays

Shared row: the ruling is not measuring the gravity of the defect. It is measuring whether the defect can be undone from inside the prayer.

Reading: the obligation does not evaporate with the prayer that failed. Starting again is the ordinary sequence restored to its proper order, not a punishment.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The tashahhud as a boundary in time.

The boundary: the sentence of the tashahhud, spoken.

- At the boundary. Position: the crossing itself. Annotation: an address completed, turning toward the Prophet, then toward the worshippers, then toward the righteous servants of Allah, and closing.

Annotation on the whole: the source does not say after he has begun the tashahhud, and does not say in the final sitting. It quotes the sentence.

Attribution: the words the source names as the crossing, quoted in the ruling on impurity reaching the garment.

Diagram type: Process flow. The nosebleed and the vomiting.

Start: a man is afflicted with a nosebleed while praying, or with vomiting.
  Condition tested at every step: does his face turn away from the qibla.
  Path A, he washes the blood and stops it, or rinses away the vomit, without turning his face from the qibla: he does so and continues building on his prayer.
  Path B, he turns away from the qibla: he repeats the prayer from the beginning.
End states, two only: a structure still under construction, or a thread cut.
Attribution: the book of purity.

Reading: the hinge is not the blood. The hinge is the face. The same man, doing the same washing, breaks the prayer the moment his face leaves the direction.

Diagram type: Named-axis 2x2. The governing test of the first movement.

Axes:
- Horizontal: can the defect be undone from inside the act (No → Yes)
- Vertical: where the prayer stands when the defect arrives (Before the tashahhud → After the tashahhud)

Quadrants:
- Top-right, Yes and after the tashahhud: nothing to decide. The prayer is sealed and the remedy is available anyway.
- Top-left, No and after the tashahhud: the prayer is permissible and is not repeated, because the act's business is finished and trouble cannot reach back.
- Bottom-right, Yes and before the tashahhud: the remedy is applied and he continues building on his prayer. Water at hand, blood washed without turning.
- Bottom-left, No and before the tashahhud: the prayer falls and is repeated. No water near, or a face that left the qibla.

Reasoning for the axes: the horizontal is decided by reach, not by the gravity of what happened. The vertical is decided by position in the act, not by how the worshipper feels about it.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The threshold of evidence for passing wind.

Whole: the event of ritual impurity, as the reports establish it.
Parts admitted as evidence, with annotation:
- A smell perceived. Annotation: something actually registered.
- A sound heard by the worshipper. Annotation: the same, by the other sense.
- A sound heard from him by another person. Annotation: evidence admitted from outside the worshipper's own body.
Parts refused as evidence, with annotation:
- Suspicion. Annotation: not the event.
- Sensation without a smell or a sound. Annotation: not the event.
- The worry that something may have happened. Annotation: formless anxiety, which the tradition has already refused to legislate for.
Annotation on the whole: the compression is legal rather than delicate. The event has to be established before the prayer answers for it.

Reading: what settles the matter is not how sure the worshipper feels but whether something registered, to him or to someone beside him. A prayer is not dismantled on a suspicion he cannot pin to anything.

## The Latecomer's Entry

Diagram type: Process flow. The man who joins at the final tashahhud.

Start: a worshipper joins the imam during the final tashahhud of one of the five prayers.
  Down to: he intends to pray as a follower.
  Down to: he sits with the imam during his sitting, and prays the supplication.
  Down to: the imam salutes and completes his prayer.
  Down to: the latecomer rises.
  Down to: he completes his missed rak'ahs.
End state: his prayer is valid, and he has thereby attained the congregational prayer.
The definition that carries the verdict: the tashahhud is the concluding limit of prayer.
Attribution: the first question-and-answer book arranged according to the chapters of the Pillars of Islam.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. Two ways of counting the same man.

Column A: Counting by shared actions
- Rak'ahs shared: none
- Bowing shared: none
- Prostration shared: none
- Recitation shared: none
- What he did: sat down beside a congregation in its last moments and stood up again when it was over
- Verdict by this counting: he missed the prayer

Column B: Counting by the source's definition
- The prayer's outer edge: the tashahhud, its concluding limit
- His position relative to the edge: inside it
- What made him a participant: intention and presence
- What he owed afterward: the missed rak'ahs, paid standing
- Verdict by this counting: he attained the congregational prayer

Shared row: belonging to a congregation is not a quantity. A man is inside it or outside it, and the door was still open.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. One line doing two opposite jobs.

Whole: the single definition, the tashahhud is the concluding limit of prayer.
Parts, each the use the chapter makes of it:
- First movement, trouble arriving. Position: looking backward from the edge. Annotation: impurity arriving after the tashahhud can no longer invalidate, because the act's business is finished.
- Second movement, a man arriving. Position: looking forward at the edge. Annotation: a latecomer arriving before the edge closes is admitted, because he is inside the act.
- The shared fact. Position: the edge itself. Annotation: anything inside the edge is inside the prayer.
Annotation on the whole: one line answers two opposite questions, which is the sign that the source is not improvising. It has a shape for the prayer, and it reads both trouble and lateness off the same shape.

Diagram type: Process flow. The more common case, joining while the imam bows.

Start: a person joins the imam while he is bowing.
  Down to: he says the opening takbir, which enters the prayer.
  Down to: he says the takbir of bowing, which enters the bowing.
  Down to: that rak'ah is counted for him.
  Branch, he fears missing the bowing: he is rewarded with one takbir for both the opening and the bowing.
End state: two thresholds crossed, the door used once.
Blocked transition: the opening takbir being skipped. The concession says the two may coincide, never that one may be dropped.
Attribution: a book, in the summary of the classification.

Reading: the reward is named, not merely the validity. His haste toward the congregation is not counted against him.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The latecomer's cases and what each yields.

Rows: joins during the final tashahhud, joins while the imam is bowing, joins fearing he will miss the bowing, joins in whatever state and can build upon it, joins in whatever state and cannot build upon it, joins with the imam's head not yet raised from bowing.
Columns: what he does | what it counts as | what remains owed.
- Joins during the final tashahhud: intends to follow, sits, prays the supplication, rises at the imam's salutation | the congregational prayer, attained | the missed rak'ahs, completed alone.
- Joins while the imam is bowing: opening takbir, then the takbir of bowing | that rak'ah, counted for him | the rak'ahs that preceded him.
- Joins fearing he will miss the bowing: one takbir for both thresholds | the rak'ah, counted, and the reward named | the same remainder.
- Joins in whatever state and can build upon it: enters with the imam as he finds him | what he builds upon is counted for him | what he missed, made up afterward.
- Joins in whatever state and cannot build upon it: enters anyway | voluntary prayer, which is a real thing with its own worth | the whole obligation, still owed.
- Joins with the imam's head not yet raised from bowing: a single takbir | that rak'ah, attained with him, the intention formed in the heart by entering | the rak'ahs already past.

Cell commitment: no case is dismissed and no case is inflated. Every row names both what was gained and what is still owed.

> Whoever joins the imam having been preceded should enter with him in whatever state he is in.
Attribution: the general rule as the source states it.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The obligation built upon, against the volunteer.

Column A: What can be built upon
- The man's standing: a follower discharging an obligation
- What is credited: the obligation, in part, with the remainder made up
- The image the source uses: what he builds upon is counted for him
- What he does next: he prays what he manages to catch and makes up what he missed
- The ledger: one entry closed, one remainder paid

Column B: What cannot be built upon
- The man's standing: a volunteer
- What is credited: voluntary prayer, named without embarrassment
- What is not credited: the obligation, which is untouched
- What he does next: he pays the obligation entire
- The ledger: nothing closed, and nothing wasted either

Shared row: his presence is not wasted and it is not fraudulent.

Diagram type: Visual metaphor. Two ledgers kept open at once.

Abstract relation: how the source refuses two opposite errors about a latecomer.
Metaphor: two ledgers on the same desk, both open, neither allowed to absorb the other.
Element assignments:
- The left ledger, worship performed: what he prayed is worship and is credited as worship
- The right ledger, obligation owed: what he owed is still owed
- The entry that closes the right ledger: the remainder, paid afterward, in the same standing, by the same man, alone
- The error of collapsing the right into the left: a man walks out of the mosque believing he discharged a duty he did not, and every later reckoning is wrong
- The error of collapsing the left into the right: a man who ran to join a congregation is told his running counted for nothing, which is false and teaches him not to run next time
Reading: there is no ceremony around the making-up, no penalty attached to it, and no suggestion that a prayer completed after the congregation disperses is a lesser prayer. It is the remainder, and he pays it.

Diagram type: Process flow. The imam who does not know what he was preceded in.

Start: something happens before him, and he does not know what the imam missed before him.
  Down to: he continues upon his certainty.
  Down to: he waits, rather than resolving it by introspection or by stopping.
  Down to: he is informed by someone behind him.
  Down to: he returns to the correct position.
End state: no repetition, no collapse of the prayer, just a return.
Direction of correction: from behind, forward. The line of followers holds the knowledge the leader lacks in that moment.
Attribution: the reports.

Reading: the first half is the principle the previous chapter established about proceeding on what one is sure of. The second half is new. The congregation standing behind the leader is expected to speak.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The epistle of Muhammad ibn Fahd, three things settled at once.

Whole: the answer to the latecomer who cannot form the intention by uttering it with his tongue, given in an honorable epistle bearing the title From Him: With Love for the Path of Guidance for the Converted.
Parts, with annotation:
- The threshold. Content: the imam's head has not yet come up from bowing. Annotation: named as a fact about a body rather than a unit of time, because a man hurrying into a mosque can see a bowed back and cannot see a clock.
- The instrument. Content: a single takbir. Annotation: one word does the entering.
- The intention. Content: formed in the heart by the act of entering. Annotation: the man who cannot say it with his tongue has already made it.
Annotation on the whole: the questioner says he also put this question before Allah Almighty, seeking His pleasure, which marks it as a real anxiety rather than a technicality.

> You too must pray in this, with the help of Allah Almighty, for it is true and correct, as it has come down in the books of the saints of Allah, peace be upon them.
Attribution: our master and Mawlana Muhammad ibn Fahd, may Allah sanctify him, answering the latecomer who cannot utter the intention.

Reading: this book has taught elsewhere that intention resides in the heart while the tongue merely expresses it. That principle is not restated here so much as spent, cashed out for a man running toward a bowed imam with no breath left for a formula.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The man refused entry, against the man admitted.

Column A: The man who must repeat everything
- Where he came from: he was already praying
- What he had: fluency, an opening takbir already spent, no external misfortune
- What he did: stepped forward into following and bowed with the imam
- What he omitted: a new opening takbir for the new act
- Verdict: repeat the whole prayer

Column B: The man admitted to a rak'ah in progress
- Where he came from: the street
- What he had: one takbir and no words of intention available to him
- What he did: entered while the imam's head was still down in bowing
- What he supplied: everything he had
- Verdict: he attains that rak'ah, and the intention is counted as formed in his heart

Shared row: the difference is not effort or fluency. The first changed the identity of his act without passing through the door. The second passed through the door with everything he had.

Diagram type: Visual metaphor. A narrow door in a wide room.

Abstract relation: why the tradition is strict about the takbir and generous about everything after it.
Metaphor: a single narrow door set in the wall of a wide room.
Element assignments:
- The door: the opening takbir, the act by which a particular prayer is entered
- The wall: the boundary between not praying and praying, and between one prayer's identity and another's
- The room: the interior of the act, where the dog passes, the nose bleeds, the stomach turns, and the prayer holds
- What the door refuses: a man who changes which prayer he is in without passing through it
- What the door grants: a rak'ah already in progress, to a man with one takbir and no breath
Reading: the takbir is not a formality that a sincere man may skip and an anxious man must fuss over. It is the entrance, and the tradition is exacting about the entrance precisely so that it can be generous about everything inside.

## The Forming Of Children

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The instruction in three parts.

Whole: the saying of the four hundred manners attributed to Amir al-Mu'minin on children and prayer.
Parts, in the order given, with annotation:
- Teach your children what Allah will benefit you with. Annotation: the benefit is named as returning to the one who gave the instruction, not to the child.
- Command your children to pray. Annotation: the clause that requires a sentence, and nothing more.
- Accompany them in it when they reach the age of eight years. Annotation: the clause that decides the instruction's character, and the only one that costs the adult's own day.
Annotation on the whole: the child is not handed an obligation and left to discharge it. He is put beside someone already praying.

> Teach your children what Allah will benefit you with, and command your children to pray and accompany them in it when they reach the age of eight years.
Attribution: the four hundred manners attributed to Amir al-Mu'minin, peace be upon him.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. Command alone, against accompaniment.

Column A: A child taught by command
- What is required of the adult: a sentence
- What the child receives: a rule
- What it costs: nothing standing
- What it transmits: the content of the obligation

Column B: A child accompanied in it
- What is required of the adult: being there, at that hour, in that posture, for as long as it takes a child to learn it
- What the child receives: a rule, and the sight of someone keeping it
- What it costs: a standing obligation on the adult's own day
- What it transmits: the act itself, by being performed beside him

Shared row: the source has chosen the second. Nothing in the passage suggests the eight-year-old's prayer is expected to be excellent. What is expected is that he is in it, beside someone who is in it too.

Diagram type: Genealogy chain. One mechanism appearing three times in the chapter, the arrow reading as is admitted by joining.

The latecomer at the final tashahhud → a congregation already sitting in its last moments
The latecomer at the bowing → a rak'ah already standing, entered with one takbir
The child of eight → a household already praying, entered beside his father

Reading: a person who cannot yet carry the act alone is admitted by joining someone who can. None of the three is asked to build from nothing.

Diagram type: Timeline. The developmental scheme, in the intervals given.

Time axis: years of a boy's life, in intervals of seven.
Position 1, birth to seven: the boy is formed.
