## The two lines and the delay between them

Diagram type: Annotated structure. Two lines drawn across one circle, and the gap between them.

Whole: the sun's daily circuit, drawn as a circle with a tangent line laid across its top.
Parts, with position:
- The first line. Position: at the halfway mark of the arc, exactly.
- The second line. Position: past the halfway mark, at the intersection of circle and tangent where passage becomes apparent.
- The gap between them. Position: the blind band, of unfixed width.
Annotations:
- First line: perceived by the vision of the soul. The true disappearance that makes the prayer obligatory.
- Second line: perceived by natural vision. Corresponds to more than half of the arc.
- The gap: reaching the first line involves descent even when the senses detect no movement in the shadow and no displacement of the sun at all.
Extension of the same annotation: the reasoning that governs seeing the sun governs the sun's rays equally. By the time the rays report the change, the shadow has had to come into existence, and the crossing that produced it is already behind us.

## Obscured skies and two ranks

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The objection, set out whole before anything is done to it.

Whole: the opponent's best shot.

> If the matter is as you have described, and fasting is obligatory on the basis of the soul's sighting as you maintain, then what is the meaning of the Prophet's words, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him: If you are obscured, then count thirty days?

Attribution: the opponent, quoted at length and answered afterward.

Parts of its force, with position:
- Presupposition one. Position: first. A sky you are trying to look at.
- Presupposition two. Position: second. A sky that can be blocked.
- Presupposition three. Position: third. A remedy for the nights when looking fails.
Annotation, the question in one line: why legislate a remedy for the failure of an instrument that was never the instrument.
Annotation, the implication: a doctrine that says the month is settled by knowledge should have no use for clouds at all.

Diagram type: Process flow. The reply, which begins by refusing the frame rather than answering inside it.

- Start: that is not an objection against psychic vision.
  - Down to: the soul's sighting has been established by the evidence already presented.
  - Down to: it is more honourable and more appropriate to apply it in the fulfilment of obligatory duties.
  - Down to: we therefore did not restrict the ruling to naked-eye vision alone.
  - Down to: because both modes of vision are foundational in performing obligatory duties, and one is superior to the other.
- End: the thirty-day instruction is not a rival doctrine that must be explained away. It is provision made for the junior foundation.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. What was conceded against what was withheld, in the same breath.

Column A: Conceded
- Eyesight is a foundation
- The foundation is real
- It is sufficient to discharge the duty
- The fallback rule genuinely serves those who hold it
- The man who uses it is not in error

Column B: Withheld
- Eyesight is the junior of two foundations
- It does not define when the obligation begins
- It is not the only foundation the law recognises
- It cannot be the frame in which the doctrine is judged
- Its provision explains the hadith rather than being explained by it

Shared row: the letter never says eyesight is irrelevant to the law.

Diagram type: Hierarchy. The two ranks, and what each is given.

- Level 1: Vision, as a foundation for discharging obligatory duties
  - Level 2: The superior mode, the sighting of the soul
    - Level 3: Held by the select, who are advanced in knowledge and are the first to understand
    - Level 3: Can distinguish between the two modes
    - Level 3: Obliged to act upon what they have learned
  - Level 2: The other mode, naked-eye sighting
    - Level 3: Held by the general public, who are obliged to follow the people of knowledge
    - Level 3: Lack the insight to distinguish between the two modes
    - Level 3: Obliged to act upon what they have come to know
Annotation on the lower branch: this is not an insult. The public are not described as wrong, or lazy, or disobedient. They are described as unable to tell the two visions apart.
Annotation on the rule that follows: if you cannot tell them apart, you cannot be given a rule that requires you to choose between them. So you are given a rule that works without the distinction: look, and if you cannot see, count thirty.

Diagram type: Visual metaphor. The relation between the two modes, made spatial through the one comparison the letter chooses.

Abstract relation: two valid ways of discharging one duty, unequal in rank, equal in sufficiency.
Metaphor: the two permitted times of a single prayer, laid on one strip.
Element assignments:
- The earlier time: superior. Assigned to the sighting of the soul.
- The later time: valid, lower in rank. Assigned to the sighting of the eye.
- The man who prays at the later time: has not sinned, has not incurred a debt, does not repeat.
- The man who waits for the sky to clear and then counts: is a man praying at the later time.
Reading: what the letter denies is not the validity of his practice but the claim that his practice is the only one the law recognises, and more pointedly, the claim that his instrument defines when the obligation begins.

Diagram type: Visual metaphor. The generalisation behind the ranks, stated flatly and without hedging.

Abstract relation: every existing thing carries two sides, and both sides bind.
Metaphor: two faces of one body, an apparent side and a hidden side.
Element assignments:
- The apparent side: known by the select and the general public alike.
- The hidden side: known only by those of virtue.
- Duty attached to the hidden side: the select are obliged to act upon what they have learned.
- Duty attached to the apparent side: the common people are obliged to act upon what they have come to know.
Reading: nobody in this scheme is excused from action, and nobody is given knowledge that carries no duty. Rank here is not privilege. It is the measure of what you are answerable for.
Annotation: this is the epistemology the earlier chapters on guardianship and knowledge assumed without setting out, and it is set out here in two sentences.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The astronomer at dawn set beside the scholar at the crescent, attribute by attribute.

Rows: the astronomer at dawn, the scholar at the crescent.
Columns: the faculty used | what it settles | what becomes forbidden or obligatory | who must follow | is the practice disputed.
- The astronomer at dawn: hidden knowledge, not inspection of the horizon | that dawn has arrived | eating, drinking and marital relations are forbidden, and fasting has begun | the person ignorant of dawn's timing | no, nobody objects to this
- The scholar at the crescent: hidden knowledge of the soul's sighting | when fasting becomes obligatory | the month begins and the fast is owed | the ignorant, who must follow the scholar in this | yes, this is the whole dispute

Cell commitment: the community has always accepted that one end of the fasting day is settled by a person who knows rather than by every individual's inspection of the horizon. The earlier chapters have already granted that a man bound at dawn is bound whether or not he can see the sky.
- Closing line of the matrix: if a knowledgeable man's calculation can start your fast this morning, why can a knowledgeable man's calculation not start your month.

## The literal reading taken apart

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. Four charges laid against the literal reading, each with the absurdity it produces and the authority that forbids it.

Rows: the weather charge, the forbidden-day charge, the geography charge, the counting charge.
Columns: what the literal reading requires | the result it produces | what forbids that result.
- The weather charge: fasting by sight depends on obstacles clearing away | a month standing at twenty-nine days rather than thirty, so a divine command generated haphazardly, with a day added or taken away | sincerity stands apart from doubt and suspicion
- The forbidden-day charge: the command to complete thirty holds even where the month fell short | the Prophet commanding his nation to fast an additional day on which, but for the command, fasting is forbidden and breaking the fast is required | worship performed in uncertainty about whether it is owed is not what the verse describes
- The geography charge: each community follows its own sky | fasting obligatory for some people on one day and for others on the next, and on the same logic a prayer obligatory for one person at one time and another at a different time | a prayer time comes in which the whole community is obliged to establish the prayer together
- The counting charge: count the days of Sha'ban, with the end of the fast determined by counting the days of Ramadan | a count that needs someone to count and knowledge of the first day, in months where the crescent cannot be sighted at the outset at all | Allah does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear

Cell commitment: error can fall in naked-eye sighting. Error does not fall in the sighting of the soul, which is knowledge.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. Two towns, one rule of sight, two months.

Column A: Kirman
- Prevailing sky: overcast
- Rule followed: naked-eye sighting
- School followed: the same school as Column B
- Crescent seen at the outset: frequently not
- Day the fast begins: one day

Column B: Shiraz
- Prevailing sky: may be clear
- Rule followed: naked-eye sighting
- School followed: the same school as Column A
- Crescent seen at the outset: more often yes
- Day the fast begins: the next day

Shared row: the divergence is not theoretical and it is not small. Run the same logic on the prayer and it is worse still, a prayer obligatory for one person at one time and another at a different time.
Second shared row: whoever delays the prayer and abandons it has neglected an obligation. The same holds for the fast. When the time comes for the community to fast, the one who fasts and the one who breaks are both bound by that time.

Diagram type: Process flow. The counting instruction examined on its own terms, ending where it started.

- Start: the crescent cannot be sighted at the outset of Sha'ban, because of some impediment.
  - Named places where this is the ordinary case, not the exception: Tabaristan, the mountain regions, the coasts, the islands of the sea, and anywhere the air is turbid.
  - Down to: the person has no way of knowing how to prepare for Sha'ban.
  - Down to: still less for a Ramadan whose beginning has never been established.
  - Down to: but the instruction to count requires two things, someone to count, and knowledge of the first day of the month.
  - Loop back to start: the fallback needs the very thing it exists to replace.
- End state: whoever counts the days of a month without knowing its beginning and without having seen the crescent cannot fulfil the duty at all.

Diagram type: Process flow. The fork with only two prongs.

- Start: on the literal reading, the report asks of people what they cannot do.
  - Down to: attributing incapacity-imposing legislation to the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, is inconceivable. He is far above burdening the servants of Allah with what they cannot do, or casting them into dispute.
  - Branch, and only two branches exist:
  - Prong A: the report is not correct.
  - Prong B: he and his family said it in a sense that is sound, that settles the matter properly and without ambiguity for him and for everyone else.
  - Down from Prong B, which the letter takes: the testimonies confirm what the whole argument has been driving at.
- End: what creation has been entrusted to is the sighting of the rational soul.

Citations carried in this movement, preserved with attribution:

Attribution: chapter 98, verse 5.

Attribution: chapter 2, verse 185.

Annotation on the middle citation: the permissibility of an incomplete month under naked-eye sighting, along with the doubt attached to it, was only ever meant to point toward the sighting of the soul, whose count neither increases nor decreases. It was an exhortation toward completing the prescribed period.

## The pillars held in trust

Diagram type: Process flow. The hinge, and how abruptly it turns.

- Start: the discussion has established that naked-eye sighting is not what was intended.
  - Down to: the general public who possess knowledge must follow accordingly.
  - Down to: the opposing view on this has been invalidated.
  - Down to, with no paragraph of preparation: any pillar of Islam brought by Muhammad, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, and his family, whether prayer, fasting, zakat, Hajj, or jihad, is entrusted to the infallible community that is safeguarded from error.
- End: the subject has stopped being the moon.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The hinge sentence, dissected into the two phrases carrying its weight.

Whole: one sentence covering the entire religion.
Parts, with position:
- The phrase any pillar. Position: at the front, before any instance is named.
- The list of five. Position: inside the claim, supplied after the rule.
- The description of the holder, a community safeguarded from error. Position: at the end, where the licence sits.
Annotations:
- Any pillar: the letter is not arguing from the fast outward by analogy, cautiously, one step at a time. It states the rule at full generality first and then supplies the instances.
- The list: two of these have had chapters in this series, two have not appeared until now.
- The holder: a duty can only be deposited with a holder reliable enough to keep it. The argument of the last three chapters has been that the instrument which errs cannot time an obligation. The same test is now applied to the holder rather than to the faculty, and it yields the same kind of answer.

Column B: The obligation as deposited trust
- Issued to: a body
- Discharged by: the body's act, in which the individual takes part

- Test of success: position relative to the leader
- What a timing error costs: the duty itself

Shared row: the letter walks the five pillars one at a time to show that Column B is not a novel doctrine but a description of what every one of them already looks like.

Diagram type: Quadrant map. Why the eye was disqualified, positioned against what a shared duty requires.

Space: what an instrument must be if it is to time a duty held in common.
- Top, what the duty requires: a single trigger, identical for every member of the body at once.
- Left, the eye: returns a different answer to every holder. The cloud over Kirman is not over Shiraz. Each person's sky belongs to him.
- Right, knowledge: what the scholar knows about the moon's position is the same in Kirman as in Shiraz, on a clear night and under cloud, and the same for the man who worked it out and the man who accepted it from him.
- Bottom, the congregation as the test case: a congregation could not pray behind a leader while each member kept his own count of the bowings.
Reasoning for the left position: the objection to naked-eye sighting was never really that eyes are weak. The letter argued weakness too, at length, in the previous chapter, but weakness alone would only mean eyes need help.
Reasoning for the right position: a shared obligation needs a shared trigger, and only one of the two visions can supply one.
Reading: the deeper problem is that eyes are private.

## What this episode lands

Diagram type: Process flow. The chapter's whole arc, from ruin to doctrine.

- Start: a ruin, a lost figure of which a handful of broken labels survive and nothing else.
  - Down to: the argument it was meant to prove survives intact. Near the top of its arc the sun produces no shadow change an eye can register, so the senses fall silent.
  - Down to: a person who does not know might think the sun has halted. It has not. It is crossing.
  - Down to: the obligation sits at that crossing, the midpoint of the arc, available to the soul, not at the later moment when the lengthening shadow announces what already happened.
  - Down to: the objection, a hadith that seems to legislate for failed eyesight. Not denied. Ranked.
  - Down to: the literal reading dismantled on its own terms, and the fork, and the prong the letter takes.
  - Down to: what creation is entrusted to is the sighting of the soul. Which asks who holds the trust.
- End: a doctrine covering every pillar of the religion.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The closing statement of the chapter, reduced to its two halves.

Column A: The reason the eye was never the instrument, as the opponent would have expected it
- The charge: eyes are weak
- The remedy that would follow: eyes need help
- Who it convicts: the poor observer
- Scope of the finding: the crescent dispute alone

Column B: The reason the letter actually gives
- The charge: eyes are private
- The remedy that would follow: none, because no sharpening of a private instrument makes it shared
- Who it convicts: the account of obligation itself
- Scope of the finding: prayer, fasting, alms, pilgrimage, and the struggle in the path of Allah

Shared row: the alms paid to the wrong office are not paid. The pilgrimage performed alone is not performed. The fighter who draws blood outside the order is condemned. And in the prayer, where the acts themselves are all correct, the man who moves early has still deviated, because what he owed was not the bowing but the bowing with.

Closing annotation: not because it is weak. Because it is yours alone, and the duty is not.
