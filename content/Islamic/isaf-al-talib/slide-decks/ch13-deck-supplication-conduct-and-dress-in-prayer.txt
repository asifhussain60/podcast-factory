# Supplication, Conduct, and Dress in Prayer

## Where this episode picks up

Annotated structure. The whole: a completed prayer, dissected into the three edges the source defends.

Whole: the prayer as an act with living edges, not a closed box.

Parts, with position and one-sentence annotation:
- Edge AFTER the salutation, positioned to the right of the act: the public form is complete, and supplication begins. The question is whether the servant may ask, when, and with what gesture.
- Edge INSIDE the act, positioned at the center: speech, motion, carrying, eating, drinking, recitation, and voice. The question is what the body may do while the prayer is running.
- Edge AROUND the body, positioned as the surrounding ring: garment, covering, ornament, image, impurity, and the ground of prostration. The question is what the prayer is performed in and upon.

Reading of the layout: right, then center, then outward. A manual of thresholds rather than a treatise on prayer's core.

Comparison matrix. The three edges, each with its governing question and decisive test.

| Edge | Governing question | Decisive test | What failure looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| After the salutation | May the servant ask, when, and with what gesture | Received practice, order, and mediation | Improvised asking severed from obedience |
| Inside the act | What the body may do while the prayer runs | Whether the required form survives | Gesture, carrying, eating, drinking, competing voice |
| Around the body | What the prayer is performed in and upon | Covering, purity, visibility, bodily capacity | Exposed 'awra, known impurity, visible image, disabling wrap |

- Level 1: Revelation and prophetic word
  - Level 2: The Qur'an, cited for the ascent of good words, chapter 35, verse 10
  - Level 2: Sayings of the Prophet, peace be upon him, on the earth as mosque, on the Fatiha, on the basmala and wiping over the feet
- Level 1: Imami transmission
  - Level 2: Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him
    - Level 3: The Four Hundred Etiquettes, on promptness, gates, and the thrower without an arrow
    - Level 3: Rulings on pictures, dirhams, prostration on food, distress, shirt without waist-wrapper
  - Level 2: The pure Imams, may God's blessings be upon them, from whom the loyal ones received the wiping practice
- Level 1: Learned corpus and councils
  - Level 2: The interpretation of the Shari'a
  - Level 2: The Mustansiriyya Councils
  - Level 2: The Musannaf
  - Level 2: Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman, may God sanctify him, book of the subconscious and book of obligatory laws

## The Answerable Plea

Process flow. The two opening questions and their rulings, with conditions on each transition.

Start: A questioner reads in the books of the guiding call that supplication is not recited after the afternoon prayer.
- ↓ Question 1. Does the ruling concern the special supplication of drawing near, or all supplications?
- ↓ Answer. It refers to drawing near and to other acts.
- ↓ Qualifier. No one is obligated to commit a sin through supplication.
- ↓ Question 2. Should the worshipper supplicate after the obligatory 'Asr prayer, and wipe his face at the place of prostration in every prayer?
- ↓ Answer, branch one. It is not permitted until after 'Asr and Fajr.
- ↓ Answer, branch two. The loyal ones prescribed wiping after 'Asr and Fajr in particular, received from the pure Imams, may God's blessings be upon them.
- ↓ Answer, branch three. A lifespan is not prolonged by a supplication made after 'Asr.
- ↓ Answer, branch four. If he prolongs it, he bears no sin for that.
End state: not a ban on asking, but a boundary between received practice, devotional extension, and a false mechanical expectation.

Comparison matrix. Three registers the ruling keeps apart.

| Register | What it holds | What it denies |
|---|---|---|
| Received practice | Wiping after 'Asr and Fajr, taken from the pure Imams | That the practice is a personal invention |
| Devotional extension | Prolonging supplication carries no sin | That prolonging is prescribed |
| False expectation | None | That post-'Asr supplication lengthens a lifespan |

Contrast pair. Time-consciousness as the frame for the plea, from the interpretation of the Shari'a.

Column A: Daytime prayer
- Voice: recited silently
- Marker: the noon and afternoon prayers are not audible
- Bodily state: the body prays under open light
- Threshold effect: silence trained by day does not spill into unbounded speech afterward

Column B: Night prayer
- Voice: voiced in the depth of darkness
- Marker: dawn announced like glass catching the light
- Bodily state: the body prays under cover of dark
- Threshold effect: the voiced act still ends at the salutation, not wherever the worshipper wishes

Shared row: Prayer is never detached from time, and the afterword of prayer has its own discipline.

Annotated structure. The Mustansiriyya Councils place supplication at the culmination.

Whole: the completed discussion of prayer's jurisprudence, the outward with its exposition, the inward with its signs.

Parts, in sequence:
- Outward jurisprudence, positioned first: exposition of the visible act
- Inward jurisprudence, positioned beneath it: the signs of the hidden act
- Supplication, positioned last and highest

> which is the culmination of prayer, and what it contains.
Attribution: the Mustansiriyya Councils.

Annotations:
- Culmination, not decoration: duʿāʾ is where completed standing, recitation, bowing, and prostration become explicit dependence.
- Prayer trains the body; supplication trains the need.
- Prayer binds the worshipper to inherited form; supplication binds his desires to repentance, praise, blessing, authority, and refuge.

Comparison matrix. The seven forms of supplication, each with its bodily commitment.

| Form | Hand or body position | Direction | Setting or instrument | What the body is saying |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supplication of appeal | Hands spread, palm insides facing the face, then turned so the backs face the face | Inward, then reversed | Any | Calling for help against what is feared |
| Supplication of seeking refuge | Palms turned outward | Outward | Any | Pushing away, not drawing toward |
| Supplication of al-istikana | Hands clasping his own shoulders | Inward and self-enclosing | Solitude only | Lowliness before Allah, unwitnessed |
| Supplication with the rosary | Finger counts the beads | Downward to the hand | Rosary | Need told one bead at a time |
| Supplication by moving the finger | The finger moves | Minimal motion | Any | The smallest possible gesture still speaks |
| Supplication of raising | Palms stretched outward, hands extended upward | Upward toward the sky | Any | Reaching past the ceiling of the room |
| Supplication of al-ta'affur | Cheeks pressed to the ground | Downward to earth | Ground | Dust-marked humility |

Visual metaphor. Why the seven are called roots.

Abstract relation: seven fixed gestures standing beneath an open field of personal asking.
Metaphor: the root system of a tree.
Element assignments:
- Root system, below ground: the seven supplications, each with mention in the Qur'an
- Trunk: the twelve principles and foundations of supplication
- Branches: the particular askings of particular servants
- Fruit: an answered plea

Reading: what has been mentioned consists of roots, like the roots of a tree waiting to branch out and bear fruit. Everything above ground is fed from below.

Process flow. The twelve foundations for the one who wishes his supplication answered, in the source's own order.

Start: A servant who wants his supplication answered.
- Step 1: He precedes it with repentance from what God has forbidden.
- Step 2: He gives charity.
- Step 3: He fasts.
- Step 4: He prays, because God Almighty says that good words ascend to Him and righteous deeds lift them higher, chapter 35, verse 10.
- Step 5: He perfects his intention in supplication, accepts it, and gathers his heart.
- Step 6: He begins by glorifying God Almighty and praising Him as He deserves.
- Step 7: He invokes blessings upon the Prophet, peace be upon him.
- Step 8: He draws near to God Almighty through the imams of the religion, naming them one by one until he reaches the imam of his own time.
- Step 9: He beseeches him and them before God in answering his supplication.
- Step 10: He asks for the good of this world and the Hereafter.
- Step 11: He seeks refuge from the evil of this world and the Hereafter.
- Step 12: He calls upon God for whatever he wishes and wills.
End state: ordered dependence, not raw emotion released into heaven.

Annotated structure. The twelve read as four movements rather than a flat list.

Whole: the shape of an answered plea.

Parts, with position:
- Turning away, steps 1 through 4, positioned at the base: repentance, charity, fasting, prayer. The grip on forbidden things is loosened, wealth is loosened, appetite is disciplined, the body stands.
- Gathering, steps 5 and 6, positioned above the base: intention perfected, heart gathered, praise offered before any request.
- Approaching, steps 7 through 9, positioned as the ascent: blessings on the Prophet, peace be upon him, then the imams of the religion named in order to the imam of the time, then beseeching through them.
- Asking, steps 10 through 12, positioned at the summit: this world, the Hereafter, refuge from both evils, then whatever he wishes and wills.

Annotation on the order: personal want is last. It is not diminished, it is positioned.

Genealogy chain. Step 8 rendered as a directed approach, one chain per line.

Servant → repentance → charity → fasting → prayer → praise of God Almighty
Praise → blessings upon the Prophet, peace be upon him → the imams of the religion, named one by one
The imams of the religion → the imam of the servant's own time → beseeching him and them before God
Beseeching → the request itself → answer

Contrast pair. What Step 8 presupposes, set against what it supplies.

Column A: Presupposed doctrine
- Guardianship and knowledge
- Sacred ranks
- Disciplined loyalty
- Affection as law

Column B: What the approach supplies
- Authority supplies the supplicant with names
- Authority supplies order, one by one to the imam of the time
- Authority supplies mediation before God
- Authority supplies belonging, so the plea is not a solitary invention

Shared row: the plea is personal; its grammar is communal and hierarchical.

Timeline. The six times the gates of heaven are opened, from the Four Hundred Etiquettes of Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him.

Axis: the arc of a day and its events, not clock hours.
- At the rising of dawn
- At the call to prayer
- When the Qur'an is recited
- When the army advances
- At the descent of rain
- At the setting of the sun

Period bands: fixed daily openings, being dawn, the call, the setting sun. Occasional openings, being recitation, rain, the advancing army.

Reading of the axis: three gates keep the day's rhythm; three open when the world moves.

Comparison matrix. The maxims of Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, on supplication.

| Maxim | Timing it commands | What it promises | What it warns against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remember God Almighty and He will accept your supplication | Continuous | Acceptance, preservation, care | Forgetfulness |
| Be prompt in supplication before calamity descends | Before the event | Gates of heaven opened at six times | Waiting for disaster |
| Supplication responds to a decree already concluded | After the decree | Response even to what is settled | Assuming the matter is closed |
| Ward off affliction through supplication before calamity descends | Before the event | Affliction turned aside | Delay, because affliction reaches the believer faster than a torrent from mountaintop to valley |
| The one who does not supplicate is like the thrower who aims without an arrow | Always | Nothing, if he does not ask | Strength and aim without launch |
| Whoever recites "Say: He is God, the One" ten times until sunrise and "Indeed, We sent it down on the Night of Power" ten times | Until sunrise | No sin recorded against him even if Iblis exerts his utmost effort | Neglecting the dawn window |

Visual metaphor. The thrower without an arrow.

Abstract relation: a believer who has knowledge, duty, and recognized authority but does not supplicate.
Metaphor: an archer's posture, complete but empty.
Element assignments:
- The stance: understanding of duty
- The aim: knowledge of the prayer
- The strength of the arm: recognition of authority
- The missing arrow: supplication
- The target, untouched: the need itself

Reading: everything visible is correct and nothing flies. Supplication does not replace righteous action, since righteous deeds lift good words. It is the launch.

## Speech And Motion

Comparison matrix. The rulings on conduct inside the prayer, each judged by its own test.

| Case | The test applied | Ruling | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signalling to an intoxicated imam by bow or gesture without completing the prayer properly | Deliberate and without excuse | Prayer invalidated, whether directed at the imam or accompanied by praise | Invalidity |
| Bowing excessively out of habit | Done deliberately | He must repeat the prayer | Repetition required |
| A woman praying while carrying her infant to prevent him from crying, in congregation or alone | Chosen strategy | Not permissible to engage in prayer in such a manner | Prohibition |
| Praying while experiencing internal discomfort | Unavoidable | Nothing falls upon him | Excused |
| Hair gathered inside the turban, drawn from the back of the neck to the forehead above the temple | Whether it is drawn back to its origin | Nothing wrong with that; the forbidden form is gathering it to the middle of the head | Permitted |
| Something in the mouth resembling a lobe or a dirham | Whether it prevents proper formation of the letters | The prayer is complete | Permitted |
| Something held in the hand | Whether it prevents fulfilling the conditions of prayer | Nothing falls upon him, though being free of encumbrances is preferable | Permitted, better left |
| Eating or drinking during prayer, forgetfully or deliberately | Nature of the act itself | He has interrupted his prayer | Interruption |

Named-axis 2x2. The logic beneath those rulings, both axes committed.

Axes:
- Horizontal: origin of the act, from Unavoidable on the left to Chosen on the right
- Vertical: effect on the required form, from Does not obstruct at the bottom to Obstructs or replaces at the top

Quadrants:
- Top-right, Chosen and Obstructing: deliberate gesturing at the imam, deliberate excessive bowing, carrying the infant, eating and drinking. The worshipper chose a thing that damages the act, so the prayer is invalidated, interrupted, or must be repeated.
- Top-left, Unavoidable and Obstructing: the constrained cases the chapter handles later under travel, necessity, and the single garment. The act is performed as it can be, and repaired when repair becomes possible.
- Bottom-right, Chosen and Not obstructing: the object in the hand, the arranged hair, the earring or dirham. Permitted, with freedom from encumbrance named as preferable.
- Bottom-left, Unavoidable and Not obstructing: internal discomfort that cannot be helped. Nothing falls upon him.

Reasoning for the axes: the source distinguishes chosen disorder from constrained endurance, then asks separately whether the required form survives.

Process flow. The discipline of the voice, from the interpretation of the Shari'a.

Start: The worshipper rises.
- ↓ Speech stops. He does not speak until he offers the salutation.
- ↓ Recitation comes before bowing.
- Branch by time of day:
    - Branch A, noon and afternoon: recitation should not be audible, because these are daytime prayers.
    - Branch B, night: the prayer is recited aloud.
- ↓ In congregation, he does not raise his voice above the voice of the imam.
- ↓ Two prophetic exclusions. The Prophet, peace be upon him, forbade wiping over the feet, and left aside the public utterance of the basmala.
End: The salutation, which is where speech resumes.

- Level 1: Approach

- Level 1: Entry
  - Level 2: Opening
  - Level 2: Adhan
  - Level 2: Iqama
  - Level 2: Orientation
- Level 1: The voiced spine
  - Level 2: Recitation
- Level 1: The bending body
  - Level 2: Bowing
  - Level 2: Praise
  - Level 2: Praise
  - Level 2: Prostration
- Level 1: Articulated completion
  - Level 2: Tashahhud
  - Level 2: Taslim
- Level 1: Supplication kept inside the structure
  - Level 2: Du'a' al-Qunut
  - Level 2: Du'a' al-Witr

Source statement: these are fourteen doors that the worshipper must observe; they are the pillars of prayer, and without them his prayer is not complete.

Visual metaphor. Why the doors change the weight of small rulings.

Abstract relation: a sequence of required acts whose interruption is not trivial.
Metaphor: a building with thresholds rather than a list of steps.
Element assignments:
- The approach path: Wudu'
- The outer gate: Opening, Adhan, Iqama, Orientation
- The load-bearing spine: Recitation

- The rooms kept within the walls: Du'a' al-Qunut and Du'a' al-Witr

Reading: if prayer has doors, then careless speech, needless gesture, eating, drinking, obstructed letters, and disorderly movement are breaches in an architecture. To pass through them the servant must accept their shape.

## Garment And Ground

Process flow. The image test, with its branch conditions.

Start: Rugs or furnishings decorated with engravings, facing the qibla or another direction.
- Branch on distraction:
    - If the decoration distracts the worshipper: his prayer is not permissible in that space.
    - If it does not distract him: the prayer is permissible, except that one should not pray in the direction of images.
- ↓ The Musannaf on a carpet with depicted figures. It may be prayed on, unless the figure is in the place of prostration, where it distracts from prayer.
- ↓ A stronger answer. A rug or garment bearing figures is not permissible for prayer if the worshipper can see them; if he cannot see them, it is permissible.
- ↓ Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him. A man should not prostrate on a picture, nor on a carpet on which he stands facing one.
- Branch on the dirham:
    - Tied into the garment while praying, bearing a picture: he should not.
    - In a money-pouch or garment, and pure: permissible.
End: the decisive variables are visibility, placement, and purity, not the mere existence of an image.

Comparison matrix. Four registers of judgment the chapter refuses to collapse.

| Case | Valid | Disliked | Better left | Sinful |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A man praying in a garment a woman is permitted to wear | Yes | Matter of etiquette | Not stated | No |
| A woman praying in pure silk or a very red garment, saffron-dyed or not | Yes | Matter of etiquette | Not stated | No |
| Pure silk on a man | Yes | Yes, disliked for men | Implied | No |
| A man praying in forged gold or silver weights and similar items | Yes, complete and valid | Not stated | Not stated | No |
| A man wearing rings for adornment rather than practical benefit | Yes | Men should not | Yes | Not sinful |
| A woman wearing rings for adornment | Yes | Permitted to do so | Leaving it is better | No |

Reading of the matrix: the prayer is valid, the adornment lacks proper reason, leaving it is better, and the person is not sinful. These are registers, not contradictions.

Named-axis 2x2. Validity against etiquette, with the garment rulings placed.

Axes:
- Horizontal: effect on validity, from Prayer stands on the left to Prayer falls on the right
- Vertical: standing in adab, from Approved at the bottom to Disliked or unfitting at the top

Quadrants:
- Bottom-left, Stands and Approved: the plain sufficient garment, the pure clothing of Muslim boys and Muslim slaves, the uncovered head in public.
- Top-left, Stands but Disliked: pure silk on a man, rings for adornment on a man, a very red garment. Valid, yet not fitting.
- Top-right, Falls and Disliked: praying with a visible figure on rug or garment, praying knowingly with impurity on the clothing, ishtimal al-samma'.
- Bottom-right, Falls though not blameworthy in intent: a woman whose 'awra becomes exposed during prayer, knowingly or not; a free woman or an umm al-walad in the same case. The prayer is void without the wearer being cast as defiant.

Reasoning: the two axes are independent. A garment can be disliked and the prayer still stand; a garment can be innocently worn and the prayer still fall.

Comparison matrix. Covering, by the source's own categories.

| Body or status | Covering rule | If neglected | Qualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Women generally | Covering is obligatory for all women | If any part of her 'awra becomes exposed, she may not pray | The respondent states he knows this ruling |
| Women of the people of the da'wa in India whose legs are sometimes exposed once or twice | Same obligation | Same | The respondent suggests the question perhaps concerns special cases |
| A free woman | If she prays with her 'awra exposed, knowingly or not, the prayer is void, per the Musannaf | Void | Knowledge is not the variable |
| An umm al-walad, on covering during prayer | Same as the free woman, per the Musannaf | Void | Knowledge is not the variable |
| A free woman wearing a net over her clothing | Not covered by the net if the shield or veil is not thick | Not sufficient | Thickness is the test |
| An umm al-walad, a correspondence slave woman, a concubine, a slave girl who has not reached menstruation | May pray without a face-covering | Those among them who are disobedient and do not know this must repeat every prayer performed without a face-covering from the time of that lapse | The source's own social categories |

Special cases the respondent names as possibly governing the question: the person swimming in water, the person in fear, and the traveler.

Contrast pair. Firm rule against recognized exception.

Column A: The rule held firm
- Covering is obligatory for all women
- Exposure voids, knowledge or not
- A thin net does not suffice
- Purpose: the ruling is not softened into vagueness

Column B: The exception recognized
- The swimmer, the fearful, the traveler
- Accommodation for fear, travel, water, and necessity applies
- Exceptional constraint governed by its own conditions
- Purpose: exception stays exception rather than becoming habit

Process flow. Impurity on the garment, with its two decision branches.

Start: The worshipper's clothing.
- Branch 1, borrowed clothing: it is permissible to pray in the clothing of Muslim boys and Muslim slaves, unless the garments show impurity or one does not pray in them.
- Branch 2, impurity noticed after the prayer: if he knew of it beforehand, he has invalidated his prayer and must repeat it, whether he was making up a missed prayer or not.
- Branch 3, only one garment and it is impure, with no other garment and no water: he prays in it, then repeats the prayer when he finds another garment or water to purify it.
- Branch 4, two garments, one impure, and he does not know which: he prays twice, once in each garment, intending the obligatory prayer with each.
End: obligation is never delayed into nonexistence. He prays now under constraint and repairs later when repair becomes possible. Uncertainty is answered by doubling the act, not by ignoring impurity.

Comparison matrix. Travel, weapons, and the limits of repetition.

| Encumbrance | Example given | Must he repeat? | Governing variable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weapons that prevent praying properly | Feathers in arrows, daggers | No repetition on that account | The burden is not the test |
| Garments of pure silk while travelling | Pure silk garments | No repetition on that account | The burden is not the test |
| Impurity on the worshipper he has not removed | Any removable impurity | Repetition required | Purity and removability |

Source formulation: a prayer need not be repeated except when clothing is pure and there is nothing impure on the worshipper that he has not removed. The traveler may be encumbered; the prayer remains required; what cannot be changed is endured, and what is impure and removable must be removed.

Contrast pair. The servant's own prayer against his fitness to lead.

Column A: Praying for himself
- Condition: wearing only a small cloth on his shoulders, having only that garment
- Ruling if no one else can be found: his own prayer in that state is permissible
- Governing principle: necessity permits a person to pray as he is
- Also under this column: praying in distress, in darkness, or in a garment that distresses him, when no alternative is found

Column B: Leading others
- Condition: the same small cloth on the shoulders
- Ruling: he may not lead others in prayer
- Governing principle: the leader's public representational role demands a dignity private necessity may not meet
- Also under this column: no one should stand before Allah Almighty in a state of distress

Shared row: validity and leadership are not identical. A prayer that stands for the man himself may still be unfit to stand before others.

Annotated structure. Ishtimal al-samma' dissected, part by part.

Whole: a single garment wrapped so that it disables the acts prayer requires.

Parts, with position:
- The middle of the garment, placed over the head or shoulders
- One end, held in the left hand against the body
- The other end, turned from his right side behind him
- That end, gathered with his right hand
- The result, a wrap that becomes like a sewn garment with both hands underneath it

Annotations, one per consequence:
- Hands underneath: he cannot place his hands on the ground as required.
- Body bound: he cannot bow or prostrate correctly.
- Arms enclosed: he cannot complete the raising of his hands.
- Verdict: such a prayer is incomplete and does not earn reward.

Cross-reading: the objection is not unfamiliarity of appearance. Dress is not an aesthetic layer over worship; it either enables or obstructs obedience.

Comparison matrix. Three garment forms the source interprets side by side.

| Garment form | What the phrase means | Consequence for the act | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A single dense shirt | If the worshipper bows, his side may become apparent | Bowing and prostration remain possible | Nothing wrong with praying in it |
| "like the wings of the hook" | A garment wrapped around the back with the limbs resting on the shoulders | Used when no other option is available | Permitted under necessity |
| Ishtimal al-samma' | Wrapped so both hands are underneath and the garment becomes like a sewn one | Hands cannot reach the ground; bowing, prostration, and raising the hands are blocked | Forbidden; the prayer is incomplete and earns no reward |

## The Ground Of Prostration

Hierarchy. Permissible and impermissible surfaces for prostration.

- Level 1: Permitted
  - Level 2: The ground itself
    - Level 3: Earth, per the Prophet, peace be upon him
  - Level 2: What grows from the ground
    - Level 3: Grass and the like, per the interpretation of the Shari'a
  - Level 2: Material transformed into part of the prayer-space
    - Level 3: Plaster and the like, burned with the dead, counted as part of the mosque and the house, since fire has purified it, per the Musannaf
- Level 1: Not permitted
  - Level 2: Materials that are not earth in the complete sense
    - Level 3: Stone, milk, and the like. Only the obligatory prayer is binding upon him; the nafl and sunnah prayers are not.
    - Level 3: Stone and plaster, per the answers of Idris ibn al-Hasan, may Allah sanctify him, in the untreated case
  - Level 2: Edible things
    - Level 3: A heap of wheat or barley, anything edible, bread. Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, says a man should not prostrate on such things.
  - Level 2: Images
    - Level 3: A picture, or a carpet on which he stands facing one

> The earth has been made for me a mosque and a means of purification.
Attribution: the Prophet, peace be upon him, cited in the answers of Idris ibn al-Hasan, may Allah sanctify him.

Annotation on the principle: anything that does not qualify as earth in the complete sense cannot be prayed on. The forehead makes the lowest bodily sign of servanthood, and the act needs ground, not appetite.

Comparison matrix. The closing miscellaneous rulings on material contact and place.

| Material or place | Ruling | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| A small amount of milk, fat, or eggs from camels, cows, sheep, and chickens on clothing or body | Permissible | Small amount |
| Camel pens | There is no prayer in them | None given |
| Sheep pens | Nothing wrong with praying in them | After they have been swept and sprinkled with water |
| A shirt alone without a waist-wrapper | A man should not pray in it, per Amir al-Mu'minin Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him | None given |
| A garment worn loosely around the neck or immodestly | Not permissible to pray in it | None given |
| A state of distress | None should stand before Allah Almighty in it | Unless no alternative is found, in which case he prays as he is |
| A prayer without the Fatiha | Incomplete and deficient, per the Prophet, peace be upon him | Every such prayer |

Reading: the last ruling ties the whole surface discipline back to prayer's recited center.

Process flow. The practical test the whole movement has been building.

Start: A worshipper, dressed, standing on a surface, holding or wearing something.
- Test 1: Does the thing prevent motion? If yes, the act fails.
- Test 2: Does it block the conditions of prayer? If yes, the act fails.
- Test 3: Does it obstruct the letters? If yes, the act fails.
- Test 4: Does it introduce impurity he knows of and can remove? If yes, the prayer is undone.
- Test 5: Does it distract the eye, by visible image or figure? If yes, the prayer is not permissible there.
- Test 6: Does it leave covering insufficient? If yes, the prayer is void.
- Test 7: Does wrapping make bowing and prostration impossible? If yes, the prayer earns no reward.
End state, all tests passed: the prayer stands, even with an uncovered head, an earring or dirham, or a constrained hand, though freedom from encumbrance remains preferable.

Visual metaphor. The praying body inside its material loyalties.

Abstract relation: prayer as an act surrounded by objects that either protect or disturb it.
Metaphor: concentric rings around a prostrating body.
Element assignments:
- Center: the forehead at the point of prostration
- Ring 1: the ground, earth or what grows from it, never an edible heap or an image
- Ring 2: the garment, judged by covering, purity, thickness, and whether it permits the acts
- Ring 3: ornament and object, gold, silver, rings, pouches, nets, dirhams, judged by use and effect
- Ring 4: the room, its rugs, figures, engravings, darkness, and the direction of images
- Outermost: the servant's state, distress or composure

Reading: obligations propagate inward. The outer rings do not create the prayer, but any of them can undo it.

## Closing

Contrast pair. The chapter's severity against the chapter's mercy, held together.

Column A: Severity
- Prayer cannot be casually interrupted
- It cannot be wrapped in disabling garments
- It cannot be performed knowingly with impurity
- It cannot be directed toward distracting images
- It cannot be placed upon unsuitable surfaces

Column B: Mercy
- Unavoidable discomfort is excused
- Constrained travel is recognized
- Hidden objects are tolerated when they do not obstruct the act
- Necessity permits a person to pray as he is
- Later repair is possible when water or pure clothing becomes available

Shared row: prayer is not fragile because Allah needs perfect fabric, flawless rooms, or empty hands. It is guarded because the servant needs a form strong enough to train desire, discipline the body, and make supplication rise with righteous action.

Annotated structure. The three edges closed, in the order the chapter opened them.

Whole: the act with living edges, now complete.

Parts, with position and closing annotation:
- After the salutation, to the right: no dissolving into private wishes. Supplication has roots, foundations, gestures, repentance, praise, blessing, and approach through the imams of the religion.
- Inside the prayer, at the center: devotion is not permission for ordinary behavior. Speech, motion, carrying, eating, drinking, recitation, and gesture are governed by the doors of the act.
- Around the prayer, as the surrounding ring: clothing, ornaments, images, impurity, and ground are never neutral. They preserve or disturb the dignity of standing before Allah.
