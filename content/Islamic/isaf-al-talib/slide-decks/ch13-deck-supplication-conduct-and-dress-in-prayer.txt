## The Answerable Plea

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The opening questions on supplication after prayer, each with its ruling and the limit set inside that ruling.

Rows: the four questions put to the source.
Columns: the ruling given | the limit inside the ruling.
- Do the books of the guiding call forbid the special supplication of drawing near after the afternoon prayer, or all supplication: it refers to drawing near and to other acts | no one is obligated to commit a sin through supplication.
- Should the worshipper supplicate after the obligatory afternoon prayer: it is not permitted until after ʿAṣr and Fajr | the prescription came from the pure Imams, may God's blessings be upon them.
- Should he wipe his face at the place of prostration during every prayer: the loyal ones prescribed wiping after ʿAṣr and Fajr in particular | these two prayers, not every prayer.
- Does a supplication after ʿAṣr lengthen a life: a person's lifespan is not prolonged by it | if he prolongs it, he bears no sin for that.

Cell commitment: not a ban on asking, but a placement of asking inside inherited order.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. What the ruling protects, against what it refuses.

Column A: Received practice, protected
- Supplication located after ʿAṣr and Fajr
- Wiping the face prescribed for those two prayers
- Devotional extension permitted without sin
- Origin: received from the pure Imams, may God's blessings be upon them

Column B: Improvisation, refused
- Self-directed invention after the salutation
- The thought that a post-ʿAṣr plea mechanically lengthens a life
- Supplication treated as an obligation to transgress
- Origin: the worshipper's own eagerness, unanchored

Shared row: not asking against silence, but received asking against self-directed asking.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. One day, dissected into the three registers in which prayer is voiced, as the interpretation of the Sharīʿa presents them.

Whole: the daily cycle of voice and silence.

Parts, with position and annotation:
- Daytime prayer, recited silently. Position: the lit half. Annotation: silence belongs to the light.
- Night prayer, voiced in the depth of darkness. Position: the dark half. Annotation: voice belongs to the dark, where the act must be heard.
- Dawn, announced like glass catching the light. Position: the seam between them. Annotation: the hinge is announced rather than hidden.

Reading: prayer is never detached from time, and its afterword inherits that discipline.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The seam the Mustanṣiriyya Councils mark, where the jurisprudence of prayer closes and supplication opens, read across rather than down.

Column A: The prayer now completed
- What it trains: the body, through standing, recitation, bowing, prostration
- What it binds: the worshipper to inherited form
- What governs it: the outward, which has its exposition, and the inward, which has its signs
- Where its words come from: the fixed order of the act
- What it produces: a servant whose limbs have obeyed

Column B: The supplication that culminates it
- What it trains: the need
- What it binds: the worshipper's desires to repentance, praise, blessing, authority, refuge
- What governs it: times, gestures, roots, foundations, mediating authority
- Where its words come from: what the servant actually wants, ordered before it is voiced
- What it produces: a servant whose wanting has obeyed

Shared row: the councils place the second immediately after the first, so the act does not end at the salutation but culminates past it.

The Mustanṣiriyya Councils, on what follows the jurisprudence of prayer:

> Supplication, which is the culmination of prayer, and what it contains.

## The Answerable Plea, Roots And Foundations

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The seven named forms of supplication, each with the body that performs it.

Rows: the seven forms.
Columns: what the body does | condition or purpose.
- Supplication of appeal: hands spread, inside of the palms facing the face, then the palms turned so their backs face the face | calling for help against what he fears.
- Supplication of seeking refuge: the palms turned outward | seeking refuge from what threatens.
- Supplication of al-istikāna: the shoulders clasped with the hands | performed only in solitude.
- Supplication with the rosary: the finger counts the beads | counting sustains the plea.
- Supplication by moving the finger: the finger is moved | motion alone carries the plea.
- Supplication of raising: the palms stretched outward, the hands extended upward toward the sky | lifting the request.
- Supplication of al-taʿaffur: the cheeks pressed to the ground | humility marked in dust.

Cell commitment: each entry is a bodily grammar, not a preference. Palm inward, palm outward, shoulder clasped, cheek to earth each speak differently.

- At the crown, the fruit: the answered plea
- Reading: the gestures are root forms, waiting to branch out and bear fruit

  ↓

  Glorification of God Almighty, and praise as He deserves
  ↓ praise before request

Reading: the request is reached last because nothing before it may be skipped.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The sayings of Amīr al-Mu'minīn Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, in the Four Hundred Etiquettes.

Rows: the seven sayings.
Columns: the force each one carries.
- Remember God Almighty and He will accept your supplication: remembrance opens acceptance.
- Remember Him and He will preserve you and care for you: remembrance secures protection.
- Be prompt in supplication before calamity descends: supplication is preventative, not consoling.
- Supplication responds to a decree that has already been concluded, so call upon God and hasten: a concluded decree is not the end of asking.
- Ward off affliction through supplication before calamity descends: affliction reaches the believer faster than a torrent from the mountaintop to the valley below.
- The one who does not supplicate is like the thrower who aims without an arrow: aim without an arrow launches nothing.
- Whoever recites Say: He is God, the One ten times until sunrise, and Indeed, We sent it down on the Night of Power ten times, no sin will be recorded against him: even the utmost effort of Iblīs is outmatched.

## Speech And Motion

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The cases inside the prayer, each with its ruling and the test that produced it.

Rows: the eight cases put to the source.
Columns: the ruling | the test applied.
- A man prays behind an intoxicated imam and signals to him with a bow or another gesture without completing the prayer properly: invalidated if deliberate and without excuse, whether directed toward the imam or accompanied by praise | improvised correction.
- A man bows excessively out of habit: if deliberate, he must repeat the prayer | deliberate excess in form.
- A woman prays while carrying her infant to prevent him from crying: it is not permissible for her to pray in such a manner | chosen encumbrance.
- A person prays while experiencing internal discomfort: if unavoidable, nothing falls upon him | constrained endurance.
- A man prays with his hair gathered inside his turban, drawn from the back of the neck to above the temple: there is nothing wrong with that | the forbidden form is drawing the hair back to its origin, to the middle of the head.
- Something inside the mouth resembling a lobe or a dirham: complete, if it does not prevent the proper formation of the letters | obstruction of letters.
- He holds something in his hand: nothing falls upon him if the conditions are met, and freedom from encumbrance is preferable | obstruction of conditions.
- A worshipper eats or drinks during prayer, forgetfully or deliberately: he has interrupted his prayer | an ordinary bodily activity carried into worship.

Diagram type: Named axis 2x2. The moral scale the cases together produce.

Axes:
- Horizontal: origin of the act, from Unavoidable on the left to Chosen on the right
- Vertical: effect on the required form, from Does not obstruct at the bottom to Obstructs at the top

Quadrants:
- Bottom left, unavoidable and not obstructing: internal discomfort that cannot be helped. Nothing falls upon him, because the body is enduring rather than deciding.
- Bottom right, chosen and not obstructing: an object in the hand, a lobe or dirham in the mouth, hair gathered within the permitted form. Valid, though freedom from encumbrance is preferable.
- Top left, unavoidable and obstructing: the traveler burdened by weapons, and the person with no alternative garment. The prayer stands, with repair owed where repair becomes possible.
- Top right, chosen and obstructing: deliberate gesturing, habitual extra bowing, carrying the infant, eating and drinking. Invalidation or repetition.

Reading of the axes: chosen disorder is separated from constrained endurance, and the top right is where prayers are lost.

Annotation: encumbrance is judged by function, eating and drinking by kind.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The discipline of sound, as the interpretation of the Sharīʿa sets it out.

Whole: the worshipper's voice inside the act, dissected by where each rule falls.

Parts, with position and annotation:
- Recitation comes before bowing. Position: early in the act. Annotation: order, not preference.
- The voice is not raised above the imam's. Position: wherever the imam speaks. Annotation: sound may not compete with leadership.
- Recitation in the noon and afternoon prayers is not audible, these being daytime prayers. Position: the lit half. Annotation: voice follows time.
- Night prayer is recited aloud. Position: the dark half. Annotation: the dark is where the act is heard.
- The Prophet, peace be upon him, forbade wiping over the feet and left aside the public utterance of the basmala. Position: the margin of public sound. Annotation: some acts are withheld from public voice.
- Once the worshipper rises, he does not speak until the salutation. Position: the closing stretch. Annotation: speech has a closing boundary as well as an opening one.

Diagram type: Process flow. The fourteen doors of prayer, as Jaʿfar ibn Manṣūr al-Yaman, may God sanctify him, names them in the book of the subconscious on the meaning of prayer.

Start: a worshipper outside the act.

Approach: Wuḍūʾ
  ↓
Entry: Opening, then Adhān, then Iqāma
  ↓
Turning: Orientation
  ↓
Voice: Recitation
  ↓
Descent: Bowing, then Praise, then Praise
  ↓
Lowest point: Prostration
  ↓
Rising toward completion: Tashahhud, then Taslīm
  ↓
Supplication held inside the act: Duʿāʾ al-Qunut, then Duʿāʾ al-Witr

End state: a prayer that is complete, because its doors have been observed.

Annotation: these are fourteen doors the worshipper must observe. They are the pillars of prayer, and without them his prayer is not complete.

Reading: a descent and a rise, not a flat list, with supplication kept inside the structure. Careless speech, gesture, eating, drinking, obstructed letters, and disorderly movement are breaches in an architecture.

## Garment And Ground

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. Dress rulings sorted by the register of judgment each one occupies.

Rows: the seven cases.
Columns: the verdict | the register it belongs to.
- A man prays in a garment a woman is permitted to wear: not invalidated | etiquette.
- A woman prays in pure silk, or a very red garment, dyed with saffron or not: not invalidated | etiquette.
- A man prays in pure silk: disliked for men, and the prayer stands | dislike without invalidity.
- A man prays wearing forged gold or silver weights and similar items: complete and valid | validity.
- A man wears rings on hand or foot for adornment rather than practical benefit: he should not, there is no valid reason, and whoever does it is not sinful | impropriety without sin.
- A woman wears such rings: permitted, and leaving it is better | permission with a better option.
- A man goes out to the congregation and prays with his head uncovered: permissible | validity.

Cell commitment: validity, etiquette, dislike, and sin are four registers, and one case can occupy several without contradiction.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The covering rulings for women, as the source states them.

Rows: the seven questions and cases.
Columns: the ruling.
- The books of the Sharīʿa on a woman whose ʿawra becomes exposed during prayer: invalidated, whether she knew of it or not.
- Women of the people of the daʿwa in India whose legs are sometimes exposed once or twice: covering is obligatory for all women, and if any part of her ʿawra is exposed she may not pray.
- The respondent's own reading of the question: it perhaps concerns special cases, the person swimming in water, the person in fear, the traveler.
- The Musannaf on a free woman or an umm al-walad praying with the ʿawra exposed: void, knowingly or not.
- A free woman praying with a net over her clothing: the net does not suffice if the shield or veil is not thick.
- An umm al-walad, a correspondence slave woman, a concubine, and a slave girl not yet of menstruating age: they may pray without a face-covering.
- Those among them who are disobedient and do not know this: they must repeat every prayer performed without a face-covering from that lapse.

Annotation: the covering rule stays firm, and exceptional constraint stays exception, not habit.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. Constraint and necessity, and what each one costs.

Rows: the four constrained cases.
Columns: the ruling.
- A traveler carries weapons that prevent proper prayer, such as feathers in arrows, daggers, and pure silk garments: no repetition is owed except when clothing is pure and nothing impure remains on him that he has not removed.
- A person in distress, in darkness, or in a garment that distresses him: he should not pray so, and finding no alternative, he prays as he is.
- A man who has only a small cloth on his shoulders: he may not lead others.
- The same man, with no one else available to lead: his own prayer in that state is permissible.

Cell commitment: repetition is tied to purity and removable impurity, not to every burden of travel.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The praying self against the leading self, in one and the same inadequate garment.

Column A: His own prayer
- Verdict: stands under necessity
- What decides it: whether the act can be fulfilled
- Who bears the consequence: himself alone
- What the garment is: a private constraint

Column B: His leadership of others
- Verdict: refused while he wears only a small cloth on his shoulders
- What decides it: the dignity the front position requires
- Who bears the consequence: everyone behind him
- What the garment is: a public representation

Shared row: the same cloth is judged twice, and private necessity carries what public representation cannot.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The three garment forms the source interprets.

Rows: the three forms.
Columns: what the phrase means | effect on the act.
- A single dense shirt: there is nothing wrong with praying in it, and if the worshipper bows, his side may become apparent | permitted.
- Like the wings of the hook: a garment wrapped around the back with the limbs resting on the shoulders, when no other option is available | permitted under necessity.
- Ishtimal al-samma': a single garment wrapped so that it becomes like a sewn garment with both hands underneath it | forbidden.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. Ishtimal al-samma' dissected, part by part, against the act each part disables.

Whole: one garment, wrapped so that it becomes like a sewn garment with both hands underneath it.

Parts, with position and what each one blocks:
- The middle, placed over the head or shoulders. Position: the top of the figure. Blocks: free movement of the upper body.
- One end held in the left hand against the body. Position: the left side. Blocks: the left arm.
- The other end turned from the right side behind him and gathered with the right hand. Position: the right side, passing behind. Blocks: the right arm.
- Both hands enclosed underneath. Position: the centre, hidden. Blocks: the hands entirely.

Disabled acts, set beside the figure:
- He cannot place his hands on the ground as required
- He cannot bow or prostrate correctly
- He cannot complete the raising of his hands

End state: such a prayer is incomplete and does not earn reward.

Annotation: the objection is not unfamiliarity. Dress is not an aesthetic layer over worship; it either enables obedience or obstructs it.

Diagram type: Process flow. The single test the source keeps applying to anything worn, held, or nearby.

Start: a thing present on or near the praying body.

Does it prevent the motions of prayer?
  ↓ if yes, the act fails
Does it block the conditions of prayer?
  ↓ if yes, the act fails
Does it obstruct the formation of the letters?
  ↓ if yes, the act fails
Does it carry impurity that is known and removable?
  ↓ if yes, the act fails
Does it capture the eye toward an image?
  ↓ if yes, the space is refused
Does it leave the covering insufficient?
  ↓ if yes, the prayer may not proceed

End state, if none of these: the prayer stands, and freedom from the encumbrance is still preferable.

Annotation: Jaʿfar ibn Manṣūr al-Yaman, may Allah sanctify him, in his book on obligatory laws and the limits of religion, runs the same test over an earring, a dirham, and a thing held in the hand that constrains without preventing.

Diagram type: Named axis 2x2. The image rulings, organised by where the figure sits and whether the worshipper can see it.

Axes:
- Horizontal: position of the figure, from Outside the place of prostration on the left to At the place of prostration on the right
- Vertical: visibility to the worshipper, from Unseen at the bottom to Seen at the top

Quadrants:
- Bottom left, unseen and away from the place of prostration: a dirham bearing a picture kept in a money-pouch or garment, and pure. Permissible, because nothing draws the eye and nothing is impure.
- Top left, seen and away from the place of prostration: a decorated rug or furnishing that does not distract. Permissible, except that one should not pray in the direction of images.
- Bottom right, unseen at the place of prostration: a figure the worshipper cannot see. The Musannaf permits it, because attention is untouched.
- Top right, seen at the place of prostration: a figure where the forehead goes. Refused. Amīr al-Mu'minīn Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, says a man should not prostrate on a picture, nor on a carpet on which he stands facing one.

Reading of the axes: a picture at the place of prostration competes with the forehead's act, and a visible figure draws the eye into an image when the body should face Allah.

Annotation: the deciding factors are distraction, direction, placement, visibility, and concealment with purity. A man should not tie a dirham bearing a picture into his garment while praying.

## Garment And Ground, Earth And Impurity

Diagram type: Process flow. Impurity on the garment, traced by what the worshipper knew and what he had.

Start: a garment worn into prayer.

Branch on ownership and source:
- The clothing of Muslim boys and Muslim slaves is permissible, unless the garments show impurity or one does not pray in them.

Branch on knowledge after the fact:
- He finishes the prayer and notices impurity on his garment, and he knew of it beforehand. The prayer is invalidated and must be repeated, whether he was making up a missed prayer or not.

Branch on having no alternative:
- He has nothing but an impure garment, cannot obtain another, and has no water to purify it. He prays in it, then repeats the prayer when he finds another garment or water to purify it.

Branch on uncertainty:
- He has two garments, one of them impure, and does not know which. He prays twice, once in each garment, intending the obligatory prayer with each.

End states:
- Obligation is never delayed into nonexistence
- Constraint is prayed through now and repaired later
- Uncertainty is answered by doubling the act, not by ignoring impurity

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. Minor contact and place.

Rows: the three cases.
Columns: the ruling.
- A small amount of milk, fat, or eggs from camels, cows, sheep, and chickens contacting clothing or body: permissible.
- Prayer in camel pens: there is no prayer in them.
- Prayer in sheep pens after they have been swept and sprinkled with water: there is nothing wrong with it.

Diagram type: Hierarchy. What qualifies as a place for the forehead, sorted by kind.

- Level 1: Fit for prostration
  - Level 2: The ground itself
    - Level 3: Earth, which the Prophet, peace be upon him, named mosque and means of purification
  - Level 2: What grows from the ground
    - Level 3: Grass and the like, named by the interpretation of the Sharīʿa
  - Level 2: What has been changed into fitness
    - Level 3: Plaster and the like burned with the dead, counted as part of the mosque and the house, since fire has purified it
- Level 1: Unfit for prostration
  - Level 2: What is not earth in the complete sense
    - Level 3: Stone, milk, and the like
  - Level 2: What is ordinarily consumed
    - Level 3: A heap of wheat or barley, anything edible, bread
  - Level 2: What captures attention
    - Level 3: A picture, and a carpet on which one stands facing one

The Prophet, peace be upon him, on the ground of prostration:

> The earth has been made for me a mosque and a means of purification.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. What each surface ruling carries beyond the surface.

Rows: the four surfaces.
Columns: the attached consequence.
- Stone, milk, or something similar: not permissible, and only the obligatory prayer is binding upon him, the nafl and sunnah prayers are not.
- Stone and plaster, in the answers of Idris ibn al-Hasan, may Allah sanctify him: the Prophet's saying is the measure, and what is not earth in the complete sense cannot be prayed on.
- Plaster burned with the dead: fire has purified it, and it becomes part of the mosque and the house.
- A heap of wheat or barley, anything edible, or bread: Amīr al-Mu'minīn Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, says a man should not prostrate on such things.

Annotation: the act needs ground, not appetite. A substance can change its legal character through treatment, which is why the law attends to fire, water, thickness, and material fact.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The chapter's two closing voices on the body before Allah.

Column A: The Prophet, peace be upon him
- What he names: every prayer in which the Fatiha is not recited is incomplete and deficient
- Where he locates the defect: the recited centre
- What the ruling protects: the words the act is built on

Column B: Amīr al-Mu'minīn Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him
- What he names: none should stand before Allah Almighty in a state of distress, a man should not pray in a shirt alone without a waist-wrapper, and it is not permissible to pray in a garment worn loosely around the neck or immodestly
- Where he locates the defect: the covered and composed body
- What the ruling protects: the bearing the words are spoken in

Shared row: the recited centre and the covered body are one concern.

## Closing

Diagram type: Visual metaphor. The opening figure redrawn, the same three rings around the same standing body, each ring now carrying the refusal the chapter established for it.

- Innermost ring, the act. New label: the refusal to treat devotion as permission for ordinary behaviour. What it holds: the fourteen doors, and the invalidation that follows a chosen breach of them.
- Middle ring, the covering. New label: the refusal to let dress become an aesthetic layer. What it holds: the garment that binds the hands, the covering that does not suffice, the ornament worn without reason.
- Outer ring, the material world. New label: the refusal to treat surrounding material as neutral. What it holds: the figure at the place of prostration, the impurity known and left, the heap of wheat beneath the forehead.
- The centre, unchanged: the forehead's contact with the ground.
- The ring the close adds, drawn outside all three: the moment after the salutation, where supplication carries roots, foundations, gestures, repentance, praise, blessing, and approach through the imams of the religion.

Reading: the opening figure showed how far a breach travels inward. This one shows what each ring refuses, and adds the ring the salutation opens.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. Severity and mercy, held together rather than traded off.

Column A: Where the chapter is severe
- Prayer cannot be casually interrupted
- It cannot be wrapped in disabling garments
- It cannot be performed knowingly with impurity
- It cannot be directed toward distracting images
- It cannot be placed upon unsuitable surfaces

Column B: Where the chapter is merciful
- Unavoidable discomfort is excused
- Constrained travel is recognised
- Hidden objects may be tolerated when they do not obstruct the act
- Necessity permits a person to pray as he is
- Later repair is possible when water or pure clothing becomes available

Shared row: prayer is guarded not because Allah needs perfect fabric, flawless rooms, or empty hands, but because the servant needs a form strong enough to train desire, discipline the body, and make supplication rise with righteous action.
