# Zakat, Obligation and Intention

## Where this episode picks up

Annotated structure. Whole: one sentence used twice, and everything the chapter puts between the two uses.

> There is no prayer for one who does not pay zakat.
> Attribution: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him and his family.

- Part 1, the first placement: near the opening, immediately after the arithmetic of the fifth and the tithe.
- Part 2, the body between: the exclusion of a household, the scope of the obligation, an address delivered in council, four submitted questions with replies.
- Part 3, the second placement: the final word of the chapter, followed by one further sentence.

> The one who withholds zakat is in the Fire.
> Attribution: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him and his family.

- Annotation on the frame: the sentence is not about prayer. It is about what an unpaid charity does backwards through a life to an act of worship that was, by every visible measure, correct.

Contrast pair. What this chapter settles, against what it deliberately leaves standing.

Column A: settled here, before any sum is named
- Who is barred from receiving
- What the obligation falls upon
- Where the payment has to be delivered
- What counts as withholding when the money visibly changed hands
- What a man is if he believes everything and spends nothing

Column B: not touched here, deferred to the next chapter
- How many sheep are owed on forty
- What a mine yields
- What a household pays at the end of the fast
- What happens to a man's obligation when he is himself in debt

Shared row: the thresholds are named and the categories fixed in this chapter. The counting belongs to the next one.

Genealogy chain. Every claim in the chapter arrives attributed. One chain per line, direction of transmission left to right.

- The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family → our master Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman, may Allah sanctify him and grant us his intercession, in the book the manuscript names as the book of sub nursing → the opening section.
- Allah Almighty → our master Zayd ibn Muhammad, may Allah be pleased with him, in the work the source calls the hidden message → the list of ten.
- Mawlana the Commander of the Faithful Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, in his four hundred manners → the closing aphorism of the first section.
- Our master and Mawlana, the owner of the councils of al-Mustansiriyya, may God's peace be upon its owner → the twelfth council, delivered as a formal address → the longest passage in the chapter.
- The Ahl al-Bayt, peace be upon them, by successive transmission → the book The Pillars of Islam → the book of questions and answers arranged according to the order of the chapters of that book → the final section.

Hierarchy tree. The two source headings, and what each one carries.

- Heading 1: the intentions in giving zakat and related matters
  - The exclusion of the Prophet's household, the nesting of the fifth and the tithe, the scope stated as ten things, charity and zakat paired as medicine and fortification
- Heading 2: the hardening and the making of zakat
  - The twelfth council rendered whole, four submitted questions with their named replies, the ruling on a misdirected payment, the closing censure and the two prophetic sentences

Process flow. How the chapter moves, in three passes.

Start: the Book of Funerals has closed, leaving the reader outdoors among people he can no longer help.
  ↓
Pass 1: the obligation itself. Who is excluded, how the fifth contains the tithe, what the payment falls upon.
  ↓
Pass 2: the twelfth council, an address that reads zakat as a structure of sevens and then fills each seven in.
  ↓
Pass 3: failure. Concealment, the narrowed prohibition, the misdirected payment, the man with sound belief and closed hands.
  ↓
End: the chapter puts the reader back inside a household with a ledger in it, and tells him the first family he thought of may not be paid from it.

Visual metaphor. Relation: what kind of act zakat is in this chapter. Metaphor: a parcel with an address on it, not a weight removed from a scale.

- The weight reading, rejected here: zakat is a quantity surrendered, and the obligation is met when the holdings drop by the right amount.
- The delivery reading, held throughout: zakat is a delivery to be completed, and a delivery has an address.
- Consequence 1: a partial payment counts as concealment rather than shortfall.
- Consequence 2: a man who paid the wrong recipient may take the money back by any means available to him.
- Consequence 3: the council states as a bare obligation that the payment must be brought to the Imam.

Reading: money that leaves a man's hands has not thereby been paid. It has only left his hands.

## Khums Above Tithe

Annotated structure. Whole: the prohibition that opens the section by removing a family from the system.

> No charity is permissible for me, nor for my family.
> Attribution: the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, cited by our master Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman.

- Part 1, what the rule is about: a channel, not a condition. It makes no reference to whether the family has anything.
- Part 2, what it costs: the household at the summit of the community's sanctity is the one barred from the institution by which that community moves wealth toward need.
- Part 3, what it tells the giver: the most honourable recipients he can think of may not have it.
- Annotation: whatever else the people of the House are owed, they are not owed this.

Contrast pair. Two routes to the same household, and only one of them is open.

Column A: charity, the closed route
- Trigger: visible need
- Mechanism: the reflex of a man who sees need and responds
- Source of funds: the zakat channel
- Status for the people of the House: forbidden absolutely

Column B: provision, the open route
- Trigger: the caller's obligation upon one who is able to provide
- Mechanism: what he will beyond instinct, done outwardly
- Source of funds: abundant wealth, drawn from obligatory rights and voluntary acts at once
- Status for the people of the House: this is what he must do for them

Shared row: the household is not left unsupported, it is moved to a different account. The reflex is what has been disqualified, because the reflex routes through charity and charity is shut.

Visual metaphor. Relation: how the fifth and the tithe stand to one another. Metaphor: nested containment, the larger vessel holding the smaller.

> Whoever fulfils the khums is spared from paying the tithe, except for voluntary giving; but whoever pays the tithe alone is not spared from paying the fifth that he must fulfil, because the tithe does not encompass the khums as the khums encompasses the tithe.
> Attribution: the source text of the opening section.

- Outer vessel: the khums, the fifth.
- Inner vessel: the tithe, the tenth, wholly contained by the outer.
- Outside both vessels: voluntary giving, which no containment reaches.

Reading: discharging the larger has already discharged what the smaller would have covered. The smaller cannot work in reverse.

Comparison matrix. The two payments, and what each one settles.

| Payment | Relative size | Does it discharge the other | What remains owed | What remains available |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Khums, the fifth | Larger | Yes, it encompasses the tithe | Nothing of the tithe | Voluntary giving, untouched |
| Tithe, the tenth | Smaller | No, it does not encompass the fifth | The balance of the fifth, still standing | Voluntary giving, untouched |

Annotation on the exemption: what is optional stays optional and stays available. The exemption reaches only the required tenth, never the offered one, so the arithmetic is a reason to stop owing, never a reason to stop giving.

Annotated structure. Whole: the sentence the chapter is built to explain, dissected against what a legal censure normally says.

> There is no prayer for one who does not pay zakat.
> Attribution: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him and his family.

- Part 1, what it does not say: that the prayer is diminished.
- Part 2, what it does not say: that the prayer is unrewarded.
- Part 3, what it does not say: that the prayer is accepted with difficulty.
- Part 4, what it says: that there is no prayer, that the category is empty for him.
- Annotation: prayer is the act this series has spent thirteen chapters regulating down to the placement of a garment, and here a failure that happens nowhere near the prayer mat removes the whole of it.
- Cross reading: the two pillars are not separate duties a man may discharge one at a time and score independently. One can void the other in retrospect.

## Khums Above Tithe, The Scope Of The Obligation

Hierarchy tree. The scope as the hidden message gives it: ten things, falling into three kinds without being sorted into them.

> Allah Almighty has made zakat obligatory on ten things: first, gold; second, silver; third, camels; fourth, cows; fifth, sheep; sixth, dates; seventh, raisins; eighth, wheat; ninth, barley; tenth, grains.
> Attribution: our master Zayd ibn Muhammad, may Allah be pleased with him, in the hidden message.

- Kind 1: metals, stored value that does nothing on its own
  - Gold
  - Silver
- Kind 2: livestock, wealth that eats, breeds and can die
  - Camels
  - Cows
  - Sheep
- Kind 3: crops, wealth that appears once a year and then has to be kept
  - Dates
  - Raisins
  - Wheat
  - Barley
  - Grains

Annotation on the boundary: nothing on the list is a house, a tool, a garment or a coin in circulation. Everything on it either holds value or produces it.

Forward note: the twelfth council gives a different list, of seven, and the difference is not a disagreement about how much is owed.

Contrast pair. The closing aphorism, read on its two halves.

> Cure your people through charity, and fortify your wealth with zakat.
> Attribution: Mawlana the Commander of the Faithful Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, in his four hundred manners.

Column A: charity
- Target: people, outward
- Image: medicine
- What it acts on: a community that is ill
- Direction of benefit: away from the giver

Column B: zakat
- Target: the giver's own property
- Image: fortification
- What it acts on: the wall around what he keeps
- Direction of benefit: back toward the giver

Shared row: the payment that removes a portion of a man's wealth secures the remainder. The money handed over is the defence of everything not handed over, not a loss offset by reward later. A man who withholds it has left his wealth in the open.

## The Twelfth Council's Reckoning

Timeline. The curriculum the address names, in the order the assembly received it.

Axis: the sequence of recitation in the councils, as the twelfth council states it.
- Position 1: guardianship, the foundations of allegiance.
- Position 2: purity, the whole law of purification.
- Position 3: prayer, the shape of the act itself.
- Position 4: zakat, introduced here as the next right.
Period bands: completed business, then the present recitation.

> You have heard what was recited to you above in jurisprudence concerning guardianship, purity, and prayer, what constitutes the sincerity of testimony, which is the foundation of religion and worship and the first cause of mercy and happiness. Now hear what we recite to you: the mention of zakat, which is one of its rights as established above.
> Attribution: the twelfth council of al-Mustansiriyya.

Reading of the axis: those doctrines were taught in their place and are not re-opened. The address invokes them as completed business.

Hierarchy tree. What generates what, in the council's account.

- Level 1: testimony, the foundation of religion and worship, the first cause of mercy and happiness
  - Level 2: the rights belonging to testimony
    - Level 3: guardianship
    - Level 3: purity
    - Level 3: prayer
    - Level 3: zakat
      - Level 4: its noble boundaries, to be known
      - Level 4: its rights, to be fulfilled
      - Level 4: its seven obligatory duties, to be performed

Annotation: zakat is not the fourth item on a list of five. It is one of the things a single testimony demands, and the pillars are described as the contents of an oath.

Process flow. What sincerity consists of, in the council's ordering.

Start: knowing the noble boundaries.
  ↓
Then: fulfilling the rights.
  ↓
Then: performing the seven obligatory duties that are the pillars of Islam.
  ↓
End: sincerity, as this address defines it.

Annotation: knowledge comes first, before payment. A man who hands over the correct sum without knowing its boundaries has not been credited with sincerity in the council's sense, which is why a chapter on intention spends its length enumerating thresholds. The enumeration is the intention.

Annotated structure. Whole: the numerical correspondence the address asserts, its signature move.

> In zakat likewise there are seven obligatory duties and twelve years, like the number of chapters of testimony and its letters.
> Attribution: the twelfth council of al-Mustansiriyya.

- Part 1, the pillar side: seven obligatory duties, twelve years.
- Part 2, the testimony side: the number of its chapters, the number of its letters.
- Part 3, the precedent: the same council collection made the same manoeuvre for purity earlier in the series, matching its obligations and sunnahs to the chapters of the witnessing and its letters.
- Annotation on method: a pillar is shown to be the numerical image of the testimony that generates it.
- Annotation on the manuscript: twelve years is what the source gives and is left as it stands. The correspondence asserted is a count, not a span of time.

Annotated structure. Whole: seven words with no argument attached, on which every ruling in the chapter's last section depends.

> Zakat must be brought to the Imam.
> Attribution: the twelfth council of al-Mustansiriyya.

- Part 1, what it is not: a duty discharged by a reduction in a man's holdings.
- Part 2, what it is: a duty discharged by a delivery to a named recipient.
- Part 3, where the recipient stands: the authority at the start of the process, not the poor man at the end of it.

Comparison matrix. The two enumerations of what zakat is taken from, set beside each other.

| Item | The hidden message, ten things | The twelfth council, seven things |
|---|---|---|
| Gold | First | First |
| Silver | Second | Second |
| Camels | Third | Third |
| Cows | Fourth | Fourth |
| Sheep | Fifth | Fifth |
| Dates | Sixth | Folded into crops |
| Raisins | Seventh | Folded into crops |
| Wheat | Eighth | Folded into crops |
| Barley | Ninth | Folded into crops |
| Grains | Tenth | Folded into crops |
| Crops, as one kind | Not named as a kind | Sixth |
| Minerals | Absent | Seventh |

Reading: the council has one word where the other list had five, and then admits a kind the other did not carry, wealth taken out of the ground rather than grown on it. Seven is not an abbreviation of ten. It is the same field sorted by kind rather than by commodity, so the two enumerations disagree about taxonomy and agree about scope, except at the mine.

Comparison matrix. The disbursement list, eight names divided into seven categories.

| Category | Name or names | What the payment is for, and what it is not |
|---|---|---|
| First | The poor and the needy, one heading with two words in it | Zakat is given to them |
| Second | Those who work in its collection | The wages of collection, so the fund finances its own administration, not unpaid service |
| Third | Those whose hearts are to be reconciled | Paid from it for protection, securing a disposition rather than relieving a hardship |
| Fourth | Those in bondage | Buying a person out of a condition |
| Fifth | Those burdened with debts incurred in settling the affairs of the community | Reimbursement of men who went into debt on everyone's behalf, not debtors in general |
| Sixth | In the way of Allah, glossed immediately as jihad | A collective obligation, owed by everybody and discharged by somebody |
| Seventh | The wayfarer who is cut off and stranded | Rescue of a man whose problem is that he is stuck, not a means test |

Annotation on the arithmetic: eight names divided into seven categories is a way of saying that one category holds two names, and the list shows which.

Annotation on the sixth: this is the same category the funeral prayer was placed in earlier in the series.

Annotation on the seventh: a man of means far from his means is a legitimate recipient.

## The Twelfth Council's Reckoning, Thresholds And Protections

Annotated structure. Whole: the list of sunnahs, reproduced with two irregularities the chapter does not repair.

- Part 1: it is introduced as two sunnahs.
- Part 2: eleven items follow.
- Part 3: the numbering runs one to six, skips seven, and resumes at eight.
- Annotation: both features are in the source and both are left as they stand, because straightening them would require deciding what the missing item was.

Contrast pair. What the eleven items actually contain, sorted by who they restrain.

Column A: floors, which restrain the payer
- Timing: no zakat is taken until after the completion of the year
- Gold: the nisab is twenty dinars
- Silver: the nisab is two hundred dirhams
- Camels: the nisab is five head
- Cows: the nisab is thirty head
- Sheep: the nisab is forty head
- Effect: below each number the obligation does not arise, so small holdings are outside the system entirely rather than taxed lightly

Column B: protections, which restrain the collector
- Quality: zakat is taken from cattle of middling quality, neither the finest nor the lowest
- Headcount, inflation: a joint must not be added to the herd
- Headcount, division: nor must the community be divided
- Breeding: the stud animal must not be taken from them
- Effect: the collector may not select the best animal, the owner may not offload the worst, and two manipulations of a headcount are closed off, which tells us both were tried

Shared row: the stud exemption is reasoned from what the animal does rather than what it is worth. Remove the breeding male and the assessment collects a year's value while destroying the herd's capacity to produce any. The rule keeps the assessment from consuming the thing assessed.

Contrast pair. The eleventh item, which reverses the direction of every rule before it.

Column A: every other threshold in the list
- Function of the number: a wall
- Below it: nothing is owed
- Clock: the completion of the year
- Object measured: a stored quantity weighed against a threshold

Column B: crops that have not reached the nisab
- Function of the number: five awsaq, measured with the squeeze and the fist
- Below it: charity is still given by the owner
- Clock: the day of his harvest
- Object measured: the crop as it comes in, before it has become a stored quantity at all

> and give its due on the day of its harvest.
> Attribution: chapter 6, verse 141, quoted by the twelfth council.

Shared row: a farmer whose crop never reaches the taxable minimum still has a due, and he owes it on the day the crop comes in.

Named-axis 2x2. The boundary of the whole institution, as the closing exclusion draws it.

Axes:
- Horizontal: what the thing does with value (holds it → produces it)
- Vertical: how the law treats it (exempt → assessed)

Quadrants:
- Top-left, holds value and exempt: houses, jewelry, and whatever is for use. A house shelters and a bracelet is worn, and neither produces anything that could be shared out.
- Bottom-right, produces value and assessed: livestock that breed, crops that come in, metals that accumulate, mines that yield.
- Top-right, produces value and exempt: empty in this chapter. Nothing that increases is released from the obligation.
- Bottom-left, holds value and assessed: empty in this chapter. This is the quadrant the exclusion exists to keep empty.

> There is no zakat on houses, jewelry, and whatever is for use.
> Attribution: the twelfth council of al-Mustansiriyya.

Reading of the axes: the line does not run between necessity and luxury, since jewelry is exempt. It runs between wealth that grows and wealth that is held. Zakat falls on increase.

Annotated structure. Whole: the council's closing gesture, which declines to finish.

> Each of these words carries many meanings and effects of wisdom.
> Attribution: the twelfth council of al-Mustansiriyya.

- Part 1, what has been delivered: the outward form, correct as far as it goes.
- Part 2, what has been withheld: the inward reading.
- Part 3, who it is withheld from: an audience given the boundaries and told that knowing them is what sincerity consists of.
- Annotation: this is the collection's habitual closing gesture, and it means what it says.

## Concealment and Misdirection

Comparison matrix. Four submitted questions and their named replies, taken from the book of questions and answers arranged according to the order of the chapters of the book The Pillars of Islam.

| Question | What the questioner brings | The reply |
|---|---|---|
| First | A contradiction: it is forbidden to conceal zakat from the imam, yet the payment is handed to him openly | The hadith refers to one who pays some of the zakat while withholding the rest, he has abandoned an obligatory duty |
| Second | Does the saying that no charity is permissible for the people of the House cover the Imams only, or all his descendants through Fatima, peace be upon her | What is meant by this is the Imams, peace be upon them |
| Third | What is stated in The Pillars of Islam, that charity does not resolve the five for one of the men who bore the beauty, and what is meant by the sling | Either a punishment from an unjust authority, or blood money for a competent woman |
| Fourth | The covenant written for a man of obedience between the island and Kufa and the land of Damascus, whose charity was going to the agents of Damascus | Zakat is not required from those who are not Shi'ites who have been seized by the enemy |

Annotated structure. Whole: the first objection, which is exact.

- Part 1, the premise: concealment is a property of things done out of sight.
- Part 2, the fact: this payment is by definition done in the open, delivered in person to a named authority.
- Part 3, the fork: either the prohibition is empty, or it names something other than hiding.
- Annotation on the answer: the concealment is not of the money but of the remainder, and what hides it is the part that was handed over.

Contrast pair. Two ways of not paying, and which one the prohibition names.

Column A: the man who pays nothing
- Visibility: he has withheld visibly
- Evidence produced: none
- What conceals the shortfall: nothing
- Named by this hadith: no

Column B: the man who pays most of it
- Visibility: the withholding is covered
- Evidence produced: a receipt
- What conceals the shortfall: the part that was handed over
- Named by this hadith: yes

Shared row: what the answer names as abandoned is the obligatory duty in whole, not the fraction unpaid. A partial delivery is not most of a delivery, it is a failure dressed in evidence.

Annotated structure. Whole: the second reply, read against the first section of the chapter.

> We are the people of the House, no charity is permissible for us.
> Attribution: the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, as recorded in the Book of Pillars and quoted by the questioner.

- Part 1, what is narrowed: descent from Fatima, peace be upon her, is not by itself what closes the channel. The bar falls on those who hold the office.
- Part 2, the symmetry it produces: the one household forbidden to draw on the fund is the household to which the fund is brought.
- Annotation: the ranks this series established earlier do the work without being re-argued. The position that receives on the community's behalf may not receive on its own.

Annotated structure. Whole: the third exchange, which the manuscript has damaged.

- Part 1, the question: rendered as it stands, not reconstructed. Its own wording has not survived intact.
- Part 2, reading one: a punishment from an unjust authority.
- Part 3, reading two: blood money for a competent woman.
- Annotation: each reading names a liability imposed from outside a man's own dealings, and each is a charge he might hope to meet out of a fund established for the burdened. The answer does not choose between them.

## Concealment and Misdirection, The Delivery Doctrine

Process flow. The fourth case, from the questioner's reading of the covenant to the reply's reading of it.

Start: the covenant written by the Commander of the Faithful for Hanaf ibn Salim, one of the people of obedience to us, between the island and Kufa and the land of Damascus. The manuscript gives the name in two forms, Hanaf ibn Salim and al-Muhanaf ibn Sulaym, and both are kept.
  ↓
Claim reported: his charity is being directed to the agents of Damascus while he is in our possession, and it is forbidden for our horses and men to intercept it.
  ↓
Questioner's branch, the reading he arrives with:
  - He has no right to give the zakat of his wealth to our enemy.
  - Is it obligatory for the Sultan to forbid the land of India with his forces and men.
  - Does he owe zakat or not.
  ↓
Reply's branch, the reading of the same document:
  - The covenant describes people from whom the payment is not demanded at all, because they are held by a power standing between them and the party they would deliver to.
  ↓
End: zakat is not required from those who are not Shi'ites who have been seized by the enemy. Where delivery is impossible because someone else holds the ground, the obligation does not stand as a debt against the person who cannot cross it.

Annotation: the document the questioner read as a licence being abused turns out to be a relief. He arrived wanting an enforcement and was answered with an exemption.

Process flow. The delivery doctrine at its strongest, stated as the paths a payment can take.

> One who handed over zakat to someone whom Allah did not command him to hand it to may reclaim it by any means, until he hands it over to the guardian or to whoever receives it by his command.
> Attribution: the successive transmissions from the Ahl al-Bayt, peace be upon them, recorded in the book of Pillars and elsewhere.

Start: a man holds wealth on which zakat has fallen.
  ↓
Branch A: he brings it to the Imam, or to the guardian, or to whoever receives by his command.
  → End state: the delivery is complete. The duty is discharged.
  ↓
Branch B: he hands over part and keeps the rest.
  → End state: concealment. The obligatory duty is abandoned in whole.
  ↓
Branch C: he hands it to a party Allah did not command him to hand it to.
  → The money has not been paid. It is still his.
  → He may reclaim it by any means, a phrase conceding he may have to take it rather than ask for it, then deliver it to the guardian.
  → End state: the delivery he failed to make is completed. The recovery is not permitted for his benefit.
  ↓
Branch D: an enemy holds the ground between him and the authority.
  → End state: the payment is not demanded. No debt stands against the crossing he could not make.

Reading: every other obligation here could be satisfied by a man who parted with the correct amount. This one cannot. A sum that reached the wrong party is, in the eyes of the ruling, still in his hands.

Named-axis 2x2. The closing censure, placed against what it does and does not concede.

Axes:
- Horizontal: inward conviction and outward prayer (absent → present)
- Vertical: spending of what Allah has provided, the zakat on wealth and the fifth on gains (absent → present)

Quadrants:
- Top-right, prayer present and spending present: the obligation completed. The purchase made and paid off.
- Bottom-right, prayer present and spending absent: the man the council describes. He has bought himself, yet failed to pay off the debt he owed.
- Bottom-left, prayer absent and spending absent: outside the frame of this censure, which is addressed to a believer.
- Top-left, prayer absent and spending present: not treated here.

Reading of the placement: the council grants him the outward prayer and the inward conviction both, which is more than most censures concede.

Visual metaphor. Relation: the standing of a believer who does not pay. Metaphor: the contract of a slave who purchases his own freedom.

- The purchase: real, and made. He has bought himself.
- The debt the purchase creates: also real, and outstanding.
- The instalments: zakat on his wealth, and one fifth of his gains.
- The diagnosis: default, not disbelief. He owns something he has not finished buying.

> and who spend out of what We have provided for them.
> Attribution: quoted by the councils of al-Mustansiriyya. The source does not name the chapter or verse and none is supplied here.

Reading: the verse fixes the diagnosis by quoting the description he fails to match, which locates the failure at the disbursement rather than at the belief.

Annotated structure. Whole: the two sentences the chapter ends on.

> There is no prayer for one who does not pay zakat.
> Attribution: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him and his family.

> The one who withholds zakat is in the Fire.
> Attribution: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him and his family.

- Part 1: the first sentence returns for the second time, closing the frame it opened.
- Part 2: the second sentence is new, and says where such a man is.

## What this episode lands

Hierarchy tree. What the chapter has settled, in three headings.

- Heading 1: the shape of the duty
  - The Prophet's household is barred from charity absolutely, the exclusion falling on the Imams, and what is owed to them travels by another road, deliberately and out of abundance
  - The fifth contains the tenth and the tenth does not contain the fifth, while voluntary giving stays outside the calculation entirely
  - Zakat falls on wealth that increases, never on a house, a piece of jewelry, or anything a person simply uses
- Heading 2: the structure, as the twelfth council supplied it
  - Zakat must be brought to the Imam, taken from seven kinds, the same field the earlier list of ten described, sorted differently and extended to minerals
  - Disbursed under eight names in seven categories, with thresholds keeping small holdings outside the system
  - Rules keep the collector from the best animal, from padding or splitting a herd, and from the stud that makes the herd possible
  - Below every threshold there is still a due on the day of the harvest
  - Delivered as boundaries to be known, then closed with words said to carry meanings the council was not unfolding there
- Heading 3: failure
  - Concealment is what a partial payment does, and the duty abandoned is the whole of it
  - A man prevented from reaching the authority by an enemy holding the ground owes no debt for the crossing he could not make
  - A man who delivered to the wrong party has not paid at all and may recover the money by any means in order to deliver it properly
  - A man with sound belief and closed hands has bought himself on credit and stopped paying

Annotated structure. Whole: what the manuscript damaged, kept rather than smoothed.

- Part 1: the title of the book of sub nursing, which survives unevenly.
- Part 2: the question about charity and the sling, whose answer offers two readings and settles on neither.
- Part 3: the name in the covenant, which reaches us in two forms, Hanaf ibn Salim and al-Muhanaf ibn Sulaym, and both are kept.
- Part 4: the council's list of sunnahs, introduced as two, running to eleven items, skipping the seventh.
- Annotation: the count is left as the source leaves it, in every case.

Contrast pair. This chapter against the next.

Column A: this chapter, before the arithmetic
- Question answered: what the obligation is
- Numbers used: as thresholds and boundaries

Column B: the next chapter, the counting
- Question answered: what the obligation costs
- Numbers used: as sums owed

Shared row: the thresholds have been named and the categories fixed. The counting is what has not yet been touched.
