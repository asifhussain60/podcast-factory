# Friday, Eid, and Prayer Corrections

## Where this episode opens

Annotated structure. Whole: three pressures on worship, as three panels side by side.

- Left panel, Friday prayer: public authority and lawful attendance. The pressure is other people.
- Center panel, the two Eid prayers: festivity against disclosure. The pressure is joy.
- Right panel, the prostration of omission: a real error against an unsettled mind. The pressure is his own memory.
- Printed beneath all three: when worship takes place under pressure, how does the community know where the valid act is?
- Printed along the base, as settled ground not reopened here: the summons, attentive prayer, sacred times, ranked leadership, following without fracture, shared congregational boundaries, prayer's shape, speech and motion, garment and ground.

Named-axis 2x2. The space this chapter occupies, committed here and redrawn at the close.

Axes:
- Horizontal: Site of the act (Private worshipper to Public congregation)
- Vertical: Register of the ruling (Outward form to Inward meaning)

Drawn as a loop inside the frame, the chapter's method of accumulation: a ruling, a condition, an exception, then, for some cases only, an inward meaning, and the loop restarts at the next ruling. Friday and Eid take the inward branch, at the sermon read as two displaced rak'ahs and at fitr read as emergence; omission takes it once, at the devil's nose forced down. Density is the number of turns, not their length, and no named debate is staged.

Quadrants:
- Bottom-right, Public and Outward: three miles, one pulpit, one adhan, one sermon, the congregation of five. Reason: each fixes where the authorized center stands.
- Top-right, Public and Inward: the sermon replacing two rak'ahs, the sitting as two lights, fitr as emergence. Reason: the public act is given a reading.
- Bottom-left, Private and Outward: the fifth rak'ah, the missed tashahhud, the extra bowing, the two prostrations. Reason: stage and sequence decide each.
- Top-left, Private and Inward: intention contracted in the heart, certainty undisplaced by doubt. Reason: nothing visible marks these, and the source rules on them anyway.

Genealogy chain. Voice, then the kind of source it speaks through, then what it settles. Direction of transmission left to right.

- The scholars of al-Hind and the preachers of Yemen → question-and-answer rulings → the three-mile measure, the two-cities test, the Eid takbir, intention.
- The summary of the Musannaf, the Mukhtasar, the Book of Purity → abbreviated legal summaries → the ranked places of Friday, the fifth rak'ah, the Eid ghusl, the opening takbir, addition.
- The Ahl al-Bayt, peace be upon them → transmitted reports → the report of al-Husayn ibn 'Ali, peace be upon him, the qunut, the prostrations after the salaam.
- Mawlana al-Baqir Muhammad ibn 'Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him → interpretive reading of the Shari'ah → sermon before prayer, the Nasibi and the Haruri.
- Qadi al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad, may God sanctify his soul → interpretation of the Pillars, verified by Sayyiduna Idris ibn Hassan → Eid at Mina.
- Our master Zayd ibn Muhammad ibn al-Fadl, may Allah honor him → hidden epistle → the two invitations, the two lights, the meaning of fitr.
- Ja'far ibn Mansur of Yemen, may God sanctify him → the Book of Suckling → fasting after the two feasts.
- Amir al-Mu'minin, and al-Mu'ayyad, may Allah sanctify him → sayings of earlier authorities → the three hours of petition, certainty against doubt, the warning on the sermon.
- Al-Mustansiriyya → councils of instruction → the seven places of omission, the twelve doubts.

## Friday's Gathered Order

Process flow. The three-mile ruling, with the sub-branch the questioner tested.

Start: two congregations wish to hold Friday prayer.
  ↓
Measure the distance between them.
  ↓
Branch A, three miles or more: it may be held in each, in one city or two.
  ↓
Branch B, less than three miles: only one of them holds it.
  ↓
Sub-branch tested on Branch B: the two groups are in two cities, not one. Does the ruling change.
  ↓
Answer node: it does not, whether in one city or two.
  ↓
End: Friday prayer is not multiplied because people can gather in more than one place.

Annotation: shared congregational boundaries take a spatial form, and a city wall is not one of its units.

Contrast pair. Two ways of standing behind a leader who is not rightful.

Column A: rightful following
- The leader: a true imam
- Basis: obligation and allegiance
- What the worshipper does: follows him
- What it concedes: that this man is one's imam, which is the norm

Column B: taqiyya accommodation
- The leader: a Nasibi, a Haruri, or a person of injustice
- Basis: protection under pressure
- What the worshipper does: stands at the pillars of the mosque and recites to himself as though praying alone
- What it concedes: nothing, since the man is not made an imam, and this is the narrow permitted exception

Shared row: neither permits casual withdrawal from the sermon, nor makes attendance a surrender to every leader. It is not permissible to pray behind the people of injustice except out of taqiyya.

> Whoever deliberately abandons the Friday sermon or treats it with contempt without valid excuse, whether the imam is just or unjust, will not be gathered by God.
> Attribution: al-Mu'ayyad, may Allah sanctify him.

> Do not make a Nasibi or a Haruri an imam in prayer; rather, stand at the pillars of the mosque and recite to yourself as though praying alone.
> Attribution: the Shari'ah, from Mawlana al-Baqir Muhammad ibn 'Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him.

> Taqiyya is my religion and the religion of my fathers.
> Attribution: cited as the sunna of the Ahl al-Bayt, peace be upon them.

Annotated structure. Whole: one recorded prayer line, drawn front to back.

- Front of the line: the agent of Mu'awiya, placed there in the prayer for al-Hasan ibn 'Ali, peace be upon him.
- Behind him: al-Husayn ibn 'Ali, peace be upon him, who placed him there.
- Printed beside the line: were it not for the sunna, he would have placed him ahead. The wording is compressed in the source and left as it stands.
- Printed beneath: this refers to the sunna of the Ahl al-Bayt of praying behind such figures in taqiyya.
- Annotation: from outside, the arrangement looks identical to making a Nasibi one's imam, and only the reason beside the line separates them.

Process flow. What a community does with an openly unjust preacher.

Start: a preacher has openly displayed injustice and transgression.
  ↓
Test: openly displayed, or only suspected.
  ↓
Branch A, openly displayed: in that circumstance, prayer behind him becomes obligatory.
  ↓
The people observe, refer the matter to the guardian, and await his response.
  ↓
End of Branch A: worship continues while the question is held open above them.
  ↓
Branch B, forbidden: they become doubtful after certainty and dissolve the act into their own judgment.
  ↓
End of Branch B: closed, the admonition printed across it.

> O righteous people, investigate and do not become doubtful after certainty, for you have taken refuge in the strong fortress of God.
> Attribution: the source's admonition on referral to the guardian.

Hierarchy tree. Friday prayer's ranked places, from the summary of the Musannaf.

- Level 1: the Imam of the Muslims
  - Level 2: prayer behind him, better than behind his deputies
- Level 1: the Imam delayed by a matter of Muslim welfare
  - Level 2: his mosque, designated for prayer before all others
    - Level 3: this prayer takes precedence
  - Level 2: his deputies, praying on his behalf
    - Level 3: in the nearby mosques

Reading: the designated mosque outranks the deputy even when the deputy is there and the Imam is not.

Annotated structure. Whole: the mosque on Friday, in three zones, with the binding summons inside it.

- Zone 1, at the top, the pulpit: the preacher sits where he delivers the sermon, his sitting marked as the trigger point.
- Zone 2, the floor before the pulpit: the muezzin gives the adhan, and in the hidden epistle after the Imam has ascended.
- Zone 3, a closed boundary around zones 1 and 2: the call is given in the mosque to inform the public, and the drawing stops the sound there.
- Outside it, a second mosque: whoever remains there that day has erred, and if deliberately, is penalized and commanded to perform the Friday ghusl.
- Annotation: the binding adhan is the one given in the preacher's presence when he sits, so the sound does not float in the city.

Comparison matrix. Practical Friday cases, and what each ruling preserves.

Columns: the ruling | what it preserves
- The two officers accept the pledge of allegiance at the appointed time: not cancelled | civil acts alongside ritual time
- The imam is thirsty on the pulpit: he may drink | the preacher's body inside the pulpit's formality
- He delivers the khutbah, descends, performs ablution: he may repeat the sermon or proceed to pray, both rewarded | completeness without compulsory repetition
- Distress interrupts the sitting between the sermons: the ruling turns on whether that sitting was completed | the sitting as a unit with a threshold

Comparison matrix. When the gathering fails, what act is left.

Columns: what is done | which act this now is
- Sermon delivered, the people leave, one man remaining: he prays four rak'ahs | noon prayer
- He addresses them, prays two rak'ahs with them, all leave, he remains to pray two: it depends on timing | Friday, if the decisive point was passed
- He opened the prayer and they departed before he bowed: four rak'ahs | noon prayer
- He finishes the second rak'ah, rises, then sits back down: the testimony, then the two prostrations of forgetfulness | still Friday, repaired
- He sleeps waiting, the prayer is held without him, his purity does not let him join in time: he repeats it four times | noon prayer, since sleep grants no new Friday

Reading: two questions are asked at once. Has Friday remained Friday, or become Dhuhr. Has the imam erred within the act, or has the act lost the condition that made it Friday.

Annotated structure. Whole: the Friday act, as two stacked halves of equal ritual space, from the hidden epistle of Zayd ibn Muhammad ibn al-Fadl, may Allah honor him.

- Printed above: Friday prayer points to Muhammad, peace be upon him and his family, the gate of Paradise.
- Upper half, the sermon, in the space of the two rak'ahs it replaced. Because prayer on other days is four rak'ahs, Friday was made two, the sermon replacing the other two.
  - Upper band, for the first displaced rak'ah: praise, then exhortation.
  - Lower band, for the second: supplication, then a statement from the Lord of Manifestation, which is warning.
- Dividing line between the halves: the sitting between the two sermons, read as the separation of an upper light above the line from a lower light below it. The one reminded of this stands and says peace upon the two lights.
- Lower half: the prayer, two rak'ahs, recited aloud, which is what distinguishes Friday from the noon prayer of other days.
- Printed at the first rak'ah below the line: Surah al-Jumu'ah after the Opening. At the second, before its bowing: the Opening with Surah al-Munafiqun.
- Printed at the pulpit: the preacher leans on his sword, and if he does not draw it, on a staff.
- Printed to the right, the act's two invitations: the outward, to the exoteric dimension of his Shari'ah, answered by lawful attendance and protected by the three-mile measure, the designated mosque, the binding adhan and the quorum; the inward, to the esoteric interpretation of his law, answered by silence and trust and protected by the prohibition on prompting the preacher.
- Annotation: praise and exhortation do in speech what a bowing and a prostration do in the body, supplication is the asking a rak'ah carries, and warning is what no posture can say.

Comparison matrix. The Friday congregation as pattern for the da'i's council.

Columns: who speaks | from where | what the hearers owe | what is forbidden to them
- The Friday congregation: the preacher | the pulpit | silence once he mounts and greets them, then listening as the sole obligation when the imam recites | speaking or acting without attending, turning away
- The council of the da'i: the da'i | the seat of instruction | the same sunna, said to resemble the Friday congregation | prompting the preacher, questioning him about every kind of knowledge

Shared row: both depend on silence, direction and hierarchy, and the sermon is received rather than negotiated.

> The preacher knows how to raise his children.
> Attribution: the source's reason for forbidding the respondent to prompt or question him.

Timeline. The three hours in which a petition is opened, attributed to Amir al-Mu'minin.

Axis: the course of the day and night, marked at three points in order.
- Point 1, Friday, as the sun begins to set: the one with a need petitions God.
- Point 2, when the wind blows: the gates of heaven open and mercy descends.
- Point 3, the final hour of night at daybreak: two angels call out.

> Is there anyone who repents? Is there anyone who seeks forgiveness and is forgiven? Is there anyone who asks for something? So answer the call of God.
> Attribution: the call of the two angels at daybreak.

Annotation: one opening falls inside Friday's own sacred time, and the cluster closes with the admonition to know Friday's virtue until God reunites the people on Saturday.

Annotated structure. Whole: one man crossing a threshold during a Friday sermon.

- Left of the threshold: an independent worshipper, arranging his own prayer.
- The threshold: the mosque door, entered while the preacher delivers the sermon, disrupting it and the prayer of those praying with him.
- Right of the threshold: the same man, now a follower. The preacher owns him, and he may not come before his master or pray without him.
- Annotation: the language of ownership is not to be softened. Crossing the line changes what the man is, not where he stands.

## Eid's Revealed Interior

Comparison matrix. Place against obligation, which is what Eid's rulings on geography sort.

Columns: the form the prayer takes | who is bound
- The mosque of a city, where Tashriq takes place: congregational, at sunrise, the worshipper described as radiant | the inhabitants
- Mina: not in congregation, prayed individually, each on his own | each person for himself
- The traveler: the mosque, only in Egypt | not obligated except there
- Outlying valleys, and travelers who attend by choice: the city's congregation | nobody, since witnessing it is meritorious
- A city within two leagues or less: the city's congregation | binding on the inhabitants

> Eid is not performed there in congregation but prayed individually, each person on his own.
> Attribution: Qadi al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad, may God sanctify his soul, in his interpretation of the Pillars, verified by Sayyiduna Idris ibn Hassan.

Process flow. The forgotten Eid takbir, with its decisive branch.

Start: a worshipper realizes he has omitted one or more of the Eid takbirs.
  ↓
Test: has he passed the point of bowing.
  ↓
Branch A, not yet bowed: he completes them before bowing, the prayer intact.
  ↓
Branch B, past bowing. Second test: is he certain he omitted them.
  ↓
Branch B1, certain: he performs the two prostrations of omission.
  ↓
Branch B2, not certain: this belongs to the map of doubt, not omission.
  ↓
End: repair is keyed to stage first and certainty second, which the source states twice in this movement.

Annotated structure. Whole: the count of takbir from 'Arafah to the last day of Tashriq, as one line with a disputed mark past its end.

- The line: a single count running left to right, position 1 at the left, position 23 at the right, where the source closes it.
- Beyond the right end, one mark off the line: the takbir added after the Eid prayer in the questioner's country, making twenty-four. It sits off the line because the answer does not admit it.
- First band beneath the line: the five pronouncements of the first intentions, aligned with the five prayers.
- Second band: the spiritual ten hudud.
- Third band: the ten corporeal hudud.
- Fourth band: the male of the presiding speaker and his guardian.
- Annotation: the source says the twenty-three correspond in a manner to these and does not do the arithmetic, so no sum is supplied here. The correction closes with the words, as for what has passed, take what you have received and be grateful.

Comparison matrix. Eid's boundaries, by the pressure each ruling refuses to treat as invalidating.

Columns: the case | the ruling
- An unpaid ritual debt: Eid prayed while a makeup of Fajr is owed | valid, not repeated, and the same for the funeral prayer
- An unpaid ritual debt: Eid prayed before Zakat al-Fitr is distributed | no repetition, and it may be given afterward
- A preparatory Sunnah: the ghusl of the two Eids | an annual Sunnah from daybreak until the prayer ends, or in the abridgment of the Musannaf until the prayer lapses, a valid excuse permitting prayer before that
- Distance: within two leagues, against the outlying valleys | binding on the first, meritorious for the second
- A missing unit: whoever joins the Imam at the second rak'ah | he adds another rak'ah with the additional takbirs
- A missing quorum: the minimum congregation | five, one the Imam, as with Friday, obligatory before the custodians
- A boundary of time: the span of the takbir sunna | from the completion of the fast until the prayer ends, hands raised at each takbir

> O God, forgive me, have mercy on me, and pardon me in religion and in life; You are over all things Most Powerful.
> Attribution: the qunut of the two Eids, from the transmitted reports.

Contrast pair. The two feasts, attribute by attribute.

Column A: Eid al-Fitr
- Day: after the completion of the Ramadan fast
- Adhan and iqama: neither is given
- Order of the sermon: after the prayer
- Opening of the sermon: praise, as every sermon opens
- Fasting the day after: permissible, since what ended was concealment, not abstinence
- The marriage bed: intercourse permissible on the night of Fitr and before the prayer
- The name's meaning: appearance and emergence
- What it discloses: interior realities and hidden knowledge, after the fast's silence

Column B: Eid al-Adha
- Day: the day of al-Adha, the day of the Hajj
- Adhan and iqama: the source rules only on Fitr, and supplies none for Adha
- Order of the sermon: its beginning is disputed
- Opening of the sermon: four takbirs, the one sermon that does not open with praise
- Fasting the day after: not permissible in the same way, since Adha carries its own prohibition
- The marriage bed: not ruled on here
- The name's meaning: sacrifice
- What it discloses: a disclosure announced by magnification rather than praise, which is why the different opening is read as a sign and not ornament

Shared row: recitation is aloud at both, and the opening of sermons is itself proof that the matter appears differently from what was hidden in the depths. Attribution, the marriage bed from the question-and-answer book, the fasting rows from Ja'far ibn Mansur of Yemen, may God sanctify him, in the Book of Suckling.

Visual metaphor. Why the feast is called fitr.

- Abstract relation: the appearance of what was hidden.
- Metaphor: a sealed surface that cracks and lets out what was within.
- Element 1: the earth fatarat when it cracks and what was within appears.
- Element 2: a tree fatarat when its concealed fruit appears.
- Element 3: a camel's tusk fatara when it emerges.
- Element 4: the sky, in "when the sky was split open", chapter 84, verse 1.
- Element 5: the new mushroom.
- Element 6: the fitr of God, the beginning of His creation in one day.
- Reading: fasting is silence and secrecy, so fitr signifies the appearance of interior realities and hidden knowledge.

> I have vowed to the Most Merciful a fast, so I shall not speak to anyone today.
> Attribution: chapter 19, verse 26, cited as Mary declining to reveal what she carried within her. The whole inward reading is from Zayd ibn Muhammad ibn al-Fadl, may Allah honor him.

Contrast pair. Two ways of arriving at the feast.

Column A: the one who fasts with knowledge
- What he did: fasted the days of Ramadan
- What he attains: al-Fitr, the feast, and divine pleasure
- Outcome: gain, exertion carrying knowledge

Column B: the one who fasts without knowing its limits
- What he did: fasted without knowing the meaning of fasting and its proper limits
- What he attains: nothing from God
- Outcome: loss, exertion without knowledge, figured by the source in the warning of one who prays over a blazing fire

Shared row: both performed the same visible abstinence, and only inward knowledge separates the outcomes. The takbirs, the ghusl, the sunrise prayer and the parting of Fitr from Adha all point toward emergence, so the feast is worship at that point, not release from it.

## Certainty And Omission

Contrast pair. The hinge of this movement, from the councils of instruction.

Column A: the one who commits an omission
- State of mind: certain of what he omitted
- What is established: a known lack
- Repair: the prostration of omission applies
- Risk if misread: an established defect goes unrepaired

Column B: the doubter
- State of mind: not certain of what he doubts
- What is established: nothing yet
- Repair: the map of doubt applies, and most of its cases require nothing
- Risk if misread: every clouded thought becomes a repair rite

Shared row: doubt is said to be rooted in the prostration of omission because it causes bewilderment, and many agreed cases resemble omission without being it. The test follows: if an omission is established he prostrates, otherwise he does not, which answers one who stands in for another and does not know all its places.

Comparison matrix. Structural failure against repairable slip, from the Book of Purity.

Columns: the ruling | class of defect
- The opening takbir not pronounced, by error, deliberately or ignorantly: repeat the prayer | structural failure
- No sitting after each pair of rak'ahs in four and six-rak'ah prayers: invalidated | structural failure
- An extra bowing added without having sat for the tashahhud: repeat the prayer | structural failure
- An extra prostration added: complete the prayer, then the two prostrations of omission | repairable slip
- A bowing or prostration added after sitting for the tashahhud and completing two rak'ahs: he has done so in order | judged differently from an addition before the order was secured

Reading: prayer is not repaired because the worshipper feels sorry, nor invalidated because a movement was added. The same act falls in two classes depending on what preceded it.

Contrast pair. What the Imam carries, and what he cannot.

Column A: carried by the Imam
- Scope: the follower's neglected pillars, in general, while following
- Basis: the Imam bears responsibility for the omission
- Effect: the follower's prayer stands

Column B: not carried by the Imam
- Scope: the bowing and the opening takbir
- Basis: the first thing is that the follower should not neglect those
- Effect: these are the acts that make his own participation real

Shared row: following is applied rather than reopened. A follower is carried in much, and in exactly two things he is not.

Timeline. The repairs of this movement, on the axis it runs on.

Axis: the course of one prayer, marked at five points in order.
- Point 1, before bowing: a missed Eid takbir is completed, the prayer intact.
- Point 2, after bowing: the same takbir, known with certainty, takes the two prostrations instead.
- Point 3, before the salaam: a forgotten prostration is performed, the first tashahhud given if he has not knelt.
- Point 4, at and after the salaam: a missed rak'ah with its tashahhud and salaam, then the two prostrations.
- Point 5, after leaving: no recourse.

Columns: point on the axis | outcome class
- Forgotten prostration, remembered before the salaam: point 3 | repaired inside the prayer
- The same, remembered after leaving: point 5 | no recourse
- Three rak'ahs of Dhuhr found after the salaam: point 4 | completed, then repaired
- Obligatory prayer recited in reverse order: off the axis | no repair required
- First tashahhud neglected, final one recited, remembered before the salaam: point 3 | no explicit text requires prostration
- A single surah repeated after the Opening: off the axis | likewise not stipulated
- One obligation omitted, or three: any point | not multiplied, two prostrations cover them
- Enough of 'Asr remains when the doubt arises: within the time | he acts within that time
- The sun has set, and only then the doubt: past the time | no need to make it up
- Doubt about Fajr or Maghrib: unresolved by time | answered by praying them
- The same prayer repeated without cause: off the axis | no prostrations required

Reading: the same forgotten act is repaired, completed and then repaired, or past recourse, and only its position on this line decides which.

Contrast pair. Where intention lives.

Column A: the heart
- Function: the intention is contracted here
- Status: the essence of the act
- Ruling if present alone: if he has made the intention in his heart, he has fulfilled it
- Coverage: purification, beginning prayer, fasting, any duty of God

Column B: the tongue
- Function: expression only
- Status: not the place where intention lives
- Ruling if omitted: if one omits the utterance while striving to hold the intention in the heart, the action is valid
- Coverage: repeated in questions to the scholars of India and letters to the preachers of Yemen

Shared row: deeds are accepted only with intentions, and the disagreement is only over where intention resides. Held apart: a follower who declares he intends to follow is carried only if the Imam intends the prayer on his behalf, and if the Imam gives the salaam of the dhikr while not intending it, the prayer lapses.

Named-axis 2x2. The chapter's one ruling on intent rather than stage.

Axes:
- Horizontal: how the act was done (from uncertainty to deliberately)
- Vertical: who did it (an ordinary worshipper to the imam)

Quadrants:
- Bottom-left, worshipper and from uncertainty: not knowing whether he missed two, three or four prayers, he follows the imam in praying aloud what is prayed silently. Nothing is obligatory upon him, settled or travelling.
- Bottom-right, worshipper and deliberately: the same recitation aloud, on purpose. He must perform the prostrations of omission.
- Top-left, imam and from uncertainty: he does not deliberately utter his intention. His prayer stands.
- Top-right, imam and deliberately: he utters it on purpose. His prayer is void.

On the bottom edge between the lower quadrants: the neglected obligatory recitation after the first rak'ah, with a surah recited from the beginning in the second, asked by the scholars of al-Hind and the preachers of Yemen. The answer is that this is neither stipulated nor deliberate.

Reading: everywhere else the ruling turns on where the worshipper stood. Here it turns on what he meant, and the penalty rises with office.

Annotated structure. Whole: one outward fact, a man risen at the fifth position, ruled four ways. Attribution: the Mukhtasar al-Musannaf.

- Quadrant 1, fourth witnessed, remembered while standing: he gives salaam standing and departs.
- Quadrant 2, fourth not witnessed: he sits, gives the testimony, and performs the two prostrations.
- Quadrant 3, fourth witnessed, remembered after bowing: his prayer is sound, and the additional rak'ah becomes voluntary.
- Quadrant 4, fourth not witnessed and no sitting the length of the testimony: he repeats the prayer.
- Printed across all four: the outward fact is identical, and only the secured conditions and the moment memory returned separate them.

Annotated structure. Whole: the salutation as a vertical line, every repair placed on one side of it.

- Left of the line, inside the prayer: the completions, performed before the salutation.
- On the line: the salutation.
- Right of the line: the two prostrations of omission, which the transmitted reports place here and nowhere else.
- Attached to them, the reason for the name: they force the devil's nose down when he causes forgetfulness.
- Printed beneath them, the words recited:

> In the name of Allah, and Allah is great, and I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and may Allah bless Muhammad and his family.
> Attribution: the brief testimony recited after the prostration of omission.

- Annotation: the repair sits on the far side of the line that ended the prayer, so it can never reopen what it repairs.

Hierarchy tree. The seven places of omission, six of which take the prostration. Attribution: the Mustansiriyya council.

- Level 1: the places of omission, seven in number
  - Level 2: place 1, a prostration neglected
    - Level 3: he remembers it, and performs the two prostrations
  - Level 2: place 2, the first tashahhud forgotten, standing for the third rak'ah
    - Level 3: if he has not knelt, he sits, gives the testimony, and prostrates at the salaam
  - Level 2: place 3, the omission exceeding the first tashahhud and accumulating
    - Level 3: he prostrates, completes the prayer in the most complete manner, the two prostrations upon him
  - Level 2: place 4, the second tashahhud neglected before the salutation
    - Level 3: the two prostrations
  - Level 2: place 5, two rak'ahs prayed inattentively, then two more after them
    - Level 3: the two prostrations
  - Level 2: place 6, rising inattentively from the completed final tashahhud to add a fifth rak'ah
    - Level 3: the two prostrations
  - Level 2: place 7, following an imam
    - Level 3: he bears no responsibility, the Imam bears it, which is why six of the seven take it

Comparison matrix. The twelve situations of doubt. Attribution: the Mustansiriyya council.

Block one, the five that require action. Columns: what he does | prostration
- Whether he has bowed, still in prayer: he bows | the prostration is upon him
- One rak'ah or two: the lesser certainty | as the council directs
- Two or three: certainty | as the council directs
- Three or four: he sits | the two prostrations
- Two or four: he gives salaam, then prays two rak'ahs standing | the two prostrations

Block two, the six that require nothing, each already settled by a later act performed. Columns: the later act | what is required
- The opening takbir: bowing | nothing
- The recitation: bowing | nothing
- The bowing: prostrating | nothing
- The first prostration: the second | nothing
- The prostration: the tashahhud | nothing
- Anything at all: the salutation | nothing

Reading: in block two the prayer has moved past the disputed point, and moving past it settles it. A doubt attaching to no obligation creates none: one who doubts while leading or praying supererogatory prayer has omitted no obligatory act, and one who stood for what was written, then found he was excused and believes it voluntary, treats it so without harm.

Visual metaphor. Certainty and doubt as ground and weather.

- Abstract relation: how a later uncertainty bears on an earlier settled state.
- Metaphor: solid ground with weather passing over it.
- The ground: what he was certain of. The passing weather: the doubt that visits afterward. The effect on the ground: none.

> Whoever is upon certainty and is then visited by doubt should proceed upon his certainty, for doubt does not displace certainty or diminish it.
> Attribution: the line attributed to Amir al-Mu'minin.

Process flow. The closing rule of the chapter.

Start: a worshipper doubts and does not know what he has prayed.
  ↓
Test: is he certain of anything.
  ↓
Branch A, certain of something: he acts on certainty, then the two prostrations after the salutation.
  ↓
Branch B, certain of nothing: he restarts from the beginning, and so does the imam.
  ↓
End: a real omission has a repair, a structural invalidation has repetition, intention lives in the heart, and certainty is not displaced by doubt.

## What this episode lands

Named-axis 2x2. The opening space, redrawn with the chapter's difficult edges in it.

Axes, unchanged from the opening:
- Horizontal: Site of the act (Private worshipper to Public congregation)
- Vertical: Register of the ruling (Outward form to Inward meaning)

Quadrants, populated now by the edges tested rather than the chapter's categories:
- Bottom-right, Public and Outward: the second congregation under three miles away, the obligation within two leagues, the congregation of five, the gathering that fails and turns Friday into Dhuhr. Reason: Friday asks whether the community is gathered around the right center, and every answer is a number.
- Top-right, Public and Inward: the openly unjust preacher, the sermon replacing two rak'ahs, the Eid takbir counted against the hudud, the sermon of Adha opening with magnification. Reason: Eid asks whether celebration reveals what fasting concealed, so these are justified by what they disclose.
- Bottom-left, Private and Outward: the missed tashahhud, the doubtful rak'ah, the fifth rak'ah, the late realization after the salaam. Reason: omission asks whether a defect can be told from an uncertainty, and stage answers it.
- Top-left, Private and Inward: the unspoken intention, the imam's utterance that voids it, certainty proceeding through doubt. Reason: nothing visible marks these, and no measure reaches them.

Reading: the chapter added no category. It took each settled teaching to the corner where it is hardest to hold, and the quadrant no measure reaches is where it ends.

Contrast pair. What the chapter refuses, against what it establishes.

Column A: what the chapter refuses
- Private improvisation, each worshipper arranging his own act
- Frightened repetition, every unease answered by starting over
- Casual withdrawal, the sermon abandoned because the leader is unjust
- Surrender, the unjust leader taken for a rightful imam
- Formless anxiety, doubt ruling what certainty had settled

Column B: what the chapter establishes
- Ordered action, held by authority, meaning and certainty
- Repair keyed to stage, before bowing, after bowing, before salaam, after salaam
- Presence with protection, taqiyya as the narrow permitted category
- Referral, to investigate, refer to the guardian, and await the response
- Certainty proceeding, since doubt neither displaces nor diminishes it

Shared row: read across, each refusal has its answer opposite it, and the answer is always measure rather than intensity.

Visual metaphor. The chapter's word, made spatial.

- Abstract relation: how worship holds form under pressure.
- Metaphor: a measured vessel, marked inside.
- The walls: the outward rulings, distance, pulpit, adhan, congregation, takbir count, sequence.
- The marks inside: the inward meanings, the two lights, the emergence of what was hidden, the heart's intention.
- What is poured in: the community's worship under pressure.
- Reading: the walls keep the act from spilling, the marks say how much is there. Prayer is preserved by measure.
