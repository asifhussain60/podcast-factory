# Imamate in Prayer

## Where this episode opens

Named-axis 2x2. The tension the chapter is built on, committed here and redrawn at the close.

Axes:
- Horizontal: where the duty is carried, from the worshipper on the left to the imam on the right.
- Vertical: whether the act is ordered, from disordered at the bottom to ordered at the top.

Quadrants:
- Top-right, imam and ordered: the fit leader, reciting without changing meaning. Reason: the front position must be truthful before it is convenient.
- Top-left, worshipper and ordered: the man on the moving ship who can stand, and stands, alone. Reason: congregation never cancels a capacity a worshipper holds.
- Bottom-right, imam and disordered: the salaried leader, the melody that bends meanings, the deaf man who cannot hear a warning. Reason: each holds the front without meeting what it requires.
- Bottom-left, worshipper and disordered: the follower in front of the imam, or raising his head first. Reason: the body announces independence while the mouth claims to follow.

Reading: neither ordered quadrant is reachable without the other.

Genealogy chain. What the book has already taught flows into this chapter, which reopens none of it.

attentive prayer → missed obligations → sacred times → the summons → the adhan's witness → entering the mosque → guarded direction → imamate in prayer

Callbacks, narrowed on arrival rather than re-taught:
- taqiyya → whether one phrase of praise may be lowered, and whether the prayer is deficient.
- bodily purity → what followers repeat when the imam recalls an impurity after finishing.
- sacred rank → who takes the front when two possible leaders differ in standing.
- attentive prayer → the follower who waits instead of moving first.

Comparison matrix. The cases named in the opening, sorted into the band each lands in.

Rows: the opening cases. Columns: band | the question it forces.
- Imam in the qibla corner: where he stands | can architecture spoil a lawful act
- Traveler joining a resident imam: where he stands | can unequal obligations share one form
- Two congregations on one ship: where he stands | can one place hold two prayers
- An imam seated from necessity: where he stands | posture from the leader or the body
- A taller person arriving beside the rows: where he stands | does shifting sideways invalidate
- The muqayyad, one placed under qayd: who should not lead | what a restraint on the limbs does
- A slave who is a jurist: who may lead | is legal freedom the only axis of rank
- A child with authority: who may lead | does office outrank age
- A blind leader: who should not lead | do necessity and payment change it
- One who takes salary: who should not lead | can the front position be hired
- A leader who cannot pronounce letters: who should not lead | does meaning survive the mouth
- One who exchanges mercy and punishment: who should not lead | is the congregation carried into false speech
- Taqiyya around a phrase of praise: how followers follow | may prudence erase inherited form
- Bismi Llāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm aloud or lowered: how followers follow | are audibility and obligation one thing
- An imam leaving for bodily need: when he cannot finish | is leadership transferable
- An imam recalling impurity afterward: when he cannot finish | how far his state reaches
- Two men who joined late: when he cannot finish | does repair improvise or stay ordered

Reading: a scatter of questions on the surface, and five bands underneath, stacked in the order a congregation actually fails. Where the imam stands, then who may lead, then who should not, then how followers follow, then what is done when the leader cannot finish.

Contrast pair. The two truths the source refuses to choose between.

Column A: Prayer as an intensely personal act
- Purification, recitation, travel status, bodily capacity: answered for by the worshipper
- Failure mode if taken alone: a crowd of solitary people sharing a room
- Case that proves it: the man who can stand, and therefore stands, alone

Column B: Prayer as a deliberate dependence
- Timing, audible phrases and their register: set by the imam
- Failure mode if taken alone: a follower treating the leader's state as no concern of his
- Case that proves it: followers repeating obligatory prayers after the imam recalls an impurity

Shared row: no one is absolved of personal responsibility, and no one may pretend that standing behind an imam is the same as praying alone in a crowd.

Process flow. How the source reasons, as a cycle with one branch and a return edge.

Start: a questioner states a concrete situation, usually with a place name attached.
  ↓
The ruling is given in one sentence, then a condition is attached, most often necessity, rank, or knowledge.
  ↓
Branch, for some cases only:
  - Branch A: a follow up presses the wording, what restriction means, who counts as below him in rank.
  - Branch B: no follow up, the case closes.
  ↓
Return edge: the cycle restarts at the next case, whether or not the wording was pressed.
End state: no essay on imamate is ever written, and the doctrine stands complete anyway.

## Standing Behind

Annotated structure. The whole: the mosque interior from above, as the first cluster is decided.

Whole: a public mosque with an arched qibla corner.
Parts and positions:
- The arch, at the front wall. Annotation: the imam prays inside it.
- The prostration point, just outside the arch. Annotation: his prostration does not occur within it.
- The rows, filling the mosque behind. Annotation: they stand in public space while he stands in the recess.
- Written across the plan, the ruling. Annotation: there is nothing wrong with praying and prostrating in it.
Cross reading: theatrical symmetry is not what is protected. The only question is whether he can lead and prostrate lawfully.

Contrast pair. Travelers and residents, when a shortened prayer meets a complete one.

Column A: The traveler is superior and leads
- What he prays: two rak'ahs, then gives the salām
- What the others do: rise and complete the prayer
- What is preserved: four rak'ahs, finished after the leader has stopped

Column B: The resident is superior and leads
- What he prays: the complete prayer
- What the others do: depart after two rak'ahs
- What is preserved: the shortened prayer, ended inside a longer one

Shared row: the difference is never erased, it is ordered under rank and completion. For a traveler residing somewhere who prays two rak'ahs with an imam, it is preferable to remain standing until he prays with the imam.

Comparison matrix. The unequal pairs a congregation contains, and what imamate does with each.

Rows: the pairs the source names. Columns: what differs | how the chapter orders it.
- Traveler and resident: rak'ahs owed | superiority decides who leads, departure decides who leaves
- Free and slave: legal status | juristic knowledge can outrank legal freedom
- Jurist and non jurist: knowledge of the law | knowledge lifts a socially lower man to the front
- Healthy and sick: bodily condition | the healthy man does not lead the sick
- Able to stand and unable: capacity now | whoever is able prays alone while standing
- Eloquent and mute: speech | the mute does not lead the eloquent
- Adult and child: maturity | boys do not lead obligatory prayers unless the boy holds authority
- Sighted and blind: sight | fitness turns on necessity and on refusing salary
- Dry ablution and water ablution: ritual status | the one who performs tayammum does not lead the others

Reading: the source never claims these people are equal. It asks what each inequality does to leadership, following, validity, preference and necessity.

Process flow. Two congregations in one place, decided by barrier first and rank second.

Start: one imam has already led Dhuhr in a long wide ship, or in a large house.
  ↓
Test 1, is there a barrier between the groups?
  - Yes → there is nothing wrong with it. End state: both valid on separation alone.
  - No → proceed to Test 2.
  ↓
Test 2, who is the later imam?
  - One who outranks the first, from the people of distinction → no objection. End state: valid on rank.
  - One who does not outrank him → the stated condition is unmet.

Reading: separation makes two congregations possible, and rank makes a second congregation intelligible when separation is absent.

Named-axis 2x2. Seated leadership on a moving ship, where necessity and capacity run independently.

Axes:
- Horizontal: the ship's constraint, from absent on the left to genuine on the right.
- Vertical: this worshipper's capacity, from unable to stand at the bottom to able at the top.

Quadrants:
- Bottom-right, genuine constraint and unable: prayer seated is permissible on account of the ship, and an imam may lead seated. Reason: necessity real, capacity absent.
- Top-right, genuine constraint and able: whoever is able should pray alone while standing. Reason: the seated form must not absorb a capacity this man holds.
- Bottom-left, no constraint and unable: the incapacity is personal, not the vessel's, and is ruled on elsewhere in the book.
- Top-left, no constraint and able: leading seated is not permitted. Reason: without necessity the source closes the option.

Reading: the top right corner costs the worshipper something, which is why the chapter states it.

Annotated structure. The whole: the lateral field around an imam, as one plan with five marked positions.

Whole: the space beside and behind a leader, once the row is no longer a simple line.
Parts and positions:
- Directly behind the imam. Annotation: the default every other case is measured against.
- To the imam's right. Annotation: where believers stand when a taller man arrives and they shift, leaving him on their left. Valid.
- To the north. Annotation: the alternative offered in the same answer, valid on the same terms.
- In line with the row. Annotation: permitted for a eunuch praying with women.
- To the right and slightly behind. Annotation: where a mahram woman stands when a man leads her.
Excluded from the plan: a non mahram woman praying with him, not permissible unless a third person is present.
Cross reading: the relation that fails does so because it is too private and unguarded, not because of a measured distance.

Process flow. A man praying alone when an imam arrives and steps forward.

Start: a man has begun an obligatory prayer alone, from Mukhtasar al-Muṣannaf.
  ↓
An imam arrives and steps forward to lead.
  ↓
Branch:
  - Branch A, recommended: he bows, prostrates, completes his cycle, counts it as voluntary, then begins the prayer with the imam.
  - Branch B, also permissible: he continues alone, outside the congregation.
  ↓
Separate case, decided by position rather than choice: some believers stand in front of the imam.
  - The imam's prayer: valid.
  - The prayers of those beside him: valid.
  - The prayers of those in front: not valid.

Reading: the man alone is offered a choice and the man in front is not. Standing in front makes the body speak against the act.

Visual metaphor. The rows in Mecca, where the line behind the imam becomes a ring.

Abstract relation: following, when the congregation surrounds what it faces.
Metaphor: concentric rings around the Kaaba.
Element assignments:
- Center: the Kaaba, faced from every side.
- Inner boundary ring: the imam's row, fixing the nearest permitted radius on every side.
- All outer rings: the followers' rows.
- The forbidden region: any arc closer to the walls than the imam's row, on any side.
Reading: the geometry changes from a line to a circle and the meaning of following does not change with it.

Annotated structure. The whole: a solitary prayer that becomes a congregation while it is already running.

Whole: one man praying alone after his ablution was nullified, with a second man arriving.
Parts and positions:
- At the front, the man who began alone. Annotation: not known to have led anyone, and not intending to lead now.
- Behind him, before he bows, and again after the bowing is complete. Annotation: the joiner may enter at either point, with the same answer.
- Between them, the new relation. Annotation: his prayer is permissible with the imam even if the imam did not intend to lead anyone else.
Cross reading: intention is not made irrelevant everywhere, only at this joint. The first concept of the chapter lands here. Imamate is a visible order, flexible about architecture, posture, crowd shape and late arrival, and unbending about who leads and who follows.

## Ranked Leadership

Process flow. The ranking from the tenth council of the Book of Councils al-Mustansiriyya, as a cascade of equality tests.

Start: several men are present and one must stand in front.
  ↓
Test 1: who is eldest in terms of emigration? One man is → he leads. Equal → continue.
  ↓
Test 2: who has the most Quran memorized? One man does → he leads. Equal → continue.
  ↓
Test 3: who is most learned in jurisprudence? One man is → he leads. Equal → continue.
  ↓
Test 4: who is eldest? One man is → he leads.
  ↓
Claims entering above the whole cascade rather than at any step:
  - The owner of the mosque has the greater right in his own mosque.
  - The imam, peace be upon him, and whoever establishes it, has the greater right over all of them.
  ↓
Parallel case, never entering the cascade: a woman prays with other women and does not precede them. They stand to her right and to the north.

Attribution: Mawlana Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Sadiq, in the tenth council concerning imamate in prayer.

Reading: each equality passes the decision down one rung, so the front position is never a scramble.

Contrast pair. Two rulings about the same slave, which look opposed and are not.

Column A: The slave who is a jurist
- What is weighed: juristic knowledge
- Who may stand behind him: anyone below him in rank, even if free
- What the ruling refuses: making legal freedom the only axis of rank

Column B: The master at the slave's dwelling
- What is weighed: ownership and household authority
- Who has the stronger claim: the master, in his own slave's dwelling
- What the ruling refuses: treating knowledge as the only claim in every setting

Shared row: leadership is situational. One who is not a jurist may be excused from following him in order to reunite with his family, and the question throughout is who holds the strongest claim here, among these people, for this prayer.

Quadrant map. The kinds of claim that reach the front, positioned by what each rests on. The space is qualitative, so no axis is committed.

Space: what makes a claim to the front position strong in this chapter.
Positions:
- Top-center: the imam, peace be upon him, and whoever establishes the prayer. Reason: their right overrides the rest and is never reached by comparison.
- Right-center: knowledge, as memorization and jurisprudence. Reason: it cuts across social standing, which is why the jurist slave appears at all.
- Left-center: place, as ownership of the mosque or the dwelling. Reason: it decides exactly where personal superiority cannot be determined.
- Bottom-center: precedence, as emigration and age. Reason: it settles cases only after stronger claims have tied.
- Center: fitness for this prayer, as sound recitation, hearing and lawful condition. Reason: every other claim is void without it.

Reading: the center is not a compromise between the four, it is the floor beneath them.

Annotated structure. The whole: the Rajpur and Jalalpur question, where superiority cannot be established.

Whole: two men, one residing in Rajpur and one in Jalalpur, and a prayer that must still be led.
Parts and positions:
- At the left, case one: they meet in Rajpur, and it is not known who is more virtuous.
- At the right, case two: both are in Rajpur, then meet in Jalalpur, and one leads the other.
- Beneath both, the shared blockage: personal superiority cannot be determined.
- At the foot, the ruling resolving both: the owner of the mosque has the greater right in his mosque.
Cross reading: where the ladder of persons gives no answer, the ladder of places does.

Comparison matrix. The twelve who may not lead, read as mismatches rather than defects.

Rows: the twelve categories. Column: whom he may not lead.
- The healthy man: the sick
- The leper: those without it
- The one afflicted with leprosy: those without it
- The insane: those of settled mind
- The one of limited capacity: those of full capacity
- The child born of adultery: those not so born
- The Arab: the Muhajirin
- The divorced man: those not in that state
- The one who performs dry ablution: those who performed water ablution
- The mute: the eloquent
- The resident: the traveler
- The one who opposes by innovation: those who mourn in the traditional manner

Third column, identical for all twelve rows and therefore written once: each, however, may lead others in a similar situation.

Reading: this is a source era legal classification, and its operative principle is mismatch, not ruin.

Hierarchy tree. The same twelve, sorted by what kind of thing is mismatched.

- Level 1: grounds on which a leader is unfit for this congregation
  - Level 2: bodily condition
    - Level 3: the leper, the one afflicted with leprosy, the healthy man over the sick
  - Level 2: capacity of mind
    - Level 3: the insane, the one of limited capacity
  - Level 2: ritual status
    - Level 3: dry ablution over water ablution
  - Level 2: obligation owed
    - Level 3: the resident over the traveler
  - Level 2: speech
    - Level 3: the mute over the eloquent
  - Level 2: standing in the community
    - Level 3: the child born of adultery, the divorced man, the Arab over the Muhajirin
  - Level 2: doctrine
    - Level 3: the one who opposes by innovation over those who mourn traditionally

Reading: seven kinds of mismatch, not twelve separate prohibitions.

Annotated structure. The whole: the muqayyad, and the two questions pressed on the word.

Whole: one term in the prohibition, narrowed twice.
Parts and positions:
- At the top, the first question: what is meant by restriction, and who is the one restricted?
- Directly beneath, its answer: the muqayyad is one upon whom a qayd has been placed.
- Below, the second question: does the statement that the muqayyad does not lead the divorced ones refer to all restraints and bonds, or specifically restraints on the legs?
- At the foot, its answer: restraints and bonds placed on the legs and similar limbs.
Cross reading: the narrowing runs downward until the ruling names a part of the body.

Process flow. The recitation test, which decides leadership by meaning rather than by ability.

Start: a man leads and cannot pronounce certain letters, having lost his teeth or not knowing the articulation points.
  ↓
Test: does the mispronunciation change the meaning?
  - No → the prayer proceeds with him leading.
  - Yes → it invalidates the prayer.
  ↓
Second entry, same test: an imam recites with alterations and distortions.
  - Is a verse of mercy substituted for a verse of punishment, or falsehood for truth? Yes → whoever cannot recite well should not be taken as imam.
  - No → whoever recites well should lead, if called upon and if he consents.
  ↓
Third entry, same test: one who recites with a melody that distorts the meanings of the Quran.
  - Outcome: he may not lead the prayer, since the voice must serve the words rather than perform over them.

Reading: a broken tooth is not the issue, ignorance of articulation is not an embarrassment to be mocked, and beauty of sound is no defense. The example the source gives is the corruption of "Allah is innocent of the polytheists and His messenger," where guidance is misassigned by a changed phrase, and the congregation cannot audit the leader's mouth while praying behind him.

Annotated structure. The whole: hearing, the one requirement about receiving rather than producing.

Whole: a deaf leader who cannot hear even if he is warned.
Parts and positions:
- At the front, the leader who cannot hear. Annotation: he may not lead.
- Behind him, the congregation holding a correction. Annotation: the warning exists and cannot arrive.
- Between them, the broken channel. Annotation: the source does not elaborate the reason, and the context supplies it.
Cross reading: every other requirement here runs outward from the imam. This one runs inward. Imamate requires not only speaking correctly but being correctable.

Contrast pair. The non adult, where office and age answer in opposite directions.

Column A: The boy who holds authority
- His standing: governor of the Muslims in his era
- May others lead him: no, it is not permissible for anyone to lead him
- What decides it: public authority

Column B: Boys in general
- Their standing: ordinary non adults
- May they lead obligatory prayers: no
- What decides it: maturity, against the weight of the obligatory prayer

Shared row: no single trait settles a leadership question here, and the same boyhood points two ways.

Comparison matrix. The blind imam, the collections preserved side by side rather than merged.

Rows: the preserved voices. Columns: the verdict | the condition attached.
- The author of the footnotes: shortening his imamate is appropriate | when he is compelled to lead and does not take a salary
- The national collection: not permitted to lead | unless he performs it without charge and without salary
- The Nāṣib: oppose this | no condition preserved
- Qāḍī al-Nu'mān ibn Muhammad, in an epistle answering questions from some judges: there is nothing wrong with him leading | provided he performs it without charge
- The People of the House: disapproved his imamate, with the leper, the one afflicted with leprosy, the child born of adultery, one under obligation and the possessed | except when necessity compels it, or when he takes an imam for a salary

Reading: the wording around salary is compressed and the emphases differ, so the rows stay separate. The same report did not regard the Arab, the one of limited capacity, or the madman as fit to lead, and permitted people in similar situations to lead one another.

Named-axis 2x2. What the collections agree on, once necessity and payment are separated.

Axes:
- Horizontal: necessity, from absent on the left to compelling on the right.
- Vertical: payment, from salary taken at the bottom to service without charge at the top.

Quadrants:
- Top-right, compelling necessity and no charge: the clearest permitted case, described by Qāḍī al-Nu'mān and by the footnotes. Reason: both stated conditions are met at once.
- Top-left, no necessity and no charge: still permitted by the national collection, whose stated condition is payment. Reason: this is where the collections visibly differ.
- Bottom-right, compelling necessity and a salary taken: named as an exception in the further report, against the chapter's flat statement that an imam should not take a salary. Reason: necessity and payment pull opposite ways.
- Bottom-left, no necessity and a salary taken: refused on every reading preserved here. Reason: nothing compels it, and the front position has been hired.

Reading: the disagreement lives in the top-left corner, the agreement on the diagonal. The bottom row is where the chapter's flat sentence bites, that an imam should not take a salary, because the front position is a religious trust and not a commercial performance.

Visual metaphor. Each may lead his like, drawn as mirrors rather than as exclusions.

Abstract relation: a condition that disqualifies a man before one congregation and not before another.
Metaphor: a row of mirrors, each reflecting only what stands before it.
Element assignments:
- The man before the mirror: a leader carrying some condition.
- The reflection: the congregation whose condition matches his.
- The permitted act: leading the reflection.
- The blocked act: leading anyone standing outside the frame.
- What the mirrors are not: a wall, since no one is excluded from communal prayer altogether.
Reading: the second concept lands here. The rule protects hierarchy and inclusion at once, and the front position belongs to ranked fitness, sometimes the jurist even if enslaved, sometimes the owner of the mosque, sometimes the one who memorizes more Quran, sometimes the eldest, sometimes the one whose speech preserves meaning, and sometimes no one but his like or under necessity.

## Following Without Fracture

Annotated structure. The whole: the scene the narration preserves, with the Prophet injured and still leading.

Whole: one prayer led from a seated position, from the interpretation of the Shari'ah by al-Mu'izz.
Parts and positions, left to right:
- Before the prayer: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him and his family, rides a horse, falls, and is injured on his right side.
- Next: his companions come to him.
- At the front: he attends the prayer and leads them while seated.
- Behind him, first posture: they stand.
- Between them: he gestures to them to sit.
- Behind him, second posture: they sit.
- After the salām: he states the rule the whole movement rests on.
Cross reading: the correction is made inside the prayer, by gesture, before it is put in words. The lesson is not that sitting is preferred, since an imam should sit only from necessity, but that followers do not invent their own choreography once they have entered the imam's prayer.

> The imam is to be followed; if he stands, stand; if he recites, listen; if he says "Sami'a Allāhu liman ḥamidah," say "Rabbanā laka l-ḥamd," glorifying God greatly; if he gives the salām, give the salām; and if he prays seated, sit together with him.

Attribution: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him and his family, in the interpretation of the Shari'ah by al-Mu'izz.

Comparison matrix. The narration clause by clause, against the faculty each clause binds.

Rows: the five clauses. Columns: what the imam does | what the follower does | the faculty bound.
- Clause one: he stands | stand | posture
- Clause two: he recites | listen | hearing, and the withholding of speech
- Clause three: he says Sami'a Allāhu liman ḥamidah | say Rabbanā laka l-ḥamd, glorifying God greatly | speech, at an appointed moment
- Clause four: he gives the salām | give the salām | the timing of the ending
- Clause five: he prays seated | sit together with him | posture again, now against the follower's own capacity

Reading: five clauses, one instruction. The fifth is the hardest, and it is the one the companions had to be corrected on.

Named-axis 2x2. Taqiyya and the inherited formulas, where audibility and retention are separate questions.

Axes:
- Horizontal: the phrase itself, from dropped on the left to kept on the right.
- Vertical: the voice, from lowered at the bottom to audible at the top.

Quadrants:
- Top-right, kept and audible: Bismi Llāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm in Maghrib, recited aloud. Reason: the source says he must recite it aloud.
- Bottom-right, kept and lowered: the same phrase lowered to where only he himself hears it, when the people of al-Ẓāhir gather with him. Reason: taqiyya is granted over audibility, and the obligation is untouched.
- Bottom-left, dropped and lowered: Rabbanā laka al-ḥamd omitted because common people are present. Reason: there is nothing wrong with taqiyya, and he must not omit it except for taqiyya, reciting it as the pure guardians of God did.
- Top-left, dropped and audible: empty. Reason: no one openly abandons a formula he is at that moment concealing.

Reading: the prayer's form lives in the right hand column and prudence in the bottom row. Taqiyya moves a case downward, and only a stated necessity moves it leftward.

Process flow. Timing, and the only repair given for moving too soon.

Start: the imam begins a complete act of prayer.
  ↓
Rule: those behind him must not begin any complete act before he begins it.
  ↓
Test: has one of them preceded him in any act, through omission?
  - No → he continues as a follower, bowing after the imam and rising after the imam.
  - Yes → he must return to his position, so as to remain a follower.
  ↓
End state of the repair branch: following is restored by going back, not by going on.

> Whoever raises his head before the imam fears that Allah will disfigure his head into the head of a donkey.

Attribution: the Prophet, quoted in the Essential Councils.

Reading: if the imam is the visible hinge, premature movement breaks the hinge, and the congregation fractures internally while still standing in one room.

Process flow. The imam who does not know how much of the prayer has passed.

Start: the imam leads, and does not know what he has completed and what remains.
  ↓
First instruction: he must rely on certainty.
  ↓
Branch on what those behind him do:
  - Branch A, they separate from him → they should return to what they affirmed, until the truth becomes clear.
  - Branch B, they stay and also do not know → neither he nor they can determine whether the prayer was more or less complete, and the matter is left open.
  ↓
End state: uncertainty is answered with certainty where certainty exists, and separation from the imam is not treated lightly.

Process flow. The imam who must leave, and the transfer that keeps the prayer alive.

Start: a man leads people in prayer and needs to relieve himself during it.
  ↓
Question: may he designate someone he deems fit to complete the prayer in his place, even if that person has not been divorced from the prayer?
  ↓
Test: is the designation unconditional?
  - Yes → there is nothing wrong, and he may offer the leadership to whomever he wishes.
  ↓
What the replacement does: completes what was entrusted to him, rather than beginning an entirely new prayer.
  ↓
End state: the congregation does not collapse because the first imam cannot finish.

Reading: imamate is personal and transferable at once. Personal, because fitness decides who may be designated. Transferable, because the communal act must be completed under lawful continuity.

Comparison matrix. Four repairs, and how far each one reaches.

Rows: the four cases. Columns: what went wrong | what is required now.
- Impurity recalled after the prayer: the imam knew of it beforehand and forgot to wash it off | those who followed him repeat the obligatory prayers only, not the voluntary ones
- A second congregation in the same mosque: one man led Dhuhr, the people departed, another later led another group in the same prayer, unaware at the time | nothing, the prayer has been performed and need not be repeated, whether they knew or not, and let them be informed and be grateful
- Arrival after the imam has gone: a man departed the mosque and no one joined him, and others come after he has left with a man who was not with him | their prayers are invalid, and the only permitted wait is a short one, for the imam to speak before he exits
- Two men who joined late: they missed part of it, and the imam gives the salām | they make up what remains, one completing it in the manner of the imam with the other, otherwise the prayer of the one being led is invalid

Reading: the four reach different distances. One reaches the followers' obligatory prayers only, one reaches nobody, one invalidates outright, and one extends the form of imamate past the imam's own salām.

Timeline. One congregational prayer along its own axis, with each repair point marked where it falls.

Axis: relative order within a single prayer, from before the takbir to after the salām.

Events:
- Before it begins: the imam's fitness is settled, his recitation, hearing, rank and condition.
- Before it begins: an impurity is known, and forgotten.
- At the start: the rows form, and no follower stands in front of the imam.
- Early, before the first bowing: a joiner may attach to a man praying alone.
- Throughout: each follower waits, and returns to his position if he precedes the imam.
- Mid prayer, at bodily need: leadership is designated, and the replacement completes what was entrusted.
- Mid prayer, at uncertainty: certainty is relied on, and the affirmed portion returned to.
- At the salām: the followers give the salām when he gives it.
- Just after the salām: two late joiners complete what they missed, one leading the other.
- Later, outside the prayer: the forgotten impurity is recalled, and obligatory prayers repeated.
- Later still: a second group learns what it did not know, and is told to be grateful.

Period bands: eligibility, before the takbir | attachment and following, inside the prayer | repair, at and after the salām.

Reading: the band a case falls in decides what its answer can be.

Annotated structure. The whole: the combining report, placed here rather than among the sacred times.

Whole: the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him and his family, combined the noon, afternoon, sunset and night prayers.
Parts and positions:
- At the center, the act: four prayers combined.
- Ringed around it, the three excuses explicitly absent: without travel, without illness, without fear.
- Beneath it, where the report is not taken: not expanded here into a doctrine of prayer times, already taught.
- Above it, what it does here: the communal performance of obligatory prayer has transmitted precedents that cannot be reduced to emergency excuses.
Cross reading: the third concept lands here. To pray behind an imam is to let another person's lawful movement become the measure of one's own, and the leader's own practice, not the follower's preference, is what reveals lawful breadth in the form.

## Closing

Visual metaphor. The imam as the chapter has described him throughout, made spatial.

Abstract relation: many bodies performing one act through a single person.
Metaphor: a hinge, with a door on either side.
Element assignments:
- The pin: the imam's lawful movement, which everything else turns on.
- The leaf fixed to the frame: the inherited form of the prayer, which does not move.
- The leaf that swings: the congregation, which moves only as the pin allows.
- A pin withdrawn: the imam who leaves for bodily need, replaced before the door can fall.
- A leaf that swings first: the follower who raises his head before the imam, which is the hinge breaking rather than the door opening.
Reading: small, plain and load bearing, which is the chapter's estimate of the front position exactly.

Comparison matrix. What the congregation trusts, against what the imam accepts in return.

Rows: the six things named at the close. Columns: what is trusted | what he accepts.
- Rank: his claim is the strongest present | his standing was examined, not assumed
- Recitation: the words arrive undistorted | his words may preserve or distort meaning
- Purity: his state does not spoil their act | his mistakes may require their repetition
- Bodily capacity: he can complete what he began | his incapacity may require appointment
- Timing: his movement is the measure | his choices affect others
- Ability to be followed: following him is legible | his front position is not private devotion with spectators behind him

Reading: every row is one fact seen from two sides, which is what a grammar of trust means.

Named-axis 2x2. The opening axes redrawn, with what the chapter has settled written into each quadrant.

Axes:
- Horizontal: where the duty is carried, from the worshipper on the left to the imam on the right.
- Vertical: whether the act is ordered, from disordered at the bottom to ordered at the top.

Quadrants:
- Top-right, imam and ordered: fitness, sound recitation, refused salary, seated posture only from necessity, appointment when he cannot finish.
- Top-left, worshipper and ordered: standing when able, waiting for the imam, returning after moving early, repeating the obligatory prayers only.
- Bottom-right, imam and disordered: the hired leader, the distorting melody, the leader who cannot hear, the second imam who does not outrank the first.
- Bottom-left, worshipper and disordered: the man in front of the imam, the row nearer the Kaaba than his, the head raised first.

Reading across the top row: the follower cannot abandon his own capacity, and the imam cannot treat his own act as isolated.

Contrast pair. The inversion the chapter closes on.

Column A: How congregational prayer appears
- Decisions the worshipper makes: fewer, since one person leads
- Difficulty against praying alone: lower
- What seems delegated: timing, recitation, and the shape of the act
- What seems retained: attendance

Column B: How congregational prayer works
- Decisions the worshipper makes: his purification, obligation, travel status, capacity, and timing relative to another body
- Difficulty against praying alone: higher, because personal obligation and communal dependence run at once
- What is actually delegated: the measure of movement, and nothing else
- What is actually retained: every duty his own condition still allows

Shared row: between the two columns stands the delicate discipline by which many bodies become one prayer without losing the truth each body owes.
