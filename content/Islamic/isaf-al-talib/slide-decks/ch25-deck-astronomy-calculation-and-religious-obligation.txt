# Astronomy, Calculation, and Religious Obligation

## Where this episode opens

Shared row: Both are called sight. The dispute is not whether the eye works. The dispute is which instrument times a duty.

## Two kinds of sight

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The two visions, jurisdiction by jurisdiction.

Column A: Natural vision
- Seat: the eye
- Objects, first class: colors, white, black, red, yellow, and others
- Objects, second class: forms, circular, triangular, square, and so on
- Domain: the senses
- Basis of reliance: it falls within the domain of the senses, and on that basis it is relied upon
- Temporal reach: things physically present, within limits and time
- What it reaches of a thing: the outward appearance

Column B: Intellectual vision
- Seat: the heart and the soul
- Objects, first class: what natural vision cannot perceive
- Objects, second class: the causes hidden from natural perception
- Domain: knowledge
- Basis of reliance: its knowledge of things reaches their realities
- Temporal reach: what the eye misses from sight, including things in their absence
- What it reaches of a thing: the reality

Shared row: The eye sees the effect in front of it. The other vision sees why it is there. Nobody claims the eye lies about whether a thing is red.

- Part: what is not meant, anything at all about his eyes.
- Annotation: everyone who uses the insult already believes there is a kind of seeing a person with perfect eyesight can fail at, and note its object, things in their absence, which is precisely the case the chapter is building toward.

Diagram type: Genealogy chain. The doctrine of pairs, arriving in a single breath.

All created things → divided into two states and two aspects → to complete the oneness of Allah → so that nothing shares in that oneness → therefore sight also is of two kinds, each coupled with the other

Annotation: this is the preceding argument used as a tool, not defended again.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. One drop taken from a pool, and two reports of the same event.

Column B: The report of intellectual vision

## Two kinds of sight, from precision to obligation

Start: Intellectual vision is the more prior and the more refined in perception
  Then: When two instruments are available and one is better, using the worse one for God's business is not caution
  Then: The first principle is to restrict oneself not to what is inferior, the level of the ignorant, but to what reason demands, which binds people to the truth
End: It is all the more obligatory to act upon the more precise and superior, the vision of knowledge and the soul

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. Three applications, arranged from the uncontroversial to the contested.

Rows: the prayer, the dawn of the fast, the crescent of the fast
Columns: what is known in the heart | which sense is blocked | who disputes it | the ruling

- The prayer: that the time for one of the prayers has come | hearing, no call to prayer has reached him | nobody | the prayer becomes obligatory for him even if he has not heard the call
- The dawn of the fast: that dawn has arrived | sight, his eyesight is prevented from seeing it | nobody | he would be obligated to abstain from food
- The crescent of the fast: that the cause of fasting, the crescent's appearance, has occurred at its greatest strength | sight, prevented by light, clouds, dust, or mountains | this is the one the letter exists for | the fast becomes obligatory

Annotation on row one: the call, taught earlier in this book as the summons, announces a time. It does not create one.

Annotation on row two: a blind man at dawn is bound exactly as a sighted man is. The daily beginning of the fast is already governed by knowledge rather than eyesight, every morning, for anybody whose window faces a wall.

Annotation on row three: the same logic, extended one step, and the step is the whole dispute.

Diagram type: Process flow. The reasoning given is causal, not legal.

  Then: It is customary that when the cause occurs, its effect becomes obligatory
  Then: Neither should precede the other except in rank

Annotation: causes do not wait for witnesses.

Annotation on reliability: the call in their entering the fast by virtue of intellectual vision is one in which they scarcely fall into error.

## What blocks the eye

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The lunar month, defined as a circuit rather than a run of nights.

Whole: one complete circuit of the moon, measured from a named geometrical instant back to the same instant.
- Part: the degree, one of three hundred and sixty positions around the circle of the sky.
- Part: the minute, one of sixty divisions inside a degree.
- Part: the conjunction, the moment the moon and the sun occupy the same position in that reckoning.
- Part: the starting point, the moon one degree and one minute ahead of the sun.
- Part: the closing point, the moon returned to that same design and conjunction.
- Annotation: on this definition a month is not a stretch of nights and not a sequence of appearances, and these months are not complete until their appointed periods are fulfilled.
- Annotation: the definition is openly borrowed, this is the conjunction recognized by astronomers, and the letter does not hide the borrowing.

Diagram type: Process flow. The moon across one month, with the crossings marked.

Start: The moon's degree and minute within its zodiacal position become identical to those of the sun
  Then: It has departed from the old month, and leaves the sun on its way to the next
  Then: At the conjunction, the light of one half of the moon's mass is drawn away from the side facing the earth until it passes over
  Then: The upper half of the mass is illuminated
  Then: The light of the half facing us appears at the crescent, and grows as the moon increases its distance
  Branch, the night of opposition: the moon stands opposite the sun, facing the centre, which the letter glosses for us as the earth, and this is the full moon
End: The circuit closes and the next conjunction arrives

Diagram type: Contrast pair. What survives of the optical passage against what does not.

Column A: Legible, and essentially the modern account
- The moon is a sphere with a lit half and a dark half
- The lit half is always lit, and what changes is how much of it faces us
- The crescent is the first sliver swinging into view as the moon pulls away from the sun
- The night of opposition is the full moon

Column B: Damaged, and not repaired
- The phrase rendered here as the ocean side
- The clause comparing what it reflects to what each half of the mass does when facing the centre
- Treatment: left standing as transmitted, not reconstructed

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The four obstacles, and the fifth that does not belong with them.

Rows: cloud, dust, a wall, a mountain, the sun
Columns: where it stands | does it stop the eye | does it stop the moon | has any legal effect ever been attached to it

- Cloud: between the observer and the sky | yes | no | no
- Dust: between the observer and the sky | yes | no | no
- A wall: on the observer's side | yes | no | no
- A mountain: on the observer's side | yes | no | no
- The sun: in the sky, and it is the source of the crescent's light | yes | no | this is the one under dispute

Annotation: nobody has ever claimed that a mountain stops a month. If the crescent has risen and a ridge stands between you and it, the crescent has still risen. The letter puts the sun in that list and asks why it should be treated differently.

Annotation on the mechanism: the causes that prevent the eye are many, and the eye's inability arises from within itself, and from the fact that it sees light emanating from the moon's body as the moon moves toward conjunction at the end of the month.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The everyday demonstration, which costs nothing to check.

Whole: the daytime sky.
- Part: the planets, overhead at noon, and the observer who sees nothing.
- Part: the sun's light and its overwhelming radiance.
- Part: the same sky after that light is broken, when the eye can perceive them.
- Annotation on the cause: light is diminished by refraction.
- Annotation on the law: nobody concludes the planets are gone, and no legal consequence has ever been attached to that invisibility.

Diagram type: Visual metaphor. The paradox of the young crescent, drawn as a single source with two opposed outputs.

Abstract relation: one body both generates the evidence and suppresses it.
Metaphor: a single lamp casting a beam and a glare from the same filament.
- Element, the filament: the sun.
- Element, the beam: all of the crescent's light, borrowed, since the light at the moon's departure would not exist were it not for the sun's own rays.
- Element, the glare: the sun's light standing as the barrier, just as other barriers are, like clouds and the atmospheric cycle.
- Reading: the sun both makes the crescent and hides it, and the young crescent is most vulnerable when it is nearest the sun.

Diagram type: Process flow. The conclusion, in one chain.

Start: The eye is prevented by sunlight from seeing the crescent after its departure
  Then: The soul, from the perspective of knowledge, is not prevented from seeing it as it truly is
  Then: Fasting becomes obligatory through the vision of knowledge, derived from the established signs already outlined
  Then: By these testimonies, the people of the Sayyid's call are on the truth and the right path in their entry into fasting through the soul's vision
End: And if they are correct, then whoever disagrees with them is in error

Annotation, the question then put to the other side: where does the person of right derive the basis for seeing by sight alone, without the vision of the heart and knowledge. The evidence rests on seeing from two angles, and no one denies this except out of apparent arrogance.

## What blocks the eye, the opponent pressed three times

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The opponent's doctrine, stated fairly in his own voice.

Whole: a two-clause position.
- Clause one: a person does not know anything until his sight falls upon it.
- Clause two: once he sees it, the ruling of knowledge-vision is lifted from him.
- Annotation: knowledge is a stand-in for sight, and the moment sight is available the stand-in retires.
- Verdict returned: we say your argument is corrupt and invalid, for three reasons.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The three pressures.

Rows: the doctrine cannot see itself, the doctrine is not how learning works, the thirty-day case
Columns: the question put | the answer he is forced to | where that answer lands

- Cannot see itself: have you seen that Allah Almighty has made this obligatory upon you | no | his sight does not fall upon the very rule he enforces
- Not how learning works: the learned man has commanded the world regarding its events, and if we asked you about something, what would you tell him | that you are wise enough to employ the vision of knowledge and proof | proof does not rule by the eye alone, which is what the letter says
- The thirty-day case: a man broke his fast without seeing the crescent, but thirty days of Ramadan had passed, was his breaking valid | a fork with no third exit | either branch ends in the letter's own position

Annotation on the first: he is compelled to abandon sight-seeing and return to the hadith of knowledge. The letter puts the answer in his mouth, because it is the only one available, that we know, and we have the proofs, and we have confirmed it. Which is the other party's position, adopted under pressure and renamed.

Annotation on the third: every party to this dispute accepts the thirty-day rule, that if the new crescent is not sighted the month completes at thirty. That is what makes the question a fork.

Diagram type: Process flow with a branch. The thirty-day trap.

Start: A man breaks his fast, no crescent seen with the eye, thirty days of Ramadan elapsed
  Branch A, you say it was valid:
    Then: But the crescent was not seen with the eye, and your doctrine forbids breaking the fast until after seeing it
    Then: You are compelled to say that we know that after thirty days the crescent has been seen, even though it was obscured
    Then: You have used knowledge to certify a sighting nobody made
    End A: You have abandoned the ruling of sight-vision and returned by necessity to the hadith of knowledge-vision, which is our position
  Branch B, you say it was not valid:
    Then: You must hold that one cannot know anything except through the physical senses, things physical in their form and quality only
    Then: That is false about the soul, which dispenses with the aid of the senses in its knowledge by making use of other faculties within it
    End B: The fate of the senses and their yield is that the soul rises above the need for them

Annotation: branch B pushes the opponent into a claim about the human mind, and the next movement goes after it directly.

## What seeking the crescent seeks

Diagram type: Hierarchy. The instruments of perception within man.

- Level 1: The instruments by which man perceives things
  - Level 2: The five senses
    - Level 3: hearing
    - Level 3: sight
    - Level 3: smell
    - Level 3: taste
    - Level 3: touch
  - Level 2: The soul
    - Level 3: receives the images of the senses' perceptions
    - Level 3: rises above all of them after receiving those images
    - Level 3: perceives what hearing, sight, smell, taste, and touch do not perceive

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. Sealed domains, by design.

Rows: hearing, sight, smell, taste, touch
Columns: what it perceives | what it cannot perceive | why the seal is deliberate

- Hearing: sound | color, form, scent, flavour, texture | so that no part of creation goes unregistered
- Sight: shapes and colors | sound, scent, flavour, texture | the same
- Smell: scent | the other four domains | the same
- Taste: flavour | the other four domains | the same
- Touch: texture | the other four domains | the same

Annotation: each sense has its own domain in the wisdom of Allah Almighty, lest man be deprived of perceiving some part of what Allah has created in the worlds. Sight is deaf, hearing is colorblind, and each is sovereign in its own territory and blind everywhere else.

Annotation, the question this invites: if each sense is sealed inside its domain, what perceives the world as a whole. His perception is thus multiple, and beyond the five it extends to what the senses do not reach, for he perceives by inference what they cannot hand him.

Diagram type: Process flow. The mechanism given, which is memory.

Start: A thing must be known
  Then: The soul accepts the image of the thing as perceived by the senses at the first perception
  Then: That image remains present to the soul in itself
  Then: The soul returns to that thing without needing the senses again
End: A second perception of the image in itself, with sight dispensed with

Worked case, chosen because it is the case at issue: images of the planets and their qualities were perceived through sight, and the soul then dispenses with that sight for a second perception.

Annotation: you looked once, the look deposited something, and the soul works with the deposit. Which lets the letter ask what nobody in the dispute had thought to ask, what is the sighting actually for.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. What the sighting is not for, against what it is for.

Column A: Not what is meant in seeking the crescent
- Seeing it with the eye every month and year
- Knowing its appearance in its form
- Knowing its precision
- Knowing its coarseness
- Instrument this would require: sight

Column B: What is actually meant in seeking it
- Knowing the validity of its being in the west in front of the sun, after having been in the east
- Category of the fact sought: position
- Instrument this requires: the soul
- Reason: sight, even without obstructions, only perceives shapes and colors

Shared row: you are not going out at sunset to find out the moon's color or shape. You have known both since childhood. You are going out to settle whether it has crossed over.

Annotation on the phrase even without obstructions: the whole section on cloud, dust, mountain, and glare is set aside as beside the point. Give the eye a perfect night and a sharp young crescent, and it still reports only a shape and a color. The obstructions were a nuisance. The real problem is jurisdiction.

Diagram type: Process flow with two closed exits. The remaining escapes, and why each is shut.

Start: Suppose the request regarding the crescent were about something the eye can supply
  Exit 1, its color and shape:
    Then: That would already be impossible to request from one who knows how it looks from having seen it
    Then: And who trusts that it will not change as long as the heavens and the earth endure
    Shut: you cannot be sent to learn what you already know and what cannot change
  Exit 2, its fineness and thickness, its precision and coarseness:
    Then: It would be impossible to make fasting obligatory if the crescent is precise while the condition is coarseness
    Then: Or coarse while the condition is precision
    Shut: if the sighting existed to certify a thickness, the law would have had to specify which thickness, and it never has
End: This condition of shape, color, precision, and coarseness was not present in the obligation of fasting, so it is invalid to say the obligation is attached to perception of the crescent by sight. Once invalidated, fasting is established as attached to the perception of the soul, because what is sought is whether the crescent is in front of the sun.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. The reliability of the two instruments, listed like a physician's symptoms.

Rows: error of magnitude, error of grain, error of range, error of scope
Columns: how sight-vision fails | how knowledge-vision stands

- Error of magnitude: it sees the large as small and the small as large | it does not err
- Error of grain: it sees the fine as coarse and the coarse as fine | it does not err
- Error of range: it may only see from near and not from far, or from far and not from near | range does not apply to it
- Error of scope: it can barely err in what it encompasses, the narrow band where it is safe | its knowledge reaches the realities of things

Annotation: taking what does not err in establishing obligatory duties, according to the people of hearts, is more deserving than taking what does err. So when the people of the call entered the fast, they took what does not err.

## What seeking the crescent seeks, the lam and the lost diagram

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The text the other side holds, and the single letter under dispute.

Whole: the Prophet's instruction.

> Fast upon seeing it and break the fast upon seeing it.
> Attribution: the Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him.

- Part: the lam, the Arabic prefix carrying the sense of for or toward.
- Part: the speaker's own claim about himself, that he is the most eloquent of the Arabs.
- Annotation: a man who called himself that did not attach a preposition by accident, and there is no significance in the lam without some other purpose being served by the expression.
- Annotation on the transmission: the letter says the saying is not without two possible interpretations, but the two branches as they reach us read almost identically, both describing the community fasting from the time of the crescent's sighting. The distinction cannot be recovered and is not reconstructed.

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. Three examples of the lam from ordinary speech, all doing the same work.

Rows: the guest, the poem, the Hereafter
Columns: the saying | when the work is done | why it cannot mean at the moment of

- The guest: prepare such-and-such a thing for the coming of so-and-so | before he walks in | nobody begins cooking when the guest arrives
- The poem: they composed a poem to meet so-and-so, to recite it to him | before the meeting | they will have it ready when they find him, not compose it from the moment of meeting
- The Hereafter: work for the Hereafter | before the time of the Hereafter arrives | it is known that in the Hereafter there is no work, so the deadline is absolute

Annotation: the third is decisive. The lam cannot mean at the moment of. It must mean in readiness for.

Annotation on the conclusion: with these evidences it is established that the lam was not used to mean that the people fast when seeing the crescent with their own eyes. And the letter concedes in passing that even if the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, did intend the crescent to be seen and the community to fast upon it, that is what we have said, and we have demonstrated its proof.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The opponent's counter, which is his best move, and the reply.

Column A: The counter
- Ground conceded: the lam is from the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him
- Instrument used: the identical construction in scripture
- The verse produced:

> "Establish the prayer for the decline of the sun."
> Quran 17:78

- Force of the move: the verse does not require that the prayer precede the sun's decline, nor that it be offered before the sun
- Consequence if the letter were right: the noon prayer would be early every day

Column B: The reply
- Move made: the parallel is conceded and turned around
- Statement: the lam in the words of Allah Almighty about the sun is like the lam in the saying of the Prophet, to see it
- Definition supplied: the sun's decline is its disappearance from the dome of the sky, as has been explained
- Question reopened: yes, you pray at the decline, not before, but when is the decline
- Verdict: the opponent's verse is not a counter-example, it is the same case

Diagram type: Contrast pair. Two perceptions of one instant.

Column A: Psychological perception
- Object: the decline in reality within the dome of the heavens
- Instrument: the soul, through the perspective of knowledge
- Order: it precedes natural perception

Column B: Natural perception
- Object: a sun already on its way down
- Instrument: natural vision, which is limited in its perception
- Order: it follows, and only once the sun reaches the azimuth point and descends, securing its range in its descent at the intersection of the circle line and the straight line

Shared row: even for the noon prayer, the event is one thing and the perception of it another, and the knowing runs ahead of the looking.

- The pool: one drop taken from the water | unchanged | less by one, and the mind is right

Annotation: causes do not wait for witnesses.

Diagram type: Hierarchy. The opponent, pressed three times, and what each pressure reaches.

- Level 1: The doctrine that nothing is known until sight falls on it
  - Level 2: It cannot account for his own knowledge of the obligation, which he has never seen
  - Level 2: It cannot account for how any scholar has ever settled anything
  - Level 2: It cannot survive the thirty-day case

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The deepest move, which came last and was the quietest.

Column B: What a sighting is for
- To settle whether the moon now stands west of the sun, having been east of it
- Category: a fact about position

Shared row: the eye was being asked a question outside its jurisdiction.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The closing condition of the text.

- Part: the proof of the last point, which was a diagram.
