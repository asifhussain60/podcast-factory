# Zakat on Wealth, Livestock, Crops, and Fitr

## Where this episode picks up

Diagram type: Process flow. What happens to an estate when the man who owed zakat is dead.

Start: a man dies owing zakat, Hajj, khums, expiation, and al-Sharwa.
  Down to: the estate is opened, whatever it holds, a house, its furniture, the servants, the common goods.
  Down to: the obligations are subtracted in a fixed order, not in proportion.
  Down to: the heirs receive the remainder.
End state: the remainder may be nothing at all.
Transition condition: debt precedes inheritance, so the subtraction is not capped by what the heirs expect.

- A field's rate falls by half when a man lifts the water onto it, because half the increase was his labour.

## Coin, Debt, and the Threshold

Diagram type: Process flow. The guardian who paid in secret.

Start: a man dies having appointed two guardians.
  Down to: one fears the second will neglect khums, zakat, Hajj, and the rest.
  Down to: he pays it himself, secretly.
  Branch on the question, is anything wrong with that:
    - Path A, the estate's debts were discharged on behalf of the deceased: nothing is wrong with it.
    - Path B, the saints are not settled: nothing else clears his obligation, secretly or publicly.
End state: the ruling is not about trust between two men, but the ranking of claims.

Diagram type: Hierarchy. The order five claims leave one estate.

- Rank 1: Zakat
  - Reason: a debt owed from his wealth
- Rank 2: Hajj
  - Reason: a debt, and debt comes before inheritance
- Rank 3: Khums
- Rank 4: Expiation
- Rank 5: Al-Sharwa
Annotation, unsoftened: the other duties are likewise obligatory if something remains in the estate, even if nothing is left for the heirs.
Case producing it: a man dies leaving two hundred dinars, more or less, liable for all five.

Diagram type: Process flow. The clock over money, and what does not stop it.

  Down to: a full year passes over it.
  Branch on what the owner did:

## Grazing Herds and Watered Ground

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The animal that feeds itself against the animal that is fed.

Column A: The grazing animal

- Effect on the owner: wealth increased without his expenditure
- Liability: zakat is due

Column B: The fed animal
- Condition: it is fed by its owner
- Who paid for its increase: the owner
- Effect on the owner: the increase was bought from the same purse
- Liability: none

Shared row: the test is not quantity. In Mukhtasar al-Musannaf, there is no zakat on livestock until they have grazed freely. In the interpretation of these pillars, if a given level yields nothing then nothing is due on it.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The split year, given as it stands.

Whole: a herd that grazes in some of the year and is fed in other parts.
Parts as transmitted:
- The majority of rulings hold that whoever has five camels owes nothing on them in the first part of the year. One sheep is due.
- And nothing more in the second, because in the second a sheep becomes due as our religion stipulates.
Annotation: those clauses contradict themselves in the form they reach us, and are given as they stand.
Two rulings arriving intact beside them:
- If a camel owner owes a camel and has a sheep of his own flock acceptable as a sacrifice, that is also permissible.
- Buffaloes and cows are treated in zakat the same as sheep and goats.

Diagram type: Process flow. The camel scale, as the source gives it.

- Down to thirty five, above ten: a daughter of Labun.
- Down to forty five: a right of the stallion.
- Down to sixty, with every additional five above ten, and when one is added to sixty: a trunk is due in it, and above the trunk something is taken in charity.
- At sixty: one of the camels is a stallion that resembles a stallion and can bear what it can bear.
- End state, one hundred and twenty: two rights are due, because one hundred and twenty is two times sixty.

## The Charity of the Head

Diagram type: Contrast pair. A common practice against the ruling answering it.

Column A: The common practice among Indian scholars
- What is combined: four types, rice, corn, barley, and salt
- What is handed over: their monetary value
- What the payer needs: a price
- The question raised: is a sa' of barley sufficient

Column B: The Shari'ah ruling
- What is owed: a sa' of dates, raisins, barley, or wheat
- What is handed over: the food itself
- What the payer needs: what he has already been eating
- The majority view, in the Pillars: a sa' of barley, wheat, dates, or raisins

Shared row: the guiding principle is that it be from the best food one eats and the best food one stores. Where grains are combined, whatever one ate during the past month of Ramadan is divided proportionally, and whoever ate nothing other than barley will have fulfilled it with barley.
Annotation: rich and poor pay the same volume of different things, and neither is asked for money.

Diagram type: Process flow. The careful man who wanted to pay early.

Start: a man fears distance or travel will prevent him paying at the proper time.
  Down to: he proposes to pay before Ramadan begins.
  Down to: his own objection, the noble Shari'ah holds that obligatory duties are performed at their appointed time and made up only after being missed.
  Branch on why he is early:
    - Path A, out of need: it is mentioned in the Book of Pillars that zakat may be accepted in advance from those obligated to pay it, if there is a need, and such a person is blessed with the answer.
    - Path B, out of ignorance rather than need: if he submits it to one entitled to receive it, it is permissible.
  Down to: the reason both paths clear, the prohibition here is that a person should not break his fast before submitting it, and there is no delay in that.
End state: the obligation is to deliver it.
Annotation: the questioner assumed a fixed appointment. The reply says the guarded boundary is the far one.

Diagram type: Timeline. The hour at which the same act changes category.

Axis: the day of al-Fitr, from the night before to after the prayer.
Events, each with the account fixing it:
- Before dawn, a dhimmi submits his payment. He has discharged his zakat al-fitr. From Mukhtasar al-Musannaf.
- After dawn, the same submission. He has not. From Mukhtasar al-Musannaf.
- Before sunrise. It is zakat al-fitr. From the Keys of Grace, attributed to our master Judge al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad, may God sanctify him, according to what has been transmitted.
- After sunrise. It is charity. From the same book.
- Before the prayer. It is zakat al-fitr.
- After the prayer. It is merely voluntary charity.
Period boundary, disputed: the two accounts set the line at different moments, the prayer in one and sunrise in the other, and both are recorded.
What they agree on: the same food, the same recipient, and the same act change category by the clock. A man who is late has performed a different act and left the required one unperformed.
Name attached: the charity of fitr is called the charity of the head.

Diagram type: Hierarchy. Who counts as a head.

  - Level 2: Servants

  - Level 2: The newborn

  - Level 2: The unborn
    - Level 3: In the Book of Pillars it was also said it is paid on behalf of the foetus before he is born
Annotation: a child a few minutes old is a head, and a head owes a measure of food. The levy counts persons, not earners, and on one reading reaches back past birth.

Diagram type: Contrast pair. The household barred from receiving, and what it still owes.

## What this episode lands

Diagram type: Comparison matrix. Four currencies, one levy.

Rows: coin, animal, measure of grain, head.
Columns: what triggers liability | the rate | what cancels it | when it is taken.
- Coin: the threshold reached, free of encumbrances, a year passed | a fortieth, five dirhams on two hundred, half a mithqal on twenty | debt equal to the holding cancels it outright | after the year, whether the money worked or sat.
- Animal: free grazing, at five camels, thirty or forty cows, forty sheep | one head from forty, and the rising camel scale | being fed rather than grazing | as the herd stands.
- Measure of grain: the kernel forming, at five awsaq, or a tenth of little or much | a tenth by sky and river, half a tenth by channel and bucket, three quarters of a tenth when balanced | nothing, not debt, not the cost of the land | when soundness becomes apparent.
- Head: being a person in the house | one sa' of the food actually eaten | nothing | before the fast is broken.

Diagram type: Process flow. What death does not extinguish.

Start: the man dies.
  Down to: zakat, a debt owed from his wealth.
  Down to: Hajj, because debt comes before inheritance.
  Down to: khums.
  Down to: expiation.
  Down to: al-Sharwa.
End state: the heirs take what is left, if anything is.
Threshold restated: one hundred and ninety five dirhams of silver, or twenty gold dinars, assessed against the cheaper when their prices diverge. Two further figures for the same line are recorded and none reconciled.
Rule restated: swapping one asset for another does not restart the clock.

Diagram type: Visual metaphor. One principle over herd and field alike.

Abstract relation: why an animal and a field are taxed by such different rules and still by one rule.
Metaphor: a levy charged against increase that arrived on its own.
Element assignments:
- Livestock are liable only once they have grazed freely.
- A field watered by sky or river pays a tenth.
- A field watered by channel and bucket pays half of that, because the man lifted the water.
- Liability on grain begins at the kernel, collection waits for soundness.
- The camel scale starts at five and is paid in sheep until twenty five.
- Cows run in bands of thirty and forty.
- Sheep begin at forty and thereafter add one per hundred.
- The rules closing the scale restrain the collector rather than the payer.
- Land carries its own status, tithe or kharaj, through every sale.
Reading that closes it: a man who keeps more than nine tenths of his apparent gain has not underpaid, he has declined to obey.

Diagram type: Annotated structure. The end of the fast, calculated from nothing.

Whole: zakat al-fitr, where no property is measured at all.
Parts:
- The unit: every person owes a measure of the food he actually ate, counted by head.
- The count: his own, his children's, the free and the enslaved in his house, male and female, a child born that morning, and on one reading a child not yet born.
- The recipient: the Imam or his appointee.
- The hour: before the fast is broken.
- The prohibition: never as money in place of food.
- The line: paid before it, zakat al-fitr. Paid after, a voluntary gift, and the obligation stands unperformed.
- The pairing: the household forbidden to receive it is not excused from paying it.
