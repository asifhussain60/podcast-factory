# Special Prayers And Voluntary Devotion

## Where this episode picks up

Timeline. The event that opens the chapter.

Axis: the darkening of Dhu al-Qa'dah, in the year eight hundred and thirty-seven.
- Held beforehand: the treatise of the Brethren of Purity on astronomy, which states that a solar eclipse occurs only at the end of the month.
- Remembered beforehand: the sky has done this before, in the first days of Ramadan, while they were fasting.
- Monday, two hours after sunrise: the eclipse begins.
- Midday: the sun is darkened.
- After: the confusion is written down and sent as a question, and the reply neither defends the astronomy nor discards it.
- Period bands: What the books said, What the sky did, What the answer settled.

Annotated structure. What this chapter collects, in the order it collects them.

- Whole: prayers occasioned by something the ordinary calendar does not schedule.
- Pass one, events: a body that cannot stand, a sun that goes out, an enemy too close to afford the usual length, a sky that will not rain.
- Pass two, the seam at the end of every night: Witr, its qunut, the shafa'ah that precedes it, and the moment dawn closes the door on all of them.
- Pass three, voluntary devotion: the night vigil, the prayer of praise, the prayer of sufficiency, and the prostration a reader owes when a verse of prostration passes under his eye. Here the questions turn from what is owed to what is permitted.
- Annotation: the first pass is forced on the worshipper by the world, the third is chosen by him.

Contrast pair. This chapter against the chapter before it.

Column A: The chapter before
- Who moves: the worshipper
- Question: how displaced is this man really
- Verdict: less displaced than he feels

Column B: This chapter
- Who moves: the world, around a worshipper who stays put
- Question: what does the prayer become when the conditions it assumed are gone
- Verdict: smaller, later, or somebody else's, but it does not stop

Shared row: the direction of travel reverses and the same instinct survives it.

Visual metaphor. The one instinct under all three passes.

- Metaphor: four side doors and no exit.

- Transferred: the man who cannot move his limbs prays with his heart.

## Sickness, Eclipse, Fear, And Drought

Hierarchy. The ladder of the sick man, from the first book of questions and answers and the Book of Purity.

- Level 1: He prays standing, or standing with bowing and prostrating, according to whatever God has excused the servant from in his condition
  - Level 2: If he is unable to do so, he nods with his eyesight
    - Level 3: And if he cannot do that either, he nods with his heart

Annotation: the bottom rung is not an exemption but a prayer inside a body that can signal nothing to anyone. The prayer never stops, it only becomes invisible.

Contrast pair. Two kinds of debt left by illness, from the abridged book of Athar.

Column A: The obligatory prayers missed
- On recovery: must be made up
- The duty: obligatory

Column B: The Sunnah and voluntary prayers missed, for one unable to pray beyond the obligatory prayer
- On recovery: he may make them up if he wishes
- The duty: a virtue and a reward, not obligatory as the obligatory prayers are

Shared row: illness shrinks the worshipper to his obligations and leaves the rest an open invitation rather than an unpaid bill.

Annotated structure. The man who loses his mind, from the abridged classified book.

- Whole: whoever takes something to cure himself and thereby loses his mind.
- The branch the ruling refuses to split on: whether his action was within the character permitted by God, or disobedient.
- The ruling either way: he makes up whatever prayers he missed in that state.
- Annotation: how the man got there changes nothing he owes.

Contrast pair. What the eclipse answer corrects, and what it refuses.

Column A: The astronomy as the questioners held it
- Solar eclipse: only at the end of the month
- Consequence in that year: the sky appeared to contradict the book
- The premise buried in the question: if the sky disagrees with a book, faith is in trouble

Column B: The astronomy as the answer restates it
- Lunar eclipse: on the night of the fifteenth, and on the morning of the tenth
- Solar eclipse: at the beginning of the month or at the end of it
- Consequence: the framework stands, the rule inside it was stated too narrowly
- The premise refused: the science is left standing, the matter placed with God

Shared row: narrated from Mawlana the author of the letters, and explained by the pure Imams and those who follow their guidance.

> The matter is in the hands of God, and there is no power except in God, the Most High, the Great.
> Mawlana the author of the letters, may God's peace be upon him

Process flow. The latecomer to the eclipse prayer, whose arithmetic is unlike the others. The book has already taught how a latecomer joins a congregation under way.

Branch A, he joins at the third rak'ah
  ↓
He follows the imam through his first prayer, bowing and prostrating with him
  ↓
The imam rises from prostration, he stands with him and prays the five rak'ahs following him, prostrating with him

Branch B, he joins at the beginning, before the first two prostrations
  ↓
He prays what remains with the imam, prostrating in those first two prostrations, since one who joins at the beginning but is unable follows along
  ↓
He tracks the imam through the five rak'ahs, prostrating when he prostrates, sitting when he sits

Both branches rejoin
  ↓
The imam makes the final testimony, the latecomer sits and waits
  ↓
The imam says the salutation
  ↓
The latecomer stands and completes the remaining rak'ahs, reciting the qunut, bowing, prostrating, making the final testimony, saying the salutation
  ↓
End: the first two prostrations performed with the imam are not counted, as one who catches up during the prostration of the final obligatory prayers does not count them

Annotated structure. The uncounted prostration.

- Whole: an act performed fully that enters no total.
- Forbidden: to sit idle while the congregation prostrates, and to bank what he did.
- Annotation: the congregation's motion binds him where it earns him nothing.

Contrast pair. Calculation against sight, from the Book of Purity.

Column A: The eclipse that is calculated
- Source of the claim: an astrologer, or anyone else
- Sky: obscured by clouds or something else, so it cannot be seen with the naked eye
- Ruling: the prayer is not performed

Column B: The eclipse that is seen
- Source of the claim: the naked eye
- Sky: the eclipse actually seen
- Ruling: the prayer is prayed

Shared row: the science is accepted as description and refused as trigger. A calculation may not start a prayer.

Annotated structure. The prayer of fear, compressed rather than postponed, from the Book of Purity.

- Whole: the prayer performed in a situation involving danger, conflict, and combat.
- The practice: they perform their prayers with takbir, enlarging the place of each takbir.
- The question put: is the takbir shortened as the prayer itself is shortened.
- The answer: they shorten it just as shortening is obligatory in the prayer.
- Annotation: nothing inside the prayer is exempt from the emergency that shrank it.

Annotated structure. The guard as a condition of the act, from Mukhtasar al-Musannaf.

- Whole: the imam prays the prayer of fear with three people as his guard.
- The failure case: their number falls below three.
- The ruling: it is not recommended for them to pray it, so the group does not fall to fewer than three.
- Annotation: the guard is not an escort outside the act, it is one of the act's conditions.

Hierarchy. The order when extraordinary prayers converge, from Mukhtasar al-Musannaf.

- Level 1: First, the funeral prayer
- Level 1: Second, the eclipse prayer
- Level 1: Third, the prayer of fear

Annotation: the dead go first. The sky and the enemy wait.

Annotated structure. The prayer for rain, from al-Iqtisar, al-Akhbar, and al-Muntakhabah.

- Whole: four hundred utterances in four directions, in a raised voice.
- Facing the people: one hundred takbirs.
- Turned to his right: one hundred praises.
- Turned to his left: one hundred expressions of joy.
- Facing the people again: one hundred praises, and he glorified Him.
- Annotation: the only prayer here whose shape is liturgical rather than defensive.

Process flow. The order of the rain day, framed by the Book of Purity.

Start: two sermons, as is done for the two feasts, whose gathered order the book has already set out
  ↓
He descends, departs, and dismisses them
  ↓
He performs the prayer in a bow, in Arabic if he finds the Arabic, as the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, did
  ↓
Branch on the day: going out on a day other than Monday is also acceptable
  ↓
Correction from the abridgement of al-Musannaf: whoever delivers the sermon first should pray and then deliver the khutbah

Contrast pair. What the four emergencies ask of the worshipper.

Column A: Sickness, eclipse, and fear
- Direction of the demand: downward
- What is lacking: capacity, visibility, or safety
- The response: descend the ladder, wait on sight, shorten the prayer and its takbir

Column B: Drought
- Direction of the demand: upward
- What is lacking: rain
- The response: four hundred utterances, four directions, a raised voice, two sermons, in the open
- Why more and not less: what the community lacks it cannot shorten its way out of

Shared row: three ask less, and the fourth asks the largest sound this book has yet required.

## Witr, Qunut, And The Dawn

Annotated structure. The sentence that contradicts itself on purpose, from the landmarks of the religion.

- Whole: the standing of Witr among the categories of prayer.
- Claim one: Witr prayer is obligatory and it is not permitted to abandon it.
- Claim two: even though it is a Sunnah.
- Claim three: and it is among the appendages of the obligatory prayers, hanging off them rather than standing beside them as a peer.
- Annotation: almost every question here follows from that instability, and the chapter spends more words on Witr than on the eclipse, the sick, the enemy, and the drought together.

Comparison matrix. Three reports on a forgotten qunut, reported and not reconciled.

Rows: the short answer in Witr, the short answer in Fajr, the fuller answer, the third report
Columns: what the worshipper does | the prayer's standing

- Short answer, Witr: he must repeat the qunut | the prayer continues
- Short answer, Fajr: the prostration of omission, the remedy the book has already taught for a gap in an obligatory prayer | the prayer is repaired
- Fuller answer, remembering after he has bowed, prostrated, or sat: no repetition required, and no prostration of omission in the Sunnah prayers | the prayer stands
- Third report, carried elsewhere in the compilation: the two prostrations of forgetfulness after the salutation | the prayer is repaired

Annotation: the compilation reports the three rather than settling among them.

Contrast pair. The axis the fuller answer turns on.

Column A: The qunut forgotten
- The will behind the gap: none
- Verdict: the prayer stands, repetition is not required

Column B: The qunut deliberately omitted
- The will behind the gap: deliberate
- Verdict: whoever deliberately omits it in Witr repeats it

Shared row: two opposite verdicts on the same missing words, a strikingly interior test for a law that spends most of its pages on limbs, garments, and directions.

Annotated structure. Having made the qunut serious, the chapter makes it small.

- Whole: the minimum that fulfils the qunut.
- Position: recited after the worshipper says, "Our Lord, to You belongs all praise".
- The minimum: three tasbihs, or three takbirs.
- The verdict: whoever achieves that has fulfilled it.
- Annotation: what must never be deliberately abandoned had better be something nobody has an excuse to abandon.

Contrast pair. What breaks a sequence, tested on the shafa'ah.

Column A: The man who gets up
- The act: he prays the shafa'ah then engages in something until dawn arrives
- Reading: departure
- Ruling: he repeats the shafa'ah as a make-up prayer

Column B: The man who stays in his place
- The act: he prayed three rak'ahs and remained sitting until dawn breaks
- Reading: continuation
- Ruling: he does not repeat the three prayers but sits on with the intention of making them up

Shared row: the man who stayed in his place has not left the prayer though he stopped praying.

Annotated structure. The two maxims the questioner sets against each other.

- The question: is it permissible to pray from the first rak'ah of Witr, speaking or connecting the ihram to the ihram.
- Maxim one: the prayer of one who is seated is half the prayer of one who is standing.
- Maxim two: the prayer is begun by the takbir and concluded by the salutation.
- The question inside the question: which does he act upon, and the answer goes not to a third maxim but to verse.

Visual metaphor. The thread, from al-Mukhtar of our master the Judge al-Nu'man, the highest of the world, may God sanctify him.

- Abstract relation: what severs a prayer in progress.
- Metaphor: a thread, not broken by being touched but by being cut.
- The cut: the salutation.
- Not a cut: actions, and words unless the speech is prolonged.
- The measurement withheld: how long is prolonged is left, like the qunut's verdict, to the worshipper.

> The thread of prayer may be broken by the salutation
> Without any actions or words.
> If one must speak, there is no harm in that, unless the speech is prolonged.
> Our master the Judge al-Nu'man, in al-Mukhtar

Contrast pair. The two dawns.

Column A: The first dawn, which the Arabs of the desert call al-kadhib
- As a boundary: none
- Ruling: the shafa'ah prayer and Witr are performed
- Also permitted: the two rak'ahs of the Sunnah of Fajr

Column B: The true dawn, the second Fajr
- As a boundary: absolute
- Ruling: the shafa'ah prayer and Witr are precluded once it has set in

Shared row: the false light is no boundary, and only the true one closes the door.

Process flow. The man who bows before his imam at Fajr.

Start: he prays with the imam at Fajr and bows before him
  ↓
He raises his head and bows again with the imam in bowing
  ↓
Ruling: his prayer is valid, being like that of one who prays alone
  ↓
Branch: if the imam prays alone, he repeats the prayer

Process flow. The greeting of the mosque offered after the seal.

Start: a person has prayed the prayer of intercession
  ↓
He enters the mosque and prays its greeting during the time of intercession
  ↓
Ruling: he must repeat the intercession, having no right to pray after it until he has prayed Fajr
  ↓
Correct order: when dawn comes he prays the greeting, then Fajr

Annotation: the book has already taught that entering a mosque carries its own two rak'ahs. That courtesy is suspended here because the shafa'ah has sealed the night. The greeting is not wrong, it is out of order, and being out of order costs him a completed prayer.

Annotated structure. The form of Witr in full, from the Book of Purity.

- Whole: two rak'ahs that are part of Witr.
- First rak'ah: the opening of the book and a surah, "Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of the daybreak".
- Second rak'ah: the opening of the book and a surah, "Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind".
- Then: testify and give salutation.
- Seated: one spreads the palms upon the thighs, fingertips resting on the knees, and reads.
- Bowing: one places the palms on the knees, likewise when standing.
- Rising from bowing: one places the right leg behind, lifts the feet, prostrates, then sits in testimony as one sits in prayer.
- Annotation: not a second lesson in posture but a closing choice. The two surahs of refuge are the last words a believer says over his own night.

Process flow. The misjudged seam, the twenty-second question put to our master Muhammad ibn Fahd in his honorable message.

Start: a person prays the prayer of intercession and Witr, sits as long as he is able, then prays Fajr as an obligatory prayer
  ↓
It later becomes clear that his dawn had come before it
  ↓
Ruling one: his prayer of intercession and his Witr stand confirmed
  ↓
Ruling two: he is required to repeat, as a Sunnah, and to make it obligatory
  ↓
Exception: if dawn is near him, the Sunnah prayer is also confirmed, because the books of the saints of God, peace be upon them, mention that it is permissible to pray it before Fajr on account of the noble meaning that permits this
  ↓
End: wrong about the sky and right in his intention, he keeps what he did and is still asked to do it again

> Place your trust in God when you bow down at Fajr after you have finished it, for in it desires are granted.
> The Commander of the Believers, Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, from his four hundred teachings

Annotated structure. Arrears before extras, from our master Sharaf al-Din Ja'far ibn Sayyidina Sulayman, in Elegant Psychological Gardens.

- The question: as for one who must make up Witr, is it permissible for him to pray voluntarily.
- The permission: it is permissible.
- The priority: offering the Witr as a make-up for what was missed comes first.
- Annotation: the order the sick man's ruling set at the start of this chapter, restated at its end.

## Voluntary Devotion And Its Limits

Comparison matrix. Fixed recitations, asked for and not found.

Rows: the opening prayer, the prayer of praise, the prayer of Tahajjud, Witr and the Eid prayer
Columns: is a recitation transmitted | what the worshipper reads

- The opening prayer: nothing fixed | whatever he wishes, as in Sunnah and nafl prayers
- The prayer of praise: nothing fixed | whatever he wishes
- The prayer of Tahajjud: nothing fixed | whatever he wishes
- Witr and the Eid prayer: a recitation is specified | where he does not know it, he recites as much as he is able and should not omit the prayer

Annotation: the prayer outranks its own contents. A man who cannot supply the words is never told to stay away.

Annotated structure. Tahajjud, latitude and then a shape.

- Has memorized its supplication: he prays with the supplication.
- Has not: he prays as much as he is able and intends Tahajjud.
- The count: twelve rak'ahs.
- The times: it has two, one of them between Maghrib and Isha'.
- Annotation: intention carries what memory cannot.

Process flow. Tarawih under taqiyyah, a question about survival rather than devotion.

Start: the believers pray with the imam for taqiyyah purposes, holding the intention of voluntary prayer
  ↓
What the imam does: recites the Quran from beginning to end, which is not the practice in the obligatory prayers, nor in Witr, nor in the Sunnah of Fajr, nor in the greetings
  ↓
The ruling: there is nothing wrong with taqiyyah, but they do not complete it
  ↓
End: precaution is granted without embarrassment and without a lecture, and the line is drawn at completion

Comparison matrix. The reckoning of rak'ahs from the book of evidence and statement, and what taqiyyah does to it.

Rows: Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha'
Columns: the count given | if taqiyyah costs him one

- Dhuhr: ten | he makes it up
- Asr: eight | he makes it up
- Maghrib: five | he makes it up
- Isha': ten | he makes it up

Annotation: the prevailing view is based on the pillars, the gathering of rulings, and a person who omits something from the Sunnah for taqiyyah makes it up. Concealment suspends the performance and does not cancel the debt, the sick man's ruling in different clothes.

Process flow. The mechanics of the prayer of praise.

Start: the worshipper raises his head from the second prostration
  ↓
He sits in the position of alertness, upright, not standing, and glorifies "My Master" ten times
  ↓
He rises for the second rak'ah, and after it sits in that position again and glorifies ten times
  ↓
He sits in testimony and gives salutation
  ↓
Loop: the same procedure in the last two rak'ahs
  ↓
Alternative permitted: to testify after the glorification while remaining seated in the glorification posture

Comparison matrix. The prayer of al-Kifayah, an arithmetic of its own.

Rows: first rak'ah, second rak'ah, third rak'ah, fourth rak'ah
Columns: al-Fatihah | al-Ikhlas | al-Kafirun | the verse of the Throne

- First rak'ah: once | once | once | once
- Second rak'ah: once | twice | twice | twice
- Third rak'ah: once | three times | three times | three times
- Fourth rak'ah: once | four times | four times | four times

Annotation: asked whether al-Fatihah climbs with the rest, the answer is that one recites it once in each rak'ah, and God knows best. It is the prayer's spine, not one of the items being multiplied.

Annotated structure. The loose permission set beside those precise forms.

- The counsel from the summary of effects, and the Quranic sentence that says the same:

> Whoever has not memorized the Quran, let him recite what is available to him.
> So recite what is available of the Quran.
> Quran 73:20

- The options: whoever wishes may pass the whole night with one surah, repeating it, or with part of one, or with two or more.
- Annotation: a night spent on one repeated surah is not a poor night.

Contrast pair. The night prayer against the make-up, from the brief speech in the jurisprudence, where Sheikh Hasan ibn al-Mawla Adam al-Hindi asks and the masters of Yemen answer.

Column A: The night prayer
- The case: whoever prays whatever he wishes of nafl prayer between the obligation of the latter prayer and its negation
- What it is not: a make-up, and he does not intend it as one

Column B: The make-up
- The case: a prayer owed and missed
- What it is not: discharged by something offered under another name

Shared row: that prayer is its own thing with its own name, not an instalment against arrears.

## The Limits Devotion May Not Set For Itself

Timeline. The occasion behind the Umm Dawud letter, written to the compiler of this book by our master and Mawlana Ja'far ibn Sayyidina Sulayman.

Axis: the year 1042, with the men in prison.
- Rabi' al-Awwal of 1042: the fifteenth falls on a Friday.
- That month: Qasim ibn Feer, the caller of the band al-Akmahiyyah al-Dawudiyyah, commands his companions to pray Umm Dawud, to fast, and to supplicate.
- Ramadan of that year: he commands it again.
- The question sent: was that permissible, and was that its practice.

Annotated structure. The answer that holds the line.

- What Mawlana al-Sadiq, peace be upon him, wrote: Umm Dawud is mentioned only for the month of Rajab, and he commanded that one purify oneself for it.
- Asked whether it might be called upon with supplication outside Rajab, he said:

> On the day of Arafah, and if it falls on a Friday, his companion will not be emptied until he has been forgiven.

- What is absent: no symbol indicating otherwise, and no stipulation beyond seeking forgiveness in the month named, the opening supplication being sufficient.
- The second permitted occasion: the day of Arafah that is not a Friday, and that day alone.
- The instruction: offer it only in accordance with what is stipulated. If they have it, let them follow it, otherwise let them do a little.
- The ground:

> A little done consistently in accordance with the Sunnah is better than a great deal of innovation.
> The Prophet, peace and blessings of God be upon him

- Annotation: men in prison, reaching for a named prayer and a named fast on a day that looked auspicious. Nothing in the reply doubts their sincerity. It refuses to let devotion generate its own calendar.

Contrast pair. Two voluntary acts that look symmetrical and are not, answered by some of the loyal preachers of Yemen to the sheikhs of India.

Column A: The Sunnah fast abandoned
- The question: should the believers fast the Sunnah fasts as they pray the Sunnah prayers
- The ruling: it is permissible to break it without making it up
- What is left behind: nothing, and they have their reward from God if they fast

Column B: The Sunnah prayer missed
- The ruling: it must be made up, as the Imams, peace be upon them, and their people did
- What is left behind: a debt

Shared row: Witr's strange position at the top of this chapter is the rule for prayer in miniature. What is offered freely does not stay free.

Process flow. The widest correspondence, from Salih ibn Ahmad to our master the gift of God ibn Sayyidina Ibrahim, in 1135.

Start: the books mention that the prophets, such as Jesus and others, and the guides of the household, Ali ibn Abi Talib, Mawlana Zayn al-Abidin, and others among the Imams, may God's prayers be upon them all, would at the time of a great calamity call for water, perform ablution, and pray two rak'ahs, and in some cases four, for the relief of distress
  ↓
The gap: it is not clear at what time they prayed
  ↓
The question: at any and all times of hardship, calamity, and disaster, or only at certain times, according to Sunnah and nafl prayer
  ↓
A distinction first: the testimony of Mawlay Badr al-Din, the inspiration, makes clear that such prayers of the prophets and the Imams are distinguished, arising from their gratitude for what He has imparted to them of the secret of things
  ↓
Then the gap explained: their reports mentioned it in a general statement rather than specifying times
  ↓
The ruling: it is permissible to pray Sunnah and nafl prayers at all times when such prayers are permitted
  ↓
The limit: only the known prohibited times, not hidden to the sound mind, which the book has already fixed and does not fix again
  ↓
End: the answer routes the matter back through the guidance the book has taught from its first chapter

Annotated structure. The closing instruction of that answer.

- Who is addressed: their slaves and Shi'a, for whom the picture is illuminated.
- The wish: to pray that mentioned prayer and thereby repel all calamities and satisfy all evils and defects.
- The five instructions: let them be guided by their guidance, follow their Sunnah, obey them, be supported by their intercession, be sustained by their secret and their blessings.

Contrast pair. That answer set beside the Umm Dawud letter.

Column A: Time
- Verdict: open
- Evidence: Sunnah and nafl prayers are permissible whenever such prayers are permitted
- Only limit: the known prohibited hours, fixed elsewhere in the book

Column B: Form
- Verdict: not invented
- Evidence: Umm Dawud belongs to Rajab and to the day of Arafah, and to nothing a devout man selects for himself
- Only remedy: let them do a little consistently

Shared row: the chapter is generous about when and immovable about what.

Contrast pair. The verse of prostration, and the teacher who cannot obey either rule.

Column A: The reciter of a supplication containing a verse of prostration
- The question: is he required to prostrate
- The answer: if it is a firm recitation, he must
- Workability: a single occasion, easily met

Column B: The teacher of boys teaching them the Quran
- The question: must he prostrate every time he and those who hear him pass a place of prostration
- The answer: I prefer that he prostrate, and I also prefer that he leave it
- Workability: either rule taken strictly makes his day unworkable, since he would spend the lesson on the floor, or pass the verses without honouring one

Shared row: approving both paths is not evasion, it is what lets the lesson continue.

Annotated structure. The remaining prostration rules, from Mukhtasar al-Musannaf and the sayings set beside it.

- What is said in it: others say that what a man says in the prostration of the Quran is what is said during prostration.
- First recitation: if the reciter recites a prostration from among the prayers, he prostrates in his position.
- Second recitation in the same place: neither of them is required to prostrate again as long as he remains in his position.
- The remaining prostrations: whoever wishes to prostrate may do so, and whoever does not wish need not.
- Where the hedging stops: prostration is praiseworthy, and the better course is to prostrate.

> Prolong the prostration, for nothing is harder on Satan than to see the son of Adam prostrating. Satan was commanded to prostrate and refused, while the son of Adam was commanded to prostrate and obeyed, and was saved.
> Mawlana, the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, in his four hundred points of conduct

## What this episode lands

Comparison matrix. Every interruption, and what it is not allowed to do.

Rows: the sick man, the community under threat, the concealed believer, the eclipse behind cloud, the misjudged dawn, the man who lost his mind
Columns: what is taken away | what happens to the prayer | what never happens

- The sick man: the use of his limbs | he descends from standing to his eyes to his heart | no rung of that ladder is marked excused
- The community under threat: safety and time | the prayer shortens, and the takbir with it | the prayer is not abandoned
- The concealed believer: the freedom to pray as his own | he declines to complete an unfamiliar row and makes up afterwards | the debt is not cancelled
- The eclipse behind cloud: sight of the event | no prayer is performed | no astrologer's word starts it
- The misjudged dawn: certainty about the hour | his intercession and Witr stand confirmed and he repeats them | what he did honestly is not taken from him
- The man who lost his mind: his reason, by his own remedy | he makes up whatever he missed | how he got there changes nothing

Named-axis 2x2. Where the chapter gives and where it holds.

Axes:
- Horizontal: who set the occasion, from The world set it to The worshipper chose it
- Vertical: what the tradition does, from Grants latitude to Holds the line

Quadrants:
- Top-left, the world set it and latitude granted: the sick man nodding with his heart, the shortened prayer of fear, the taqiyyah row left uncompleted. Hardship is met by shrinking the act.
- Bottom-left, the world set it and the line held: the eclipse behind cloud. Science accepted as description, refused as trigger.
- Top-right, the worshipper chose it and latitude granted: relief prayed for at any hour, whatever is memorized recited, a whole night on one repeated surah. Time and contents are open.
- Bottom-right, the worshipper chose it and the line held: Umm Dawud offered in prison on an auspicious Friday outside Rajab. Devotion may not generate its own calendar.

  - Level 2: obligatory and not permitted to be abandoned
  - Level 2: and a Sunnah
  - Level 2: and an appendage of the obligatory prayers

  - Level 2: a missing qunut is judged by the will, not the gap

Contrast pair. The asymmetry the chapter leaves standing.

Column A: The voluntary fast abandoned
- What remains: nothing

Column B: The voluntary prayer missed
- What remains: a debt

Shared row: a chapter about what may be shortened, postponed, concealed, restricted, or made up ends by confirming that prayer, alone among voluntary acts, keeps its claim.

Annotated structure. The closing image.

- Whole: the last word of a chapter built out of interruptions.
- What precedes it: hundreds of rulings about reduction, deferral, transfer, and repayment.
- The act itself: the cheapest thing in the book to perform.
- The recommendation: the only one given without a condition, prolong the prostration.
- The reason given: not legal but old, reaching back to the first refusal, and to the creature asked the same thing who agreed.
