# Foundations of Faith and Guardianship

## Where this episode opens

Annotated structure. Whole: the chapter's two layers, an authorial wrapper and the teaching it encloses.

- Part 1, the wrapper, outermost: Faidh Ali bin Sayyidna Sulaiman names the work, Iṣāf al-Ṭālib fī Jamīʿ al-Maṭālib.
- Part 2, the wrapper's claim: he has gathered scattered rulings from the books of the saints of God and from their learned preachers.
- Part 3, the wrapper's purpose: so that seekers may understand the doctrine of the Ahl al-Bayt.
- Part 4, the teaching, innermost: faith, walaya, the difference between Islam and iman, and the line of guidance by which religious life becomes more than imitation.
- Annotation on the frame: the wrapper orients, it does not instruct. Everything the chapter teaches begins at Part 4.

Contrast pair. Two ways to hold religion, set against each other attribute by attribute.

Column A: imitation
- What is copied: the worship of God and the call of the Ahl al-Bayt
- What is absent: knowledge
- What is absent: action
- Standing before God: no covenant, no honor
- Standing before His guardian: no covenant, no honor
- Verdict of the source: no right to claim the name of faith, because he has departed from faith

Column B: formed faith
- What is present: right attachment
- What is present: right knowledge
- What is present: right practice
- What is present: right recognition of the guides through whom the meanings of religion are preserved
- Standing: faith as a formed life, not a badge attached to inherited behavior

Shared row: both may look identical from outside. The movements are copied, the names repeated, the group identity inherited. The difference is not in the forms but in whether heart and act have been joined to knowledge.

Process flow. The instability the opening proposition exposes.

Start: imitation without knowledge
  ↓
Unstable, nothing anchors the resemblance
  ↓
Knowledge without action
  ↓
Unproven, the claim is never tested
  ↓
Action without walaya
  ↓
Loses the line by which religion is interpreted
  ↓
End: knowledge, action, and walaya held in one house

Visual metaphor. Relation: what kind of text this chapter is. Metaphor: a stocked treasury, not a courtroom.

- The courtroom reading, rejected: arguments advanced against an opponent, each carrying the next.
- The treasury reading, held throughout: numbered structures, scriptural phrases, inherited sayings, legal and devotional practices, and stories in which knowledge exposes authority, gathered and shelved.
- Reading: the chapter is not proving a thesis to a skeptic. It is laying in what the rest of the book will draw on.

Hierarchy tree. The chapter's three movements and what each one carries.

- Movement 1: levels and duties
  - The eight levels of faith, the first level named
  - The boundary line against imitation
  - The seven duties and the twelve obligations
  - The saying on the seventy-three sects
- Movement 2: the walaya of the Father of Imams
  - Scriptural phrases read through the Imams
  - The pen and the tablet in a Friday sermon
  - Three interrogations in which questions reveal who carries knowledge
- Movement 3: peace and belonging
  - The Sunnah of salām, seven forms above and twelve below
  - The prayers for the borders of religion
  - The etiquette of greeting and reply

Annotation: faith begins as an inward claim, and the author at once tests it through parents, neighbors, prayer, greeting, truthful speech, and the living chain of guidance.

## The levels of faith

Annotated structure. Whole: the sentence from the noble books with which the teaching begins.

> Know, my brother, that faith has eight levels.
> Attribution: the noble books, as the source cites them.

- Part 1, what is counted: levels, plural and ordered.
- Part 2, what is named here: the first of them only.
- Part 3, what the first level contains: not an inward state but a map of obligation.
- Annotation: the first face of faith is not a private feeling. Faith reaches outward before it explains itself inwardly.

Hierarchy tree. The first level, opened into its named classes of claim.

- Level 1 of faith: charity toward those who hold a claim
  - Class 1: parents
  - Class 2: relatives
  - Class 3: orphans
  - Class 4: the needy
  - Class 5: the neighbor who is a relative
  - Class 6: the neighbor who is nearby
  - Class 7: the companion at one's side
  - Class 8: the traveler
  - Class 9: those whom one's faith obligates one to care for

Comparison matrix. Each named class, and what the source's method puts to the test through it.

| Class of claim | Relation to the person | What it examines |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | Origin of life | Gratitude for a debt that precedes any choice |
| Relatives | Given, not chosen | Loyalty beyond preference |
| Orphans | No standing protector | Compassion toward those who cannot repay |
| The needy | Dependent on others | Whether mercy survives inconvenience |
| Neighbor who is a relative | Both kin and proximity | Two claims arriving at once |
| Neighbor who is nearby | Proximity alone | The difference between convenience and duty |
| The companion at one's side | Temporary adjacency | Whether care extends past the permanent circle |
| The traveler | Passing through | Whether care reaches one who brings no leverage |
| Those whom faith obligates | Created by religion | Whether the eye has been trained to see a claim |

Annotation on the last row: the phrase leaves the circle open on purpose, because religion may create claims that ordinary social instinct would never register.

Genealogy chain. Every claim in this movement arrives attributed. One chain per line, direction of transmission left to right.

- The noble books → the statement that faith has eight levels → the opening of the teaching.
- The Mustaṇṣiriyya Councils → the fourth council of the Quran → the Imams of religion described as seven, and the twelve proofs appearing in the world.
- Mawlānā the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib → his four hundred precepts → the saying on the seventy-three sects.

Annotated structure. Whole: the boundary line, placed immediately after the first level.

> Whoever imitates the worship of God and the call of the Ahl al-Bayt without knowledge and without action has no right to claim the name of faith.
> Attribution: the source text of the opening section.

- Part 1, what is withdrawn: the name of faith.
- Part 2, what is withdrawn: covenant and honor before God.
- Part 3, what is withdrawn: covenant and honor before His guardian.
- Part 4, the stated reason: because he has departed from faith.
- Annotation: the phrase is not casual. The warning is against religion reduced to performed resemblance.

Visual metaphor. Relation: what the fourth council establishes about the Imams of religion. Metaphor: recurrence, a cycle that returns without ending.

- The figure given: the Imams of religion are seven, recurring in this world as Fridays recur.
- The horizon: until the Resurrection.
- Alongside them: the twelve proofs, appearing in the world.
- Consequence stated by the source: to know the limits of the testimony of God is to perform its rights.
- Reading: the structure is not a single historical arrangement that lapsed. It returns, and the returning is what makes the rights performable in any age.

## The levels of faith, the seven and the twelve

Hierarchy tree. The rights of the testimony of God, given in two groups.

- Group A: seven duties for religion
  - Duty 1: walaya
  - Duty 2: purity
  - Duty 3: prayer
  - Duty 4: zakāt
  - Duty 5: fasting
  - Duty 6: Ḥajj
  - Duty 7: jihad
- Group B: twelve obligations for the pious
  - Obligation 1: honoring parents
  - Obligation 2: maintaining ties of kinship
  - Obligation 3: protecting the neighbor
  - Obligation 4: enjoining good
  - Obligation 5: forbidding evil
  - Obligation 6: truthfulness in one's home
  - Obligation 7: good conduct with one's spouse
  - Obligation 8: kindness to those in one's care
  - Obligation 9: spreading peace
  - Obligation 10: feeding others
  - Obligation 11: building brotherhood
  - Obligation 12: visiting the sick

Annotation on Group B: the source adds that these are not obligatory for women. Presented here as the source's own legal formulation, with no expansion the source does not make.

Annotated structure. Whole: the order of the seven, dissected for what the ordering asserts.

- Part 1, position one: walaya, placed first.
- Part 2, what the placement rules out: guardianship as a later decoration appended to the ritual duties.
- Part 3, what follows: purity, prayer, almsgiving, fasting, pilgrimage, struggle, six acts.
- Part 4, what the order asserts: action must be joined to recognition.
- Annotation: religion is not a schedule of obligations. It is a life ordered by an acknowledged guide, and walaya is the gate through which the rest are rightly understood.

Contrast pair. The two groups as two axes of one structure, set against each other.

Column A: the seven duties, the vertical structure
- Direction: upward, toward the source of guidance
- Opens with: walaya
- Governs: purity, prayer, zakāt, fasting, Ḥajj, jihad
- What it prevents: duties becoming private self-discipline detached from guidance
- Failure mode if taken alone: the chapter collapses into a list of offices

Column B: the twelve obligations, the horizontal spread
- Direction: outward, into the world
- Opens with: parents
- Governs: kin, neighbor, moral command, household, spouse, dependents, peace, food, brotherhood, illness
- What it prevents: walaya becoming a claim of attachment that never becomes mercy
- Failure mode if taken alone: the chapter collapses into a moral handbook

Shared row: the author wants neither reduction. The point is a formed religious life, where obedience, knowledge, and recognition occupy the same house.

Process flow. The pedagogy in the order of the twelve, traced as a movement outward.

Start: those through whom life is received, the parents
  ↓
Kinship, the given circle
  ↓
The neighbor, proximity without blood
  ↓
Moral speech, enjoining good and forbidding evil
  ↓
Inside the house, truthfulness and conduct with a spouse
  ↓
Dependents, those in one's care
  ↓
Outward again, peace, feeding, brotherhood
  ↓
End: a bedside, the visiting of the sick

Reading: the list runs from the authority of God and His guardian all the way to a sickroom. The highest doctrine is not betrayed by the smallest mercy, it is confirmed there.

Named-axis 2x2. The central tension of the movement, organized on two independent axes.

Axes:
- Horizontal: conduct, from absent to embodied
- Vertical: walaya, from unrecognized to recognized

Quadrants:
- Bottom-left, conduct absent and walaya unrecognized: the imitator. He copies worship and the call without knowledge or action, and the source withdraws the name of faith from him.
- Bottom-right, conduct embodied and walaya unrecognized: the moral handbook. Parents honored, neighbors protected, the sick visited, with no line by which religion is interpreted.
- Top-left, conduct absent and walaya recognized: the list of offices. The hierarchy known and named, attachment claimed, mercy never reached.
- Top-right, conduct embodied and walaya recognized: the formed religious life the chapter is building, where the seven and the twelve are one structure and not two.

Timeline. The saying of the Commander of the Faithful on division, ordered as succession rather than date.

- Before: the Children of Israel divide into seventy-two sects.
- After: this nation divides into seventy-three.
- Outcome band 1: one of them is in Paradise.
- Outcome band 2: the rest are in the Fire.
- Attribution: Mawlānā the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, in his four hundred precepts.

Annotation: not every religious affiliation is an equal path here. The many sects are the visible result of losing the true center, and salvation is joined to the living line of interpretation and obedience.

## Guardianship and knowledge

Comparison matrix. Scriptural phrases as Jaʿfar ibn Manṣūr al-Yaman reads them in the Book of the Foster-Suckling.

| Phrase cited | Broad moral reading | Reading through the Imams | What the reading fixes |
|---|---|---|---|
| God is his master, Jibrīl and the righteous of the believers | An unnamed company of the virtuous | The Imam of the believers, who is ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, the guide of every believer | An office, not a description |
| Their father was righteous | An ancestor of good character | An Imam | A station, not a reputation |
| The earth is inherited by My righteous servants | Virtue eventually prevails | The Imams from among God's servants | A mode of inheritance, not a sentiment |

Annotation on the method: words like righteous and heirs are not left as broad moral terms. They are tied to an office, a lineage, and a mode of inheritance. In this reading the earth is not inherited by anonymous virtue, it is inherited through the righteous servants who carry the knowledge and authority of God among creation.

Visual metaphor. Relation: how guidance stands between the seen and the unseen. Metaphor: a pen inscribing a tablet, given by Mawlānā the Imam al-Muʿizz li-Dīn Allāh in a Friday sermon.

- The likeness he draws: himself in the physical world as a pen in the spiritual world.
- Beneath it: four ranks, set in order.
- Each rank: has a spiritual rank guiding it toward the one beyond.
- The foundation: faces the tablet and engraves in it what it derives from the spiritual world, as a pen engraves in a tablet.
- Reading: guidance is not only historical succession. It is a correspondence between the seen and unseen orders, where knowledge descends, is inscribed, and is made available through proper ranks.

Hierarchy tree. The same sermon read downward, levels named.

- Level 1: the spiritual world, source of what is derived
  - Level 2: the pen
    - Level 3: four ranks set beneath, each guided toward the one beyond it
      - Level 4: the foundation, facing the tablet
        - Level 5: the tablet, in which what was derived is engraved

## Guardianship and knowledge, the three interrogations

Comparison matrix. Three reports in which questions reveal who carries knowledge.

| Report | Who sends | Who carries | Where | Who fails first | Who answers | Domain of the questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Report 1 | The king of Rūm | A messenger with money and a test | Medina | Abū Bakr, then ʿUmar | ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib | Theology, the limits of divine knowledge |
| Report 2 | Ibn al-Aṣfar, through Muʿāwiyah | A Syrian messenger | al-Raḥba | No one is asked before the household | al-Ḥasan, chosen by the questioner | Creation, scripture, geography, law |
| Report 3 | ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb himself | ʿUmar himself | Not named | Not applicable, he comes directly | ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib | The soul, dreams, love, memory |

Process flow. Report 1, from instruction to distribution.

Start: the king of Rūm sends a messenger to Medina with money and a test
  ↓
Instruction carried: go to Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, and if the Prophet does not agree with him, ask about his successor and put proof before him
  ↓
Three questions posed: what is not God's, what is not with God, what God does not know
  ↓
Branch A: Abū Bakr grows angry and treats the questions as disbelief
Branch B: ʿUmar responds in much the same way
Branch C: ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās sees that the problem is not the questions but the inability to answer them, and directs the man to ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib
  ↓
The Father of Imams comes out with a pen behind his ear and ink on his finger
  ↓
The three answers are given
  ↓
End: the messenger bears witness, and the money passes to al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn to distribute among the Muslims

Comparison matrix. The three questions of Report 1, and what each answer protects.

| Question | How it could be heard | The answer given | What the answer protects |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is not God's | As impiety, a denial of His dominion | God has no partner | Divine unity |
| What is not with God | As accusation | Injustice | Divine justice |
| What does God not know | As a denial of omniscience | That the Messiah is the son of God, meaning no such thing exists and God has no knowledge of what has no existence | That nonexistence is not a hidden fact inside God's knowledge |

Annotation: the knower does not panic before difficult speech. He discerns what the speech is asking and restores it to truth.

Annotated structure. Whole: the messenger's closing testimony, dissected.

> I bear witness that there is no god but God, that Muhammad is the Messenger of God, and that you are his guardian.
> Attribution: the messenger of the king of Rūm, in Report 1.

- Part 1: the confession of God's unity.
- Part 2: the confession of prophecy.
- Part 3: the confession of guardianship, arrived at last and through the first two.
- Annotation: knowledge reveals walaya. The proof of knowledge then ends in generosity, the money passing to al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn for distribution, so the right answer is followed by right distribution.

Annotated structure. Whole: Report 2 at al-Raḥba, and the lament placed before the answers.

- Part 1, the approach: a man greets the Commander of the Faithful and is discovered to be a messenger sent by Muʿāwiyah, carrying questions from Ibn al-Aṣfar.
- Part 2, the lament, spoken before any question is answered: the people severed his ties of kinship, wasted his days, denied his right, diminished his station, and conspired against him.
- Part 3, the delegation: he calls al-Ḥasan, al-Ḥusayn, and Muḥammad, and tells the Syrian to ask whichever he wishes.
- Part 4, the choice: the Syrian chooses al-Ḥasan.
- Annotation: the report places knowledge inside injustice. Guardianship is not a solitary brilliance that dies with one figure, the household itself is a school of inherited knowledge.

Comparison matrix. The Syrian's questions and al-Ḥasan's answers, with the measure each answer supplies.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| The distance between truth and falsehood | Four fingers, what the eyes see is truth, what the ears may hear is often falsehood |
| The distance between heaven and earth | The prayer of the oppressed and the reach of sight |
| The distance between east and west | The arc of the sun in a single day |
| The meaning of the galaxy | The lights of the sky from which rain descends |
| The rainbow | God's bow, and a sign of safety from drowning |
| The darkening of the moon | Explained through the verse on the two signs |
| The first thing that appeared on earth | The valley of Dab |
| The first thing that moved upon it | The palm tree |
| The spring to which the souls of the polytheists flee | Salma |
| The hermaphrodite | A legal question of discernment, with signs by which the body is examined |

Annotation: the answers train the listener to see measures, a bodily span between eye and ear, a moral span and an optical one between heaven and earth, a span of time rendered as distance between east and west. Knowledge here is the power to measure creation rightly and to place each thing at its level.

> And We made the night and the day two signs, and We erased the sign of the night and made the sign of the day visible.
> Attribution: the verse cited by al-Ḥasan in explaining the darkening of the moon.

Hierarchy tree. The ten things, each harder than the last, given as an ascent.

- Rank 1: stone
  - Rank 2: iron
    - Rank 3: fire
      - Rank 4: water
        - Rank 5: cloud
          - Rank 6: wind
            - Rank 7: the angel
              - Rank 8: the Angel of Death
                - Rank 9: death itself
                  - Rank 10: the command of God, Lord of all worlds

Process flow. Report 2, from question to recognition.

Start: the Syrian writes the answers down and leaves
  ↓
He returns to Ibn al-Aṣfar with the record
  ↓
Ibn al-Aṣfar recognizes that this knowledge does not come from Muʿāwiyah
  ↓
He assigns it to its source, the substance of prophecy and the seat of the message
  ↓
End: a foreign ruler's recognition exposes domestic illegitimacy

Reading: those who possess power do not thereby possess knowledge, and those whose right was diminished remain the seat of prophetic inheritance. Knowledge is not an ornament of authority, it is one of the signs by which authority is known.

Comparison matrix. Report 3, the three questions ʿUmar neglected to ask the Messenger of God, and their explanations.

| Question | The puzzle in ordinary experience | The explanation given |
|---|---|---|
| Why some dreams are vivid and some are nothing | A dream that feels true and a dream that dissolves | Every sleeper is touched by a likeness of death. What the soul sees while separated from the body and journeying through the realm is truth, what it sees upon its return is falsehood and the delusion of Satan |
| Why one loves or hates a person without knowing why | Affection that arrives before reason, dislike with no cause | God created souls before bodies by two thousand years, they met and mingled like horses, what they knew then they recognize now, what they disliked then they dislike now |
| Why a thing remembered long is forgotten when needed, then recalled | Memory that disappears at the moment of need | The heart carries a scar like the scar of the moon, and when it is covered over the servant forgets what he has seen and heard |

> God takes souls at the time of their death, and those that have not died, during their sleep; He keeps those for whom He has decreed death, and releases the others until an appointed term.
> Attribution: the verse with which the answer on dreams begins.

> May God not spare me a land where ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib is absent and his name is not mentioned.
> Attribution: ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, closing Report 3.

Visual metaphor. Relation: why the household's knowledge must range as widely as it does. Metaphor: one key cut for every lock in the house.

- Lock 1, metaphysics: distinguishing nonexistence from divine ignorance.
- Lock 2, cosmology: connecting celestial signs to revelation.
- Lock 3, law: judging bodily ambiguity.
- Lock 4, the soul: reading dreams.
- Lock 5, the interior: the memory of the heart.
- Reading: a guide who could only answer ritual details would not be sufficient for the world this chapter imagines. The household's authority is comprehensive because creation, scripture, law, and the soul all require interpretation.

## Peace and belonging

Hierarchy tree. The twenty-third Mustaṇṣiriyya council on the Sunnah of salām, the revelation of peace.

- Above, seven sections: what comes from God and from His angels
  - Section 1, the highest, first, most particular, most exalted, most sincere: God's word, Peace be upon you
  - Section 2: the address to Noah, O Noah, descend in peace from Us
  - Section 3: the greeting on the day people meet God
  - Section 4: greetings and peace being conveyed
  - Section 5: the angels entering upon them from every gate, saying, Peace be upon you
  - Section 6: the command that God and His angels pray for the Prophet, peace be upon him, followed by the address, O you who believe, pray for him and submit to him in full submission
  - Section 7: Peace be upon the family of Yasin, where Yasin is the Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him and his family, and the family of Yasin is the family of Muhammad
- Below, twelve sections: what comes from human beings

Annotated structure. Whole: the ruling of the twenty-fourth assembly on what salām is.

- Part 1, its legal weight: an obligatory Sunnah of Islam.
- Part 2, what it speaks of: affection and submission.
- Part 3, its highest reference: salām is one of the names of the One possessed of majesty and honor.
- Annotation: greeting is not small talk. Divine name, social affection, and religious submission meet in a single exchanged word.

Visual metaphor. Relation: how peace circulates. Metaphor: a descent and a return, one current.

- Source: God gives peace.
- Carriage: the angels carry peace.
- Reception: the faithful receive peace.
- Return: the faithful return peace.
- Result: the community becomes visible through the words it exchanges.
- Reading: to offer peace is to rehearse the order of the world in miniature.

## Peace and belonging, the order of salām

Hierarchy tree. The twelve human divisions, as the source opens them.

- Division 1: the greeting of the people of the right hand to the faithful prophet, peace be upon him and his noble family, Peace be upon you for your patience
- Division 2: the greeting upon entering houses
- Division 3: the peace of one who concludes the prayer
- Division 4: the peace of the believer in the face of the ignorant

> When you enter houses, greet one another with a greeting from God, blessed and good.
> Attribution: the verse cited for the greeting upon entering houses.

> And when the ignorant address them, they say: peace.
> Attribution: the verse cited for the believer's peace in the face of the ignorant.

Genealogy chain. The ordered etiquette of who initiates, direction of initiation left to right.

- The rider on a camel → the rider on a horse
- The rider on a horse → the rider on a donkey
- The rider on a donkey → the one walking
- The one walking → the one standing
- The one standing → initiates, and also returns

Comparison matrix. The three kinds of reply.

| Kind of reply | What it returns | Whom it governs | The rule behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The virtuous reply | Something better than what was received | The one greeted by an initiator | Greet the initiator with something better |
| The complete reply | The same greeting, in kind | The one greeted by an initiator | Return it with something better, or return it as it is |
| The just reply | And upon you | Dhimmis, who do not initiate the greeting, but if they do, this is the response | Whoever said something good received its like, whoever said something bad received its like |

> And when you are greeted with a greeting, return it with something better, or return it as it is.
> Attribution: the verse cited for the complete reply.

Annotation on the just reply: the source explains it through a report that some deceived Muslims in their greeting, saying Death be upon you instead of Peace be upon you. The Sunnah therefore answered, And upon you.

Named-axis 2x2. The believer's response space, organized on two axes.

Axes:
- Horizontal: what was received, from hostile to good
- Vertical: what is returned, from matched to exceeded

Quadrants:
- Bottom-left, hostile received and matched return: the just reply. And upon you, refusing to be naive about deception.
- Bottom-right, good received and matched return: the complete reply. Return it as it is.
- Top-right, good received and exceeded return: the virtuous reply. Return it with something better.
- Top-left, hostile received and exceeded return: shake hands with your enemy if he dislikes it, repelling with what is better until the enemy becomes like a close friend.

Reading: peace is ordered. It descends from God and the angels, is embodied in prophetic and communal practice, and is returned according to justice, completeness, or excellence. A greeting can disclose affection, and it can also conceal harm. The believer is trained to answer both without losing balance.

Comparison matrix. The prayers for the borders of religion, by the day on which each is given.

| Day | Who prays | Ranks named |
|---|---|---|
| Sunday | Mawlānā Amīr al-Muʾminīn, after the higher borders | The lower five, the spokesman, the foundation, the culmination, the successor, the wing |
| Saturday | Mawlānā Amīr al-Muʾminīn, asking the purest and most noble prayers | The seven days and the twelve months |
| Monday | The saints of the religion, praying for the proclaimers and their gates | The seven proclaimers, the seven and eighteen, the seven caliphs, the twelve successors |
| Tuesday | The saints of the religion, asking God to reach His manifest borders | The righteous guardians, the spokesmen who call to His light, the proven foundations, the culmination, the successor, the wing, through whom the gates of mercy were opened |
| Other prayers | The saints of the religion, sending holy prayers | Their gates, their proofs, their prayers, days, months, hours of day and night, manifest and hidden |

Annotation: the believer does not merely know a list of offices, the believer prays through them. Cosmology becomes liturgy, and the ordered hierarchy of guidance is carried on the tongue as blessing and orientation rather than as dry classification.

Annotated structure. Whole: the Four Hundred Counsels returning the subject to ordinary behavior.

> When someone greets you, return the greeting with something better than it, or return it in kind.
> Attribution: the Four Hundred Counsels.

- Part 1, the counsel toward an enemy: shake hands with your enemy if he dislikes it, for God has commanded His servants to repel with what is better until the enemy becomes like a close friend.
- Part 2, the counsel toward a brother: if he says, May God greet you with peace, the answer should be, You are the peace of God, and the finest of abodes and stations.
- Annotation: the chapter has moved from divine salām to social courage. Peace is not sentimental avoidance, it can be an active discipline toward an enemy, a way of answering hostility with a better form without pretending the hostility is not there.

Comparison matrix. The two cited questions and their replies, the last exchanges of the movement.

| Question asked | Where it comes from | The reply given |
|---|---|---|
| Bowing or lowering the head before sheikhs, professors, and those close to them, instead of proclaiming peace with the hands, a gesture some feared resembled the practice of unbelievers | India | Offering peace is better |
| How much of a greeting a Muslim must hear, whether replying is obligatory, what is required when someone sneezes and praises God | Not named | None of these is required by duty and one may do as one wishes, except for replying to a greeting, whether aloud or silently |

Contrast pair. What the law makes binding, against what it commends without binding.

Column A: binding
- Returning a greeting, aloud or silently
- Status: obligatory, and stated as the single exception in the reply

Column B: commended, not binding
- Offering peace in place of a bow or a lowered head
- A response to a sneeze accompanied by praise of God
- The measure of how much of a greeting must be heard
- Status: one may do as one wishes

Shared row: after the grandeur of the seven heavenly forms of peace, the twelve human divisions, and the prayers for borders, the practical ruling could have become heavy with anxiety. Instead the source distinguishes better practice from legal duty, honoring hierarchy without turning reverence into confusion.

Process flow. How the salām material completes the earlier teaching on faith.

Start: faith requires knowledge and action
  ↓
Greeting becomes a small action with large meaning
  ↓
Faith requires walaya
  ↓
Prayers upon Muhammad and his family, and remembrance of the borders, locate the believer within the line of guidance
  ↓
Faith requires social obligation
  ↓
Returning peace is the most ordinary way that obligation is fulfilled
  ↓
End: few will face a messenger from Rūm or a caliphal inquiry about dreams and souls, and everyone will be greeted, ignored, confronted by an enemy, or asked to answer with more grace than he received

## What this episode lands

Annotated structure. Whole: the chapter's definition of faith, dissected into the four things it requires.

- Part 1: recognition of the guided line.
- Part 2: action carried out with knowledge.
- Part 3: obligations fulfilled toward parents, kin, neighbor, household, dependents, and the sick.
- Part 4: speech trained, so that even a greeting becomes part of submission.
- Annotation: faith is a complete formed life. The source will later arrange rulings according to the pillars of Islam, and it begins by making sure the listener knows what gives those rulings life.

Genealogy chain. The closing citations, direction of transmission left to right.

- Jaʿfar ibn Manṣūr al-Yaman → the verse that the earth is inherited by God's righteous servants → the Imams among His servants.
- Jaʿfar ibn Manṣūr al-Yaman → the verse on God wishing to be gracious to those oppressed in the land and to make them Imams and heirs → the descendants of ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, who were made weak in the land in his very person.
- The transmitted teaching of the Imams of the family of Muhammad, peace be upon him → he is the proof of Muhammad, and the earth is as he is.
- Mawlānā the Imam al-Muʿizz li-Dīn Allāh → a Sunday supplication → connected with the completion of the prayer of Raphael.

Visual metaphor. Relation: what kind of ending this is. Metaphor: stones laid, not a building finished.

- What was laid, stone 1: faith defined.
- What was laid, stone 2: walaya centered.
- What was laid, stone 3: the household's knowledge displayed.
- What was laid, stone 4: salām ordered.
- What was laid, stone 5: the borders of religion prayed over.
- What was not attempted: exhausting any one subject in isolation.
- Reading: the abruptness is the method. The chapter gathers the foundations from which later rulings can be read, and the episode should land with a sense of foundation rather than closure.

Contrast pair. Two ways a listener could carry the chapter forward, only one of which the author permits.

Column A: a ruling as instruction
- What it is: an item to be performed
- What it needs: a text and a compliance
- What it lacks: any account of why the text binds
- Where it leads: the imitation the chapter opened by refusing

Column B: a ruling as part of a guided life
- What it is: an act carried by recognition
- What it needs: walaya, knowledge, and conduct in one house
- What it supplies: the line by which the act is interpreted
- Where it leads: the later pillars, entered on a foundation already set

Shared row: the reports about ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, al-Ḥasan, and the questions from Rūm, Syria, and ʿUmar are not decorative marvels. They show the rightful guide recognized through knowledge, of God, scripture, creation, law, dreams, souls, and the heart.
