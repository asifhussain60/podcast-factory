## The Two Paths: Reward, Punishment, and the Opposites

### The Fifth Chapter: The Return of the Righteous Among the Ahl al-Zahir

The discourse now opens its fifth chapter, and it does so by taking up a statement already established and applying it to a new class of souls. Having treated the return — the ma'ad, the hereafter — of the two types belonging to the People of Truth, the Ahl al-Haqq, the text now extends that same statement to the ma'ad of the righteous, the sulaha', who belong not to the People of Truth in the fullest sense but to the Ahl al-Zahir, the People of the Outward. The phrase that governs this opening speaks of the ma'ad of the two examples, the two types, of the People of Truth; and once that statement had been given its due precedence and fully clarified, it is then carried over and made to apply to the sulaha' of the Ahl al-Zahir.

Who, then, are these sulaha'? They are those among the People of the Outward who hold fast to the outward matter — who cling to the literal observance, who follow the apparent ordinances, the ahkam of the Shari'ah. They believe in the Prophet of the Shari'ah; they keep the Islamic rulings; their lives are ordered by the outward law. Yet something has not entered them. Love — mawaddat — has not fully taken root in their hearts, nor has obedience, ita'at, to the Imam fully come to dwell in them. They follow the outward rulings faithfully, but the inward attachment to the living Imam of the time, the gnosis of him, has not been granted to them. The statement applied to them is, in essence, a statement about a good end, a khatima bil-khayr, and it is drawn from the noble Hadith. For these are people who believe in the Prophet, who observe the law, and who therefore are not cut off from a reward, even though the full reality has not opened to them.

This class is broad, and it explicitly includes the Ahl al-Tashayyu', the Shi'a — and indeed whoever they may be, all are gathered under this heading. The question naturally arises: do such people not possess wilayah? They do possess a wilayah, but it must be precisely understood. They hold the wilayah of the Prophet, and they hold the wilayah of Ali; with Ali, however, the chain of recognized authority, the hukm, was broken for them, so that they did not carry it forward to the living Imam who succeeds in his place. Still, these are people who trouble no one and harbor no enmity. Theirs is a path of universal peace, sulh-e-kul — they go about their affairs in peaceableness, neither persecuting nor opposing the Ahl al-Haqq. It is the return of such righteous ones, the ma'ad of the sulaha', that is now the subject of the chapter.

### The Portrait of the Chaste and Virtuous Man of the Outward

The text draws the portrait of this righteous person with care, and every quality named is meant to be weighed. Indeed, says the discourse, the chaste, the virtuous, the pure man from among the People of the Outward — `inna al-rajul al-'afif`, the chaste man, the one possessed of 'iffat, of continence, who has not entangled himself in fornication and base deeds; al-khayyir, the good one who does good; al-tahir, the pure one — this is a man who is pious through and through. He is a man of the Ahl al-Zahir who establishes the pillars of Islam, who raises up the prescribed acts of the faith and stands firm upon them.

What sets him apart is what he does not carry in his heart. He bears no enmity toward anyone from among the Ahl al-Haqq; the one who holds enmity against the People of Truth is decidedly not this man. He harbors no rancor, no concealed malice — `wa la ghilatan`. He keeps no hypocrisy, no nifaq. He commits no injustice, no zulm, against any of the people. There is in him no falsehood, no lying — `wa la kidhb` — and no betrayal, no khiyanah, toward anyone. Each of these vices is named and then denied of him, for it is precisely the absence of these wickednesses, joined to the presence of outward piety, that defines his station.

And there is one further mark, the decisive one. He is al-multazim — the adherent, the one firmly attached — to the wilayah of the Natiq of the era and his Wasi. That is to say, he believes in the Speaker-prophet of his age and holds wilayah for him; the outward rulings of the Prophet have reached him and he keeps them; and he extends his wilayah also to the Wasi, the legatee and successor. Beyond this, he recognizes whoever among their pure progeny he is able to recognize — the awlad al-tahirin, the offspring of the pure ones — acknowledging as many of them as the truth has made known to him. Such persons exist; in the world of outward Islam there have always been individuals who, on the strength of the Prophet's own sayings, accept the Wasi and revere the pure descendants, who would say with conviction that the true religion lies along this line. Yet the text is emphatic on a final point: such people are very few. `Wa qalla wujud dhalik` — and few indeed is the existence of that. The man who unites outward piety, freedom from enmity, and adherence to the wilayah of the Natiq and his Wasi without having received the full inward reality is a rare figure; his kind is scarce upon the earth.

### The Death of the Righteous: The Stirring of the Spiritualities and the Cooling of the Body

When such a righteous man is steadfast in this condition to the end — when he persists upon outward piety and dies in that state, having taken his faith from the noble Hadith without having attained the gnosis, the ma'rifat, of the Imam of his time — then his death approaches and the great machinery of the return is set in motion. As death comes upon him, the spiritualities of the sun and of Venus, the ruhaniyat of the shams and of Venus, move him. These spiritualities — the angelic powers appointed to the sun and to Venus — are the agents that act upon him at the threshold of death.

The effects in his body are precise. His soul, his nafs, spreads throughout his body. His heat grows cold; his blood freezes and congeals. And this spreading of the soul into the body is not a neutral sign — it is a mark of incapacity. For the soul to spread into the body is the very sign that one cannot cross the Pulsirat, the bridge over which the saved must pass. It must be noted that the soul of the disbeliever, the kafir, also spreads into his body in this manner; his body too is overspread by the soul. There is, however, a difference between them as to what follows: for the righteous of the Ahl al-Zahir, who dwell for a time in the Barzakh, the intermediate realm, the return will be swift, whereas for others it will not be so. But the spreading of the soul into the body is, in itself, the token that the Pulsirat will not be crossed.

At this same moment of death, the righteous man is separated from that which he had conceptualized of his good deeds — from the tasawwur, the conception, of all the good he had practiced in his life. He leaves behind that inner image of his righteous works even as the body cools and stiffens. To grasp what becomes of those works, we must first understand a principle that governs the whole economy of reward.

### The Imperishability of the Good: No Deed Is Ever Wasted

There is a foundational law at work here, and the discourse states it without qualification: not the smallest particle of good is ever permitted by Allah to go to waste. Down to the naqir and the qitmir — the proverbial specks, the tiny notch on a date-stone and the thin skin upon it, the very least and most trifling of measures — whatever good a person does, Allah the Exalted does not let it perish. He preserves it. And He does so without regard to the doer's standing: whether the one who performs the good deed is a believer, or a hypocrite, or even a disbeliever, the good itself is kept and never destroyed. Good, in its essence, is never destroyed.

This stands in deliberate contrast to evil, for evil is precisely what is liable to destruction. Here the discourse anchors itself in a great metaphysical principle: `Al-sharru la asla lahu fi al-ibda'` — evil has no root in the primordial creation, in the ibda'. Evil, considered at its origin, possesses no foundation of its own; it did not arise as a thing created. For what was there at the origin was good, and good alone. What, then, is evil? Evil is nothing but distance from the good. The matter is illumined by the relation of light and darkness. What is darkness, zulmat? It is simply remoteness from light, from nur. The farther one withdraws from the light, the more the darkness deepens; if one remains near the light, one receives some measure of it; the more one departs from it, the further it recedes, until at last darkness sets in. Darkness is not a positive substance that contends with light on equal terms; it is the mere privation of light, the measure of one's distance from it. Just so, evil is the privation of good, the measure of one's distance from the good. This is why it is said that evil has no root in the ibda': it was never laid down as a created reality.

If evil has no root in creation, where does it come from? It is created because of us. Corporeality itself — jismaniyyat, in which there is density, kasafat — why did it come to be? It was created on our account, not as something present in the primordial order. There was a teaching given in Sindh, in a lesson upon these very realities, the haqa'iq, that bears directly upon this: when Allah first created the souls, He created them luminous, He created them in such a noble and radiant state — and the question pressed upon the hearers, where were we at that time? We were luminous, and the density that now encases us is no part of that original making; it accrued through our own descent. Corporeality and its density are the residue of our falling away from the light, not a thing that Allah laid into the foundation of creation.

### Reward Without Wilayah: The Pull of the Good Deed Toward the Wali

A pressing difficulty must now be confronted, for the Imams have taught a saying that seems, at first, to stand against everything just established. Our Imams have said that without wilayah, even were a man to pray night and day within the Sacred Mosque, the Haram Sharif, and to sit in worship between the door of the Ka'bah and the Maqam of Ibrahim, nothing of it would be accepted. Nothing whatsoever is received without the love of Ali; without that love there is no salvation, no khalasi. How is this to be reconciled with the principle that Allah lets no good deed, however small, go to waste — that here, the Exalted is giving the reward, the ajr, for every single thing, while there, the Imams declare that nothing is accepted?

The reconciliation lies in distinguishing two things that the difficulty has run together. It is true, and it remains a firm principle, that good can never become non-existent, can never become as nothing — `kal-'adam`. This principle is established and abides. Good can never lapse into nothingness; it is evil that lapses into nothingness. If a person has performed even a small good deed, that deed cannot become non-existent; it is a thing that endures and remains. And the Qur'an itself confirms this: `Inna al-hasanat yudhhibna al-sayyi'at` — indeed, the good deeds drive away the misdeeds; the hasanat efface the sayyi'at. The good a man does has real, persisting effect.

What, then, of the saying that nothing is accepted without the wilayah of Ali? Consider a person who spoke the truth but does not possess the wilayah of Ali. His truthful speech is a genuine good. Having the wilayah of Ali is a separate matter altogether. It is true that, lacking that wilayah, his end will not be a good end, a khatima bil-khayr; he will not be able to cross the Pulsirat — that consequence stands. But the good deed he performed will assuredly be given its reward. More than this: that good deed becomes a force that pulls him toward itself, and through itself toward the wilayah of the Wali, in the end. The good he did draws him; it takes him, in the final reckoning, toward the very wilayah he had not consciously embraced. The reward is not cancelled; it is deferred and redirected, and it works upon the doer as a magnet drawing him toward the truth.

It is precisely such people who are now in view — those who held no enmity toward Ali. There are many who bear no hatred toward Ali at all; they accept the Prophet and they accept Ali; they have committed nothing forbidden, nothing haram, nothing against the Shari'ah; far from opposing Ali, they love him and love the Imams. Something of the truth has reached them, and that is why they love them. Yet they live by sulh-e-kul, the way of universal peace, without entering into the inward bond of the living da'wat. What, then, will their end be? The answer returns us to where the body lay cooling: their soul will spread in the body, which is the sign of one who cannot cross the Pulsirat. There is a real distinction between their case and the disbeliever's, for though the soul spreads in both, the righteous man dwells in the Barzakh and his return is swift; but the outward sign is shared.

### The Luminous Form and Its Ascent to the Moon and Sun

We return now to the conception, the tasawwur, of good that separates from the righteous man at his death. That inner image of his good works does not vanish; it becomes a luminous form, a surat-e-nurani. The discourse enumerates what this conception is made of: what he preserved of the Book of Allah, and his love for the Messenger and for his Wasi, and his mercy toward the children of his own kind, and his chastity and purity, and his modesty and shame, and his fear of Allah the Exalted. Each of these is a strand woven into the luminous form. The man who guarded the Book — who recited it with tajwid, who memorized it and became a hafiz — does not see that labor go to waste. His love for the Messenger and the Wasi abides. His mercy toward humankind, the merciful gaze he cast upon his fellows, abides. His chastity, his modesty, his fear of God — all of it gathers into a radiant shape.

This conception then ascends. It rises with the spiritualities — the same ruhaniyat of the sun and Venus mentioned at the threshold of death, the angels appointed to those celestial bodies — and they bear the conception upward toward the moon, the qamar. There at the moon it abides for a time. Then the moon hands it over to the sun, the shams. There is a fine point of doctrine to mark here: at the moon nothing of consequence is yet worked upon the conception; the moon and Venus are but the path along which it travels, while it is at the sun that the real work is accomplished. The moon delivers the conception to the sun, and the sun receives it into its sphere, into its heat. There the conception is fermented and purified — it undergoes a refining, a perfecting, as new wine ferments and clarifies. And there it remains until Allah the Exalted gives permission for its return; the conception waits in the solar realm, purified and matured, until the command for its return is issued.

### The Dissolution of the Body and the Long Road of Return

While the conception ascends and is refined at the sun, the body it left behind enters upon its own slow transformation. The conception has departed; what remains is the body, and that body dissolves bit by bit, a little after a little. It does not vanish at a stroke but is undone gradually. As it dissolves it ascends as vapor, rising upward, and it enters into the temperament and the mixture — the mizaj — taking its place among the four elemental natures of fire, air, water, and earth. From within that elemental mixture it falls again as rain.

Having fallen as rain, it is taken up into the chain of pure nourishments. It becomes a constituent of pure foods, of sweet and wholesome drinks, of fragrant and agreeable smells. Through these it nourishes a human being. Drawn into the body of a person through pure food and drink, it appears at last as a sperm-drop, a nutfah. From there it ascends through the ranks — progressing stage by stage through the grades of bodily formation — until it appears as a human being, born into the world. And at its exit from its mother's womb, the form connects with it: that very form which had been refined in the sun, the conception that had been borne there by way of Venus and the moon, descends and joins itself to the newly born child.

One ought not to imagine that the body's matter alone is at issue here, as though the soul were absent from the process. The text does not spell out every mechanical detail of the dissolution; it says only that the matter enters the temperament and the mixture and dwells among fire, air, water, and earth, and from there falls as rain. The full passage of the substance through the zodiacal spheres, the buruj, is not laid out in this place. It is worth recalling the order of the cosmos, however, for the path is real even where unstated. Venus and the moon are a portion of the path, and the work is completed at the sun; but the buruj lie above all of these. The structure ascends thus: first the world of lights, the 'alam-e-anwar; then the sphere of life, the falak-e-hayat; then the encompassing sphere, the falak-e-muhit, and it is from the encompassing sphere downward that materiality, maddiyyat, begins — above it there is no materiality save what is illusory, wahmi. Below the encompassing sphere lies the sphere of the buruj, the zodiacal constellations, in which materiality is greater; and below that, the seven wandering stars: Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Venus, Mercury, and the Moon. So when the discourse speaks of dissolution into the mixture and return as rain, it sketches the lower stretch of a far longer cosmic road.

### The Two Souls: The Vegetative from the Moon, the Sensory from the Sun

The return of the form to the newborn does not happen in isolation; it is accompanied by the joining of the soul that the new life deserves. Here a precise doctrine of the souls must be set out. There connects with the child, at its birth, that portion of the sensory soul, the nafs hissiyah, which it deserves to receive. And the two souls of the growing human being have their distinct seats and their distinct moments of connection.

The vegetative or growth-soul, the nafs namiyah, has its station, its maqar, in the moon. Before a person is born, the vegetative soul resides in the sphere of the moon, while the sensory soul, the nafs hissiyah, resides in the sun. When the sperm-drop is placed within the mother's womb and takes form, the vegetative soul connects to it from the sphere of the moon; this is the first joining. Then, when birth occurs and the child comes forth into the world from the womb, the sensory soul connects with it, and this connection is from the sun. Thus the moon furnishes the growth-soul at conception, and the sun furnishes the sensory soul at birth — each soul descending from its proper celestial seat at its proper moment.

As for the human being through whom the dissolved matter is nourished — the human who consumes the pure food and drink into which the body had been transformed — this need not be any particular person. It may be the deceased's own parents, if some connection with them yet remains; but it may equally be any human at all. There is no restriction binding the returning substance to one household. The soul may come into the world through any human channel and may there choose the truth; the door is not closed against it. The point is simply that the substance re-enters life through human nourishment, appears as a sperm-drop, ascends through the ranks, and is born, at which birth the refined form and the deserved sensory soul are joined to it.

A further subtlety concerns the soul that lingered with the dissolving body. When the conception of the pious — the tasawwur of the du'at-e-kiram, the noble summoners, and of the righteous — becomes elevated and ascends, their sensory soul tends to ascend along with it; and some hold that the faculties of the brain, the powers of intellect, ascend and develop together with it as well, in proportion to the measure by which the person had governed himself according to the rulings of the Shari'ah. Yet this varies from one person to another. With some, a portion of the vegetative soul remains behind; it is possible that a part of the sensory soul has ascended while a remnant of it lingers with the body. And it is precisely on account of such a remnant that the punishments of the grave fall upon the body. As the person was in life, so will his planning and his recompense be: each receives according to what he wrought. Thus two cases present themselves — some whose bodies retain no sensory soul, the body wholly ascending, and others in whom a remnant remains and with it a measure of grave-punishment. At the new birth, then, the form connects, and with it the deserved sensory soul; the two mingle, and the sense and the form, the nafs hissiyah and the surat, become a single conjoined reality, just as in the original body the soul had dwelt with the flesh.

### The Scale of Justice and the Reward of the Returning Conception

Why does the conception return at all, and why does it return enhanced? The answer lies in a single governing principle: the scale of justice is established — `mizan al-'adl qa'imun`. Because the balance of justice stands firm, and because of the purity of the righteous man and the good deeds that were in him, that conception returns to him. And it returns not as it departed but transformed. It had separated from him in a certain state at the moment of death, in the very condition he was in when his soul and body were taken; but it comes back greater, more glorious, and more noble than it was at the parting.

The reason is that the conception did not merely wait idle. It went to the sun; it was fermented there, purified, perfected. So when it returns, it does not return as the same conception that left, but in a state higher and more refined than before — like wine that has fermented into something finer than the must from which it began. This is still, be it noted, the conception alone; the rational soul, the nafs-e-natiqah, is not yet under discussion. The conception is the initial form of the rational soul, its first shape, and it is this that returns, enlarged and ennobled. When the man separated from his body, Allah took his soul and his body; and what returns to him is greater, more glorious, more noble than what was taken.

For this is a kind of reward. The discourse gives a luminous comparison. It is like a man who travels — who leaves behind his dwelling and his family, who goes out into a far country and remains there as a stranger, an exile, until he has earned wealth — and who then returns home in a state more abundant, more prosperous, than when he set out. So it is with the righteous man's conception: his body, as it were, traveled to the sun; and having labored and traveled there, it brought back wealth from that journey, returning at last in a condition richer than its first. The exile that looked like loss was in truth a means of gain, and the returning soul comes home enlarged.

### The Disobedient Have No Return: Punishment as Permanent Exile

Against this stands the fate of the conception of the wicked, and the contrast is exact. The conception of one other than the righteous — the conception of the disobedient and the rebellious, the sinners who defy the truth — at its separation from the body and soul at death, has no return whatsoever, never, to that body and soul. The good man's conception comes home greater than before; the wicked man's conception does not come home at all.

The same image of the traveler is invoked, but inverted. The wicked one is like a man who departs from his family, his dwelling, and his beloved, and who has no intention of ever returning to them — who goes away meaning never to come back, leaving everything behind for good. This is in accordance with what the Qur'an declares: it is recompense according to what his hands have put forth. Where the righteous traveler goes out to earn and returns enriched, the wicked traveler abandons all and does not return. And that perpetual non-return is itself a kind of punishment — one type of the torment that befalls the rebellious. We seek refuge in Allah from His punishment and from the evil of its consequence. And praise be to Allah who has favored us over many of His servants among His awliya', and who has made us among those who adhere to the awliya' and among the people of guidance; and the blessings of Allah be upon His Messenger, our master Muhammad, and upon his pure family, and peace.

### The Fourth Chapter and the Turn to the Opposites

The discourse now passes to the fourth chapter of the third Siraj, which gathers within it five sections; we take up the first of them. The opening words set the transition: `Wa lamma intaha bina al-qawl ila ma intaha wa awzaha'na ma sabaqa min al-izah` — "And when the speech brought us to that which it reached, and we had clarified what preceded of the clarification." That is, when the discourse had carried us to the point it was bound for, and we had set forth what came before, it then names what was clarified: `intiqal al-awliya wa taraqqihim wa intihaihim fi abwab al-jinaan` — the transition of the awliya', the saints, and their progress, and their arrival at the gates of paradise, the abwab al-jinaan. And after this, `thumma tawfiyat al-sulaha min ahl al-zahir` — then the fulfillment, the full recompensing, of the righteous from among the People of the Outward.

Having treated the bulughat, the believers, and then the sulaha', the righteous, the discourse announces its next subject: `Wa nuridu an nutbi'a dhalik al-kalam 'ala al-azdad` — "And we intend to follow that speech with a discourse upon the opposites, the azdad." We now come, that is, to the azdad: the opposites, the antitheses of the believers and the righteous. The text states what awaits them: `wa ma yantahuna ilayhi min qawm al-fasad` — and what the people of corruption arrive at, the qawm al-fasad — `wa ma yasiruna ilayhi min 'adhab wa sharr al-khwab` — and what they proceed toward of torment and the evil of dreams. The discourse turns, then, from the bright destiny of the faithful to the dark destiny of the corrupt: to the people of corruption and the torment they reach.

### The Dark Jawzahar and the Magnet of the Wicked

The mechanism of the wicked one's death is now set out, and it stands as the precise dark mirror of the death of the righteous. The text begins: `Fa naqulu bi-'awnillah ta'ala wa ma tawallihi fi ardih salamullahi 'alayhi. Inna al-mawt idha ja'a wahidan minhum, harrakahu hina'idhin sar al-jawzahar al-zulmani al-falaki alladhi huwa al-'uqdatan li-annahuma al-mighnatis lil-khabith wal-khasis` — "So we say, with the help of God the Exalted and what His wali undertakes in His land, peace of God be upon him: indeed, when death comes to one of them, he is moved at that time by the flowing of the dark celestial jawzahar, which is the two nodes, for the two of them are the magnet for the wicked and the vile."

When death comes to one of the opposites, he is moved by the flowing — the sar — of the dark celestial jawzahar, the jawzahar-e-zulmani. This jawzahar has been placed beneath the celestial sphere of the moon, the falak-e-qamar. What are these nodes? They are the 'uqdatan, the two nodes, named the ras and the dhanab, the head and the tail. And the word sar, "flowing," denotes that which moves and courses — the streaming substance of the dark jawzahar. As the spiritualities of the sun and Venus moved the righteous man toward his luminous ascent, so the flowing of the dark jawzahar moves the wicked one at the hour of his death, drawing him downward.

To grasp this we must set it beside its luminous counterpart. For the believers and the people of good there is the 'amud al-nur, the pillar of light. Whence does it come? It descends from the aql-e-awwal, the First Intellect. And what is its connection? It rests upon the principle that between the Originator and the originated there is a word — `bayn al-mubdi' wal-mubda'i kalima` — and that word is the kalima of la ilaha illallah. The connection of the 'amud al-nur is established through this kalima. The Exalted Originator, the mubdi' ta'ala, is connected to the First Intellect, and from the First Intellect the pillar of light descends; and wherever anyone establishes his connection with that pillar of light — that is, everyone who stands upon the 'ahd-e-sharif, the noble covenant — the connection of the 'amud al-nur is established with him. This is what is called the pillar of light.

In direct opposition to the 'amud al-nur, Allah the Exalted has established the dark celestial jawzahar, the jawzahar-e-zulmani al-falaki, beneath the sphere of the moon. Why was it given so elevated a station? Because, unlike the parts that descend all the way to the sakhra, the rock at the foundation, this jawzahar did not enter the sakhra; it is not bound to the rock but remained above it. The reason is that it is made from the parts, the ajza, of people who had once been elevated and had begun upon the path, but who ended in wickedness, in khabasat. For them, this is a justice from Allah the Exalted, an 'adl: they were placed beneath the sphere of the moon and were not made to reach the rock, and from that station they carry out all their ploys, their tadabir. Both the pillar of light and the dark jawzahar are, in the proper sense, wahmi — conceptual, imaginary in their substance — even as both possess a real beginning above and a flowing-down below; the two nodes are likewise spoken of as wahmi nodes, each a kind of 'uqda, one the ras and one the dhanab. And of the two, which is the more wicked, the more khabith? The dhanab, the tail, is more wicked than the ras, the head.

The whole matter reduces to a stark alternative: there are only two things. Either one's connection, one's ittisal, is with the 'amud al-nur — and this is the connection of the believers, the ashab-e-khair, the people of good whose end was good — or, failing that, one's connection is with the dark jawzahar. Those whose end was not good are connected to the jawzahar-e-zulmani. If a person is not upon the pillar of light, then automatically he is drawn there, to the dark jawzahar; there is no empty space between the two, no neutral ground. The flowing of the dark jawzahar may also be understood as a force, a quwwat, that draws them; for the two nodes are magnets — `li-annahuma al-mighnatis` — magnets for the khabith, the wicked, and the khasis, the vile.

### Decline as the Soul Becoming Body: True Progress Reversed

The text now describes the bodily signs of the wicked one's death: `Fa-yashi'u nafsuhu fi jismihi wa yajmudu damuhu fa-yabrud wa yasiru nafsuhu wa jismuhu shay'an wahidan` — "And his soul spreads in his body, and his blood freezes and grows cold, and his soul and his body become a single thing." The dark jawzahar, having moved him, produces this effect: the soul spreads into the body. We have met the spreading of the soul into the body before, as the sign of one who cannot cross the Pulsirat; here that same spreading appears in its fullest and gravest form.

What does it mean for the soul to spread into the body? To understand this we must grasp what true progress is and what decline is. True progress, taraqqi, is the body becoming soul — the dense ascending into the subtle. And the reverse — the soul becoming body — is decline, tanazzul. The soul is the higher reality; for it to descend and become enmeshed in materiality, maddiyat, is a fall. We must look to the very root of things. What did God create at the beginning? Intellect, aql. The first and most important description of creation is that Allah the Exalted created intellects. We, through our falling behind, our ta'khir, turned that intellect into soul; and with a further falling behind, the soul turned into body, into materiality. And materiality has many grades: there is the human body, the jism-e-insani, the animal body, the jism-e-haywani, the vegetable body, the jism-e-nabatati, and yet other forms. As a being falls further, its materiality increases in proportion to the fall. So when the wicked one's soul and body become a single thing, the meaning is grave: where formerly soul and body were distinct, now they coalesce, soul collapsed into body, decline in its extremity. And `wa yastahilu ba'duhu ba'dan` — one part of it is transformed into another, its parts mutating one into the next.

### The Building of the Dark Wicked Form

Just as the righteous man's good conception built itself into a luminous form, so the wicked man's evil conception builds itself into a dark and wicked form, and the parallel is exact at every point. The text reads: `wa yufariqu ma kana qad tasawwarahu min 'ulum al-zulmaniya al-muhlika wa ma tasawwarahu fi ahl al-haqq min al-kufr wa ma akannahu lahum min al-'adawati wal-baghda'i wal-nukr wa ma iktasabahu min af'al al-khabiha wa ma'taqadahu min al-i'tiqadat al-khabitha al-fasida` — "And it separates from what he had conceived of the dark, destructive sciences, and what he had conceived concerning the People of Truth of disbelief, and what he had concealed against them of enmity, hatred, and aversion, and what he had earned of wicked deeds, and what he had believed of corrupt, wicked beliefs."

There are three things to keep distinct: the body, the soul, and the form. At death the soul and the body fall apart, and the form — the surat-e-zulmani, the dark form that the man had built from his knowledge and his actions — separates from them. As a luminous form arises from good knowledge, so a dark form arises from evil knowledge, and the wicked one's conception was precisely such an evil, dark conception. From this man, then, separate: the dark and destructive sciences he had conceived; the disbelief, the kufr, he had conceived concerning the Ahl al-Haqq; the enmity, hatred, and aversion — the 'adawah, the baghda', the nukr — that he had concealed in his heart against the People of Truth; the wicked deeds he had earned; and the corrupt, wicked beliefs he had held.

The reason all of this separates and gathers is given: `li-anna jami'a dhalika yabtani suratan latifan zulmaniyatan khabithatan mutashakkilatan 'ala shibh al-surat al-sharifa al-nayyira al-latifa 'allati ibtanat min al-'ulum al-ta'wiliyya wal-hikam al-rabbaniyya wal-ma'arif al-ilahiyya` — "because all of that builds a subtle, dark, wicked form, shaped in the likeness of the noble, luminous, subtle form, which was built from the sciences of interpretation, the lordly wisdoms, and the divine gnoses." All of that wickedness builds itself into a subtle form — a dark and wicked form — that takes its shape in precise likeness to the noble luminous form, but as its inversion. The luminous form is built from the ulum al-ta'wiliyya, the interpretive sciences, from the lordly wisdoms and the divine gnoses; the dark form is built from the destructive sciences and the corrupt beliefs. Just as a luminous form is created, so too is a dark form created, each mirroring the other.

It must be observed, however, where the parallel stops. One might suppose that if a dark form is created, then it too should have its haykal, its temple, and that there should be an Imam of the opposites, an Imam al-azdad. But this is not so; it is not necessary. The gathering of these dark forms takes place in the dark jawzahar, and there no haykal is spoken of. The haykal is a thing belonging only to the side of light. On the dark side there is a gathering of forms in the jawzahar, but no temple is constituted there.

### The Iblisi Soul and the Imperishability of Existence

The dark form, once built, receives its name and its nature. The text continues: `yusamma dhalik al-nafs al-zulmaniyya al-iblisiyya al-shaytaniyya. Fa-tasiru suratan nariyyatan muhriqah, kama qila fi al-surat al-nayyira: nafsun malakutiyyatun natiqa` — "that soul is called the dark, Iblisi, satanic soul; and it becomes a fiery, incinerating form, just as is said of the luminous form: an angelic, speaking soul." The dark conception is named the nafs-e-zulmani, the iblisiya, the shaytaniyya — the dark soul, the Iblis-like soul, the satanic soul. And it becomes a fiery form that burns and incinerates. This stands opposite to the luminous form, which is called the nafs-e-malakutiyya natiqa, the angelic speaking soul. The radiant soul speaks with the speech of the angels; the dark soul burns with the fire of Iblis.

Now a metaphysical question presses: how can such a wicked form have any real subsistence at all, if evil has no root in creation? The answer rests upon the imperishability of existence. The text gives an instance: `Wa rubbama sadafat tilka al-nafs al-shaytaniyya insanan, fa-tamaththalat lahu naran hatta idha kharuba minha lam yajid shay'an, wa rubbama harrakat ma yattasilu biha min shajarin aw thawbin aw baytin aw ghayri dhalik, wa rubbama baqiya shay'un min dhalik al-tasawwur al-khabith fi jismihi al-khabith` — "And sometimes that satanic soul encounters a person, and it appears to him as a fire, until when he draws near it he finds nothing; and sometimes it moves what is connected to it, like a tree or a cloth or a house or other than that; and sometimes a thing of that wicked conception remains in his wicked body." Sometimes the satanic soul-form encounters a man and shows itself to him in the likeness of fire; he approaches the fire and finds nothing there. Sometimes it stirs the things connected with it — a tree, a garment, a house. And sometimes a portion of the wicked conception remains within the wicked body itself.

Consider the body that is burned — as, for instance, a body given to the flames. The body burns; yet its parts remain. However thoroughly it is burned, every part of it persists in some form even after burning, for once God has brought a thing into existence, that thing cannot be annihilated. What is fana, annihilation? It is that a thing which has come into existence should become non-existent. And this cannot be. The form, the shakl, of a thing can change, but its existence cannot be annulled. The shapes of existence may shift, but existence itself cannot become non-existent. No created being possesses the power to do this. The Originator, the mubdi', has brought existence into being, and no one but the Originator has the power to turn existence into non-existence. So the dark form, though built of evil, cannot simply cease; its existence persists even as its shape changes.

### The Flaming Grave and Its Tormented Tenant

The fate of the dark form in the grave is now described: `fa-yuqbaru ma'ahu fa-tasiru naran muhriqatan mu'adhdhibatan li-dhalik al-jism muddatan min al-ayyam, fa-yura dhalik al-qabr yashta'ilu naran fi al-layl wa yushahadu tahajjujuhu, fa-idha kharuba insanun min dhalik al-qabr lam yudrik tilka al-nar li-kawnih la yudrikuha man kharuba minha` — "so it is buried with him, and it becomes an incinerating, tormenting fire for that body for a period of days; and that grave is seen to be aflame with fire at night, and its flaring is witnessed; yet when a person draws near that grave, he does not perceive that fire, because he who draws near it does not perceive it."

The dark, wicked soul-form, the satanic conception, is buried together with the body. There it becomes a fire that burns and torments that body for a span of days. And so that grave is seen to be alight with fire by night; its flaring, its tahajjuj, is witnessed from afar. Yet here is the strange thing: when a person actually approaches the grave, he perceives no fire at all, for the one who draws near to it does not apprehend it. The fire is real but not of the order that the bodily eye, brought close, can register.

And who is the tenant of such a grave? `Wa sahibu hadha al-qabr alladhi tasarrafa lahu tasawwuruhu huwa min akthar al-nas khubsan wa a'zamuhum jurman wa athaman wa ashaddahum 'adawatan wa nifaqan li-ahl al-haqq wa aktharuhum zulman lil-khalq` — "And the owner of this grave, upon whom his own conception has acted, is the most wicked of people, the greatest of them in crime and sin, the most severe of them in enmity and hypocrisy toward the People of Truth, and the greatest of them in oppressing the creation." The man who is burned by his own conception in this manner is the most wicked of people, the greatest in crime and in sin, the most violent in his enmity and hypocrisy toward the Ahl al-Haqq, and the most oppressive toward all of creation. The flaming grave is the portion of the supreme antagonist of the truth.

### The Testimony of Sayyidna Ikramali: The Fire at Bharuch

Such incidents, the discourse notes, were once heard of very often, though in our day they have become rare — these accounts of clothes catching fire or trees catching fire, the workings of the jawzahar; such things are seldom reported now. But a firsthand testimony is preserved. Sayyidna Ikramali Sahib relates in his travelogue an incident that occurred in the year 1255 Hijri, on a journey toward Baroda. They had set out from Bharuch, traveling onward toward Baroda. In Bharuch they had relatives, and they lodged at a house there, and he narrates the affair in full detail.

He himself was teaching at the time, and with him were two students, Sayyidna Sheikh Ibrahim Sahib and Sayyidna Fardali Sahib, both studying under him. In one room of the house, garments had been hung up; their clothes were hanging there. In another room he and his companions sat at their lessons. Suddenly a fire descended from the sky — there were heavy rains and lightning struck — and when the fire came down, it burned all the clothes that were in that one room, while he and his companions, in the other room, were every one of them kept safe. He gathered them all and continued on to Baroda; and when they reached the city, the people came out to welcome them, as was the custom in those days, going beyond the old bridge to receive them with joy, and there in Baroda a feast of thanksgiving, a ta'am-e-masarrat, was held. The lightning-fire that consumed only the garments in the one room, sparing the men in the other, stands as a witnessed instance of fire descending from the sky in the manner the text describes.

### The Testimony of Sayyidna Sheikh Adam: The Light upon the Grave

A second incident is preserved, and it draws a vital distinction — between fire and light, between nar and nur. Sayyidna Sheikh Adam Sahib, while he was renovating the house of the jamatkhana in Baroda, received a visit from two men. One was named Nizamuddin; the other's name is not recorded. They came and reported that they had gone to the cemetery at Mumin-bagh to perform ziyarat, visitation. Having done so, they lodged in a house nearby — at the place where, in later times, Asadullah Sahib's Fateh Manzil stands. That night it rained, and after the rain, from a distance, they saw a grave upon which light was descending from the sky.

There is a great difference, the discourse insists, between nur and nar: this was nur, a stream of light pouring down from the heavens, not the fire of torment. The two men came and informed Sheikh Adam Sahib of what they had seen. He said that many people had told him before that such a light descended upon that grave — that this and that occurred there — but he had always set it aside, saying, "People say things." Yet when these two came and gave their firsthand account, he went himself and verified it, and it was found to be true: indeed, nur descends upon that grave in just this manner. He wrote that he had investigated the matter by his own knowledge.

The grave belongs to Muhammad Arif Sahib. In it there is even a niche, a kendal, with a hole made for the keeping of a lamp. The reason for the light is recorded. When the du'at moved from Ahmedabad to Baroda — when strife and corruption had begun to spread and they were obliged to relocate — there was at first no land in Baroda for the community; the people prayed in their own homes. Muhammad Arif Sahib then gave his own land, the very ground in Baroda where the jamatkhana and the masjid now stand, as a waqf, an endowment, for the community, for the masjid and the jamatkhana. The reward he received for this endowment was that nur descends upon his grave; and Sheikh Adam Sahib set all of this down in full detail. Near to the grave of Sayyidna Ikramali Sahib lies that of another, Yusuf bin Yusuf Khan, who also used to attend the teaching, and the inscription `Razi'an sakhiyyan` — "content and generous" — is still written there; the grave is whitened and stands high.

### The Ruling on the Masjid: Designation by Nass

Arising from this same account is a question of law that Sheikh Adam Sahib resolved by nass. A question was put to him: they were building above a residence — downstairs was a sukna, a dwelling-house in which people lived, and they wished to build a masjid on the floor above it — and the da'wati people had told them that it could not be done in this way. Sheikh Adam Sahib sought permission and gave his ruling, saying that he had verified the matter through the nass, the authoritative text. The ruling is this: whichever portion is designated as a masjid is a masjid — whether it be one portion, a single flat, or whatever it may be; the portion you set aside as a masjid is a masjid, and tahiyyatul masjid, the greeting-prayer of the mosque, may be prayed within it, regardless of a residence below. And the portion not so designated is not a masjid. Upon this ruling the masjid was duly built.

### The Direction of the Soul's Attention After Death

What significance can be drawn from the descent of light upon the grave, given that the soul has already departed the body? The matter teaches us about the direction of the soul's, or rather the form's, attention after death. When a person passes away, his attention, his tawajjuh, is directed first toward his body and the parts of his body. Then it turns toward his shroud, his kafan, and the parts of his shroud. Then it turns toward the soil of his grave. This is precisely why the soil of his own grave should be used to fill it: the very earth dug from a man's grave should be returned upon him, not earth brought from elsewhere; at the least, his own grave-soil should not be cast away here and there but laid back upon him. And after the soil of the grave, the attention extends to a far wider compass — to his house, the dwelling he lived in; the sphere of attention is very vast. Throughout, it is the soul's own attention, the attention of the surat, the form, that is meant. The light upon the grave, then, answers to this lingering directedness of the form toward the body, the shroud, the soil, and the home it once inhabited — a sign, in the case of the righteous endower, of the reward that draws down nur rather than the torment that kindles nar.

### The Liberated Wicked Form: Free in Its Own Torment

We turn now to the fate of the dark form once it has left the body, and the text describes a dreadful liberty: `an tashakkala kayfa sha'at wa taf'ala ma sha'at, li-annaha qad sarat nihayat al-sharr, kama anna tilka al-surata nihayat al-khayr; fa-hiya tashakkalu kama sha'at wa taf'alu ma sha'at, li-annaha qad faraqat dhalika al-kathif alladhi huwa al-jism` — "that it takes whatever form it wishes and does whatever it wishes, because it has become the extremity of evil, just as that luminous form is the extremity of good; so it takes shape as it wishes and does as it wishes, because it has separated from that dense thing which is the body."

The dark form, freed from the body, takes on whatever shape it wills and does whatever it pleases. The reason is that it has become the very extremity of evil, the nihayat al-sharr — just as the luminous form is the extremity of good, the nihayat al-khayr. The wicked form stands at the furthest reach of evil as the radiant form stands at the furthest reach of good. And it has this freedom precisely because it has separated from that dense thing, the kathif, which is the body. The text continues: `wa huwa al-mani'u laha min fi'li dhalika li-kathafatihi wa naqsihi, wa idha faraqathu lam yabqa laha mani'un yamna'uha 'amma turiduhu, li-annahu laha ka-al-sijn` — "and it [the body] was what prevented it from doing that, on account of its density and its deficiency; and when it separated from the body, there no longer remained for the form any preventer to prevent it from what it intends, because the body was like a prison for it."

So long as the wicked form was joined to the body, its powers were limited; the body, by its density, kasafat, and its deficiency, naqs, restrained it, hemming in what it could do. The body was, for the wicked form, like a prison, a sijn. When the prisoners were confined, their powers were curtailed; once released from the prison, they may do as they wish. So when the dark form separates from the body, no obstacle remains to hinder it from whatever it intends. Its powers, formerly checked by the flesh, are now unbound.

Here the metaphysical caution of the whole discourse must be recalled, lest we attribute too solid a reality to this form. Evil, in itself, is not a thing; it has no existence of its own. Distance from good is what evil is; it is one's own creation. The origin is the intellect; to move away from the intellect is to become soul; and the soul too, though it is a thing, is not a thing that abides — it has no permanence, for it is a thing fashioned by one's own hands and possesses no enduring root. The dark form, then, for all its terrible liberty, is a self-wrought privation, not a substance that stands beside the good as its equal.

That this liberated form is not prevented from its torment is itself a decree of justice: `wa kana fi 'adl-illahi ta'ala an la tahjuz tilka al-nafs al-khabitha 'amma tastahiqquhu, li-anna mizan al-'adl qa'imun; fa-hiya tablughu ma tastahiqquhu min 'adhab; ma'a annaha min waqti mufaraqatiha li-jismiha fa-hiya fi jami'i ma taf'alu bi-mashiyatiha wa ghayri mashiyatiha fi zarbin min al-'adhab` — "and it was in the justice of Allah the Exalted that that wicked soul should not be prevented from what it deserves, because the scale of justice is established; so it attains what it deserves of torment; together with the fact that, from the moment of its separation from its body, it is, in all that it does, by its will and without its will, in a kind of torment."

It belongs to the justice of Allah the Exalted that the wicked soul not be hindered from what it deserves; the scale of justice, the mizan al-'adl, is established, and so the soul must complete its give-and-take, its full reckoning. Therefore it attains what it deserves of torment, and of what lies beyond torment. And along with this — and this is the heart of it — from the very moment it separates from its body, that wicked soul is, in everything it does, whether by its own will or against its will, in some kind of torment. It is never out of torment. The dark form is in a state of suffering. It possesses free will in some respects, acting by its own choice, and in other respects it acts by Allah's will, not its own; and in both cases alike its torments are complete. By its will and without its will — `bi-mashiyatiha wa ghayri mashiyatiha` — it abides in suffering. May Allah and His friends, His awliya', protect us from that; may He keep us in His protection.

### The Soul That Cannot Pierce the Veils

The wicked soul, in its new liberty, attempts an ascent — and is hurled back. The text relates: `fa-idha faraqat maskanaha alladhi huwa dhalika al-jism al-khabith, talabat al-su'uda nafidhatan li-kharq al-hujub, fa-ta'kisuha ash'iat al-aflak raji'atan ila al-ard` — "so when it separated from its dwelling, which is that wicked body, it sought ascent, striving to pierce the veils; then the rays of the celestial spheres reflect it back, returning to the earth."

When the wicked soul leaves its dwelling — the wicked body that was its home — it seeks to rise. It strives to pierce the veils, the kharq al-hujub; nafidhatan means striving to pass through, to break through, to penetrate. The soul aims upward, toward the 'alam al-anwar, the world of lights, striving to tear through the intervening veils and ascend. But it cannot. The rays of the celestial spheres, the ash'iat al-aflak, reflect it back, casting it down again toward the earth. It longs to pass through the heavens, but the Qur'an pronounces the law that binds it: `la tanfudhuna illa bi-sultan` — "you will not pass through except by an authority." Lacking that authority, the wicked soul is reflected back and returns to the earth.

What does it then seek? `fa-tatlubu dhalika al-jism alladhi kanat taskunuhu fa-la tajiduhu, fa-tabqa mar'ubatan hasiratan tahumu hawla manzil dhalika al-jism alladhi kana fihi mahalluhu wa maskanuhu` — "so it seeks that body in which it used to dwell, but it does not find it, so it remains terrified, regretful, roaming around the place of that body, which was its location and its dwelling." The soul, flung back to earth, seeks the body it once inhabited, wishing to return to its old house. But that old house is finished; where in the ruined dwelling can it go? It seeks its body and does not find it. So it remains in a state of terror, mar'ubah, and of regret, hasirah — for hasara is to feel remorse, to grieve over loss — wandering about the place where its body had been, the spot that was its location and its dwelling, circling the body and the grave. And this is the very thing that the common people, the 'ammah, call by the name mun'if. The text notes that mun'if also bears the senses of an elevated mountain and a descending valley; but the people apply the word to this roaming, regretful presence about the grave.

### The Roaming Soul Between Earth and Sky

The text continues: `wa tabqa maghbunatan 'ala manzil al-jism wa qabrihi, tajulu muta'allimatan lima hiya fihi, wa rubbama sumi'a lahu aninun mimma yu'aniyahu wa nutqun, thumma tajulu ba'da dhalika ma bayna al-ard wa al-sama'` — "and it remains grieved over the place of the body and its grave, roaming and suffering on account of what it is in; and sometimes a moaning is heard from it, from what it endures, and speech; then after that it roams between the earth and the sky." The soul remains aggrieved by the place of its body and its grave, roaming about and grieving over the state it is in. And sometimes a moaning is heard from it — a sound of crying, an anin — from the suffering it endures, and sometimes speech is heard as well. Then, after this, it roams between the earth and the sky.

For this reason the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said: `al-hawa' la khala'un wa la mala'` — "the air is neither empty nor full." The air is not empty, for there is something in it; yet neither is it altogether full. It is at once empty and full: not void of these roaming presences, yet not packed solid either. The hadith speaks to precisely this — the space between earth and sky is inhabited by such wandering souls, so that the air can be called neither wholly vacant nor wholly filled.

### The Refuge of the Wicked Soul in Filthy and Desolate Places

The text now names where these roaming souls take shelter: `wa hiya ta'wi ila al-mawadi' al-muzlimati` — "and it takes refuge in the dark places." The wicked form remains in this roaming condition for as long as the Governor, the Mudabbir, wills; and it seeks refuge — taludhu, it takes refuge — in dark places. It betakes itself to the dark and gloomy houses, the mawadi' al-muzlimah; to the foul-smelling urinals; to the desolate and terrifying wildernesses; and to the filthy slaughterhouses, the places where animals are slain.

It is on account of the presence of such souls in these places that a particular supplication is prescribed. When one enters the lavatory, one recites: `a'udhu billahi min al-rijs al-najis al-khabith al-mukhbith min al-shaytan al-rajim` — "I seek refuge in Allah from the filth, the impure, the wicked, the defiling, from the accursed Satan." For the presence of these wicked souls is considered to be in such defiled places, and the believer seeks God's protection from them upon entering.

### The Wicked Soul in the Dreams of the Like-Minded

The dark soul does not merely roam and hide; it actively seeks out those who resemble it, and works upon them in their sleep. The text reads: `wa rubbama takhayyalat li-ansarin laha min ahl al-nufus al-khabithati fi nawmihim, wa tunajihim bima yuhibbunahu fi ara'ihim wa i'tiqadatihim al-fasidah, fa-yazdaduna intizaman bima hum 'alayhi min dhalika al-ra'y alladhi ya'taqidunahu mimma qad sawwabathu lahum wa najathum bihi 'an sababihi, wa yum'inuna min 'adawat ahl al-haqq wa al-bara'ati minhum wa al-mujadadati lahum wa al-mayli ila addadihim` — "and sometimes it appears in the imagination of helpers of its own kind, from the people of wicked souls, in their sleep, and it converses with them about what they love in their opinions and their corrupt beliefs; so they increase in adherence to that opinion which they believe, by what it has made to seem correct to them and conversed with them about; and they grow in enmity toward the People of Truth, in disavowal of them, in contention against them, and in inclination toward their opposites."

Sometimes the wicked soul appears in the imaginations of people like itself — those of wicked souls, the ahl al-nufus al-khabithah — in their sleep. There it converses with them about the very things they love, confirming their corrupt opinions and beliefs. It comes to them in dream and assures them that they are upon the right path, making correct in their eyes the false opinion they hold; sawwaba is to make a thing seem right, and a sound opinion is called a ra'y sa'ib. So these wicked people increase in their attachment to the false opinion they believe, the soul having confirmed it for them and conversed with them to that end. And thereby they grow in enmity toward the Ahl al-Haqq, in disavowal and disassociation from them, in contention and obstinate opposition against them — mujadadah being from the form fa'ala yufa'ilu mufa'alatan, the mutual contending — and in inclination toward the opposites of the People of Truth.

All of this is confirmed by the word of God: `kama qala ta'ala: shayatin al-ins wa al-jinn yuhi ba'duhum ila ba'din zukhruf al-qawl ghurura` — "as the Exalted said: the devils of mankind and of the jinn inspire one another with decorated speech in delusion." The devils among men and among jinn inspire one another, whispering to each other ornamented words as a deception. The wicked soul, working in the dreams of its like-minded helpers, is an instance of this very mutual inspiration of devilry that the Qur'an describes.

### Speaking Through a Living Tongue: The Soul Called Ruh

The wicked form, lacking a body of its own, can act upon the world only by using the body of another, and it does so by mingling with an ignorant person. The text says: `wa huwa fi athna'i dhalika yajidu dhalika al-insanu al-jahilu bi-dhalika al-hal, aw imra'atan jahilatan, aw sabiyyan bi-hadha al-mathabah, wa tumazijuhu tilka al-surat al-khabithah, fa-yaqulu al-'ammatu: khalatuhu al-jinn, wa fa'alat bihi wa sana'at` — "and in the midst of that, it finds an ignorant man in that state, or an ignorant woman, or a boy of this disposition, and that wicked form mingles with him; so the common people say: the jinn has mingled with him, and has done to him and worked upon him this and that." The wicked form finds an ignorant man, or an ignorant woman, or a boy of such a temperament, and mingles itself with that person, descending into him. The common people then say that the jinn has come upon him, that it has done this and that to him — a kind of possession, an asib.

The text proceeds: `thumma idha khalat tilka al-surah bi-dhalika al-insan wa al-insanah, yantiqu 'ala lisanihi, wa takadu ta'ti fi dhalika al-nutq bi-shay'in min al-ghaybiyyat wa al-hawadith al-ka'inah` — "then when that form has mingled with that man or that woman, it speaks through his tongue, and it almost comes, in that speech, with something of the unseen matters and of events that are to occur." Once the form has mingled with the man or the woman, it speaks through his tongue. And in that speech it may bring forth something of the unseen, the ghaybiyyat, and of events yet to come. It is not necessary, however, that what it utters be true; such utterances serve to deceive and are not always true — there is something of this kind in the predictions men have famously made. The reason it can speak of unseen things at all is that it has become, on the strength of what was said before, the extremity of evil. And this is what the common people have named the ruh or the ruhiyyah — the "spirit." What it informs of, it does not leave incomplete.

A crucial limitation governs all of this: the wicked soul cannot, of itself, affect anything in the world until it makes use of another body. By itself it is unable to act; it becomes manifest and effective only through the body of another, manifesting through that borrowed frame. Hence its dependence upon the ignorant man or woman through whom it speaks.

### The Soul That Disparages the Truth Before Its Helpers

The wicked soul, when present among those who give it shelter, turns them yet further against the truth. The text relates: `wa rubbama zamma ahl al-haqq 'inda man yahduruhu min ansarihi wa ashabihi, wa yu'akkidu 'indahum ma hum 'alayhi min al-mujadadati wa al-i'tiqad al-fasid, wa yumilu qulubahum ila qawlihi, wa yusaddiqunahu wa yu'azzimunahu 'indahum wa yuti'unahu fima ya'muruhum bihi wa yanhahum 'anhu, kulla dhalika li-ma sabqa min al-munasabati allati baynahum wa al-tashakuli wa al-tamathuli fi al-mujadadati li-ahl al-haqq wa al-muwalati li-a'da'ihim` — "and sometimes it disparages the People of Truth before those of its helpers and companions who are present with it, and it confirms among them what they are upon of contention and corrupt belief, and it inclines their hearts toward its word; and they affirm it and magnify it among themselves, and obey it in what it commands them and forbids them — all of that on account of the prior affinity between them, the similarity in form, and the resemblance in opposing the People of Truth and in loving their enemies."

Sometimes the wicked soul speaks ill of the Ahl al-Haqq in the presence of those of its helpers and companions who are gathered with it; it disparages the People of Truth among them. It confirms them in what they are already upon — their obstinate contention and their corrupt belief — and it inclines their hearts toward its own word. They, in turn, affirm it as truthful, magnify and revere it in their assemblies, and obey it in whatever it commands and whatever it forbids. And all of this is on account of the prior affinity, the munasabah, that exists between them: the likeness in form, the tashakul, and the resemblance, the tamathul, in their shared opposition to the People of Truth and in their love for the enemies of the truth. Like draws to like, and the wicked soul finds in these helpers a kinship that makes them pliant to its whisperings.

This is set, finally, within its scriptural frame: `wa hadha aydan min al-iha'i wa al-waswasati wa al-ghawayati allati taziduhum jami'an bu'dan min al-haqq wa al-shaytanatan wa kamalan fi al-iblissiyyah` — "and this also is of the inspiration, the whispering, and the beguiling that increase them all in distance from the truth, in devilry, and in completeness in Iblis-like behavior." All of this belongs to inspiration, to whispering, and to beguiling — the very things named in the Qur'an of the one `alladhi yuwasswisu fi sudur al-nas min al-jinnati wa al-nas`, "who whispers in the breasts of mankind, from among the jinn and mankind." For human beings also whisper, and this wicked soul whispers too. By this whispering and beguiling, all of them are increased in distance from the truth, in shaytanah, devilry, and in completeness of Iblis-like behavior. And so this soul remains in that wicked house, in that wicked man, for as long as the Governor of the world of nature wills — `fa-yuqimu fi dhalika al-bayt al-khabith ma sha'a al-mudabbiru li-'alam al-tabi'ah` — and within that house it only increases in wickedness and in distance from the truth and its people, in stubbornness and persistence in transgression, and in the acquisition of sins.

### The Final Drawing: The Dark Node as Isthmus

At last the host dies, and the wicked form is drawn to its proper gathering-place. The text reads: `fa-idha mata dhalika al-insan wa al-insanah, faraqathu tilka al-surat al-khabithah, wa jadhabaha magnatis al-khabith al-falaki al-muzlim al-munkar alladhi huwa 'uqdata al-falak` — "so when that man or woman dies, that wicked form departs from him, and the wicked, celestial, dark, abominable magnet draws it — that which is the node of the celestial sphere." When the wicked man or woman in whom the form had lodged finally dies, the wicked form leaves him, and the magnet that is the node of the celestial sphere — wicked, celestial, dark, and abominable — draws it to itself.

These are the two nodes of the sphere, the same that are called Rahu and Ketu, the dhanab and the ras, the tail and the head. When the wicked soul leaves the body, this dark magnet pulls it; for this is the center toward which all wicked souls are drawn, just as the pillar of light, the 'amud al-nur, draws the spiritual souls to itself. The drawing happens after the soul has completed its reckoning — after it has finished whatever give-and-take was owed of it. Like Rahu and Ketu themselves, these nodes are imaginary, wahmi; they are not visible, and they do not reflect light. It has been mentioned in connection with the sun and the moon that when there is an eclipse, solar or lunar, the activity of Rahu and Ketu begins at that very time.

The reason for this drawing is stated: `li-annahuma al-barzakh al-muzlim al-jabidh li-l-nufus al-khabithah li-l-munasabati allati baynahum` — "because the two of them are the dark isthmus that pulls the wicked souls, on account of the affinity that is between them." The two nodes are the dark isthmus, the barzakh al-muzlim, which pulls the wicked souls toward itself, on account of the affinity, the munasabah, between the souls and the nodes — for the two nodes are the magnet for the evils, their root and their foundation. In Arabic both jabaza and jazaba are used, and both carry the same meaning, "to draw" or "to pull"; the isthmus draws the wicked souls as a magnet draws iron.

And so the dark form proceeds at last into the horizon of the two nodes: `fa-tasiru tilka al-surat al-khabithah fi ufuqihima... wa tabqa hunalika hatta tajtami'a bi-ansariha wa ashakaliha ma sha'a al-mudabbiru li-dhalik` — "so that wicked form proceeds into their horizon... and it remains there until it gathers with its helpers and its likes, for as long as the Governor wills for that." The wicked form enters their ufuq, their horizon, their court and sphere of influence — having become a Satan. And there it remains, gathered with its helpers and its look-alikes, its ashakil, for as long as the Governor of the world of nature wills it to remain. As the luminous form gathers with its kind in the haykal of light, the dark form gathers with its kind in the horizon of the dark nodes; and the two destinies stand, to the very end, as perfect opposites — the extremity of good and the extremity of evil, the pillar of light and the dark celestial jawzahar, with no space between the two for any soul to occupy.

## The Counter-Types and the Poison of Enmity

### The Devilish Forms in the Two Knots and Their Descent into Food and Drink

We take up the text where the previous discourse paused, with a teaching about where the devils come from and how they make their way into the world of human beings. The text reads `man tawaffa hunalika min tilka-s-surah allati qad sarat shayatin` — "the one who comes to his end there, from that form which has become devils." A precise point of recitation is to be observed: the verb is `tawaffa`, with the doubled `fa`, on the pattern of `tafa'ada` and `tafazala`; at its end there stands a written `ya` that is not pronounced — it is inscribed but silent in reading. The meaning is that there is a duration, a span of time, allotted to the one who arrives at that place, and what arrives there arrives from a `surah`, a form, that has already turned into devils, into `shayatin`.

What, then, are these `shayatin`? They are not abstractions but `surat-ha-i-shaytan` — devilish forms, the very shapes of devilry. And they reside in a definite location: the `uqdatain`, the two knots, which together constitute the dark isthmus, the `barzakh al-zulmani`. This is the standing teaching that anchors everything that follows. Those who are holders of `tasawwurat-i-khabithah`, of foul and corrupt conceptions, do not scatter; they gather. And the place of their gathering is precisely the two knots. There the `ijtima'` of the `sharir`, the assembly of the evil ones, takes place; there all of them come together and add to one another's `shararat`, their mischief and malignancy. The two knots are, in this respect, the reservoir and the meeting-house of every devilish form.

From this reservoir the descent begins. The text continues `tanzilu ila-l-ardi bi-mashi'ati-l-mudabbir fa-yasiru fi shai'in min ma'kulat wa mashrubat` — "it descends to the earth by the will of the Director, then enters into some thing of the edibles and the drinkables." These devilish forms fall — they descend toward the earth — and they do so not by their own initiative but `bi-mashi'ati-l-mudabbir`, by the will and design of the Mudabbir, the Director who governs the unfolding of all things. By that will they come down, and when they reach the earth they enter into things: specifically into `ma'kulat` and `mashrubat`, the things that are eaten and the things that are drunk. They become, as it were, lodged within food and drink, awaiting the one who will consume them.

A clarifying question is properly raised here, lest the teaching be misconstrued in a crudely material way. Are the idols that certain peoples worship — figures such as Kali and the like — are these the `majami'`, the gatherings, that have been described, or the `haykal`, the structures? They are indeed `khabith`, foul things, every one of them; that much is plain. But they do not enter into a `haykal` as such. The gathering, the `majma'`, forms in the two knots, and what gathers there is the `suwarat`, the forms — not the `maddah`, the matter. The distinction must be held firmly: there is on the one hand the `maddah`, the gross matter, and on the other the `surat`, the form. The `maddah-i-khabith`, the foul matters, are what manifest in this physical world — these idols and such objects are material things, evil things, things that have not appeared in any good form. But the gathering in the two knots is a gathering of forms, of conceptions, not of physical idols. The two registers must not be confused.

### Why the Respite Is Granted: The Logic of Retribution

A natural and pressing question arises: why is this respite granted at all? Why is the punishment of the wrongdoer postponed, so that these devilish forms are allowed to gather and descend and work their effect, rather than being struck down at once? The answer lies in the very logic of `qisas`, of retribution. The respite is given so that the one from whom retribution is due can have his `zuhur`, his manifestation in the world. Only when he has appeared, only when he stands openly present, can the full `qisas` be exacted from him. The retribution must be complete, and for it to be complete the time must come and both parties must be ready — the one who is owed retribution present, and the one from whom it is owed present as well. When both are present, then the full account can be settled, and once it is settled, everything is cleared. Even the satanic work of tormenting and inflicting pain is folded into this respite: it too serves the eventual completion of the retribution. The postponement, then, is not an oversight or a slackening of justice; it is the precondition of justice being fully done.

### Nourishment Taken by Friend or Foe, and the Food as a Passageway

The text now turns to the one who takes nourishment from these poisoned things: `fa-yagtadhi biha man yastahiqqu an yagtadhi dhalika` — "and there takes nourishment from it the one who is deserving of taking such nourishment." The `ghiza`, the nourishment, is drawn from those foods and drinks — from the `ma'kulat` and `mashrubat`, the very things into which the devilish forms have entered. And here a teaching of great consequence is delivered: the one who takes this nourishment may be a `wali`, a friend of the people of truth, or a `zidd`, an opposite and adversary. Both may eat of it. The friend can partake of these edibles and drinkables, and the adversary can partake of them as well. The poisoned substance does not discriminate at the point of consumption; what differs is what it produces through the one who consumes it.

For the substance becomes, in the text's word, a `ma'bar`: `fa-yakunu li-dhalika ma'baran` — "and it becomes for that a passageway." It is a place of crossing, a passage through which the `surat-i-khabithah`, the evil form, makes its manifestation into the world. And the examples are momentous. From this passageway Qabil had his `zuhur`, his manifestation, from Adam. In the same manner, from various Imams there were born sons who became enemies of the Imam of their time — brothers who turned into adversaries. This addresses the question that would otherwise trouble the reader: does eating the poisoned thing have no effect upon the one who eats it? The point is that the effect comes through him, not necessarily upon him — it passes through him and appears in his children, in his progeny.

The mechanism is then set out in detail. The text reads `thumma yazharu minhu 'inda-l-mulamasati baynahu wa bayna zawjatihi` — "then it appears from him at the time of intimacy between him and his wife." At the moment of `mulamasah`, of intimacy between the man and his wife, the evil form appears from him. Then `fa-yakunu minhu waladun yatarakka fi-r-rutab` — "and there comes from him a son who progresses in the ranks." A son is born from him, and this son ascends through the `rutab`, the ranks, stage by stage. The text continues `ila an yazhara mawludan wa yatarabba fa-yablughu-l-huluma` — "until he appears as a newborn, and is raised, and reaches maturity." He is born as a `mawlud`, a newborn; he is given `tarbiyah`, upbringing and nurture — for the verb is `tarabba`, which means to receive upbringing, an intransitive form that takes a subject but no object, distinct from `rabba` (`rabbahu`), which means to give upbringing and does take an object — and so, having received his nurture, he reaches `bulugh`, the age of maturity. The progression is complete: from the form that appeared at intimacy, to the newborn, to the nurtured child, to the matured man.

### From Devil-in-Potential to Devil-in-Actuality: The Making of the Zidd

What has this matured son become? The text answers: `fa-yakunu ziddan li-dhalika-t-tawr wa wasiyyihi aw Imami-l-'Asr` — "and he becomes the opposite of that cycle, and of its legatee, or of the Imam of the Age." He becomes the `zidd`, the opposite — the opposite of the `natiq` of that `tawr`, that cycle, or the opposite of the natiq's `wasi`, his legatee, or the opposite of the `Imam al-'Asr`, the Imam of the Age. Here we should mark the breadth of the teaching: the son who eats the poisoned thing — whether he is the son of a `wali` or the son of a `zidd` — may himself become a `zidd`. The son of a friend can become an opposite, and the son of an opposite can become an opposite. The line of descent does not fix the outcome; the passageway and what passes through it do.

And with this the decisive transformation is named: `wa huwa-sh-shaytanu bi-l-fi'l` — "and he is the devil in actuality." This son who has matured into a `zidd` has passed from being a `shaytan bi-l-quwwah`, a devil in potentiality, into being a `shaytan bi-l-fi'l`, a devil in actuality. The potentiality that lay latent in the descended form has now been actualized in a living adversary who walks the earth and opposes the holders of the divine stations.

### The Three Acts of the Zidd: Claiming, Concealing, and Denying

How does this actualized devil conduct himself? The text describes three connected acts. First, `wa yadda'i rutbata dhalika-l-maqami-l-'ali` — "and he claims the rank of that high station." He advances a claim; he claims for himself the `rutba`, the rank, of the `maqam al-'ali`, the high station that belongs by right to the holder of truth. Second, `wa yughatti manzilatahu` — "and he conceals his status." He covers over and hides the `manzilah`, the true standing, of the rightful holder, so that it cannot be seen for what it is. Third, `wa yajhadu maqamahu` — "and he denies his station." The verb here is `jahada`, with the soft `ha`, meaning to deny and reject — not `jihada`, to strive — and it signifies that he denies and repudiates the `maqam`, the station, of the rightful one. Claiming the rank, concealing the standing, and denying the station: these three together constitute the characteristic work of the `zidd`.

### Concealment as Spiritual Killing: Al-Harith ibn Murrah and Qabil

The text then introduces a teaching about the gravity of concealment itself: `wa rubbama yakunu huwa-l-mutawalli li-haqiqati-l-hadd alladhi huwa-l-maqamu bi-nafsihi ka-l-Harith ibn Murrah alladhi ghatta manzilata Adam 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and perhaps he is the one who takes charge of [the killing of] the reality of the hadd, which is the station itself, like al-Harith ibn Murrah who concealed the station of Adam, peace be upon him." (A point of reading: the word is `mutawalli`, the one who takes charge or assumes authority, though it had been written `mutawali`.) Sometimes, then, the `zidd` becomes the one who takes charge of the killing of that `hadd`, that rank — a killing aimed at the `maqam`, the station, itself. He intends to kill the Imam. The exemplar given is al-Harith ibn Murrah, who concealed the `manzilah`, the station, of Adam, peace be upon him.

A question of the first importance is raised here: is concealing the `manzilah` itself a form of killing? The answer is yes — it is a `batini qatl`, a spiritual or inward killing. To conceal a `hadd`'s station, to cover over the true standing of one who holds a rank in the divine hierarchy, is itself a kind of murder, an inward murder distinct from the shedding of physical blood. And yet the one who commits this inward killing may also be a `qatil`, an outward killer, as well. The two need not be separate persons. This is illustrated by the next example: `wa ka-Qabil alladhi ghatta manzilata Habil wa-dda'a maqamahu wa qatalahu` — "and like Qabil who concealed the station of Habil, and claimed his station, and killed him." Qabil performed all three acts together: he concealed the `manzilah` of Habil, he claimed his `maqam`, and then he killed him outright. In Qabil the inward concealment and the outward slaying are joined in a single person, just as al-Harith ibn Murrah stood as the enemy of Adam. The discourse is showing us where such manifestations arise — they arise from the two knots — and it is naming the pairs of prophet and adversary across the sacred history.

### The Roll of the Counter-Types: Adversaries of the Prophets and Imams

The text now sets out a long and solemn roll of the counter-types, naming for each holder of truth the `zidd` who opposed him. We preserve each pairing as the text delivers it.

`wa ka-Rasib alladhi kana ziddan li-Nuh 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and like Rasib who was the opposite of Nuh, peace be upon him." Rasib was the `zidd` of Nuh. A question is raised whether Rasib was a son, a `farzand`, of Nuh; the answer given is that he was not necessarily a son — it is not a requirement of being a `zidd` that one be a son, though a `zidd` can indeed be a son. In Rasib's case it is said he was not.

`wa ka-Ham alladhi kana ziddan li-Sam wa qatalahu ba'da dhalik` — "and like Ham who was the opposite of Shem and killed him after that." Ham was the `zidd` of Sam; they were brothers, and Ham killed his brother Sam.

`wa ka-Nimrud ibn Kan'an alladhi kana ziddan li-Ibrahim 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and like Nimrud son of Kan'an who was the opposite of Ibrahim, peace be upon him." Nimrud ibn Kan'an was the `zidd` of Ibrahim, peace be upon him; he was of the line of Ham.

`wa ka-l-Walid ibn Mus'ab alladhi kana ziddan li-Musa 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and like al-Walid ibn Mus'ab who was the opposite of Musa, peace be upon him." Al-Walid ibn Mus'ab was the `zidd` of Musa.

`wa ka-Sahbuta alladhi qatala 'Isa 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and like Sahbuta who killed 'Isa, peace be upon him." Sahbuta was the one who killed 'Isa, peace be upon him.

`wa ka-Abi Lahab wa Abi Jahl alladhayn kana zidayn li-Muhammadin sallallahu 'alayhi wa alihi wa sallam` — "and like Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl, who were the two opposites of Muhammad, may God bless him and his family and grant them peace." Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl were the two `zidd`s of the Prophet Muhammad.

Then, for Ali ibn Abi Talib, the text gives two opposites and a later killer. (A textual matter is noted in passing: in the printed copy there appears a slip in the writing before this point, and the correct reading is established here.) `wa ka-Abi Bakrin wa 'Umar la'natullahi 'alayhima alladhayn kana zidayn li-'Ali ibn Abi Talib salawatullahi 'alayhi` — "and like Abu Bakr and 'Umar, may God's curse be upon them both, who were the two opposites of Ali ibn Abi Talib, may God's blessings be upon him." Abu Bakr and 'Umar were the two `zidd`s of Ali ibn Abi Talib, with the curse of God upon them, and they were his opposites firstly, `awwalan`. Then `wa ka-Ibn Muljam la'nahullah alladhi qatalahu akhiran` — "and like Ibn Muljam, may God's curse be upon him, who killed him lastly." Ibn Muljam was the one who killed Ali ibn Abi Talib at the last, `akhiran`. So the pattern for Ali is: two opposites firstly, and the one who slew him at the end.

`wa ka-Mu'awiyah la'natullah alladhi kana ziddan li-l-Hasan thumma sammahu` — "and like Mu'awiyah, may God's curse be upon him, who was the opposite of al-Hasan, then he poisoned him." Mu'awiyah was the `zidd` of al-Hasan, peace be upon him, and then he gave him poison.

`wa ka-Yazid la'nahullah alladhi kana ziddan li-l-Husayn` — "and like Yazid, may God's curse be upon him, who was the opposite of al-Husayn." Yazid was the `zidd` of al-Husayn. And `wa ka-sh-Shimr alladhi qatalahu` — "and like Shimr who killed him." Shimr was the one who killed al-Husayn.

`wa ka-lladhi samma Mawlana al-Mustansir Billah salawatullahi 'alayhi` — "and like the one who poisoned our master al-Mustansir Billah, may God's blessings be upon him." And `wa ka-l-qatil li-Mawlana al-Amir salawatullahi 'alayhi` — "and like the killer of our master al-Amir, may God's blessings be upon him." Each of these blessed masters, al-Mustansir Billah and al-Amir, was struck down by a poisoner or a killer who was, in his inner reality, a `zidd`.

### The Poisoning of the Prophet at Khaybar and the Testimony of the Goat

The roll then turns to a particular and weighty episode. `wa ka-l-Imra'ati-l-Khaybariyya la'nahallah allati sammat an-nabi sallallahu 'alayhi fi-t-ta'am` — "and like the woman of Khaybar, may God's curse be upon her, who poisoned the Prophet, may God bless him, in the food." The woman of Khaybar, al-Imra'at al-Khaybariyya, poisoned the Prophet in his food. The text continues `wa ma'ahu rajulan` — "and with him were two men." Two men were with the Prophet, and they too partook of the food. `fa-mata ahaduhuma` — "so one of them died." One of the two men passed away. `wa da'a rasulullah sallallahu 'alayhi li-ahadihima fa-salima` — "and the Messenger of God, may God bless him, prayed for one of them, and he was saved." The Prophet prayed for one of them, and he was kept safe. (On the reading of `salima`: the consonants admit `sallama` — "he handed over," or "he said the greeting of peace" — and `saluma` and `salima`, each with its own sense; here the sense is `salima`, "he was kept safe.")

Then comes the gravest part: `wa aqama dhalika-s-summu ma'a-n-nabi sallallahu 'alayhi ila an kana sababa rihlatihi` — "and that poison remained with the Prophet, may God bless him, until it became the cause of his passing." The poison, which had come from the two knots, remained in the Prophet's body from the time of the Battle of Khaybar until his passing, and it became the very cause of his departure from this world. His passing, in this teaching, was from that poison.

And here the discourse pauses upon a sign of the Prophet's surpassing greatness. When the Prophet had eaten of the food, the leg of the goat itself testified to him, saying in effect: "O Prophet of God, stop! I am poisoned" — `masmum`. Consider what manner of thing gave this testimony: not a living animal, but the leg of a goat that had been cut from the body, whose skin had been removed, which had been roasted and cooked over fire, and then set before the Prophet. From that cooked and severed limb came a testimony to the Prophet of God, peace and blessings be upon him, informing him: "I am poisoned." When Ali, may God's blessings be upon him, was once asked how the Prophet's miracle compared with the miracles granted to earlier prophets — for it was said that one prophet had this sign and another that sign — he answered that for the earlier prophets living animals had given testimony, but the Prophet's distinction was that even the cooked leg of a slaughtered goat, severed and skinned and roasted, was brought and gave testimony to him.

A reader naturally asks: why did the goat's leg testify only after he had eaten, and not before? Had it spoken beforehand, the harm could have been averted. The answer returns us to the logic already established: how, then, would the `qisas`, the retribution, be completed? The greatness of the Prophet is precisely what is being discussed, and the completion of the retribution required that the poison take its course. To see the whole pattern, consider the line of enemies once more: the enemy of Adam was al-Harith ibn Murrah, and Qabil; the enemy of Nuh was Rasib; the enemy of Ibrahim was Nimrud ibn Kan'an; the enemy of Sam was Ham; the enemy of Musa was al-Walid ibn Mus'ab; the enemy of 'Isa was Sahbuta; and the two enemies of the Prophet were Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl. This last pairing is confirmed by the hadith `li-kulli nabiyyin shaytanun wa liya shaytanan` — "for every prophet there is one devil, and for me there are two devils." For every prophet there is a single devil; for the Prophet Muhammad there are two, and they are Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl. Thereafter come the opposites of Ali — Abu Bakr and 'Umar, and Ibn Muljam who slew him at the last — and Mu'awiyah, the `zidd` of al-Hasan, and Yazid and Shimr, the enemies of al-Husayn who confronted and killed him, and the adversaries of Mawlana al-Mustansir and Mawlana al-Amir.

### The Vices upon Vegetation and Mineral: Poison and the Sword

The discourse now opens its second great movement, explaining how the vices that gather in the two knots reach the noble stations through the two channels of vegetation and mineral. The text reads `wa qad yakunu min hadhihi-l-qaba'is allati hiya surun mujtami'ah fi 'uqdatain alladhayni huma al-barzakh al-zulmani ma yujra 'ala-n-nabat fa-yakunu summan qatilan minhu talaf al-maqamat al-sharifah ka-s-summi alladhi kana sababan li-talaf al-natiq` — "and there may be from these vices — which are forms gathered in the two knots that are the dark isthmus — that which is made to flow upon the vegetation, so that it becomes a deadly poison from which comes the destruction of the noble stations, like the poison that was a cause for the destruction of the Natiq."

The teaching is this. The `qaba'is`, the vices, are forms — `surun mujtami'ah` — that gather in the two knots, the `uqdatain`, which together are the dark isthmus, the `barzakh al-zulmani`. From these gathered vices, something is made to flow upon the `nabat`, the vegetation. When it descends upon the vegetation, that vegetation becomes a `summ`, a poison — a deadly, killing poison. And by this poison the `maqamat al-sharifah`, the noble stations, are destroyed; `talaf`, destruction and death, befalls them. The exemplar is the poison that was the cause of the destruction of the Natiq himself. The text adds `wa-s-summu alladhi talafa minhu al-Hasan 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and the poison from which al-Hasan, peace be upon him, was destroyed." By this same kind of poison al-Hasan met his destruction; in the Urdu idiom, `talaf hua`, he was destroyed by it. And further, `Mawlana Muslim bin Salwat wa Mawlana al-Shams bin Salwat... jami'an` — by this poison Mawlana Muslim and Mawlana al-Shams, all of them together, were destroyed as well.

Then the text turns to the second channel: `wa minha ma yaqa'u fi-l-ma'adin` — "and from it is that which falls into the minerals." Some of these forms of vices fall not upon vegetation but into the `ma'adin`, the minerals. And from them are made `suyuf wa sakakin wa nisal` — swords, knives, and arrowheads. From these forms, when they enter the minerals, come the swords and the knives and the arrowheads with which the noble stations are killed; with these weapons the assault is carried out upon the noble stations, `al-maqamat al-sharifah al-sabiq zikruha`, those whose mention has already preceded — the very swords with which the Imams were slain, the knives, the arrows. The forms whose origin is the two knots descend, and through their parts they take their revenge, whether lodged in mineral or in vegetation. The text confirms `yuftaku bihi fi-l-maqamat al-sharifah al-sabiq zikruha` — "with which an assault is made upon the noble stations whose mention has preceded."

The reasoning is then made explicit. `Li-anna hadhihi-l-qaba'is idha waqa'a minha 'ala-n-nabat kana dhalika al-nabat sumuman qatilan li-man yasiru ilayhi min al-maqamat al-sharifah al-masmumah` — "because these vices, when some of them fall upon the vegetation, that vegetation becomes a deadly poison for those of the noble stations, the poisoned ones, who go toward it." And `wa in waqa'a 'ala ma'danin kana minhu suyufun wa sakakinu wa nisalun li-n-nabli yuqtalu biha man qutila min al-maqamat al-sharifah` — "and if it falls upon a mineral, from it come swords, knives, and arrowheads for the bow, with which are killed those of the noble stations who are killed." The two channels are thus exactly parallel: vice upon vegetation yields the poison that slays from within, and vice upon mineral yields the blade and the arrow that slay from without.

Why do these poisoned things and weaponized things find their targets? The text answers `li-annaha taqa'u 'ala amthaliha min ma'adin wa-n-nabat al-madhmumah al-mal'unah` — "because they fall upon their counterparts, from the condemned and cursed minerals and vegetation." Like falls upon like; the poisoned vegetation and the weaponized mineral fall upon their own counterparts, which are themselves condemned and cursed. So it was said of the sword of Shimr — Shimr's very sword was described in just this way, as a thing of the cursed mineral that found its cursed counterpart.

### The Preservation of the Weapon: The Dark Atmosphere as Keeper

The text then states a teaching about the safekeeping of these instruments: `fa-kullu shay'in minha mahfuzun li-sahibihi wa-l-hafizu lahu huwa al-jaww al-zulmani` — "so every thing from it is preserved for its owner, and the preserver of it is the dark atmosphere." Every such poisoned or weaponized thing is `mahfuz`, kept and preserved, for its `sahib`, its owner — the one for whom it is destined. And its preserver, its keeper, is `al-jaww al-zulmani`, the dark atmosphere. The management of these things continues unbroken: where to keep each one, at what time to deliver it — these arrangements proceed under the keeping of the dark atmosphere, which is none other than the work of the two knots. The word `mahfuz` carries the sense of a thing kept safe, protected, held in reserve; its protection is carried out so that, when the one who is to be killed arrives, he can be killed with precisely the instrument prepared for him. It is preserved for its owner, and the preserver is the dark atmosphere, which is the two knots.

And this preservation does not operate arbitrarily. `Bi-mashi'at al-mudabbir wa mujib al-hikmah wa mizan al-'adli lillahi ta'ala` — "by the will of the Director, and the requirement of wisdom, and the scale of the justice of God, Most High." It proceeds by the `mashi'at al-mudabbir`, the will of the Director; by `mujib al-hikmah`, what wisdom requires; and by `mizan al-'adl`, the scale of the justice of God. The keeping of every poison and every blade for its appointed owner is, in its inner truth, an operation of divine will, divine wisdom, and divine justice held in perfect balance.

### The Two Eras and the Two Forms of Revenge: Sword-Ascent and Punishment-Descent

The text now distinguishes how the revenge of the slain opposites returns, according to the character of the Imam's era. `Li-anna al-maqama fi dhalika al-'asri in kana sahiba sayfin wa qatlin wa quwwatin wa ghalabatin kanat suwar al-maqtulin min al-azdad al-sa'idat ila-l-'uqdatain al-muqtassah min dhalika al-maqam al-sharif` — "because the station in that era, if he is the owner of the sword and killing and power and dominance, then the forms of the slain among the opposites ascend to the two knots, taking revenge from that noble station." If the Imam in a given era is `sahib sayf` — an owner of the sword, of killing, of power and dominance — then the forms of the opposites who were slain by his hand ascend to the two knots, and from there they take their revenge upon that very noble station that slew them. And the text adds `li-annaha tasiru mu'ajjalatan` — "because it proceeds swiftly": these ascended forms return with speed to the station of that cursed one, becoming devils, and attack — they assault that station with killing, taking revenge from those who had killed them.

But if the era is of a different character, the mode of revenge is correspondingly different. `Wa in kana dawr al-maqami fi dhalika al-'asri dawra awamirin wa nawahin wa iqamati hududin wa jaldin wa habsin wa nafyin wa ma yajri hadha-l-majra kanat suwar al-azdad al-jariyah 'alayhim dhalika 'inda mawtiha wa nuzuliha min dhalika al-barzakh sumuman kama sabaqa fi-l-qawl` — "and if the cycle of the station in that era is a cycle of commands and prohibitions, and the establishing of punishments, and flogging, and imprisonment, and exile, and what runs in this course, then the forms of the opposites upon whom these were executed, at the time of their death and their descent from that isthmus, are poisons, as has preceded in the discourse." If, that is, the Imam's era is one in which he is manifest, issuing `awamir`, commands, and `nawahi`, prohibitions; establishing `hudud`, the prescribed punishments; carrying out `jald`, flogging — `jald` means to whip — and imprisonment (`habs`) and exile (`nafy`) and whatever else runs in that course; then the forms of the opposites upon whom these penalties were executed descend, at their death, from that isthmus, and they descend as poisons, just as was described earlier. So the two eras yield two channels of return: the era of the sword sends the slain opposites' forms upward to the knots to come back as devils attacking, and the era of commands and punishments sends the executed opposites' forms downward from the isthmus as poisons.

This was the case, the discourse reminds us, when the Prophet and Sayyidna Ali, peace be upon him, faced opposition in their various battles — those slain there took their revenge in the manner described.

### The Purification of Retribution and the Promotion of Husayn's Gathering

Why must this entire economy of revenge unfold? The text supplies the reason in terms of the promotions that must be accomplished. The promotions, the `taraqqiyat`, of the gathering of Sayyidna Imam Husayn must take place. But these promotions cannot occur while wrongdoings remain unsettled. In that era there were `ziyadati`, transgressions — wrongs that issued even from the army that was Ali's, against those who opposed him; the killing of women happened, flogging happened, this and that took place. Now the revenge for these must be taken. The `azdad`, the opposites, take upon themselves their respective `jaza'`, their due recompense, for the transgressions that occurred, so that their `qisasat`, their retributions, are completed and their further `taraqqi`, their promotion, can proceed. This is a purification. The text marks it: `qisasat must be completed` so that the transgressions of the wronged opposites are settled and the onward promotion of the gathering of Imam Husayn becomes possible.

And whose `tadbir`, whose planning, is this — that these things should come swiftly to their conclusion? The planning is of the era, but it operates through the `jaww al-zulmani`, the dark atmosphere; the instruments are protected there, in the two knots, and their protection is carried out so that, like everything else, they are subject to the overarching plan. All these plans belong to God, Most High, and to the Imam of the time, that the retributions should be completed. The Imam is the `wasta`, the intermediary, through whom the giving and taking of retribution is brought to completion. This is the purpose of the whole arrangement.

### The Deliverer of the Poison and the Gathered Curses

The text then turns to the human agent who delivers the poison: `wa minha shakhsun yusallimu dhalika-s-summa ila dhalika al-maqam al-sharif 'alayhi afdal al-salam` — "and from them is a person who delivers that poison to that noble station, upon whom be the most excellent of peace." From among these accursed forms there comes a person who hands over the poison to the noble station. And this deliverer is himself, in his inner reality, a collection of curses — a gathering of the cursed form that assembles in the two knots. Just so was Ja'dah, the wife of Imam Hasan, peace and blessings be upon him: she was of this very kind, a gathered collection of curses who delivered the poison to her husband.

The text continues `wa qad yakunu min suwari ha'ula'i-l-mas'ulin minhum la'anatun min al-maqamat wa-l-hudud fa-yakunu aktharuhum fi zulmihim wa akadu li-'adawatihim wa shaytanatihim` — "and there may be from the forms of these accursed ones, curses from the stations and from the punishments, so that they are the most excessive in their oppression and the most confirmed in their enmity and their devilry." From among these forms there gather `la'anat`, curses — curses that issue from the noble stations and from the prescribed punishments. When these curses gather together with the conceptions of the opposites in the two knots, they produce people who are the most extreme in their oppression and the most firmly fixed in their enmity and their devilry, their `shaytanah`. The curse of the wronged station and the curse attached to the executed punishment do not vanish; they accumulate, and they take form in the worst of oppressors.

### Al-Hajjaj and Ibn Mahdi: Curses Made Manifest

Two great historical examples are given. The first is al-Hajjaj. Sayyidna Amir al-Mu'minin Ali, peace and blessings be upon him, had cursed the people of Iraq, the people of Kufa, who committed transgressions against him, abandoned him at his hour of need, and inflicted hardships upon him. He cursed them, foretelling that such a tyrant would be set over them as would visit upon them severe hardships and severe punishments. Those curses, gathered in the two knots, came together and manifested in al-Hajjaj — al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi, the greatest of tyrants. In him the gathered forms came together, and through him every manner of oppression was committed. The text reads `ka-l-Hajjaj alladhi zahara li-ahl al-'iraq` — "like al-Hajjaj who appeared to the people of Iraq" — and notes that he became manifest for the people of Iraq, and the discourse describes how it gathered `fi dhalika al-barzakh al-muzlim`, in that dark isthmus, which is the two knots, `ila an zaharat 'ala ahl al-'iraq`, until it manifested upon the people of Iraq, so that whatever issued from his actions among them came to pass. Al-Hajjaj, it is stressed, did not torment only the followers of Ali; rather, whoever came in his path he gave hardship to, or killed — his calamity was a destroyer for all of creation, `wa li-jami' al-khalq`, and for that reason `that great calamity is a destroyer for the whole world`.

The second example is Ibn Mahdi. `Wa ka-ibni Mahdi alladhi kana min la'anat al-hudud wa tasawwur al-azdad` — "and like Ibn Mahdi, who was from the curses of the punishments and the conception of the opposites." Ibn Mahdi too arose from the `la'anat al-hudud`, the curses attached to the prescribed punishments, and from the `tasawwur al-azdad`, the conception of the opposites. The text continues `tasawwur al-azdad li-ahl al-tihamah fa-jtama'at fi dhalika al-barzakh wa subbat 'ala ula'ika al-mudillat al-mala'in` — "the conception of the opposites for the people of Tihamah, so it gathered in that isthmus and was poured upon those misguided, cursed ones." The conception of the opposites was poured out upon the people of Tihamah; it gathered in that isthmus — in the two knots — and was poured upon those misguided and cursed ones. May Allah keep us in His protection from that, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny, the blessings of Allah be upon them all: `a'adhana Allahu min dhalika bi-haqqi Muhammadin wa alihi-t-tahirin salawatullahi 'alayhim ajma'in`.

So the principle stands clearly: from the conceptions of the opposites that gather in the two knots, and the curses pronounced upon them from the stations and the punishments, all descend together and produce the worst people the world has known — al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi and Ibn Mahdi being the named exemplars. When the curse upon those who oppressed Ali and al-Husayn takes form, it descends upon the people of that later era; al-Hajjaj is the proof.

### The Meaning of the Curse: Distancing from Mercy

A question of ruling arises: is the curse the opposite of prayer? The answer is given precisely. A `la'ana`, a curse, means to seek that God, Most High, distance the person from His mercy — `la'ana Allahu min rahmatihi`, may God curse him by removing him from His mercy. To curse is to seek the distancing of someone from divine `rahmah`, divine mercy. And from this follows a point of practice in supplication: in prayer, seeking `tarahhum`, the showing of mercy, for the opposites is not permissible — but cursing them is permissible. One may not pray that mercy be shown to the `azdad`; one may pray that they be distanced from mercy. As for who accepts the curse: when we supplicate, it is the Lord of the Time who accepts; and the meaning of the curse here is, again, `la'ana Allahu min rahmatihi`, the seeking of distance from God's mercy. Through this very economy the promotion of the `awliya`, the friends of God, is also accomplished, for the giving and taking between them and the opposites must be brought to completion. The opposites, it must be remembered, do not oppress only the believers; they let loose a flood of oppression upon the entire nation — which is precisely why Ali cursed those who had abandoned him, foretelling that one would be set over them who would inflict great oppressions, not upon the believers alone, but upon all who had forsaken him.

### The Death of the Opposites: The Soul Spreading into the Body

We now enter the Fifth Chapter, which concerns the sixth Provider — `al-Bab al-Khamis fi raziq al-sadis`, in five sections — and we take up its first section. `Wa amma ajsamu ha'ula'i-l-azdad wa nufusuhum fa-inna-n-nafsa al-wahid minhum 'inda mawtihi tashri'u fi jismihi wa yasirani shay'an wahidan wa yastahilani ila-t-turab` — "and as for the bodies of these opposites and their souls, the single soul from among them, at its death, spreads throughout its body, and the two become one thing, and they both transform into dust." For the opposites, at the moment of death, the one soul spreads out through its body. And here the contrast with the believer is decisive and must be held in mind: in the believer, the bodily parts become soul — the body is, as it were, raised into the condition of soul; whereas in the opposite, the soul becomes body. The process is exactly reversed. A question is raised — does the soul spread, growing smaller and then diffusing? The answer is that the soul spreads out and takes on a bodily form; the body does not grow, but the soul diffuses through it. And it spreads in such a way that the punishments of the grave are felt by it. As for the future of that soul: no one can destroy it, for it is a thing created by God; whether a soul be evil or good, none can bring its existence into non-existence. So the soul, wherever it goes, will be a cause of torment to itself until it has completed its retributions — after which it will be given an invitation, and it will accept that invitation, and its very nature will change.

The body and the soul become one thing, and the two transform into dust, into `turab`. Then `thumma yas'adani bukharan fa-yanzilani mataran` — "then the two ascend as vapor, then descend as rain." The body and soul, having become dust, ascend together as `bukhar`, vapor, and then descend as `matar`, rain.

### The Disasters of the Opposites: Lightning, Thunderbolt, Flood, and Hail

From this rain of the opposites a train of calamities is produced. `Wa yuhdithu min dhalika al-matari al-buruqat al-muhliqat wa-s-sawa'iq al-mutlifat wa-s-suyul al-mukhribat wa-l-barad al-mudirr` — "and there are produced from that rain the destructive lightnings, and the annihilating thunderbolts, and the ruinous floods, and the harmful hail." From the rain of the opposites come `buruqat`, lightnings that destroy; `sawa'iq`, thunderbolts that annihilate; `suyul`, floods that ruin; and `barad`, hail that harms. The text concludes `wa dhalika min fi'l al-muzaddat fi jami' al-awqat` — "and that is from the action of the opposites in all times." These disasters are the very action of the `muzaddat`, the opposites, and their work continues in all times. The calamities that occur in the world — severe cold and severe heat, the harmful hail, the rain, the floods, the thunderbolts, the thunder and lightning — all bear the imprint of the opposites' bodies and souls, for the conception that reaches the two knots is precisely body and soul, and these things issue from it.

### The Substance Becomes Vegetation and Animal: The Onset of the Nearer Torment

The text continues `wa yansabbani ila-l-ardi fa-yasirani nabatan wa hayawanan wa yastaghriqu bihim al-'azab al-adna` — "and the two are poured upon the earth, and they become vegetation and animal, and the nearer torment overtakes them." The vapors form and rise, then descend upon the whole earth, and there the substance of the opposites becomes `nabat`, vegetation, and `hayawan`, animal. Those who eat of this take their sustenance from it. And by this very means the `'azab al-adna`, the nearer torment, is unleashed upon them and overtakes them.

This nearer torment is then unfolded through a sequence of seven descending levels, the `adrak` — likened to seven dragons of the torment of God, from which Allah has kept us and His friends safe.

### The First Level: Al-Rijs, the Human Tunic

`Wa awwaluha dark al-rijs wa huwa qamis al-bashar` — "and the first of them is the level of al-rijs, and it is the tunic of the human being." The first level of torment is named `al-rijs`. (Our master, it is recalled, disliked the word `qamis`, "shirt"; if one said `qamis` he would correct it to `kurta`, the tunic.) This first level is the `qamis al-bashar`, the human tunic.

The mechanism is set out: `wa yasiru dhalika al-mughtadhi bihi nutfatan` — "and that one who took nourishment from it becomes a sperm-drop." The one who consumed the poisoned vegetation or animal becomes a `nutfah`. Then `wa yartaqi ila rahimin fa-yakhruju min batni ummihi janinan fi qamisin min qumus al-zanj wa-z-zabab wa-l-barbar wa-t-turk wa ghayrihim mina-lladhina la yasluhu mukhatabat ahl al-haqq wa la yanhajuna minhaj ar-rashad` — "and it ascends to a womb and emerges from its mother's belly as a fetus in a tunic from the tunics of the Zanj, the Zabab, the Barbar, the Turk, and others besides them, from among those for whom addressing the people of truth is not suitable, nor do they follow the path of guidance." The `nutfah` ascends to a womb and emerges as a fetus in a `qamis`, a tunic, drawn from peoples such as the Zanj, the Zabab, the Barbar, the Turk, and the like — peoples for whom, in this state, the addressing of the people of truth is unsuitable, and who do not walk the path of `rashad`, of right guidance.

And he does not remain in one tunic. `Wa la yazalu yantaqilu min qamisin ila qamisin ila an yastawfi fi kulli naw'in min hadhihi al-anwa'i sab'ina qamisan` — "and he continues to transfer from one tunic to another until he completes, in every type of these types, seventy tunics." He passes from tunic to tunic, completing seventy `qamis` in each `naw'`, each type. A point of arithmetic and of mercy is added here: he does not necessarily traverse the whole sequence of tunics. It is possible that after entering one tunic he returns from it; not every person must complete all of them. Only a very few, those of the most extreme stubbornness, complete all these tunics.

A question of conscience is raised, which the teacher answers carefully. It does not appear, to the eye, that so many whole nations are in torment — there are Turks and others, and in our day there is civilization and refinement among them. Has God, then, concealed this torment within the nations? The answer turns on a principle established earlier: the ascending and the descending are gathered in one and the same place. The ascending ones have arrived at a single platform together with the descending ones, just as was explained by the example of the animals — all are animals, but there is the lawful animal, which is ascending, and the unlawful animal, which is descending, and both stand upon one platform. In the same way, among these peoples there are the Zanj who are descending, and there are also those who, upon that very path, have turned back — for turning back is possible. The manifestations are not bound to a fixed millennium for these descending souls as they are in other reckonings; rather, in one level, across seventy types, he wears seventy tunics.

### The Second Level: Khums al-'Aks, the Human-Like Creatures

`Thumma kharaja mina-l-azdad wa-l-mumtazid ila-l-khums al-'aks wa huwa al-dark al-thani al-mumathil li-t-tarkib al-bashariyyah wa hum al-qird wa-d-dubb wa-n-nasnas wa-l-ghawl wa-l-'izar wa amthal dhalika` — "then he emerges from the opposites and the blended ones into the khums al-'aks, and it is the second level, similar to the human composition, and they are the ape, the bear, the nasnas, the ghoul, the lizard, and the likes of these." The second level is `khums al-'aks`, the quintessence of reversal, and it is `al-dark al-thani`. It resembles the human composition: where the first tunic was human, this second is human-like. Its creatures are the `qird`, the ape; the `dubb`, the bear; the `nasnas` — a creature said to dwell in the Arab lands, a kind of half-creature; the `ghawl`, the ghoul; the `'izar`, the lizard; and others of their kind, all bearing a likeness to the human form.

`Wa yuslaku bihi fi kulli naw'in min hadhihi al-anwa' sab'ina qamisan ila an yastawfi jami'aha` — "and he is made to pass, in every type of these types, through seventy tunics, until he completes them all." Through seventy `qamis` in each type he is made to pass until all are completed. And `wa huwa fi jami'i hadhihi al-qumus al-waqtiyyah yatahaqqaqu lahu wujud al-'azab wa annahu waqa'a fi dhalika mukhalafat arbab al-haqq` — "and he, in all these temporal tunics, has the existence of torment confirmed for him, and that he fell into that through opposition to the lords of truth." In all these `qumus waqtiyyah`, these temporal tunics, it is confirmed and made real to him that he is in a state of torment, and that he fell into it through `mukhalafah`, opposition, to the `arbab al-haqq`, the lords of truth. And `wa yasiru 'arifan bihim mutahaqqiqan lahum 'arifan li-ahl al-batil alladhina wasala bihim wa waqa'a fi adrak al-'azab` — "and he becomes one who knows them, confirmed for them, knowing the people of falsehood through whom he reached this and fell into the levels of torment." He becomes one who knows the lords of truth and one who knows the `ahl al-batil`, the people of falsehood, through following whom he fell into the levels of torment. It is made plain to him; he himself sees why he has come into this state.

### The Third Level: Khums al-'Ars, the Predators

`Thumma yuslaku bihi fi khums al-'ars wa huwa al-dark al-thalith wa huwa siba' al-barri wa-l-bahri ka-l-usud wa-n-numur wa-d-dhi'ab wa amthalu dhalika min as-siba' al-bahriyyah` — "then he is made to pass into khums al-'ars, and it is the third level, and it is the predators of the land and the sea, like lions and tigers and wolves, and the likes of these from the predators of the sea." The third level is `khums al-'ars`, the predators of land and sea: lions, `usud`; tigers, `numur`; wolves, `dhi'ab`; and their like among the predators of the sea. The text defines the class precisely: `wa 'ala-l-jumlah kullu dhi nabin wa mikhlabin barriyyin wa bahriyyin` — "and in sum, every creature possessing a canine tooth and a claw, of the land and of the sea." Every creature with a `nab`, a canine — the teeth that tear and rend — and a `mikhlab`, a claw, whether of land or of sea. (This is precisely why the unlawful animals are unlawful, for the unlawful animals are those that possess canines and claws, while the lawful animals do not.) `Ila an yastawfi fi kulli naw'in min hadhihi al-anwa' sab'ina qamisan` — through seventy tunics in each type until completed.

And in this level a new degree of awareness is granted. `Wa huwa 'alimun bima huwa fihi min al-'azab mutahaqqiqan lahu 'arifan li-ahl al-haqq wa ahl al-batil` — "and he is aware of the torment he is in, confirmed for him, knowing the people of truth and the people of falsehood." He feels the torment; he is conscious that this is the punishment of God. For seeing human beings while himself in the state of an animal, God has granted his mind enough power to understand that he is in this state for a definite reason — for his prior opposition. It is observed that this awareness was not mentioned as being granted in the first, human tunic; it is in these animal levels that the cognition of the cause becomes explicit.

### The Fourth Level: Khums al-Khars, the Crawling Creatures

`Thumma yuslaku bihi fi khums al-khars wa huwa al-dark al-rabi' wa huwa hawamm al-barri wa-l-bahri ka-l-afa'i wa-l-hayyat wa-l-'aqarib wa ghayriha mina-l-anwa' allati tushbihuhum fa-yatakhammasu fi kulli naw'in fi hadhihi al-anwa' sab'ina qamisan` — "then he is made to pass into khums al-khars, and it is the fourth level, and it is the crawling creatures of land and sea, like vipers and snakes and scorpions and others of the types that resemble them, so he takes on his form in every type of these types for seventy tunics." The fourth level is `khums al-khars`, named for muteness, `khars`, for in it he is mute and cannot speak. It is the `hawamm`, the crawling creatures of land and sea — the whole class that creeps upon the ground: vipers (`afa'i`), snakes (`hayyat`), scorpions (`'aqarib`), and others resembling them. Through seventy tunics in each type he is made to pass. The text continues `ila an yastawfi jami' dhalika wa huwa mutahaqqiqun bima huwa fihi min al-'azab 'arifan li-ahl al-haqq wa ahl al-batil 'aliman annahu sara ila ma sara min al-'iqab bi-mukhalafati arbab al-haqq wa ittiba' ahl al-batil` — "until he completes all of that, confirmed in the torment he is in, knowing the people of truth and the people of falsehood, knowing that he reached the punishment he reached through opposition to the lords of truth and following the people of falsehood." Here the muteness is to be understood with care: in this level there is dumbness in the sense that the specific articulate speech of the human is absent — there are indeed sounds, the hiss of the viper and such, screaming and crying out, but no human speech.

### The Fifth Level: Al-Najis, the Predatory Birds

`Thumma yuslaku bihi fi dark al-khums al-musamma bi-n-najis wa hum tayr al-barri wa-l-bahri 'ala shakilat zawat al-jawarih ila an yastawfi fi kulli naw'in minha sab'ina qamisan` — "then he is made to pass into the fifth level, named al-najis, and they are the birds of the land and the sea, in the manner of the birds of prey, until he completes seventy tunics in each type of them." The fifth level is `al-najis`, and it is the `tayr al-barri wa-l-bahri`, the birds of land and sea, after the pattern of the `zawat al-jawarih`, the predatory birds — those that wound and seize. Through seventy tunics in each type until completed. So the five levels run in order: the first is human; the second human-like; the third the tearing predators; the fourth the crawling creatures; and the fifth the flying birds of prey — one human level and four animal levels, making five.

The text affirms, as in the prior levels, `wa huwa fi jami' dhalika mutahaqqiqan li-l-'azab alladhi laqiyahu 'arifan bihi muta'alliman minhu 'arifan bi-ahl al-haqq wa ahl al-batil 'aliman annahu sara ila ma sara ilayhi mina-l-'iqab bi-mukhalafati arbab al-haqq wa ittiba' ahl al-batil` — "and he, in all of that, is confirmed in the torment which he meets, knowing it, taking pain from it, knowing the people of truth and the people of falsehood, knowing that he reached the punishment he reached through opposition to the lords of truth and following the people of falsehood." In every one of these states he feels the punishment, is pained by it, and knows both the people of truth and the people of falsehood, and knows that he has reached his punishment through his opposition to the masters of truth and his following of the people of falsehood.

### Knowledge as the Precondition of Punishment and Reward

A teaching of profound consequence is now stated. `Wa lawla ma'rifatuhu bi-dhalika la-ma kana li-l-'azab 'alamun wa la li-th-thawab rahatun` — "and were it not for his knowledge of that, there would be no pain in the punishment, nor any comfort in the reward." Were it not for the soul's `ma'rifah`, its knowledge of its condition, there would be no `'alam`, no pain, in the punishment, and no `rahah`, no comfort, in the reward. Knowledge is thus the very precondition of both `'azab` and `thawab`. He feels that the punishment he receives is on account of the specific sins and wrongdoings he committed; these are all levels of sensation, and without the knowing of one's state neither torment nor reward could register at all.

### The Salvation Path of the Lawful Animals

A clarifying question is raised: are the lawful animals separate from this whole descending economy? The answer is firm: yes, they are not in these levels at all; these levels of torment have no connection with the lawful animals. The level of the lawful animals is for ascending. When they ascend, they do not pass from one tunic into another in this manner; they go directly. Suppose a pigeon is slaughtered: it does not enter these tormented forms. Rather, its part goes upward — as much of it as becomes the human body, to that extent its `najat`, its salvation, is accomplished. To the degree that its part becomes the human body, that portion is saved; and the rest of the part, wherever it goes, has its share from that. The lawful animal does not return by this descending path; it returns by the lawful, ascending path. This is the foundation, too, of the ruling that follows in due course.

### The Sixth Level: Al-Naqs, and the Loss of Knowledge

`Fa-idha stawfa jami' hadhihi-l-khumus sulika bihi fi-l-dark al-sadis al-musamma bi-n-naqs` — "so when he has completed all of these five quintessences, he is made to pass into the sixth level, named al-naqs," deficiency. After the five `khums` levels are completed, the soul is made to pass into the sixth level, the `dark al-sadis`, named `al-naqs`. And the defining feature of this level is the loss of knowledge. `Wa lam yabqa ma'ahu 'ilmun bima huwa fihi li-annahu qad faraqa 'alam al-hiss` — "and there remains with him no knowledge of the state he is in, because he has departed from the world of sense." In this sixth level, the `naks`, no knowledge of his condition remains, because he has now left the `'alam al-hiss`, the world of sense; the sense has gone, and with it the awareness that accompanied the earlier levels.

This is given scriptural confirmation. `Wa li-dhalika qala Allahu ta'ala: wa minkum man yuraddu ila ardhal al-'umur likay la ya'lama min ba'di 'ilmin shay'an` — "and for that reason God, Most High, said: 'and among you is he who is returned to the most decrepit of age, so that he knows nothing after having known.'" (Qur'an 16:70.) Just as among human beings there is one who is turned back to `ardhal al-'umur`, the most abject and decrepit old age, so that after possessing knowledge he comes to know nothing — his mind failing by degrees — so too here. The text glosses: `fa-hadha misdaqu ta'miyati-l-amri 'alayhi wa salbihi ma kana ya'lamu` — "so this is the verification of the matter being obscured for him and the snatching away of what he used to know." This is the manifestation of `ta'miyah`, the matter being made blind and obscure to him, and of the stripping away of what he once knew. A question arises whether the remembering and forgetting belong to the body or to the soul; the answer comes with the description of the plant state that now follows.

### The Forbidden Plant: The Faculty of Growth Alone

`Fa-idha balagha hadha-l-hadda lam yabqa ma'ahu ghayru 'alat al-nama'` — "so when he reaches this limit, nothing remains with him except the faculty of growth." When the descending soul reaches this limit, nothing remains to it but the `'alat al-nama'`, the instrument or faculty of growth. He enters the plant state. In this condition, the soul is stripped of all things save the faculty of growth: the growing soul, the `nafs al-namiyah`, remains, but the sensing soul is taken away. The animal had known what to eat and what to refuse, for sense gives rise to knowledge; here that sensing soul is removed, and only the vegetative power of growth persists.

`Alladhi huwa-n-nabat al-mahzur al-qatil al-muhlik li-l-hayawan` — "which is the forbidden plant, the killer, the destroyer of the living being." This sixth-level form is `al-nabat al-mahzur`, the forbidden plant — a plant that is a killer and a destroyer of the `hayawan`, the living being, destructive to both animal and man. (The word is `hayawan`, the living being; in the lexicon a vowel stands upon both the `ha` and the `ya`.) And a principle governing both directions of the soul's journey is stated: while descending, souls descend from bad bodies; while ascending, they ascend from good bodies. It is the same vegetative state through which they pass in either direction — but in ascent it is from good bodies, and in descent from bad bodies.

### The Seventh Level: Al-Daks, the Wicked Stone

`Wa sulika bihi fi-l-dark al-sabi' alladhi yusamma al-daks wa huwa al-ma'din al-sanj wa-l-hajar al-wasikh al-khabith ila an yastawfi fi kulli naw'in min anwa'ihi sab'ina qamisan` — "and he is made to pass into the seventh level, which is named al-daks, and it is the sanj mineral and the filthy, wicked stone, until he completes seventy tunics in every type of its types." The seventh level is `al-daks`, the mineral state: the `ma'din al-sanj`, a hard mineral, and the `hajar al-wasikh al-khabith`, the dirty, filthy, wicked stone. Through seventy tunics in each of its types he is made to pass. An important exclusion is noted: the descending souls do not enter the good stones — such as the stones of a mosque or the stones of the Ka'bah; all of these are excluded. Those good stones are entered when ascending; it is in descending that the soul enters the bad and base things.

### The Arithmetic of the Seven Levels: Seventy Types, Seventy Tunics

`Wa fi jumlat al-qawl inna li-kulli darkin min hadha-l-adrak sab'una naw'an, sab'ina qamisan wa sab'ina naw'an` — "and in summary, for each level of these levels there are seventy types — seventy tunics and seventy types." The reckoning is set out plainly. For every `dark` among these `adrak`, the seven levels, there are seventy `naw'`, seventy types, and within each there are seventy `qamis`, seventy tunics. Seventy multiplied by seventy yields four thousand nine hundred — 4900 — manifestations per level. As the teacher works it through: in one level there are seventy types, and in each type seventy tunics; in every tunic there are seventy kinds. The `qamis` is reckoned as one type, a distinct garment, the passage from one animal to another, a gradation of stages and grades. Thus the level of the birds of land and sea, for instance, is one `dark`, and within it lie its seventy tunics, and the full multiplication gives the 4900 manifestations of that single level. May Allah, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny, keep us and all our brethren safe from that: `wa billahi ta'ala wa bi-jami'i ikhwanina min dhalika bi-haqqi Muhammadin wa alihi-t-tahirin salawatullahi 'alayhim ajma'in`.

### Two Paths to the Mineral State, and the Mercy of Early Return

A teaching about how souls arrive at the mineral state, and about the variability of the descent, is given. The fallen things reach the mineral condition by two paths. The first is that, while falling, their intentions were so utterly bad that they became stones directly. The second is that they first ascended and then fell back. And the descent is not uniform for all: many souls return after only one level, some after two, some after three; some return after all seven levels; and after the seven levels, some reach the punishment of the peripheries and return from there. Not everyone completes the full sequence — the variability is itself a mercy. The arithmetic is restated for clarity: seven times seven is forty-nine, hence the figure of 4900 when extended; in one level there are seventy types, and in each level the 4900 manifestations arise from seventy types each bearing seventy tunics.

### The Shari'ah Ruling: Why Certain Meats Are Forbidden

From this whole doctrine follows a ruling of the sacred law. In the `shari'ah`, the animals whose meat is forbidden are forbidden for precisely this reason: that they are in the descending levels, in the state of punishment. The ascending animals, by contrast, are permissible to eat. This is the inner ground of the dietary law: the forbidden creatures are those traversing the downward `dark`, while the lawful creatures are those upon the ascending path. We seek refuge with Allah, and for all our brethren, from these matters, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny.

### The Four Peripheries: Exile of the Most Stubborn Enemies

We now take up the second section of the Fifth Chapter, concerning the third pavilion — `al-fasl al-thani min al-bab al-khamis fi-s-suradiq al-thalith`. `Thumma innahu yughmadu ba'da dhalika bi-azdadi al-maqamat al-ilahiyyah wa-r-rusul wa-l-anbiya fa-tunfa ila arba' al-ard al-kharijah min hadd al-i'tidal allati hiya ghayr maskunatin li-anna biha ahwalan 'azimatan` — "then after that he is immersed with the opposites of the divine stations, and the messengers, and the prophets, and they are exiled to the four corners of the earth which lie outside the boundary of moderation, which are uninhabited, because in them are great horrors." After the completion of the seven levels, the most stubborn enemies, the `mutakallifin`, are immersed together with the opposites of the divine stations, the messengers, and the prophets. They are then exiled to the four corners of the earth — the four regions that lie outside `hadd al-i'tidal`, the boundary of moderation, where the climate is extreme. These regions are uninhabited, for in them dwell great and dire horrors, `ahwalan 'azimatan`. It is to be stressed that only the `mutakallifin`, the most burdened and stubborn enemies, reach this peripheral punishment; most souls simply return from the seventh level.

### The North: The Frozen Cold of Zamharir

`Ahaduha nahiyat al-shimal... wa huwa madhi'u-l-kawkab al-musamma Jady` — "one of them is the northern region... it is the place of the star named Jady." The first region is the north, the place of the star `Jady`. It is a land frozen, dark, shadowy, frightening, and terrifying, where the sun is absent for six months at a time — for six months the sun does not rise there. `Wa biha yunsabbu bard al-zamharir al-mujmid al-murhiq al-muharriq` — "and in it is poured the cold of the zamharir, the freezing, the exhausting, the burning." There is poured into it the intense cold of `zamharir` — a cold that freezes, that annihilates, and that, in its extremity, burns like fire. Here a definition is supplied: `zamharir` is a mist or wind of intense cold, while `asir` is the mist of intense heat, the sphere of fire. Zamharir is the mist of cold; asir is the mist of heat.

`Wa biha kawnu azdad maqamat 'alayhim al-salam fi hayawanatin ha'ilatin ka-s-sukhur al-jamidah la haraka fiha ghayra harakat al-ra'ash min shiddat al-bard wa-l-yubs` — "and in it is the existence of the opposites of the stations of the Imams, peace be upon them, in terrifying animals like frozen rocks, with no movement in them except the movement of shivering from the intensity of the cold and the dryness." In this northern region exist the opposites of the stations of the Imams, peace be upon them, in the form of terrifying animals resembling frozen mountains — rocks in the shape of animals, in which there is no movement save the trembling of shivering, from the severity of cold and dryness, resembling minerals. And they are punished by the cold of zamharir. A subtle point is made: although those who have passed into the mineral stage had no sensation, here sensation has been re-created in them, for without it the punishment could not be felt — and punishment they certainly undergo. `Wa hiya mu'azzabatun bi-bard al-zamharir muta'allimatun bi-bardihi al-lafi' li-ghayat al-'alam 'alimatun bima hiya fihi min alim al-'azab wa shadid al-'iqab` — "and it is punished by the cold of the zamharir, pained by its piercing cold to the utmost degree of pain, knowing what it is in of painful punishment and severe chastisement." They are pained by the piercing cold to the very limit of suffering, and they know what painful punishment and severe chastisement they endure.

### The South: The Burning Heat of Asir

`Thumma nahiyat al-junub wa hiya madar Suhayl` — "then the southern region, and it is the orbit of Suhayl," the star Canopus. The second region is the south, the orbit of the star Suhayl, where it is day for a full six months. `Wa biha yunsabbu hararat al-asir wa hiya al-har al-muharriqah wa sumum al-muhliqah` — "and in it is poured the heat of the asir, and it is the burning heat and the annihilating poison-winds." There is poured into it the heat of `asir`, which is the sphere of fire: a burning heat and `sumum`, poisonous winds, that annihilate. `Wa biha 'azab azdad al-rusul wa-l-anbiya fi hayawanatin kibarin shawihat al-khalq ka-amthal al-jibal la hawassun laha zahiratun bal tuhissu bima sihiya min 'azim al-'azab wa 'azim al-'iqab` — "and in it is the punishment of the opposites of the messengers and the prophets, in large animals of ugly creation like mountains, with no apparent senses, but rather they sense the great punishment and great chastisement they are in." Here is the punishment of the opposites of the messengers and the prophets, in the form of huge animals of hideous shape, as tall as mountains, which have no apparent outward senses, yet which nonetheless feel the great punishment and great chastisement they are in. So the distinction between the two regions is exact: the north holds the opposites of the stations of the Imams, and the south holds the opposites of the messengers and the prophets.

### The East: The Lofty Mountains and the Assistants of the Prophets' Opposites

`Thumma nahiyat al-sharq min al-ard min haythu tatlu' al-shams wa hiya jibalun shamikhatun wa'iratun 'aliyatun takadu tabulughu dhuraha ila kurrat al-asir` — "then the eastern region of the earth, from where the sun rises, and it has lofty, impassable, high mountains whose peaks almost reach the sphere of the asir." The third region is the east, the place of sunrise, possessed of lofty mountains — impassable and high — whose peaks almost reach the sphere of fire, so elevated are they. `Wa fiha 'azab a'wan azdad al-rusul wa-l-anbiya` — "and in it is the punishment of the assistants of the opposites of the messengers and the prophets." Here is the punishment of the `a'wan`, the assistants and helpers, of the opposites of the messengers and the prophets — together with their learned ones, who were rebellious against the messengers and prophets and spread enmity against them — in the form of terrifying animals, smaller than those of the south, stationed upon those mountains. They do not descend to the earth, nor do they turn from their place; as if shackled and chained, they are pained by the severity of the punishment and chastisement they endure, knowing what they suffer and from what offense.

### The West: The Sea of the Jinn and the Assistants of the Imams' Opposites

`Thumma nahiyat al-dubur wa hiya maghrib al-shams` — "then the western region, and it is the setting place of the sun." The fourth region is the west, the place of sunset. In it lies a vast sea — dark, terrifying, vast, and turbulent — called the Sea of the Jinn, whose waves are like mountains; this is the Pacific Ocean. `Wa fihi 'azab a'wan azdad al-maqamat 'alayhim al-salam wa 'ulama'ihim al-muzakhrifin li-n-nas al-qawl wa-l-balaghah wa qad 'adu hayawanatin mutashakkilatan qara fi qa'r dhalika al-bahr ya'kulu ba'duhum ba'dan wa yasulu ba'duhum 'ala ba'din ashadda al-'azab wa a'zama al-'iqab 'alimin bima hum fihi min dhalika al-'azab` — "and in it is the punishment of the assistants of the opposites of the stations of the Imams, peace be upon them, and their learned ones who embellish speech and rhetoric for the people; and they have returned as misshapen animals settled in the depths of that sea, devouring one another and assaulting one another with the most severe punishment and greatest chastisement, knowing the punishment they are in." Here is the punishment of the assistants of the opposites of the stations of the Imams, and of their learned ones who adorn speech and rhetoric to deceive the people. They have returned as misshapen sea-animals in the depths of that ocean, devouring one another and attacking one another with the severest punishment, knowing fully what torment they endure. The punishment of the assistants is, in this respect, lighter than that of the principals: the symmetry across the four regions is thus complete — north, the opposites of the stations of the Imams; south, the opposites of the messengers and prophets; east, the assistants of the opposites of the messengers and prophets; west, the assistants of the opposites of the stations of the Imams.

### The Rotation Through the Four Regions

`Wa kullama istawfa ahlu kulli nahiyatin min hadhihi al-nawahi al-arba' 'azaba tilka al-'idah ila nahiyatin min hadhihi al-nawahi al-arba' hatta yakulu 'azabuha fi jami' al-nawahi 'azanaAllahu wa jami'a ikhwanina min dhalika bi-haqqi Muhammadin wa alihi-t-tahirin` — "and whenever the people of each of these four regions complete the punishment of that period, they are returned to another of these four regions, until their punishment is fulfilled in all the regions. May Allah protect us and all our brethren from that, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny." The souls do not remain in one periphery alone. When the people of one region have completed the punishment of their term there, they are turned to another of the four regions, and so on, until the punishment has been undergone in all four — the soul rotated from the northern punishment to the southern, then to the eastern, then to the western — so that the `'azab al-atraf`, the punishment of the peripheries, is endured in every direction. May Allah keep us and all our brethren safe from that, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny.

### The Saying of Sayyidna al-Mu'ayyad: Slipping from the Luminous Center

This entire teaching, the text declares, is the very reality of a saying of Sayyidna al-Mu'ayyad. `Wa hadha haqiqatu qawli Sayyidina al-Mu'ayyad, 'ala Allahu qudsahu, fi-s-sab'in min majalisihi al-hikmati min al-mi'at al-ula haythu qala: wa man mada 'ala ghayri hadhihi al-nisbati zallat 'an al-markaz al-nurani qadamuhu wa inqata'at 'an wilayati awliya'illahi ta'ala 'ala qadr 'uluwwi martabatihi wa sumuwwi ismihi` — "and this is the reality of the saying of our master al-Mu'ayyad, may God sanctify his soul, in the seventieth of his Majalis al-Hikmah from the first hundred, where he said: 'and whoever proceeds upon other than this proportion, his foot has slipped from the luminous center, and his connection to the wilayah of the friends of God, Most High, is severed, in measure of the loftiness of his rank and the exaltation of his name.'" This is the reality of what Sayyidna al-Mu'ayyad set down in the seventieth of his Majalis al-Hikmah, from the first hundred: that whoever proceeds upon other than the right proportion, the `nisbah`, has his foot slip from `al-markaz al-nurani`, the luminous center, and is cut off from the `wilayah` of the friends of God — and the severance is measured according to the loftiness of his rank and the exaltation of his name.

`Fa-sara mun'akisan taratan fi muqasati harr al-asir wa taratan bi-bard al-zamharir wa taratan fi-l-ghutati fi qa'r al-buhur` — "and so he became inverted, at times in the afflictions of the heat of the asir, at times in the cold of the zamharir, and at times plunging into the depths of the seas." Such a one becomes `mun'akis`, inverted, turning at one time in the calamities of the heat of asir, at another in the cold of zamharir, and at another plunging into the depths of the seas — precisely the rotation through the peripheries that has been described.

### The Three-Branched Shadow: The Periphery of Three Dimensions

`Intalaqu ila zillin dhi thalathi shu'abin la zalilin wa la yughni min al-lahab` — "they proceed to a shadow with three branches, neither cooling nor availing against the flame." (Qur'an 77:30–31.) They proceed toward a shadow of three branches — a shade that gives no coolness and avails nothing against the flame of the fire. `Wa la yazaluna kadhalika fi hadha-l-'azab ila ayyam al-ba'th fi zillin dhi thalathi shu'ab` — "and they cease not to be thus in this punishment until the days of the resurrection, in a shadow with three branches." They remain in this very punishment, passing from one torment to the three-branched torment, until the days of resurrection. And the three branches are identified: they are the three dimensions — length, breadth, and depth — the threefold periphery already mentioned, the three valleys of the peripheral punishment.

### The Nearer Punishment, the Inhabited Quarter, and the Greater Punishment to Come

`Wa huwa al-'azab al-adna ila an ya'udu ila rub' al-maskun ila an taquma al-qiyamah 'ala dhikrihi al-salam` — "and it is the nearer punishment, until they return to the inhabited quarter, until the Resurrection is established, upon its mention be peace." All that has been described — the seven levels and the punishment of the four peripheries — is `al-'azab al-adna`, the nearer punishment. It endures for a time, until the souls are returned to `rub' al-maskun`, the inhabited quarter of the earth, where they remain until the Resurrection is established, upon whose mention be peace. `Thumma 'uidu ila-r-rub' al-maskun` — "then they are returned to the inhabited quarter." From the four peripheries — the uninhabited extremities — they are taken out and brought back into the inhabited portion of the world, the `rub' al-maskun`, the one-fourth part of the earth where human beings dwell.

`Thumma 'uidu ila-r-rub' al-maskun alladhi huwa lahum fi janbi dhalik jannatun wa jannatun bi-l-mizaj wa-l-muntazij ila an yazharu bi-'alamat al-alfiyyah wa yuhdaruna bayna yadayhi li-l-hisab fa-yustaqbaluna bi-l-'azab al-akbar` — "then they are returned to the inhabited quarter, which for them, in comparison to that, is a garden and a paradise, through the temperament and the mixture, until they appear with the signs of the millennium and are brought before Him for the accounting, and then they are met with the greater punishment." When they are returned to the inhabited quarter, that quarter is for them — in comparison with the four peripheries they have endured — like a garden and a paradise; and they come to it through `mizaj` and `muntazij`, through the temperament and the mixture, as the rain falls and the wind blows. There they remain until they manifest with the `'alamat al-alfiyyah`, the signs of the millennium, and are brought before Him for the `hisab`, the accounting — and then they are met with `al-'azab al-akbar`, the greater punishment. For everything that has been described until now has been the nearer punishment — the seven levels and the punishment of the four peripheries. Now, after the accounting, comes the turn of the greater punishment. May Allah protect us from them, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny: `'azanaAllahu minhum bi-haqqi Muhammadin wa alihi-t-tahirin`. By Allah we seek refuge from this matter.

## The Hudud, the Rock, and the Sacred House

### The Ascending Return of the Followers

The fifth chapter of the *Futuh* opens with a sentence about a particular class of souls — not the great chiefs of misguidance, but the ordinary followers who stand behind them. The text reads `fa amma'l-atba'u min al-umud wa'l-ra'aya` (as for the followers from the pillars and the subjects). The word `atba'` means simply "followers," those who accept and walk behind another, the people who attach themselves to a cause without themselves originating it. Among these followers are two ranks: the `umud`, the pillars who support a structure, and the `ra'aya`, the common subjects who live beneath it. The Master tells us what becomes of them on the long arc of their return: `fa minhum man ya'udu min ba'di'l-adrak wa minhum man yukmiluha` (and of them are those who return after the perception-levels, and of them are those who complete them). Some of these followers turn back after passing through the levels of perception, while others carry those perceptions through to completion. Their fate is not arbitrary. It is measured precisely: `wa 'ala qadri jiddiyati kulli wahidin minhum li ahli'l-haqq wa qadri a'malihi yakunu nayluhu min al-'adhab` (and according to the measure of each one's earnestness toward the people of truth, and according to the measure of his actions, is his attainment of punishment). The earnestness a follower showed toward the people of truth, and the deeds he performed, together fix the exact share of punishment he must receive. This is the doctrine that governs the entire passage that follows: the soul's journey is a ledger, and what it suffers or escapes is the precise reckoning of how it inclined toward the truth and how it acted.

When that reckoning has run its course and the soul has been emptied of its debt, the return begins to climb. The text describes this turning point: `fa idha istakbala man yustakbalu bihi minhum al-ruju'u sa'idan li yubtada'a bihi fi anwa'i'l-ma'adin al-nafi'ati li'l-bashar` (so when the ascending return is faced by those of them for whom it is faced, it is initiated in the kinds of minerals beneficial to mankind). The phrasing is careful: the return becomes possible only for those whose business of punishment is finished, those for whom it has now been written that they may climb. And the climb does not begin in some lofty station. It begins at the very bottom of the mineral world — but at the *good* bottom, in the kinds of minerals that are beneficial and lawful, the wholesome minerals rather than the harmful ones. From the minerals the ascent continues into the vegetable kingdom: `thumma fi anwa'i'l-nabat alladhi huwa ghayru mahzur` (then in the kinds of plants which are not forbidden). The ascending soul enters only those plants that are not prohibited, the lawful and good growth from which a person may eat without sin.

Here lies the great contrast that defines the whole architecture of return and descent. While *descending* — that is, while falling into punishment — the soul passes through bad bodies, foul and harmful plants and unclean forms. But while *ascending*, the path is paved entirely with good things: the beneficial mineral, the lawful plant, the permitted animal. The two journeys traverse the same kingdoms of nature, but the descending soul touches their corrupt forms and the ascending soul touches their wholesome forms. The way up is a good road, the inverse and the redemption of the bad bodies entered on the way down.

A necessary clarification belongs here, because the reader may wonder whether the ordinary law applies to a soul caught in this machinery of descent and punishment. It does not. Upon those who are falling and undergoing punishment, none of the rulings of the Shari'ah apply. The Shari'ah and its obligations bind only a person of intellect — a responsible, rational human being. They do not bind animals, and they do not bind a soul that is presently inside the period of `qisas`, the period of retribution in which an old debt is being paid out. Such a soul is not in a condition of moral agency where commands and prohibitions could be addressed to it; it is simply discharging a sentence. There is no fresh "grasp" or acquisition for it during this time, no new accumulation of merit or fault. It is in the period of `qisas`, retribution is being taken from it, and only when that punishment is complete will the return begin — `illa ma sha' Allah` (except what Allah wills). What appears, during punishment, to be a further wrong is not a new sin incurring a new penalty; it is the working-out of the old retribution itself.

The ascent then reaches the third kingdom: `thumma fi anwa'i'l-hayawan al-muhallalah` (then in the kinds of permitted animals). The soul now enters the lawful animals, and a precise mechanism is described: `thumma yasta'miluhu man yajibu lahu isti'maluhu min waliyyin wa ziddin fa yakunu lahu ma'baran hatta yakhruja fi'l-khama al-alfiyyah wa yu'radu 'alayhi'l-balaya` (then he is used by whoever must use him, whether a friend or an adversary, so that he becomes a passage for him until he emerges in the fifth millennium, and tribulations are presented to him). The lawful animal is used by whoever is obliged to use it — and this user may be a `wali`, a friend and ally of the truth, or a `zidd`, an adversary. The animal becomes a `ma'bar`, a passage or crossing-place, through which the soul travels until it emerges as a human being in the fifth millennium, the era in which the tribulations of the call will be set before it. The reason the user may be friend or adversary is itself instructive about the soul's standing: a soul brought into the lawful animals on account of some of its good actions, but whose punishments are not yet wholly complete, passes through an adversary; a soul whose punishments are complete passes through a friend. This is a natural process, an orderly grading of the soul's emergence according to how much of its debt remains. The animal is the crossing; the human form in the fifth millennium is the far bank.

And on that far bank, the soul is met by the call. `wa yu'radu 'alayhi'l-balaya` (and tribulations are presented to him): the tribulations are presented by none other than the Imam, peace be upon him, whose invitation is established throughout the universe. He presents his call in his various ways — a great and wondrous invitation. The soul is not lectured on the whole of its metaphysical origin; it does not need to be made to understand the entire doctrine of its beginning and end. It is simply summoned toward the truth, called by some appropriate method to accept what it once denied. This call is precisely for the returning soul, the one who is being given a fresh chance to incline toward the truth. The tribulation it faces is the presentation of the `wilayah` it had once rejected: `li man kana ankara min al-hudud` (for the one who had denied of the hudud) — the soul is now shown the very `wilayah`, the spiritual authority, of the `hadd` whom it had once denied.

What follows is the great fork in the road. `fa in qabila naja wa dakhala fi jumlati ahli'l-haqq` (and if he accepts, he is saved and enters into the company of the people of truth). If the soul accepts that authority, it finds salvation and is admitted into the company of the people of truth. The Master describes this acceptance with a luminous image: it is a clean slate. Once the soul accepts and the matter is fixed, it is as though water has been poured over a written tablet, washing it clean, so that whatever one wishes may now be written upon it. Every prior thing is finished and erased; faith is presented anew, and if the soul accepts, it arrives at its first state — the state of `kamal-e-awwal`, the first perfection, the unblemished original condition in which it first stood before the truth. This is the highest mercy of the whole system: that the returning soul is not merely paroled but restored, given back the spotless tablet of its beginning.

But the fork has another arm. `wa in aba wa'stakbara 'ada saqitan ka ma sabaqa min qawlihi fi asnafi'l-'adhab` (and if he refuses and grows arrogant, he returns falling, as has already been stated concerning the kinds of punishment). If the soul refuses and shows arrogance, it does not stay where it is — it turns back and *falls*, descending once again into the classes of punishment already described. And the Master fixes the duration of this whole alternating process of ascent and fall: `wa 'ala dhalika yakun munha ila qiyami'l-qa'im 'alayhi'l-salam` (and upon that will its course continue until the rising of the Qaim, peace be upon him). This cycle of offered salvation and arrogant refusal continues, soul after soul and turn after turn, until the rising of the Qaim. With his rising, the entire economy of return is brought to its consummation.

### The Universal Gathering Before the Qaim

When the rising of the Qaim draws near, the whole of creation is set in motion toward him. The text marks the moment: `fa idha ana zuhuruhu wa qiyamuhu zaharat bi zuhurihi jamee'u'l-khala'iq min haddi Adama 'alayhi's-salam ila 'ahdi dawrihi 'alayhi's-salam` (so when his appearance and his rising draw near, all of creation appears with his appearance, from the era of Adam, peace be upon him, to the era of his own cycle). The verb `ana` here carries the sense of drawing near, of a thing whose time has come. As the Qaim's appearance approaches, there manifests along with it the whole of creation — every being from the age of Adam, peace be upon him, down to the era of the Qaim's own cycle. No one is left behind. As the text insists: `fa la yabqa ahadun min jamee'i atwari'l-mustafa min muti'in wa 'asin illa zahara ba'da yasiruhu ila'l-ard thumma ila'l-mizaj wa'l-muzdajat thumma fi'l-aghdhiyati wa zuhuruhum nuqafan wa'taraqqihim fi'l-maratib ila an yuzhiru shakhsan 'ala halatihim al-ula al-mad'uwwah ila'l-haqq` (so no one remains from all the stages of the Chosen One, neither obedient nor sinner, except that he appears after his journey to the earth, then to temperament and compounding, then in the foods, and his appearance as seminal drops, and their ascent through the ranks, until he appears as a person upon his first invited state — invited to the truth).

This is the full sequence of every soul's reappearance, traced step by step. There is no one — not from any of the stages of the Chosen One, whether that soul was obedient or a sinner — who does not reappear. Each first journeys to the earth, then enters the realm of temperament (`mizaj`) and compounding (`muzdajat`), then passes into the foods, then appears as the seminal drops (`nutfah`), and so ascends through the ranks until at last it stands forth as a full human person. And the person it becomes is not arbitrary: it appears upon its "first invited state," the precise condition it was in when it was first called to the truth. The phrase `al-mad'uwwah ila'l-haqq` — the one invited to the truth — fixes the reference point. Every believer and non-believer, every soul without exception, will be summoned and made to reappear, and the form of that reappearance is the form it bore at the moment of its first invitation.

The Master makes the exactness of this restoration vivid: `wa hum 'ala silsilah halatihim min shaykhukhatin wa shababiyyatin wa sughurin wa kuburin wa sababin wa inkarin wa dhalika kulluhu 'ala halatihim al-ula allati du'iya fiha` (and they are upon the chain of their states — of old age, youth, smallness, bigness, acceptance, and denial — and all of that is upon their first state in which they were invited). Each soul is restored upon the very condition in which it was first invited: if it was an old man then, it returns as an old man; if a youth, a youth; if a child, a child; whether it had accepted or denied, in that same posture it returns. The text presses the point even into the matter of the body's surface: `la yutraku 'ala halatin saghiran wa la kabiran ... wa la fi shay'in min al-alwan allati hiya abyadu wa zurqatun wa sukhumatun wa sawadun, sawa'un bi sawa'in` (he is not left in a state, small or large ... nor in anything of the colors, whether white, or blue, or dusky-brown, or black — exactly equal to what it was). Not the size of the body, not even the color of the skin, is altered. White, blue, dusky, black — whatever the soul's complexion was at the moment of its first call, in exactly that complexion it stands forth again, equal measure for measure. The restoration is total and faithful to the smallest particular.

Once the souls have all reappeared, each in his original form, the hierarchy of the truth is itself made manifest among them. `thumma yashkhasu kullu nabiyyin wa wasiyyin wa imamin wa haddin li ahli 'asrihi` (then every prophet, successor, imam, and hadd is personified for the people of his era). Every prophet (`nabi`), every successor (`wasi`), every imam, and every `hadd` is made to stand forth in person — individualized and embodied — for the people of his own time, so that each rank of the truth confronts the people who lived under its dispensation. This personification is grounded in scripture. The Master cites the word of the All-Hearing, All-Knowing Master: `wa ji'a bi'l-nabiyyin wa'l-shuhada' wa qudiya baynahum bi'l-haqqi wa hum la yuzlamun` (and the prophets and the witnesses will be brought, and it will be judged between them with truth, and they will not be wronged). The prophets and the witnesses are brought forth, and judgment is rendered among all the gathered with perfect truth, and none is wronged. The crucial point of interpretation lies in the word `shuhada'`, "witnesses." This must not be read in its ordinary, literal sense of someone who merely testifies. Here every `maqam` — every station of the hierarchy — is appointed as a witness over the people of its own time, a witness to their deeds, whether good or evil. The `shahid` is therefore a `salih`, a righteous one of a higher rank, set over the people of an era to witness their actions. It is not a courtroom witness in the common meaning but a station of authority that observes and attests. And the judgment passed "between them" is not a dispute between the prophets and the witnesses — for they are present precisely *as* witnesses, not as litigants — but a judgment rendered, by means of their witnessing, among all those gathered: the prophets, the witnesses, and the whole assembled creation. Allah brings the prophets and witnesses and judges between the gathered souls with truth, and they are not wronged.

A second verse confirms the universality of the gathering. The Master cites it as a narration of their assembling: `qul inna'l-awwalina wa'l-akhirin la majmu'una ila miqati yawmin ma'lum` (Say: indeed the former and the latter will surely be gathered for the appointed time of a known day). The first and the last alike — the whole sweep of creation across all eras — will certainly be gathered at the appointed time, the `miqat`, of a known day. This is the scriptural seal upon the doctrine of total reappearance.

And the gathering is not passive. Each station of the hierarchy actively confronts the people of its time. `thumma inna kulla wahidin min maqamat wa'l-hudud yunafishu ahla 'asrihi'l-qa'im fihim wa'l-mab'uth ilayhim wa yuwafiquhum 'ala ma kana minhu wa minhum min takdhibihim lahu wa 'inadihim wa mu'adatatihim wa 'ala jami'i af'alihim wa aqwalihim qalilatiha wa kathiratiha` (then indeed each one of the stations and the hudud disputes with the people of his era — the Qaim being among them and the one sent to them — and confronts them concerning all that passed between him and them, of their belying him, their stubbornness, and their hostility, and concerning all their actions and sayings, the small and the great of them). Every `maqam` of the imams and every `hadd` enters into `munafashah` — a searching dispute, an admonition and contention — with the people of his age. He brings them to a standstill and makes them face everything that passed between them: the dealings, the persuasion that was offered and refused, their belying of the Imam, their denial, their hostility and enmity, and the whole record of their deeds and words, the trivial alongside the grave. Nothing is omitted from this confrontation; the small and the great of their actions are all laid before them.

### The Plain of Resurrection: Ma'rib and the Released Opposites

Where does this universal gathering physically occur? The Master is precise: `fa yakunu ijtima'uhum fi 'arasati'l-qiyamah wa huwa khatt al-istiwa' al-gharbi alladhi huwa ard al-ma'arib li anna qiyam al-qa'im 'alayhi'l-salam yakunu hunalik` (so their gathering is in the plains of resurrection, which is the western line of the equator, the land of Ma'rib, because the rising of the Qaim, peace be upon him, will be there). The gathering of all the souls takes place in the `'arasat al-qiyamah`, the open plains of the resurrection, and these plains are identified with the western line of the equator — the `khatt al-istiwa al-gharbi` — which is the land of Ma'rib. The reason for this location is that the rising of the Qaim, peace be upon him, will itself be there; the place of his rising is the place of the gathering.

But this raises an obvious difficulty: how could the entire population of all the ages possibly fit into a single region? The text answers by limiting who is gathered. `wa ya'tuna jami'u'l-khala'iq ilayhi min jami'i'l-jaza'ir yahra'un la yabqa minhum saghiratun wa la kabiratun illa yadiqu bihi mawdi'uhu alladhi huwa fihi wa watanuhu alladhi rubbiya fihi` (and all the creatures come to him from all the islands, rushing; none of them, small or great, remains except that his place in which he is becomes constricted for him, and his homeland in which he was raised). The creatures rush toward the Qaim from every island, hastening from all directions, until the place each soul occupies — the very homeland in which it was raised — becomes too narrow to hold the press of the gathering. Yet the Master is careful to clarify that this does *not* mean the literal whole of humanity, or even the whole of the Arab world, physically assembles at Ma'rib, for then the place would be impossibly constricted. Rather, only those creatures whose appointed time for `qiyamah` has come will gather there. And the term `khala'iq`, creatures, is broader than mankind: it includes the animals and all created beings, not humans alone. The ones for whom the dealings of resurrection are due — and only those — rush in from all the islands. The whole world does not come; the world cannot come; only those whose hour has struck are summoned.

The text continues: `wa yakhruju 'inda qiyamihi 'alayhi's-salam kullu wahidin min awla'ika'l-khala'iq mujifan ilayhi musri'an ladayhi ila an yuhdara bayna yadayhi` (and at his rising, peace be upon him, every one of those creatures emerges, hastening toward him, hurrying to him, until he is brought before him). At the rising of the Qaim, each of these designated creatures emerges and rushes toward him, speeding to his presence, until it stands before him.

This rushing-in of the gathered is then read into a passage of scripture concerning the release of Gog and Magog. The Master cites: `hatta idha futihat ya'juju wa ma'juju wa hum min kulli hadabin yansilun` (until, when Ya'juj and Ma'juj are released, and they descend from every elevation). He glosses `al-hadab` as `al-muttafa' min al-ard`, the high or raised ground of the earth, from which they come pouring down. The verse continues: `waqtaraba'l-wa'du'l-haqq fa idha hiya shakhisatun absaru'lladhina kafaru` (and the true promise has drawn near; then behold, the eyes of those who disbelieved will stare). As the true promise draws near and Ya'juj and Ma'juj are made manifest, the eyes of those who disbelieved stare fixedly in stunned amazement, gazing in wonder at what has come to pass and how it has come to pass. The Master then unfolds the inner meaning of the names. Ya'juj and Ma'juj are the `azdad`, the opposites — the antagonists of the truth. Their followers are the disbelievers. And the verse goes on to name their objects of worship: `wa ma'budahum alladhina 'abaduhu min dunillahi ru'asa'uhum al-muqimuna lahum maqama a'immati'l-haqq al-naziluna fi manazilihim` (and their objects of worship, those whom they worshipped besides Allah, are their chiefs who set themselves up for them in the station of the Imams of truth, descending into their ranks). The objects of their worship, worshipped in place of Allah, are the chiefs of the opposites — the leaders who arrogated to themselves the station of the true Imams, planting themselves in the very ranks that belonged to the Imams of truth, so that their followers followed them as though they were the rightful guides. Thus the eschatological imagery of Gog and Magog is decoded as the doctrine of the opposites, their disbelieving followers, and their usurping chiefs.

### The Striking of the Opposites and the Two Knots

With the creatures gathered and the hierarchy of the truth confronting them, the judgment turns to its execution. The text — opening the fourth chapter of the fifth section of the third *Surah* — sets the scene: `fa-idha-jtama'at al-khala'iqu bayna yaday al-Qa'im, salamullahi 'ala dhikrihi, wa-tashakhkhasa kullu wahidin min al-hudud wa-al-a'immah wa-al-awsiya' wa-al-nutaqa', salamullahi 'alayhim, li-ahli zamanihi wa-'asrihi` (so when the creatures are gathered before the Qaim — Allah's peace upon his mention — and every one of the hudud, the imams, the successors, and the speaking-prophets is individualized — Allah's peace upon them all — for the people of his time and his era). When all the creatures are assembled before the Qaim, and each rank of the hierarchy — every `hadd`, every imam, every `wasi`, every `natiq` — is made to stand forth in person for the people of his own age, the confrontation reaches its climax: `wa-waqafahum 'ala jami'i af'alihim wa-a'malihim wa-istaqarra kullu wahidin minhum bima sana'a umira bihi` (and he makes them stand before all their deeds and their actions, and each one of them is settled with what he did, for which he was commanded). Each station makes the people of its time aware of the entirety of their deeds, and every soul is fixed and settled in the judgment of what it had done.

Then comes the sentence of execution: `fa-duribat a'naquhum wa-ursilat 'alayhim narun fa-ahraqathum bi-ajma'ihim fa-istahalu fi al-waqt fa-saru bukharan` (so their necks are struck, and a fire is sent upon them that burns them all entirely, and they are transformed in that moment, and they become vapor). The necks of the opposites are struck — and the careful reader notices that the verb has no explicitly named subject in the immediate clause: whose necks? The pronoun must refer back to the opposites, the `azdad`, of the people of that time, for the preceding sentence had spoken of "the people of his time and his era," and it is their antagonists who are now struck down. Their necks are struck, all of them; a fire is sent upon them; the fire burns them entirely; in that very instant their state is utterly changed; and they are reduced to vapor. The Master pauses to register the strangeness of this annihilation — that a being should in a single moment be wholly undone and turned to vapor. These are matters, he notes, that had not entered our understanding before; yet in our own age we have seen the laser, the atom bomb, and other new inventions that can annihilate an entire region in a single stroke. The image of instantaneous total destruction, once unimaginable, has become conceivable in our time. So the opposites become vapor in a moment.

That vapor does not simply dissipate. It rises with purpose: `thumma yas'adu dhalik al-bukhar ila al-'uqdatayn li-annahuma yajdhibanahu li-al-munasabah al-khabithah allati baynahuma` (then that vapor ascends toward the two knots, because the two knots attract it, on account of the foul affinity that is between them). The vapor of the destroyed opposites ascends toward the `'uqdatayn`, the two knots, because the two knots draw it to themselves. The cause of this attraction is `al-munasabah al-khabithah` — a foul, evil affinity — that exists between the vapor and the two knots. Like is drawn to like; the corrupt vapor is pulled toward the corrupt knots by the sympathy of their shared foulness. This is a doctrine of spiritual magnetism: it is the wicked correspondence between them that does the drawing.

The two knots, however, are not the final destination. `fa-idha saru fi al-'uqdatayn wa-sara taht al-ard jadhabat al-sakhrah allati hiya asfal al-ard dhalik al-bukhar bi-al-munasabah allati baynaha wa-baynahu` (and when they enter the two knots — and the two knots are beneath the earth — the Sakhrah, which is in the lowest part of the earth, draws that vapor, by the affinity that is between it and the vapor). When the vapor has entered the two knots, and those knots lie beneath the earth, the next stage of attraction begins: the Sakhrah — the great Rock that lies in the lowest part of the earth — draws the vapor out of the two knots into itself. Again the mechanism is affinity: there is in the Sakhrah a correspondence to the foul vapor, and by that affinity it pulls all of it inward. Here the Master clarifies a point that recurs throughout this section: the phrase "beneath the earth" (`taht al-ard`) is not to be understood crudely. It does not mean a direction in physical space such as north; it means *opposite the Ka'bah* — the lower face of the earth relative to the Ka'bah, the southern side in the schematic sense, the other surface entirely.

The Master also marks a doctrinal boundary at this point: this whole account describes the `qiyamah` of the *dawr*, the resurrection of the cycle, and not the `qiyamah` of the `kashf`, the resurrection of the unveiling, which is greater still. In the `dawr al-kashf` the two knots themselves do not figure at all, for by then nothing of them remains — all the components of the two knots will have been drawn into the Sakhrah, and so there is nothing left of them to mention. The two knots have existed since the very time of creation, when Allah created them and set them in their place; but in the resurrection of the unveiling their effects are spent. Their satanic energies can no longer operate, for their "arising" has ended, and a thing that does not arise can do no work. So the present passage concerns the cyclical resurrection, in which the knots still serve as a way-station drawing the vapor down toward the Rock.

The vapor, drawn into the Sakhrah, penetrates it to the core: `fa-yatadakhalu fi azda'iha wa-masamiha ila an yasira dhalik al-bukhar fi wasatiha` (and it enters into its parts and its pores until that vapor reaches its center). The vapor permeates the parts and pores of the Sakhrah — into its body, its inner substance, its very pores — until it arrives at the center of the Rock.

### The Re-Forming in the Center of the Sakhrah

At the center, by divine will, a transformation occurs. `thumma yanfa'ilu bi-mashi'at al-mudabbir li-dhalika fa-yu'idu ashkhasan kullu shakhsin 'ala halatihi` (then a reaction takes place by the will of the Director for that purpose, and He restores individuals, each person upon his state). A `yanfa'il` — a reaction, an acting-upon — occurs in the center of the Sakhrah by the will of the `Mudabbir`, the divine Director who governs all things. By this reaction the vapor is re-formed into individual persons; each person is brought back, restored to his own form, exactly as he was. And the precise form to which each is restored is fixed by his moment of opposition: each is re-formed upon the state — the `qamat al-alfiyyah`, the millennial stature, the full human form he bore — in which he had set himself against the `Sahib al-Zaman`, the Master of the Age. There is no question here of the individuals being formed elsewhere and then carried in; they do not "emerge" from somewhere and arrive. They are *there*, in the center of the Sakhrah, and it is there that their individuality is reconstituted. In that very form they had worn in the world — the millennial form in which they opposed the Master of the Age — they take shape again in the heart of the Rock.

The Master now describes the structure of this Rock in remarkable physical detail. `wa-hadhihi al-sakhrah mithalu qidr al-sufr` (and this Sakhrah is like a pot of brass): `allati asfaluha salbun amlas` (whose bottom is hard and smooth), `wa-wasatuha mutajawwif ka-wasat al-qidr` (and whose middle is hollow, like the middle of a pot), `wa-jami'u hijarat dhalik al-wast kibritiyyah` (and all the stones of that middle are sulfuric). The Sakhrah is shaped like a brass cooking-pot. Its bottom is a hard, smooth surface; its middle is a hollow cavity, like the interior of a pot; and all the stones filling that middle cavity are `kibritiyyah`, sulfurous — a kind of sulfuric lava. The roof over this cavity is itself rock: `wa-alladhi huwa laha ka-al-saqf sakhratun saldah` (and that which serves it as a roof is a hard rock). The Master draws out the inverted geometry: it is as though the bottom of a pot were turned to the top and the top to the bottom — the floor is hard rock and the roof is hard rock, and between them lies the sulfuric lava. The whole, he says, mirrors the roundness of the earth, with this difference: that in the middle there is rock, the bottom is rock, and in the cavity between flows the molten sulfur.

The text emphasizes how sealed this structure is: `fa-hiya min jami'i jihatiha mulamlamah` (so it, from all its sides, is sealed solid). The word `mulamlamah` is glossed in the margin as `mushtami'ah`, compacted and gathered together; the Rock is `saldan malasan`, a hard smooth mass, closed off on every boundary. `wa-al-tajwifu fi wasatiha` (and the cavity is in its middle): the hollow, the `jawf` — its very belly — lies at the center. Yet it is not entirely without openings: `wa-innama fi asfaliha masamun wa-manafidh li-yasubb dhalik al-bukhar al-mutasa'id ilayha` (but in its lower part there are pores and vents, so that the ascending vapor may be poured into it). Only in the lower portion of the Sakhrah are there pores and channels, and these exist so that the vapor rising toward the Rock may be admitted and vented through them. In sum, as the Master puts it: `wa-'ala jumlat al-qawl fa-innaha ka-bayt al-hammam` (and to sum up the matter, it is like a bathhouse) — `al-harr` — a *hot* bathhouse, `mulamlam min jami'i jawanibiha`, sealed on every side. The Rock resembles a hot bathhouse closed on all its sides, with pores only below to draw in the heat and vapor.

This Rock is identified by scripture under a particular name: `wa-hiya al-musammat fi al-kitab al-karim bi-sijjin` (and it is the one named in the Noble Book as Sijjin). The Sakhrah is what the Noble Book calls Sijjin. And the wise — `wa-innama wada'at al-hukama' bayt al-hammam 'ala mithaliha` (and indeed the wise ones modeled the bathhouse upon its pattern) — fashioned the bathhouse, the `bayt al-hammam`, in imitation of the Sakhrah's very design: a sealed hot chamber heated from below, an earthly replica of the Rock. The Master interjects the prayer that punctuates this whole grim description: `na'udhu billahi minha` — we seek refuge in Allah from it.

What is the fuel of this furnace? `fa-al-waqid min tahtihi ka-hararat al-shams allati tukhmiha min tahtiha hazwan bi-hazwin` (so the fuel beneath it is like the heat of the sun, which heats it from below, motion for motion). The `waqid`, the fuel that heats the Sakhrah, is the heat of the sun. As the sun passes beneath the earth in its course, it heats the Rock from below — motion answering motion, the sun's passage timed to the Rock's heating. The Sakhrah's own sulfuric lava is already present within; the sun's heat reaching it from below intensifies that heat manifold, exactly as the bottom of a hot pot grows hotter when fire is set beneath it. So the sun is the fire under the cosmic cauldron.

### The Confession of the Punished and the Verdict of Scripture

When the re-formed individuals reach the center of the Rock, an act of recognition takes place. `wa-'inda masir hadhihi al-shukhus hunalik yu'ayinuna biha man qad taqaddamahum min amthalihim fa-yastahhiquna ma hum fihi min al-'iqab` (and upon the arrival of these individuals there, they observe those of their like who preceded them, and they recognize that they deserve the punishment they are in). Upon arriving at the center — these individuals who have been re-formed and brought there — they engage in `mu'ayanah`, direct observation: they see with their own eyes those like themselves who went before them into this torment, and from that sight they recognize that they have indeed earned the punishment in which they now find themselves. They perceive `wa-li-ayyi sababin kanu wa-li-ayyi halatin uridu dhalik al-butah` — for what cause they were placed in the Rock, and in what state they were made to enter that crucible. Here the Master notes another name for the Sakhrah: `al-butah`, the crucible or smelting-pot, the vessel in which metal is melted down — a name fitting for a place of fiery transformation.

And then the confession breaks from them, in the words of scripture: `fa-yaqulu ba'duhum li-ba'din: ya waylatana qad kunna fi ghaflatin min hadha bal kunna zalimin` (so some of them say to others: O woe to us, we were indeed heedless of this; rather, we were wrongdoers). Some cry out to others: woe to us, we were in heedlessness of this very thing — rather, we were wrongdoers. They acknowledge not merely that they were negligent but that they were positively unjust. The reply comes, again in the words of revelation: `fa-yuqalu lahum: innakum wa-ma ta'buduna min dunillah hasabu jahannam antum laha waridun` (then it is said to them: indeed you and what you worship besides Allah are the fuel of Hell; you will come to it). It is said to them that they, together with the false objects they worshipped besides Allah, are the very fuel of Hell, and that they will be brought to it. The "what you worship besides Allah" refers to the chiefs they took as objects of worship; both the worshippers and their false gods become the fuel — and the Master specifies which part of the Sakhrah is meant by "the fuel of Hell": the middle part, Sijjin itself, where the lava flows. They and the leaders they idolized alike feed that molten core.

Then the helpers of those false gods speak — or rather, the verdict is rendered upon the false gods through their followers' own realization: `fa-yaqulu a'wanuhum: law kana ha'ula'i alihatan ma waraduha, ay a'immata haqqin` (and their helpers say: had these been gods, they would not have entered it — that is, had they been true Imams). The helpers say: had these chiefs we followed truly been gods — had they truly been Imams of the truth — they would never have entered the Sakhrah. The very fact of their entry into the Rock is the proof of their falsehood; a true Imam of the truth could not be cast into Sijjin. The leaders' presence in the fire refutes the worship that was offered to them.

Now the Master draws a decisive scriptural and doctrinal distinction concerning the duration of this abiding. The verse declares: `wa-kullun fiha khalidun lahum fiha zafirun wa-hum fiha la yasma'un` (and all of them abide therein; for them therein is sighing, and they do not hear). All abide in the Rock; for them within it there is `zafir`, the heaving sigh — the labored breath, the gasping and crying out, the sound that escapes from one in torment — and they do not hear. The critical point is the word `khalidin`, "abiding." The Master observes with great care that for the disbelievers the Quran says `khalidina fiha` — abiding therein — but does *not* add the word `abadan`, "forever." He marshals the supporting verses: `ula'ika hum sharr al-bariyyah; innalladhina kafaru min ahl al-kitab wa-al-mushrikina fi nari jahannama khalidina fiha` — those are the worst of creatures; indeed those who disbelieved among the People of the Book and the polytheists are in the Fire of Hell, abiding therein — and here, decisively, the word `abadan` does *not* appear. It has been deliberately withheld. By contrast, of the righteous the Quran says: `innalladhina amanu wa-'amilu al-salihat ... ula'ika hum khayr al-bariyyah ... lahum jannatun tajri min tahtiha al-anhar khalidina fiha abadan` — indeed those who believed and did righteous deeds are the best of creatures; for them are gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein *forever* — and here the word `abadan` is present.

From this contrast the doctrine follows: the abiding in Hell is `khulud 'aridi`, a temporary or accidental abiding — an abiding "for a duration," not for eternity — whose very purpose is purification, the cleansing of the soul. The abiding in Paradise is `khulud abad`, an everlasting abiding, eternal without end. The Master is careful to base this not on a single verse but on the consistent withholding of `abadan` from the disbelievers and its consistent attachment to the righteous; the absence of the word for the one and its presence for the other carries the whole weight of the distinction. Hell, then, even at its most terrible, is a furnace of purgation with a term, while Paradise alone is endless. The `zafir`, the sighing of the damned, fills a measured time, not an eternity.

### The Nightly Heating and the Specific Targets of the Sakhrah

The Master returns to the mechanism of the torment, describing its nightly rhythm. `wa-'inda maghib al-shams wa-masiriha taht al-ard kullu laylatin tuhmi al-sakhrah fa-tu'hiju kama yuhmi asfal al-qidr min waqid al-nar az'afan muda'afatan` (and when the sun sets and its course passes beneath the earth, every night it heats the Sakhrah, and it blazes as the bottom of a pot is heated by fire-fuel, manifold multiplied). Each night, when the sun sets and its path runs beneath the earth, the sun heats the Sakhrah, and the Rock blazes up exactly as the bottom of a pot grows incandescent when fire is heaped beneath it — and the heat is multiplied manifold over. The Master clarifies the relation between sun and Rock: the punishments are conveyed through the sun's rays, while the Sakhrah's lava is already present within; when the sun's rays reach that lava, the heat is intensified many times. As a hot pot heated from below by fire grows hotter still, so the sun's heat compounds the Rock's own fire. He adds that this comparison to a pot is given only as an approximation — `wa-ma massalnaha hadha bi-dhalika taqriban` (and we have likened this to that only by way of approximation) — a figure to aid understanding, not an exact equivalence.

`wa-'indama yahmi asfal al-sakhrah ishta'alat tilk al-hijarah al-kibritiyyah ... al-kalsiyyah ... al-zujajiyyah naran` (and when the bottom of the Sakhrah grows hot, those stones ignite — the sulfuric, the calcareous, the glassy — into fire). When the lower part of the Rock is heated, the stones within it catch fire: the `kibritiyyah`, the sulfuric stones; the `kalsiyyah`, the calcareous or lime-stones; and the `zujajiyyah`, the glassy or crystalline stones — all of them blaze up into fire. And in this inferno: `wa-sara ula'ik al-mala'in yataqallabuna fi wastiha wa-hum yastaghithuna fa-la yughathun` (and those accursed ones tumble over in its midst, crying for help, but they are not helped). The accursed ones tumble and are turned over and over in the molten middle of the Rock — like boiling water that heaves up and down — there in the midst of Hell, the midst of Sijjin. They cry out for help, they call for rescue, but no help comes to them; their cries go unanswered.

The Master then specifies, with great precision, exactly which class of beings this Sakhrah-punishment is for. `fa-hadha al-qawl yakhtassu bi-azdad al-nutaqa' wa-al-awsiya' wa-al-a'immah wa-sa'ir hududihim, salamullahi 'alayhim ajma'in` (so this statement is specific to the opposites of the speaking-prophets, the successors, the imams, and the rest of their hudud — Allah's peace upon them all). This punishment in the Rock is not for everyone who erred; it is reserved for the `azdad`, the opposites — the antagonists of the `nutaqa'`, the `awsiya'`, the `a'immah`, and all their `hudud`. The Master here makes a vital observation about the *manner* of this punishment: it is suffered in a fully personal, bodily state. In these re-formed individuals the vegetative faculty, the sensory faculty, and the very forms are all present — even the foul forms that came down from above. Their entire being is present in the torment: their senses, their consciousness, the totality of what they are. Earlier, in the two knots, the components had been separated and dispersed; now the whole being is reconstituted and present, every part of them gathered into the suffering. The punishment is not abstract; it falls upon the complete person, body and faculty and form together.

The list of the condemned is then expanded to name precisely who counts among these antagonists. They are: `al-munasibin lahum`, those who set themselves in hostile opposition to the people of truth, the enemies and antagonists; `al-mudda'in manazilahum`, those who falsely claimed the ranks of the rightful guides for themselves; `wa-kibar atba'ihim al-mu'inin lahum 'ala zulmihim`, their great followers who aided them in their oppression; `al-muta'assibin ma'ahum`, the fanatics who stood bigotedly with them; `al-salikin maslakahum`, those who walked deliberately in their path; `al-muwaswisin li-du'afa' al-'uqul`, those who whispered doubts into the minds of the weak; and `al-mustahzi'in bi-ahl al-haqq`, the mockers who scorned and ridiculed the people of truth. These — the hostile claimants, the fanatical helpers, the doubt-whisperers, and the mockers — are the inhabitants of the Sakhrah's torment.

But there is a class that is explicitly *spared* this severest punishment. `fa-amma al-huhud wa-al-ru'a' al-tabi'un lahum fa-yu'adu bihim min akhiri naw'in min al-idrak` (as for the foolish rabble and the herd who followed them, they are turned back from the last kind of perception). The `huhud` and the `ru'a'` — the foolish masses, the mere herd who merely tagged along behind the opposites — are returned from the last level of perception. They are not cast into the punishment of the peripheries nor into the punishment of the Sakhrah. They are turned back from the second or third level of perception, before that final degree, and so escape the worst. The reason is precisely their lack of knowing intent: these followers acted without understanding what they did, without malicious purpose. Their ignorance, which in the chiefs would be no excuse, here works in their favor — it is better for such people to be without understanding, for they will be released quickly. Their intentions were not evil; they were merely caught in the crowd, following the herd, going along with the multitude. The Master sets the antagonists, who knew and chose, against these followers, who did not know — and the difference in knowledge is the difference in fate. As scripture had already established it, `kama sabaqa bihi al-qawl` — as the statement has already preceded. The Master adds a temporal note on the whole order of reckoning: the chiefs would appear and exercise their rule in the world, and the people of the world would take their worldly benefits and rewards; but when the Qaim appears, that is the time of reckoning, the time of taking account and of retribution. The accounts deferred through the worldly reign are settled at the rising.

### The Geography of the Rock: Pores, Vents, and the Ka'bah

A pressing question now arises about the pores of the Sakhrah: are they, like the pores spread over every part of a body, distributed everywhere, or are they confined to specific locations? The Master answers that the pores of the Sakhrah are located *beneath the Ka'bah*. Entry into the Rock occurs from below the Ka'bah; from there the souls go in. The governing principle is a rule of interpretation: whenever the word `ard`, earth, is used in this context, what is always meant is the Ka'bah. The Ka'bah is the one fixed reference of the "earth," and so the pores are taken from beneath it, and through those pores beneath the Ka'bah the souls enter.

The Master then unfolds the full cosmic geography. He first sets aside the merely scientific account: science says the earth is elliptical, round, and in motion, but its proofs are flawed, and the round shape is a metaphorical figure made for the sake of understanding. The true detail is this: the earth is a plane, and it is flat; and on its other side is the Sakhrah — that is, Sijjin, which is `Jahannam-e-Kubra`, the Great Hell. The center of the earth's surface is the Ka'bah, the House of Allah, `Baytullah`; and on the opposite surface of the earth is the Sakhrah, whose lowest part is the abode of the `Ahl al-Tabut`, the People of the Ark. So the earth is, in this doctrine, both `baydwi`, elliptical, and `musattah`, flat — a flat platform for human beings, with the Ka'bah at the center of one face and the Sakhrah/Sijjin at the center of the opposite face. The Sakhrah's lowest depth, the most wretched part, is the dwelling of the People of the Tabut.

The earth, the Master continues, has four corners, the `atraf al-zamin`, the extremities of the earth. It is upon these extremities that the `'adhab al-atraf`, the punishment of the peripheries, befalls the opponents — a separate punishment from that of the Sakhrah's core. These four corners are adjacent to the Sakhrah, and it is upon them that the Rock's vents, its `manafiz`, are located. At these extremities the cold is so severe that it is impossible for a bird to fly past — a frigidity beyond endurance marking the periphery of the world.

Besides these vents at the extremities, the Master notes, there are many vents in the `rub' al-maskun`, the inhabited quarter of the earth. The greatest of these is called `Durdur`, located in the sea between China and India. Toward this vent flow all the rivers, and it is said that from this opening various kinds of sounds emanate. Again the prayer: `na'udhu billahi minha` — we seek refuge in Allah from it. The Sakhrah, whose shape is like a cauldron, like a pot — its lowest part, the most wretched, is shown in the schematic image as the abode of the People of the Tabut.

The Master then describes the reversed day and night of the Rock's inhabitants. When the sun, in its revolution, comes before the earth, it is daytime for the people of the earth — and at that same moment, on the other side, for the `Ahl al-Sakhrah`, it is night. And when the bright sun rises upon the people of the Sakhrah, it is day for them and night for the people of the earth. But for the Ahl al-Sakhrah, neither day nor night brings relief; both are a calamity. In their day there is intense heat, and in their night there is intense cold, a cold beyond all measure. And when the sun's revolution comes upon them, the state of their punishment increases a hundredfold — the torment multiplied a hundred times over with the sun's turning. `Na'udhu billahi min dhalika` — we seek refuge in Allah from that.

Two further points are to be remembered concerning the earth and the Sakhrah. First, Allah created the Sakhrah *before* the earth. And in the Glorious Word of Allah, the Sakhrah is given a separate individuality: earth, Sakhrah, and sky are mentioned as three distinct individualities together in a single verse, three separate created realities named side by side. Second, around this earth and Sakhrah revolve nine `aflak`, celestial spheres, and seven `kawakib`, planets. The spheres — the `aflak` — have lines that are imaginary, and their revolution is upon a plane. The seven planets, the `kawakib al-saba'`, are circular, and their revolution is of two kinds at once: both around a center and upon their own axes.

The Master then conveys an important point that Sayyidna al-Malik states at this place. Beneath the surface of the earth the Sakhrah is spread out like a cauldron, like a pot — *but* where the Ka'bah stands, and Maqam Ibrahim, and Ghadir, and the places of the holy Rawda, there, on the other side, in that place, there is *no* Sakhrah. Rather, there is a state like a void, an empty space. And in this void is the abode of all the `arwah al-nuraniyyah`, the luminous souls, and the `arwah al-hasanah`, the good souls. Contrary to this, at `amyal`, at `wadi al-shaytan` (the valley of Satan), and at the `jamarat al-thalathah` (the three stoning-pillars), there is a great multitude of `arwah al-khabithah`, the filthy souls — `la'anahumullah`, may Allah curse them. So beneath the holiest places lies a void filled with pure and good souls, while beneath the places associated with the rejection of Satan and the casting of stones gather the foul souls.

To the humble questioner's inquiry about what lies *above* the Ka'bah and these holy places — in the upper part, the part opposite the spheres — Sayyidna al-Malik answered that there is air. And he noted that this matter is not found in the books. As for the pores of these places: their existence is affirmed, but their location must not be conceived as lying within the `aflak`, the physical spheres. Rather — and this is the authoritative clarification of Sayyidna al-Malik — the pores are free from physical limits, free from the `hudud` of materiality; they are all spiritual and imaginary, not physical at all. The map of vents and pores is not a map of matter but of spiritual reality represented imaginatively.

The Master returns to the dual nature of the earth, citing the date of the statement — 1773 — at which it was said that, along with being `baydwi`, the earth is also `musattah`. The maps have all been drawn as elliptical, `baydwi`, because a curved, elliptical projection has been made wherever maps are found; and the books say that the sun is the center of the world, toward which all things are oriented. But in reality the entire revolution happens around the *earth*. The Master reconciles the two statements drawn from the `rasa'il`, the epistles: in one respect the author says that the sun is the center of the entire cosmic system, and in another respect he says that the whole revolution is around the earth and that all the planets are turned toward the earth. The reconciliation lies in the purpose of `istikhraj`, extraction: the revolution is centered on the earth in the sense that the human leaven — human dispositions, the very substance of humanity — can be brought forth, "extracted," from nowhere else but the earth. Nowhere else in the cosmos has the human being been created; the progress and emergence of humanity is only from the earth. So from the perspective of extraction, all is oriented toward the earth; while from the perspective of illumination, it is from the sun and the moon that the earth gains its light. Both statements are true, each in its own place. If one were to travel into space, to the moon, and photograph the earth, it would outwardly appear round; yet it is `musattah` in the sense that its upper layer is round while the real earth — the platform for human beings — is flat. The real earth, in truth, is only the place of the Sakhrah and the Ka'bah and the holy places; the remaining earth spread around is the `kura al-ard`, the globe of earth, but it is not the land of `da'wat`, the land of the call. The land of `da'wat` is `musattah`, flat, platform-like.

The Master reiterates the point about the void: beneath the Sakhrah — that is, beneath the Ka'bah — there is no Sakhrah; and beneath all the other holy places likewise there is no Sakhrah, but rather a void. The Sakhrah is `asfal al-zamin`, the lowest of the earth, meaning the other side of the Ka'bah, the opposite face; and right beneath the Ka'bah itself there is no Sakhrah, but a void. As shown in the schematic picture, the Sakhrah lies on both sides, but in the middle, beneath the Ka'bah, is the void — the place where the fire of the Sakhrah is not lit. This void extends beneath not the Ka'bah alone but also Ghadir Khumm, the noble Rawda, and Maqam Ibrahim. And the progress of the `aflak`, too, proceeds from the `qamat al-alifiyyah`, the millennial form, of the earth.

This whole picture is sealed by a verse: `wa in minkum illa wariduha kana 'ala rabbika hatman maqdiyya` (and there is none of you except he will come to it; this is, upon your Lord, an inevitability decreed). There is no one among you who is not an arriver at it — none who does not "come to" the earth, the Sakhrah included. This arrival is upon your Lord an inevitability decreed, a fixed and binding decree. The Master explains: everyone must arrive in human form upon this earth, and it is only after coming into human form that progress occurs. Even when a soul is found in the Sakhrah, in `asfal`, the lowest, it will be in human form; when it goes into the depth it adopts a human form. Every soul, without exception, must pass through this human arrival on the earth — the Rock not excepted — and from that arrival its onward progress is measured.

### The Order of Creation and the Making of the Rock

The Master now turns back to explain the very origin of the Sakhrah and the order in which the cosmos was made. The priority of the Sakhrah's creation is grounded in its origin: from what was the Sakhrah made? It traces back to the management, the `tadbir`, and to the two rays that were first cast — and the Rock is the place of Iblis, a place for him to dwell. The `kibr`, the primal pride, had produced three classes of souls. The first class was the `nadimin`, the remorseful, those who felt regret. From the `nadimin` were made the `aflak`, the spheres. The second movement produced the `ummahat` — the "mothers," the four elements: fire, air, water, and the ball of earth. And those who were `musirrin`, the insistent, the ones who persisted obstinately without remorse — from them was made the Sakhrah. Then, when fifty-one thousand years had passed in the revolution of the spheres and the elements of fire, air, water, and earth, from that revolution the earth was created — the very earth upon which we perform the prayer. The earth, then, was created last, after the spheres and the elements.

The Master sets out the full sequence with care, working backward and forward through it. First, the `'alam al-'uqul`, the World of Intellects, was made — and it was made in the `'alam al-nurani`, the Luminous World. Then, among those who were delayed (`ta'khir`), the form of the `nafs`, the soul, was created. The tenth intellect, the `'ashir`, was called the `nafs al-mutma'innah`, the contented soul — as in the verse `ya ayyatuha al-nafsu al-mutma'innah`, "O soul at peace" (and the Master refers it to the Quranic address, 89:27). This is a soul which, having attained contentment, is now to enter the domain of the intellect: through its delay, from `'aql` came `nafs`, the form of soul; and when the final tenth became the contented soul, it ascends to take the place of the final intellect in the World of Lights, the `'alam al-anwar`. The delayed tenth, purified and contented, rises to occupy the station of the last intellect.

Then came the command `ihbitu` — descend! fall down! As soon as the command came, some souls immediately confessed, saying that they had erred through a slip; these few who repented at once — from them was made the `'alam al-hayat`, the World of Life. And seeing these, others who had gone astray were also gathered, and from the whole of them the World of Life was constituted. Those who were yet more delayed, in whom — on account of their greater delay — materiality (`maddiyyat`) began to enter, formed the next stage. Among these, what was made first was the `falak al-muhit`, the all-encompassing sphere. The `falak al-muhit` is imaginary, `wahmi`; its delay being greater than that of others, its materiality is only imaginary, and it represents the state of the `nafs`, the soul. It was commanded that the `falak al-muhit` should complete a revolution of the entire system of spheres. Then, after it, was made the `falak al-buruj`, the sphere of the constellations. Here, in the sphere of the constellations, true materiality first appears. The light (`nur`) found there is not like the light of the Luminous World; it is a light close to material light — the light of the `buruj`, the constellations. And within the `falak al-buruj` are the stars: the `thawabit`, the fixed stars.

The Master clarifies the technical distinction between two kinds of heavenly bodies. The `thawabit`, the fixed stars, are those that do not revolve around anything else; they revolve upon their own center, and their center is fixed. The `kawakib` or `sayyare`, the planets, are those that revolve around something else — around some larger star. This, he says, is established by research: the fixed stars are fixed upon their own center and revolve upon it, while the planets revolve around a greater star. This distinction recurs and is fundamental: `thawabit` revolve on a fixed center of their own; `sayyare` revolve around a larger body.

The Master continues the descent through the spheres and correlates each with its class of origin. The state of things is graded: in the Luminous World there are the `'uqul`, the intellects — not souls, for the souls have progressed and become intellect. In the `falak al-muhit` there are the `nufus`, the souls, and the contented soul advances in that station; the term `'uqul` is also used of them in one sense, but only in the sense that they have drawn near to intellect, having attained all purity and cleanliness. After the `falak al-muhit` comes the `falak al-buruj`, in which are the constellations, the fixed stars. After that come the spheres of the planets: the `falak al-zuhal`, the sphere of Saturn; the `falak al-mushtari`, the sphere of Jupiter; then the `falak al-marrikh`, the sphere of Mars; then the `falak al-shams`, the sphere of the Sun. These four are all of materiality, but they derive from the `nadimin` and the `tawabin`, the `ta'ibin`, the repentant — they are parts of those souls who adopted modesty and repented. They were delayed, yet they did repent. After these come the spheres of Venus (`falak al-zuhra`), Mercury (`falak al-'atarud`), and the Moon (`falak al-qamar`). These three, it is said, are all from the `nadimin` — all of the remorseful. The Master qualifies this: it is not said that they are one hundred percent of the `nadimin`, because some bad effects also remain in them. Among them there will also be `mutahayyirin`, the bewildered, when they rise from below. But in essence they are of the `nadimin`.

The Master dwells on this nuance with the case of Saturn (`Zuhal`). Saturn has bad effects, and yet in essence the souls associated with it are of the `nadimin` and `ta'ibin`, the remorseful and repentant. The point is that those who repented were accepted, but among the people who followed them some elements of stubbornness had accumulated — because some had gone astray by following them. Therefore, of the `nadimin` and `ta'ibin` who are in Saturn, it was commanded that the stubborn ones who attached themselves to them would remain *with* them, and that their purification would be the responsibility of these repentant souls. So the sphere is not bad in its essence; essentially it is of the repentant and remorseful, but badness is created in its *effects* on account of the residual stubbornness mixed in with it. Hence the effects of Venus, Mercury, and the Moon — though their souls are of the `nadimin`, the more delayed but repentant — can carry a residual badness.

Then, when the period of the bewildered (`mutahayyirin`) and of the `nadimin` and `ta'ibin` had passed, the next class was taken up: the `shayatin`, the devils, and the `mutahayyirin`. From these were made the elements — fire, air, water — and the earth. And how was the earth made? In the revolution of the world over fifty-one thousand years, as already explained, the existence of the earth came about; over those fifty-one thousand years the earth came into being. The earth, in reality, is earth resting *upon* the Sakhrah: the globe of `ard`, the earth, has the Sakhrah at its middle. The soil of our earth is, in reality, a part of the `ummahat`, the elemental mothers. The real earth, in truth, is only the earth and the Ka'bah; the rest of the soil spread around is the globe of `ard`, a part of the `ummahat`, and upon it is water.

And then, for the Sakhrah: it was made from the `musirrin` and the `mustakbirin`, the insistent and the arrogant. The most insistent and most arrogant of all became the station of Sijjin. And within Sijjin is the `Tabut`, the Ark — the station of the Tabut, the most arrogant of all. It is for this reason that the Prophet said: `Mu'awiyah min al-Tabut` — Mu'awiyah is from the Tabut. By this saying the Prophet placed Mu'awiyah in `asfal`, the very lowest station, among the most arrogant in the Ark of Sijjin. The Master notes that for other obstinate ones no such station is explicitly named; but for Mu'awiyah, as for Yazid, a severe statement was made, placing him in the lowest depth.

### The Great Cycle, the Four Vents, and the Renewed Choice

Returning to the fate of the opposites within the Rock, the Master fixes its immense duration. The text reads: `wa la yazal ha'ula'i al-ad'dad fi jawfi hadhihi al-sakhrah 'ala hadhihi al-halah ila intida' al-kawr al-a'zam alladhi huwa dharb thalath mi'ah alf sanah wa sittin alf sanah fi mithliha` (and these opposites remain in the belly of this Sakhrah in this state until the completion of the Great Cycle, which is three hundred and sixty thousand years multiplied by its like). The opposites remain in the belly of the Sakhrah, in this state of torment, until the completion of the `kawr al-a'zam`, the Great Cycle. This Great Cycle is the product of three hundred and sixty thousand years multiplied by three hundred and sixty thousand years. The text gives the total: `wa huwa mi'at alf alf wa tis'ah wa 'ishrun alf alf wa sitt mi'ah alf alf` — and it is one hundred and twenty-nine thousand six hundred million: that is, 129,600,000,000 years, an almost unimaginable span. The Master works through the magnitude of the figure to impress upon the reader its vastness — the product of the great cyclical number by itself, an abyss of time.

Within that immense cycle, the opposites do not rest even in their punishment. `wa hum fi zimn dhalika yal'anu ba'duhum ba'dan wa yaqtulu ba'duhum ba'dan wa baynahum al-munasabah wa al-muharabah qa'imah abadan sarmadan` (and within that, they curse one another, and kill one another, and between them enmity and warfare stand forever and everlastingly). Throughout the Great Cycle the opposites perpetually curse one another and kill one another; between them enmity and warfare are established forever, eternal and unceasing. And the death of one among them is not an escape but a worsening: `wa man qutila minhum istahala fi al-waqt wa infa'ala shakhsan mushawwahan 'ala aqbahi ma yakun` (and whoever among them is killed is transformed at that moment and reconstituted as a deformed person in the ugliest possible form). Whoever is killed among them is, in that instant, dissolved and re-formed into an uglier, more deformed person than before — the new form yet more hideous than the one it replaced, growing worse with each killing. The Master details the deformities, which mirror the very idols these souls had fashioned and worshipped: `fa minhum man yakunu lahu ra'san wa minhum man yakunu lahu thalath ru'us wa minhum man yakunu wajhuhu ila khalfih wa akharu wajhuhu ila yaminih` (so among them is one with two heads, and among them is one with three heads, and among them is one whose face is toward his back, and another whose face is toward his right). Some have two heads, some three heads; some have their faces turned to their backs, others to the right side, others to the left. These monstrous forms, the Master notes, are precisely the depictions of the gods these souls had made — their deformed idols become their own deformed bodies. `wa hum 'ala aswa'i halatin wa aqbahiha wa hum yataqallabuna fi wahaji tilka al-niran wa yata'ajjajuna` (and they are in the worst and ugliest of states, and they are turned over in the blaze of those fires, and they are set ablaze). They are in the most wretched and hideous condition, tumbled over and over in the flames and embers of Sijjin, burning and feeling the heat — `yata'ajjajun`, glossed as `tawaqqud`, kindling and igniting, so that they become like fuel itself, ceaselessly burning.

When the appointed measure is at last fulfilled — when the people of every cycle have completed this stated duration of the Great Cycle and fully discharged this severe punishment — the cycle of torment ends and the exit begins. `fa 'inda wafa'i ahli kulli dawr hadhihi al-muddah al-madhkurah wa istifa'ihim hadha al-'adhab al-shadid fi hadha al-kawr al-ba'id al-tawil al-ba'id, yaqtulu ba'duhum ba'dan wa yastahiluna wa ya'uduna bukharan wa yas'aduna wa yakhrujuna min a'la al-sakhrah ila zahir al-ard min arba' manafiz` (so upon the completion, for the people of every cycle, of this mentioned duration, and their fulfillment of this severe punishment in this distant, long, far cycle, they kill one another, and are transformed, and return as vapor, and ascend, and exit from the top of the Sakhrah to the surface of the earth from four vents). At the close of the long, distant Great Cycle, the opposites kill one another a final time, are transformed, and return again to vapor; this vapor ascends and exits from the top of the Sakhrah out to the surface of the earth through four vents, `arba' manafiz`, situated at the Rock's upper part.

The Master enumerates the four vents. `ahaduha bi'run fi wadin min awdiyat Hadramawt yuqalu laha Barhut` — the first is a well in one of the valleys of Hadramawt, called Barhut. `wa al-thaniyah bi'run fi Khurasan` — the second is a well in Khurasan, in Iran. `wa al-thalithah bi'run fi al-'arab` — the third is a well in Arabia. `wa al-rabi'ah fi bilad ukhra ghaba 'anni ismuha` — and the fourth is in another land whose name the author says is hidden from him, which he does not remember. The Master then gives the four vents by their directional names: `manafiz al-ard al-arba'` — the four vents of the earth — `wa hiya allati fi al-mashriq tusamma Barhut wa Shafahu` (and the one in the east is called Barhut, and Shafahu — both being its names); `wa allati fi al-maghrib tusamma Buqa wa Han wa Har` (and the one in the west is called Buqa, and Han, and Har); `wa allati fi al-shamal tusamma Saydum wa Jub wa Kharqa` (and the one in the north is called Saydum, and Jub, and Kharqa); `wa allati fi al-janub tusamma Sabr wa Sahiq` (and the one in the south is called Sabr and Sahiq). The Master is careful to distinguish these vents from the four extremities discussed earlier: the `atraf`, the extremities, are a place of *punishment* (the punishment of the peripheries), whereas these four `manafiz` of the Sakhrah are places of *exit*. The two are separate: the extremities are for torment, the vents are for emergence.

The Master pauses over the persistence of devotion even amid such doctrine, reflecting on the spectacle of misguided worship. He marvels at the difficulty of comprehending the devotion of those who fashion a god with their own hands and then worship it — for these are precisely the wretched souls who, in the Rock, curse one another and fight among themselves. He notes that even within the community such cursing has occurred — a Shafi'i would rise against a Hanbali and each would call the other `kafir`, unbeliever. Yet the case of those who carve and shape an idol with their own hands and then bow to it as their god is, he says, beyond human understanding — what could be more foolish than to make a thing oneself and then take it as one's lord? It is to the rejection of this very nature that the Prophet, that Islam, has called. (The Master clarifies that those presently engaged in such worship are still in *this* world; they have not yet gone toward the punishments — they remain in the world, their reckoning still ahead.)

The Master reaffirms the place where the deforming transformation occurs: `infa'ala shakhsan mushawwahan` — the soul is "made into," `munfa'il`, a deformed person — and this deforming, this worsening into uglier and uglier forms, takes place *within the Sakhrah itself*. After one Great Cycle, the souls come back out; they exit from the vents of the earth. The worsening into deformed states happens inside the Rock; the exit happens *as vapor*. The Master clarifies the manner of both motions: when the souls go into Sijjin, they go in the state of vapor; and when they exit, they likewise exit in the state of vapor. Both the entering and the leaving are vaporous. As the killing and transforming and return-to-vapor are described, so the exit through the four vents follows: they leave as vapor.

The text continues with the imagery of ascent: `wa shazza su'uduhum min hadhihi al-shu'ab al-jahannamiyyah ka-su'ud al-sahab wa al-dhabab al-tha'ir min butun al-awdiyah ila ru'us al-jibal` (and their ascent from these valleys of Hell is like the rising of a cloud and the swirling mist that rises from the bellies of the valleys to the peaks of the mountains). Their ascent from the infernal ravines is like a cloud rising, like dust and mist swirling up from the depths of the valleys to the mountain peaks — the vaporous souls billowing upward from the pit. Then begins the renewed ascending return, which exactly mirrors the upward path described at the very opening of this section: `fa idha sa'aru fi sath al-ard 'adu ila al-mizaj wa al-muntadhar thumma ila al-ma'adin al-mahmudah thumma ila al-nabat al-mahmud thumma ila al-hayawan al-muhallal ila an yadh-haru fi al-qamat al-alifiyyah ashkhadan wa yud'awna ila al-haqq wa yu'radu 'alayhim al-walayah wa al-dukhul fi da'wat ahl al-haqq` (so when they move upon the surface of the earth, they return to the temperament and the compounded, then to the praiseworthy minerals, then to the praiseworthy plants, then to the lawful animals, until they appear in the Millennial Form as individuals, and they are called to the truth, and the wilayah is presented to them, and entry into the call of the people of truth). Upon reaching the earth's surface, the souls re-enter the world through `mizaj wal-muntazij`, temperament and compounding — for there is an orderly process by which they pass into temperament and compounding — then through the praiseworthy minerals, then the praiseworthy plants, then the lawful animals, until at last they appear once more in the `qamat al-alifiyyah`, the millennial human form, as full individuals. And there, once again, they are called to the truth: the `walayah` is presented to them, and entry into the call of the people of truth is offered.

And so the great fork recurs exactly as before. `fa in qabilu najaw wa in ankaru wa takhallafu 'ada kullun minhum 'ala qadri munasabatih lahum` (so if they accept, they are saved; and if they deny and turn away, each of them returns according to the measure of his enmity toward them). If they accept, they attain salvation; if they deny and hang back, each returns according to the measure of his hostility. The Master stresses that this is a true clean slate, just as Sayyidna said: the souls come back as a clean slate, and now the choice given to them at the very beginning is given to them again — like the first perfection, `kamal-e-awwal`. They may now use that choice as they wish. The whole machinery of punishment, in the end, returns the soul to the same pristine freedom with which it began.

The Master answers a careful question about the *timing* of this renewed call: is the `da'wat` presented before the soul takes individual human form, or after? The call is given to them as individuals in human form. And those to whom the call comes are not all of one kind. On the platform of the world there are many who are people of the truth, and even among the people of the truth there are many degrees: some are quick to accept, some accept a little, some a great deal — they are not uniform. Then there are some who are bewildered in doubt, uncertain whether they will incline this way or that. And there is a third kind: those to whom the call is being presented for the very first time, whose inclination, this way or that, rests entirely upon their own choice. These re-emerging souls belong to this kind of free choice. So when the call is given, not everyone who receives it responds alike. Some have, as it were, already accepted the call and need only a push, coming at once — as it is said of Salman, `wa law kana fi Thurayya`, that even were he in the Pleiades, he would come running from there to accept the call. As a soul's prior leaven is, so will be its acceptance of the call. And there are some who are `musirr` and `mustakbir`, insistent and arrogant, now in a state of falling, who will never accept the call. All these kinds of people, the Master notes, are mentioned in the Pure Word: there are those upon whom the call will be presented and who will have the choice to accept it or not.

### The Indestructible Three and the Cycle of the Qaim

A profound clarification follows, prompted by the question of whether the soul's original "leaven" — its primordial intention — ever truly changes within the Sakhrah. In the Sakhrah, the Master explains, the original leavens are not changed; the souls have come into the world, but in the Rock their initial intentions are not altered. What they suffer there is the *punishment* corresponding to those intentions; their entire existence, with all its forms and faculties, has reached them, and they undergo the penalty. The "leaven" itself — that primordial disposition — does it never change? Here the Master sets out one of the deepest doctrines of the whole section. There are three things created by Allah that can *never* be destroyed, but only temporarily hidden. The first is `wujud`, existence itself — the existence Allah has bestowed cannot be annihilated. The second is knowledge and intellect — the powers that reside within that existence. And the third is `ikhtiyar`, free choice, freedom. These three — existence, knowledge/intellect, and free choice — that Allah has given can never at any time be changed or destroyed; for a span they may become hidden, that can happen, but they cannot be obliterated.

And the primordial intentions themselves belong to this realm of choice. The intentions a soul made in the `'alam al-azal`, the eternal pre-existence, are matters of `ikhtiyar`, of choice — intention *is* a choice. Therefore those primordial intentions are never destroyed; like existence and knowledge, they may be hidden for a time but they endure. While the souls are undergoing punishment in the Rock, it is not the time for the exercise of choice; but when they exit, freedom is again granted to them — now come toward the right path; you have the choice. If they come, well and good; if not, they return into the Rock once more.

The Master then traces the relation between actions and the primordial intention. The punishments are given according to the soul's actions — and the basis of those actions is the leaven, the intention, made in the `'alam al-azal`. When a soul exits the Sakhrah, that primordial thing has not changed in its essence; rather, its *purification* has taken place. The intention of the eternal pre-existence bore its fruit in the soul's arrangement across the various places and stations of the world. After coming into the world, the soul passed through many cycles, and in each cycle, by its own choice, it performed deeds — oppressions and good works alike. According to those deeds it receives its requital in the next cycle, and the next, and the next, until the manifestation (`zuhur`) of the Qaim, peace be upon him. The full reckoning — the complete punishment and reward for all the deeds heaped up across the cycles — is rendered at this time, at the `qiyamah`, at the `qiyamat al-kubra`, the Great Resurrection, and it is then that the soul went into the Sakhrah.

This raises the question of which resurrection is meant — the cyclical or the universal. The Master distinguishes them sharply. The resurrection here described is the `qiyamah` of the *cycle* (the `dawr`); this is the real resurrection. There is also a cyclical resurrection of a lesser order, but that is a *partial* resurrection. The true, universal resurrection — the `qiyamat-e-kubra` — is the resurrection of the cycle of cycles, signified by the verse `yawm tubaddalu al-ardu ghayra al-ard` (the Day the earth will be changed to another earth — Quran 14:48). On that Day the earth will be changed into another earth; the sky and the earth will be destroyed and a new sky and earth created. This is the true `qiyamat-e-kubra`, distinct from the partial, cyclical resurrection.

The Master then situates our own moment within the vast scheme of cycles. The present `qaim al-muntadhar`, the Awaited Qaim, is cyclical: he is the *first* cycle of the four-hundredth cycle. Three hundred and ninety-nine cyclical resurrections (`qiyamahs`) have already passed; we are at the beginning of the four-hundredth cycle, in its first cycle. The real resurrection is the resurrection of the cycle, and 399 such cyclical resurrections have gone before. Within the present cycle, the Master adds, we have passed seven `kash` and six `satr`, and stand now in the seventh cycle of `satr` — six cycles of `satr` having passed, we are in the seventh.

From this follows a striking claim about the age of the earth. A "cycle" (`dawr`) is four lakh — four hundred thousand — years. At the beginning of this present cycle the first `jussa`, the first manifestation, appeared at Sarandip (Ceylon). The reason a `jussa` manifests at the beginning of a cycle is that before it, the universe is destroyed; and the manifestation of the new `jussa` comes not from the human race but from the earth itself. Because at the end of each cycle everything is destroyed and the earth begins anew, the earth cannot be older than four lakh years. We are in the final part of the first cycle of the four-hundredth great cycle, and this earth is, on this reckoning, only four lakh years old. The Master notes the contrast with the findings of research, which speak of things crores or billions of years old; he allows that, from the standpoint of the things of the *sky*, such great ages may be understandable, but the things of the *earth* — life, animal life, human life, and the effects of the dwellers of that life — cannot be older than four lakh years. The old minerals may remain, for matter cannot be annihilated; when the earth was dissolved at the cycle's end, its substance mixed with air and flew off, but it did not cease to exist. Yet animate life and its traces upon the earth are confined within four lakh years; the earth is new, and so the dwellers of its life cannot exceed that span.

### The Jussas: The Ka'bah, the Holy Places, and the Mawri Succession

The Master closes with the doctrine of the `jussas`, the manifestations, and their hierarchy. The four-hundredth `jussa`, the one now to manifest, will appear in the `Khana-e Ka'bah`, the House of the Ka'bah — and in this there is a great wisdom, for the four-hundredth `jussa` will gather, will be the collector of, the three hundred and ninety-nine cycles that have passed. The first `jussa` of this great cycle manifested at Sarandip; 399 cycles have since passed; and so the `jussa` now to appear is the `jami'-e-arba'`, the gatherer of the four hundred. Its greatness is such that its manifestation will be in the House of the Ka'bah. The Master explains the grading: in the four hundred cycles that have passed, there are forty `jussas` that each manifested over ten cycles — cyclical manifestations whose greatness exceeds the ordinary because each spans ten cycles. But greater still is the `jussa` that manifests at the beginning of a `mawr`, the `mawri jussa`: no other `jussa` in the entire `mawr` can reach it. Across an entire `mawr`, four lakh `jussas` will manifest, and among them the greatest is the first, the one that manifested at the beginning of the `mawr`. The `mawri jussa` holds a very great wisdom.

And greater than all is the one that manifested at the very beginning of the *first* `mawr` — the `jussa-e-ibtida'iyyah`, the initial manifestation at the world's beginning. His greatness is such that none can reach him; and if any is to equal him, it will be only the final `jussa` at the world's very end, whose `zuhur` comes when the world is about to end. None other can attain his rank.

The cyclical `jussas`, by contrast, have manifested at various holy places, and these are precisely the places of `ziyarat`, of visitation. Some manifested at the station of Ghadir, some at the station of the Rawda, some at Najaf, some at Karbala, some at Maqam Ibrahim, some at the Hijr Aswad, some at Bayt al-Muqaddas. The Master mentions Ghadir Khumm, the noble Rawda, Bayt al-Muqaddas, and the stations of visitation in Karbala, Najaf, and Sham — these are their manifestations, and all the `jussas` that have manifested before are revered, and we visit them. But Sarandip is *not* counted among the holy places, even though the manifestation of Janab Adam is mentioned from there — because it belongs to the present time, and so it is not numbered among the holy places. The previous `jussas` that manifested are holy places; Sarandip is not. The reason is that in those earlier eras the manifestations were mostly of Arab manifestation. The Master recalls, in this connection, the victory of Janab Ali in the Masjid of Kufa, in its `mihrab` — the `mihrab` of Kufa, where, he says, the worshippers tremble to pray, so awesome is the place.

Finally, the Master sets out the doctrine of `mawri` succession. The one who comes as the last `jussa` of the second `mawr` will gather his `mawr` — for every manifestation has its sphere — and where will he arrive? He will reach the station of the one of the first `mawr`. The first `mawr` is the beginning of the world, the first `jussa-e-ibtida'iyyah` of the first `mawr`. In the court — the `ufuq`, the horizon — of that first one, the last of the second `mawr` will establish his *second* seating: his first seating was his own, and this, with the first `mawr`, is his second. And gathered with him will be all the `jussas`: as each cyclical `jussa` comes, its seating is established there; and the `jussa` of the `mawr` hands over the two `jussas` gathered with it to the `sahib-e-mawr` of the next coming `mawr`. For who is the true successor of the one of the first `mawr`? Not the cyclical `jussa`, but the `mawri jussa` — the `jussa` of the next coming `mawr` is his true and complete successor.

So the one who will now manifest in the House of the Ka'bah in the coming four-hundredth cycle — whose `khalifah`, whose successor, is he in reality? He is the successor of the one above him, of the previous cycle, but only *partially*. The `sahib` of the initial `jussa` of the second `mawr` is the highest; and the four-hundredth `jussa` will become his successor, but partially, until the four lakh `jussas` are completed. When the four lakh `jussas` are over, the third `mawr` will come, and its initial `jussa` will be the true and complete successor of the second `mawr`. The present `sahib-e-mawr` is preparing for that next one — preparing a `mawri jussa` like himself. This is the abiding law: a `da'i` prepares a `da'i`; a `mad'u` prepares a `mad'u`. The pattern fixed for the succession of the manifestations is that each prepares its like for the one that comes after, so that the chain of guidance — from the first `mawr` to the last — is never broken, and the true successor of each universal cycle is the initial manifestation of the cycle that follows.

## The Creative Sphere and the Resurrection Cycle

### Returning to the Discourse of the Qa'im

We resume the discourse of the Qa'im from the point at which we had earlier set it down. This is the Fourth Suradiq of the book al-Anwar, which gathers five gates (abwab); the first of those gates gathers five chapters (fusul), and it is to the first of those chapters that we now return. The Arabic of the text opens by recalling its own thread: `Narjaʿu ilā siyāqihi al-qawl min ḥaythu intahā` — "We return to the flow of the discourse from where the discourse ended." The word *siyaqah* is worth pausing over, for it is built upon the root *saqa*, "to make something move" or "to drive forward." Just as in everyday speech one drives or makes a thing flow, so here the author is making the discourse itself flow onward from where it had stopped — and where it had stopped was within the state of the Qa'im, upon whom be remembrance and peace.

The text continues: `wa-waqaʿa al-ḥisāb wa-istaqbala bi-ahli al-ʿiqāb wa'l-ʿadhāb fa-ḍāʿafa al-thawāb ahli al-thawāb` — "and the reckoning occurs, and he confronts the people of punishment and torment, then the reward for the people of reward is multiplied." The phrasing uses the perfect tense (*ẓahara*, "he appeared"), yet because the conditional particle *idhā* governs it, the sense becomes that of the imperfect: "when he appears." This is a feature of the grammar that the reader should hold in mind throughout this passage — the events described are not past but are the contours of what is to come. When the Qa'im appears, then, the reckoning (*hisab*) takes place. The people of punishment — the *ahl al-ʿiqab*, those who are the opposites (*azdad*), the wicked who set themselves against the truth — are confronted with their torment and are made to reach *saqar*, the fire. But the reckoning is not only a settling of accounts against the wicked. For the people of reward, the *ahl al-thawab*, those who have done good deeds, the recompense is not merely paid but multiplied. Their reward is doubled and increased. The appearance of the Qa'im is thus a single event that resolves in two directions at once: the consummation of punishment for the opposites and the amplification of reward for the faithful.

### The Establishment of the Da'wah and the Ascent to the Tenth Intellect

From this point the text turns to the great mechanism by which the Qa'im and those gathered around him are promoted through the ranks of the intellects. The Arabic reads: `Fa-qāma al-daʿwah ilā an yastakhrija minhā man yakūnu maqāmuhu fī iqāmat al-daʿwah wa-takhallafa fī man fī ḍimnihi ilā rutbat al-ʿaql al-ʿāshir` — "So he established the da'wah so that he may extract from it one whose station will be in establishing the da'wah, and he will succeed, among those within his fold, to the rank of the Tenth Intellect, al-ʿaql al-ʿashir." And this Tenth Intellect is further described: `ʿaqlan tāmman kāmilan nūrāniyyan aḥad al-ʿuqūl al-ibdā'iyyah` — "a perfect, complete, luminous intellect, one of the Primordial Intellects."

The structure of the teaching is this. The Qa'im establishes the da'wah, and from that very da'wah he extracts a successor — one whose own station will lie in the continued establishment of the da'wah. By means of this extraction, the Qa'im, together with those within his fold, is transferred upward to the rank of the Tenth Intellect. To be raised to the rank of the Tenth Intellect is to enter the rank that stands above; and that rank holds ten levels (*maratib*), among which is found the Primordial Corporeality, the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. The point of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* is the most exalted of all, and within its court a seat is reserved.

This brings us to the principle that governs every promotion in this cosmology. No person can be promoted until his successor is born — but not just any successor will serve. The successor must be a universal one (*kulli*), not a partial one (*juzwi*). Promotion does not occur by means of a partial successor; it occurs only when a universal successor is in place. The text states the principle in its most compact form: `Lā yukhlifu aḥadun illā mithluhu` — "No one succeeds except one like him." No one can serve as a universal vicegerent (*khalifah kulli*) except a person like the one he succeeds.

Consider how this works with the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* itself. The Imam has risen; can the Imam simply go and become the deputy of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*? He cannot, for in relation to the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* he is partial. The Qa'im, upon whom be blessings and peace, is indeed a successor of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* — but a partial one. Who, then, can be its universal successor? Only one like the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. Within the cycle (*dawr*) of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* there are four great cycles (*qawrs*); each great cycle contains four hundred thousand smaller cycles (*dawrs*), and each smaller cycle is four hundred thousand years. However many individuals arise within all of that span, every one of them goes and abides in the horizon (*ufuq*) of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*, and every one of them is partial. The universal successor is a *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* like the first; when such a one appears, it goes and becomes the universal successor, and the prior *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* is thereby exalted.

The same principle is established at every level. There is an Imam; who is the Imam's vicegerent? The second Imam. The seventh Imam comes, and his rank is exalted; who can be the universal vicegerent of that seventh Imam? A seventh Imam just like him. The lesser Imams, the Imams of a single rank, cannot become the universal vicegerent of one above them; they may serve as vicegerents, but only in a partial manner. They cannot be universal.

To this principle the author appends a precision that he received from the chain of teaching. Sayyidna Sadiq would say to Sayyidna Jumali that wherever the word "partial" (*juzwi*) is used, the meaning intended is "universal" (*kulli*), and wherever "universal" is used, "partial" should be kept in mind. That is, the one term always carries the other alongside it; the partial is implied within the universal and the universal within the partial. So when the text speaks of "a perfect, complete, luminous intellect, one of the Primordial Intellects," the words "in the likeness of" hover over the phrase — it is in the likeness of such a perfect, complete, luminous intellect, like one of the *ʿuqul ibdā'iyyah*, that the promotion is conceived.

### The Perpetual Ascent of the Chain of Intellects

The text now describes the chain of ascents that follows from this single mechanism. `Wa-saʿada al-ʿaql al-ʿāshir ilā rutbat al-ʿaql al-tāsiʿ` — "And the Tenth Intellect ascended to the rank of the Ninth Intellect." Here it is being explained that the first *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* belongs to the world of spirits (*ʿalam al-arwah*) — it is the first that appeared. That first *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*, the most excellent, goes and takes the place of the Tenth Intellect; and the Tenth ascends toward the Ninth. We are not speaking here only of the final cycle but of the very first death (*mawt*) that ever occurred — the original event from which the whole pattern unfolds.

The Qa'im, now established in the rank of the Tenth Intellect, becomes something vast in scope. `Wa-ṣāra hādhā al-qā'im fī rutbat al-ʿaql al-ʿāshir mudabbir al-jamīʿ ʿālam al-ṭabīʿah jirmānihā wa-jismānihā` — "And this Qa'im came to be, in the rank of the Tenth Intellect, the administrator of the entire world of nature, both its celestial and its terrestrial aspects." This Qa'im, in the rank of the Tenth Intellect, becomes the administrator (*mudabbir*) over the whole world of nature (*ʿalam al-tabiʿah*), whether celestial (*jirmani*) or terrestrial (*jismani*).

And this administration is itself not static, for the ascent continues. `Ilā an yastakhliṣa qā'iman yaqūmu fī maqāmihi wa-intaqala huwa ilā rutbat al-ʿaql al-tāsiʿ` — "Until he appoints a Qa'im who will stand in his station, and he himself will be transferred to the rank of the Ninth Intellect." He finds and refines a pure Qa'im, a successor who will stand in his place; and once that successor is appointed, the one who had occupied the Tenth ascends to the Ninth. `Wa-ʿalā hādhā al-manhā intaqala dā'iman` — "And in this manner he will be transferred perpetually." This quality of transference never ceases; the ascent continues always.

`Fa-idhā intaqala al-qā'im ʿalayhi al-zikr al-salām bi-man fī ḍimnihi` — "So when the Qa'im, upon whom be remembrance and peace, is transferred together with those in his fold" — the chain ripples upward through every rank. The Ninth Intellect takes the place of the Eighth; the Eighth takes the place of the Seventh; the Seventh ascends to the Sixth; the Sixth to the Fifth's place; the Fifth to the Fourth's place; the Fourth to the Third's place; the Third to the Second's place. But here the ascent reaches its limit. The Second does not move on; it remains in its station. For the Second Intellect — who is the *Munba'ith al-awwal*, the First Emanation — possesses a circle that is specific and proper to itself. Above it lies the circle of the First Intellect (*ʿaql awwal*), which is the circle of unity (*wahdat*), and no one can reach there. Whatever light is found there is the light of the Second; the *Munba'ith al-awwal* is exalted in its second perfection (*kamal-e thani*), and that second perfection is immense and vast. But the circle of the First Intellect — the circle of *wahdat* — can be attained by no created being. This is the ceiling of the whole ascent.

When the Qa'im is transferred with those in his fold, the work of succession continues below him. `Wa-qāma waladuhu al-mustakhraj min daʿwatihi fa-qāma bi-amr al-daʿwat al-hādiyah` — "And his son, who was extracted from his da'wah, will rise, and he will undertake the affair of the guiding da'wah." The son whom the Qa'im had extracted from his da'wah now rises and takes up the affair of the guiding da'wah (*al-daʿwat al-hadiyah*). And this son, in turn, continues the same labor: `ilā an yastakhrija man yaqūmu maqāmahu wa-yūfī mā ʿalayhi min al-khidmah wa-intaqala ilā ufuq abīhi` — "until he extracts one who will stand in his station, and he fulfills the service that is upon him, and he is transferred to the horizon of his father." He extracts a successor of his own, discharges fully the service (*khidmah*) laid upon him, and is then transferred to the horizon (*ufuq*) of his father. `Wa-lā yazāl al-daʿwah qā'imatan jāriyatan` — "And the da'wah does not cease to be established and flowing." The da'wah remains forever established and flowing.

`Wa-kullu maqām yantaqilu ilā ufuq al-ʿāshir ʿalā al-ʿādat al-ma'lūfah ilā tamām al-muddah wa-inqiḍā' al-ʿiddah wa-hiya sabʿat ālāf sinīn` — "And every station is transferred to the horizon of the Tenth, upon the customary habit, until the completion of the period and the end of the term, which is seven thousand years." Every station is transferred to the horizon of the Tenth Intellect according to the ancient and appointed custom (*al-ʿadat al-maʿlufah*, the *ʿadat qadimah*), and this proceeds until the appointed period is completed and the term comes to its end — a term that is seven thousand years. Then: `Wa-qāma qā'imun fa-yaṣīru ʿindahu jamīʿ hādhihi al-maqāmāt al-ṣā'irah fī ufuq al-ʿāshir` — "And a Qa'im will rise, and with him will be all these stations that have come to be in the horizon of the Tenth." A Qa'im rises, and gathered with him are all the stations that have been arriving in the horizon of the Tenth Intellect.

### The Cycles of Unveiling and Concealment: Seven Thousand and Ten Thousand Years

A clarification is in order regarding these spans of years, for the figures differ depending on which cycle is in view. The figure of seven thousand years belongs to the cycle of concealment, the *dawr-e satr*. In the cycle of unveiling, the *dawr-e kashf*, the figure is ten thousand years. The reader must attend to which cycle is named in each passage.

Let us trace the *dawr-e kashf* carefully. The *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* manifests; after it, the son comes; the son carries out the da'wah; the son passes away. Where does he then arrive? In the horizon of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* — and there, within its court, a point is established and one of his seats is fixed. So it continues until ten thousand years pass in the *dawr-e kashf*. (In the *dawr-e satr* the corresponding span is seven thousand years.) Through the ten thousand years of the *dawr-e kashf*, each Imam and each Qa'im, upon completing his term, goes and obtains his seat. When the ten thousand years are over, a Qa'im of those ten thousand years manifests. What does that Qa'im do? When he reaches the horizon, the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* places under his authority the forms of all the Imams of those ten thousand years. Then a second ten thousand years comes, and a third, and the same occurs in each.

Thus the first Qa'im of a ten-thousand-year span is, in effect, preparing his future successor — the Qa'im of the next ten-thousand-year span — to become his vicegerent; and from that future Qa'im he obtains all the forms, which lie in the horizon of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. And the Qa'im who manifests after fifty thousand years does something greater still: he gathers not merely the Imams but the *qawa'im* — all five of the Qa'ims — and he becomes a universal Qa'im (*qā'im kulli*). This belongs to the fifty-thousand-year reckoning of the *dawr-e kashf*.

After this, the *dawr-e satr* manifests. The Qa'im who arises within the *dawr-e satr* does not merely gather the Imams of his own cycle; as his rank increases, he also gathers the Qa'im of the *dawr-e kashf*. The Qa'im of the *dawr-e satr* gathers the Qa'im of the *dawr-e kashf* — how? In the horizon of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*, from which the necessary forms are obtained for him. In truth, the genuine successor of a Qa'im of the *dawr-e satr* is the one who will be the second Qa'im of the *dawr-e satr*; he is the true vicegerent.

So it proceeds until seven cycles have passed: seven cycles of unveiling and seven cycles of concealment. Then there arises a universal Qa'im of a universal Qa'im. He gathers neither merely the Imams nor merely the individual *qawa'im*, but rather the *qawa'im* of the seven *dawr-e satr* — and beneath the *qawa'im* of the cycles of concealment sit the Qa'ims of the cycles of unveiling, for they are subsumed under them. Thus his rank reaches from one extreme to another; yet even he stands under the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. The word *ufuq*, horizon, is used precisely for this: a throne (*kursi*) is appointed and remains fixed. This continues until a great cycle (*qawr*) ends; then a new *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* comes. When the new *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* comes and is promoted, it takes the place of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* that preceded it, and the prior one is exalted, the new one becoming its successor. In this manner the whole structure is sustained.

### The Resurrection of the Bodies and the Sequence of the Prophetic Cycles

We turn now to the resurrection of the bodies, which the administrator of the affair (*mudabbir al-amr*) brings about. When the *dawr-e kashf* begins, the bodies of the people of the *dawr-e satr* are resurrected; their resurrection (*ba'th*) occurs. The administrator turns his attention to the affair of raising the bodies of the people of the cycle of concealment and their excellent ones (*fudala'*). The text affirms the reality of this with the saying: `Innal-ba'tha haqq` — "Indeed, the resurrection is true." Upon the finishing of the *dawr-e satr*, the resurrection of its people's bodies commences.

The sequence of this raising is precise and meaningful. `Fa-awwalu mā ukhidha bihi ajsām ahl al-dawr al-muhammadī li-qurbatihim min dawr al-qā'im ʿalayhi al-zikr al-salām wa-sharafihim wa-kawnihim kānū fī dawr al-satr ākhiran` — "So the first thing to be taken will be the bodies of the people of the Muhammadan cycle, on account of their nearness to the cycle of the Qa'im, upon whom be remembrance and peace, and on account of their nobility, and their having been last in the cycle of concealment." The first bodies to be taken up are those of the people of the Muhammadan cycle (*dawr al-muhammadi*). Three reasons are given: their nearness (*qurbat*) to the cycle of the Qa'im, since they stand closest to it; their nobility (*sharafat*); and their position as the last in the cycle of concealment. Their lateness in the *dawr-e satr* places them nearest to what comes next, and so they are first in the rising.

The text then specifies whose bodies these are. `Fa-yubʿathu ajsām hā'ulā' al-awliyā' wa-alladhīna irtaqat nufūsuhum ilā ʿālam al-ṣafā' wa-takhammarat ajsāmuhum al-sharīfah wa-takhammarat shay'an ba'da shay'in` — "So the bodies of these awliya' will be resurrected — those whose souls ascended to the world of purity, and whose noble bodies fermented, and fermented bit by bit." First, then, the bodies of the *awliya'* are resurrected: those whose souls (*nufus*) had ascended to the world of purity (*ʿalam al-safa'*), and whose noble bodies had undergone a fermentation (*takhammur*) — a process by which the body, hidden away little by little, was gradually transmuted. The verb *khammara* here carries the sense of remaining hidden, lying concealed and quietly transforming over long stretches, a little after a little.

After the *awliya'* come the others. `Thumma ajsām sā'ir al-firaq wa-nufūsuhum allatī istaḥālat fī ajsāmihim wa-udhīqū al-ʿadhāb al-adnā dūna al-akbar` — "Then the bodies of all the sects, and their souls which were transformed within their bodies; and they will be made to taste the lesser punishment, not the greater." Then the bodies of all the sects (*firaq*) are raised — these are the opposites (*azdad*), whose souls did not ascend but rather transformed and degenerated within their own bodies. They are made to taste the lesser punishment (*al-ʿadhab al-adna*), not the greater (*al-akbar*). The *awliya'* are those who love, the believers whose souls rose to purity; the *azdad* are those whose souls turned within them, and their portion is the lesser torment alone.

Now the cycles unfold in their full sequence. `Fa-yaqūmu dawruhum sabʿat ālāf sinīn` — "And their cycle is established for seven thousand years." Each such cycle is established for seven thousand years. `Thumma qāma qā'imuhum wa-istaqbala baʿth ahl dawr ʿĪsā ʿalayhi al-salām` — "Then their Qa'im rises, and faces the resurrection of the people of the cycle of ʿIsa, peace be upon him." When the seven thousand years are completed, their Qa'im rises and confronts the resurrection of the people of the preceding prophet's cycle. The order proceeds backward through the prophetic cycles. After seven thousand years, the Qa'im faces the resurrection of the people of the cycle of ʿIsa, peace be upon him. Then, after another seven thousand years, the next Qa'im rises and faces the resurrection of the people of the cycle of Musa, peace be upon him. Likewise, after seven thousand years, the Qa'im faces the resurrection of the people of the cycle of Ibrahim, peace be upon him. Then, after seven thousand years, the resurrection of the people of the cycle of Nuh, peace be upon him. And finally, after seven thousand years, the Qa'im faces the resurrection of the people of the cycle of Adam, peace be upon him. With this, the *dawr-e kashf* is completed: `Wa-tamma dawr al-kashf` — "And the cycle of unveiling is completed." For each prophetic cycle, seven thousand years; in each, the bodies and souls of its people are resurrected; and when the cycle of Adam is reached and a Qa'im rises, the whole cycle of unveiling reaches its end.

### The Cycle of Concealment and the Spans of the Great Cycle

When the cycle of unveiling is complete, the cycle of concealment follows. `Wa-akhadha bi-dawr al-satr ʿasharat ālāf sinīn` — "And it was followed by the cycle of concealment for ten thousand years." After the *dawr-e kashf* comes the *dawr-e satr*, which is reckoned here at ten thousand years. (Although in many places the *dawr-e satr* is given as seven thousand, here the figure is ten thousand; it appears that the interim period, the *dawr-e fatrah*, is included within the count.) Of these ten thousand years, the text apportions them: `Wa-minhā thalāthat ālāf sinīn dawr ahl al-nujūm wa'l-falsafah wa-ʿilm al-riyāḍiyyah wa-sabʿat ālāf sharīʿah` — "And of them, three thousand years is the cycle of the people of astronomy, philosophy, and the science of mathematics, and seven thousand of Shari'ah." Three thousand of the ten thousand years belong to the cycle of the people of astronomy (*nujum*), philosophy (*falsafah*), and the science of mathematics (*ʿilm al-riyadiyyah*); the remaining seven thousand are the years of Shari'ah. This proceeds, as the text says, `ʿAlā mā taqaddama bihi al-qawl` — "as the discourse has preceded."

The author now states the great continuity that binds the whole structure together, and it is worth giving the Arabic in its fullness: `Wa-lā yazāl al-ḥāl kadhālika dawr al-kashf wa-dawr al-satr wa'l-imām wa'l-imāmah mustamirratan jāriyatan ʿalā al-ʿādah fī tasalsul min wāḥid ilā wāḥid wa-mawlūdun bi-ʿaqab wālidin abadan sarmadan lā inqiṭāʿa lahā wa-lā ʿudūla bihā ʿan al-ʿaqab ʿalā mā huwa mashrūḥ fī mā taqaddama ilā wafāt qawr Ādam alladhī huwa thalāthumi'ah alf sinah wa-sittūn alf sinah maḍrūbun fī mithlihā` — "And the situation will not cease to be thus: the cycle of unveiling and the cycle of concealment, and the Imam and the Imamate, will be continuous, flowing according to custom in a chain from one to another, a child succeeding a parent, forever and ever, with no interruption and no deviation from the lineage, as has been explained in what has preceded, until the passing of the great cycle of Adam, which is three hundred sixty thousand years multiplied by its like."

The teaching here is one of unbroken continuity. The cycle of unveiling and the cycle of concealment, and the Imam and the Imamate, remain established and flowing according to the appointed custom, in a chain that passes from one to the next, a newborn (*mawlud*) succeeding a parent (*walid*), always and forever. There is no break (*inqitaʿ*) in this chain and no turning away (*ʿudul*) from the lineage (*ʿaqab*). And it continues until the passing of the great cycle of Adam — the *qawr* of Adam — which is three hundred sixty thousand years multiplied by its like.

This requires a definition of terms. A small cycle (*qawr asghar*), which is also simply called a *dawr*, is four hundred thousand years. The great cycle (*qawr aʿzam*) is three hundred sixty thousand years multiplied by itself — that is, three hundred sixty thousand years taken times its own measure. These two scales — the small cycle and the great cycle — govern the whole cosmic economy that follows.

### The Universal Resurrection and the Reign of Saturn

We arrive now at the universal resurrection, the *qiyamat-e kulli*. `Fa-idhā inqaḍā dhālika istarkhat rubāṭāt al-aflāk` — "So when that comes to an end, the tethers of the celestial spheres will loosen." When the great cycle reaches its conclusion, the bonds of the celestial spheres (*rubatat al-aflak*) slacken and come loose. These bonds are the things that hold the spheres in their order — one may liken them to the magnetic force, to the pegs of the sphere (*awtad al-falak*), the spiritual fastenings that maintain the bonds of all the spheres, from the encompassing sphere (*falak al-muhit*) downward. One could call it gravity. When the great cycle ends, these gravitational forces give way, and the bonds of all the spheres loosen.

`Wa-fanā jamīʿ mā ʿalā wajh al-arḍ` — "And everything on the face of the earth will perish." Everything upon the face of the earth perishes — plants (*nabat*), animals (*hayawan*), and human beings (*bashar*). `Wa-ṣāra al-amr ka-awwalihi fa-yakūnu al-tadbīr li-zuḥal` — "And the affair will become as it was at first, and the administration will be for Zuhal." The affair returns to its first state, the condition that obtained when nothing existed — no plant, no animal, no human — and the administration (*tadbir*) of the world passes to Zuhal, that is, to Saturn.

It is important to grasp the scope of this resurrection precisely. It is the world (*dunya*) — the earth and what lives upon it, plants and animals and humans — that perishes utterly. This is what is called the universal resurrection. The spheres themselves are not destroyed; it is their bonds (*rubatat al-aflak*) that loosen, the magnetism of the spheres that slackens. The great cycle is established, and after that a new cycle begins. The administration passes to Saturn.

The text describes in detail the desolation that follows. `Fa-yafnā jamīʿ mā ʿalā al-arḍ min jibāl wa-ḥijārah fa-yaṣīru mudaddak` — "And everything on the earth, of mountains and stones, will perish, and it will become pulverized." All that is upon the earth — its mountains (*jibal*) and its stones (*hijarah*) — perishes and is ground to powder. `Wa-yarṣidu al-bard al-mufriṭ thumma al-salūj al-mutarākimah wa'l-ghuyūm wa'l-ḍabāb wa-ẓalām al-jawwī` — "And an extreme cold will set in, then the piled-up snows, and the clouds, and the fog, and the darkness of the atmosphere." An extreme cold (*al-bard al-mufrit*) takes hold; then snows piled one upon another (*al-suluj al-mutarakimah*); then clouds (*ghuyum*) and fog (*dabab*) and the darkness of the atmosphere (*zalam al-jawwi*). `Wa-taṣʿad al-bukhār wa-ghadrān al-amṭār wa-tafajjur al-anhar ḥattā takūn al-arḍ jamīʿan ka'l-baḥr yastalib amwājuhā` — "And vapor will rise, and pools of rain will form, and rivers will burst forth, until the entire earth becomes like the sea, its waves snatching." Vapor (*bukhar*) ascends; rains gather in pools; rivers and springs burst forth, until the whole earth becomes like a turbulent sea whose waves are tossed in agitation. `Wa-fī jamīʿ al-āfāq ẓulmah lā tanqaṭiʿ wa-lā tatajallā li-kathāfat al-ghuyūm bayn al-shams wa'l-arḍ` — "And in all horizons there will be a darkness that is not cut off and does not clear, on account of the density of the clouds between the sun and the earth." In all the horizons (*afaq*) there is a darkness that is neither cut off nor lifted, because of the density of the clouds that stands between the sun and the earth. Through the influence of Saturn, the clouds and their causes become so dense that the light of the sun cannot reach the earth at all, and there is complete darkness. First the whole earth is covered with water; and over that turbulent sea hangs an unbroken darkness, the sun's light unable to pierce the thick cloud.

`Wa-yastamirru tadbīr al-kawākib ʿalā mā ḍakarnāhu` — "And the administration of the stars will continue as we have mentioned." The administration of the stars (*kawakib*) continues in the manner already described. There are no humans now to feel the cold; there is no one. The cosmos is given over to this long winter of dissolution under Saturn's rule.

### The Appearance of the Qa'im of the Millennium and the New Corporeality

Out of this desolation a new manifestation begins. `Fa-idhā āna ẓuhūr qā'im al-alfiyyah min mughārāt kamā kāna awwalan saʿada min laṭā'if al-arḍ wa-aṣfā mā fīhā shay'un yakūnu khalīfatan li'l-shams wa-sā'ir al-kawākib wa'l-burūj` — "So when the time comes for the appearance of the Qa'im of the millennium from the caves, as it was at first, there will ascend from the subtle parts of the earth and the purest of what is in it, a thing that will be a successor to the sun and to the rest of the stars and constellations." When the time arrives for the Qa'im of the millennium (*qa'im al-alfiyyah*) to appear from the caves (*mugharat*), just as happened at the first beginning, there ascends from the subtle parts of the earth (*lata'if al-ard*) and from the purest of what the earth contains a luminous substance — a thing that will become a successor (*khalifah*) to the sun and to the rest of the stars and constellations.

The mechanism is one of exchange between earth and heaven. Things from the House of the Ka'bah ascend and take the place of the sun, while from the sun things descend. The substances prepared above, having been refined there, are the material from which the next *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* — the next Primordial Corporeality that is to manifest — will be nurtured. The pure substance ascending from the earth becomes the vicegerent of the sun; the substances descending from the sun become the nourishment of the new *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. But the earth must first be given the chance to settle, and this settling itself takes a very long time. (The full span of the great cycle, the *qawr aʿzam*, in which all these things run their course, is reckoned at fifty-one thousand years for the stars to complete their measure.)

The descent of the heavenly bodies is then specified. `Wa-tanḥadiru al-shams wa-kawākib al-sittah wa'l-burūj al-ithnā ʿashar ilā al-arḍ wa-yaʿūdūna turāban saḥīqan` — "And the sun, and the six stars, and the twelve constellations will descend to the earth, and they will become fine dust." The sun, together with the six other stars and the twelve constellations, descends to the earth and becomes fine dust (*turab sahiq*). Note the reckoning: the sun, the six remaining planets (seven in all), and the twelve constellations — these nineteen bodies descend to the earth and become fine dust. The encompassing sphere (*falak al-muhit*) is not among them; it is these nineteen that descend. The earth itself, with its bedrock (*sakhr*), remains in its own condition; what falls is the substance of the spheres, falling upon the earth. From this fine dust the body of the new *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* is formed once the earth has settled.

`Wa-minhā yakūnu al-samāniyah wa'l-ʿishrūna shakhṣan fuḍalā' al-kuramā' wa-ashrafuhā fī al-jussat al-ibdā'iyyah wa-yakūnu al-shams hiya imāmuhum wa-ra'īsuhum wa-bāqī al-kawākib wa'l-burūj hum al-sabaʿat wa'l-ʿishrūna al-ẓāhirah bi-ẓuhūrihi wa-yakūnu al-ḥāl ʿalā mā kāna awwalan kadhālika` — "And from it will be the twenty-eight persons, the excellent ones, the noble, the most honored of them in the primordial corporeality. And the sun will be their Imam and their chief, and the rest of the stars and constellations will be the twenty-seven who appear with his appearance. And the situation will be as it was at first." From that settled earth there arise twenty-eight persons — the excellent and noble ones (*fudala al-kurama*), the most honored within the primordial corporeality (*jussa al-ibda'iyyah*). The sun becomes their Imam and chief; the remaining stars and constellations are the twenty-seven who appear together with his appearance. And so the whole situation returns to what it was at the first beginning, the entire pattern renewed.

### The Hudud as Servants and the Dissolution of the Body

The second chapter of the first gate, from the fourth treatise, turns to the fate of the Hudud and the long return of their bodies. It opens: `Fa'l-Hudūd ʿalayhimu's-salām hum khuddām fī ʿālam al-jirm wa ʿālam al-din` — "And the Hudud, peace be upon them, are servants in the world of the body and the world of religion." The Hudud, peace be upon them, are servants (*khuddam*) both in the world of the body (*ʿalam al-jirm*) and in the world of religion (*ʿalam al-din*).

`Wa dhalika annahum ʿinda intiqāli ata'ibihim ilā 'ālam al-rūhānī, tabqā ajsāmuhum fi'l-ard` — "And that is because, when their subtle parts transfer to the spiritual world, their bodies remain in the earth." When a Hudud passes, his subtle essences ascend to the spiritual world, while his body remains in the earth. `Fa yasʿadu ilā thalāthati ayyāmin ma baqiya fi'l-jism min aʿqab al-nafs al-hissiyyah` — "Then there ascends, for three days, whatever remained in the body from the remnants of the sensory soul." For three days, whatever remained in the body of the remnants (*aʿqab*) of the sensory soul (*nafs al-hissiyyah*) ascends. The word *aʿqab* denotes something small, a remnant, a very minor portion — it is also the name of a small bird. So for three days the small remnant of the sensory soul ascends from the body. This sensory soul that remains is also called the vegetative soul (*nafs al-namiyyah*) — the two terms are at times used for one another — and it bears the further name of the airy or windy soul (*nafs al-rihiyyah*).

How much of the soul ascends and how much remains depends upon the rank and conduct of each Hudud. According to the rank a Hudud holds, and according to how he has spent his life in the world, his constituents progress. For some of the highest rank, the entire vegetative soul ascends together with their passing, and even the faculties of the brain ascend with their soul and form. Others, who did not show full respect for the Shari'ah during their lives, leave more of the vegetative soul behind. It is according to each person's state how much of him remains in the grave and how much is taken up. As the text says: `Wa huwa al-musamma bi'l-nafs al-rihiyyah allatī tasʿadu ilā falaki'sh-shams ma sabaqa bihi'l-qawl anifan min inhilāli jumlati'l-jism` — "And it is named the windy soul, which ascends to the sphere of the sun, as the discourse has just stated, from the dissolution of the entire body." This airy soul ascends to the sphere of the sun (*falak al-shams*), as was stated just before in the discourse, following the dissolution of the whole body.

`Wa maṣīruhā turāban sahiqan` — "And its becoming pulverized dust." The body's destiny is to become pulverized dust. `Wa minhā ma yasʿadu wa huwa al-laḥm wa'l-dam wa'l-ʿasab wa'l-ʿurūq wa sā'ir fuḍulāti'l-jism illā'l-ʿizām` — "And from it, what ascends is the flesh, the blood, the nerves, the veins, and all the other excesses of the body, except for the bones." From the dissolved body, certain things ascend: the flesh (*lahm*), the blood (*dam*), the nerves (*ʿasab*), the veins (*ʿuruq*), and all the bodily excesses (*fudulat al-jism*) — all save the bones (*ʿizam*), for the skeleton has a different fate. (The hard bones are meant here, along with the hair and nails, whose condition differs from the soft, dissolvable parts.)

`Fa yatalaṭṭafu wa yasīru bukhāran thumma yasīru ilā an yasīru fi falaki'l-burūj` — "Then it becomes subtle and turns to vapor, then it proceeds until it reaches the sphere of the constellations." These ascending parts become refined, turn to vapor (*bukhar*), and proceed upward until they reach the sphere of the constellations (*falak al-buruj*), where they take the place of one of the constellations. `Wa yanhadiru dhālika al-burju ilā'l-ard fa yasīru turāban thumma yasīru ilā'l-mizāj wa'l-muntazij wa taʿūdu ilā'l-ma'kūlāt wa'l-mashrūbāt allatī la tadkhulu nāran` — "And that constellation descends to the earth, and becomes dust, then proceeds to the temperament and the blended, and returns to the edibles and drinkables that do not enter fire." That constellation then descends to the earth and becomes dust; it proceeds to the temperament (*mizaj*) and the blended (*muntazij*); and it returns to the foods and drinks (*ma'kulat wa mashrubat*) — specifically those that do not enter fire.

A point of understanding is needed here, for the descent of a constellation to the world may seem strange if one imagines the constellations to be merely figures of the imagination. They are not imaginary; they are matter. It is the encompassing sphere that is imaginary; the constellation is material substance, the place from which the stars proceed and where they hold their station. The ascending vapors go up and take a place there for their appointed span, while the substances that had ascended in a prior age — those that have completed their thousand years — now descend. The cycle of a constellation is reckoned: the ascended thing must remain there for its term, and after seven hundred twenty years it descends again, as the next ascends from below to take its place. This is the *miqat*, the appointed measure: the substances of the constellations ascend toward the *miqat*, are made subtle, and return toward the foods that do not enter fire.

`Thumma yaghtadhī bihi fa yasīru nutfan thumma yasīru ashkhāsan wa tudʿā fa tujīb fa taṣīru min jumlati'l-awliyā'` — "Then it is nourished by it and becomes a sperm-drop, then it becomes persons, and it is called and it answers, so it becomes one of the company of the awliya'." From these foods nourishment is taken, and it becomes a sperm-drop (*nutfah*); then it manifests as a person; and that person is invited (*tudʿa*) and answers (*tujib*), thereby joining the company of the *awliya'*. `Wa tartaqī suʿūdan fi'l-maʿārif fa takūnu min hudūd al-khādimah fi daʿwati'l-haqq wa ʿalā qadri rutbatihā awwalan takūnu ākhiran` — "And it ascends an ascension in the gnostic sciences, so it becomes one of the serving Hudud in the Da'wat of Truth; and according to its rank in the beginning, so it will be in the end." Such a person ascends in the gnostic sciences (*maʿarif*) and becomes one of the serving Hudud in the Da'wat of Truth (*daʿwat al-haqq*); and his final rank matches his initial rank — whatever station he had held in the former era, the same station is established for him again.

Here a ruling on the pace of this ascent is given. A believer (*mu'min*) has ten ranks open to him, and he crosses one rank per cycle. If in a given cycle he advanced in the first rank, then in the next, within the domain of believers, he attains the second. It is not that the believer crosses several ranks in a single cycle; for the believer the pace is one rank per cycle. The exception is the martyr (*shahid*), of whom alone it is said `darajatan aw darajatayn` — "a rank or two ranks" — that he crosses one or two ranks at once.

### The Long Sojourn of the Bones and the Du'a of Light

The bones have a fate apart from the soft parts of the body. `Wa amma'l-ʿizām fa tabqā muddatan fi'l-ard li-ṣalābatihā ma shā'a al-mudabbir` — "As for the bones, they remain for a period in the earth, on account of their hardness, as the Disposer wills." Because of their hardness (*salabah*), the bones remain in the earth for a long period, for as long as the Disposer (*mudabbir*) wills.

In connection with the structure of the body it is fitting to recall the du'a of light, the supplication that names the internal places of the body and the directions about it — the seventeen-fold du'a, `Nūran fi...` ("Light in..."), the du'a of the Seven Sabts. It consists of ten and seven, seventeen in all. The ten internal locations are: `qalbī` (in my heart), `samʿī` (in my hearing), `baṣarī` (in my sight), `lisānī` (in my tongue), `shaʿrī` (in my hair), `basharī` (in my skin), `damī` (in my blood), `laḥmī` (in my flesh), `ʿizāmī` (in my bones), and `ʿaṣabī` (in my nerves) — these are the ten. Then follow the seven directions: `bayna yadayya` (in front of me), `wa min khalfī` (and behind me), `ʿan yamīnī` (on my right), `ʿan shimālī` (on my left), `min fawqī` (from above me), `min taḥtī` (from below me), and `wa nūran fi qabrī` (and light in my grave) — these are the seven. Ten and seven together make seventeen.

The bones, then, remain for a span in the earth on account of their hardness, as the Disposer wills. `Thumma yanṭaḥinu wa taṣīru bukhāran ila mizāj al-muntazij thumma yaʿūdu ila'l-ard` — "Then they are ground, and become vapor unto the temperament of the blended, then they return to the earth." Because of their hardness, the bones do not readily yield fine dust and vapor as the softer parts did; their power to do so is not as great. But in time they too are ground; they become dust, ascend as vapor toward the temperament of the blended, and then return to the earth. `Fa yanʿaqidu fi'l-maʿādin yāqūtatan wa durran wa lu'lu'an wa jawāhira sharīfatan thamīnatan makhzūnatan ʿalā afdali ma yakūnu wa ashrafihi` — "And they solidify in the mines as rubies, pearls, and precious, valuable, stored-up jewels, upon the best and most noble state they can be." Returning to the earth, the bones solidify within the mines (*maʿadin*) as rubies (*yaqut*), pearls (*durr* and *lu'lu'*), and precious, valuable jewels — the kind of jewels that are stored and treasured up by kings — in their finest and most honorable state. Unlike the softer parts that journey up to the constellations, the bones, descending from the temperament and the blended, solidify directly in the mines as these precious stones.

### The Composition of the Human Being and the Greatness of the Form

It is worth setting out plainly the constituents of the human being, for the whole doctrine of the return rests upon them. A human being comprises, first, his essence or soul — the real thing, which Allah, the Originator (*al-Mubdiʿ*), created. This essence is what is truly meant by his soul: its form changed and it became the soul. Alongside the essence there is the sensory soul (*nafs al-hissiyyah*) and the vegetative soul (*nafs al-namiyyah*) — two souls. The vegetative soul is received at the conception of the sperm-drop; the sensory soul is received when the person comes into the world, gained from the rays of the form. These two souls become one. And beyond these there is the luminous form (*surat al-nuraniyyah*), the rational soul, which had been prepared earlier and is attained from the Originator.

Now a profound point arises. This luminous form is developed in the world through knowledge and action, and through such development it becomes greater than the soul itself. One might object: if Allah the Most High, the Originator, created the intellect and created the soul, how can the form — itself developed — become greater than the very thing Allah created? The answer is that Allah has granted to the human being free will. He has given these faculties so that man may use his free choice in the way of truth, and this exercise of free will, joined to knowledge and action, grants a thing greater than the form as first given. Through knowledge and action the rank of the luminous form is raised above the soul.

And what does this form do? It takes the sensory soul along with it, to the extent that the soul is capable of ascending. For some, the sensory soul ascends entirely; for some, the vegetative soul also rises in part; for others, the greater part of the vegetative soul is left in the grave, given over to the punishment of the grave — all according to the person's state. The form takes up the sensory soul as far as that soul can rise; the vegetative soul, the soul also named the airy soul, remains in the grave to undergo its own process.

### The Thousand-Year Return and the Refinement of the Vegetative Soul

How do the form and the soul progress upward? They progress through the High Rank, until the Gate of Gates (*Babu'l-Abwab*) is assembled and the soul becomes connected with the progeny of the Imam — at which point it becomes Lahut, divinity. The path of the vegetative soul is described in full: `Tartaqī ila'l-qamari bi-ashiʿʿati'sh-shams` — it ascends to the moon by the rays of the sun. The vegetative soul, which is a part of the human being's essence, ascends in the form of the soul: it reaches the moon by the rays of the sun, drawn upward and gathered by the rays of the moon from various places; from the moon it is joined with the sun, where its purification and refinement take place; from there it passes to the other planets, such as Mercury and Venus; and from there it descends again to this lower realm. Out of it, finally, the physical form (*nasut*) of the progeny of the Imam is formed.

Once the *nasut* is formed, the connection of Lahut is established with it — as the lineage of the Imam continues, the connection of Lahut is set in place. The *nasut* and the Lahut together: this is the very meaning of `Lā ilāha illa'llāh` — "There is no god but God." For the *nasut* is `Lā ilāha`, and the Lahut is `illa'llāh`. Whatever power there is, it is the power of Lahut. The bond of the physical form and the divinity, the union of *nasut* and Lahut, is what the testimony of unity proclaims.

With this in place, the bodily remains complete their cycle. The body remains in the grave — the whole structure of the body is described — and from it, those parts that can quickly dissolve ascend to the heavens and reach the constellations. In every constellation they remain thirty years. From there, things fall down from below — those whose manifestation in the world is yet to come, those who have completed their thousand years. The reckoning is precise: there are twelve constellations, and the soul abides thirty years in each, so twelve times thirty is three hundred sixty years of ascent through the constellations, and another three hundred sixty years to return — that is seven hundred twenty years. Then for the ascending and descending required to manifest again as a human being, a further two hundred eighty years are needed. Added together, these make one thousand years. After a thousand years, a like human being manifests once more in the world: a believer returns as a believer, a respondent as a respondent. As his cry for help (*istighath*) had been, and as his point (*nuqtah*) had been consolidated — for this is called the consolidation of the point — in just that manner he comes again, with his point consolidated as before.

### The Transformation of the Soul Across the Cycles

There is one further refinement that takes place across these returns. During the period of purification and progress, the human being's heart — which is crimson blood — takes on the form of the soul, and in every cycle one soul is consumed and transformed. This continues across many cycles. It happens through three hundred sixty cycles — and since each cycle is a thousand years, this is three hundred sixty thousand years. In the course of three hundred sixty cycles, a person of the straight path — and this is a condition, that his gait be appropriate and that he has not committed the major sins the Shari'ah has forbidden — reaches the station of his heart, his tablet coming to the rank of the heart; and a respondent attains the rank of a *natiq*, a speaker.

There remains the matter of the bones, which return after a thousand years like the rest of the body — yet the bones, it was said, remain a very long time. The reconciliation is this: in the past cycles, the bones that were preserved in various places now progress and take the place of these jewels. When the present body has gone, the old bones preserved from former cycles come forward to fill its place. The various constituents — the vegetative soul, the sensory soul, the form, the parts of the body — all progress along their differing levels and at their differing paces.

### The Gathering at the Qa'im and the Court of the Universal Intellect

At the time of the return, are all these dispersed constituents required to come together as a single unit, upon one platform? They are. They all go and gather at the Qa'im; they are assembled at the Qa'im. It is a wondrous court that gathers there. When the constituents come, they come fully purified, and they gather before the Imam. The integration runs through the whole hierarchy: the gathering is with the Imam; the assembly of the Imams is with the seventh Imam; the seventh's assembly is with the Qa'im; the assembly of the seven-thousand-year Qa'im is with the Qa'im of the Millennium. It is as though there were a single factory to which all things are brought to one place, and there they become one — until, as the text explains, the tablet becomes the *natiq*, the speaker.

And yet in this gathering the individuality of each is not destroyed. It will merge, and it will also remain distinct; then it becomes one again. For the soul that was there, the essence, had descended in various forms — some in a descent so deep that the soul was here and the feet there, so to speak, a descent occasioned by delay. All these scattered things are gathered, and at last a single entity, a single whole, is formed. The integration is for this very purpose; but the individuality still remains. With the Qa'im they abide — in the Universal Intellect (*ʿaql al-kulli*) of the Qa'im their assembly takes place. What they ultimately seek is to become the Universal Intellect: to return to the very state in which God Almighty first created them, the state in which they were at the first. No one can be a partner there. Each must finally attain the state in which it stood at its origin. Thus, after salvation, the essence is restored to what it was, yet it remains with the Qa'im, gathered into the Universal Intellect of the Qa'im.

This gathering continues until it reaches the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*, the Primordial Corporeality that is the collector of the great cycle. With the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* are all the points (*nuqat*) that exist — from a single respondent up to a Qa'im, every point is present there. Each believer's point is present, and the intensity (*hiddat*) of each point is established according to each one's power. And how was a believer's point established? According to the intensity of faith that he had adopted in the cycle before — according to that intensity is his point fixed. So the points of all — believers, respondents, and Qa'ims alike — are present with the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*.

It is worth retracing here, in summary, how the souls reunite at the time of passing. At death, what remained of the sensory soul and the vegetative soul become one. For the believer, it is said that his vegetative soul goes into his body while the sensory soul departs with the form. The remnant of the sensory soul ascends together with the form; what does not ascend, the lower portion of the sensory soul, merges with the vegetative soul, which remains in the grave. The order of their original union is instructive: first the vegetative soul came, in the sperm-drop; then, advancing and advancing, when it manifested in the world it became sensory, and the vegetative and sensory became one. At passing, the lower part — the vegetative — remains, while the higher part ascends with the form, the lower portion of the sensory merging with the vegetative. The portion that ascended with the form went up; the vegetative, with what merged into it, then undergoes its long process, rising as vapor toward the temperament and the blended — by way of the bones — and then returning to the earth, where it solidifies in the mines as rubies and pearls and precious stored jewels, in their finest and most honorable state.

### The Great Cycle's Fulfillment and the Forming of the New Corporeality

`Fa idhā ana wafā'u al-kawr al-aʿzam, inṭaḥanat tilka al-jawāhir wa saʿadat fa sārat kawākiba khālifatan li sabʿati al-kawākib wa'l-burūj al-ithnā ʿashar` — "So when the fulfillment of the Great Cycle arrives, those jewels are ground up and ascend, and become planets succeeding the seven planets and the twelve constellations." When the fulfillment of the Great Cycle (*kawr aʿzam*) draws near, those jewels — which are the transformed bones — are ground, and they ascend, becoming planets that succeed the seven planets and the twelve constellations.

A difficulty may be raised. However precious a mine's yield may be, a bone is still a bone; it had progressed to become a part of the *hadqah*, the physical body of a Hudud. However precious a jewel it becomes, surely the original state — the part of a Hudud's body that it once was — is greater than a jewel. To become a jewel would seem to be a regression, a going backward, since the natural order ascends from mineral to plant to animal: for the bone to become a mineral is to descend the ladder. The resolution is this: those bones take a very long time to be transformed precisely because they must manifest in a particular place — with the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. Their manifestation is reserved for the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*, and so they are kept preserved in the mines, that their manifestation may occur there at the appointed time. If they come late, they come late; but the complete purification is accomplished, and they arrive and assemble at the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. In this Great Cycle there is, for some, no further dissolution; what has reached its term remains as it is, and the nourishment, whatever it becomes, is no longer subject to dissolution again. The old bones from before the Great Cycle are now given a place, coming forward to take the place of the body that has gone.

So when the fulfillment of the Great Cycle draws near — and let us recall that the *kawr*, the small cycle, is the cycle of four hundred thousand years — those jewels are ground and ascend, and they become planets succeeding the seven planets and the twelve constellations. They must now go up; in the measure of time the great cycle allows, they go to assume the succession of the seven planets. The seven planets that will fall in the four-hundred-thousand-year cycle: when they fall, these ascend in their place, so that the heavens are not left empty. At the time the Great Cycle is ending, what descends from above are the bones of the Hudud; and the planets and constellations become dust, and from that dust the bones are created.

`Fa sārat kawākib khālifatan li sabʿati al-kawākib wa'l-burūj al-ithnā ʿashar ḥāfizatan li amākinihā` — "So they became planets, succeeding the seven planets and the twelve constellations, preserving their places." Thus they become planets, succeeding the seven planets and the twelve constellations, and preserving their places (*hafizatan li amakiniha*). The pronoun "their" refers to the planets: the ascending bones guard and hold the places of those constellations from which the substances will fall. As the bones ascend, the bodies that had occupied those places fall; and the ascending bones take and preserve those places, arranged according to their ranks. The first ones fall, and the bones remain as their successors in the heavens, holding their places until the supports of the heavens loosen once more at the coming of the next cycle.

`Wa saʿada aʿqabuhā wa inḥadarat fa yakūnu minhā tilka al-jussatu al-ibdā'iyyah` — "And their successors ascended, and it descended, and from it will be that Primordial Corporeality." Their successors ascend; and from the descending substance is formed the *nasut* of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* — the physical form of the Primordial Corporeality is constituted from the bones that remain there. And the sun becomes that unique individual of the rank of oneness, the Qa'im in the station of the First Originator (*al-Mubdiʿ*); and the moon becomes its gate (*bab*). The moon is the gate that receives the ascending forms: `Al-mutasallim li's-suwari's-sā'idah` — "the one who receives the ascending forms."

### The Final Purification of the Hudud and the Abode of Eternity

`Wa bāqīhim sā'ir al-hudūd ʿalā hādhā an-namaṭ wa'n-niẓām taʿqubuhu fi'l-khidmah ilā an yuṣfā jamīʿu ajsāmihim ʿalā murūri'l-awqāt wa'l-aʿsār` — "And the rest of them, all the Hudud, upon this pattern and this system, follow him in service until all their bodies are purified over the passage of times and ages." All the remaining Hudud follow upon this same pattern and system, serving in succession, until every one of their bodies is purified over the passing of times and ages. `Wa lā yabqā minhā shay'un fī ʿālami'l-kawn wa'l-fasād` — "And nothing of them remains in the world of generation and corruption." Not a single thing of them remains in the world of generation and corruption (*ʿalam al-kawn wa'l-fasad*). `Illā ʿā'idatan ilā maḥalli's-safā wa dāri'l-baqā, ʿazīzatan maḥbūratan ghaniyyatan maghfūratan ḥayyatan bāqiyatan masrūratan` — "Except returning to the abode of purity and the abode of eternity, honored, gifted, rich, forgiven, living, eternal, happy." Everything of them returns to the abode of purity (*mahall al-safa*) and the abode of eternity (*dar al-baqa*), and there it is honored (*ʿaziza*), gifted (*mahbura* — that is, given to, the recipient of a gift), rich (*ghaniyya*), forgiven (*maghfura*), living (*hayya*), eternal (*baqiya*), and happy (*masrura*).

To this the author appends the Quranic exhortation: `Wa li-mithli hādhā fal-yaʿmali'l-ʿāmilūn` — "And for the like of this, let the workers work" (Quran 37:61). For a recompense such as this — the eternal return to the abode of purity and the abode of eternity — let those who labor labor. And he closes with a prayer: may Allah join us with them and not part us from them, and make us of their company, by the right of Muhammad and his pure family, may the blessings of Allah be upon them all.

We may here gather again the whole movement of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*, for the processes are given precisely so that they may be understood. The *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* that is to appear — whose vicegerent is it? It is the vicegerent of the first *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. And what is that first *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* doing? It is preparing all the souls and forms — for whom? For its progeny. That progeny becomes the Qa'im; the Qa'ims gather at the Qa'ims of the cycles; the Qa'im of a cycle gathers at the Qa'im of the great cycle; and the great cycle becomes a gate (*bab*) that connects with the next *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. Just as an Imam prepares for his progeny, so the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* of the past prepares for the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* that is to come. All the forms and souls are gathered; once gathered, the vegetative souls have entered into the *nasut* and the sensory souls into the form — the *nasut* and form of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. Then the bones ascend and become the holy *nasut* of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*. So all things are gathered in one place. The servants (*khuddam*) from whom the manifestation will come, the heavens that fall and become dust, and from those pits the bones formed — the bones of the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah* are formed, the souls and forms are constituted, and the people of that age have their constituents attained for them. The points of all — believers, respondents, and Qa'ims — are present with the *jussa-i ibda'iyyah*.

### The Two Paradises and the Removal of Hardship

A distinction must be drawn between the paradise of the soul and the paradise of the body. The paradise for the soul is Jannat al-Ma'wa, the Sidrat al-Muntaha; this is the paradise to which only the soul ascends, the body not going there. The paradise for the body is the paradise of the presences (*jannat al-hadarat*). What is this paradise of the presences? It is that a person lives in the world in peace and dwells in comfort — this is a paradise for him. The world's troubles arise from our mutual relations, from social strife; but in the realm of the presences there is no strife. There, every kind of scenery and joy and happiness is found. This is not a description of unveiling (*kashf*) — the realm of *kashf* is a different thing — but a description of the *hadarat*. And it should be noted that even within the cycle of concealment (*dawr-e satr*) there are stations of presence; in the *kashf* there are presences, and that is the same realm, where likewise there is such happiness.

A ruling follows concerning the lofty paradises. In those lofty paradises (*jannat al-ʿaliyat*) the body does not undergo hardship. One might ask whether the body too experiences hardship there, as by fasting; but fasting is not obligatory there. Fasting belongs to the obligations of hardship, and in the state of free choice such obligations are removed. There is no question of hunger, and fasting is not made obligatory; the hardships are taken away. When fasting is not obligatory, then if a person in that state desires to eat something — any fruit — he may eat it. In those lofty paradises the obligations of hardship do not apply, for the state is one of free choice and ease.

### The Sacred Numbers and the Best Days of the Year

Two further points of reckoning conclude the discourse, drawing the cosmic numbers down into the calendar of worship. First, the figure of the seven planets and the twelve constellations together makes nineteen — and nineteen is equal to the numerical value of `Bismillāh al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm`. The seven planets and the twelve constellations, joined, answer to the numerical worth of the opening of every act in the name of God.

Second, there is the matter of the best days of the lunar year, marked in the *tasbih* of the days — the rosary recitation `Astaghfirullāha yā Dhā'l-Jalāli wa'l-Ikrām`. How many days are there in the year? In the Hijri year there are three hundred fifty-four days (and in a leap year three hundred fifty-five). The count arises thus: the months alternate thirty and twenty-nine days; twelve times thirty would be three hundred sixty, but subtracting the six days of the shorter months yields three hundred fifty-four. Of these three hundred fifty-four days, one hundred are the highest, and these are marked by the *tasbih*. The bulk of them fall in the great months — the days of Rajab, the days of Sha'ban, and the days of Ramadan together account for the greater portion — and to these are added the high single days of the year.

Within these hundred, eighteen days are singled out as especially excellent — and here the *tasbih*'s structure of ninety-nine beads is instructive, for the Imam is understood as separate from and above the count of ninety-nine, so that the ninety-nine are recited and the Imam comprehended beyond them; from within these, eighteen are set apart. The eighteen special days are: the first of Muharram; the tenth of Muharram; the first of Rajab; the fifteenth of Rajab; the twenty-seventh of Rajab; the fifteenth of Sha'ban; then the odd nights of Ramadan the noble — the seventeenth, nineteenth, twenty-first, and twenty-third; then Eid al-Fitr; then Yawm Nuzulika, the day of the descent; then the day of 'Arafah; then Eid al-Adha; then the three days of Ayyam al-Tashriq; and the eighteenth of Dhu'l-Hijjah.

Of all these, the eighteenth of Dhu'l-Hijjah is the most excellent — for it is Yawm al-Ghadir, the most excellent day of the entire year. Concerning it, Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq affirmed by a threefold oath that no day is better than it. When asked whether any day could be better than this, he answered, "No, by God! No, by God! No, by God!" — swearing three times by the oath of God that no day surpasses the day of Ghadir.

## The Knots, the Barzakh, and the Great Kawr

### The Two Angels at Death: A True Saying Whose Reality Was Hidden

We now return to the thread of speech where it left off, intending to join what follows to what preceded so that the earlier discourse may be enlarged in its explanation and made clearer in its meaning — `An nashfaʿa mā qasamahu min al-qawl bimā nazīdu fī bayānihi wa īẓāḥihi` ("that we add to what was mentioned of the speech with what we add in its explanation and clarification"). The word *shafāʿat* here carries its root sense of joining, of placing one thing beside another; and so we join this matter to that which came before, that its exposition may grow fuller.

It is a settled saying in the common speech of the exoteric people — a teaching accepted and circulated among every sect, and one transmitted from the Prophet himself — that at the time of death there must of necessity come a *mubashshir* and a *bashīr*, bearers of glad tidings, for the people of good, and a *Munkar* and a *Nakīr* for the people of evil; and that these are two angels who are present with a person at the moment of his death. As the Arabic has it: `Fa-naqūlu inna fī kalām al-mutaʿārif ʿind ahl al-ẓāhir an lā budda ʿind al-mawt min mubashshir wa bashīr li-ahl al-khayr wa munkir wa nakīr li-ahl al-sharr wa humā malakān yaḥḍurān al-insān ʿind mawtihi` ("So we say that in the common speech among the people of the exterior, it is necessary at the time of death to have a *mubashshir* and a *bashīr* for the people of good, and a *Munkar* and a *Nakīr* for the people of evil, and they are two angels who are present with the person at the time of his death"). This is the doctrine as the *ahl al-ẓāhir*, the people of the outward, have received it and repeat it among themselves.

And this saying is true. It was heard from the *ahl al-ḥaqq*, the people of truth, and the words of it survive — yet its inner reality became hidden from the world: `Wa huwa qawlun ṣaḥīḥun sumiʿa min ahl al-ḥaqq wa gubira ʿan al-ʿālam ḥaqīqatuhu` ("And it is a true saying, heard from the people of truth, and its reality was hidden from the world"). The form of the teaching endured in the mouths of men, but its meaning was veiled. It was not our wish to leave this book empty of that reality — `Wa aradnā an lā nukhalliya kitābanā hādhā ʿan dhālik` ("And we intended not to leave our book empty of that") — and so we set down here what its truth is.

The saying concerning these matters is that every *Nāṭiq* of a cycle, the *dawr*, and his *Waṣī* are themselves the two angels who were pointed toward in their own era: *mubashshir* and *bashīr* for their friends, their *awliyāʾ*, and *Munkar* and *Nakīr* for their opponents, their *aḍdād*. As the text declares: `inna kulla nāṭiqa dawrin wa waṣiyyuhu humā malakān al-mushār ilayhimā fī ʿaṣrihimā min mubashshir wa bashīr li-awliyāʾihimā wa munkir wa nakīr li-aḍdādihimā` ("every *Nāṭiq* of a cycle and his *Waṣī*, they are the two angels referred to in their time as *mubashshir* and *bashīr* for their friends and *Munkar* and *Nakīr* for their opponents"). These two are, in reality, a single personality. They appear as *Munkar* and *Nakīr* to the *aḍdād* and as *mubashshir* and *bashīr* to the *awliyāʾ*. Whether the one face or the other comes to a dying person depends upon how his life closes: if his end is sealed with good and with love, the *mubashshir* and *bashīr* come to him and tell him to which station of paradise he is bound; if his end is otherwise, the *Munkar* and *Nakīr* come and disclose to him the secret of where he is headed.

And just as this is so for the *Nāṭiq* and *Waṣī* of every cycle, so it is likewise for every Imam of the Time and his *Ḥujjat*: `Wa kadhālik kullu imām al-zamān wa ḥujjatihi fa-huwa mubashshir wa bashīr li-awliyāʾihi wa munkir wa nakīr li-aḍdādihimā` ("And likewise every Imam of the Time and his *Ḥujjat*, he is *mubashshir* and *bashīr* for his friends and *Munkar* and *Nakīr* for their opponents"). The Imam of the age and his *Ḥujjat* bear the same office for their friends and their opponents that the *Nāṭiq* and *Waṣī* bore in the prophetic cycle. Thus this reality does not belong to one age alone; it persists and abides in every time and era, for in every age there is a living Master through whom it works.

For this very reason Amīr al-Muʾminīn ʿAlī spoke in verse to Ḥārith of Hamdān. The line is a poem of Janāb ʿAlī, and in it — `Yā Ḥārith Hamdān man yamut yaranī min muʾminin aw munāfiqin ajala yaʿrifunī ṭarfahu wa aʿrifuhu bi-ismihi wa al-kunā wa mā faʿalā` ("O Ḥārith of Hamdān, whoever dies will see me, be he a believer or a hypocrite, soon; his eye recognizes me, and I recognize him by his name and his patronymics and what the two of them did"). Here *ḥār* is in truth *ḥārith*, the final letter clipped away for the sake of the poetic meter, as Arabic permits. The sense is this: whoever passes away — whether a *muʾmin* or a *munāfiq* — will see ʿAlī at the moment of his departure, and that swiftly; this is the form that appears out of that holy *ḥadīth*. The dying man knows ʿAlī with his own eye, and ʿAlī knows him by his name, by his *kunya*, and by what he did — `wa mā faʿalā`, "what the two of them did," meaning whether it was a believer who acted or a hypocrite who acted, ʿAlī knows the whole of his state. This is why, on the Day of accounting, a man's own hands and feet bear witness against him, testifying that he did this and that — for nothing of his deed is hidden from the Master. According to whether the man is a friend or a hypocrite, one face of that single *haykal* appears to him: the *mubashshir* and *bashīr*, or else the *Munkar* and *Nakīr*.

### The Pillar of Light: From the Word to the First Intellect

The reality beneath this is that the *khayāl* — the form, the imaginal presence, the directed attention — of every station, whether *Nāṭiq*, *Waṣī*, or Imam, at the time of each one's death, sets in motion the Pillar of Light, the `ʿumūd al-nūr`. As the text states: `Wa ḥaqīqat dhālik annahu khayāla kulli maqāmin min nāṭiqin wa waṣiyyin wa imāmin fī waqti kulli wāḥidin minhum ʿalayhim yuḥarriku ʿumūd al-nūr alladhī huwa al-sārī min ʿālam al-quds al-muttaṣil bi-ṣūrat kulli waliyyin ṣāḥib al-zamān ʿalayhi afḍal al-salām` ("And the reality of that is that the imagination of every station — *Nāṭiq*, *Waṣī*, and Imam — at the time of each one of them, moves the Pillar of Light, which flows from the world of holiness, connected to the form of every friend, the Master of the Time, upon him be the best of peace"). The word *khayāl* may here be read as *tawajjuh*, the focused attention or turning of the station toward the dying soul. This Pillar of Light flows down from the `ʿālam al-quds`, the world of holiness, and is joined to the form of every *walī* of the Master of the Time.

The detailed explanation of this Pillar of Light has been given, with great fullness, in *Akhbār al-Laṭīfa*; there one may learn how its connection is constituted. The structure of that connection is this: `Bayna al-mubdiʾi wa al-mubdaʿi kalimah` ("between the Originator and the originated is a Word"). Between the *Mubdiʿ* — the Originator, the Creator of the universe — and the *mubdaʿ* — all the originated beings that exist — there stands a *kalimah*, a Word. And this *kalimah* is the *kalimah* of `Lā ilāha illā Allāh`. The light of that Word exists, and from it the Pillar of Light is connected; the Pillar's whole connection takes its origin from it.

All the forms that exist, up to and including the First Intellect, the `ʿaql al-awwal`, have their luminous form connected to this *kalimah* of `Lā ilāha`. And what lies above the First Intellect? The *kalimah* itself. `Lā ilāha` is connected to the *Mubdiʿ*, and no one can pass beyond the Originator. The light that descended from the *nūr* of the *Mubdiʿ* and was joined to the First Intellect — that is what is called the *kalimah*, and that same thing is called the *ʿumūd al-nūr*. From it, all the good forms that exist are connected, gathered to it like the cells of a beehive. So the order is fixed: every luminous form up to the First Intellect is bound to the Word `Lā ilāha`; above the First Intellect is the Word, which reaches up to the Originator; and beyond the Originator none may go.

This Pillar of Light has different centers, graded one above another, as there are graded centers of electric power — a great central station and lesser stations drawing from it. The largest center is the First Intellect; then the *ʿāshir*, the tenth; then the Imam of the time, the *ʿayn aʿẓima*, the great eye of the age. From these centers the Imam's *khayāl*, at the moment of a person's death, gives motion to that light; and that motion travels down and connects to the form of every *walī*, for the Pillar is `al-muttaṣil bi-ṣūrat kulli walī`, joined to the form of every friend. All the good forms hang upon it as upon a single connected lattice.

### The Luminous Form: The Believer's Own Master and Angel

Thus, at the last minute of his life — of his divine life and his righteous deed — the soul that is departing imagines a luminous person, and rejoices in it: `Fa-yatakhayyalu lil-muntaqil ʿind ākhir daqīqah min ʿumrihi al-ilāhī wa ʿamalihi al-ṣāliḥ shakhṣan nūrāniyyan fa-yasurru bihi wa yabtahiju bi-sababihi wa yaghtabiṭu ʿind muʿāyanatihi wa yaʿlamu bi-ladhdhatihi annahu ṣāʾirun ilā khayr wa yufāriqu al-waqt wa al-ḥīn` ("And so it is imagined by the one transitioning, at the last minute of his divine life and his righteous deed, as a luminous person; so he is pleased by it, and rejoices because of it, and is delighted at seeing it, and he knows by its pleasure that he is headed toward good, and he departs from the time and the moment"). The dying believer beholds this luminous figure arising out of his knowledge — his divine *ʿilm* — and his righteous *ʿamal*; he is gladdened by it, takes joy in seeing it, and knows by the very sweetness of the sight that he is bound for good. This happens for every believer; with this knowledge and this delight, he departs the world at that time and moment.

And that luminous figure is the believer's own form — the form he acquired across his life: `Wa dhālik huwa ṣūratuhu allatī iqtanat min walāyat ahl al-ḥaqq wa ʿulūmihim al-ilāhiyyah wa aʿmālihim al-ṣāliḥah maʿa awliyāʾ Allāh taʿālā` ("And that is his form, which he acquired from the friendship of the people of truth, their divine knowledges, and their righteous deeds with the friends of God, the Exalted"). It is built up out of his *walāyah* toward the *ahl al-ḥaqq* — his devotion to the people of truth — and out of their divine knowledges and their righteous deeds done in the company of the friends of God. From the love a man bears for the people of truth, from the *ʿilm* he takes from them and the *ʿamal* he performs with them, this luminous form is fashioned within him; the one who is dying is himself, by this very devotion, counted among the *ahl al-ḥaqq*.

To this our master al-Ṣādiq alluded, in a tradition transmitted from him by Sayyidnā al-Nuʿmān — may God sanctify his spirit — in *Daʿāʾim al-Islām*, where he said: `idhā baʿatha Allāhu ʿazza wa jall al-muʾmin min qabrihi kharaja maʿahu mithālun ḥasanun fa-idhā marra bi-tilka al-shadāʾid qāla lā takhaf laysa ʿalayka baʾs` ("When God, the Mighty and Majestic, raises the believer from his grave, a beautiful image emerges with him; and when he passes through those hardships, it says: 'Do not fear, there is no harm upon you'"). When God raises the believer from the grave, a *mithāl ḥasan*, a beautiful image — a luminous form — comes forth together with him. This luminous form is the believer's true master and guardian, his real *āqā*. It stands as a supervising angel over his body, keeping watch over every single part of it. As the believer passes through the terrors of the grave and the hardships of that crossing, the image says to him, *Lā takhaf* — "Do not fear; there is no harm upon you; no difficulty shall touch you."

And the believer does not cease to be reassured and given glad tidings by this image until it brings him before God: `Fa-lā yazālu al-muʾminu yuʾamminuhu wa yubashshiruhu ḥattā yūridahu ʿalā Allāh taʿālā wa yuḥāsibuhu ḥisāban yasīrā` ("And the believer does not cease to be given security and good tidings by it until it brings him to God, the Exalted, and He calls him to account with an easy accounting"). The image continually grants him security and continually bears glad tidings to him, conducting him at last to God, the Exalted, who calls him to a reckoning that is easy — *ḥisāban yasīrā*. Upon every part of the body the radiance of this luminous form is shed, and by that radiance the scattered parts of the body progress and reach their station, while the believer's account is taken in mercy and ease.

Then the image passes with the believer toward Paradise, and the believer asks it: `man anta raḥimaka Allāh wa qad waʿadtanī fa-ṣadaqtanī wa ammantanī khawfī` ("Who are you, may God have mercy on you? You promised me, and you were true to me, and you secured me from my fear"). The believer says: Who are you? You promised to give me security, and you made that promise good; you proved true to me, *wa ṣadaqtanī*, and you gave me safety from my fear. And the image answers: `anā khalqun min khalqi rabbī khalaqanī min al-surūr alladhī kunta tudkhiluhu ʿalā al-muʾminīn fa-anā asurruka al-yawm` ("I am a creation from the creation of my Lord; He created me from the joy that you used to bring upon the believers, so today I make you happy"). I am a creature among the creatures of my Lord; He fashioned me out of the *surūr*, the joy, that you used to bring into the hearts of the believers — out of the gladness you caused them by your knowledge and your deeds — and so today I am the one who makes you glad. That luminous form, which accompanies him, itself becomes the angel that carries him through all his stations and protects them.

This is secured by God's own decree, that no particle of the believer can stray to any place but its own. Whichever particle has a bond with another draws it, as a magnet draws iron: `like a magnet, each particle pulls all its connected particles, and so they cannot be scattered`. By this magnetism the parts of the believer cannot be dispersed; each is drawn to its kin, and together they progress to their appointed station. This, then, is the reality of *mubashshir* and *bashīr* for the *walī*, the friend: `Fa-dhālika ḥaqīqatu mubashshir wa bashīr lil-walī` ("So that is the reality of *mubashshir* and *bashīr* for the friend").

### The Two Knots and the Dark Magnet: The Reality of Munkar and Nakir

And as for the opposite case — the *aḍdād*, the opponent whose end is not sealed with good — there is the dark counterpart of the Pillar of Light. The good magnet is the *ʿumūd al-nūr*; set against it are the two knots, the `ʿuqdatān`. As the text states: `Wa ammā al-ẓiddu fa-inna al-maghnāṭīs al-khabīth al-muẓlim alladhī huwa al-ʿuqdatān bi-ḥarakat al-maqām lahumā yuḥarrikān al-muhill al-iblīsī wa ʿamalahu al-khabīth alladhī kāna yaʿmaluhu wa yaʿtaqiduhu fī ahl al-ḥaqq wa yukinnuhu lahum min al-ʿadāwah wa yuḍmiru fīhim min al-munāṣabah wa al-bughḍ lahum wa al-aʿmāl al-khabīthah` ("And as for the opposite, indeed the evil, dark magnet, which is the two knots, by the movement of the station for them, they move the satanic dissolver and his evil deed which he used to do and believe regarding the people of truth, and what he used to conceal for them of enmity, and what he used to harbor in them of dispute and hatred, and the evil deeds"). The evil, dark magnet is the *ʿuqdatān*, the two knots, standing in opposition to the single Pillar of Light. Note the asymmetry: one center was made for the good, but two centers were made for the evil. In the Pillar of Light only one is mentioned, never two; but the knots are reckoned as two. The Pillar's chain descends from very high up, from the *kalimah* itself; the *ʿuqdatān*, by contrast, belong to the lower order.

How were the two knots formed, and where? They were formed at the head of the snake, when God made the celestial spheres, the *aflāk*. When the *aflāk* were brought into being, those beings of the world of light who had once gone forward and taken precedence — high beings who then inclined toward satanic nature, who adopted *iblīsiyyat*, being good at first and then turning — these were placed there. For though they had inclined toward Satan, they repented; and on account of that repentance they were not consigned together with the hellfire, the *saqar*, but were set high up, beneath the sphere of the moon. All that pertained to them was placed in the *ʿuqdatān*, and the *ʿuqdatān* themselves were kept aloft by a wisdom — `bi-qaṣdin min al-ʿināyat al-ilāhiyyah`, "by an intention from the divine providence." This is a specific intention of the *ʿāshir*, the tenth, who has only a few such particular intentions, and this placement of the *ʿuqdatān* is among them. So although these are essentially things belonging to *saqar*, they are held high, beneath the lunar sphere, by the *ʿāshir*'s deliberate design within God's providence.

Through the movement of the Imam's station, the decree, the *tadbīr*, of the Imam is in force upon these two knots. The opponents of the Imam, the *aḍdād*, and the evil deeds they wrought — their requital too is granted by him. By the motion of his station, the knots `yuḥarrikān al-muhill al-iblīsī`, set in motion the satanic dissolver, together with the man's satanic knowledge and the evil deed he used to do; they move all that he used to believe and conceal against the *ahl al-ḥaqq* — the enmity he hid for them, the dispute and hatred he harbored in his breast, and his foul deeds. As the Pillar of Light gathers the good for the friend, so the dark magnet gathers the evil for the opponent.

And that dark, disfigured figure is the opponent's own form, acquired bit by bit from his evil over a lifetime: `Wa huwa ṣūratuhu allatī iqtanat min dhālik shayʾan baʿda shayʾin fa-yatakhayyalu lahu fī ākhir daqīqah min ʿumrihi shakhṣan muẓliman mushawwahan ʿalā abhaʾ mā yakūnu ashwahan wa ashbahihi` ("And that is his form, which he acquired from that, bit by bit; and so it is imagined by him, in the last minute of his life, as a dark, disfigured person, in the most ugly and disfigured state possible"). Little by little, out of his evil, this dark form was built up within him; and so, in the last minute of his life, he imagines before him a person dark and deformed, ugly beyond all ugliness. `Fa-yunkiruhu wa yufziʿuhu wa yuqalqiluhu wa yahūluhu thumma yanqaṭiʿu ʿanhu dhālik wa qad ʿalima annahu min ahl al-sharr wa hādhā ḥaqīqat al-Munkar wa al-Nakīr` ("So he rejects it, and it frightens him, and agitates him, and horrifies him; then that is cut off from him, and he has known that he is from the people of evil; and this is the reality of Munkar and Nakir"). He recoils from this figure and denies it; it terrifies him, troubles him, and fills him with horror; then it is severed from him, and he knows in that severing that he is of the *ahl al-sharr*, the people of evil. This is the very reality of *Munkar* and *Nakīr*.

### The Companion Buried Alive: Your Deed Is Your Provision

To this the Messenger of God — blessings of God be upon him and upon his family — alluded in his sermon of counsel to Qays ibn ʿĀṣim, saying: `yā Qays innahu lā budda laka min qarīnin yudfanu maʿaka wa huwa ḥayyun wa tudfanu maʿahu wa anta mayyitun` ("O Qays, indeed it is necessary for you to have a companion who will be buried with you while he is alive, and you will be buried with him while you are dead"). O Qays ibn ʿĀṣim, the Prophet counseled him, you must of necessity have a *qarīn*, a companion, who will be buried together with you — he living, and you dead. That luminous (or, in the evil case, dark) form is this companion; with it the burial is shared, and with the body it shall be raised.

`Fa-in kāna karīman akramaka wa in kāna laʾīman aslamaka` ("If he is noble, he will honor you, and if he is base, he will abandon you"). If this companion is noble, he will be gracious to you; if he is base, *aslamaka* — he will hand you over and forsake you. `Thumma lā yuḥsharu illā maʿaka` ("Then he will not be resurrected except with you") — the resurrection of that form will not be apart from you; with your body and with him together the resurrection takes place. `Wa lā tubʿathu illā maʿahu` ("And you will not be raised except with him") — you shall not be raised save in the company of that very companion. `Wa lā tusʾalu illā ʿanhu` ("And you will not be asked except about him") — and on the Day you will be questioned about nothing but him: why you made that form ugly. `Fa-lā tajʿalhu illā ṣāliḥan` ("So do not make him except righteous") — therefore make him nothing but righteous, by *ʿilm* and by *ʿamal*. Fashion your luminous form to be righteous through knowledge and deed. This form is the *zād-e ākhirat*, the provision for the hereafter — that same luminous form, which is higher than your very self, your *zāt*. Your self stands in its own place; but the knowledge and deed you perform raise up a thing that is higher than the self.

`Idhā kāna ṣāliḥan lam taʾnis illā bihi wa idhā kāna fāḥishan lam tawḥash illā minhu wa huwa ʿamaluka` ("If he is righteous, you will not find comfort except in him, and if he is obscene, you will not feel terror except from him, and he is your deed"). If the companion is righteous, you will find your solace in no one but him; and if he is obscene, you will dread no one but him, recoiling in terror from him. For he is your deed, your *ʿamal* — the very substance of what you did made manifest as a companion who comforts or torments.

To the contrary side of this, the dark side, Sayyidnā Ḥamīduddīn alluded — may God rectify his reward — in the third *mashrab* of the fourth *taṣawwur* of *Rāḥat al-ʿAql*, where the opposite is set forth: `Wa bihī ramaza Sayyidunā Ḥamīduddīn qaddasa Allāhu sawwābahu fī al-mashrab al-thālith min taṣawwur al-rābiʿ min Rāḥat al-ʿAql ḥaythu dhikru al-ẓidd, fa-qāla wa tafsīru aʿmālihi al-sayyiʾah ḥayawānan muʾdhiyan yuʾdhīhi wa yurawwiʿuhu` ("And with this our master Ḥamīduddīn, may God rectify his reward, alluded in the third *mashrab* of the fourth *taṣawwur* of *Rāḥat al-ʿAql*, where the opposite is mentioned; so he said: and the interpretation of his evil deeds is a harmful animal that harms him and terrifies him"). The interpretation, the *tafsīr*, of a man's evil deeds takes the shape of a harmful animal, a beast that does him injury and casts him into fear; his foul deeds become for him a noxious creature that harms and affrights him.

The sayings of Sayyidnā Ḥamīduddīn are exceedingly profound — *mughlaq*, sealed and difficult. *Rāḥat al-ʿAql* is a book of such depth that it has been called the "Comfort of the Intellect" only by those who can enter it. Concerning this there is the account of Sayyidnā Ghulām Ḥusayn: he was studying *Rāḥat al-ʿAql* when the Nawab of Rāmpur, a learned scholar of Arabic, came to him and asked, "Mawlānā, what book are you studying?" He answered, "It is a book, *Rāḥat al-ʿAql*." The Nawab asked to see it, and looked into it for fifteen or twenty minutes, and then said, "I understand nothing of this; what book is this?" To which the reply came: "The book is *Rāḥat al-ʿAql*, the Comfort of the Intellect; but for you it is *Kulfat al-ʿAql*, the Agony of the Intellect." So profound is that work that an accomplished Arabist could make nothing of it — which only confirms how far the inner sciences lie beyond the reach of the merely outward learning.

### The Dark Form and Its Wandering: How the Soul Acquires Materiality

We turn now to the nature of this dark form and what becomes of it after death. The dark form is the one that separates from the souls of the bodies and from their forms — `al-jāʾilatu fī al-hawāʾ`, "the one that roams in the air" — and into the vile places, the dirty places, in turns; and it flows toward the *barzakh*, which is dark and vile, which is the *ʿuqdatān*, the two knots. As the saying concerning it has already preceded, these places named here are visited by it in succession: the empty valleys and the terrifying wells. And these are the blameworthy jinn, who are punished there with what they deserve of the kinds of subtle punishment, each according to his measure.

How is this dark form constituted? It is formed from the knowledge and the deeds that such people did in opposition to the commands of God — knowledge they acquired from persons from whom knowledge ought never to be taken, and the enmity they nursed against the *ahl al-ḥaqq*, the people of truth. This is the description of their *ṣūrat-e-ẓulmāniyyah*, their dark and shadowy form: it is compounded of forbidden knowledge wrongly sought and of hatred wrongly harbored against the people of truth.

This dark form departs from the souls of the bodies and from the bodies themselves. And here lies a crucial division. The form separates off, and its station is the *ʿuqdatayn*, the two knots. But the soul, the *nafs*, does not progress with the form. Instead, the body expands in the grave, and the soul expands within its body; rather than rising and traveling with the form, the *nafs* spreads itself through the body, becomes material, and acquires materiality — it grows more material than before. This is precisely the opposite of what befalls the souls of the believers, whose souls progress upward together with the form. This is the great difference between the two destinies: the believer's soul ascends with its luminous form, while the opponent's soul thickens into matter and is left behind, its form alone going off to the knots.

The dark form, then, is the one that roams — *al-jāʾilah*, the one that does *jawlānī*, that wanders. It wanders in the air at times, and at times in the vile places. The vile places have been specified: the latrine, and every place where blood flows — for the eating and drinking of blood has been made forbidden to us, and these forms are inclined toward what is forbidden — and the places where wine is made and where filthy, unlawful things are produced. There they roam, because they have an inclination toward such things, drawn to the forbidden by their own corrupt nature.

And in deep, wide wells at times they descend, until they reach toward the *barzakh*, which is dark and vile — *sāʾilatin ilā al-barzakh* — the one reaching toward the *barzakh* `which is dark, which is vile, even as it is the ʿuqdatān`. What is meant by *barzakh*? The *barzakh* is the path that lies between paradise and hell, between *jannat* and *jahannum*. Now where is the hell of these dark forms? It is the *sakhrah*, the great rock. Before they reach the *sakhrah*, the station in which they are held is the *ʿuqdatayn*; the forms first arrive at the *ʿuqdatayn*. Their final destination is the *sakhrah*; the *barzakh* leading to it is the *ʿuqdatayn*. It is a quarantine station, a place built for a short stay — that is what the *ʿuqdatayn* is.

Is the *ʿuqdatayn* in the *barzakh* or in this world? For the punished, the *muʿadhdhabīn* — those who are yet to be punished, whose future chastisement is still to be administered — the *ʿuqdatayn* is the *barzakh*. They are held in the *ʿuqdatayn*, which lies beneath the sphere of the moon. The *barzakh* signifies a place of brief residence; their final destination is the *sakhrah*, where they shall remain until the *kawr-e-aʿẓam*, the Great Cycle. And how great is the *kawr-e-aʿẓam*? It is four hundred thousand cycles, four hundred thousand *dawr*, in each of which a single *dawr* is itself four hundred thousand years. That is their true and final station, where they must come; and before coming there, their forms abide in the *ʿuqdatayn*.

Do they pass through all these places before going to the *ʿuqdatayn*? Yes — they pass through all of them in turn. Sometimes in the air, sometimes in the vile places, sometimes in the others, just as has been said; and after passing through all these places, they go toward the *barzakh*, which is the *ʿuqdatān*. As the saying concerning it was established before, they come in turns to these mentioned places, one after another, the forms passing through these places one by one — and through the empty habitations, and the terrifying wells. The word *bīr* means a well, and *muhawwishah* means terrifying wells, the *bīr muhawwishah*. And these are the blameworthy jinn, who are given a subtle, light punishment in the midst of that passage, according to what they deserved — even as Mawlā ʿAlī punished them, when they passed through various stations of chastisement, each according to his measure, every person according to his capacity.

### The Imam's Call to the Punished Jinn

It is necessary that every Imam, in his own time, call these punished jinn to Islam. For the Imam — peace be upon him — knows them, by their names and by their places. So it was in the incident reported of Sayyidnā ʿAlī, which is told with great detail: he called each one of them by name, declaring "You are of this name, you are of this name," and nothing of their affair was hidden from him — not a single thing of their condition lay concealed from his knowledge.

Then, whoever among them completes what is upon him of the lower punishment, the *ʿadhāb al-adnā*, and is granted permission for deliverance, submits to the one who calls him, repents and turns back, and becomes one of the praiseworthy jinn, the *jinn al-maḥmūdīn*. When the man's reckoning of the lower punishment is discharged — when the give-and-take of that chastisement is finished — permission is given him for deliverance, and he brings Islam, *aslama*, submitting to the one who, at that station, invited him; he repents, he turns back, and so he passes from the blameworthy jinn, the *jinn-e-madhmūmīn*, to the praiseworthy jinn, the *jinn-e-maḥmūdīn*. The blameworthy jinn was, before, one whose end was not good; but when the little that remained upon him is purified and he accepts the station of the *dāʿī*, the inviter, he becomes one of the *jinn al-maḥmūdīn*.

Upon this transformation, he ascends — climbing from the side of the moon up to the sphere of the sun — and there he serves as a servant, a *khādim*, among the *mustaqdimīn*, in a glorious station. Their station is that of the *mustaqdimīn*, those who go in front and are advanced.

### The Adhan of the Saints and the Jinn-e-Mustaqdimin

That the *jinn-e-mustaqdimīn* respond to the call to prayer, the *adhān*, is attested by Sayyidnā ʿAlī bin Sulaymān, who recounted the following. Some of the companions had gone to perform *ziyārat* at the shrine of Sayyidnā Ḥasan Pīr. Returning from there, they lost their way and wandered without direction; Sayyidnā Ḥasan Khān was among them. As they wandered, ʿAlī sahib saw a crow coming from a distance, cawing as it approached. He told Sayyidnā Ḥasan Khān to climb the small hill — or a tree — and give the *adhān*. He went up and gave the *adhān*, and at the giving of it a crow came cawing from afar. Then ʿAlī sahib said: this is one of the *jinn-e-mustaqdimīn*; follow him, and we will reach a village. They walked along its path and came to the village where the people were settled. So in this account the *jinn-e-mustaqdimīn* are especially set forth.

Of the same order are the wonders worked through the *adhān* of the saints. When a river once flooded and the water rose to a man's full height, Hazrat Fatḥullāh — the sanctified master — climbed up and gave the *adhān*, and at its giving the water slowly, slowly began to subside. In the same way, in Bombay, Hazrat Isḥāq — the sanctified master — climbed upon the Jamani building and gave the *adhān* when a fire had broken out behind, and Hazrat Naṣrullāh — the sanctified master — gave the *adhān* when a fire had broken out, and it was quelled. And in our own time, when we had gone for Hajj, a glorious instance occurred: the tent in which we were staying, and before it at a distance, a gas cylinder burst, and the whole field of ʿArafāt was set ablaze. There was a stampede, and the fire was coming toward us. We came out, leaving the tent behind, and gave the *adhān* — gave the *adhān* in a loud voice — and at the giving of it the direction of the wind changed. The wind turned and went toward the mountains; and those who had fled to the mountains were caught in it there, having climbed up in great numbers, while we who had given the *adhān* were delivered, some reaching Makkah and some other places. These are signs of how the *adhān* of the people of truth turns back flood, fire, and wind.

So the praiseworthy jinn remains in service as a *khādim*; he becomes one of the company of the ministering angels, the *malāʾikah*, appointed for that office, in the circle of the sun, among the *mustaqdimīn* whose center is the sun. They are entrusted with many affairs there. And how many are they? The *mustaqdimīn* — the *suwarāt-e-mustaqdimīn* — number one hundred and forty thousand, the entrusted ones, ministering angels centered in the sun.

Among these one hundred and forty thousand are those who attract, the *jādhibah*: their office is to draw upward the praiseworthy forms, the *suwar al-maḥmūdah*, the good forms that ascend — to attract them is their work. And among them are those who repel or send down, the *dāfiʿah*: their office is to send down to the world those forms that are to descend. And among them are those who hold or restrain, the *māsikah*: their office is for what travels within. Thus there are *jādhibah*, the attractors; *dāfiʿah*, the repellers; and *māsikah*, the restrainers. For whenever one ascends from there, another is brought down from there in his place; as one rises, in his stead another descends. And the one who descends does not necessarily go to the *sakhrah*; rather he comes to the world for service, to be born a believer. So when one of the one hundred and forty thousand in the sun descends, he descends to the world, in a good state, that he may be born a believer and so himself begin to ascend and progress — his service now complete. Thus it is, forever and ever, in this way, perpetually and without end: as one ascends another descends to be born a believer, the cycle never ceasing.

Here a caution against confusion must be sounded. How many are these forms? One hundred and forty thousand — the *mustaqdimīn*. But how many prophets, *anbiyāʾ*, did the Prophet say there are? One hundred and twenty-four thousand. These are two separate counts, and they must not be run together. The one hundred and forty thousand are the *mustaqdimīn*, the *suwarāt-e-mustaqdimīn*; the one hundred and twenty-four thousand are the prophets. They are wholly distinct things, set apart precisely so that there be no confusion between them.

### The Mustakhdamin: Service, Descent, and Swift Ascent

We come now to the second chapter, *al-bāb al-thānī*, of the fourth path, which contains five sections, *fuṣūl*; and from it, the first section. Whoever among the *mustakhdamīn* — those who are made to serve — completes what is upon him of service there is brought down from the sun to the earth, and travels into a thing from among the edibles and the drinkables, the *maʾkūlāt* and the *mashrūbāt*. The word *mustakhdam* must be understood: *khadama* means "he served," and *istakhdama* means "he sought to make serve" — so a *mustakhdam* is one for whom service has been arranged, one made to serve; its plural is *mustakhdamīn*. When the *mustakhdam* has completed the service obligatory upon him there, he is brought down toward the earth and reaches a thing from the edibles and drinkables, taking from it the sustenance suited to him — the sustenance he is worthy to receive.

Then he appears at the moment of union, the *mulāqah* — between the seed of the *dāʿī*, the inviter, and his wife — as a trustee, a *ḍāmin*. As the text gives it: he is `absent near the mulāqah and appears near the mulāqah, between the nutfah of the dāʿī and his wife as a trustee`, and he progresses, *tarqiyatan*, through the stages of the fetus, the *janīn*, until he appears as a newborn, a *mawlūd*, a complete and whole person; and he is reared until he reaches puberty, *bulūgh*, until he becomes *bāligh*. Then he is called, and he responds — he is called, and he accepts — and he enters the circle of the people of truth, the *ahl al-ḥaqq*. He may be born to anyone, to this man or to that, born of a Zayd or of a Zayd; but he will come, in the end, to the people of truth.

And because his purification has been accomplished and his service accepted, he progresses through the religious ranks, the *marātib al-dīniyyah*, and traverses them in the swiftest of all swift ways and the nearest of all near ways. He completes the ranks; and even faster than the swift, his progress is made, so that in the shortest and nearest span he becomes one of the people of high rank and lofty stations, the *manāzil al-sāmiyah*. And there is no return upon him; he never has to come back again. He never returns `because he has fulfilled what was upon him of sins, and he repented and turned back, and he accepted the dāʿī Allāh, the caller of God, in the beginning and in the end`. In the beginning, in the *ʿālam-e-nūrānī*, the world of light, he accepted the covenant, the *qawl*; and here too his acceptance has taken place — for after being given the lower punishment he was called, and he came among the people of truth. So these two are the beginning and the end: he was made to serve, he served, and he accepted. And when he comes now, he comes with a clean slate for so many days, and the reward of that is that he is granted the form, granted sharpness of mind, granted the gift of acceptance.

His swift progress is owing to this: that he rendered what he was tasked with of service, and stood firm by what was obligatory upon him, and entered the sacred sanctuary, the *ḥaram-e-amīn*, and obeyed the Lord of the worlds, *Rabb al-ʿālamīn*. This is why such speed came into him — because he discharged his service, kept his obligation, entered the secure sanctuary, and obeyed the Lord of the worlds.

### The Realm of Free Choice: Halal and Haram Among the Arrivals

Upon the platform of this world, various kinds of people are gathered together. There are those who are bound toward punishment, whose former states are such that chastisement is owed to them; and there are those who arrive with a clean slate, who have come fresh, now in the realm of free choice, where it rests with them whether to accept or not. Both kinds are present here.

It is as was told before concerning the animals: all are animals, yet among them some are *ḥalāl*, lawful, and some are *ḥarām*, forbidden. The *ḥalāl* are those who will progress; the *ḥarām* are those who will descend. And even within the *ḥalāl* there is a further division: one kind of *ḥalāl* will become food for a believer, and another kind of *ḥalāl* will become food for a disbeliever, a *kāfir*. So upon the platform of this world every kind has come, and here too the *mustakhdamīn* have come and are presented with the call. When the call is presented to a man who stands in the state of full choice, the choice is his whether to walk the straight path or the crooked path, and no one can pull him away from his choice. In the realm of free choice, none may interfere: the call is set before him, and he chooses for himself which way he will go.

### From the Knots to the Sakhrah: The Two Punishments

These dark forms, the *suwarāt-e-ẓulmah*, can progress only by becoming *mustakhdam*. There is no other road for them. Held in the *ʿuqdatayn*, this is the path appointed for them; in the form of the *suwarāt-e-mustakhdamīn* their progress at last takes place. Those who go into the lower punishment, the *ʿadhāb-e-adnā*, progress only by becoming *suwarāt-e-mustakhdamīn*: they complete the lower punishment, they serve, and then they are presented with the call; if they accept the call, they become *mustakhdam*, and their services are taken.

Let the sequence be made plain. The person whose end is not good — his form goes to the *ʿuqdatayn*, while his body goes into the lower punishment. The *ʿuqdatayn* is where the cursed forms, the *ṣurat-e-laʿīnah*, are seated; this is the station of the *suwarāt-e-laʿīnah*. Their final station is the *sakhrah*, and the *barzakh* leading to it is the *ʿuqdatayn*. Their bodies appear in the world and there undergo the lower punishment; and when they have completed the lower punishment and the time comes for them to return — before the *sakhrah* — they are presented with the call. If they accept the call, their form moves from there toward *istikhdām*, service. And after performing this service, when they come again into the world, here too the call is presented to them: if they accept, they go upon the straight path; if they do not accept, they go toward the punishment of *adnā*. And the purification of the bodies is necessary, for without it the meeting cannot take place; this bodily purification comes about through the *ḥalāl* animals.

How long do they remain in the service of the *ṣurat-e-mustakhdamīn*? As has been told, they remain until the rising of the Qāʾim, the *qiyām-e-qāʾim* — peace be upon him. Until the Qāʾim stands, they remain there. Then, for the accounting, all shall be present, and the reckoning shall take place; and from the sky a fire shall descend that will burn the bodies of these enemies. Through the mouth of the *sakhrah* they shall enter the *sakhrah*, and there they shall remain for one *kawr* — and that is the greater punishment, the *ʿadhāb-e-akbar*. At the time of the Qāʾim — peace be upon him — there you shall confront them and fight them; their bodies shall be burned by fire, and the fire shall burn their forms as well, and they shall pass through the mouth of the *sakhrah* and remain there for one *kawr*; this is the *ʿadhāb-e-akbar*.

The lower punishment, the *ʿadhāb-e-adnā*, has two forms. One is the punishment of the seven levels of hell, the *adrak*; the other, its final form, is the punishment of the peripheries, the *aṭrāf*. After they are gathered up from the punishment of the peripheries, at the time of the Qāʾim, the events just described come to pass. So the lower punishment is of two types — the punishment of the *adrak* and the punishment of the *aṭrāf* — while the great punishment, the *ʿadhāb-e-akbar*, is the punishment of the *sakhrah*.

And whoever refuses, and is arrogant, and persists in his disobedience and stubbornness and his disbelief, is made to tread, *sulika bihi*, the bridges, the *qanāṭir*, of the lower bodily punishment, the *ʿadhāb al-adnā al-jismānī*, until he completes it and the Qāʾim stands. If he does not complete it, the process is repeated; the state of choice must come again, that he have the choice to go this way or that. And when the Qāʾim stands — upon his mention be peace — he is made to tread the greater punishment, the *ʿadhāb al-akbar*.

### The Halt at Every Transition and the Mercy of the Lower Punishment

To this Sayyidnā Zuʿayb alluded in his *Risālat al-Nafs*, the Treatise on the Soul, where he said: `wa lahum fī kulli intiqālin yantaqilūnahu tawqīfun, fa-in tābū rajaʿū ilā martabatin hiya aʿlā wa in aṣarrū hawū ilā adnā martabatin ilā al-kawr al-aʿẓam alladhī huwa ḥāṣilu ḍarbi thalāthi miʾati alfin wa sittīna alfan fī mithlihā` ("And for them, in every transition they make, there is a halt; if they repent, they return to a higher rank, and if they insist, they descend to the lowest rank, to the Great Cycle, which is the product of three hundred and sixty thousand multiplied by its like"). For these people, in every transition into which they pass, there is a *tawqīf*, a halt; they are made to stop. If at that halt they repent, they return to a rank higher than the one they held; and if they persist obstinately, they are sent down to the lowest rank, down to the *kawr-e-aʿẓam*, the Great Cycle, which is the product of three hundred and sixty thousand multiplied by its like — three hundred and sixty thousand times three hundred and sixty thousand. And there is, in this, a halting of those from whom return is yet hoped for before the completion of the *kawr* — `fī tawqīfi man yurjaʿu minhu qabla tamām al-kawr`, the staying of the one whose return is awaited before the cycle is finished.

To this God Himself bears witness, for He said: `wa-la-nudhīqannahum min al-ʿadhāb al-adnā dūna al-ʿadhāb al-akbar laʿallahum yarjiʿūn` ("And We will surely make them taste the lower punishment, short of the greater punishment, that perhaps they may return"). We shall make them taste the lower punishment, which is less than the greater, *so that they may return*. God has declared that in the lower punishment they are granted several opportunities to return; this is a mercy of God — the lower chastisement is itself an open door, offered again and again, that the soul may yet turn back before the final cycle closes upon it.

To this very matter Amīr al-Muʾminīn — blessings of God be upon him — indicated in his saying: `wa yaṭḥanuhum al-dawru fī madārij al-kawr` ("and the cycle grinds them in the stages of the cycle"). The *dawr* grinds them, crushing them in the *madārij*, the graded stages, of the *kawr*. They are ground down; and after being ground, the cycle runs again. In every cycle they are presented with the call: if they return, the lower punishment is theirs and the door of return stands open; but if they reach the *sakhrah*, then from there the lava comes out of it — molten fire issues from the *sakhrah* upon those who have come to its end.

### The Distinguished Days and the White Nights

Finally, a word concerning the sacred reckoning of days, for this teaching was given on such a day. The white days, the *ayyām-e-bīḍ*, are the thirteenth, the fourteenth, and the fifteenth of the month — the middle days, and the best of days. We possess a narration, the narration of Lakdhārī, transmitted to Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq, in which the question is raised: how many days are there in a year? Three hundred and fifty-four. And of these three hundred and fifty-four days, how many are distinguished, set apart in excellence? About one hundred and fifty are distinguished days — drawn especially from certain sacred months, beginning with the first of Muḥarram and continuing through the appointed days of the year. Among the very best of these stand the white days, the thirteenth, fourteenth, and fifteenth, when the moon is full and its light is whole.
