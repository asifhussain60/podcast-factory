# Ascent, Decline, and a God

## Where this episode picks up

Visual metaphor, one origin point, two opposite vectors.

Shared origin: a single platform of flesh and blood, born into one world.
- Vector up: rising toward Allah, drawing near the first principle.
- Vector down: sinking below the level of cattle, driven from the first principle.

Reading: identical composition is no guarantee of identical destiny. From one starting point the two set out in opposite directions, and the whole difference turns on a single thing. The contrast runs through the world of men, then the world of animals, and carries the seeker into the bewilderment that begins his ascent.

Annotated structure, what the platform inherits from the prior teaching.
- Prior lesson (metaphysical): the Mukathir's substance is refined though he looks like any other man; he becomes a house in which revelation alights.
- This lesson (moral): the refined man is set beside his exact outward double, and the shared composition becomes the stage for a moral drama.

## Two men, one platform

Contrast pair, the Mukathir against the man who is not a Mukathir, attribute by attribute.

Column A: The Mukathir
- Identity of the other party: the refined man already described.
- Temperament: refined.
- Essence: pure.
- Trajectory: ascent, drawing near the first principle.
- Destination: rising toward the spiritual world.

Column B: The one who is not a Mukathir
- Identity: the ordinary person, or the opponent, the "other than him."
- Temperament: unrefined.
- Essence: impure.
- Trajectory: decline, distance from the first principle.
- Destination: pushed back toward the last of the human ranks, below it.

Shared row: Both stand outwardly in the very same state, on the very same platform. Both are of flesh and blood, both inhabit the same human form, both have come into the same world.

Visual metaphor, two men leaving one doorstep.

The image of the parting:
- Same point of departure: gathered together on a single platform.
- One goes to the cinema.
- One goes to the mosque.

Reading: the parting of two eternal destinies looks, from the outside, like nothing more than two men leaving the same doorstep in different directions. Nothing in the platform, nothing in the shared flesh, tells you which man will rise and which will fall. The difference lies not in what they are made of but in which way they have turned.

Contrast pair, what the shared platform means, refused reading against true reading.

Column A: The platform as comfort (refused)
- Claim: a shared low starting line excuses the man who fell.
- Plea offered: "I was made of baser stuff."
- Plea offered: "I was set upon a lower platform than the man who rose."

Column B: The platform as summons (the teaching)
- Claim: the common starting line makes each man fully answerable for his direction.
- Ground: same materials, same opportunity, same point of departure.
- Verdict: what is not common is the turning, and it is the turning, not the stuff, that decides where a man ends.

## The parting of prayer

Process flow, the single hinge and the two destinies that swing on it.

Hinge: the establishing of prayer, taken in its full inner sense.
- The inward prayer is the standing-up to enact the command.

Branch up (establishes the inward prayer):
- The Mukathir ascends.

Branch down (establishes neither the inward prayer nor even its outward form):
- Proceeds away from the first principle.
- Declines toward the last of the human ranks.
- Is pushed back beneath even the lowest of them.

Reading: the hinge of two destinies is this single act. To establish it is to ascend; to let it fall is to descend.

The Prophetic tradition draws the same line from the outward side:

> Between a man and disbelief and idolatry stands the abandonment of prayer. Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings upon him, narrated by Jabir ibn Abdullah, Sahih Muslim, Kitab al-Iman, no. 82.

Contrast pair, the same frontier seen from two sides.

Column A: The hadith (outward side)
- Marks abandonment of prayer as the boundary line of faith itself.
- To let prayer fall is to step across the frontier separating a believer from one who has lost his faith.

Column B: The teaching (inner depth)
- The prayer whose abandonment is so grave is, in its reality, the standing-up to enact the command.
- The man who lets it fall loses not merely a ritual but the very motion by which a human being ascends.

Process flow, the descent pressed to its terminus.

Start: failure to establish prayer.
- Pushed below the thousand-fold form.
- Becomes more astray in his path than the cattle.
- Turned onto the crooked path.
- Ends at the isthmus of fear.

The Qur'an passes the same judgment in almost the same image:

> They have hearts with which they understand not; they have eyes with which they see not; and they have ears with which they hear not. Such are like cattle. Nay, they are even further astray. It is they who are heedless. Quran 7:179 (the chapter of the Heights), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., The Study Quran.

Annotated structure, why the heedless man sinks below the beast.
- The equipment: heart, eyes, ears, the outward apparatus of a human being.
- The failure: not used to understand, to see, to hear the command.
- The result: sinks below the beasts, for the beast at least fulfils what it was made for.

Note: the master breaks off into prayer that Allah keep us, and all our brethren, far from such a matter, for the sake of the master of the prophets, upon him and his pure progeny be peace and blessings.

Comparison summary, the stark lesson restated as two outcomes from one stuff.
- Same flesh, same blood, same world.
- Mukathir: establishes the inward prayer, becomes a Mukathir who ascends.
- The other: establishes neither inward nor even outward prayer; temperament and deeds harden him; descends below the beasts.
- The verdict: to be made of human stuff is not yet to be safe. The human form is a platform from which one may climb to the spiritual world or fall beneath the animals, and the establishing of prayer decides the direction.

## The lesson of the animals

The teaching reads the same pattern in the animal kingdom, drawing a proof from it:

> And truly in the cattle there is a lesson for you. Quran 16:66 (the chapter of the Bee), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., The Study Quran.

Annotated structure, the proof named.
- The whole: the animal forms.
- The lesson: an ibrah, a crossing-over lesson, for one who takes a lesson.
- The faculty: insight for one who reflects and contemplates.

Contrast pair, lawful animals against forbidden predatory animals.

Column A: The lawful animals (cow, sheep, camel, and their kind)
- Outward creation: flesh, blood, bones, nerves, veins, skin, hide, fur.
- Relation to man: take intimacy, draw near, become his food.
- Through man: become part of the human who is the caliph of Allah upon His earth.
- Trajectory: ascent and progress upon the straight path.

Column B: The forbidden predatory animals
- Outward creation: flesh, blood, bones, nerves, veins, skin, hide, fur.
- Relation to man: averse to him, hostile to him.
- Through man: do not draw near to be his food, do not ascend through him.
- Trajectory: decline, moving ever farther from the first principle.

Shared row: Both are of one and the same created stuff. In their physical composition there is no difference between them. The proof of their difference lies in their relation to man.

Annotated structure, what the straight path is here.
- The straight path: the human person himself.
- Why: the human form is close to the first principle, which is our origin and our destination.
- The lawful animal, eaten and assimilated into the caliph of Allah, ascends through him toward the source.

Comparison matrix, the animal pattern mapped onto the human pattern.

Rows: Lawful animal | Predatory animal | Obedient man | Disobedient man
Columns: Relation to the first principle | Direction | Human parallel | Distance

- Lawful animal: draws near through intimacy with man | ascends | like the Mukathir | within the shared interval
- Predatory animal: flees man, hostile | declines | like the man who falls | within the shared interval
- Obedient man: draws near directly | ascends | the Mukathir himself | separated by a distance greater than between the two beasts
- Disobedient man: turns away | declines | the falling man himself | separated by a distance greater than between the two beasts

Reading: although the two kinds of animal are of one created stuff, they are not equal, and just so it is with men. Among human beings the obedient and the disobedient are separated by a distance greater even than that which divides the two kinds of beast. One rises and one descends, and between them lies the whole interval between the first principle and the furthest distance from it.

Note: with this the fifth chapter of the first gate is brought to its close.

## The transmuted soul

The second gate opens with a closer examination of the soul of the Mukathir: how it became transformed and what flowed into it as a result.

Contrast pair, the sensory soul, ordinary against transmuted.

Column A: The sensory soul of the ordinary person
- Faculty: the soul of perception, possessed by everyone.
- Condition: unrefined.

Column B: The sensory soul of the Mukathir
- Faculty: the same soul of perception.
- Condition: itself become refined, because of all that he acquired in his ascent to that rank.

Shared row: Both possess the sensory soul of perception, yet the two are not the same.

Process flow, the ascent that refined the soul, then the inflow that transmuted it.

Stage 1: passes through the ten stations of the Mustajib.
- then Stage 2: passes through the ten stations of the Mu'min.
- then Stage 3: passes through the degrees of the Mukathir to whatever level he reached.
- result: from all this his very soul took on a subtlety.

Inflow upon the refined soul: the divine sciences and the spiritual secrets connect, and they
- perfect it with a total purity,
- refine it with a spiritual refinement,
- dye it with a divine dye,
- make it shine, clarified and transparent.

End state: a man possessed of a holy spirit, his sensory soul itself transmuted.

Contrast pair, the two faces of one transformation.

Column A: Outward face (already described)
- The Mukathir's flesh was refined though it looked unchanged.

Column B: Inward face (described here)
- His very soul of perception is dyed and made luminous by the divine sciences flowing into it.

Shared row: The two go together. A man who climbed through all those stations and held the command before him does not acquire knowledge as an external possession; the knowledge enters and remakes the soul that receives it, until the soul itself shines with the light of what it knows.

The Father of Imams named knowledge as the soul's life, not its ornament:

> Knowledge is the life of the hearts and the light of the sight. Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Father of Imams, Ghurar al-Hikam wa Durar al-Kalim (compiled by al-Amidi), among the maxims on knowledge.

Reading: if knowledge is the life of the heart, the soul into which the divine sciences flow is not merely informed but quickened, brought to a higher life and lit from within. To receive the divine sciences is not to be furnished with a possession but to be made newly alive.

## Where purity is real

Named-axis 2x2, appearance of the soul against the reality of the rank.

Axes:
- Horizontal: the soul's appearance (appears refined on the left, the question of true purity on the right).
- Vertical: who may judge (we may read it off on top, the station rests with Allah on the bottom).

Quadrants:
- Top-left (appears refined, we may judge): the credulous error. Crowning every impressive soul with a public title. Refused.
- Bottom-left (appears refined, judgment is Allah's): the honest limit. Outwardly it may appear he is a Mukathir, his soul indeed refined, yet the formal designation belongs to Allah; we cannot pin a public title.
- Top-right (genuinely pure, we may judge): not ours to confer. The rank is not read off the surface and announced by us.
- Bottom-right (genuinely pure, judgment is Allah's): the demand of justice. If a soul has genuinely become pure, the person must in fact have become a Mukathir, having passed the ten stations of the Mu'min and reached the station of the Mukathir; the subtlety is the consequence of that ascent, present and real though concealed from all but Allah.

Contrast pair, the two errors the teaching refuses, and its single resolution.

Column A: Credulity (refused)
- Move: crown every impressive soul with a public title.
- Fault: the outward signs can be misread, and the station is not ours to confer.

Column B: Cynicism (refused)
- Move: treat the ranks as bare names with no reality answering to them.
- Fault: as though "Mukathir" were merely an honorific bestowed by men.

Shared row (the resolution): the rank is a real fact about a real soul, grounded in a real ascent; only the fact is hidden, lodged in the knowledge of Allah rather than displayed for our verdict. The seeker is taught neither to appoint ranks nor to doubt that ranks exist, but to trust that where a soul has genuinely been purified, a genuine station stands behind the purity, whether or not any human being is permitted to name it.

## The birth of a god

We reach the heart of the chapter: the precise sense in which a higher rank becomes a "god" for the one beneath him.

Process flow, how the teacher becomes a "god" in the disciple's experience.

Start: a seeker comes to the Mukathir with sincerity, soundness of intention, and purity of nature, and converses with him.
- then: the Mukathir bestows the spiritual speech and the divine knowledge, knowledge so sublime it had never crossed the seeker's heart, nor had his ears or the ears of his likes ever suspected it.
- then: confronted with such elevated realities, the disciple's intellect is bewildered and astounded; he cannot fathom how his teacher attained this station.
- end: in this bewilderment the Mukathir becomes for him a god, a master of his neck, and the seeker recognizes his guidance and great precedence.

Contrast pair, the two meanings of the word "god."

Column A: God as origination (denied)
- Sense: the creator of beings, the power of origination.
- Status: negated from all the ranks at the very outset of the chapter.

Column B: God as center of the circle (affirmed, in this sense alone)
- Sense: the center around which the bewildered seeker circles.
- Sense: the master who holds the seeker's neck for its release.
- Sense: the one whose guidance, greatness, and precedence the seeker has come to recognize.
- Status: the second meaning of the word, deferred from the opening of the chapter, now supplied.

Annotated structure, the circle and its center.
- The whole: a circle in motion.
- The center: the teacher, the Mukathir.
- The revolving: the very motion of bewilderment.
- The orbit: the disciple, drawn into orbit by his astonishment, revolving around his teacher as a circle revolves around its center.
- The limit: it is in this sense alone that the Mukathir is a "god," emphatically not as the creator of beings.

Reading: the word, so carefully qualified, is not a lapse into associating partners with Allah. It is the name for a real relation between a dazzled student and the teacher whose knowledge has overwhelmed him, and it is the first rung of a ladder that ascends, rank by rank, toward the One.

That the seeker should need such a master is the settled wisdom of the path:

> Whoever travels without a guide needs two hundred years for a two-day journey. Rumi, Mathnawi, Book III, trans. Reynold A. Nicholson.

Contrast pair, the bewilderment read two ways.

Column A: Bewilderment as snare (rejected)
- Reading: a danger to be stamped out, an error in the seeker.

Column B: Bewilderment as mercy (the teaching)
- Reading: it binds the dazzled seeker to the one who can carry him across in two days the distance he would otherwise wander for two centuries.
- The only danger: that he mistake the guide for the goal, against which the teaching has already set its guard by fixing the limited sense of "god" and reserving origination to Allah alone.

Annotated structure, why the teaching disciplines the dangerous word rather than banning it.
- The tension: to call any creature a "god" is, on its face, the very thing tanzih forbade, the placing of divinity upon a rank.
- The method: the teaching does not flinch from the word; it disciplines it, fixing its meaning as center of a turning circle, master of the seeker's neck, object of his bewildered orbit, and not the creator of his being.
- The point: the bewilderment that makes a teacher into a "god" is not an error to be stamped out but an experience to be understood and then surpassed; the seeker really does experience his teacher this way, and pretending otherwise would falsify the path.
- The remedy: not to deny the bewilderment but to follow it upward until it discloses the One before whom even the highest teacher is himself bewildered.

## What this teaching lands

Comparison matrix, the four threads of the chapter, gathered.

Rows: Two men | The animals | The transmuted soul | The birth of a god
Columns: Shared ground | The dividing factor | What it lands

- Two men: identical flesh on one platform | the establishing of prayer in its inner sense | one ascends toward the first principle, the other is driven below the cattle.
- The animals: one created stuff | nearness to man or aversion to him | lawful and forbidden creatures part ways; the obedient and disobedient among men are separated by a still greater distance.
- The transmuted soul: the sensory soul shared by all | the divine sciences flowing in | the Mukathir's soul dyed luminous; where purity is genuine, a true rank is genuinely present, though hidden with Allah.
- The birth of a god: the bewildered disciple | the overwhelming knowledge of the teacher | the teacher becomes a "god," not a creator but the center of the circle around which astonishment turns.

Genealogy chain, the ladder the bewilderment is to climb.

Seeker bewildered before his teacher → the teacher, himself a rank, bewildered before the rank above → rank above rank, ascending → the First Intellect bewildered before its Originator → Allah alone.

Reading: from the disciple's bewilderment the seeker is to be carried upward, rank by rank. By negating divinity from every rank and affirming it for Allah alone, he performs the true act of Tawhid.
