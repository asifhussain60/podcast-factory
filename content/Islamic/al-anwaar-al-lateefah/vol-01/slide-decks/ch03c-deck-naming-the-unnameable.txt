# Naming the Unnameable

## Where this episode picks up

Annotated structure, the unresolved paradox at the threshold.

Whole: a paradox left standing at the start of the teaching.
Parts:
- Right to name: if you cannot know a thing at all, by what right do you give it a name?
- Cost of naming: and what does that naming cost you?
- The misfire: we say "Allah," yet the word lands not on the Originator but on the first thing He made.
- The common condition: the bewilderment behind that word is the shared state of everything created.

Process flow, the thread this episode follows from threshold to resolution.

Start: the paradox pressed to its root, how the First Intellect, which cannot comprehend its Maker, became the first to speak of Him.
1. The primal negation and affirmation
2. The reason we name Him so poorly
3. The justice that never reclaims its gifts
4. The sin that arrives already forgiven
5. The ladder of ten ranks on which the whole drama is staged
End: the first speech of the First Intellect shown as at once the noblest act in creation and, in a precise sense, a sin.

## The First Intellect's first act

Process flow, the founding act of the whole created order.

Start: the First Intellect contemplates the One who had brought it forth.
Step 1, Negation: it pronounces the great denial, there is no god, stripping divinity from itself, "I am not the creator of myself."
Step 2, Extension of the negation: it clears divinity from itself and from its entire genus, the order of beings like it.
Step 3, Affirmation: it affirms that divinity for Allah alone, for Him and no other.
End: the great testimony of oneness, spoken by creation through its first member.

Contrast pair, the two halves of the founding act.

Column A: The negation
- Content: there is no god
- Scope: itself and its whole kind
- Claim: no power to create itself or its genus
- Direction: clears divinity away from the created

Column B: The affirmation
- Content: divinity belongs to Allah
- Scope: Him alone, no other
- Claim: points beyond itself with a single word
- Direction: turns toward the One it cannot name

Shared row: one act, negation then affirmation, becomes the pattern the Qur'an records as the testimony of oneness.

> God bears witness that there is no god but He, as do the angels and the possessors of knowledge, upholding justice. There is no god but He, the Mighty, the Wise.
> Source: Quran, chapter 3, verse 18, trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Annotated structure, the startling identification inside the verse.
Whole: the "Allah" who testifies in the verse.
- Speaker: the First Intellect, bearing witness.
- The One witnessed: the Originator, indicated by nothing more than the bare pronoun "He."
- The confession: the whole order of created being, through its first member, confessed it could not create itself or its kind.
- The gesture: it then pointed beyond itself with that single word, He alone, toward the One it could not name.

Visual metaphor, the narrowing of available words toward zero.
Metaphor: a funnel closing to a point.
- Widest: "Allah," the highest word available, drawn from the manner in which the First Intellect was made.
- Narrower: "He," the bare pronoun, all that lies past "Allah."
- The point: nothing, past those two words there is nothing left with which to interpret Him at all.
Reading: even the First Intellect could express Him only through what belonged to its own origination, "He gave us life, He gave us knowledge, He gave us power." The true glorification befitting Him lay beyond it.

## Naming from inadequacy

Process flow, why inadequate words instead of reverent silence.

Start: our own poverty and the sheer narrowness of our essence, the essence of speech and articulation.
1. We lack the inner capacity to comprehend Him.
2. We reach for the last word that comes to mind, the word beyond which the mind finds nothing further.
3. We let that name fall upon the exalted "He."
End: naming arises not as a triumph of language but as its breaking point.

> Praise is due to Allah whose worth cannot be described by speakers, whose bounties cannot be counted by those who reckon, and whose claim to obedience cannot be satisfied by those who attempt to do so.
> Source: Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Father of Imams, *Nahj al-Balagha* (compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi), Sermon 1, trans. Sayyid Ali Reza.

Contrast pair, what the created intellects were actually given.

Column A: Knowledge granted, the knowledge of life
- Object: what powers and faculties reside in life itself
- Scope: the whole structure of life in its fullness
- Access: nothing of that order is hidden from the intellect
- Result: it understands everything about the gift of life

Column B: Knowledge withheld, knowledge of the divine essence
- Object: the knowledge by which to know Allah
- Scope: unreceivable by its very nature
- Access: cannot even be comprehended, so it was never given
- Result: it understands nothing about the Giver

Shared row: the most exalted creature knows everything about the gift of life and nothing about its Giver.

Hierarchy, the knowledge of life expounded by Sayyidna Mu'ayyad.
- Knowledge of life
  - Life: what it means to live
  - Knowledge: what it means to know
  - Power: what it means to have power
  - Choice: what it means to choose

Annotated structure, the exact shape of our predicament when we name Him.
Whole: a namer handed one complete knowledge and no knowledge of the One named.
- Not lazy: not careless namers who could do better with more effort.
- Fully equipped on one side: a complete knowledge of life and its faculties.
- Empty on the other: no knowledge whatever of the One being named.
- The consequence: we name Him with the vocabulary of life because it is the only vocabulary we were ever given.

## The gift not withdrawn

Annotated structure, the permanence of what Allah bestows.
Whole: a teaching of Sayyidna Mu'ayyad on the gift that is never reclaimed.
- The principle: once Allah has given existence, He does not take it back.
- The human comparison: a person who gives a gift does not afterward snatch it away.
- The conclusion: what He gives, He gives.

Contrast pair, the omnipotence debate with a follower of the Arya Samaj.

Column A: The challenger's trap
- Premise: "Your Allah can do everything?" Yes.
- The snare: "Then can He do injustice?"
- Fork one: if He can, He can do wrong as well.
- Fork two: if He cannot, you have set a limit on His power.
- Aim: force omnipotence to swallow its own coherence.

Column B: The Muslims' answer
- Counter-question: "Can you eat anything? You have the power to eat, so eat this filth."
- The outcome: the man could not bring himself to it.
- The principle: possessing the capacity to eat does not oblige a man to consume what is vile.
- The release: the power is altogether real, and its non-use is not the absence of the power.

Comparison matrix, capacity set beside its exercise.

| Subject | Has the capacity | Uses it on the vile or unjust | What the non-use means |
|---|---|---|---|
| The man | yes, he can eat | no, he will not eat filth | the power is real, non-use is not its absence |
| The Originator | yes, capable of all things | no, He does no injustice | the perfection of His justice, not a limit on His power |

> Truly God wrongs not [even] so much as a mote's weight, and if there be a good deed, He will multiply it and grant a great reward from His Presence.
> Source: Quran, chapter 4, verse 40, trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Annotated structure, why the gift of existence is secure.
Whole: a single steadiness underwriting both justice and the gift of being.
- One refusal: the justice that refuses to do injustice.
- A second refusal: the same justice refuses to betray a gift already given.
- The result: our being is not on loan, to be recalled at a whim.
- The ground: it rests on the One who, able to do anything, does only what is right.

## A sin coupled with forgiveness

Genealogy chain, the line behind the prayer that opens this teaching.
Mawlana Sayyidna Mu'ayyad al-Din, author of the Mabda' wa Ma'ad → Hibatullah ibn Musa al-Shirazi, Sayyidna Mu'ayyad, his spiritual heir → the prayer that confesses, on behalf of all who name God, the Qur'anic word.

> They did not measure God with His true measure, while the whole earth will be in His grasp on the Day of Resurrection, and the heavens will be rolled up in His right hand.
> Source: Quran, chapter 39, verse 67, trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Annotated structure, the confession inside the prayer.
Whole: an admission of shortfall coupled with a plea.
- The admission: we have not measured His worth as it deserves.
- The reason: we cannot, because we do not know Him.
- The most we can say: judging from our own existence, He has made something good.
- The plea: pardon for the one caught in the noose of servitude, who sets himself to remember God by means unworthy of Him.

> I cannot enumerate Your praise. You are as You have praised Yourself.
> Source: Prophet Muhammad, *Sahih Muslim*, Book of Prayer (Salat), no. 486.

Annotated structure, the technical term established by the Du'at.
Whole: "a sin coupled with the seeking of forgiveness," a sin paired at the root with its own asking of pardon.
- Part one, the sin: to take up His remembrance by what is unworthy of Him.
- Part two, the pairing: joined at the root to its own asking of forgiveness.
- Part three, the cause: it arises under the rule of compulsion.
- The reading: because it is compelled, it is accepted, for what is compelled is excused.

Hierarchy, who needs this forgiveness, every world and not the physical one alone.
- All who name Him
  - The created intellects, first through tenth
    - The First Intellect, whose luminous act of bearing witness still falls short of His majesty
  - The bound servant in the noose of servitude
Reading: from the first intellect down to the tenth, none can do more than this, all are compelled, all are equal before the Originator whom none can recognize.

Process flow, the case judged in the time of Umar that seals the principle of compulsion.

Start: a woman with child is brought before Umar, saying she has no husband.
1. Her account: traveling through the desert, dying of thirst across three days.
2. The bargain: a shepherd would give her water only if she gave herself to him.
3. The yielding: near death, she yielded for the water's sake, and afterward bore the child.
4. The first ruling: "Stone her," said Umar.
5. The intervention: the Father of Imams arrives and asks what the matter is.
End: "She is compelled, helpless. Let her go, for this happened under compulsion." Where there is true helplessness, the act does not carry the full weight of a sin.

Annotated structure, the mapping from the case to the soul that names.
Whole: the soul that in its helplessness names the Unnameable.
- The act: it sins, in a manner of speaking, by speaking inadequately of Him.
- The condition: it is compelled, as the woman was compelled.
- The pairing: the sin comes paired with the asking of forgiveness.
- The verdict: it is excused, for what is compelled is excused.

## The ten ascending ranks

Genealogy chain, the ladder of spiritual stations in ascending order.
Imamate of spirituality, the rank of Ilahiyyah, the lowest named → Hujjiyyah → Hijabiyyah → Khayaliyyah → Fathiyyah → Jiddiyyah → Anza'iyyah → Qa'imiyyah → Jussiyyah → Ahadiyyah → Samadiyyah → Huwiyyah, the rank of the bare "He," the Ipseity at the summit.

Annotated structure, the two ends of the ladder.
Whole: the great ladder by which the soul climbs toward its origin, set out in ascending order.
- The foot: the Imamate of spirituality, the rank of Ilahiyyah, the lowest named station.
- The rungs: each a step above the last, mounting upward.
- The summit: Huwiyyah, the Ipseity, the rank of the bare "He."
- The reality: the ladder is real, its rungs are ordered, and a soul truly mounts them.

Contrast pair, the quiet rhyme in where the ladder ends.

Column A: Where the ladder begins
- Position: far down, in the stations a soul can name
- Movement: a soul can climb toward them
- Character: named rungs, ascending knowledge

Column B: Where the ladder ends
- Position: at the very word in which all naming gives out
- Movement: the highest rung is not a fuller name but the emptiest one, "He"
- Character: the same bare pronoun the First Intellect found to point at the Originator

Shared row: the ascent arrives, at its summit, back at the confession the whole doctrine began with, the top is the threshold of the unknowable, not its conquest.

## The hidden pairs

Annotated structure, the single word that opens this last matter.
Whole: the word for pairing inside "a sin coupled with forgiveness."
- Root: it means, in its root, "paired."
- The hidden meaning: among the Du'at the word carries an inner referent, as do its two companion terms.
- The source: all three are borrowed from the language of prayer.

Comparison matrix, the three prayer terms and their inner referents.

| Prayer term | Outer form | Inner referent |
|---|---|---|
| The paired prayer | two units of bowing | the Prophet, together with Lady Fatima al-Zahra, the noble pair |
| The odd prayer | the single, unpaired unit | the Father of Imams, the singular one |
| The sitting prayer | two units performed seated | Imam Hasan and Imam Husayn together |

Annotated structure, the witness and the single sign.
Whole: a correspondence named at Karbala.
- The witness: it is related that Imam Husayn himself, at Karbala, would name these correspondences.
- His naming: the paired prayer is the Prophet, the odd prayer was his own father, and so onward.
- The teaching's affirmation: these together form a single sign.

Contrast pair, the turn the chapter ends on.

Column A: Where the teaching began
- Subject: the bare pronoun "He"
- Claim: the impossibility of naming the Originator
- Effect: it emptied our names of any grip on the divine essence

Column B: Where the chapter closes
- Subject: the postures of daily prayer
- Claim: hidden ranks of the Prophet and his pure household folded into pairing and oddness
- Effect: it fills the simplest acts of worship with concealed meaning

Shared row: what cannot be said of the Originator directly, the tradition says obliquely.

## What this teaching lands

Process flow, the resolved thread end to end.

Start: the paradox carried to its resolution.
1. First act: the First Intellect negated divinity from itself and affirmed it for Allah alone, and the Qur'an's testimony of oneness is, at its root, that act.
2. The naming: we name the Originator only out of inadequacy, given a complete knowledge of life and no knowledge of its Giver.
3. The pardon: because that naming is compelled, the sin of speaking unworthily comes already joined to the asking of forgiveness, as true helplessness excuses the one who acts under it.
4. Above: a ladder of ten ranks whose summit is the bare "He."
5. Below: the daily prayers conceal the ranks of the Prophet and his household within their pairings.
End: the paradox is not dissolved but inhabited.

Contrast pair, the question and the answer that inhabits it.

Column A: The question we began with
- By what right do we name what we cannot know?
- What does that naming cost?

Column B: What the teaching answers
- The cost itself is made part of the worship.
- Naming is at once the noblest act of the First Intellect and a sin, yet a sin that arrives already coupled with its own forgiveness, excused because compelled.
- The Unnameable stays unknowable, the bare "He" still all our language can hold of Him.
- What changes is the believer, who now says "Allah" knowing how poor the word is, and who finds in the pairing and oddness of the daily prayers the hidden ranks of the Prophet, Lady Fatima, the Father of Imams, and his two grandsons.

Shared row: the Unnameable is never named, but the one who reaches to name Him is taught, in that reaching, both his own helplessness and the secret company kept within his prayer.
