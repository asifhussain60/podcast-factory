# Origination From Nothing

## Where this episode picks up

Contrast pair, the two halves of the teaching, one now complete and one now beginning.

Column A: The half already traveled (how the ranks are known)
- Subject: the gnosis of the spiritual hierarchy, rank above rank
- Movement: the seeker ascends to the First Intellect and the Originator beyond it
- Act: a soul declares the Oneness of Allah by negating divinity from every created rank
- Status: established in the prior episodes

Column B: The half now opening (how the ranks came to be)
- Subject: not how the ranks are known, but how they, and everything else, came to be
- Movement: the teaching turns from the act of knowing to the act that produced what is known
- Act: the bringing of being out of sheer nothing
- Status: founded in this episode

Shared row: the whole cosmology depends on holding firm the distinction between origination and ordinary making, the point this episode is built to guard.

Annotated structure, the opening question and the answer that undoes the question.

Whole: the question, before anything at all existed, where was the world that was about to be made?
- The wrong picture: a "nowhere," an empty place, a blank canvas waiting to be painted
- The teaching's answer: there was no "where" at all, no place, no prior stuff, no canvas
- The reflex it corrects: we always picture "create" as a making-of-something out of some material at hand
Callout: the origination of the world was not a making-out-of-material. It was the bringing of being out of sheer nothing, and the answer is stranger than "nowhere," for the question itself assumes a place that did not yet exist.

## The world of origination

Annotated structure, the single originated realm and the cluster of names that allude to it.

Whole: the world that Allah originated in a single instant, toward whom passing thoughts dare not turn and toward whom the imaginations and the inmost selves cannot rise.
- Name: the world of command
- Name: the world of intellect
- Name: the world of sanctity
- Name: the world of purity
- Name: the world of subtlety
- Name: the spiritual world
Callout: these are several names for one originated realm, not several realms. Our thought and our inner being alike fall back, unable to ascend to the Being who originated it.

Verbatim, scripture names this very act in one of the names it gives to God, al-Badi', the Originator.

> The Originator of the heavens and the earth! When He decrees a thing, He only says to it, "Be!" and it is. (chapter 2, verse 117), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Reading: the name does not describe a craftsman who shapes a waiting material. Al-Badi' is the One whose sheer decree is the thing's coming-to-be, with no "how" and no interval between the command and the existence it calls forth.

Comparison matrix, the three negations, each closing one route by which the mind smuggles a "something" back into the picture.

Rows: first negation, second negation, third negation
Columns: what is denied | the imagination's reflex | the everyday image stripped away

- first negation: originated not from any thing that preceded it, no prior substance, no prime matter, no first leaven | the mind reaches for a raw material to be worked | clay for the pot, dough for the bread
- second negation: originated not with any accompanying thing, no co-present matter, nothing to resemble or take after | the mind supplies a companion-stuff, a model alongside the act | a mold the new thing is shaped beside
- third negation: originated not upon any thing bearing it, no underlying substrate, no first location | the mind clutches at a bare place where the new thing could appear | an empty floor the thing is set upon

Callout: no prior matter, no concurrent matter, no underlying place. Only when all three props are knocked out does the mind confront what origination means, brought forth with no material, no model, and no place.

Contrast pair, the fundamental distinction that governs everything to follow.

Column A: Ordinary making, or creation in the common sense
- What it does: changes the arrangement of already-existing things
- The matter: a pre-existent thing is presupposed, broken or reshaped
- The form: a new composite form is altered out of what is on hand
- Examples: a potter makes a pot, the clay was already there; a builder raises a house, the stone and timber existed before him
- Reach: the only kind of "creating" we ever witness

Column B: Origination, in the strict sense reserved for the Originator alone
- What it does: brings being out of nothing, where no prior thing exists at all
- The matter: none; the Originator brings the very stuff
- The form: none presupposed; the Originator brings the form and everything
- Examples: nothing in our ordinary experience is an image of this
- Reach: a unique act belonging to the Originator alone

Shared row: if even a trace of prior matter, concurrent matter, or underlying place had been involved, origination would collapse back into mere making, a reshaping of something already there. The word "origination" is reserved precisely to keep it from being confused with the making we know.

## Pure favor, not need

Annotated structure, the nothingness from which the existents were drawn.

Whole: the nothingness out of which Allah drew the existents into a world of existence, as a favor, a generosity, and a bestowal.
- What it is not: a world or realm, a place where things waited
- What it is: simply the absence of being
- The Sindh teacher's question to his students: when all this was taking place, where were we?
- Why the question has no answer: it assumes a place and a presence that did not yet exist; we were not anywhere, because we were not at all
Callout: there was nothing but non-existence from which we were drawn.

Verbatim, the Qur'an puts the same truth as a question to every human being.

> Has there come upon man a period of time when he was a thing unremembered? (chapter 76, verse 1), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Reading: there was a "time," if it can even be called that, when each of us was not a thing at all, not so much as a name to be mentioned. Our entire being is something drawn out of that "unremembered" nothing.

Process flow, Mohyuddin's method of eliminating every motive until only favor remains.

Start: do not assert the motive; begin by eliminating every motive that would imply a lack in the Originator
  to
Test a motive: did He create to gain something? Then He lacked it before
  to
Test a motive: did He create to be served, or known, or praised? Then He needed the service, the knowledge, the praise
  to
Test a motive: did He create in play, for diversion? Then He was a being who could be diverted, with idle time to fill
  to
Result of each test: the supposed purpose attributes to the Originator some deficiency a true God cannot have, and is struck down
End: what remains is the only "purpose" compatible with absolute self-sufficiency, not an end He needed to reach, but the sheer overflow of a generous nature that gives because giving is what generosity does

Comparison matrix, the supposed motives Mohyuddin refuted, chapter after chapter.

Rows: to gain something, to be served or known or praised, to create in play
Columns: what it would secure | the lack it would imply | the verdict

- to gain something: an acquisition He did not have | He lacked it beforehand | refuted
- to be served or known or praised: service, knowledge, praise from creation | He needed what creation supplies | refuted
- to create in play: diversion, the filling of idle time | He could be diverted and had time to fill | refuted

Callout: the favor is not a means to anything; it is the whole of the matter. He originated out of sheer favor, generosity, and bestowal, and nothing else.

Verbatim, the household of the Imams placed this confession at the very opening of its prayer.

> Praise belongs to God ... who originated the creation through His power with an origination, and devised it in accordance with His will with a devising. (Supplication 1), Ali Zayn al-Abidin, *al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya*, trans. William C. Chittick, *The Psalms of Islam*.

Reading: creation is "devised in accordance with His will," not extracted from Him by any lack, not owed to any prior claim, but freely willed. The Originator gains nothing and completes nothing by originating; the whole transaction runs one way, from His generosity outward.

Contrast pair, a god of need set against the Originator of fullness.

Column A: A god who created out of need
- Source of the act: a lack to be filled
- Inner state: deficiency, something missing before creation
- Consequence: such a god would have a nourisher of his own
- Verdict: exactly the mark of the created gods the teaching set aside earlier

Column B: The true Originator
- Source of the act: pure favor, generosity, bestowal
- Inner state: absolute self-sufficiency, the bestowing nature of the Generous and Mighty
- Consequence: free of need and free of frivolity, neither needing creation nor sporting with it
- Verdict: the One who creates out of fullness, not lack

Shared row: it is of the very nature of the Generous and the Powerful to bestow favor, and of the disposition of the Mighty and the Great to give; the origination flows from that nature.

## The luminous forms, created equal

Annotated structure, the manner in which the originated beings were brought forth.

Whole: He brought them into existence as forms, purely imaginal, pure, and luminous, possessing thousand-fold statures, distinct from their material souls.
- Substance: purely imaginal, pure, and luminous
- Measure: statures of a thousand-fold measure, like the human bodily stature
- Distinction: distinct from the material souls, which did not yet exist and belong to a later stage
Callout: He brought them into being as forms; the souls are things of a later stage, and the bodies of flesh later still.

Contrast pair, the direction of the likeness, which the teaching is careful to set rightly.

Column A: The way it is said, to make the matter intelligible
- Statement: the originated forms were like the human bodily stature
- Purpose: to give us a familiar measure we can picture
- Direction implied: from us to them, the human body as the reference

Column B: The way it truly is
- Statement: the human bodily stature is patterned upon the originated forms
- Purpose: to set the order of reality straight
- Direction implied: from them to us, the primordial luminous forms as the pattern

Shared row: human bodies that later came into the world were fashioned on the pattern of those primordial luminous forms, not the forms upon them.

Genealogy chain, the descent from luminous form to dense body, each stage a fall from the one above.

Luminous imaginal forms (originated first) → the original, luminous subtleties near the source of light
Luminous forms → the material souls, formed later, a fallen, degraded condition derived from the forms
Material souls → the bodies of flesh and blood, formed later still, a yet more fallen condition
Bodies of flesh and blood → dark densities at the furthest reach from the source

Verbatim, the Sufi tradition fixed this vision of a darkened cosmos lit only from above.

> The cosmos is all darkness; it is illumined only by the manifestation of God within it. (aphorism 14), Ibn Ata Allah al-Iskandari, *Kitab al-Hikam*, trans. Victor Danner, *The Book of Wisdom*.

Reading: if the whole created order is darkness except where the divine light shows through, the luminous form is that light near its source, and the dense body is the darkness at its furthest reach.

Contrast pair, the reversal that recasts the whole human predicament.

Column A: The common assumption
- The original: the body of flesh
- The spiritual form: a faint abstraction drawn from the body
- To be embodied: to be at home in one's native condition
- The spiritual life: a flight into something thinner and less real

Column B: The teaching's reversal
- The original: the luminous form, the real and first thing
- The spiritual form: not abstracted from the body; the body is its dim, heavy shadow
- To be embodied: to be at the furthest remove from one's own origin, a place of exile
- The spiritual life: a movement of ascent and refinement, a homecoming

Shared row: the refinement of the Mukathir, whose flesh was made subtle though it looked unchanged, is the first stirring of that return, the dense shadow beginning to recover the luminosity of the form it was copied from.

Annotated structure, the doctrine of the first perfection.

Whole: the first perfection, the original state in which Allah created the luminous forms, equal and without disparity.
- Component: existence
- Component: life
- Component: strength
- Component: ability, which the teaching glosses together with knowledge
Callout: in this first perfection the forms were created perfectly equal; the Originator created the first exactly as He created the last, with not the slightest difference among them.

Comparison matrix, the four denials that spell out the equality of the first perfection.

Rows: disagreement, preference, distinction, inclination
Columns: what is denied | across what range

- disagreement: no disagreement among the forms | none whatever in the first perfection
- preference: no preference of one over another | none whatever
- distinction: no distinction set between them | none whatever
- inclination: no inclination | neither in the subtle nor the great, neither in much nor in little

Callout: equal in existence, life, strength, and ability, with none of these four disparities introduced.

Process flow, why the equality follows from justice and admits no disparity.

Start: the equality was an act of justice from Him, the Mighty and Majestic
  to
The justice of the Just is equal in its distribution; the grace of the Gracious is universal in its reach
  to
Therefore the originated forms were created in perfect equality, given to none more and to none less
  to
In the abode of origination the forms are free of the matter that obstructs
End: where there is no obstructing matter, there is nothing to introduce disparity among them

Verbatim, scripture anchors the equality by tying absence of discrepancy to single divine authorship.

> Do they not contemplate the Quran? Had it been from other than God, they would surely have found much discrepancy therein. (chapter 4, verse 82), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Reading: if the originated order had proceeded from any other than Allah, much discrepancy would be found within it; only the perfect justice of Allah could produce a creation without disparity. This is the very verse that answers whether the equal creation of all beings is attested in the Qur'an. It is attested here.

## What this teaching lands

Annotated structure, the four pillars on which the cosmology now stands founded.

Whole: the founded cosmology of origination.
- Pillar one, the manner: the world of origination brought into being in a single instant, from no prior matter, no concurrent matter, and no underlying place, an act utterly unlike the making we know
- Pillar two, the cause: He originated not from any need, for a god who needed his creation would be a god in lack, but from pure favor, generosity, and bestowal, drawing the existents out of a nothingness that was no place at all
- Pillar three, the substance: luminous imaginal forms of thousand-fold stature, upon which the human body was later patterned, the soul a fallen copy of the form and the body a fallen copy of the soul, so that salvation is a return from dark density toward luminous subtlety
- Pillar four, the equality: all forms created perfectly equal in their first perfection, existence, life, strength, and ability, as an act of the justice that gives to none more and to none less, a justice the Qur'an itself attests
Callout: the act of origination has been established; the cosmology is, even so, only half-founded.

Process flow, the open questions the equal beginning sets in motion.

Start: the world is originated, and it began in perfect equality
  to
Question one: how did the whole order come to exist in a single instant?
  to
Question two: if the forms were created perfectly equal, how were they nonetheless not yet infallible?
  to
Question three: how, out of that undifferentiated equality, did one of them become the very first to declare the Oneness of Allah?
End: what that origination sets in motion is the unfolding for which the equal forms now stand ready
