# The Two Gnoses and the Mukathir

## Where this episode picks up

Contrast pair, the one gnosis of the ranks divided in two. The central question that opens the episode: how can a saint's most devoted disciple and his sworn enemy know him equally well? The answer is that one whole register of the gnosis is shared by both, and another whole register is closed to the enemy entirely.

Column A: Outer knowing, shared by friend and enemy
- Object: the rank's person and his station
- Who can possess it: lover, enemy, and indifferent alike
- What it registers: only the outward, visible person
- Advantage of the friend over the enemy: none whatever
- Limit reached: it tells who a rank is and where he stands

Column B: Inner knowing, reserved for the people of truth
- Object: the beginning, the level, and the return of a rank's essence
- Who can possess it: only those admitted within the truth
- What it registers: the hidden origin, not the visible person
- Advantage of the friend over the enemy: total, the enemy cannot touch it
- Stake reached: without it, a man's Tawhid is emptied of all reality

Shared row: both columns are the gnosis of the ranks, and to affirm the Oneness of Allah is to know the ranks of the hierarchy in their order. This is why the same body of knowledge is at once something an enemy may partly possess and something an enemy can never possess at all. The episode draws this line and then walks the seeker up to the threshold of the ranks themselves.

## Outer gnosis of the ranks

Annotated structure, the outward person that any pair of eyes can register. The whole is the outer gnosis, by which the person of a rank and his station are recognized.

Whole: the outer gnosis of a rank, the knowledge of who he is and where he stands.

Parts, the outward person dissected feature by feature:
- Stature: how tall he is
- Face: whether it is round
- Beard: whether he wears one
- Hands: what his hands are like
- Feet: what his feet are like

Annotations, one callout per aspect:
- Distinguishing mark: in this outer gnosis the friend and the adversary stand on exactly equal footing
- Why it is shared: it concerns only the outward person and the outward station, the things any pair of eyes can register
- Why it is not trivial: its difficulty is real, for to know a rank truly even in his outward person and station is a genuine accomplishment
- Where it stops: it tells you who a rank is and where he stands, but it does not yet tell you what he is

Inline contrast that fixes the point: the bitter enemy who studies a saint closely may describe his every feature with great precision and be his enemy still. A man may know all of this and stand entirely outside the deeper knowledge.

## Inner gnosis of the ranks

Process flow, the origin of the ranks, which is the object of the inner gnosis. This second and far higher kind of gnosis is specific to the people of gnosis from among the people of truth, and its object is the very beginning of the existence of a rank: when it began, where it was created, and from whom.

The account of the origin, in compressed form:
Start: the hudud were brought into being where those whose sins were few had been
  then: a first call was issued, and in that first call they did not confess
  then: a second call was made
  then: a third call was made
  then: a fourth call was made, in each room in turn
  then: confession was made, and one must know in which room each rank finally confessed
End: the gnosis of this beginning belongs to the Imam al-Zaman, upon him be peace

The full unfolding of this long story belongs elsewhere, but the point stands: the gnosis of where and from whom and through how many summons a rank came into being is precisely the inner gnosis.

Contrast pair, two kinds of difficulty that explain why the one gnosis splits. The outer is hard the way effort is hard; the inner is hard the way a sealed thing is hard.

Column A: Outer gnosis, a wall any climber might scale
- Kind of difficulty: the difficulty of effort
- Source of the difficulty: it takes attention and care to observe well
- Who can overcome it: anyone, friend or foe, since effort is available to all
- Image: a wall that any climber might scale

Column B: Inner gnosis, a door that opens only from within
- Kind of difficulty: the difficulty of hiddenness
- Source of the difficulty: it is sealed off from all but those whom the truth has admitted
- Who can overcome it: only those who stand within the truth, since the beginning of a rank can be known only from within
- Image: a door that opens only from within

Shared row: the friend and the foe see the same face; only the friend admitted to the people of truth sees where that face began. No amount of effort by an outsider can open the inner door.

Scripture draws this very line between the surface of a revealed thing and the hidden sense that only the few may reach:

> And none know its interpretation save God and those firmly rooted in knowledge. Quran 3:7 (Al 'Imran), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., The Study Quran.

The interpretation here is the ta'wil, the tracing of a thing back to the origin from which it came, which is exactly the inner gnosis. The enemy may read every word; only the firmly rooted know from where it came.

## Beginning, level, and return

Process flow, the arc of a created essence from origin to homecoming. The inner gnosis does not concern the beginning alone; it comprises the whole trajectory of the essence, its beginning, its level, and its return.

The arc of the essence, in order:
Origin: the point at which the essence was first established
  then: ascent through the levels, the ninth degree of the believer, or the eighth, or the seventh
  then: further ascent, from believer to become a Mukathir, and from Mukathir to a higher rank still
  then: the level at departure, the precise station at which a person stood when he passed from this world
End: the return, the final terminus to which the essence is destined

This homecoming is no peculiarity of the ranks; it is the law of every created thing, fixed in the words a believer says in the face of affliction:

> Truly we are God's, and unto Him we return. Quran 2:156 (al-Baqara), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., The Study Quran.

Annotated structure, the limits and stakes of this gnosis. The whole is the inner gnosis of extreme difficulty; the parts are who may reach it and what hangs upon it.

- Who can reach it: only the disciplined ones and the verifiers, those who have purified themselves and proven the truth within themselves
- The severity stated plainly: for one whose gnosis of this inner reality is deficient, there is no Tawhid at all
- The consequence drawn out: a man could master every outward feature of every rank and still, lacking the inner knowing, have nothing of Tawhid in him
- The master's response: he announces his intention to clarify something of this matter, as a help to his brethren in the gnosis of the Oneness of Allah

## The closest rank

Hierarchy tree, the ranks of the hudud read upward from the nearest door. The exposition begins with a foundational principle, set down with an invocation of the help of Allah and the glances of His Wali upon His earth.

Principle fixed at the outset: the closest of the ranks to us is the Mukathir, and whatever is established of the saying regarding the lower of the ranks is established, raised to its degree, for the higher.

The hierarchy, from nearest to highest, in the order the teaching names them:
- Mukathir, the closest rank to us, the one through which we make our first true contact with the hierarchy
  - Ma'dhun
    - Da'i Mutlaq
      - Da'i Mahsur
        - every rank above

Genealogy chain, the rule of proportion as cumulative containment. The higher rank contains the lower within himself, having been that lower rank before he rose:

Mukathir → Ma'dhun, who was a Mukathir before he was a Ma'dhun and carries the Mukathir within him → every higher rank, each containing the lower as a man carries his earlier self

Annotations on the rule of proportion:
- Whatever is established for the lower is established for the higher, raised to its proper degree
- Because of this, the ranks are not a heap of unrelated dignities but a single ordered series
- Because the higher contains the lower, the series is not merely ordered but cumulative, and nothing is left behind in the ascent
- Therefore to understand the closest rank truly is already to hold the key to all the ranks above it, read upward in their proper measure
- This is why the teaching begins at the nearest door, the Mukathir, rather than at some lofty rank the listener could not yet reach

## The two thresholds

Comparison matrix, the two preliminary levels that stand below the hudud proper. Before reaching the Mukathir, one must pass through the Mustajib and the Mu'min, neither of which falls within the ranks.

Rows: Mustajib, the responder. Mu'min, the believer.
Columns: Definition. Defining mark. Number of levels. Relation to the hudud.

- Mustajib: he takes the noble pledge and declares with his tongue, I accept the noble pledge, whether or not his heart and deeds have yet caught up | profession with the tongue, affirming the Imam of the time and binding himself by that word | ten levels | below the ranks, a necessary threshold but not yet of them
- Mu'min: profession with the tongue, affirmation with the heart, and action with the limbs, all three joined in him | the joining of tongue, heart, and limb | ten levels of his own | below the ranks, a necessary threshold but not yet of them

The ten levels of the Mustajib, graded by the intensity with which the pledge is taken:
- Lowest intensity: one who comes to the pledge as casually as one comes to a marriage
- Highest intensity: one who takes it with the burning intensity of a Sayyidna Ja'far
- And the Imam himself takes the pledge

According to the intensity, the sharpness, the force, the keenness of the one pledging, his level is established, and so there are ten degrees of the noble pledge.

Visual metaphor, the ascent to the door of the ranks. The two thresholds stand outside; the Mukathir stands within.

- Mustajib, who binds himself by the tongue, stands outside the door
- Mu'min, who joins tongue, heart, and limb, still stands outside the door
- The door of the hudud
- Mukathir, the first to stand within it

Reading: the believer, for all the seriousness of his belief, still stands outside the door; the Mukathir is the first to stand within. It is only after these two thresholds that one truly enters the ranks.

## Breaking the self

Annotated structure, the meaning of the word Mukathir. The whole is the name itself; the parts are what it breaks, and in what order.

- Derivation: Mukathir comes from breaking, for the Mukathir is the one who breaks with his proofs
- First object of the breaking: not his opponents, but his own self
- Indispensable beginning: this breaking of the self is where the rank begins, the door through which a believer enters the ranks at all
- The price: the surrender of the one thing a man is most reluctant to surrender, his own license over himself

Annotated structure, the threefold examination by which the self is broken. To break one's self is to subject every word, deed, and intention to a single relentless measure: the command of Allah and the Prophet ﷺ.

- Speech: is this statement I am uttering in accordance with the command of Allah and the Prophet?
- Deed: is this action I am performing in accordance with their command?
- Thought: is this very thought passing through my mind in accordance with their command?

Annotation: to bind one's self entirely to that measure, to make oneself captive to the command in speech, deed, and thought alike, is what it means to be a Mukathir.

This captivity to the command is itself a striving, and the Prophet redirected the very name of the warrior toward it, away from the outward fight:

> The one who truly strives is he who strives against his own self in obedience to God. Prophet Muhammad, narrated by Fadalah ibn Ubayd, Jami' al-Tirmidhi, Kitab Fada'il al-Jihad, no. 1621.

Process flow, the exacting logic of order. Only the man who has broken his own self can break another:

Start: he acquires the sciences, for he must first know the command of Allah and the Prophet
  then: he measures his own self against that command
  branch:
    Path A: he sets himself in order, gains standing, and can break the one before him with his proofs
    Path B: he indulges his self, granting himself license that this is permissible and that is permissible, gains no standing, and cannot break another
End: having first judged and broken himself by knowledge, he is then able to break the one before him

The Father of Imams fixed this same order, the reform of the self before the reform of others, as the first duty of anyone who would lead:

> Whoever sets himself up as a leader of the people, let him begin with the teaching of his own self before the teaching of others; and let his teaching be by his conduct before his tongue. Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Father of Imams, Nahj al-Balagha (compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi), Saying 73, trans. Sayyid Ali Reza.

Annotations, why knowledge is indispensable to the Mukathir:
- Without knowledge no Mukathir can be formed, for he must know the command in order to test himself against it
- Knowledge is the measuring-rod against which the self is broken, not an ornament held as a possession
- Only when he knows the command can he recognize that a word of his fell short, a deed diverged, a thought strayed
- A man who gathered knowledge while sparing himself its judgment would not be a Mukathir at all
- By the rule of proportion, this same description holds, in its proper degree, of every rank of the hudud above

## What this teaching lands

Contrast pair, the gnosis of the ranks divided at its joint. This is the spine the whole episode has drawn.

Column A: Outer knowing
- Object: a rank's person and station
- Friend and enemy: made equal in it
- Why: it concerns only what any eye can see

Column B: Inner knowing
- Object: the beginning, the level, and the return of a rank's essence
- Friend and enemy: the enemy can never possess it
- Why: reserved for the disciplined and verifying among the people of truth, and so decisive that without it a man has no Tawhid at all

Visual metaphor, the seeker's full ascent as a ladder from below the ranks to the first rank within:
- Mustajib, binds himself by the tongue, ten levels, below the door
  - Mu'min, joins tongue, heart, and limb, ten levels, still below the door
    - The door of the hudud
    - Mukathir, enters by first breaking his own self against the command of Allah and the Prophet, which is why knowledge is indispensable to him

What this teaching lands: the door into the ranks is not a title conferred from without but a self subdued from within, the seeker made captive to the command in speech, deed, and thought, his proofs backed by his conduct, his knowing turned first upon himself. What stands closest to us is also what costs the most to reach: the breaking of one's own license, which is the price of the first rank and, by the rule of proportion, the seed of every rank above it.
