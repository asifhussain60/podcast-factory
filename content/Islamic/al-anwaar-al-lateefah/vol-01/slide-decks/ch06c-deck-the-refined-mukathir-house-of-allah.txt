# The Mukathir, House of Allah

## Where this episode picks up

Annotated structure, the question that opens the episode. The whole is a single paradox the seeker is forced to hold; the parts are what is identical and what is utterly different.

Whole: the holiest man one ever met looks, to the eye, exactly like everyone else.

Parts, the paradox dissected:
- What is identical: same flesh, same bone, the same five feet and odd inches, the same face and hands
- What differs: something no inspection could ever detect
- Where the teaching stands: it has just brought the seeker across the threshold of the ranks to the Mukathir, the first and closest rank
- How the Mukathir is entered: by breaking one's own self against the command of Allah and the Prophet

The pressing question, stated exactly:
- If the Mukathir is outwardly indistinguishable from any other human being, flesh, blood, bone, nerve, and vein, then in what does his rank actually consist?

The teaching's answer, given in advance:
- An immense transformation has taken place, one that reaches even into his outward composition, though no eye perceives it
- From that transformation flows the most exalted of the descriptions given to him

## Same flesh, different reality

Contrast pair, the ordinary man and the Mukathir set against each other on the one ground where they are identical.

Column A: The ordinary human being
- Composition: flesh, blood, bones, nerves, veins, and all the rest
- Outward inspection: of flesh and bone, nothing more
- Bodily limits: gathered within the same boundaries of body and place
- Order of being: the common human order

Column B: The Mukathir
- Composition: flesh, blood, bones, nerves, veins, and all the rest
- Outward inspection: of flesh and bone, indistinguishable from the ordinary man
- Bodily limits: gathered within the very same boundaries of body and place
- Order of being: a wholly different order, though no surface mark betrays it

Shared row: their outward composition is completely identical, bone for bone and vein for vein, and yet they are not the same. An immense difference separates two men who share every measurable feature.

Annotated structure, the mistake the listener must surrender. The whole is the eye's natural assumption; the parts are why it fails.

Whole: the assumption that what a thing is must show in what it looks like.

Parts, why the assumption collapses:
- The expectation: a being of higher order would betray its rank in some visible mark
- The refusal: the teaching denies that two men, identical feature for feature, must belong to the same order of being
- The instrument that fails: the eye, which knows only outward sameness and is blind to the reality beneath it
- The wider warning: this repeats the volume's lesson that the outer gnosis knows a rank's person but not his reality

The decisive consequence:
- If even the Mukathir, the first and lowest rank, is outwardly indistinguishable from any common man, the eye is no instrument for measuring rank
- The hierarchy of the spiritual world cannot be read by the senses; it must be known by the inner gnosis or not known at all
- The very invisibility of the Mukathir's transformation is the first proof of this

## The refined essence

Process flow, the ascent through the stations that refines the Mukathir's very substance. The transformation is not merely inward; it reaches even the outward, though the ignorant could not discern it.

Start: the seeker enters the lowest threshold
  then: he passes through the ten levels of the Mustajib
  then: he passes through the ten levels of the Mu'min
  then: he rises through the degrees of the Mukathir
  then: from this ascent his temperament is refined and his essence made pure
  then: his flesh is no longer common flesh, his blood no longer common blood, everything of his transformed
End: he has drawn near to the first principle, the very source from which his creation proceeded, and is now close to returning there

The Prophetic tradition names this transformation from the divine side, as the fruit of exactly this slow drawing-near:

> My servant draws near to Me with nothing more beloved to Me than what I have made obligatory upon him; and My servant continues to draw near to Me through supererogatory works until I love him. And when I love him, I become his hearing with which he hears, his sight with which he sees, his hand with which he grasps, and his foot with which he walks. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, hadith qudsi narrated by Abu Hurayra, *Sahih al-Bukhari*, Kitab al-Riqaq, no. 6502.

Annotated structure, the hinge of the whole matter. The whole is the joining-point where the human order meets the spiritual; the parts are what stands on each side.

Whole: the summit of the human is the threshold of the malakut, the spiritual world.

Parts, the two rungs that meet at one point:
- Topmost rung of the human order: the highest stations of humanity, which the Mukathir has reached
- Bottommost rung of the angelic order: the first of the spiritual ranks, which those same human stations already are
- Where the Mukathir stands: precisely at that joining-point, where the top of the human is the bottom of the angelic

Why his composition can be refined while his appearance is unchanged:
- He has not ceased to be human; he has reached the highest reach of the human, which is already the doorway into something higher
- The ascent was not a flight out of the body but a purification carried through it
- The human stuff itself, without altering its look, became the first stuff of the spiritual world

## A house of Allah

Annotated structure, why holding the command makes a man a house of Allah. The whole is the most exalted description given to the Mukathir; the parts are the discipline that earns it.

Whole: the Mukathir is a place of descent for the revelation of Allah, a house from among His houses, the very locus upon which revelation alights.

Parts, the threefold holding that builds the house:
- In thought: he kept the command of Allah and the Prophet before him in his every thought
- In speech: he kept that command before him in his every word
- In deed: he kept that command before him in his every deed
- The result: having made the command the standing presence of thought, speech, and action, he became a house fit for the descent of revelation

The teaching anchors this in the Qur'an, in the chapter of Light, on the houses Allah permits to be raised:

> In houses God has permitted to be raised up and wherein His Name is mentioned, He is glorified therein, morning and evening, by men whom neither commerce nor trade diverts from the remembrance of God, the performance of prayer, and the giving of alms. The Quran, chapter 24, verses 36 to 37, trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Contrast pair, the outward word against its inner reality. The verse names one thing on the surface and another in its depth.

Column A: The outward word of the verse
- The houses: buildings of stone
- The men: worshippers within a physical structure
- The reading: literal, the surface of the revealed phrase

Column B: The inner reality of the verse
- The houses: the ranks of the hierarchy, of whom the Mukathir is the first
- The men: those same ranks, whom neither commerce nor trade diverts from the remembrance of Allah
- The reading: the house and its inhabitant turn out to be one reality

Shared row: the rank made a dwelling for revelation is itself the man whom no trade can pull away from the remembrance of his Lord.

Visual metaphor, the house as a cleared and furnished vessel. A house is defined by what it is built to receive and to shelter.

- The clearing-out: he has emptied himself of every rival preoccupation
- The furnishing: he has furnished himself entirely with the command of Allah and the Prophet
- The room that remains: there is room in him for nothing but what comes from above
- What revelation requires: not a mind crowded with its own concerns, but one cleared and consecrated to receive

Reading: the man who is a house and the man undistracted by commerce are one. The two descriptions name one discipline from two sides, the clearing-out of worldly distraction and the furnishing with the divine command.

## Establishing the da'wah

Contrast pair, the inner sense of establishing prayer against its outer act. What is intended by these men's establishing of prayer is, in its depth, the establishing of the da'wah, the divine summons.

Column A: The outward prayer
- What it is: the visible act of worship
- What it serves: the inward reality, the da'wah, which is its very object
- What it cannot do alone: stand as an empty husk once its kernel is understood
- Its necessity: the da'wah has no standing except through it

Column B: The inner da'wah
- What it is: the divine summons, the establishing of the call to Allah
- What it depends on: the prayer that supports and carries it
- What it is not: a replacement that discards the outer act
- Its necessity: it is kept standing only by the prayer that upholds it

Shared row: the outer prayer and the inner summons are bound together, each upholding the other. The man who establishes prayer is establishing, through that very act, the summons to Allah.

The outward language of commerce and trade is illuminated by the parallel passage of the chapter of the Congregation:

> O you who believe! When the call to prayer is made on the day of congregation, hasten to the remembrance of God and leave off trade. That is better for you, if you but knew. Then when the prayer is completed, disperse throughout the land and seek the Bounty of God, and remember God much, that haply you may prosper. The Quran, chapter 62, verses 9 to 10, trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Annotated structure, the objection the doctrine of inner meanings invites, and its foreclosure. The whole is a tempting but false conclusion; the parts are how the teaching shuts it down.

Whole: if establishing prayer really means establishing the divine summons, one might conclude the outward prayer is a mere husk to be discarded.

Parts, the foreclosure:
- What the teaching does not say: that the inner summons replaces the outer prayer
- What the teaching does say: that the summons has no standing except through the prayer
- The status of the outward act: not a dispensable shell but the very support on which the inward reality rests
- The cost of abandoning it: to abandon the prayer would be to topple the summons it upholds
- The Mukathir's position: having grasped the inner meaning, he is bound to the outward observance more tightly than before, for he now sees the great inner reality the small outward act carries

## The inner meaning of the pillars

Comparison matrix, each pillar of the religion against the inner meaning it carries beneath its outward form. This inner reading is no improvisation; the classical Ismaili tradition devoted an entire treatise to it, Qadi al-Nu'man's *Ta'wil al-Da'a'im*, the interpretation of the pillars of Islam.

Rows: the pillars and obligations, in the order the teaching names them. Columns: outward form | inner meaning.

- Loyal allegiance: the outward bond of allegiance | to repent in the world of motes, turning in repentance in that primordial world of the smallest particles of being
- Purity: the outward cleansing of the body | to cleanse oneself from the filth of Iblis through the knowledge of the Tenth
- Prayer: the outward standing in worship | to act upon the commands of the Tenth, or of whichever Imam in whose sphere one has arrived; this is why, when the prayer is established, it is announced that the prayer has stood, since the standing of the prayer is the standing-up to enact the command
- Alms: the outward giving of wealth | to give a portion of the knowledge that has been given to you, once it reaches a certain measure
- Fasting: the outward abstention from food | to conceal knowledge, keeping it hidden from those unfit to receive it
- The fifth: the outward fifth of an unexpected windfall | to give a fifth part of knowledge that descends upon you unexpectedly from the saints of Allah
- Pilgrimage: the outward journey to the House | to intend toward the Imam al-Zaman, who is the place of Allah, preparing one's form and bearing with a true heart toward that inner gate
- Struggle in the way of Allah: the outward fight | to sacrifice and slay one's own commanding self when the command comes from the Imam al-Zaman

Annotated structure, the two kinds of the alms of knowledge, mirroring the two kinds of material alms.

Whole: just as material alms divides in two, so the alms of knowledge divides in two.

Parts, the parallel drawn out:
- Material alms of breaking the fast: owed by every person | knowledge owed by nature to every person, which must be explained to all
- Material alms of wealth: falls due only when wealth accumulates | knowledge owed only when one's store has grown to the threshold that makes a portion obligatory to dispense

Annotated structure, the example of the fifth, anchored in Imam Husayn.

- The outward law: a fifth of an unexpected windfall must be given out
- The example: Hazrat Imam Husayn, upon him be peace, used each month to set aside the fifth of his very salary, reasoning that he had not expected such money to come
- The inner application: if some knowledge descends upon you unexpectedly from the saints of Allah, its fifth part must be given out

Annotated structure, the single movement that unifies every inner meaning. The whole is one act repeated across the pillars; the parts are its forms.

Whole: each outward pillar, in its depth, is an act performed upon knowledge and upon the self in relation to the appointed guide.

Parts, the one movement in its forms:
- Purity: a cleansing by knowledge
- Prayer: the enacting of the guide's command
- Alms and the fifth: the dispensing of knowledge received
- Fasting: the guarding of knowledge from the unworthy
- Pilgrimage: the turning of the whole self toward the inner gate
- Struggle: the slaying of the self that resists, the greatest struggle being the slaying of the commanding self, so that the true hero is the one who has killed within himself that great beast which is his own commanding soul

The Father of Imams drew the distinction that explains why dispensing knowledge counts as alms, since alms ordinarily names a parting-with that leaves the giver poorer:

> Knowledge is better than wealth. Knowledge guards you, while you have to guard wealth. Wealth decreases by spending, while knowledge multiplies by spending. Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Father of Imams, *Nahj al-Balagha* (compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi), Saying 147, trans. Sayyid Ali Reza.

Contrast pair, the alms of wealth against the alms of knowledge, on what spending does to the store.

Column A: The alms of wealth
- What it is drawn from: a finite store of possessions
- Effect of spending: it diminishes the store from which it is drawn
- The giver afterward: poorer than before

Column B: The alms of knowledge
- What it is drawn from: the knowledge received from the saints
- Effect of spending: it enlarges the very store it is drawn from
- The giver afterward: increased, not lessened

Shared row: this is why guarding knowledge from the unworthy and giving it to the worthy are, in the end, the same care for a single trust. The law, read this way, is one continuous training of a soul in its knowledge and its allegiance, the very wilayah and action by which a soul reaches the Oneness of Allah.

## What this teaching lands

Process flow, the first rank now opened to view, traced from identical flesh to indwelling revelation.

Start: the Mukathir is made of the same flesh and blood as any other man, gathered within the same bodily bounds
  then: yet his temperament has been refined and his essence purified through an ascent that reaches even into his outward composition, though no eye can see it
  then: standing at the summit of the human, which is the threshold of the spiritual world, he holds the command of Allah and the Prophet before him in his every thought, word, and deed
  then: by that holding he becomes a house from the houses of Allah, a place upon which revelation descends
  then: the Qur'an's houses, and the men whom no commerce diverts, are these very ranks, whose establishing of prayer is, in its depth, the establishing of the divine summons
  then: beneath every pillar, allegiance, purity, prayer, alms, fasting, the fifth, pilgrimage, and struggle, there lies just such an inner meaning
End: the first rank is no mere title on the lowest rung; the Mukathir is a human being whose every thought, word, and deed has been bound so wholly to the command that he himself becomes a dwelling upon which the revelation of Allah alights, and in whom the entire inner law of the religion has been lived out from within
