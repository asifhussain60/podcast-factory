# Equal but Not Infallible

## Where this episode picks up

Annotated structure, the sharp problem the episode opens with.

Whole: a single equal beginning that somehow gives rise to an ordered hierarchy.
- The premise: the Originator brought the luminous forms into being from nothing, in perfect equality, as an act of justice
- The strain: the whole volume has been about ranks, ascents, and declines, some rising toward the source and some sinking below the beasts
- The contradiction: if all began equal, with nothing to separate the first from the last, no preference, no inclination, no disparity in great or small, then where did error enter
Callout: the resolution of the whole episode is one fact, that the equal forms were not yet infallible, and from that single fact the drama of ascent, error, and the first declaration of Oneness unfolds.

Contrast pair, two states the same forms pass through.

Column A: The beginning
- Condition: perfect equality
- Cause: an act of justice by the Originator
- Standing: no rank, no preference, no disparity

Column B: What emerged from it
- Condition: an ordered hierarchy of ascent and decline
- Cause: the use or misuse of equal powers
- Standing: some rise toward the source, some sink below the beasts

Shared row: the ordered hierarchy did not contradict the original equality, it grew out of it through the freedom that lay between the two states.

## No obstacle, a single instant

Process flow, why the originated order came into being all at once.

Start: the abode of origination and the abode of emanation are free of the obstructing matters
  to
The forms there are abstracted from matter, pure forms attached to no matter, with no matter in them
  to
Matter is what obstructs, so where there is no matter, no hindrance or impediment arises
  to
Power and ability are present and can show their effect freely, no obstruction set in their way
  to
End: with no obstacle, the existence of the existents came to be not in time but in a single instant, all at once, in one moment

Contrast pair, two ways existence can come to be.

Column A: The world of matter
- Mode: sequence, first this, then that
- Image: a builder laying one course of bricks after another
- Why: one thing must be moved aside before another can take its place
- Time: an interval divides cause from effect

Column B: The abode of origination
- Mode: a single instant, the whole at once
- Image: light filling a lit room without traveling across it
- Why: no matter is present to delay the effect
- Time: cause is present and the whole effect is there at once

Shared row: we imagine creation as a builder's sequence, but sequence belongs to matter alone, and where there is no matter there is no delay.

Visual metaphor, the two images of instantaneous origination.

Metaphor: illumination that fills a space all at once.
- First image: sunlight spreading through the expanse of the air, the whole air illumined at once, not piece by piece
- Second image: a fire kindled in a dark house, the entire room filled with light the moment it is lit
- The thing figured: the whole world of origination came into existence in a single instant, like air flooded with sunlight, like a dark house suddenly illumined
Reading: the cause is present and the effect is total and immediate, the way light does not cross a room but simply fills it.

Verbatim, scripture measures the most total event by the swiftest image the eye knows.

> And unto God belongs the Unseen of the heavens and the earth. And the Command of the Hour is but as the twinkling of an eye, or nearer still. Truly God is Powerful over all things. (chapter 16, verse 77, Surat al-Nahl), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Reading: if even the Hour comes as swiftly as the eye blinks or faster, then the origination of the world, where there is no matter at all to slow it, is the more reason that no interval of time divides the command from the existence it calls forth.

Verbatim, the citation the teaching fixes its point upon.

> In the abode of origination there is no obstacle, precisely because the forms there are abstracted from the matter that obstructs.
> Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani, *Rahat al-'Aql*; on this work see Paul E. Walker, *Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani: Ismaili Thought in the Age of al-Hakim*.

## Potential and actual together

Annotated structure, the simultaneity of the intellects.

Whole: in that abode, no thing's existence preceded another's.
- The actual intellects: those subsisting in actuality, established and realized
- The potential intellects: the powers given to the intellects, afterward actualized and established
- The relation: the existence of the actual did not come before the existence of the potential, the existence of all was together
Callout: both the intellect subsisting potentially and the intellect subsisting actually existed at one and the same time, neither preceded by some prior existing thing.

Contrast pair, distinction in time versus distinction in order.

Column A: A distinction in time (rejected)
- Picture: the forms first existed merely possible, only later became real
- Sequence: the first perfection happens, then after an interval the second
- Source: our time-bound imagination, which needs a span to follow the matter
- Error model: one thing happening after another

Column B: A distinction in order (the teaching)
- Picture: the potential and the actual co-existed from the outset
- Sequence: no temporal gap stands between the first perfection and the second
- Source: the character of the first perfection, light that fills the whole the instant it is lit
- Error model: a failure to move from one perfection to the perfection given alongside it from the first

Shared row: holding the distinction of order apart from the distinction of time is the key to everything the rest of the episode says about error.

Process flow, why no interval divides the two perfections.

Start: our understanding seems to need some span of time to follow the matter, even a kindled fire seems to take a moment before the room fills
  to
But the light here is not ordinary light that spreads gradually
  to
The instant it is lit, the whole is filled at once, and this is the character of the first perfection
  to
End: there is no interval of time separating the first perfection from the second, the gap is in order, not in time

## Equal but not infallible

Annotated structure, what the first perfection does and does not include, following Sayyidna Mohyuddin.

Whole: the state of first perfection, equal yet not the perfect, infallible state.
- Equality: in their first existence the beings are all equal, as already established
- The qualification: along with that equality, in action and deed, they are not infallible from error
- The contrast of states: the first perfection is the lower, initial condition, the second perfection is the perfect, infallible state
Callout: in the first perfection, though the beings are equal, they are not protected from error, and error can proceed from them.

Annotated structure, what "error" precisely means.

Whole: error is the failure to attain the second perfection.
- First face: a delay in responding to the invitation, a refusal to come when called
- Second face: the misleading of others, the setting of others upon the wrong path
- The condition that allows it: the beings are in the state of first perfection, in which they are not infallible
Callout: the first perfection was given as a test, go straight and use rightly the powers given to you, it is the trial-state of equality from which one must journey to the second perfection.

Process flow, how the ranks emerged from a single equal beginning.

Start: the equal forms did not start out ranked, but they started out free, given powers and the freedom to use them, not yet sealed against error
  to
Some used their powers to attain the second perfection
  to
Some failed to attain it
  to
End: the difference between attaining and failing is the difference that opened up the ranks, the ascents and declines, the Mukathir who rises and the man who falls below the cattle

Verbatim, the Qur'an frames the soul in this double light, fashioned equal then taught both roads.

> By the soul and the One Who fashioned it, and inspired it as to what makes it iniquitous or reverent! Indeed, he prospers who purifies it, and he fails who corrupts it. (chapter 91, verses 7 to 10, Surat al-Shams), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Reading: the soul is not made iniquitous or reverent, it is fashioned and then inspired as to both, taught the one road and the other. Prospering and failing are not stamped into it from the first, they are what becomes of it according to whether it purifies or corrupts what it was equally given.

Annotated structure, how this preserves the justice the teaching insists upon.

Whole: the justice of the beginning and the order of the end are not at odds.
- The equality: an act of justice, the Originator gave to none more and to none less
- The danger avoided: had ranks been stamped in from the start, some made high and some low by no doing of their own, the justice would be hollow
- The arrangement: the forms were made equal and then set free
Callout: the order that later emerged was the fruit of the forms' own use or misuse of equal powers, so the hierarchy is not a violation of the original equality but its consequence.

## Slipping and being made to slip

Comparison matrix, the grammatical contrast that fixes the two faces of error.

Rows: he slipped, he made another slip
Columns: form of the verb | who acts | who is affected

- he slipped: the simple form | the being itself | the being itself, falling short
- he made another slip: the causative form | the being itself | another being, set upon the wrong path

Reading: just as one form means "he cut" and another "he had another cut", or one means "he prostrated" and another "he made another prostrate", so one verb carries the sense of going astray and a related form the sense of leading another astray.

Annotated structure, Sayyidna Mohyuddin's prayer at this point.

Whole: a prayer that Allah protect us from slipping, or from being made to slip, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny.
- First petition: protection against our own slipping
- Second petition: protection against our being made to slip, or our making others slip
- The grammar's work: the steady rule that one form means "he did" and another means "he had it done" carries the whole moral weight
Callout: in the trial-state of equality, a being can ruin itself, and a being can ruin others, and the seeker prays to be kept from both.

Verbatim, the Qur'an turns on the causative form at the first catastrophe of the human story.

> Then Satan made them slip therefrom, and expelled them from that wherein they were. (chapter 2, verse 36, Surat al-Baqarah), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Reading: of the fall it does not say merely that Adam and his wife slipped, it names a slipper who made them slip. The verb is the causative the teaching dwells upon, not "they slipped" but "he made them slip", carrying the whole weight the morphology was meant to bear, that error has one who suffers it and, sometimes, one who inflicts it upon another.

Contrast pair, the two faces of the single error.

Column A: Error against oneself
- Act: the form's own slipping
- Failure: its own failure to rise when called
- Reach: it falls short of the second perfection itself

Column B: Error against others
- Act: making others slip
- Failure: setting them on the crooked path so they too fail to rise
- Reach: it becomes the cause of another's falling short

Shared row: both are forms of the same failure the first perfection leaves possible, and Mohyuddin's prayer asks protection from both at once.

## The first to declare oneness

Process flow, the pivotal act of the first form to declare Oneness.

Start: one of the imaginal forms looked, with his essence, at his own essence and at the others of his kind
  to
He knew that they had an Originator unlike them, one different from them all, who had brought them into being
  to
He negated divinity from himself and from the others of his kind, he could not claim "I am the god", nor could any of his kind
  to
He affirmed divinity for their Originator, the Exalted, and bore witness to His oneness
  to
End: that very form became the Possessor of Tawhid, the first to declare the divine unity

Annotated structure, the three branches performed in a single act.

Whole: in one act the first form enacted the whole of Tawhid in its threefold completeness.
- The declaring of Oneness: bearing witness to the Originator's unity
- The divesting of names from the Originator: affirming Him beyond all that can be predicated
- The negating of divinity from the ranks: denying godhood to himself and all his kind
Callout: by looking at his own essence and his kind and recognizing an Originator unlike them, by negating divinity from all that was originated and affirming it for the Originator alone, he performed all three branches at once.

Contrast pair, the first form's act and the believer's Tawhid.

Column A: The first luminous form
- Setting: the dawn of existence, the originated world
- Act: looked at his essence and his kind, knew an Originator unlike them, negated and affirmed
- Status: the very first occurrence of the recognition

Column B: The believer who declares the Oneness
- Setting: his own horizon, climbing the chain of bewilderment
- Act: negates divinity from every rank and affirms it for the Originator alone
- Status: a repetition of the primordial recognition

Shared row: what the believer does when he declares the Oneness of Allah, the first luminous form did at the dawn of existence, the believer's Tawhid is a repetition of that first act.

Verbatim, the Commander of the Faithful gives this same movement its most exact formulation.

> The foremost in religion is the acknowledgment of Him ... and the perfection of His purity is to deny Him attributes, because every attribute is a proof that it is different from that to which it is attributed, and everything to which something is attributed is different from the attribute.
> Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Father of Imams, *Nahj al-Balagha*, Sermon 1, trans. Sayed Ali Reza, *Peak of Eloquence*.

Reading: to know the Originator and in the same breath to negate from Him every likeness to the originated is to do with a single recognizing glance what the Imam later set down as the perfection of sincerity, to affirm the Originator by denying that anything originated could be what He is.

## Seeking the means

Process flow, what the first form did after his act of recognition.

Start: he acknowledged Lordship for the Originator
  to
He glorified Him, was reverent, humbled himself, and submitted
  to
He sought means through the Originator's own greatness toward Him
  to
End: he supplicated, confessing his own lowliness through the means of that greatness

Annotated structure, the special point on which the teaching dwells.

Whole: the first form took the very greatness of the Originator as the means by which to reach the Originator.
- The barrier: the greatness of the Originator cannot itself be reached directly
- The move: he took that greatness as his means of approach, the intermediary by which he turned toward Him
- The confession: through that means he gave testimony to the Originator's Lordship and divinity, confessing his own lowliness
Callout: this is the meaning of seeking the means, to take the greatness of the Originator as the means to the Originator, when the Originator Himself cannot be reached.

Visual metaphor, the inversion at the heart of seeking the means.

Metaphor: the obstacle becomes the road.
- The height as barrier: the Originator's greatness is exactly what places Him beyond reach, the loftiness of His state is why no thought can rise to Him
- The turning point: that same greatness becomes the means of approach
- The height as ladder: the form reaches toward Him by way of that very greatness, making the unreachable height itself the ladder
Reading: what barred the approach becomes the means of approach.

Verbatim, seeking the means is the Qur'an's own imperative, not a later refinement.

> O you who believe! Reverence God, and seek the means of approach unto Him, and strive in His way, that haply you may prosper. (chapter 5, verse 35, Surat al-Ma'idah), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Reading: the command would be empty if the means were superfluous, that the believer is told to seek a means is itself the confirmation that the approach to the Originator runs through what He has appointed, and not through a bare, unaided turning of the self.

Contrast pair, the direct approach against the appointed means.

Column A: The direct approach (impossible)
- Claim: the self turns toward the Originator unaided
- Outcome: nothing rises, for His greatness bars the way
- Verdict: not a higher piety but the very error this chapter traces

Column B: The appointed means (the only road)
- Claim: the seeker reaches Him through the ranks, the wali, the appointed guide
- Outcome: he walks the only route there has ever been
- Verdict: the very pattern set by the first form at the dawn of existence

Shared row: the believer who binds himself to a guide is not settling for an indirect route in place of a direct one, he is walking the route the first Possessor of Tawhid walked.

Verbatim, the Sufi path reached the same conclusion in the homeliest possible form.

> Take a Pir, for without a Pir this journey is exceedingly full of woe and fear and danger.
> Jalal al-Din Rumi, *Mathnawi*, Book I, trans. Reynold A. Nicholson.

Reading: Rumi's Pir and the teaching's appointed guide name one necessity, the One who cannot be reached directly is reached by way of an appointed means, and to refuse the means in the name of a more direct approach is the very error this chapter has been tracing.

## What this teaching lands

Process flow, the hinge of the cosmology turning.

Start: the abode of origination is free of the matter that obstructs, so the whole order came into being not in time but in a single instant, sunlight flooding the air, fire filling a dark room
  to
The potential and the actual intellects existed together, with no interval of time between the first perfection and the second
  to
The equal forms, though created without the slightest disparity, were not yet infallible
  to
Error is exactly the failure to attain the second perfection, whether by one's own slipping or by making others slip, the two faces of the single fault Mohyuddin's prayer names
  to
One form looked at its own essence, recognized an Originator unlike it and its kind, and negated, affirmed, and bore witness in a single act, becoming the first Possessor of Tawhid
  to
End: he reached toward the Originator through the means of His own unreachable greatness, and from a beginning of perfect equality the whole order of ascent and decline was set in motion

Annotated structure, what the hinge holds together.

Whole: the first act of the cosmic order was a creature's recognition that it was not God.
- The beginning: perfect equality, free and not yet infallible
- The turning: the creature's turning, by way of God's own greatness, toward the God it could not reach
- The pattern: from this single equal beginning the whole drama of ascent, error, and the declaration of Oneness unfolds
Callout: the justice of the equal beginning and the ordered hierarchy of the end are reconciled in the freedom that lay between them, the freedom to attain the second perfection or to fall short of it.
