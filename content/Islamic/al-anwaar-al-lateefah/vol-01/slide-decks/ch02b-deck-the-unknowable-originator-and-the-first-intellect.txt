# The Unknowable Originator

## Where this episode picks up

Annotated structure, the question this episode opens.

Whole: how do you point at something that no pointing can ever reach?
Parts of the problem:
- The road of allegiance and action leads the exiled soul back toward its origin
- The origin is the One who made the soul
- The moment we turn to face that One directly, every faculty of grasping fails at the threshold
- He is not far the way a distant mountain is far, He is beyond the very faculties by which nearness and distance are measured at all

Process flow, the four hard turns this episode walks in order.

Start: the soul turns to face its Originator directly
  down
Turn one: why no indication and no thought can fall upon Him
  down
Turn two: why even the names we give Him do not actually touch Him
  down
Turn three: what those names land upon instead
  down
End: why the word "Allah" on our lips is less a description than the sound of a mind that has come to the end of itself

## The Originator beyond reach

Process flow, why the discourse must begin with the name before it can deny everything about the named.

Start: there is no path even to the mention of these realities except by first mentioning Allah
  down
By the mention of Allah every door is unlocked
  down
By His most beautiful names a means is sought to Him in every request
  down
End: success is granted, so the discourse about the Originator begins, as everything begins, with His name

Contrast pair, the direction of glorification, who acts upon whom.

Column A: The creature
- Role: the glorifier
- Action: the act of praise rises from us toward Him
- Status: performs the glorification

Column B: The Originator
- Role: the One glorified, never the glorifier
- Action: receives praise, never the reverse
- Status: exalted so high that no indication of any indicator can fall upon Him so as to point Him out

Shared row: the cornerstone of right belief turns out to be a wall. The foundation of correct doctrine is that no indication reaches Him. Point as you like, the pointing finds nothing to land on, because He is not a thing among things that a gesture could single out.

Scripture sets the incommensurability at the root.

> Naught is like unto Him, and He is the Hearing, the Seeing. — Quran 42:11, the chapter of Consultation, trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Reading set as structure, why the verse builds the wall.

- Premise: nothing whatever resembles Him
- Consequence: there is nothing of His kind for a gesture to find
- Rule of indication: an indication can only single out one thing among others like it
- Conclusion: He keeps no such company, so a gesture meets nothing it could fasten to

Contrast pair, the two faculties that fail at the threshold.

Column A: The pointing gesture
- What it attempts: to single Him out among things
- Why it fails: He is not one thing among others like it
- Verdict: no indication of any indicator can fall upon Him

Column B: The traveling thought
- What it attempts: to cross from the heart to Him by imagination or reasoning
- Why it fails: the image is a bridge that cannot be crossed, no span is wide enough to carry the thought across the gap
- Verdict: neither imagination nor reasoning ever reaches the far side, the objective is never attained

Shared row: we may imagine and we may reason, but the objective is not the kind of thing that imagining or reasoning can close upon.

Annotated structure, the exact boundary of honest knowledge.

Whole: what we can claim to know of Him, and no more.
- The whole of what we know: since we were created, there must be One who created us, the bare fact of our own existence forces the affirmation of an Originator
- What lies past that boundary: what He is in Himself, what His nature is, how He is to be conceived, of this we know nothing whatever
- Where the method meets its hardest test: the book's declared method is to state pure reality directly, with no veiling interpretation and no symbol, the thing itself rather than a code for it
- The collision: the thing itself, when the thing is the Originator, is precisely what no statement can deliver, the pure reality of God is that He exceeds every reality we are able to state

## Seen only by insight

Process flow, the famous exchange from the time of the Commander of the Faithful, a trap with no safe exit.

Start: Ibn al-Kawwa comes meaning to trap him, asking "Have you seen your God? Have you seen Allah?"
  down
The snare, set both ways:
  - Say yes: you have made Him a visible object like any other
  - Say no: what kind of worshipper serves a God he has never seen?
  down
The answer: "Woe to you. Could I worship a God I had not seen? How could I worship a God without having seen Him?"
  down
The follow-up press: "How, then, did you see Him?"
  down
End: "I did not see Him with the eyes. I saw Him with insight, with the basirah."

Contrast pair, the two kinds of seeing the answer threads apart.

Column A: The physical eye
- Object: forms and things among things
- Capacity toward Him: cannot fall upon Him, any more than a finger or a traveling thought can
- Doctrine: absolute, the answer does not retreat from it for an instant

Column B: The basirah, the inner sight of the heart
- Object: not His essence, which remains sealed
- Capacity toward Him: an affirmation so certain that it deserves the name of vision
- Doctrine: the worshipper does not behold a form, he sees with a clarity beyond the senses that He is

Shared row: the denial of sight and the affirmation of vision are spoken in a single breath, and the seam between them is nothing other than the difference between the outward eye and the eye of the heart.

The Quran draws the line between the eye that fails and a perception that does not.

> Sight comprehends Him not, but He comprehends all sight. And He is the Subtle, the Aware. — Quran 6:103, the chapter of the Cattle, trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Annotated structure, the asymmetry the verse affirms in one breath.

Whole: a single verse that denies and affirms at once.
- First half: no faculty of vision encompasses Him, sight comprehends Him not
- Second half: He encompasses all vision, He comprehends all sight
- The eye: is comprehended, it is the object
- He: is not comprehended, He is never the object
Reading: what the basirah affirms is not a sight that grasps Him but a certainty that He is, held by the one faculty the verse never strips away. So He is not worshipped blindly, the whole of right worship lives in that distinction.

## Names fall only on their like

Annotated structure, the rule that unsettles the most ordinary religious speech.

Whole: when we call Him "Allah," "the Originator," or the bare exalted "He," none of these names and no attribute actually falls upon Him.
- The claim: neither the name nor the quality lights upon Him at all
- The reason, stated as a rule: names and attributes fall only upon their like
- The rule unpacked: a name applies only to something of its own kind, something already resembling it
- The conclusion: none can fall on the One who has no like at all

The Father of Imams makes the very denial of attributes the perfection of declaring God one.

> The foremost in religion is the acknowledgement of Him... and the perfection of sincerity to Him is to deny Him attributes, because every attribute is a proof that it is different from that to which it is attributed, and everything to which something is attributed is different from the attribute. — Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Father of Imams, *Nahj al-Balagha* (compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi), Sermon 1, trans. Sayyid Ali Reza.

Reading set as structure, why fastening an attribute fractures the unity.

- To fasten an attribute upon Him: is to set the attribute beside Him as a second thing
- To set a second thing beside Him: is to fracture the very unity the word was meant to honor
- Therefore: a name can fall only on its like, and none can fall on the One who has no like

Genealogy chain, where a name actually comes from. One link per line, direction of making marked.

We observe mercy in some creature we can perceive → we coin the word "Merciful"
The word "Merciful" → falls upon its like, a kind of knowledge and arrangement we could actually perceive
A human pronounces a name, a human assigns it → you named the child, you are the one who gave the name
The originated name → cannot fall upon its Originator as an equal, the made cannot characterize its maker as though the two were of one kind

The Ismaili witness on why created letters cannot reach their source.

> Since the very letters of the alphabet are originated things, they cannot serve as a true and verifying indicator of the Originator who made them. — Sayyidna Mu'ayyad al-Din al-Shirazi (paraphrase of the teaching).

Process flow, the objection raised and met.

Start: objection, there is a limit to our making, countless things proceed by His command before any word issues from us, so are we truly the makers of anything at all?
  down
Reply, holding to the precise point: the words I am speaking now, these I am genuinely creating, of these I am the maker
  down
The crux: it is exactly with this handiwork of ours, the tongue and the names it forms, that we are attempting to characterize our own Creator
  down
End: that is what cannot be done, our power of choice is real but it does not extend to recognizing Him, He dwells in an unseen whose curtains we have no power to lift

## Where the names land

Process flow, if the names do not fall upon Allah, where do they fall?

Start: name and attribute are negated from Allah the Exalted, they cannot simply hang in the air
  down
They fall instead upon His origination
  down
And what is that origination? It is the First Intellect
  down
End: whatever names and attributes we possess are stripped away from Allah and descend upon this first origination

Comparison matrix, the several names the teaching gives the first thing He brought forth. Rows are the names, columns name the sense and the role.

Rows: First Intellect, first existent, first emanation
Columns: what the name marks | the role it plays
- First Intellect: the first thing He brought forth | the highest and first of all created things
- First existent: the first thing that came to be | marks priority in coming-to-be
- First emanation: the first to issue forth from the originating act | marks issuance from the act of origination

Reading: when we speak, we are in truth speaking of what appeared from His act in His creation, not of any quality belonging to Him directly. All direct predication is severed from Him, it comes to rest on what He made.

Contrast pair, the two distances, and why they are not the same kind of distance.

Column A: The distance from our words to the First Intellect
- Nature: the names sit uneasily even on the highest and first created thing
- Size: already a kind of shortfall
- Verdict: even here the predication does not fall fully

Column B: The distance from our words to the Originator beyond it
- Nature: a gap of another order altogether, not a longer distance of the same kind
- Size: if the names do not fall even fully upon His origination, how could they ever be imagined to fall upon Allah Himself?
- Verdict: they never reach the Originator at all

Shared row: this relocation is one of the quiet hinges of the whole doctrine. It does not leave our religious language meaningless, the names still land somewhere and do real work, but it moves the whole apparatus of naming off the divine essence and onto the created order. What we describe when we describe "God" is the highest thing He made, while the Originator stands behind even that, affirmed but never described.

## An incapacity that spares none

Hierarchy tree, the reach of the incapacity to know the Originator. Every level named, no level skipped.

- Level 1: the whole of created existence
  - Level 2: the world of intellect, the realm of pure intelligences above
    - Level 3: the First Intellect, the loftiest of all created things, cannot know the Originator
  - Level 2: the world of sense, the realm of bodies below
    - Level 3: the dullest lump of matter, the lowest order, cannot know the Originator

Reading: the incapacity is not a peculiar weakness of human beings to be cured by sharper minds or longer study. It is comprehensive, embracing the entire world of intellect and the entire world of sense, with nothing left standing outside its reach.

Comparison matrix, the perfect leveling in the one matter of knowing Him. Rows are the extremes, columns name their ordinary rank and their standing before the Originator.

Rows: First Intellect, the human soul, the lowest body
Columns: ordinary rank in creation | standing before the Originator
- First Intellect: the loftiest of all created things, flooded with every light a creature can hold | cannot know the Originator, reaches the very same wall
- The human soul: far down the order, a dimmer portion | cannot know the Originator, simply equal to the First Intellect in this one respect
- The lowest body: the dullest lump of matter | cannot know the Originator, exactly as far as the mightiest intelligence

Reading: in the matter of knowing the Originator, the powerful and the overpowered are level, the sensed and the intelligible are level, the world of intellect and the world of body stand on even ground. The mightiest intelligence and the dullest matter are exactly as far from comprehending Him, which is to say, infinitely far.

Annotated structure, the strange consolation buried in the severity.

Whole: the inability to grasp the Originator is no mark of our particular failure.
- Not a deficiency we might have avoided with more diligence
- It is the condition of being a creature as such, to be made is to be unable to comprehend the Maker
- The limit is built into the very structure of created existence, not into our share of it alone
- The First Intellect, flooded with every light a creature can hold, reaches the very same wall we reach with our far dimmer portion

Annotated structure, the floor laid before the ladder is built.

Whole: the doctrine is about to build an immense hierarchy of ranks, an ordered ladder of spiritual stations rising one above another.
- Before a single rung is laid: the teaching plants a floor beneath the whole structure
- The floor: with respect to the Originator, every rung is equal, because every rung is created
- The reason: no created thing of any height can close the distance to the One who originated it
- What the ladder measures: the heights creatures reach toward each other and toward Him, never any nearer approach to His unknowable essence

## The cry of bewilderment

Genealogy chain, where the word "Allah" actually comes from. One link per line, direction marked.

The First Intellect contemplates the One who made it → finds no name adequate to Him
Finding no adequate name → the Intellect is overcome, bewildered before the Originator
Out of that bewilderment → the word "Allah" issues
The word "Allah" → is not a label fastened onto a known object, it is the sound a mind makes when it has reached the end of what it can know and still must speak

The Ismaili philosophers gave this collapse of language a precise discipline.

> He is not a thing and not not-a-thing; He is not existent and not nonexistent; He is not knowing and not ignorant, not powerful and not powerless. — Abu Ya'qub al-Sijistani, *Kitab al-Yanabi'* (The Wellsprings of Wisdom), trans. Paul E. Walker.

Process flow, the double negation as the disciplined form of bewilderment.

Start: the tongue offers an assertion, He is a thing, He is knowing
  down
First negation: deny Him the attribute, He is not a thing, He is not knowing
  down
Second negation: deny Him the denial itself, He is not not-a-thing
  down
End: no predicate, not even the predicate of having no predicates, comes to rest on Him, nothing remains standing but the bewildered silence out of which the bare word "Allah" escapes

Contrast pair, who shares the one word left, the highest and the most fervent.

Column A: The Imam, the most exalted knower
- Standing: the loftiest in knowledge of Him
- Before the unknowable: equal in the bewilderment
- What remains: the same single word "Allah"

Column B: The ardent lover, the most fervent seeker
- Standing: the most intense in longing for Him
- Before the unknowable: equal in the bewilderment
- What remains: the same single word "Allah"

Shared row: "Allah" is the common cry of every creature that has come up against the unknowable and confessed it. Whoever stands bewildered before Him reaches for the same word, united in having, at the very last, only this one word left.

Visual metaphor, the whole created order as circles turning about a center they cannot reach.

Abstract relation: every creature confronts an Originator it cannot comprehend and cannot rest in.
Metaphor: circles revolving about a fixed center.
- The center: the Originator, the fixed point about which a circle turns
- Why the circle turns at all: it is bewildered about its center, it cannot rest in a comprehension it can never reach, and so it revolves
- The many circles: one circle wheels around its center in bewilderment, then another circle comes bewildered about its own center, every circle turns, the reason always the same
- The heart of the image: the very energy by which the circle revolves is its bewilderment, the motion is not something added to the perplexity, the motion is the perplexity itself made visible as a turning

Reading: the whole created order circles eternally about a center it cannot comprehend, and the circling is nothing other than its unceasing wonder before the Originator. Our word "Allah" is one note of that turning. We do not name Him as we name a thing we have caught and examined, we name Him as the circle names its center, calling out from the motion itself the one word that confesses we cannot stop.

## The hard line drawn

Annotated structure, the hard line now fully drawn.

Whole: the cornerstone of right belief about the Originator, stated in full.
- No indication falls upon the Originator
- No thought bridges the gap to Him
- The names and attributes we use slide off Him entirely, because what is made can never characterize its maker
- Those names come to rest instead upon the First Intellect, the first thing He brought forth, and even there they sit uneasily, so that they never reach the Originator at all
- The incapacity to know Him is not ours alone but the common condition of everything created, the highest intelligence as much as the lowest body
- Our very word "Allah" is, at bottom, the cry of a bewildered creation circling a center it cannot reach

Contrast pair, what the hard line settles against the question it leaves standing.

Column A: Settled by this episode
- No indication, no thought, and no name reaches the Originator
- The names relocate onto the First Intellect, the first thing He brought forth
- The incapacity to know Him is universal, shared by the highest creature and the lowest alike
- The word "Allah" is the bewildered cry of a circling creation

Column B: Still unanswered
- If the First Intellect cannot know the Originator, yet it is the First Intellect that first speaks of Him, first negates divinity from itself and affirms it for Allah alone, then by what right does it name Him at all?
- What does that naming cost?

Shared row: the doctrine will press toward a strange and unsettling answer, that to name the Unnameable is itself a kind of sin, and that the mercy of the Originator joins that unavoidable sin, from the very first, to its own forgiveness. That is the burden the affirmation of Oneness must still take up.
