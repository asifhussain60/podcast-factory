# Trust and the Science of Realities

## A guarded invitation

Contrast pair, two instincts toward knowledge, attribute by attribute.

Column A: The ordinary book
- Aim: spread as widely as possible
- Measure of success: the wider the audience, the better
- Status of the content: information to be acquired
- Posture toward the reader: open to all, indiscriminate

Column B: These lessons (Anwaar al-Latifah)
- Aim: protected, carried only by those prepared to receive it
- Measure of success: faithful guarding, not reach
- Status of the content: an inheritance to be protected
- Posture toward the reader: admitted under conditions

Shared row: both are called knowledge. The inversion in Column B sets the tone for everything that follows, because the same reason it must be guarded is the reason it is worth having, it claims to be the only road by which the soul finds its way home.

Annotated structure, what this opening episode lays down.

Whole: the foundation before any doctrine of tawhid is taught.
Parts, in order:
- Origin, where these lessons come from
- Covenant, the obligations that govern their custody
- Architecture, how the book is built
- Doctrine, the path itself and the soul's return

## A trust bequeathed

Annotated structure, the nature of the content.

Whole: the lofty contents of the sciences of realities.
- Not drawn from speculation
- Not drawn from the ordinary disciplines of exoteric learning
- Interpreted and unfolded entirely upon the foundation of the sciences of the prophets and of the saints
- Every realization traces back to that prophetic and saintly fountainhead

Genealogy chain, how the knowledge reached the listener. One link per line, direction of transmission marked.

Sciences of the prophets and the saints → Hadrat Abdullah Sahib (the one to whom this knowledge is ascribed)
Hadrat Abdullah Sahib grants explicit permission → the recorder sets the lessons down
The recorder records → for his father and the other members of his family, that they might partake of these realities
By the express command of the Aaqa and Mawla → the recording is deposited in the treasury of the sacred mission

Contrast pair, the two sanctions that stand behind the words.

Column A: The permission of Hadrat Abdullah Sahib
- Function: authorized the recording in the first place
- Effect: allowed the words to be set down

Column B: The command of the Aaqa
- Function: consigned the recording to its proper place
- Effect: placed the words in the treasury of the sacred mission

Shared row: two sanctions, unambiguous and twofold. The listener is not overhearing a private lecture, he is being admitted, under conditions, to something authorized, gathered, and formally entrusted.

The old instinct to guard rather than broadcast, in the tradition's own words.

> These hearts are vessels, and the best of them is the one that retains [its contents] most.... Here, and he pointed to his breast, is abundant knowledge, could I but find bearers for it. — Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Father of Imams, in *Nahj al-Balagha* (compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi), Hikam (Saying) 147.

The difficulty was never a scarcity of knowledge, it was the scarcity of those fit to carry it.

## The covenant of safekeeping

Annotated structure, the request stated plainly at the outset.

Whole: a most humble request, because these lessons are a trust.
Prohibited handling:
- Do not email these lessons
- Do not exchange them in any manner upon the internet
- Do not store them upon your computer
Reasoning, not arbitrary caution:
- However secure an exchange appears, once placed upon the web a thing may become accessible to everyone
- The lessons may thereby fall into the hands of the undeserving
- The sciences of realities are not fit for indiscriminate circulation

The Quran's own framing of a weighty thing received as a trust.

> Truly We offered the Trust unto the heavens and the earth and the mountains, but they refused to carry it, and were wary of it, yet man carried it; truly he has proved himself an ignorant wrongdoer. — Quran, the chapter of the Confederates (chapter 33, verse 72), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

To be human is already to bear a trust the mountains declined. The covenant asked here is a small, deliberate echo of that primordial one, a thing accepted is a thing one becomes answerable for.

Process flow, breaking the trust and where accountability lands.

Start: the request is made known to the listener
  ↓
The forbidden act: copying, listening and passing onward, transmitting, handing to another, modifying, or any such deed without permission
  ↓
Verdict: this is a sin in the sight of Allah and His Messenger
  ↓
The reckoning on the Day of Judgment, before Allah and His Messenger
  ↓
Branch:
  - The doer: stands solely responsible for it
  - The recorder: free of any responsibility for the act
  ↓
End: the burden falls upon the hand that breaks the trust, not the one who faithfully delivered it

Contrast pair, the two destinies of the same knowledge.

Column A: The one who scatters it
- Standing of the act: violation of the trust
- Warning given: by the Friends of Allah themselves
- Outcome: the punishment of cold iron, described as the most severe and terrible of chastisements

Column B: The one who guards it
- Standing of the act: faithful keeping of the trust
- Blessing prayed for: that Allah prolong the shadow of Sayyidna al-Da'i and the noble dignitaries, and make their lights flow continually
- Outcome: illumination by the very knowledge that could condemn the careless

Shared row: the safeguarding of this knowledge is not etiquette but consequence reaching into the next world. The covenant and the blessing are spoken in one breath.

## The five-fold architecture

Annotated structure, the meaning of the book's parts.

Whole: each major division is called a Suradiq, and the work opens with the First Suradiq from the Book of Lights.
The senses the word carries:
- A curtain
- A palace
- A grand encampment, a great tent
- A canopy or royal pavilion within which the matters of the book unfold
Reading: an enclosing pavilion, and inside it the realities are set out in order.

Hierarchy tree, the architecture built upon the number five. Every level named, no level skipped.

- Level 1: The whole book, five Suradiqs
  - Level 2: Each Suradiq, five chapters (abwab)
    - Level 3: Each chapter, five sections (fusul)

Computation: five Suradiqs, each of five chapters, each chapter of five sections.
5 Suradiqs → 25 chapters → 125 sections in all.

Reading: the design is not decorative. It tells the listener that what follows is a single ordered structure, not a loose collection of talks, and that every section has a fixed place within a larger architecture of fives. With this fixed, the book turns to the very first section of the first chapter of the first Suradiq.

## The method of pure reality

Contrast pair, what the book refuses against what it undertakes.

Column A: What the book does NOT do
- Not the outer exoteric meaning of these matters
- Not ta'wil, the ascent into esoteric interpretation
- Not a veil lifted to show a secret beneath the letter
- Not a code to be deciphered

Column B: What the book DOES
- Treats the noble affections (mawaddah) that lead the soul toward the noble obligations
- Takes them as a pure reality, whole and unmixed
- Sets down that pure, unmixed reality directly
- States the realities as realities, as far as they can be stated at all

Shared row: much of the Ismaili tradition is famous for ta'wil, reading an inner meaning beneath scripture and law. Here the author signals he is doing something different, the thing itself, not a code for it.

Annotated structure, the triple negation and its grammatical force.

Whole: in this pure reality there is no interpretation and no symbol.
The negations, each emphatic:
- No interpretation, voiced as the negation of the genus, an absolute categorical denial, not merely no particular interpretation but no interpretation whatsoever
- No symbol from it
- No allusion
Reading: to negate the genus leaves no room for some interpretation somewhere. The intention is pure reality, stripped of veiling interpretation, of symbol, and of allusion alike. The whole drama of the chapters to come is the strain of keeping this promise about an Originator who exceeds all speech.

## The twin path to tawhid

Comparison matrix, the components by which guidance toward tawhid is given. Rows are the components, columns name what each is and its role.

Rows: gnosis, obedience and love, nearness, hudud, lawful action
Columns: Arabic term | what it is | role on the road
- Gnosis: marifah | true knowing of all that came into being before us | sets the soul upon the road
- Obedience and love: directed to the Friends of Allah | devotion and submission to them | by them the soul draws near to Allah
- Nearness: drawing near by means of the Friends | proximity won through them | advances the soul along the road
- Hudud: the ranks and bounds appointed for the believer | appointed from the direction of the Riser himself | adherence keeps the soul on the road
- Lawful action: amal | the deed laid down in the religious law | completes the road

By the whole of this, guidance is given toward the tawhid of Allah, the Most High. This is the only path by which one may reach the Oneness of Allah.

Contrast pair, the two pillars distilled, upon which all access rests.

Column A: Wilayah
- Also named: loyal allegiance, and love
- Substance: the bond of devotion to the Friends of Allah and submission to the ranks they appoint
- Failure mode alone: devotion alone does not arrive

Column B: Amal
- Also named: the deed, the action
- Substance: the work laid down by the law
- Failure mode alone: knowledge alone does not arrive

Shared row: access to the tawhid of Allah is attained through these two and these two only. By loving allegiance and by righteous action joined, one attains the Oneness of Allah. There is no other way through, the two must be joined.

The Prophet's final counsel, the source of the twin pillars.

> Verily I am leaving among you two weighty things: the Book of Allah and my progeny, the people of my household. So long as you hold fast to them both, you will never go astray after me. — narrated from Zayd ibn Arqam; *Sunan al-Tirmidhi* 3788 (the hadith al-thaqalayn); cf. *Sahih Muslim*, Kitab Fada'il al-Sahaba, 2408.

Mapping of the bequest onto the pillars, one line each.
The Book of Allah → amal, the action laid down by the law
The progeny, the people of the household → wilayah, allegiance to the appointed ranks
Reading: scripture and the appointed ranks are handed down together, in a single breath. To grasp one while letting the other fall is to lose the very guidance the Prophet entrusted.

Annotated structure, the twofold fruit of treading the path.

Whole: what the twin pillars of allegiance and action are for.
- Fruit one: the ranks of the afterlife are raised up for the soul, an elevation of standing in the world to come
- Fruit two: salvation from this lower world of being and corruption, release from the realm of generation and decay in which we are presently confined
Reading: the path does not merely improve a life here, it lifts the soul's standing in the next world and frees it from the very order of coming-to-be and passing-away.

## The return to the origin

Process flow, the soul's journey understood as a homecoming.

Start: the world of spirits (alam al-ruh), the soul's original homeland, the source from which the noble beginnings first issued
  ↓
Descent into this lower world of being and corruption, which is not the soul's native country
  ↓
The religious life entered, a leading-back directed toward the origin
  ↓
The ascent and the arrival, aided by Allah
  ↓
End: dwelling together with the Friends of Allah in the Abode of Sanctity

The mystic's image of the soul as exile, in Rumi's own opening.

> Listen to the reed how it tells a tale, complaining of separations, saying, "Ever since I was parted from the reed-bed, my lament hath caused man and woman to moan.... Every one who is left far from his source wishes back the time when he was united with it." — Rumi, *Mathnawi*, Book I, lines 1 to 4, trans. Reynold A. Nicholson.

The reed cut from the bed and the spirit cut from the alam al-ruh are the same figure, the homesickness of something that knows, however dimly, that it does not belong here.

The origin as a covenant once made and forgotten.

> When thy Lord drew forth from the Children of Adam, from their loins, their descendants, and made them testify concerning themselves, [saying]: "Am I not your Lord?", they said: "Yea! We do testify!" — Quran, the chapter of the Heights (chapter 7, verse 172), trans. Abdullah Yusuf Ali.

The knowledge of the origin is in one sense a recollection, a turning back toward a Lord the soul once acknowledged with its own tongue and then lost sight of amid generation and decay.

Visual metaphor, the knowledge of the origin as a center that creatures circle.

Abstract relation: every creature longs toward the knowledge of the root, the knowledge of the origin, the answer to where we came from.
Metaphor: a center with revolving bodies around it.
- The center: the knowledge of the origin, around which all creatures revolve and toward which they incline
- The revolving many: every person, every creature, making their rounds, yearning to know where their origin lies
- The barrier: for the generality there is no path at all to it, they revolve forever about a center they cannot attain

Reading: all creation circles a goal it cannot reach, except for one decisive exception.

Contrast pair, the many against the few.

Column A: The generality of creatures
- Posture: revolve about the center, yearning toward it
- Access: no path at all to the knowledge of the origin
- Outcome: circle endlessly around a goal they cannot reach

Column B: The few
- Posture: held fast to the Imams from the progeny of Muhammad, peace be upon them
- Access: bound themselves to the Imams' true and blessed hudud
- Outcome: only these break out of the circle and reach that side

Shared row: the knowledge of the origin is not available to bare seeking, however intense. It is granted only through fast adherence to the Imams and submission to their appointed ranks, which is to say, through exactly the wilayah and amal the path was built upon.

## The foundation laid

Annotated structure, the four foundations now in place.

Whole: the ground laid before the hardest question can be asked.
- Origin: a sanctioned trust, drawn from the sciences of the prophets and saints, recorded by permission, deposited in the treasury of the sacred mission, bound by a covenant of careful safekeeping
- Architecture: a single ordered structure of fives
- Method: to state pure reality directly, with no interpretation and no symbol
- Doctrine: the soul reaches the Oneness of Allah only by loving allegiance and lawful action, and by that road returns from this world of corruption to the homeland of spirits

Contrast pair, what is settled against what still stands open.

Column A: Settled by this episode
- A guarded trust with a known provenance
- An ordered book of fives
- A declared method of pure reality
- A road of allegiance and action that leads the exiled soul home

Column B: Still unanswered
- The nature of the One toward whom the entire return is directed
- Why no indication, no thought, and no name can reach Him at all

Shared row: the creatures circle a center they cannot reach, only those who hold fast to the Imams break the circle. The ground for even asking the hardest question has now been laid.
