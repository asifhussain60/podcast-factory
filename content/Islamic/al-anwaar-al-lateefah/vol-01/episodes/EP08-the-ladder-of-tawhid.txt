# The Ladder Of Tawhid

## Opening directive
Open with a brief, warm welcome. Greet the listener and name the book: *The Subtle Lights*, Volume One — "The Oneness." Preview in one sentence: the disciple who called his teacher a "god" climbs that bewilderment to the One. Spine verbatim (1 of 3): **No rank is a god — only the One bewildered before none.** No "today we'll discuss."

## Name discipline
ONE label per figure, never rotating → the seeker, the teacher, the Mukathir, the Imam, the First Intellect, the Originator, the Commander of the Faithful, the Prophet, Rumi. No other Arabic names. Concepts → English: tawhid = the Oneness; tanzih = transcendence; tajrid = stripping attributes; da'wah = the summons.
- The Commander of the Faithful; first mention only: the Commander of the Faithful, peace be upon him. Never pair his leadership-title with his personal name.
- The Prophet; first mention only: the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- Mukathir: Mukathir (perfected master)
- ilah: ilah
- alif, lam, ha: alif, lam, ha
- Imam: Imam
- Allah: Allah

## Three-part focus
Walk in order; each beat lands once.
1. The crisis. Your guide to Allah is himself bewildered before the rank above — rank upon rank to the First Intellect, itself before its Originator. Bewilderment is no weakness but the first step of knowledge: the confession that one does not know.
2. The pivot. The seeker negates divinity from every rank and affirms it for Allah alone. A created god bears one mark — a nourisher above him, a need calling upward; only the One above whom there is none is God. He raises transcendence above limited and unlimited ranks, then strips every attribute — the Commander of the Faithful taught that any attribute fastened on Him sets a second beside Him. The linked circles and the letters of the word for "god" carry the same shape. Spine verbatim (2 of 3): **No rank is a god — only the One bewildered before none.**
3. The stakes. The climb is a circle, not a flight: "O tranquil soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and well-pleasing" (chapter 89, verses 27 and 28) — back to the station. The whole is sealed in the Qur'an's opening chapter and its three classes (chapter 1, verses 6 and 7).

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least three times and concedes once.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — why call a bewildered rank a 'god'?"; "That sounds like wordplay — strip God of every attribute, yet claim to know Him." Steelman each side. Host B must NOT open a turn with: Exactly, Yeah, Right, Of course, Wow.

## Tone constraints
Short sentences, thinking aloud. Name positionality: the classical Ismaili teaching of the ranks; flag uncertain dating. EXACTLY three governing analogies, the chapter's own; invent no others.
Analogy 1: a circle and its center — the seeker revolving around the rank above.
Analogy 2: linked circles ascending, each center orbiting the one above.
Analogy 3: the reed cut from its bed, aching to return — the soul called home.

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No surprise-performance or AI-cliche. Cite Quran verses by chapter-and-verse number in plain English, never an Arabic surah name. No cross-chapter or cross-episode references; treat it as standalone and don't pre-announce what comes next. R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. No formal-essay transitions (Firstly, Furthermore, In conclusion). Do not read this prompt aloud.

## Landing
Close on the return — the tranquil soul sent home, serene from its gnosis. Don't summarize "what we learned" or announce a next chapter. Spine verbatim (3 of 3): **No rank is a god — only the One bewildered before none.** End on a real question tied to action: which rank in my life have I let stand in for the One above all — and what would it cost to look past it?

Do not read this prompt aloud. The instructions above shape the conversation but are never spoken.
