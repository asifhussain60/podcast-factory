# The Ladder of Tawhid

*Episode eight of Al-Anwaar al-Lateefah, Volume One — The Oneness. The bewilderment in which a teacher became a "god" now becomes the first rung of an ascent. The seeker verifies that his teacher is himself bewildered before the rank above him, and that one before the next, rank upon rank up to the First Intellect bewildered before the Originator — and so he negates divinity from every rank and affirms it for Allah alone. This episode climbs that ladder, explains how the linked circles of the divine summons were formed and how the very letters of the word for "god" encode them, distinguishes the created gods from the one true God, and follows the tranquil soul home to its Lord — closing with the Qur'an's own summary of the whole matter in its opening chapter.*

## Where this episode picks up

If the guide who is leading you to Allah is himself lost in bewilderment before someone greater than he — and that one before another greater still — is your guide still your god? Or is his very bewilderment the thing that points past him to the One he cannot reach? The teaching has just left the seeker dazzled, circling his teacher as a circle turns about its center, and calling that teacher, in a carefully limited sense, a "god." That is where the danger and the opportunity both lie. To stop there would be to mistake a created rank for the Creator. But the bewilderment that fixed the seeker upon his teacher contains within it the means of its own correction, for the seeker can look and see that his teacher is bewildered too. This episode follows that looking all the way up, and shows how the climb of bewilderment becomes the true act of declaring the Oneness of Allah.

## The chain of bewilderment

The decisive step is this: the seeker comes to verify that the very teacher before whom he stands bewildered is himself bewildered before the rank above him. The seeker sees that he is bewildered by the Mukathir, but that the Mukathir in turn is poor, needy, and bewildered before the one who bestows upon him. And the one above the Mukathir is in turn bewildered before the one above him, and so the chain continues, rank upon rank, in the same manner through the physical ranks, until it reaches the Imam, upon him be peace. And from the Imam it ascends further, toward the abstract intellects — those intellects that are not in the physical world at all, the angelic realities of the higher realms. The ascent ends, at the summit, at the First Intellect, which was itself bewildered before all the originated and created beings, perplexed at how it attained its precedence, even though all were brought into existence together at one time. And then the seeker verifies the bewilderment of the First Intellect itself before its Originator — the Originator of itself and of all its kinds — and its perplexity before Him.

There is a deep lesson embedded in this universal bewilderment. The first step in acquiring knowledge is humility, the confession that one does not have knowledge. One must first become, as it were, ignorant — must own one's not-knowing — before one can acquire. This is the very posture the angels themselves assumed when they were confronted with a knowledge that had not been given them:

> They said, "Glory be to You! We have no knowledge save what You have taught us. Truly You are the Knower, the Wise." (chapter 2, verse 32), trans. Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al., *The Study Quran*.

Asked to name what they had not been taught, the angels do not bluff; they confess the limit of their knowing and hand all knowledge back to its source, and it is precisely out of that confession that they are made ready to receive. The seeker's bewilderment is the same confession enacted before the ranks above him. The station of bewilderment is therefore not an obstacle to be overcome but a precondition to be embraced: as the circle revolves around its center, so the seeker revolves in bewilderment, and that very revolving is the beginning of gnosis. What looked, at first, like a vertigo that might trap the seeker turns out to be the engine of his ascent. Every rank above him is caught in the same motion; bewilderment is not his private weakness but the universal condition of every created thing before what stands above it, all the way to the First Intellect before the Originator.

## Negating divinity, affirming Allah

Having traced the bewilderment to its source, the seeker performs the act of Tawhid: he negates divinity from the rank in his own horizon, and affirms it for Allah the Exalted, and proclaims His greatness — He besides whom there is no god. The seeker negates divinity from the teacher in his own horizon — that is, from the Mukathir — and from every rank above the Mukathir, physical and spiritual, all the way to the end of the chain. He declares: none of these is a god who gives me my being; each of them is bewildered before another above him, and the First Intellect itself is bewildered before the Originator.

The very condition that the First Intellect held toward the Originator — its bewilderment — the seeker now recognizes reproduced at every level, including in his own relation to his teacher. So he affirms: this Mukathir, for all his greatness and the majesty of his station, which I have truly recognized, is not my god; above him is one, and above that one another, up to the summit, and all of them are bewildered, and the truly Real is the Originator alone. And so the seeker affirms divinity for Allah alone and proclaims His greatness — He besides whom there is no god. This is the structure of the ascent made explicit. The teacher has his circle; above him is the circle of the one who bestows upon him; above that, another; and all these circles are linked one to another, ascending until the final circle, which is that of the First Intellect. The believer's path of Tawhid is precisely this: first he confesses that his teacher has become a god for him, in the sense of the center of his bewildered orbit; then he verifies that the teacher is himself bewildered before another, and so denies that the teacher is his god in the absolute sense; and so on up the linked circles until he reaches the true God beyond them all. The bewilderment is not denied; it is followed home.

## The circles of the da'wah

How did these linked circles come to be? The teaching explains by recounting the formation of the divine summons in the spiritual world. When the summons was given there, those who joined and repented with the repentance of the caller ascended along with him; they went up with him. Those who did not join were given the summons anew. The pattern works thus: a circle has at its center a forerunner; he calls, and those who answer and repent with his repentance ascend with him; the final individual left behind, who has not ascended, is given the command — "Our work is finished; now you do the work" — and he becomes the center of a new circle. Around him gather those who repent with his repentance, and again the last individual left is given the command in his turn. In this way circle was generated from circle, each formed upon the pattern of the first, the first pattern having been established there in the spiritual world. And the bewilderment that orders each circle is this: the center of a given circle, around whom others revolve, must himself revolve around the center of the circle above his own; he too is in orbit, so that the structure of bewildered revolution is reproduced at every level.

All of this, the teaching says, is contained in the testimony there is no god but Allah — and this brings it to a remarkable reading of the very word for "god," which it finds to encode the three conditions present in every one of these circles. The word for "god," ilah, is composed of three letters: alif, lam, ha. The alif shows that there is precedence without an inspirer — that is, the First Intellect, who preceded all the rest by his own act, with none above him to prompt him. The lam shows the condition of the second: advancing not by independent precedence but by seeing another and repenting with his repentance — the station of the one emanated from the First, who attained his rank by following. And the ha shows the third condition: the giving of the summons, which was performed by the seven intellects, the tenth not giving it. These three conditions are present in every circle, for in every circle there is everything; the whole story of the life of the universe is contained in this composition. The letters of the word for "god" thus trace the very structure of forerunner, follower, and summons that constitutes each circle of the hierarchy.

## Created gods and the true God

The teaching now draws out fully the contrast between the created "gods" and the one true God. The seeker affirms divinity for Allah and proclaims His greatness, declaring that there is no god from among these deities that are created, invented, and originated — and here is their distinguishing mark: each one of these created gods carries within himself the name of the nourisher who stands above him, and the need that calls him upward toward that nourisher. A created god is recognizable precisely as a created god by this: that he is sustained by one above him and impelled by a need toward one above him; he is never self-sufficient. The only being who is not such a created god, who has no nourisher above him and no upward-calling need, is the one before whom the bewilderment of high and low alike is occasioned by the loftiness of His state — the one above whom there is no higher, whose every subordinate is bewildered before Him while He is bewildered before none. So there is no god besides Him.

The seeker then completes the threefold movement at the highest level. First comes the fullness of tanzih: he declares the one God transcendent above the physical ranks, which are limited, and above the spiritual ranks, which are unlimited — for the true God, the nourisher of all, is beyond both the bounded corporeal hierarchy and the unbounded spiritual hierarchy alike. Then comes tajrid: he strips from the Originator all the names, attributes, descriptions, and relations that he had affirmed for the ranks, for these belong to the physical and spiritual ranks, but the Originator is beyond them, God in the absolute sense, divested of all that can be predicated. The Father of Imams placed this very stripping of attributes at the summit of declaring the Oneness:

> The foremost in religion is the knowledge of Him, and the perfection of professing His Oneness is the affirmation of His purity, and the perfection of His purity is to deny Him attributes — because every attribute is a proof that it is other than that to which it is attributed, and everything to which something is attributed is other than the attribute. — Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Father of Imams, *Nahj al-Balagha*, Sermon 1, trans. Sayed Ali Reza.

His reasoning is exactly what the seeker's tajrid performs: to fasten any attribute upon the Originator is already to set a second thing beside Him — the attribute that is "other than" Him — and so to compromise the very Oneness one means to affirm. The stripping is not an impoverishment of God but the only honest way to leave Him truly one. And finally the seeker returns to his own rank, and to his bewilderment within it, and to his obedience. The believer is bewildered before his teacher; above the teacher are ranks, and above them ranks, up to the First Intellect, which is itself bewildered before another; and having traced all this, the seeker comes back and says, in effect: each must do his own work, and I will do mine. He returns to his own station, to his bewilderment within it, and to his obedience and commitment to his teacher. At that moment he becomes the one who performs true Tawhid, true tanzih, and true tajrid — with gnosis, with religion, with knowledge, and with certainty.

## The tranquil soul

To such a one Allah speaks the words of the Qur'an: O tranquil soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and well-pleasing (chapter 89, verses 27 and 28). The crucial word is "return," for the soul ascends to the very top and then returns. This is the movement: it goes up, and then it comes back down. To "return to your Lord" means to return to the one who bestowed upon you; and "well-pleased and well-pleasing" means that you yourself are well-pleased, and the one who bestowed upon you is well-pleased with you — both pleased together. The soul's tranquility lies in what it has come to know; its serenity is the fruit of its gnosis. And its pleasure lies in the command of its Lord, the Lord to whom it is returned. It is well-pleasing in His sight, and in the sight of all the ranks to whose gnosis the soul has reached and arrived.

There is something important in the shape of this. The ascent the seeker made — up the chain of bewilderment, negating and affirming — was not a flight that abandons the world below. The tranquil soul, having climbed to the summit, is sent back: "return to your Lord," return to the one who bestowed upon you, take up again your own station and your obedience. The Sufi tradition heard this same homeward longing in the cry of the reed flute, cut from its bed and aching to return:

> Listen to the reed, how it tells a tale, complaining of separations — saying, "Ever since I was parted from the reed-bed, my lament has caused man and woman to moan. Every one who is left far from his source wishes back the time when he was united with it." — Rumi, *Mathnawi*, Book I (the Song of the Reed), trans. Reynold A. Nicholson.

The reed's lament is the soul's: every being severed from its origin yearns back toward it, and the tranquil soul is the one whose yearning has at last been answered, called home to the source from which it was cut. The journey of Tawhid is a circle and not a one-way road; the soul that has seen the truth of the whole hierarchy comes home to its own place, and it is exactly there — content in its station and accepted by those above it — that its tranquility is found. The teaching closes this movement with words of grateful praise: praise be to Allah who guided us to this; we would not have been guided had Allah not guided us; indeed the messengers of our Lord came with the truth (chapter 7, verse 43).

## The Originator in the Qur'an

A question naturally arises: this Originator who created — is there mention of Him, and of the world of souls, in the Holy Qur'an, in its verses and in our supplications? The teaching affirms that there is. Different phrases at different places convey the matter — as in the verse that we were created from a single soul. But the summary of all that the Qur'an teaches about the Originator is its opening chapter, the chapter of praise, whose details are founded upon its first verse: Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds (chapter 1, verse 2). For He is the Lord of all the worlds, and the qualities of Allah are unfolded from this. The matter is thus mentioned both in supplication and in the Qur'an, its summary being the opening chapter, within which the whole teaching has been explained.

The opening chapter also encodes the three classes of people. The teaching is that Allah made three kinds: the repentant, then those who incur wrath, then the astray. Where in the opening chapter are these found? In the verse: Guide us upon the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor — not of those upon whom is wrath, nor of the astray (chapter 1, verses 6 and 7). Those upon whom favor was bestowed are the repentant: the favor given them was that they should return. Those upon whom is wrath are the insistent ones, who persist in their error, and upon whom wrath has therefore descended. And the astray are the bewildered, the ones lost in perplexity who have not found their way through. So the path that must be crossed is set before three classes: the repentant who are favored to return, the insistent who incur wrath, and the bewildered who go astray. Yet the reality of the Originator Himself cannot be grasped, and this too the Qur'an establishes, declaring Him glorified above whatever they describe. Whatever attributes are ascribed to Him, He is pure of them, beyond them all — and this is the very meaning of tanzih. Even the First Intellect, their own bestower, was incapable of the gnosis of the Originator; it did not possess that gnosis but was bewildered before Him, and it was on account of this bewilderment that the First Intellect became a god for those below. Even the Prophet, at the summit of created beings, confessed that the praise of God outruns every tongue that would attempt it:

> I cannot enumerate Your praise. You are as You have praised Yourself. — Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, narrated by Aisha, *Sahih Muslim*, Kitab al-Salat, no. 486.

If the most God-knowing of creatures must hand the praise of God back to God, unable to compass it himself, then the bewilderment of the First Intellect is no defect peculiar to it but the universal condition of every creature before the Originator. And so, when a word must at last be ventured: what word did they finally use of the Originator? "He" — and even that under compulsion, for they had no other word; the naming is a fault coupled with a plea for forgiveness, uttered out of necessity, because to affirm our own existence we must affirm the Existent, and to affirm Him we must address Him by some word, and the only word that does not falsely circumscribe Him is "He."

## What this teaching lands

The act of Tawhid has now been performed in full. The seeker who was left circling his teacher in bewilderment looked upward and saw that the teacher is himself bewildered before the rank above him, and that one before the next, rank upon rank through the physical and spiritual hierarchy to the First Intellect bewildered before the Originator. Recognizing that very bewilderment as the universal condition — and as the first step of all knowledge, which is the confession that one does not know — he negated divinity from every rank and affirmed it for Allah alone, completing tanzih above both the limited and the unlimited ranks and tajrid of every name and attribute, before returning to his own station and his obedience. The linked circles of the divine summons, and the very letters of the word for "god," were shown to carry this same structure of forerunner, follower, and summons; the created gods were distinguished from the one true God by their need of a nourisher above them; and the tranquil soul was sent home to its Lord, well-pleased and well-pleasing, its serenity the fruit of its gnosis — the whole matter summarized, the Qur'an teaches, in its opening chapter and its three classes of men. With this the first great movement of the volume comes to its close: the seeker who began circling a single teacher in bewilderment has followed that bewilderment up the whole ladder of the ranks and arrived, by negation and affirmation, at the One before whom all of them are bewildered and who is bewildered before none.
