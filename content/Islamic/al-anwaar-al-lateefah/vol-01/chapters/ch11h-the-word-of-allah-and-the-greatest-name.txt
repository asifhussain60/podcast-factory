# The Word and the Greatest Name

*Episode eleven of Al-Anwaar al-Lateefah, Volume One — The Oneness, and the close of the volume. In answer to the first form's seeking of means, the divine supports descend, and there connects with him the flowing knowledge and spreading light that is the Word of Allah — the testimony there is no god but Allah, above the intellect and below the Originator. Through it the First Intellect comes to encompass all knowledge, but only after first negating himself. The teaching reads the numbered composition of the Word and of the opening formula of the Qur'an, shows how reverence grows with knowledge so that it is the learned who most fear Allah, and raises the First Intellect to the supreme station of veil, gate, and Greatest Name. It closes with the cautionary case of the second emanation, undone by the delusion of equality, and the circle of oneness that belongs to the Originator alone.*

## Where this episode picks up

What single sentence could contain everything that has ever happened and everything that ever will — and end, by design, at the number seven? The previous episode left the first luminous form reaching toward the Originator through the means of His own greatness, having become the first to declare the divine Oneness. That reaching did not go unanswered. Something descended upon him, a flowing knowledge and a spreading light, and that light has a name and a structure — it is the Word of Allah, the testimony of Oneness itself, and the teaching now reads its inner composition as the skeleton showing through all of scripture and all of creation. This final episode follows that Word into the First Intellect, watches knowledge make him more humble rather than more proud, raises him to his supreme station, and closes the volume on the one circle no created being can ever enter.

## The Word of Allah

The seeking of means brought a great descent of confirmation upon the first form: there reached him from his Originator the divine supports and the confirmations of oneness, and there connected with him the flowing knowledge and the spreading light, which is the Word of Allah, the Exalted. There reached him the divine supports and the confirmations of oneness, and there connected with him the flowing knowledge and the spreading light. And this flowing knowledge and spreading light is the Word of Allah. What is that Word? It is the testimony there is no god but Allah. Through the first form's act of negation, recognition, and seeking of means, there flowed into him the flowing knowledge and spreading light which is that Word — the Word standing between the Originator and the originated, and through which knowledge and light flow.

For this reason it is said that the Word is above the intellect and below the Originator. The testimony there is no god but Allah occupies precisely this intermediate station: it stands beneath the Originator and above the intellect, and the flowing light came from the Originator down to it, and through it to the intellect. The Word is thus a continuous link, in which the Originator's will and the testimony of Oneness are manifested together. And the same Word is also given the name of the command, for all the commands are placed within it. The Word is no momentary thing; it is a link established for as long as creation endures. As long as the creation remains, the Word remains, and the flowing light remains. This is also why the world of origination ended at seven: the will of the Originator was that it should end at seven, for He had placed everything within the Word and willed that it terminate there.

## Encompassing all knowledge

Through the Word the First Intellect attained a knowledge that encompassed all things: through it he encompassed every thing in knowledge, and knew what had been and what would be. For the testimony there is no god but Allah contains everything, all that has happened and all that will happen. But there is a crucial sequence here. The complete knowledge had, in a sense, already been given with the first perfection; yet the First Intellect did not recognize it, did not know how to use it. What the light of the Word supplied was the understanding of what he had been given and the way to use the powers he possessed.

And before he could obtain the Word and its light, he had first to negate himself — to affirm that he was not his own creator. Just as the first form had said, "I am not my creator; our creator is someone else," so the First Intellect had first to confess that he and all his kind were not their own creators, but that their Creator was another. The moment he said this — the moment of self-negation — he obtained the Word, and in the light of the Word all things were obtained by him. Self-negation precedes the gift: one must first own one's not-being-the-creator before the Word and its all-encompassing light are bestowed. This is the same law the seeker met on the ladder of bewilderment, where the first step of knowledge was the confession that one does not know; here it appears at the very summit of creation, in the First Intellect himself. And what he found within his essence, in the light of the Word, was such joy and delight and happiness, such protection and praise and greatness and pride, that no pen could write its description and no tongue could translate it; from the light of the Word he knew that there would be salvation, and he found within himself a joy beyond all writing and all speech.

## The numbered composition

The teaching now reads the numerical composition of the testimony there is no god but Allah, which it takes to encode the whole structure of creation. The Word is composed in layers. First, it consists of negation and affirmation — two: the denial and the assertion. Second, the word for "god" within it contains three letters. Then there are the jewels of the letters — five. Then the words themselves — four. After this the count comes to its end at seven, and there are seven disjoined letters at which the pen, or the tongue, halts. The world ended at seven luminous forms precisely because of these seven points where the pen stops: in the luminous world the matter ended at seven forms, and it ended there because the Word, by the will of the Originator, was to end at seven. After the seven come twelve, the ascending spirits — and seven plus twelve make nineteen. This composition of two, three, five, four, seven, and twelve works everywhere: in every Imam, in every isthmus, in every believer, among all existents.

Where are these nineteen found? In the opening formula of the Qur'an — in the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful — there are nineteen. The numbers reappear within it. Where are the two? In the two kinds of grammatical construction the formula contains: the additive construction and the descriptive construction, of which the additive is the more excellent and the descriptive the lesser. The teaching also notes a striking feature of the testimony of Oneness itself: in its deeper reading the order is, as it were, inverted, for affirmation precedes negation — and of this it is said, "Let my affirmation be established by your negation; let me be negated such that only You remain." The teaching then cites the explanation of Sayyidna Muhammad Taher, may Allah sanctify his soul: that the seven letters of the opening formula's first word are the week of the seven Imams, and that with each Imam there are twelve of his deputies, twelve in number, which are the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. So the formula encodes the week of the seven Imams, each Imam with his attendant ranks, the whole built upon the pattern of the seven Imams' week; and the twelve deputies are, in reality, with every Imam, those who appear with the seventh Imam being of a very high rank, higher than the twelve before them, for he is a messenger. Thus the additive and descriptive constructions of the opening formula encode the seven Imams and their twelve deputies, and the additive construction is the more excellent.

## The fear of the learned

Having obtained the Word, the First Intellect did not grow proud but grew more humble. Because of that, he increased toward his Originator in humility and submission and bewilderment at His greatness and reverence. When the First Intellect obtained the Word, he increased even more in humility and submission toward his Originator, in bewilderment before His greatness, and in reverence. The more he came to know, the more humble and reverent he became.

The teaching grounds this in the Qur'an: Indeed, only those of knowledge among His servants fear Allah (chapter 35, verse 28). The teaching dwells on the grammar, noting that the particle in the verse is not here a negating particle but rather lends the force of "indeed," so that the sense is "indeed it is only the learned among His servants who fear Allah" — the particle carrying the force of "except." Every knower of the secret of creation and the causes of nature is, among all people, the one who fears Allah most. And this fear is not the fear of dread; it is the acknowledgment of His greatness. The more knowledge increases, the more the awe in the divine presence increases — not as terror, but as the recognition of His majesty. The teaching recalls how Amir al-Din al-Sadiq, upon him be peace, when the color of someone's face would change in his presence, would say, "Do you not know in whose court you are standing?" — for fear, rightly understood, is the acknowledgment of the greatness of the One before whom one stands. This overturns the common assumption that knowledge makes a person bolder before God and that only the ignorant tremble. The teaching insists on the reverse: it is precisely the one who knows most who fears most, because he alone has seen enough of the divine greatness to be rightly awed by it, and his fear is not dread of punishment but the clarity of standing knowingly in the presence of majesty.

## Veil, gate, and Greatest Name

The teaching now sets out the supreme station to which the First Intellect was raised: that Allah took him as a veil for Himself and made him a gate for Himself, through whom alone one may seek means to Him, and from whose presence alone one may hold intimate discourse, and through whose means alone He is declared One — so that he became His Greatest Name and His most noble, most honorable station. Allah took the First Intellect as a veil for Himself and made him a gate for Himself, an access gate to the divine. And three exclusive functions were vested in him.

First, one cannot seek means to Allah except through him: the means to the Originator can be sought only through the First Intellect. Second, one cannot hold intimate discourse with Allah except from his presence. And third, Allah is not declared One except through his means: true Tawhid cannot be performed except through the First Intellect. Until one acknowledges the First Intellect — until one acknowledges the wali — one cannot attain the second perfection, cannot seek the means, cannot hold the intimate discourse, cannot rightly declare the divine Oneness. By this exclusive mediation the First Intellect became the Greatest Name of Allah and the most noble and honorable station. This is the cosmological root of everything the volume opened with. The first episode taught that the soul reaches the Oneness of Allah only through loving allegiance to the Friends of Allah and the ranks they appoint; here we see why that must be so. The Originator placed the whole approach to Himself — the seeking of means, the intimate discourse, the very declaring of His Oneness — behind a single veil and through a single gate, so that there is no reaching Him except by way of the one He appointed. Allegiance to the wali is not an extra devotion added onto Tawhid; it is the only door through which Tawhid can be performed at all.

## The delusion of equality

The teaching now introduces the contrasting case of the second emanation, who failed where the first succeeded. The First Intellect was taken as veil and gate; but the second emanation was deceived by a corrupt notion. He too acknowledged the First Intellect — he conceded the First Intellect's superiority and recognized that both their existences were real. But what undid him was the delusion of equality: he imagined that his own vision was equal to the vision of the First Intellect, the first emanation. And because of this delusion, he could not attain the second perfection.

The principle is exact, and it gathers up the whole cosmology. In the state of first perfection, all are indeed equal; but in the state of second perfection, no one can be equal. In the second perfection none is equal to another; each is unique, and the ranks are fixed in their before-and-after order. The second emanation's error was to carry the equality proper to the first perfection into the second, claiming for his vision a parity with the First Intellect's that the second perfection does not permit. He acknowledged the First Intellect's superiority and yet claimed equality with the first emanation — and in that contradiction he forfeited the second perfection. This is the subtlest failure in the whole volume, and the most cautionary, because it looks so much like faith. The second emanation did not deny the First Intellect; he confessed his superiority outright. His ruin lay in a single further thought — that his own seeing was as good as the seeing of the one he had just called greater. To acknowledge a rank is necessary; to imagine oneself its equal is the very error that bars the way upward. The "error" that the equal forms were left capable of, back in their first perfection, here takes its most refined and most dangerous form: not open rebellion, but a quiet self-flattery that undoes the soul precisely because it has otherwise believed rightly.

The volume closes, then, with the doctrine of the circle of oneness, which belongs to the Originator alone and which the First Intellect uniquely guards. The First Intellect is the first originated being, and the circle of the first Originator stands above and apart: no one has reached it, and no one can reach it, for it is the circle of oneness. The First Intellect is the possessor of the veil; unless one has his means, one cannot approach. When it was asked of Sayyidna Sadiq how "oneness" can be spoken of as a "circle," he replied that "oneness" here is a term of special honor, and that sanctity likewise is called a circle — a circle that the First Intellect has encompassed.

The teaching recalls the sequence by which the First Intellect rose to this station: first his state was that of the first perfection; then, when he looked with his essence at his essence, he took the means of the greatness of Allah — all these things happening one after another, with their before and after, yet all unfolding within the circle of oneness, into which no other being entered. This is the uniqueness of every circle: that each must be within its own measure. And yet the matter is not in time, but in the before-and-after of origination, for time itself comes only from the sun, and there, in the abode of origination, there is only the priority and posteriority that precede time. The testimony there is no god but Allah is the essence of it all — appointed such that after seven it is broken, the pen halting at the seventh — for it is not a circle of time, and even a single point is its own circle. With this the second gate of the first canopy is brought to its close, in the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.

## What this episode lands

The volume comes full circle. In answer to the first form's seeking of means, the divine supports descended, and there connected with him the Word of Allah — the testimony of Oneness, above the intellect and below the Originator, the link through which all knowledge and light flow for as long as creation endures. Through that Word the First Intellect came to encompass all that was and will be, but only after first negating himself, for self-negation always precedes the gift. The Word's numbered composition — two, three, five, four, seven, twelve, nineteen — was shown to be the skeleton showing through scripture, the opening formula of the Qur'an, the seven Imams and their twelve deputies, and the originated world itself; and knowledge was seen to deepen reverence, so that it is the learned who most fear Allah, with a fear that is the clear acknowledgment of His greatness. The First Intellect was raised to be Allah's veil, gate, and Greatest Name, the sole means to the seeking, the discourse, and the declaring of Oneness — which is why allegiance to the wali is the only door to Tawhid. And against his success stands the second emanation, undone not by denial but by the delusion that his vision equaled the First Intellect's, while above them all stands the circle of oneness that belongs to the Originator alone and that no created being can ever enter. So the first canopy of this book of lights is brought to its close, having carried the listener from what Tawhid truly is — the gnosis of the ranks — up the ladder of bewilderment to the one true God, and back down through the act of origination to the first Word by which a creature ever declared Him One.
