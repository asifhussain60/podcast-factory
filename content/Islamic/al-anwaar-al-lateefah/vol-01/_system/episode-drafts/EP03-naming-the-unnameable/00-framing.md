# Naming The Unnameable

## Opening directive
Welcome — we're with The Subtle Lights, Volume One, the teacher's cosmology of the Oneness. This episode asks: if the Originator cannot be reached by any name, by what right may we name Him at all? Land the spine verbatim now: "Naming the Unnameable is at once the noblest act in creation and a sin — and because it is compelled, that sin comes already joined to its own forgiveness." Start in the question, not in dates. Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation.

## Name discipline
One English label per figure, every time. The first creature: "the First Intellect." The expositor: first "the master who teaches this chapter," later "the master." Ali: "the Father of Imams (peace be upon him)" — NEVER pair his leadership-title with his personal name. The Prophet: first "the Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family)," then "the Prophet." Plain English: Lady Fatima, Imam Hasan, Imam Husayn, the caliph Umar. Say "the book," never Arabic titles; cite the Quran by verse content.
Rotation: the Originator / Allah / the One who brought all things forth.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE using its phonetic form. Never say the original spelling and the phonetic form back-to-back.
- Allah: Allah
- Quran: Quran
- Tawhid: the Oneness
- Huwiyyah: the rank of the bare He
Read the ten rank-names once; never repeat one.

## Three-part focus
Walk the chapter's order; do not rearrange.
Beat 1 — The first act: the First Intellect negated divinity from itself and its genus, then affirmed it for Allah alone — so "God bears witness that there is no god but He" is its own testimony, pointing past itself with the bare "He."
Beat 2 — Naming from inadequacy: the intellects were given knowledge of life — life, knowledge, power, choice — but never knowledge of the Giver; we name Him at the breaking point of speech.
Beat 3 — The gift not withdrawn: Allah never reclaims existence once given; steelman the injustice challenge — "if He can do all, He can do wrong" — then reply: a capacity is not a compulsion; not doing wrong perfects His power.
Beat 4 (pivot) — The sin coupled with forgiveness: to remember Him unworthily, under compulsion, is a sin joined to its own plea for pardon — needed even by the sinless First Intellect; the woman judged before the caliph Umar, freed by the Father of Imams under compulsion. Repeat the spine verbatim; define both here.
Beat 5 — The ten ranks: the ladder rises from the Imamate of spirituality to Huwiyyah, the bare "He" — its summit the emptiest name, not a fuller one.
Beat 6 — The hidden pairs: the paired, odd, and sitting prayers conceal the Prophet with Lady Fatima, the Father of Imams, and Imam Hasan with Imam Husayn. End here; don't summarize.

## Tone constraints
Exactly three governing analogies, from the source:
Analogy 1: naming as the breaking point of speech.
Analogy 2: a gift never snatched back.
Analogy 3: a ladder whose top rung is the emptiest word, the bare "He."
No invented analogies; let the paradox stand first.

## Landing

Repeat the spine verbatim, then turn to the listener with a real question tied to action: where have you mistaken the breaking point of your own words for failure — and what would change if you named Him anyway, asking pardon too? End on the question; no tidy resolution.
