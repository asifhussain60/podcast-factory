## The Prophetic Genealogy: from Abd al-Muttalib to the Qa'im

### Ibrahim as the Gathering-Point of the Eras

The lineage that runs down to the Prophet and the awaited Qa'im does not begin abruptly with the Quraysh of Makkah; it gathers up, in a single luminous channel, the whole inheritance of the cycles that preceded it. In Sayyidna Ibrahim, peace be upon him, the eras of Adam and of Nuh were drawn together. Every individual who had entered the haykal — the luminous temple — of the Natiq across those long ages was assembled in him, so that he became a confluence into which the accumulated spiritual forms of two complete cycles flowed. With that gathering of persons there gathered also the four ranks of the religious order: Nubuwwah (prophethood), Risalah (messengership), Wisayah (vicegerency), and Imamah (the imamate). All four were present and complete in Ibrahim, which is why his station stands as the archetype to which the later gathering in 'Abd al-Muttalib is repeatedly compared.

A point of careful discrimination must be set down here so that the reader does not confuse two figures who share a single name. There are, in the language of these realities, two Ibrahims. The first is Ibrahim the Natiq, the great speaking-prophet of his cycle, the one of whom the Quran says `alladhi waffa` — he who fulfilled, who paid in full what was asked of him. The second is a different man, also named Ibrahim, who was simply a Nabi of his own time and not the Natiq of a cycle; it is to this lesser Ibrahim that the Quranic phrase `kana rajulun yuqalu lahu Ibrahim` — "there was a man called Ibrahim" — refers. The honour, the gathering of the four ranks, and the role of cyclical confluence belong to the first; the name alone is shared by the second.

### The Highest Ranks of the Cycle: the Three and the Wazir of the Qa'im

Within the architecture of the Qa'im's order there are certain stations elevated above the ordinary chain of succession. Three figures in particular — Maulana Hashim, Maulana Hunayn, and Maulana Ibrahim, called together the thalathaha (the three) — hold a special khilafah and occupy a singular rank among the khilafats of the Qa'im. They are not merely links in the genealogical line; they are entrusted with a deputyship of a higher order, set apart from the ordinary transmission of the ranks.

Above even these stands the wazir of the Qa'im, who is named 'Ayn 'Azim — the great essence. His khilafah, his vicegerent succession, is taken up by Mim. Here Mim designates the line that passes through 'Abd al-Muttalib, then Abu Talib, then 'Abdullah: this is the deputyship of the 'Ayn carried in the persons of the Prophet's grandfather and his two sons. The pattern unfolds with two qiran — two conjunctions or equalities of rank — and then Mim Jadd al-Qa'im, the grandfather of the Qa'im, while Fatimah stands as the khalifah of Mim, the feminine vicegerent within this same noble succession. The whole arrangement is a hierarchy in which the great essence is served and deputized through this prophetic house.

### The Mutimmin and the Rank of the Sevenths

A further category of exalted persons must be set beside the three and the wazir: the mutimmin, the "completers" of a cycle. The mutimmin of the Dawr-e-Muhammadi — and, by the same principle, every seventh Imam — hold a very high rank. The seventh Imams named in this tradition are Muhammad bin Isma'il, Ma'add al-Mu'izz, Imam al-Tayyib, the twenty-eighth Maulana Ma'add, the twenty-second Maulana Ahmad, the forty-second Maulana 'Ali, and the forty-ninth Abu al-Qa'im. Each of these stands at the completing seventh of a heptad and therefore bears an authority above the ordinary Imams of the sequence.

Hazrat Nasrullah Sahib taught a subtle point about how this elevation operates. The atimmah — the completers — of the Prophet's own cycle reached so high a rank that a qiran, a genuine equality, occurred between them and the sevenths. The two categories, in other words, drew level: the completers of the Muhammadan cycle rose to such a degree that they became, at certain moments, equal to the seventh Imams in the taking of khilafah. The taking of the deputyship could therefore pass through either, since their stations had been brought into conjunction.

Among the atimmah themselves, one figure is exalted above the rest. Maulana Muhammad bin Isma'il holds the highest rank, for he is gathered together with a personality of the order of Imam Husayn, and his station is established in the dawr-e-kashf, the cycle of unveiling. Precisely because he belongs to the cycle of unveiling, he does not fall into the category of the seventh class at all; his rank transcends that classification rather than merely occupying its summit. Of the seven, four are the greater — Muhammad bin Isma'il, Imam al-Tayyib, Maulana Ahmad, and Abu al-Qa'im — and of these four, three each gather two ranks within themselves. But Muhammad bin Isma'il alone holds the special and unshared rank of the sahib-e-dawr-e-kashf, the master of the cycle of unveiling, a station anchored in Surat al-Ikhlas, in the verse `qul huwa Allahu ahad` — "Say: He is Allah, the One." His rank is fastened to the declaration of absolute unity itself.

### The Name of the Qa'im and the Precedence of Wisayah

The awaited one's name is 'Ali. Sayyidna, in the poetic responses he composed, referred to him as Abu 'Ali — "father of 'Ali" — a usage that both names the Qa'im and points to his generative station within the line. This identification of the Qa'im as 'Ali runs through the whole exposition that follows and must be held in mind whenever the Qa'im of Isma'il's progeny is spoken of.

A note on the very ordering of this book is warranted, for it explains a peculiarity the careful reader will notice. Because the Anwar is a foundational work — the book from which most of the Du'at draw their proofs — it deliberately places Wisayah before Imamah, contrary to the usual order followed in most other books. This is not an accident of arrangement but a doctrinal statement: Wisayah, the vicegerency, is the higher of the two ranks, and the Anwar gives it precedence in its listing to make that priority visible.

### Ibrahim's Two Sons: the Station of Isma'il and the Service of Ishaq

The great division upon which the entire later genealogy rests was made by Ibrahim between his two sons. Ibrahim established Isma'il as his Wasi, entrusting to him the ranks of Wisayah and Imamah by the direct command of Allah, because Isma'il is a maqam ilahi — a divine station — and a haykal nurani, a luminous temple. To Ishaq, by contrast, Ibrahim entrusted the ranks of Nubuwwah and Risalah, and made him a khadim (servant), a hijab (veil), and a da'i (summoner) to Isma'il. The higher, permanent ranks went to the one who was himself a divine station; the manifest, summoning ranks went to the one whose office is to serve and to call to the other.

This division opens into a doctrine of two whole lineages with two distinct natures. Isma'il and his progeny are the divine stations — maqamat ilahiyyah — and the possessors of the luminous temples; they are the ahl al-istiqrar, the people of permanence, those whose station is fixed and abiding. Ishaq and his progeny, on the other hand, are the proofs and the manifest summoners — the hujaj and the du'at zahirun — and they stand as veils placed upon the people of permanence. The one line is the settled, hidden reality; the other is the manifest, public office that both conceals and proclaims it.

From Ishaq, accordingly, ran the long chain of the Du'at and the hujub, the manifest summoners. It passed through Ya'qub, then Yusuf, and onward through the named prophets of that line down to 'Isa, peace be upon him; and after 'Isa it continued through Sham'un, then 'Abd al-Masih, and then Bahira al-Rahib, Bahira the monk. All of these are the ahl al-istida' — the people of summoning, those who hold the manifest office of calling — and all of them trace their lineage back to Ishaq, the father of the summoners. When at last the Prophet was manifested, the rank of Nabuwwah that had until then resided with Bahira the monk was taken up by Ubayy ibn Ka'b, so that the office did not lapse but passed forward into the Prophet's own community.

### The Imams of Isma'il's Line: the Chain Down to 'Adnan

If the line of Ishaq is the line of the summoners, the line of Isma'il is the line of the divine stations, the A'immah proper. The first of the A'immah, the divine stations, after Ibrahim was Isma'il ibn Ibrahim himself. From Isma'il the entrustment passed to Qaydar, and from Qaydar it descended through a long named succession of fathers to sons, generation after generation, down to 'Udad bin 'Udad — and the muqim, the establisher set up alongside this Imam, was Musa.

The principle that governs this chain is that when the appointed time bound the manifestation of a Natiq — when a new speaking-prophet was due to appear — the Imam of the age would establish a da'i to carry the manifest office. So 'Udad established Musa from among his Du'at to fill that role, and the genealogical chain itself continued forward through 'Adnan, Ma'add, Nizar, Mudar, Ilyas, Mudrikah, and Khuzaymah — and it was Khuzaymah's da'i who in turn was commanded to establish 'Isa. Thus the line of the Imams and the line of the summoners run in parallel, each Imam in his turn raising up the summoner appropriate to the unfolding of the cycles.

This pairing of each Natiq with his muqim, his establisher, can be set out plainly: the muqim of Adam is Maulana Hunayn; the muqim of Nuh is Maulana Hud; the muqim of Ibrahim is Maulana Salih; the muqim of Musa is 'Udad bin 'Udad; and the muqim of 'Isa is Khuzaymah. Each great speaking-prophet, that is, has standing with him an establisher who anchors and upholds his cycle, and these named pairings reveal the hidden scaffolding behind the apparent succession of prophets.

The chain then carries on from Khuzaymah, descending through Kinanah, Nadr, Malik, Fihr, Ghalib, Lu'ayy, Ka'b, Murrah, Kilab, Qusayy, 'Abd Manaf, and Hashim, until it arrives at 'Abd al-Muttalib. And with 'Abd al-Muttalib something occurs that had not occurred since Ibrahim: the four ranks — Wisayah, Imamah, Nabuwwah, and Risalah — gathered together in a single person exactly as they had once gathered in Ibrahim. The whole long descent was a movement toward this second great confluence.

### The Four Ranks Gather in 'Abd al-Muttalib

The fifth chapter opens precisely upon this gathering. As the text states it: `Wa lamma ijtama'at hadhihi al-rutab al-arba' 'ind Abd al-Muttalib, kanat ithnatan minhunna mudakhkharatayn li-l-qa'im min wuld Isma'il 'alayhis salam, wa huma al-wasayah wa-l-imamah, wa ithnatan li-l-qa'im min wuld Ishaq 'alayhis salam, wa huwa al-nubuwwah wa-l-risalah` — "And when these four ranks gathered with 'Abd al-Muttalib, two of them were reserved for the Qa'im from the progeny of Isma'il, peace be upon him, and they are the vicegerency and the imamate; and two were for the Qa'im from the progeny of Ishaq, peace be upon him, namely the prophethood and the messengership."

The doctrine compressed into this single line is the keystone of the chapter. When the four ranks assembled with Sayyidna 'Abd al-Muttalib, they did not simply rest in him; they were partitioned according to their two destinies. Two of them — Wisayah and Imamah — were held in reserve, stored up (mudakhkharatayn) for the Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il. Two others — Nubuwwah and Risalah — were set aside for the Qa'im of the progeny of Ishaq. And the identities behind these two titles must be grasped firmly: the Qa'im from the progeny of Isma'il is 'Ayn-e-'Azimah, the great essence; and the Qa'im from the progeny of Ishaq is the Lord Prophet himself, Muhammad. The two streams that Ibrahim first separated between his two sons are thus carried, all the way down, to their two appointed culminations — and a governing law holds throughout: Imamah and Wisayah are never separated from one another, but always travel together as a single pair.

### The Necessity of the Luminous Father

The text proceeds to one of its most searching arguments, concerning the conditions under which a divine station can exist at all. `Wa kana fi 'adlillah fadlu Muhammadin wa sharafuhu salawatullahi alaih an yakuna maqaman ilahiyyan wa haykalan nuraniyyan` — "And it was in the justice of Allah the virtue and honour of Muhammad, blessings of Allah be upon him, that he should be a divine station and a luminous structure." It belonged to the very justice ('adl) and grace of Allah that Muhammad should be a maqam ilahi and a haykal nurani.

But this exaltation carries a precondition, and the text states it without softening: `wa lan yakuna dhalika illa li-man yakunu abuhu kadhalika haykalan nuraniyyan` — "and that will not be except for one whose father is likewise a luminous structure." A man cannot become a luminous temple unless his father is himself a luminous temple. For the gathering of the suwarat — the spiritual forms — that makes a haykal nurani what it is, there must already be in place a vessel of sufficient capacity and strength (quwwat) to receive and hold all those forms; and that receptive strength is not created in a man whose father was not himself such a temple. The principle is put with finality: `wa illa fa-la sabila ila dhalik` — "and if not, then there is no way to that." Absent the luminous father, there is simply no path to the luminous son.

To make the matter intelligible, the lecture draws an image from the natural world. The rays of the sun fall everywhere — over the whole earth, across the entire universe, indiscriminately. Yet only certain things respond to them in a particular way. A mirror, when the rays strike it, catches the light and casts it back; other bodies possess the quality of grasping the sun's rays with strength and holding them. The capacity to receive is not in the light but in the receiver. So too with the luminous forms: they may be present, but only a haykal nurani has the receptive strength to gather them, and that strength is itself inherited from a luminous father. This is why the station of Muhammad as a divine structure presupposes that 'Abdullah, his father, was likewise a haykal nurani — the luminosity of the son requires the luminosity of the father as its necessary ground.

### The Two Gates of 'Abd al-Muttalib

Having established the principle, the text turns to the mechanism by which the ascending forms are received and sorted. `Wa kana min Abd al-Muttalib an nasaba lahu babayn yatasallamani al-suwar al-muntaqilah al-murtaqiyah min a'dad da'watihi` — "And it was from 'Abd al-Muttalib that he established for himself two gates which would receive the transferring, ascending forms from the numbers of his da'wah." 'Abd al-Muttalib set up two gates (babayn) whose function was to receive the suwarat as they rose up and transferred from the ranks of his da'wah.

`Wa ja'ala ahadahuma a'la min al-akhar` — "and he made one of them higher than the other." The two gates were not equal: one was set above the other, and each gathered a different grade of form. `Fa-kana al-majma' al-'ali majma'an li-l-suwar al-mu'minin al-bulagha' wa-l-hudud al-fudala'` — "so the higher gathering was a place of assembly for the forms of the accomplished believers and the virtuous hudud." The higher gate — the majma'-e-'ali — was the assembling-place for the forms of the mu'minin bulagha', the believers who had reached completion, and of the hudud fudala', the virtuous ranks of the hierarchy. `Wa-l-thani majma'an li-l-suwar al-mustajibin alladhina ukhidhat 'alayhim 'uhud al-wila' al-mustaqimin 'alayhi la ghayr` — "and the second a place of assembly for the forms of the respondents from whom the covenants of allegiance were taken, who were steadfast upon it, and nothing else." The lower gate gathered the forms of the mustajibin, the simple respondents, from whom the covenant ('ahd) of allegiance (wila') had been taken and who stood steadfast upon that loyalty — wila' being, in its root, love and devotion.

The higher gate is then named: `fa-kanat tilka al-suwar al-mujtami'ah 'ind al-bab al-'ali Abu Talib 'alayhis salam` — "so those gathered forms were with the higher gate, Abu Talib, peace be upon him." The forms collected at the higher gate were gathered with Abu Talib; and the forms gathered at the lower gate, `wa-l-suwar allati 'ind al-bab al-dani 'Abdillah`, were the forms collected with 'Abdullah, the lower gate. The two sons thus served as the two receiving-gates of their father's da'wah, Abu Talib receiving the higher order of forms and 'Abdullah the lower.

### From Bab al-Abwab to Qalb-e-Haykal

The role of a gate, however, is not static. The text and its exposition unfold a deeper movement: the bab gathers all the forms of the world, and then, when the time comes to install them (nasb) in the Imam's son, the very gate that gathered them goes on to become the qalbe haykal — the heart of the structure — of that son, the next khalifah. The gate does not merely collect; it is itself transformed into the living core of the successor. And the decision as to which form is ready to be included in the haykal at any given moment does not rest with the gate but with the Imam, and ultimately with the Imam of Imams — 'Ayn-e-'Azimah, the great essence — from whom the indication comes that a particular form is now ready, at its appointed time, to be honoured with installation.

This principle is then traced concretely through the generations, each Imam's bab al-abwab being identified as his wife, who first gathers the forms and afterwards becomes the qalbe haykal of his son. Hashim's bab al-abwab was Salma, who was then submerged — became the qalbe haykal — in 'Abd al-Muttalib. 'Abd al-Muttalib's bab al-abwab was Fatimah bint 'Amr, who was submerged in Abu Talib. Abu Talib's bab al-abwab was Fatimah bint Asad, who became the qalbe haykal of 'Ali. And 'Ali's bab al-abwab was Salman-e-Farsi, who became the qalbe haykal of Imam Husayn. The same chain, expressed in the da'wah's own cryptic term `ummuhu, ummuhu, ummuha` — "his mother, his mother, her mother" — points to the qalbe haykal of the Prophet, of 'Ali, and of Fatimah respectively.

A careful distinction is drawn here between two offices that might be confused: being a bab al-abwab and becoming a qalbe haykal. The Prophet's bab al-abwab was Khadijah, who was submerged in Fatimah — that is, Khadijah became the qalbe haykal of Fatimah al-Zahra. And 'Abdullah's bab al-abwab was Aminah, who was submerged in the Prophet — Aminah became the qalbe haykal of Muhammad himself. So Aminah, called "his mother" (ummuhu), was the Prophet's qalbe haykal, not merely his gate, having served first as 'Abdullah's bab al-abwab; and Fatimah bint Asad, "his mother," was 'Ali's qalbe haykal; and Khadijah was the qalbe haykal of Fatimah. To be a gate is to gather; to be a heart of the structure is to be installed as the living centre of the next station — and the same woman passes from the first office to the second across a generation.

### The Four Fatimahs and the Recitations of Ya Fatir

The figure of Fatimah multiplies across this line, and the devotional practice of the community preserves the pattern. The four recitations of `Ya Fateru` — the four prostrations and tellings associated with that invocation — correspond to four Fatimahs. The first sajdah, recited one hundred times, belongs to Fatimah al-Zahra. The recitation made upon the right cheek, also one hundred times, belongs to Fatimah bint Asad. The third belongs to Fatimah bint 'Amr. And the fourth sajdah, recited one hundred and ten times, belongs to the Zawjat al-Qa'im, the wife of the Qa'im, who is likewise named Fatimah. Among all four, Fatimah al-Zahra is superior, her station above the rest.

The bodily form of the prostration itself carries a teaching. In the complete sajdah, seven parts of the body must touch the ground: the forehead or face, the two hands, the two knees, and the two feet — seven points of contact in all. This sevenfold touching signifies that the complete order of the da'wah requires seven limbs, seven hudud, for its full establishment. By contrast, the position in which only the cheek touches the ground signifies a lesser rank, an incompleteness of the order; the difference between the full prostration on seven members and the partial touching of the cheek is itself a sign of the difference between a complete and a diminished hierarchy.

### The Qa'imayn: 'Ayn-e-'Azimah and the Prophet

The fifth chapter restates, as a settled conclusion, the identity of the two Qa'ims. The Qa'im min wuld Isma'il is 'Ayn-e-'Azimah, the great essence, and the Qa'im min wuld Ishaq is the Prophet. And it reaffirms the law of the luminous structure: the haykal nurani requires a receptive strength (quwwat) capable of gathering all the forms, and such strength can be formed only if the father is himself a haykal nurani. The doctrine of the gate is likewise restated in its general form: the bab gathers all the world's forms, and when the time of installation (nasb) arrives, it becomes the qalbe haykal of the Imam's son; and it is the Imam — and above him the Imam of Imams, 'Ayn-e-'Azimah — who determines which form is ripe for inclusion.

### The Sacrifice of 'Abdullah

'Abd al-Muttalib's treatment of his son 'Abdullah is then explained in the light of these stations. `Wa shahara Abd al-Muttalib amrahu bi-l-dhabh` — "and 'Abd al-Muttalib made his matter famous through the sacrifice." 'Abd al-Muttalib had bound himself by a vow that, should he be granted a certain number of sons, he would offer one in sacrifice; and when his sons were born — ten or eleven of them — the lot for the sacrifice at length fell upon 'Abdullah, in a parallel to the trial of Ibrahim with his own son. In place of 'Abdullah, one hundred camels were sacrificed; and each time the lot returned, 'Abd al-Muttalib fulfilled it, until the redemption was complete. `Wa nawwaha bi-smihi li-ya'rifa ahl al-haqa'iq fadlahu` — "and he made his name renowned so that the people of realities might know his virtue."

The inner purpose of this whole episode was not the camels but the renown. By making 'Abdullah's near-sacrifice famous, 'Abd al-Muttalib made it known among the ahl al-haqa'iq, the people of realities, that 'Abdullah was being prepared for a particular station — the station for which Nubuwwah and Risalah were the appointed ranks. The dhabh was the public sign of an inward preparation, declaring to those who could read it that this son was the chosen vessel of the prophetic ranks. The lecture pauses on a verse of Iqbal that captures the hiddenness of such inner realities from outward reason: "What is intellect (khirad)? It is a lamp on the path (chiragh-e rahguzar), by which a person's sight is illumined; within the house (darun-e khana), what commotions there are — what does the lamp on the path know?" Intellect is the lamp set in the street to light the comings and goings of travellers; but of the affairs unfolding inside the house — the inner realities of these stations — the street-lamp of reason knows nothing.

### The Present Imam as Jadd-e-Qa'im

The pattern set in 'Abd al-Muttalib is not merely historical; it is the template of what is yet to come. The present Imam, Maulana Muhammad bin 'Ali, peace be upon him, holds the lofty station of jadd-e-qa'im — the grandfather of the Qa'im — exactly as 'Abd al-Muttalib held the station of jaddiyyat-e-'ayn, the essence-grandfather. This is a very high station, and it carries with it, by the custom (dastur) of these matters, a fixed sequel. Just as 'Abd al-Muttalib had two sons, so too will the present Imam, the jadd-e-qa'im, have two sons of necessity.

One of these sons will stand at the station of Sayyidna Abu Talib, and from him will come the Qa'im. The other will stand at the station of Sayyidna 'Abdullah, and he is Sayyidna Mahdi, peace be upon him. Sayyidna Mahdi will have a daughter whose name will be Fatimah — and here the four-fold pattern of the Fatimahs and the one hundred and ten recitations of the Zawjat al-Qa'im finds its fulfilment — and she will marry the Qa'im. From that union Hasan and Husayn will be born: those who stand at the stations of Hasan and Husayn will appear again. The same diagram (naqshah) that was traced in the house of 'Abd al-Muttalib will be traced once more in the house of the awaited one.

### The Division Between Abu Talib and 'Abdullah

The text now sets out exactly how 'Abd al-Muttalib partitioned the four ranks between his two sons. `Fa-sallama Abd al-Muttalib rutbat al-wasayah wa-l-imamah ila Abi Talib, wa rutbat al-nubuwwah wa-l-risalah ila 'Abdillah` — "so 'Abd al-Muttalib handed over the rank of vicegerency and imamate to Abu Talib, and the rank of prophethood and messengership to 'Abdullah." By this handing-over the two sons became two Imams, each bearing his portion of the inheritance.

Their natures, however, differed in kind. `Ahaduhuma imamun natiq` — "one of them is a speaking imam" — and this is Abu Talib, who is like Husayn bin 'Ali, an imam-e-natiq. `Wa-l-akhar samitun wa huwa 'Abdullah ka-l-Hasan bin 'Aliyy` — "and the other is silent, and he is 'Abdullah, like al-Hasan bin 'Ali." Abu Talib was the speaking Imam in the likeness of Husayn; 'Abdullah was the silent (samit) Imam in the likeness of al-Hasan. And from this division came the two great sons: `fa-awlada 'Abdullah Muhammadan wa awlada Abu Talib 'Aliyyan` — "so 'Abdullah fathered Muhammad and Abu Talib fathered 'Ali." The bearer of Nubuwwah and Risalah fathered the Prophet; the bearer of Wisayah and Imamah fathered 'Ali.

There is also a correspondence of these stations to a Quranic measure. `Qaba qawsayn aw adna` — "two bows' length, or nearer" — maps onto these persons: 'Abd al-Muttalib and Abu Talib occupy the station of `qaba qawsayn`, the two bows' length, while 'Abdullah occupies the station of `adna`, the nearer. The graded nearness of the Quranic ascent is thus read as a hierarchy of these three.

### The Transfers of the Ranks and the Great Guardianship

The succession of the ranks across the deaths of these figures is traced with precision. `Fa-lamma anat nuqlatu 'Abdillah, istawda'a abahu 'Abd al-Muttalib` — "so when the time of 'Abdullah's transfer came, he entrusted his father 'Abd al-Muttalib." The lecture pauses to insist on the correct reading: the word is `nuqlatu` — the transfer, the subject of the sentence — and the verb is `anat`, "came," not `anta`; a reader unacquainted with the Arabic might mangle the line, but its sense is exact. When the time of 'Abdullah's transfer (nuqlatu) came — that is, when he was to pass on — he entrusted to his own father, 'Abd al-Muttalib, the ranks of Nubuwwah and Risalah, depositing them with him on behalf of his son Muhammad, in the same pattern that occurred with Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq. Then `wa intaqala ila dar karamatillah` — "and he transferred to the abode of the honour of Allah" — 'Abdullah passed to the abode of honour.

The entrustment then moved one generation further. `Fa-lamma anat nuqlatu 'Abd al-Muttalib, istawda'a waladahu Aba Talib rutbat al-nubuwwah wa-l-risalah li-waladi waladihi alladhi huwa Muhammad salawatullahi alaih` — "and when the time of 'Abd al-Muttalib's transfer came, he entrusted his son Abu Talib with the rank of prophethood and messengership for his grandson, who is Muhammad, blessings of Allah be upon him" — `li-kawnihi fi hadd al-tufuliyyah`, "because of his being in the limit of childhood." Since Muhammad was still in childhood (tufuliyyah), 'Abd al-Muttalib, on his own transfer, deposited the ranks of Nubuwwah and Risalah with Abu Talib, to be held for the grandson. In this way two of the Prophet's ranks gathered with Abu Talib, who became the custodian (kafil) of Nubuwwah and Risalah. The distinction of degrees is precise: 'Abd al-Muttalib had been the owner of all four stations; Abu Talib was the owner of two and the custodian of the other two. Then 'Abd al-Muttalib too `intaqala ila dar karamatillah ta'ala`, passed to the abode of the honour of Allah the Exalted.

`Wa qama Abu Talib 'alayhis salam bi-l-rutab al-arba' ila an balagha Muhammad salawatullahi alaih ashuddahu` — "and Abu Talib, peace be upon him, upheld the four ranks until Muhammad, blessings of Allah be upon him, reached his maturity." Abu Talib upheld all four ranks until the Prophet attained the age of maturity; `fa-sallama ilayhi ma istudi'a lahu Abu Talib`, "then he handed over to him what had been entrusted to Abu Talib for him" — `wa hiya rutbatan, al-nubuwwah wa-l-risalah`, "and it was two ranks, prophethood and messengership." When Muhammad came of age, Abu Talib returned to him the deposited Nubuwwah and Risalah.

This sequence is the basis of one of the chapter's most striking conclusions. Because the Prophet received the great guardianship — the kafalat-e-'uzma — the overwhelming power of that guardianship made it so that, although Abu Talib had assigned Nubuwwah and Risalah to the Prophet and Imamah and Wisayah to 'Ali, all the ranks in fact gathered in the Prophet. Both Muhammad and 'Ali were young; and the strength of the kafalat-e-'uzma overshadowed all the mustawda'in and mustaqarrin — all the deposited and settled ranks — that had been gathered with 'Ali. So despite bearing in his own name only Nubuwwah and Risalah, the Prophet achieved superiority over every rank gathered with 'Ali, becoming the deputy of the 'Ayn. No rank of the Qa'im remained outside this gathering except the thalathah sukra — the three intoxicated ones — who alone stood apart from the Prophet's overarching guardianship.

A further ruling on the conditions of the imamate follows from this house. After Maulana 'Abd al-Muttalib, the haykal-nurani condition requires that an Imam's father and grandfather both be haykal nurani — the luminosity must be doubly secured in the immediate ancestry. Yet within this requirement a special possibility is opened: two sons of a single father may both be Imams, one occupying the mustawda' (the deposit) and the other the mustaqarr (the settled station), exactly as occurred with the two sons of 'Abd al-Muttalib. The condition of luminous ancestry does not forbid two simultaneous Imams from one father; it provides for them in the twin offices of deposit and permanence.

### The Three Courses of the Prophet–'Ali Relationship

The relationship between the Prophet and 'Ali is not single but threefold, and the text names the three courses as the salasa majari. The first course is expressed in 'Ali's own words, `ana 'abdun min 'abid Muhammad` — "I am a slave from the slaves of Muhammad" — and this is the majra-e-adna, the lowest course, in which 'Ali declares himself one of the Prophet's servants and so illustrates, by contrast, the loftiness of the awaited one's station. The second course is one of equality, masawat, expressed in the saying `ana wa 'Aliyyun ka-hatayn` — "I and 'Ali are like these two," spoken of two fingers held side by side; yet even this equality is a balanced one, the one on the right and the one on the left. The third course is the a'la, the higher: the elevation of the Hijab-e-'Ain that took place on the day of the conquest of Makkah, when the Prophet raised 'Ali up, and the veil that had stood lower or level was lifted to the higher station. Lower, equal, and higher — adna, masawat, a'la — these are the three courses through which the single relationship moves.

### The Completion of the Religion and the Final Ascent

A question of interpretation is settled here regarding the verse of completion. When the Quran declares, "This day I have perfected for you your religion," the meaning is the completion of the obligations of the din — the bringing to fullness of all its commands. The command of wilayah was announced last of all, after the command of Hajj had become the final ordinance; only then, after every command had been revealed, was the announcement of wilayah made and the religion declared complete. The verse therefore refers to the completion of the obligations, not to any change in the Prophet's own station or rank.

For the ascent of the Prophet through the ranks themselves is described in its own terms. `Wa dhalika anna Muhammadan salawatullahi alaih taraqqa fi rutbatay al-shay' wa-l-shay'an ila an balagha rutbat al-nutq al-ilahi, wa huwa qa'im wuld Ishaq 'alayhis salam alladhi sabaqa al-qawl 'alayhim` — "and that is because Muhammad, blessings of Allah be upon him, ascended through the two ranks, thing by thing, until he reached the rank of divine speech, and he is the Qa'im of the progeny of Ishaq, peace be upon him, about whom the saying has preceded." When at maturity Abu Talib handed over the entrusted Nubuwwah and Risalah, Muhammad ascended (taraqqa) step by step through the ranks until he reached the rank of nutq-e-ilahi, divine speech — the final and highest rank, which stands above Nubuwwah, Imamah, and Wisayah alike. These latter three are the lower-order ranks; above them the higher ranks begin, and there is found the nutq-e-ilahi and the station of the hujjab, the maqamun mahmudun, the praised station.

This praised station is not fixed in magnitude but varies according to the 'ayn it serves. Because an 'ayn does not appear in a single uniform form — one 'ayn being of a single cycle (dawr), another of seven cycles — the maqam-e-mahmud changes correspondingly: as the station of the 'ayn is, so will the praised station be. At the khutbat al-tasmeem, the sermon of designation, the station of the 'ayn itself changes into the form of the Qa'im. The text marks that the Prophet, having reached the rank of divine speech, is the Qa'im of the progeny of Ishaq about whom the foregoing saying had been made, and that `wa kana judullahi 'alayhi haykalan nuraniyyan wa maqaman ruhaniyyan` — "and the beneficence of Allah was upon him as a luminous structure and a spiritual station."

### The Gathering of All the Cycles in the Prophet

The chapter rises to its summit with the Prophet's own declaration: `wa li-dhalika kana yaqul, ana da'watu Abi Ibrahim` — "and for that reason he used to say, I am the prayer (da'wat) of my father Ibrahim." The Prophet identified himself as the answered supplication of Ibrahim, and in that identification he gathered into himself the whole inheritance of the cycles: `wa ijtima'at al-maqamat al-'aliyah wa-l-hayakil al-nuraniyah fi jami' al-adwar min awwal al-thalathah alaf sana` — "and the high stations and the luminous structures gathered in all the cycles from the first three thousand years." All the lofty stations and luminous temples of every cycle were assembled in his haykal.

The exposition specifies which forms these were. Within the cycle of unveiling, the dawr-e-kashf — understood here as a span of some fifty thousand years, of which the period of interval, the dawr-e-fatrah, is three thousand — the last three thousand years bore two kinds of forms. One kind carried the qualities of the dawr-e-sitir, the cycle of concealment; the other carried the characteristics of the dawr-e-kashf, the cycle of unveiling. The higher and superior kind is not subjected to the qualities of the period of interval; these are reckoned as the suwarat of the dawr-e-kashf. Whereas the lower (adna) forms of the three thousand years of the interval gathered with Imam Junayd, peace be upon him, the higher forms of the dawr-e-kashf are those that gathered in the Prophet. As the text continues, `wa kanat bi-jumlatiha haykalan nuraniyyan wa maqaman ruhaniyyan za nasutin ka-manasik al-a'immah 'alayhim al-salam` — "and in their entirety they were a luminous structure and a spiritual station, possessing a human form like the rites of the Imams, peace be upon them." These gathered forms, taken altogether, constituted a luminous temple and a spiritual station clothed in human form (nasut), after the manner of the rites of the Imams.

And these gathered forms had a name and an outcome: `wa kanat 'Aliyyan 'alayhis salam wa kana qa'im wuld Isma'il 'alayhis salam` — "and it was 'Ali, peace be upon him, and he was the Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il, peace be upon him." The luminous structure assembled from all the high stations and temples of the cycles became 'Ali — and 'Ali is the Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il. With the Prophet, then, the two streams that Ibrahim divided are joined again: the Qa'im of Isma'il's progeny is 'Ali and the Qa'im of Ishaq's progeny is the Prophet, but the succession occurred in the person of the Prophet, who gathers them both when he is joined with 'Ayn-e-'Azima. The Prophet, by his union with the great essence, becomes the point at which the whole genealogy converges.

### The Chain of Concealed Knowledge Among the Du'at

The chapter closes upon a note about the transmission of knowledge itself, in the supplication (du'a) of the da'i during the cycle of the Qa'im. Sayyidna sahib used to teach that the work of the Du'at was a work of progressive disclosure that nonetheless preserved a reserve of hidden knowledge at every stage. One da'i, Sayyidna Sulayman, wrote an exposition of the du'as that had come down, explaining them and performing a great service; yet in some of his books he kept certain pieces of new knowledge concealed. When Sayyidna Ja'far came after him, he explained those hidden matters — but he too, from his own side, kept hidden some of what he had received from the Imam. So it ran: one da'i after another would explain the du'as and unveil what his predecessor had withheld, while himself withholding something further. This series of concealment continues from da'i to da'i, an unbroken chain in which each summoner discloses and conceals in his turn.

The exposition ends with the closing benediction of the text: `wa-l-hamdu lillah 'ala bulugh al-ghayat wa-l-ittila' 'ala khawafi al-asrar al-khafiyyat. Wa salla Allahu 'ala Muhammadin khatim al-nabiyyin wa 'ala alihi al-tahirin al-tayyibin, wa 'ala al-qasimayn min huma al-qasim al-awwal 'Abdillah, 'ala khalqihi ajma'in` — "And praise be to Allah for reaching the goals and for being informed of the hidden secrets. And may Allah's blessings be upon Muhammad, the seal of the prophets, and upon his pure and goodly family, and upon the two Qasims, of whom the first Qasim is 'Abdullah, upon all of His creation." With praise for the attainment of the goal and for the unveiling of the hidden secrets, and with blessings upon the seal of the prophets, his pure family, and the two Qasims, the chapter of the prophetic genealogy is brought to its close.

## Husayn, the Imamate, and the Line of Wilayah

### The Unmatched State of Amir al-Mu'minin and the Connection of the Tenth Intellect

The second gate of the third part opens upon the loftiest passage yet reached, a description of the singular station of Amir al-Mu'minin, peace be upon him. The text begins: `wa lamma kana Amir al-Mu'minin bi-hadhihi al-halah al-lati lam yablughha ahadun ghayruhu, ittasala bihi al-'aql al-'ashir ittisalan kulliyan, wa lahazat'hu al-'uqul al-ibda'iyah lahzan sarmadiyan, wa ramat'hu bi-ashi'atiha, wa ittasalat bihi al-mawad al-ilahiyah fawqa ma ittasalat bi-kull maqam qablahu wa ba'dahu siwa al-qa'im 'ala dhikrihi al-salam` ("And when Amir al-Mu'minin was in this state which no one else ever reached, the Tenth Intellect connected with him with a total connection, and the Creative Intellects glanced at him with an eternal glance and cast their rays upon him, and the divine substances connected with him above that which connected with every station before him and after him, except for the Qa'im, peace be upon his mention").

The phrasing `wa lamma kana` deserves a careful reading, for the Arabic verb `kana` carries both the sense of "was" and the sense of "is." When Allah Most High says `kana Allahu 'aliman` ("Allah is All-Knowing"), we do not render it as a thing belonging to the past — that Allah was once All-Knowing and is so no longer. The verb states a perpetual reality. So too here: when the text says that Amir al-Mu'minin "was" in this state, it means he is in this state, an enduring condition that no one else has reached. Such a person, the teaching insists, has never been born. The state is `al-lati lam yablughha ahadun ghayruhu` — a state that none but he attained.

To grasp the height of this station one must recall the spiritual point, the `nuqta`, of the veiled one (`muhtajib`) of Amir al-Mu'minin. The architecture of these points was established earlier. When `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah` manifested — and it manifested from Sarandip — the period of unveiling, the `dawr al-kashf`, began. Following the close of that period and the appearance of its final figure, the `nuqta` of Amir al-Mu'minin, who is 'Ayn al-'Azimah, stands at a station higher than them all, higher even than the station of `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah` himself in respect of this manifestation. This is the essential purpose of the passage: to establish that the `nuqta` of 'Ayn al-'Azimah is the most active and the most exalted point in the order of being after `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah`, and that it will remain so until the emanation of the `nuqta` of the Qa'im, the `inbi'ath al-nuqat`, which has not yet occurred. Until that emanation comes to pass, no point is more active than the `nuqta` of 'Ayn al-'Azimah.

The clauses unfold this connection in detail. `Ittasala bihi al-'aql al-'ashir` — the Tenth Intellect connected with him; and the Tenth Intellect here is `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah`. The connection was `ittisalan kulliyan`, a total and complete connection, holding nothing back. `Wa lahazat'hu al-'uqul al-ibda'iyah lahzan sarmadiyan` — the Creative Intellects glanced upon him with an eternal glance, a seeing that abides always and forever. `Wa ramat'hu bi-ashi'atiha` — and they cast their rays upon him. And finally `wa ittasalat bihi al-mawad al-ilahiyah` — the divine substances, the divine aids, connected with him, with that very `nuqta`. What is meant by Amir al-Mu'minin throughout this passage is 'Ayn al-'Azimah, who is the veil of the veiled one (the `hijab` of the `muhtajib`) in the world of life (`alam al-hayat`).

The measure of these aids is then fixed by comparison: `fawqa ma ittasalat bi-kull maqam qablahu wa ba'dahu` — above whatever connected with any station before him or after him. "Before him" reaches back to the manifestation of `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah`; "after him" extends forward to the emanation of the `nuqta` of 'Ayn al-'Azimah. Within that whole span no station received divine aids of the kind bestowed upon 'Ayn al-'Azimah. There is one and only one exception, named expressly: `siwa al-qa'im 'ala dhikrihi al-salam` — except for the Qa'im, peace be upon his mention. For the text continues, `fa-innahu yattasilu bihi fawqa ma ittasala bi-Amir al-Mu'minin` ("for it connects with him above that which connected with Amir al-Mu'minin"). The divine aid will connect with the Qa'im to a degree surpassing even what connected with Amir al-Mu'minin.

### Part and Whole: The Hierarchy from the Imams to Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah

The reason for this single exception is given through a precise metaphysics of part and whole. `Li-anna Amir al-Mu'minin bi-nisbati ilayhi juz'un wa al-qa'im kullun` — because Amir al-Mu'minin, in relation to the Qa'im, is a part (`juz'`), while the Qa'im is the whole (`kull`). And this relation of partiality ascends and descends along the entire chain. `Wa kadhalika fa-inna maqamat al-a'immah kull maqam minha juz'iyyun` — and likewise the stations of the Imams: every station among them is partial. The Imams that there are stand as parts. And who is the whole in relation to them, the Imam of Imams, the `Imam al-a'immah`? It is 'Ayn al-'Azimah. Yet 'Ayn al-'Azimah is in turn partial in relation to the Qa'im who is to manifest; and the Qa'im is partial in relation to `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah`.

The station of Amir al-Mu'minin is nevertheless called whole — `wa maqam Amir al-Mu'minin kulli` — and this is no contradiction, for the principle holds that a whole is only whole when there stands a part over against it. He is whole in relation to the Imams who are his parts, and part in relation to the Qa'im who is his whole. The hierarchy is relational at every link: the Imams are partial to 'Ayn al-'Azimah, 'Ayn al-'Azimah is partial to the Qa'im, the Qa'im is partial to `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah`. Each station is at once a whole for what lies beneath it and a part for what lies above.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's prayers and peace be upon him and his family, alluded to this very relation. `Wa qad ramaza Rasulullah bi-dhalika haythu qal: al-Hasan wa al-Husayn imamani bi-haqqin qama aw qa'ada wa abuhuma khayrun minhuma` ("And the Messenger of Allah alluded to this when he said: al-Hasan and al-Husayn are two rightful Imams, whether they rise or sit, and their father is better than them"). Both are rightful Imams — `imamani bi-haqqin` — whether they rise or whether they sit. And their father, Amir al-Mu'minin, is better than the two of them: the whole standing above its parts. The saying thus encodes the doctrine of part and whole within the household itself, the two sons as Imams whose father surpasses them.

### Qiyam and Qu'ud: Why the Prophet Placed Rising Before Sitting

This hadith holds a striking feature that repays attention. Hasan is the earlier Imam; Husayn comes after him in sequence. Yet the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, placed the condition of rising — `qiyam` — before the condition of sitting — `qu'ud`: `qama aw qa'ada`, "whether they rise or sit." In the ordinary sequence of the Imamate, Hasan precedes Husayn. But in the act of `qiyam`, the rising, it is Husayn's turn that is named, and Hasan stands with `qu'ud`, the sitting.

The meaning of the two terms must be made plain. To enact the Imamate openly among the common people, carrying its commands into the public sphere and proclaiming its authority, is what is called `qiyam` — and this is what Husayn did. To remain silent, withholding the open proclamation of the Imamate among the people while remaining in every respect the rightful Imam, is `qu'ud` — and this was Hasan's course. The Prophet's words therefore embrace both modes: whether the Imam rises in open enactment or sits in silence, he is an Imam in every condition. Hasan's silence did not diminish his Imamate, nor did Husayn's rising create it; the rank is intrinsic, and the two verbs describe only its outward manner. The Prophet placed `qiyam` first because the open enactment among the people — the deed of Husayn — is the form by which the Imamate becomes manifest to the world, while Hasan's `qu'ud` held the same rank in concealment.

### "I Am the First and the Last": Ali Across the Cycles

From this exalted state, divine miracles and brilliant signs appeared in Amir al-Mu'minin. `Wa min hadhihi al-halah zaharat al-mu'jizat al-ilahiyah wa al-ayat al-bahirat al-'azimah` ("And from this state, the divine miracles and the brilliant, great signs appeared"). And `wa anfa'ala lahu jami' ma fi 'alam al-tabi'ah hatta iftana bihi man arahu ma yurid` — all that is in the world of nature became obedient to him, so that some among those who saw what he showed them were so astonished that they fell into error. From this came the excess of certain of his Shi'a, who, out of deification (`ilahiyat`), declared him to be God when they beheld his miracles, saying `salamun Allah 'alayk` — words of greeting raised to him in their bewilderment. This was their error, born of seeing signs they could not place.

Then comes the great utterance: `wa lamma nutiqa bihi fi qawlihi: ana al-awwal wa al-akhir` ("and when he was made to speak it in his saying: I am the First and the Last"). The interpretation given is precise and guards against any misreading. He is `awwal suwar al-adwar al-mutaqaddimah` — the first of the forms of the preceding cycles. Of all the forms (`suwar`) that have occurred across the cycles (`adwar`), his is the very first in the sequence. And `wa huwa al-akhir min hadhihi al-jiha` — he is the last from this same aspect. The text glosses it: `lamma kana awwal al-adwar wa akhiraha` — when he is the first of the cycles and the last of them. "The last of them" points to the Qa'im, peace be upon him, who is the last of the cycle. The pronoun `ha` in `akhiraha` refers back to `adwar`, the plural "cycles," which is treated as feminine in the grammar — for `dawr` is singular and `adwar` its plural, and the plural takes the feminine pronoun. So Ali declares himself first and last of the cycles: first in the rank attained after `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah`, a rank no one else attained, and last in that the final form, the Qa'im, completes what he began.

### A Grammatical Excursus: The Oaths of Surat al-Shams

The discussion of grammatical gender that arises from `akhiraha` opens onto a sustained reading of Surat al-Shams, a sura that demonstrates the principle vividly. Its opening oath is `wa al-shamsi wa duhaha` ("by the sun and its brightness"). Is `shams`, the sun, feminine or masculine? It is feminine — and so the oath reads "by the sun and by its brightness," the pronoun `ha` in `duhaha` referring to the sun precisely because the sun is feminine. Were it masculine, the pronoun `hu` would have been used instead.

The sura continues, `wa al-qamari idha talaha` ("and by the moon when it follows it"). The moon follows the sun; the pronoun `ha` again refers to the sun. Then `wa al-nahari idha jallaha` ("and by the day when it reveals it") — and here too the pronoun `ha` refers to the sun: the day makes the sun manifest, displays it to sight. The word `nahar`, day, is the subject (the `waw` before it being the `waw` of oath, which is why it carries the genitive `kasra`), and `ha` is the object — that which is revealed, namely the sun. Were `nahar` itself feminine, the verb would be `jallat` rather than `jalla`. Then `wa al-layli idha yaghshaha` ("and by the night when it covers it") — the oath is now of the night, when it covers the sun and diminishes its light. Throughout these clauses, every pronoun `ha` refers back to the sun.

The oaths extend to the heavens and the earth: `wa al-sama'i wa ma banaha` ("and by the sky and what built it") and `wa al-ardi wa ma tahaha` ("and by the earth and what spread it"). The oath is taken of the sky and of the Being who built it — for `ma` here bears both the sense of "that which" and the sense of "as," so the meaning is "and as He built it." Likewise the earth and the One who spread it flat (`musattah`). Here the lesson on gender is brought to its point: in Arabic, words are made feminine or masculine by two routes. One is feminine by analogy (`qiyasi`) and one is feminine by convention (`sama'i`) — the convention of usage. The usual rule is that a terminal `ta` marks the feminine; yet there are exceptions in both directions. Talha ends in a `ta` and ought by the rule to be feminine, yet it is a man's name and is treated as masculine. Zaynab has no `ta` and yet is feminine. So too `sama'` (sky), `ard` (earth), and `shams` (sun) carry no `ta`, yet all are treated as feminine by convention — `mu'annath sama'i` — while `qamar` (moon) is treated as masculine. The whole of Surat al-Shams, from `wa al-shams` down to `qad aflaha man zakkaha` ("he has succeeded who purifies it"), is built upon fourteen oaths, and its grammar of gender carries its meaning.

### "I Am the Hidden, I Am the Manifest": The Batin and the Zahir

Returning to the sayings of Ali, the text takes up `wa qawluhu ana al-batin` ("and his saying, I am the Hidden"). The explanation: `lamma kana min 'ilm al-batin alladhi minhu kanat al-suwar al-latifah al-batinah al-ilahiyah wa al-nufus al-mutajawharah al-nuraniyah al-lati hiya iyyaha` — it was from the hidden knowledge (`'ilm al-batin`) that there came forth the subtle, hidden, divine essences and the substantial, luminous souls. From the aggregate of all these — the subtle essences and the substantial luminous souls — the `haykal`, the spiritual structure, of 'Ayn al-'Azimah was formed. These are the souls of 'Ayn al-'Azimah; his `haykal` is composed of all these subtle essences and souls. From this aspect the `haykal` of 'Ayn al-'Azimah is `batin` — hidden, not visible to the eye, though its effects are manifest throughout the world. This is the sense in which Ali says "I am the Hidden."

And `wa qawluhu wa ana al-zahir` ("and his saying, And I am the Manifest"): `lamma zahara lil-absar bi-al-mu'jizat al-ilahiyah` — when he appeared to the sights through divine miracles. His saying "I am the Manifest" refers to his appearance before the eyes by means of the divine miracles he displayed. Thus the two names hold together the two faces of the one reality: hidden in the constitution of his `haykal` from subtle essences and luminous souls, manifest in the miracles seen by all.

### The First Forms of the Awliya' and Their Connection to the Imam of Imams

The text proceeds: `wa kanat al-a'mal al-zahirah al-mardiyah al-salihah awwal al-suwar al-lati anbatat lil-awliya' min al-qulub al-ta'wiliyah wa al-hikam al-ilahiyah al-lati anbatat fi jami' al-adwar al-salihah` ("and the manifest, pleasing, righteous deeds were the first forms that were established for the friends of God from the interpretive hearts and the divine wisdoms, which were established in all the righteous cycles"). The righteous, pleasing, manifest deeds are the first forms established for the `awliya'`, the friends of God, drawn from the interpretive hearts (`qulub ta'wiliyah`) and the divine wisdoms (`hikam ilahiyah`). These were established across all the previous cycles, and they connect ultimately to Ali. Every manifest wisdom, every interpretive wisdom, every real and divine wisdom established in the past cycles became connected, through their respective Imams, to the Imam of Imams — Janab Abu Talib. The chain of wisdoms across all the cycles converges, link by link through the Imams, upon him.

This convergence operates by a definite process within the structure of the Imamate. In the `haykal` of an Imam, only lights and the wisdoms of interpretation gather; all of them collect there. So it is with the `haykal` of the Prophet: the wisdoms gathered, and from there the Prophet obtained them. The sign of the veiled one (`muhtajib`) of 'Ayn al-'Azimah lies in this gathering. An Imam establishes his `bab` (gate), and all the knowledge gathers with the `bab`. The same holds of the Noble Prophet. As was explained before, the knowledge gathers with the `bab al-abwab`, the gate of gates; and before that, it gathers with the `hujjat`; and before that, with the `da'i`. That same ascending process — `da'i`, then `hujjat`, then `bab` — is established here as well. All the Imams pass through this process by way of their `bab`, who is the Noble Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and so they gather with 'Ayn al-'Azimah. The Imams have separate `haykals` — one for the Prophet of the Universe and a distinct one for each — yet by this same process those `haykals` go and connect with 'Ayn al-'Azimah. Those forms capable of being included in that `haykal` are taken into it; the rest are given to the ones who are to come, who will then carry them forward. So the whole gathering proceeds.

### "I Transported the Prophets": The Speaker of the Forms

The text gives another saying: `wa qawluhu ana nuqiltu Adam wa Nuh wa Ibrahim wa Musa wa 'Isa 'alayhim al-salam, wa ana naba'tu al-nabiyyin, wa ana arsaltu al-mursalin` ("and his saying, I transported Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus, peace be upon them, and I made the prophets prophets, and I sent the messengers"). The verb is `nuqiltu` — "I transported," "I conveyed." Ali declares that he transported Adam, Noah, Ibrahim, Musa, and 'Isa; that he made the prophets into prophets (`naba'tu al-nabiyyin`); and that he sent the messengers (`arsaltu al-mursalin`).

The text then identifies the one who speaks these matters: `wa al-natiq bi-dhalika al-suwar huwa al-natiq 'an indih min awwal suwar al-sittat alaf sana ila qiyamihi` — the one who speaks these forms speaks from himself, from the first form of the six thousand years until his rising. He is the speaker of these `suwar`, from the close of the `dawr al-kashf` until his rising. The reckoning of years is recalled here: three thousand years is the duration of the `barzakh`, and six thousand years the larger span. And `wa hum ahl al-haqa'iq wa al-maqamat` — and they are the people of realities and of lofty stations.

The next saying ascends higher still: `wa qawluhu ana bi-kull shay'in 'alim` ("and his saying, I am knowledgeable of all things"). Its ground is given: `lamma ittasalat bihi dhalika al-nur al-jari ittisalan kulliyan, 'alima bihi ma kana wa ma huwa ka'in` — when that flowing light (`nur jari`) connected with him with a total connection, he knew through it what was and what is to be. `Wa sara jami' 'alam al-tabi'ah bayna yadayhi ka-al-kurah al-mulqat la takhfa 'alayhi minha khafiyah` — and the entire world of nature became before him like a ball thrown down, with no hidden thing concealed from him. The whole of the natural world lay open before him like a cast ball, nothing veiled.

From this aspect he said, `inna li manzilah lam takhtur 'ala qalb bashar wa haddan lam yablugh ma'rifatihi ahad` ("indeed I have a station that has not occurred to a human heart, and a limit whose gnosis no one has reached"). There is for him a rank that has never entered the heart of any human being — no heart knows that station — and a limit that no one's gnosis has ever reached.

### Lordship and Divinity, and the Reality of Ali's Gnosis

The passage then states: `wa inna al-rububiyah wa al-ilahiyah la takhturu 'ala qulub al-bashar fa-ya'rifuha illa al-qa'im minhum` ("and indeed Lordship and Divinity do not occur to the hearts of men that they might know it, except for the Qa'im among them"). Lordship (`rububiyah`) and Divinity (`ilahiyah`) do not descend upon the hearts of men such that they could know their reality — none can grasp them except the Qa'im among them. This must be understood with care. The terms `rububiyah` and `ilahiyah` are not taken here as a claim to godhead. They are interpreted as the relation of the one above to the one below — the Lord in his lordship over what is beneath him. It is in this relational sense, not in any sense of a divinity claim, that the words are meant. No human can comprehend in what rank Ali stands, or how he has gathered the forms; that comprehension belongs to the Qa'im.

The grades of knowing Allah are then set out: `wa anna al-khalqa bi-ajma'ihim ya'rifuna Allah bi-al-ma'rifah al-zahirah, wa ahl al-haqa'iq ya'rifunahu bi-haqiqat ma'rifatihi wa yuwahhidunahu min wajh tawhidihi` ("and that all of creation knows Allah with the outward gnosis, and the people of realities know Him with the reality of His gnosis and declare His oneness from the aspect of His oneness"). All creatures, without exception, know Allah with an outward gnosis (`al-ma'rifah al-zahirah`). The people of realities (`ahl al-haqa'iq`) know Him with the reality of His gnosis and affirm His oneness from the very aspect of that oneness.

But concerning Ali himself the text fixes a stricter limit: `wa anna 'Aliyyan lam ya'rifhu ahadun bil-jumlah bi-haqiqat ma'rifatihi illa Rasulullah wa al-a'immah min wuldihi` ("and that no one at all knew Ali with the reality of his gnosis except the Messenger of Allah and the Imams from his progeny"). No person whatever knew Ali with the reality of his gnosis save the Messenger of Allah and the Imams of his progeny. `Wa 'arrafahu ahl al-haqa'iq bi-zahir al-ma'rifah` — and he made himself known to the people of realities only with the outward gnosis, `bi-ithbat al-ayat wa al-burhan wa al-mu'jizat al-lati azharaha lahum marratan ba'da marratan` ("by the establishment of signs, proofs, and miracles that he manifested for them time after time"). The reality of his gnosis was the possession of the Messenger and the Imams alone, as the Prophet himself declared in the hadith; the people of realities attained only the outward gnosis, through the signs and proofs and miracles he displayed to them again and again.

### The Divergence of Voices and the Hidden Secret

When the people heard these claims, their understandings scattered. `Wa lamma sami'a al-nass hadha al-qawl qala fih qawmun huwa al-ghayb, ta'ala wa takabbara 'amma yaqul al-jahilun 'uluwwan kabira` ("and when the people heard this statement, a group said of him, He is the Unseen — exalted and greatly transcendent is he above what the ignorant say"). One group declared him to be `al-ghayb`, the Unseen. `Wa qala akharuna huwa al-mubda' al-awwal` — and others said he is the First Originated Being. `Wa qala akharuna huwa al-inbi'ath al-awwal` — and yet others said he is the First Emanation. `Wa tasha'abat fihim al-aswat wa al-aqwal` — the voices and sayings diverged among them, opinions branching in every direction. `Wa ghabba 'anhum hadha al-sirr al-daqiq alladhi lam yattasil bihi illa al-aqall min al-awliya'` — and this subtle secret was hidden from them, the secret with which none connected except the fewest of the friends of God.

What was that hidden secret, that true gnosis? It is precisely the teaching at the heart of this whole passage: that Ali's `nuqta`, the spiritual point, is the highest point after `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah`, and that the `nuqta` of the coming Qa'im will be higher still than his. This is the true gnosis, and it remained hidden from all but the very few. The text closes its movement with praise: those whom Allah favored with the effusion of the blessings of their masters — `alladhina an'am Allahu 'alayhim bi-fayd barakat mawalihi, salamun Allah 'alayhim` — and `wa al-hamdu lillahi 'ala dhalika kullih al-hamd alladhi ja'alana minhum wa lam ya'dil bina 'anhum` ("and praise be to Allah for all of that, the praise of one whom He made among them and did not turn away from them"). Praise to Allah, who placed us among them, among the friends of God who received the effusion of blessings, and did not turn us away from them.

It must be remembered that all these sayings reported of Ali were first spoken by the Prophet himself: "Ali, your gnosis was attained by none but me and Allah." During the era of the Banu Umayya great efforts were made to suppress these merits. Fabricated hadiths were circulated against him; whatever the Prophet had said in his praise in any sermon was countered by some manufactured tradition, and anyone who brought forward such a fabrication was rewarded for it. This was the Umayyad practice — a long and grievous story of the lengths to which they went to conceal the virtues of Ali.

### The Two Veils: Why Ali's Hijab Was Revealed and the Qiyam of Husayn

A puzzling outward fact must now be addressed. Outwardly, the miracles of the Prophet appeared greater than those of the Master of the Universe, Ali. Why should this be? The answer lies in the doctrine of the two veils. One is the veil (`hijab`) and one the lower veil (`adna hijab`). Ali's is the `hijab`; the Prophet's is the `adna hijab`. And Ali's veil was revealed — that is, sent into the world — after `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah` was revealed.

The reason concerns the settling of accounts. In the previous eras, the prophets had rendered service to the established ones (`mustaqarrin`) — entering into hardships and bearing troubles on their behalf. These services created accounts that had to be settled. Ali, who is the established one (`mustaqarr`), was therefore sent into the world in the veil of the `mustaqarr` so that he could settle those accounts owed to the `mustaqarrin` on behalf of the prophets who had served them. And the one who is the Qa'im over the prophets and messengers — the one with whom all the prophets are gathered — is the Noble Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Ali came as the representative of the `mustaqarr` so that all the services the prophets had performed for the `mustaqarrin` might have their accounts settled in full, every giving and taking completed. In this manner Ali came and performed `qiyam`.

And Husayn — with what did he perform `qiyam`? In the wars that occurred in the time of the Prophet and in the time of Ali, many transgressions took place; their accounts too had to be settled. To settle the accounts of those transgressions, Imam Husayn, peace and blessings be upon him, performed `qiyam`. Without that rising, the `haykal` of Imam Husayn — which was with the Master of the Universe — could not have developed. His rising was not a mere historical tragedy but the means by which a great spiritual structure was brought to completion, the requital of accounts long outstanding.

### The Durud and the Treasuries of the Unseen

Why is so vast a reward promised in the `durud`, the salutation we send upon the Prophet and his family? The reward kept for us in `salli 'ala` exists so that our spiritual connection with them may be established. To send blessings upon the Prophet and the family of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon them, is in truth a prayer — a prayer whose very acceptance is the reward. By offering the prayer one receives reward from their side as well: a reward drawn from their unseen treasuries. That treasury is immense, beyond all estimation, for within it are gathered the forms (`suwar`) of crores of years, gathered and held. From their side it is gathered and stored; and through the salutation, the one who prays draws upon it. The acceptance of the prayer is itself the reward bestowed from those vast and unmeasurable treasuries of the unseen.

### The Reckoning of the Cycles: The Qawr of Four Hundred Thousand Years

The mention of the treasuries' antiquity opens onto the great chronology. How old is this world? Not a thousand years, as one might first suppose. The reckoning runs in `qawrs` — also called `dawrs`. Since `Dhu al-Sa'y al-Ibda'iyah` manifested, three hundred ninety-nine `qawrs` have passed, and the four hundredth `qawr` of the `dawr al-kashf` is now to manifest. A `qawr` — a `dawr` — equals four hundred thousand years. The four hundred thousand `dawrs` reckoned here belong to the first such cycle of this universe; this is the first `dawr`. We stand now in the last part of this first span of four hundred thousand years, and the forms (`suwar`) of these four hundred thousand years are with 'Ayn al-'Azimah. It is a long, long expanse of time, and the treasuries gathered across it are correspondingly vast.

### Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman: Nothing Accepted Without the Wilayah of Ali

The second chapter of the second gate of the third lamp turns to a weighty pronouncement of authority. `Wa min hadhihi al-halat al-'azimah wa al-manzilat al-'aliyat al-jasimah al-sabiq dhikruha, qala Sayyiduna Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman, a'la Allahu qudsahu` — from this magnificent state and lofty, grand station already mentioned, our master Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman, may Allah elevate his sanctity, said. He related that it has been recorded in the histories and biographies (`dhukira fi al-tawarikh wa al-siyar`): `inna Allaha Ta'ala la yaqbalu nubuwwata nabiyyin wala istifa'a wasiyyin wala imamata waliyyin wala 'amala ta'atin min 'amilin, wa law taqatta'a bi al-'ibadat awsalihi wajibatin wa mustahaddah, illa bi-wilayah 'Ali ibn Abi Talib` ("verily Allah Most High does not accept the prophethood of any prophet, nor the selection of any successor, nor the imamate of any guardian, nor any act of obedience from any doer — even if he be cut to pieces in worship, in the obligatory and the supererogatory — except by the wilayah of Ali ibn Abi Talib").

The verb `taqatta'a` is vivid: it means to be cut into pieces, to destroy oneself utterly in worship, spending one's nights in devotion to the point of exhaustion. Even such a worshipper's deeds are not accepted apart from the `wilayah` of Mawla Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Imam of the names. `Wa man ata bi-ghayri wilayahhi asqatat nubuwwatuhu wa wasayatuhu wa salih 'amaluhu, wa lam yaqbal Allahu minhu wala yuzakki 'amaluhu` ("and whoever comes without his wilayah, his prophethood is nullified, and his successorship, and his righteous deeds, and Allah does not accept from him nor purify his deeds"). Whoever comes without his `wilayah` — his prophethood is voided, his successorship voided, his good deeds voided; Allah neither accepts nor purifies them. The ground is then stated: `li-annahu, salawatullahi 'alayhi, majma' al-a'immati wa al-awsiya'i min awwal al-adwar ila qiyamihi` ("because he, may Allah's prayers be upon him, is the confluence of the Imams and the successors from the beginning of the cycles until his advent").

### Ali as the Confluence of the Imams and Successors

Here lies the reason that nothing is accepted without his `wilayah`: Ali is the confluence (`majma'`) of all the Imams and successors (`awsiya'`), gathering them from the beginning of the cycles until his own advent, may the peace of Allah be upon him. Because every Imam and every successor across all the cycles converges in him, to come without his `wilayah` is to come cut off from the whole stream of guidance; such prophethood, successorship, and obedience have no validity, for they are severed from the very point in which all of them meet.

A clarifying question arises about what `wilayah` requires. The pronouncement does not say one must merely recognize Ali's station, or merely affirm that he is the caliph, or merely love him. Is mere love sufficient? It is not. `Wilayah` carries with it obedience to his commands. A servant who only declares "I acknowledge the master" but does not in fact hold him as master — who professes love yet does not obey the master's commands — has not given `wilayah`. To love without obedience is to withhold the very thing that `wilayah` means. The acceptance of which Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman speaks is contingent upon `wilayah` understood as both allegiance of the heart and obedience of the deed.

### The Service of the Master for His Slave: Abdan's Saying and the Justice of Requital

From this same direction and state, the text validates a saying of Maulana al-Da'i Abdan, may Allah sanctify his soul, recorded in his book Majmu' al-Tarbiyah — a reference duly given, for Maulana Abdan was the author of many works, above eighty in number, in which our master Ja'far also records their count. `Wa min hadhihi al-jiha wa al-hala sahha qawl al-da'i Abdan, qaddasa Allahu ruhahu, inna al-jum'ah 'ala khidmat al-mawla li-'abdihi` — from this aspect and this state, the saying of al-Da'i Abdan is proven correct: that the `jum'ah` is founded upon the service of the master for his slave.

The meaning, at first opaque, becomes clear through the history of the two lineages. The people of the manifest da'wat (`da'wat zahirah`) in the past cycles were the children of Ishaq, peace be upon them. They served as `hujjahs`, `du'at`, and servants for the masters of the esoteric da'wat — the children of Isma'il, peace be upon them, who are the lofty divine stations. From the time of our master Adam until the era of the Prophet, the children of Ishaq served the children of Isma'il. The children of Isma'il remained hidden in concealment, while the children of Ishaq endured the hardships of open service. Upon this prior service the `jum'ah` is established: the master's service for his slave, focused upon this requital in the present cycle.

The text states the turning point: `fa-lamma ijtama'at 'inda Amir al-Mu'minin, salawatullahi 'alayhi, wa ijtama'at awladu Ishaq 'inda Natiq al-dawr, salamullahi 'alayhi, wajaba fi maziyyat 'adli Allahi Ta'ala khidmatu Amir al-Mu'minin wa al-Payghambar 'alayhima qadha'an bi-ma sabaqa min khidmati awladi Ishaq li-awladi Isma'il, salamullahi 'alayhim` ("and when they gathered with Amir al-Mu'minin, and the children of Ishaq gathered with the Natiq of the era, it became obligatory in the special quality of Allah's justice that Amir al-Mu'minin serve the Prophet as requital for the prior service of the children of Ishaq to the children of Isma'il"). The forms gathered with Amir al-Mu'minin, the Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il, while the children of Ishaq gathered with the Natiq of the era, the Prophet, the Qa'im of the progeny of Ishaq. In the distinction (`maziyyah`) of the justice of Allah Most High, it then became obligatory that Ali serve the Prophet — `qadha'an`, as a requital — to fulfill what had already passed: the service the children of Ishaq had rendered to the children of Isma'il throughout the prior cycles.

### The True Meaning of Qadha': Fulfilling What Was Missed

The word `qadha'` must be rightly understood, for its common usage misleads. In Urdu we say "I performed the `qadha'` of the prayer," and take it to mean a prayer that was missed and is now being made up — as though `qadha'` meant lateness or omission. But in Arabic, `qadha'` means to complete, to fulfill. The making up of a missed prayer is called `qadha'an ma fata` — fulfilling what was missed — or `qadha'an fa'itan`; the missed prayer is fulfilled, and that fulfillment is what `qadha'` names. The root sense is completion of what is owed, not the mere fact of having missed it. So when the text says Ali's service to the Prophet is `qadha'`, it means a fulfillment, a completing requital, of the service rendered in prior eras by the children of Ishaq to the children of Isma'il.

### Measure for Measure: The Established Scale of Justice

The text gives the full content of this service: `wa hadha al-ma'na li-khidmat al-mawla li-'abdihi wa qiyamuhu bayna yadayhi wa sa'yuhu ma'ahu wa muharabatuhu li-azdadihi wa qatlihi li-man ankara manzilatahu wa janaba 'an ta'atihi wa jihadihi li-ahl al-kufr al-mu'anidin lahu, kama kana khadiman lahu fi dawri Ibrahim wa Musa wa 'Isa, 'alayhim al-salam, hazwan bi-hazwin, wala yughadiru saghiratan wala kabiratan illa ahsaha` ("and this is the meaning of the service of the master for his slave, and his standing before him, and his striving with him, and his fighting against his enemies, and his killing of whoever denied his station and turned away from his obedience, and his struggle against the hostile people of disbelief — just as he was a servant to him in the eras of Ibrahim, Musa, and 'Isa, measure for measure, and he leaves neither a small thing nor a great one but he has counted it").

This is the concrete shape of the requital: Ali standing before the Prophet, striving alongside him, fighting his enemies, contending against whoever denied the Prophet's station or turned away from his obedience, struggling against the hostile people of disbelief who set themselves against the Prophet. All of this was requital for the service the prophets had rendered in the eras of Ibrahim, Musa, and 'Isa — `hazwan bi-hazwin`, measure for measure, like for like. The masters were the Qa'im of the children of Isma'il, who is Sayyiduna Ali ibn Abi Talib, and the Qa'im of the children of Ishaq, who is the Prophet. When the Prophet's era came, the services rendered across the six thousand years by the children of Ishaq for the children of Isma'il were to be requited in the balance of justice. And so Ali served the Prophet — for "the prophets called upon Ali in times of difficulty" — giving his life in the battles, fighting, and defending the Prophet, and thereby the requital was fulfilled.

The closing clause seals the principle: `wala yughadiru saghiratan wala kabiratan illa ahsaha` — nothing small and nothing great is left out; all of it is counted and enumerated. This is so because `mizan al-'adli qa'imun` — the balance of justice is established. The scale of justice stands fixed, and on it the `mustaqarr` now serves the `mustawda'` in exact requital for what was done before, with no smallest or greatest deed left unaccounted. The justice of Allah Most High permits no item to escape the reckoning.

### The Tenth Intellect, the Veil of Muhammad, and the Mediation of Jadd, Fath, and Khayal

The chapter turns finally to the mode by which support reaches the Prophet from above. `Wa kana al-'Aql al-'Ashir huwa al-muhtajibu bi-Muhammad 'alayhi al-salam wa al-mu'ayyidu lahu al-naziru ilayhi al-mumiddu lahu bi-wasatit al-jadd wa al-fath wa al-khayal, 'inda kamalihi wa bulughihi rutbat al-hijabiyyah` ("and the Tenth Intellect is the one veiled by Muhammad, the supporter of him, the one who looks upon him, the one who provides aid to him through the mediation of jadd, fath, and khayal, upon his perfection and his reaching the rank of hijabiyyah"). The Tenth Intellect (`'Aql al-'Ashir`) is the prepared summoner (`musta'id da'iya`) who is in the world of life. He is veiled by Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him; he supports the Prophet, looks upon him with care, and conveys aid to him through the mediation of `jadd`, `fath`, and `khayal`, at the moment of the Prophet's perfection and his attainment of the rank of veilhood (`hijabiyyah`).

The `musta'id da'iya` belongs to the initial period — the one who appeared first and is dominant over all who came after in this regard, the master of Sarandip. He stands now before the Prophet as the past summoner; he may be taken as the Tenth Intellect of the beginning, whose arrangements continue ongoing. Through this veiled one — the supporter, the one who looks upon the Prophet, the one who conveys aid through `jadd`, `fath`, and `khayal` as the Prophet reaches perfection and the rank of `hijabiyyah` — the divine support is mediated.

### Instruction Rank by Rank: Every Natiq, Wasi, and Imam Must Learn

This mediation carries a general law: every `natiq`, every `wasi`, and every Imam must receive instruction and progress rank by rank. None is exempt from being taught and from ascending step by step. The principle is anchored in the word of Allah: `wa Allahu akhrajakum min butuni ummahatikum la ta'lamuna shay'a` ("and Allah brought you forth from the wombs of your mothers knowing nothing"). All are brought into the world knowing nothing, and all must learn — including the highest. The orders of guidance themselves ascend through instruction; no station is reached without being received and progressed through.

### The Prophet's Ascent: Ubayy ibn Ka'b, Zayd ibn 'Amr, and the One Above

The Prophet's own progression illustrates the law. `Fa-kana Muhammad, salawatullahi 'alayhi, akhidhun min Ubayy ibn Ka'b fi hali ta'limihi wa ibtida'ihi, wa huwa al-mukanna 'anhu bi-Jibra'il` ("and Muhammad was one who acquired from Ubayy ibn Ka'b in the state of his instruction and his beginning, and he is the one alluded to as Jibra'il"). At his beginning, in the state of his early instruction, the Prophet acquired knowledge from Mawla Ubayy ibn Ka'b, who is alluded to under the name Jibra'il. `Fa-lamma istakmala ma 'indahu, rafa'ahu ila Zayd ibn 'Amr, wa huwa da'i jaziratihi, wa huwa al-mukanna 'anhu bi-Mika'il` ("and when he had perfected what was with him, he elevated him to Zayd ibn 'Amr, who is the da'i of his island, and he is the one alluded to as Mika'il"). When the Prophet had completed what was with Ubayy ibn Ka'b, Ubayy elevated him to Zayd ibn 'Amr, the `da'i` of his island, who is alluded to under the name Mika'il. `Fa-lamma istaw'aba wa istakmala ma 'indahu, rafa'ahu ila man fawqahu, wa huwa Israfil` ("and when he had absorbed and perfected what was with him, he elevated him to the one above him, who is Israfil"). When the Prophet had fully absorbed and perfected what was with Zayd ibn 'Amr, Zayd elevated him to the one higher still, who is alluded to as Israfil. So the Prophet ascended through the ranks, instructed at each stage, lifted from teacher to teacher.

### Ibrahim's Ascent: The Star, the Moon, and the Sun

The same progression marks the Prophet's grandfather, Ibrahim. `Wa kadhalika jadduhu Ibrahim, fa-innahu taraqqa fi al-maratib shay'an ba'da shay'in, kama akhbara 'anhu al-kitab al-karim bi-qawlihi` — and similarly his grandfather Ibrahim ascended through the ranks one after another, as the Noble Book informs us. The Quranic account traces the climb: `fa-lamma janna 'alayhi al-laylu ra'a kawkaban, qala hadha rabbi, fa-lamma afala qala la uhibbu al-afilin` ("and when the night fell upon him, he saw a star and said, This is my Lord; then when it set, he said, I do not love those that set"). When night covered him, Ibrahim saw a star (`kawkab`) and said "This is my Lord"; but when it set, he said "I do not love those that set." `Fa-lamma ra'a al-qamara bazighan, qala hadha rabbi, fa-lamma afala, qala la-in lam yahdini rabbi la-akunanna min al-qawm al-dallin` — then when he saw the moon rising he said "This is my Lord," and when it set he said "If my Lord does not guide me, I shall surely be among the people who go astray." `Fa-lamma ra'a al-shamsa bazighatan, qala hadha rabbi, hadha akbaru, fa-lamma afalat, qala ya qawmi inni bari'un mimma tushrikun` — then when he saw the sun rising he said "This is my Lord, this is greater," and when it too set, he said "O my people, I am free of that which you associate as partners." Through the setting of star, moon, and sun, Ibrahim rejected each that passed away, ascending rank by rank toward the truth.

### Ali's Ascent: Khadijah, the Hujjah of Abu Talib, and Ibn Maryam

For Amir al-Mu'minin Ali the situation was the same. `Fa-innahu ta'allama 'inda Muhammad wa 'inda Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, allati hiya hujjat Imam al-'asr, alladhi huwa Abu Talib, salamullahi 'alayhim ajma'in` ("for he learned at the side of Muhammad and at the side of Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, who is the hujjah of the Imam of the Age, who is Abu Talib"). Ali learned at the side of the Prophet and at the side of Khadijah bint Khuwaylid — and Khadijah was the `hujjah` of the Imam of the Age, who was Mawla Abu Talib, peace be upon them all. `Wa li-dhalika rumiza 'alayhi bi-ibn Maryam` — and for this reason Ali was alluded to as Ibn Maryam, the son of Mary, when he was analogized to 'Isa and Khadijah was analogized to Maryam. As 'Isa learned at the side of Maryam, so Ali learned at the side of Khadijah, and the allusion follows.

This is confirmed in scripture: `kama qala al-kitab al-karim, wa lamma dhuriba ibnu Maryama mathalan, idha qawmuka minhu yasiddun` ("as the Noble Book says, and when the son of Mary was presented as an example, your people raised an outcry at it"). When Ibn Maryam was set forth as an example — and here the figure points to Ali — the people would obstruct him and turn away. The verse captures the response of the people who would stop him and disobey, just as it described the reception of the example of the son of Mary.

### A Thousand Doors, and From Each a Thousand More

The chapter closes with a measure of the knowledge Ali received: `wa kana salawatullahi 'alayhi yaqulu, 'allamani Rasulullah alfa babin min al-'ilm, infataha li min kulli babin alfa bab` ("and he, may Allah's prayers be upon him, used to say: The Messenger of Allah taught me a thousand doors of knowledge, and from every door a thousand doors opened for me"). Ali said that the Messenger of Allah taught him a thousand doors, a thousand chapters, of knowledge — and that from every one of those doors a thousand further doors opened to him. A thousand multiplied by a thousand: one hundred thousand doors of knowledge opened from this opening, a measureless expanse received from the Prophet, the fruit of instruction received and ranks ascended in the manner laid out for every `natiq`, `wasi`, and Imam.

## Muhammad, Ali, and the Light of the Ahl al-Bayt

### One Light Divided: the Hidden Secret of Muhammad and Ali

It is on account of a hidden secret (*sirr al-khafi*) and a sublime, noble meaning that Amir al-Mu'minin, peace be upon him, declared: `Ana wa Muhammad, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa alihi wa sallam, min nurin wahid` — "I and Muhammad, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, are from one single light." The light he speaks of is the light of God Himself. The two of them are not two separate luminous origins but the single radiance of the Divine, and it is precisely this unity at the root that the saying conceals beneath its surface. The grammar of the Arabic itself signals the weight of the cause: because the phrase is governed by the preposition *li-* — "on account of this hidden secret" (*wa li-hadha al-sirr al-khafi*) — the word *sirr* carries its genitive vowel, the *kasra*, as though the very inflection of the language bends toward the secret it names. For this hidden secret and this great and noble meaning, then, Amir al-Mu'minin testified that he and the Prophet are from one light, from the light of God.

From this single light proceeds the foundational act of differentiation. `Fa-amara Allahu dhalika al-nur an yanshaqqa nisfayn` — "So Allah commanded that light to split into two halves." The undivided radiance was, by Divine command, separated into two portions. To the first of these portions God said, `Fa-qala li-l-nisf al-awwal: Kun Muhammad, salla Allahu 'alayhi wa alihi wa sallam` — "And He said to the first half: Be Muhammad." And to the second portion He said, `Wa li-l-nisf al-thani: Kun 'Aliyyan` — "And to the second half: Be Ali." Thus the one light of God became Muhammad and Ali by the bare imperative of creation, *Kun* — "Be" — the same word by which all things are summoned into existence.

This splitting of the one light into Muhammad and Ali is not a mere statement about two persons; it is `isharatun ila lata'if awlad Ishaq wa awlad Isma'il, 'alayhim al-salam` — "an indication toward the subtleties of the children of Ishaq and the children of Isma'il, peace be upon them." The bifurcation of the light points to the two great branches of the prophetic lineage and to the two missions (*da'wat*) carried within them: the invitation of Prophethood and Apostleship (*nubuwwah* and *risalah*), and the invitation of Imamate and Guardianship (*imamah* and *wisayah*). What was divided here, more precisely, were the forms (*suwar*) and souls (*nufus*) — and these were of two kinds. The forms that bore the ranks of *nubuwwah* and *risalah* passed to one side, gathered in the line that would culminate in the Prophet; the forms that bore *imamah* and *wisayah* passed to the other, the line of Isma'il, peace be upon him, that would culminate in Ali. The one light, in dividing, distributed the destinies of prophecy and guardianship between its two halves.

This same truth was already affirmed in another of Amir al-Mu'minin's sayings, taken up here again: `Muhammadun al-yaqut al-asfar, wa ana al-yaqut al-ahmar min hadhihi al-jihah` — "Muhammad is the yellow sapphire, and I am the red ruby from this direction." Of the two precious stones, the more excellent is the red ruby; and it is this most excellent gem that Ali names as his own portion in the relationship, "from this direction" — that is, with respect to the particular aspect being expounded. Yet the Prophet, for his part, affirmed the same intimacy of origin from his own side, for he used to say of Ali: `'Aliyyun minni wa ana minhu` — "Ali is from me, and I am from him." And he said further: `La yu'addi 'anni illa 'Ali` — "None delivers on my behalf except Ali." No one but Ali would fulfil or discharge the Prophet's office in his stead. The two are bound so closely that each is "from" the other, and the burden of the Prophet's mission, in its delivery and continuation, rests with Ali alone.

### The Veil of the Tenth Intellect and the Three Emissaries

When Muhammad, peace be upon him, ascended through the ranks and reached the rank of veilship and the true utterance — `wa lamma irtaqa Muhammadun, salawatu Allahi 'alayh, fi al-rutab, wa balagha ila rutbat al-hijabiyyah wa al-nutq al-haqiqi, intaqala imam al-'asr al-naqib al-muqim lahu, alladhi huwa Abu Talib, salawatu Allahi 'alayh` — "when Muhammad ascended through the ranks and reached the rank of *hijabiyyah* and the true utterance (*al-nutq al-haqiqi*), the Imam of the Age, the established chief for him, who is Abu Talib, peace be upon him, transferred." That is to say: as the Prophet climbed to his final and supreme station, the Imam of his age, Abu Talib — who had been the established overseer guiding him — passed from this world, his earthly office completed precisely because the one he had nurtured had now arrived.

Here it is worth pausing over the supreme station the Prophet attained, for a question presses upon the listener. If the saying is true that had the Noble Prophet not come into the world the world and the universe itself would never have been created, does this mean that without a bearer for these lights and these forms, the creation of the cosmos simply would not have occurred? This is the meaning of the celebrated divine address, `Lawlaka lama khalaqtu al-aflak` — "Were it not for you, I would not have created the celestial spheres." Had the Prophet, peace be upon him, not existed, the *aflak*, the revolving heavens, would not have been brought into being. The explanation is this: what was gathered in the Prophet were the forms that bore *nubuwwah* and *risalah*, and upon these bearers of prophethood and apostleship rested certain retributions and reckonings — *qisasat*, the accounts of the world. The children of Isma'il — the line of *imamah* and *wisayah* — bore no such *qisasat*. It is the people of prophethood who are bound up with the *aflak*; for when they come, the chapter of *qisasat* opens together with the celestial spheres. With the *aflak* comes their friction, their revolution, and the whole sequence of created things through which accounts are settled. But those who are veiled (*muhtajib*) — and Ali is the veiled one, the head of the people of *imamah* and *wisayah* — are not destined to enter into the world's revolutions and upheavals (*inqilabat*). This must not be mistaken for a claim that the Prophet sins; it is not a question of any sin attaching to the Prophet. It is rather that those gathered with the prophetic line are people upon whom the world's reckonings remain to be discharged, and the prophetic office is the office that bears those reckonings within the turning of the spheres.

The Prophet's final station, then, is *rutbat al-hijabiyyah*, the rank of the veil, together with the true utterance. Of this it is said in the prayer, `an tab'athahu maqaman mahmudan alladhi wa'adtahu` — "that You may raise him to a praiseworthy station which You have promised him." This is the *maqam-e-mahmud*, the Praiseworthy Station, and it belongs to the Prophet. The Prophet would forever pray that he be raised to it, and that prayer abides forever, in the *haykal* of the Great Essence (*'ayn-e-'azimah*). The principle governing this is that as the *'ayn* is, so is the *mim*: of whatever rank the *'ayn* — the essence — attains, the *mim* — its manifestation — attains a corresponding rank. The two rise together, side by side. The establishment of the *'ayn* and of the *sar-mim* occurs across seven thousand years, and they advance in concert; and if, after one seven-thousand-year cycle, further such cycles follow, then as the *'ayn* ascends higher, so too does the *mim* ascend with it.

Upon Abu Talib's transfer, his station underwent a transformation: `wa sara fi maqamihi hijaban li-l-'ashir, matrahan li-shu'a'ihi, mumiddan lahu bi-wasatat al-jadd wa al-fath wa al-khayal` — "and he became, in his station, a veil for the Tenth, a place upon which its rays fall, aiding him through the mediation of *jadd*, *fath*, and *khayal*." It is Abu Talib, not the Prophet, who is the subject here: when Abu Talib passed away, his station became a veil for the Tenth Intellect (*al-'aql al-'ashir*) and a place upon which the rays of that Intellect descend, and from there he became an aider (*mumidd*) to the Prophet. The aid reached the Prophet through three emissaries — the *thalathat sufara'* — and these three are *jadd*, *fath*, and *khayal*. Through the mediation of these three, the support flowing from the Great Essence continued to reach the Prophet. The phrasing alludes to the *'ayn-e-'azimah*: it is the Great Essence that stands as the veil for the Tenth Intellect, the surface upon which its rays alight, while the aid is extended to the Prophet — to the *mim* — by way of *jadd*, *fath*, and *khayal*.

These three emissaries are described as `alladhina hum al-ruhaniyyun` — "those who are the spiritual ones." *Jadd*, *fath*, and *khayal* are spiritual. Opposite them stand `al-muqabilat al-thalathat al-jismaniyyun` — "the three corporeal ones who correspond to them," who exist `al-ka'inuna baynahu wa baynahum` — "between him and between them." And in reality these corporeal three are 'Abd al-Muttalib, 'Abdullah, and Abu Talib. The same three persons, then, are spoken of under two aspects: as spiritual, in the world of veiling (*'alam-e-ihtijab*), they are *jadd*, *fath*, and *khayal*; as corporeal, in the world of bodies, they are 'Abd al-Muttalib, 'Abdullah, and Abu Talib. To call them "spiritual" is to locate them in the world of veiling, where they function not as men of flesh but as the emissaries of the Intellect.

The reason for their transformation is stated plainly: `li-annahum qad saru 'uqulan mujarradatan 'an al-hawas... sufara'a baynahu wa bayna al-'aql al-'ashir al-mudabbir li-'alam al-tabi'ah, al-qa'im maqam al-'aql al-awwal li-dhalika al-'alam` — "because they had become intellects divested of the senses... emissaries between him and the Tenth Intellect, the governor of the world of nature, which stands in the place of the First Intellect for that world." Having shed the corporeal senses, they became pure intellects, emissaries standing between the Prophet and the Tenth Intellect. The Tenth Intellect is the governor of the world of nature, and it occupies, for that lower world, the very station that the First Intellect occupies at the summit of being — it stands in the place of the *aql al-awwal* for the world of nature. And the Great Essence, the *'ayn-e-'azimah*, takes upon itself the deputyship of the Tenth in the spiritual world, the world of life. So the Tenth Intellect mirrors the First Intellect downward into nature, while the Great Essence deputizes for the Tenth within the living, spiritual realm.

The text then names the three precisely: `Fa-'Abd al-Muttalib huwa al-jadd, wa 'Abdullah huwa al-fath, wa Abu Talib huwa al-khayal` — "So 'Abd al-Muttalib is the *jadd*, and 'Abdullah is the *fath*, and Abu Talib is the *khayal*." 'Abd al-Muttalib occupies the station of *jadd*, 'Abdullah the station of *fath*, and Abu Talib the station of *khayal*.

### Jibra'il, Mika'il, and Israfil: the Stations, Not Persons

When this same reality is rendered in the tongue of the Shari'ah — the idiom of the revealed law — these three emissaries bear the familiar names of the great angels. `Wa huwa bi-lisan al-shari'ah Jibra'il al-jabir li-Muhammadin fi awwalihi wa akhirihi` — "And he, in the tongue of the Shari'ah, is Jibra'il, the one who supports Muhammad in his beginning and in his end." Jibra'il is the supporter of the Prophet from the first to the last — at the outset, when the Prophet was raised up and trained, and at the end, when his station rose above. `Wa Mika'il 'Abdullah, wa Israfil 'Abd al-Muttalib` — "And Mika'il is 'Abdullah, and Israfil is 'Abd al-Muttalib." Thus in the language of the law, 'Abd al-Muttalib (the *jadd*) is Israfil, 'Abdullah (the *fath*) is Mika'il, and Abu Talib (the *khayal*) is Jibra'il — the one who supports Muhammad in his beginning and his end.

This raises a searching question. Does Jibra'il, peace be upon him, possess any existence in and of himself? For in an earlier lesson it was said that Ubayy ibn Ka'b was associated with Jibra'il, and here it is Abu Talib who is associated with Jibra'il. Does this mean that in every age there is a different figure who occupies this role? The answer is that Jibra'il is a station (*maqam*), not a self-subsisting entity. As the ranks of the one being trained increase, the nature of Jibra'il changes with them, and the helper becomes a different person. The image is that of a teacher: in childhood, a teacher instructs the child in the Qur'an — a *sahifah* teacher of the first letters; as the pupil advances, another teacher takes over; as he advances yet further, his aider (*mumidd*) is someone else again. So it is with Jibra'il: whoever comes to occupy that station of helper becomes Jibra'il for the one he assists.

The identity of the helper, therefore, depends upon the rank of the one receiving help. When the Prophet, peace be upon him, stood at the rank of responsiveness (*istijabat*) — that is, at the very beginning of his training — a certain figure occupied the helper's station for him. The same holds for Ali: at Ali's rank of *istijabat*, it was Ibn Maryam and the lady Khadijah who were his teachers and helpers. As the one being trained passes from rank to rank, the figure who is Jibra'il for him changes accordingly. And when at last he reaches the highest station — when he attains the luminous form of life itself (*surat-e-nurani-e-hayat*) — Jibra'il in the form of an external teacher is no longer required. From life itself the three stations are present: the three representatives who descend from the Tenth Intellect become for him Jibra'il, Mika'il, and Israfil. Jibra'il, in short, is never an independent being but always the name of a function, filled by whoever stands as the supporter at a given rank.

### Total Light and Ray: the Natiq and the Silent One

The relationship of Muhammad and Ali to the one light is now drawn with precision. `Fa-kana al-nur al-muttasil bi-Muhammadin salawatu Allahi 'alayh min al-'ashir kulliyyan, wa li-dhalika kana natiqan` — "So the light connected to Muhammad, peace be upon him, from the Tenth was total, and for that reason he was a *natiq* — a speaker." The light reaching the Prophet from the Tenth Intellect was complete, entire, undivided; and because he received the whole light, he became the one who speaks, the *natiq* who utters the revelation. `Wa huwa 'inda Amir al-Mu'minin shu'a', wa li-dhalika kana samitan` — "And the same light, with Amir al-Mu'minin, is a ray, and for that reason he was *samit* — silent." With Ali, the light was not the total radiance but a ray (*shu'a'*) of it; and because his portion was a ray, he was the silent one, the *samit*. The Prophet is thus the *natiq*, the speaker, and over against him stands Ali as the *samit*, his silent counterpart.

This complete light with the Prophet and its ray with Ali is bound up with the matter of the veil. The complete light was connected with the Prophet, and its rays were received by Ali; the entire radiance rests with the Prophet because his veil (*hijab*) was set very high, and from the Prophet, Ali received the light by way of that veil. It was the loftiness of the Prophet's veilship that made his the total light and Ali's the derived ray.

The relationship of the *mim* and the *'ayn* — of the manifestation and the essence, of Muhammad and Ali — unfolds in three conditions or modes, the three courses (*thalathah majari*). In the first and lowest course, Ali says, `Ana 'abdun min 'abidi Muhammad` — "I am a servant from among the servants of Muhammad." This is the course of the veil at its lowest: the *'ayn-e-'azimah*, the Great Essence, was sent into the world in the most humble veil so that Ali might be the companion of the Prophet, aid him, and support him — and so that the prophets of old, for all the services they had rendered, might receive their reward at the hand of the established one (*mustaqarr*). For this purpose Ali was sent, and this task he accomplished. This is the *majra-e-adna*, the lowest course. After it comes the course of equality, the *majra-e-musawat*, in which the Prophet himself declares the parity of the two, saying as he did in many of his sermons, `'Aliyyun minni wa ana minhu` — "O Ali, I am from you and you are from me" — the two set as equals. Then there is the third and highest course, expressed in the Prophet's saying to Ali, "O Ali, you have been given five things that even I have not been given." Among these the Prophet enumerates: that Ali was given such a father-in-law as the Prophet himself was not given — and the father-in-law is none other than the Noble Prophet, who speaks this of himself; that Ali was given such a wife as the Prophet was not given — and she is Fatimah; and that Ali was given such sons as the Prophet was not given — and they are Hasan and Husayn. These three gifts mark the superiority that belongs to Ali in this highest of the three courses of his relationship with the Prophet.

The earlier phrase, `matrahan li-shu'a'ihi` — "a place upon which his rays fall" — finds its sense here. The complete light was connected with the Prophet, and its rays were received by Ali; the entire *nur* abides with the Prophet, peace be upon him, because of the loftiness of his veil — and it is the veil that is the burden of this discussion. The connection of the light is from the Tenth Intellect to the Prophet directly, and from the Prophet, Ali received it as a ray, on account of the veil.

This distinction between total light and ray bears directly upon the knowledge each possessed. `Wa kana 'inda Muhammadin salawatu Allahi 'alayh 'ilmu ma kana wa 'ilmu ma sayakun` — "And with Muhammad, peace be upon him, was the knowledge of what has been and the knowledge of what will be." `Wa 'inda 'Aliyyin 'ilmu ma kana` — "And with Ali was the knowledge of what has been." The Prophet possessed both the knowledge of what had already occurred and the knowledge of what was yet to come — and so the Prophet made prophecies of future things. Ali possessed the knowledge of what had been, of what had already happened. This difference, too, arises from the veil of Ali: because Ali stood behind the veil, his portion of knowledge was the knowledge of the past, while the foreknowledge of the future belonged to the Prophet, who bore the complete light.

### Ali Becomes the Veil: the Succession of Jadd, Fath, and Khayal

The structure that obtained while the Prophet lived was transformed upon his transfer, exactly as it had been transformed at the passing of Abu Talib. `Wa lamma awfa Muhammadun salawatu Allahi 'alayh ma kana 'alayh min al-khidmah, wa nassa 'ala 'Aliyyin Amir al-Mu'minin salawatu Allahi 'alayh, wa intaqala ila dari karamati Allahi ta'ala, sara Amir al-Mu'minin fi makanihi hijaban li-l-'aql al-'ashir` — "And when Muhammad, peace be upon him, fulfilled the service that was upon him, and designated Ali, Amir al-Mu'minin, peace be upon him, by explicit appointment (*nass*), and transferred to the abode of the generosity of Allah the Exalted, Amir al-Mu'minin became, in his place, a veil for the Tenth Intellect." So long as the Prophet and Ali were both present in the world, the Prophet alone was the veil; when the Prophet passed away, Ali took his place and became the veil. `Matrahan li-shu'a'ihi, kama kana Muhammadun` — "a place upon which his rays fall, just as Muhammad had been" — Ali became the surface upon which the rays of the Tenth Intellect descend, exactly as the Prophet had been before him. `Wa al-'ashir mutajalliyan bihi, naziran ilayhi, mumiddan lahu bi-wasatat al-jadd wa al-fath wa al-khayal al-mutajarridin 'an al-ajsam` — "and the Tenth manifesting through him, looking toward him, aiding him through the mediation of *jadd*, *fath*, and *khayal*, the ones divested of bodies." The Tenth Intellect now manifests through Ali, gazes upon him, and aids him by way of the three emissaries who have shed their bodies.

But there is a crucial change in who fills the three stations. For the Prophet, the three had been 'Abd al-Muttalib, 'Abdullah, and Abu Talib. For Ali, the configuration shifts: `Alladhina hum 'Abd al-Muttalib wa Abu Talib wa Muhammad` — "those who are 'Abd al-Muttalib, Abu Talib, and Muhammad." Now the Prophet himself, having transferred, enters into the service of Ali as one of the three emissaries. The text specifies the stations: `Wa 'Abd al-Muttalib huwa al-jadd, wa Abu Talib huwa al-fath, wa Muhammad huwa al-khayal li-Amir al-Mu'minin` — "And 'Abd al-Muttalib is the *jadd*, and Abu Talib is the *fath*, and Muhammad is the *khayal* for Amir al-Mu'minin." So for Ali, 'Abd al-Muttalib stands as *jadd*, Abu Talib as *fath*, and the Prophet himself as *khayal* — these three becoming the helpers of Maula Ali.

A point of order in the list deserves note. For the Prophet, the sequence had run 'Abd al-Muttalib (*jadd*), 'Abdullah (*fath*), Abu Talib (*khayal*); for Ali, it runs 'Abd al-Muttalib (*jadd*), Abu Talib (*fath*), Muhammad (*khayal*). The reason Abu Talib moves into the *fath* position for Ali, displacing 'Abdullah, is one of sequence in passing away. 'Abdullah passed from the world earlier than Abu Talib — he was divested of his body before Abu Talib was. The emissary in each station must be one divested of bodies (*mutajarrid min al-ajsam*); only after a figure has been stripped of the corporeal can his emanation continue from the world of life. Since the order of these stations tracks the order of departure from the body, and since 'Abdullah departed first, the arrangement falls out as it does. There is a wordplay the teacher draws upon here: when 'Abdullah's name arises, it can be read either as `'Abdullah` (the proper name) or, with the dots removed, glossed as `'Abdullah 'ind Allah` — "the servant of God is with God." No dots were placed, so the reading remains open between the two.

The reason these three remain in their intermediary role, never entering the human form of the Prophet or of Ali, is then made explicit: `Wa li-hadha al-ma'na lam yazhar 'Abd al-Muttalib wa 'Abdullah wa Abu Talib fi haykal 'Aliyyin salawatu Allahi 'alayh, bal baqu fi ufq al-'ashir li-yakunu wasa'ita baynahu wa bayna Muhammadin wa 'Aliyyin 'alayhima al-salam` — "And for this meaning, 'Abd al-Muttalib, 'Abdullah, and Abu Talib did not appear in the *haykal* of Ali, peace be upon him; rather, they remained on the horizon of the Tenth (*ufq al-'ashir*) so that they might be intermediaries between it and Muhammad and Ali, peace be upon them both." They did not enter into the bodily form of Ali; they held their station on the horizon of the Tenth Intellect, serving as the channels between that Intellect and the two figures of Muhammad and Ali. The pronoun "between him" (*baynahu*) refers in the immediate sense to Ali — they are intermediaries for Ali — yet the phrase then extends, "and between Muhammad and Ali," so that they serve as intermediaries for the Prophet as well, and for both together during the time when both were present in the world. The three emissaries, that is, belong to the Prophet and to Ali alike.

As to the nature of this aid that descends through the three — is it manifest and outward, or spiritual? It is spiritual. What descends from the horizon of the Tenth are the decrees of spirituality (*ahkam*): what is to be done and what is to be left undone, the expression of the reality of things. The aid is the unveiling of realities, the granting of knowledge of how things truly are.

This whole arrangement is then raised to the level of a general rule. `Wa 'ala hadha al-manhaj fa-inna kulla muqimin li-maqamin min al-maqamat ba'da nuqlatihi fa-huwa al-khayal lahu, wa alladhi qablahu huwa al-fath, wa al-sabiqu 'alayhima huwa al-jadd` — "And according to this method, every establisher of one of the stations, after his transfer, is the *khayal* for it; and the one before him is the *fath*; and the one preceding them both is the *jadd*." Whichever Imam establishes a station, his immediate predecessor (his *muqim*), after that predecessor's passing, occupies the station of *khayal* for him; the one before that predecessor occupies the station of *fath*; and the one preceding both of them occupies the station of *jadd*. `Wa kullu wahidin 'amilun fi mandudihi` — "And each one is an agent within his extension (*mandud*)." Each of the three labours within his appointed range. The order, reading from nearest to most remote, is *khayal*, then *fath*, then *jadd*: the *khayal* is the immediate predecessor (*muqim*), who aids the establisher in all his works; then the predecessor's predecessor; then the one before them both. So the figure nearest in succession aids most directly, and the chain reaches back through *fath* to *jadd*.

This is why, in the specific case at hand, 'Abdullah stands as *fath* and Abu Talib as *khayal* for the relevant succession: Abu Talib, having outlived 'Abdullah and being the nearer in the chain of those who departed, falls into the station of *khayal*, while 'Abdullah, the earlier departed, recedes to *fath*. The principle that the nearer predecessor is *khayal* and the more remote is *fath* governs the assignment of the stations throughout.

The Prophet expressed the upward yearning of those who have passed on toward those still to come. `Kama qala salawatu Allahi 'alayh: Takhaffafu talhaqu` — "As he, peace be upon him, said: Lighten yourselves and you will catch up." The counsel is to lighten oneself of sins so as to rise and join the ranks of the angels — to reduce the burden that holds one back, that one may overtake those who have gone ahead. And he said further, `Fa-innama yantaziru bi-awwalikum quduma akhirikum` — "for the first of you only await the arrival of the last of you." Those who have already departed are waiting for those who will come after to arrive and be joined to them; the foremost stand in expectation of the latecomers, the whole community bound together across the span of time until all are gathered.

### The Sixth Natiq and the Gathering of the Islamic Jewel

The discourse now turns to the rising of the Prophet Muhammad as the sixth Natiq and the unfolding of his mission. `Wa lamma qama al-natiq al-sadis alladhi huwa Muhammad salawatu Allahi 'alayh bi-l-amr wa a'lana bi-l-shahadatayn wa aqama da'watahu al-zahirah duna al-batinah` — "And when the sixth Natiq, who is Muhammad, peace be upon him, rose with the command and proclaimed the two testimonies and established his exoteric mission, not the esoteric." The two testimonies (*al-shahadatayn*) are `La ilaha illa Allah, Muhammadun Rasulu Allah` — "There is no god but Allah; Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah." The sixth Natiq rose by Divine command, proclaimed this dual witness, and established his outward, exoteric mission — for at this stage the mission set forth was the exoteric one, the *da'wat al-zahirah*. `Aslama lahu man aslama wa jahada ma'ahu man jahada` — "Those who submitted, submitted to him, and those who struggled, struggled alongside him." People entered Islam at his call and waged the struggle (*jihad*) in his company.

`Wa qamu bi-l-fara'id allati iftaradaha Allahu 'alayhim, wa hiya al-da'a'im al-sab'` — "And they fulfilled the obligations that Allah made incumbent upon them, and these are the seven pillars (*al-da'a'im al-sab'*)." The community established the ordained duties laid upon them by God, the seven supports of the faith. `Wa al-sunan allati sannaha` — "and the practices (*sunan*) which he instituted." They upheld the practices the Prophet had established for them; the pronoun in *sannaha* refers back to the *sunan* themselves, the practices that the Prophet ordained. `Wa sadaqu fi mawalatihi wa mawalat ibn 'ammihi zahiran` — "And they were true in their love and loyalty to him, and in the loyalty to his cousin — outwardly." They proved sincere in their allegiance (*mawalat*) to the Prophet, and in their allegiance to his cousin, Ali — but, the text is careful to specify, *zahiran*, outwardly, in the exoteric sense. `Wa aslamu ta'atan lahu` — "And they submitted in obedience to him" — they surrendered themselves in obedience and performed righteous deeds.

A new phase begins as the inner reality of these believers matures. `Wa lamma tajawharat nufusuhum wa batanat 'anhu suwaruhum wa ashraqat dhawatuhum` — "And when their souls became jewel-like, and their forms became hidden, and their essences shone forth." The believers' souls were refined until they became like jewels (*tajawharat*); their outward forms receded into concealment; and their inner essences blazed with light. `Wa intaqala minhum man intaqala 'ala dhalika, awjaba Allahu ta'ala an yakuna ma'aduhum ila haykalin nurani wa maqamin sharifin li-llah` — "And those among them who passed away, passed away in that state, and Allah the Exalted made it necessary that their return be to a luminous form and a noble station with Allah." By God's justice and decree, those believers who died in this refined, jewel-like condition were assigned a return (*ma'ad*) to a luminous spiritual form and a noble standing in the presence of God.

The text then identifies that luminous form. `Fa-kana ha'ula'i al-muntaqiluna ila hadha al-haykal Fatimah 'alayha al-salam` — "So these who passed over to this form were gathered into Fatimah, peace be upon her." Those who departed with outward (*zahir*) allegiance to Ali, from within the believing community, returned and were incorporated into the luminous form of Fatimah, peace be upon her. `'Ala ma sabaqa bihi al-qawl min al-hayakil al-nuraniyyah wa al-nawamis al-tabi'iyyah` — "according to what the discourse has already established concerning the luminous forms and the natural laws." Their incorporation (*inzimam*) was into Fatimah's form. The qualification *mawalat zahirah*, outward allegiance, is deliberate and precise: these were the people of the early period of Islam who did not deny the loyalty to Ali — they affirmed it outwardly — and it is to Fatimah's luminous form that they returned. `Wa huwa khulasat al-jawhar al-islami` — "And she is the essence of the Islamic jewel (*khulasat al-jawhar al-islami*)." Fatimah is the very distillation of the jewel of Islam, the gathering-place of the purified essences of the believers.

The esoteric mission had not yet begun to operate openly at this stage. The incorporation of the believers, drawn from the meeting of the universe (*mulaqat-e-ka'inat*), was on the side of Amir al-Mu'minin. As for the famous tradition that the Prophet opened a thousand doors of knowledge for Ali, and that from each door Ali opened a thousand further doors — this belongs to the matter of teaching. It pertains to the instruction of the spiritual rank, the matter of the veil: the rank of the Prophet is high and the rank of Ali, in this respect, lower, and the Prophet taught Ali, opening for him the doors of knowledge. The thousand doors are a figure of the teaching that passed from the higher to the lower rank.

### The Wedding of Iman and Islam, and the Bringing-Forth of Hasan and Husayn

When the gathering of the luminous forms reached completion, the union that crowns the system was accomplished. `Wa lamma kamulat zawwajaha abuha min Amir al-Mu'minin salawatu Allahi 'alayh, bi-amri Allahi ta'ala wa wahyihi` — "And when they were complete, her father married her to Amir al-Mu'minin, peace be upon him, by the command of Allah the Exalted and by His revelation." The grammar carries the meaning: Fatimah is the object (the one married off), and her father — the Prophet — is the subject who gives her in marriage; he married Fatimah to Amir al-Mu'minin, and he did so not by his own counsel but by God's command and revelation. The luminous spiritual forms (*hayakil-e-nuraniyya*) — the essence of the Islamic jewel — had accumulated over thirteen years, and when their gathering was complete and Fatimah's form was full, their release (*intilaq*) occurred in this marriage.

`Wa lamma kamulat fa-tammat al-tamam wa-staqarra al-nizam wa-zdawaja al-iman wa al-islam` — "And when they were complete, the completion was fulfilled, and the order was established, and faith and submission were wedded." With the completion of the luminous forms, the totality was made whole; the system stood firm; and the marriage of *iman* and *islam* was consummated. The forms of faith (*iman*) gathered with Ali, and the forms of submission (*islam*) gathered with Fatimah, so that the wedding of Ali and Fatimah is at the same time the wedding of faith and submission. `Wa jarat al-da'wat al-zahirah 'ala haliha wa al-da'wat al-batinah fi dimniha` — "And the exoteric mission ran on in its state, and the esoteric mission was within it." The outward mission continued as before, while the inward mission was now contained and carried within it.

`Fa-stamarra amruhuma ila an istakhraja min al-da'wat al-zahirah al-Hasan 'alayhi al-salam, wa min al-da'wat al-batinah al-Husayn 'alayhi al-salam` — "So their affair continued until he brought forth from the exoteric mission Hasan, peace be upon him, and from the esoteric mission Husayn, peace be upon him." The two missions ran on together until from the outward mission Hasan was brought forth and from the inward mission Husayn. `Wa kanat al-da'wat al-zahirah qist al-natiq wa al-da'wat al-batinah qist al-wasi` — "And the exoteric mission was the portion (*qist*) of the Natiq, and the esoteric mission was the portion of the Wasi." The outward mission belonged to the Natiq as his share, and the inward mission belonged to the Wasi as his — so that Hasan, brought from the exoteric, carries the Natiq's portion, and Husayn, brought from the esoteric, carries the Wasi's.

`Ila an qawwala al-natiq khidmatahu, wa sallama ila waladihi al-Hasan rutbat al-nubuwwah wa al-risalah` — "until the Natiq completed his service and entrusted to his son Hasan the rank of Prophethood and Apostleship." Of all the magnificent ranks the Prophet held — the highest of which is the rank of veilship (*hijabiyyah*) — he entrusted to his son Hasan the rank of *nubuwwah* and *risalah*, for that was the Prophet's own proper portion. `Wa ila waladihi al-Husayn rutbat al-wisayah wa al-imamah` — "and to his son Husayn the rank of Guardianship and Imamate." The Prophet was also, for the sake of his guardianship (*kafalat*), the holder of *imamah* and *wisayah*, and this rank he entrusted to his son Husayn. `Wa ja'ala al-mustawda'ata lahu 'inda Amir al-Mu'minin` — "And he placed what was entrusted to him with Amir al-Mu'minin." That is, the rank of Prophethood and Apostleship destined for Hasan was placed in trust (*mustawda'*) with Amir al-Mu'minin — who was himself the master of the affair of *wisayah* and *imamah* — to be held until Hasan should come of age. For at the time of the Prophet's passing, Hasan and Husayn were but children, seven or eight years old. The Prophet passed away in the eleventh year of the Hijra; Hasan, born in the second year, was then about nine, and Husayn, somewhat younger, about seven or eight — their ages were seven and eight. Because they were still passing through the stages of training, the Prophet entrusted all these ranks to Amir al-Mu'minin to hold in trust for them. This recalls the very pattern of the Prophet's own beginning, of which the teacher has spoken: that the Prophet had four degrees gathered with him, *nubuwwah* and *risalah* being his own portion, while *imamah* and *wisayah* were placed in trust with Abu Talib on behalf of Ali. The same structure of held-in-trust ranks repeats itself in the next generation.

`Wa qama Amir al-Mu'minin maqamahu bi-l-rutab al-arba'` — "And Amir al-Mu'minin stood in his station with the four ranks." After the Prophet's transfer, Amir al-Mu'minin took up his station bearing all four ranks. The ranks were not conferred all at once; rather, the Prophet in his own lifetime had assigned them to Hasan and Husayn, to be realized when they completed the stages of their training, and the trust passed through Amir al-Mu'minin, who then handed it on to them in due course. The structure of these ranks is graded: just as in the human being there are ten degrees, so in the *imamah* there are ten degrees. The first is responsiveness (*istijabat*); after it comes faith (*iman*); and so through all the stages, every degree was established with Amir al-Mu'minin. Thus *imamah* and *wisayah* were his own portion, and as guardian he established for his sons the ranks of *nubuwwah* and *risalah* that had been placed in trust with him.

`Fa-kana dhalika qist al-Hasan. Wa intaqala al-natiq, wa qama Amir al-Mu'minin maqamahu bi-l-rutab al-arba'` — "So that was Hasan's portion. And the Natiq passed away, and Amir al-Mu'minin stood in his station with the four ranks." After the Prophet's passing, Amir al-Mu'minin stood in his place bearing the four ranks. `Wa sallama ila waladihi al-Hasan rutbat al-nubuwwah wa al-risalah, wa ila waladihi al-Husayn rutbat al-wisayah wa al-imamah, wa intaqala ila dari karamati Allahi ta'ala` — "and he entrusted to his son Hasan the rank of Prophethood and Apostleship, and to his son Husayn the rank of Guardianship and Imamate, and he transferred to the abode of the generosity of Allah the Exalted." Amir al-Mu'minin, having held and ordered the ranks, passed them down — Prophethood and Apostleship to Hasan, Guardianship and Imamate to Husayn — and then departed to the abode of God's honour.

`Wa qama al-Hasan 'alayhi al-salam bi-ma wajaba lahu bi-l-qiyam, ila an intaqala ba'da an sallama ila akhihi al-Husayn 'alayhi al-salam rutbat al-nubuwwah wa al-risalah` — "And Hasan, peace be upon him, rose to fulfil what was made incumbent upon him, until he passed away, after entrusting to his brother Husayn, peace be upon him, the rank of Prophethood and Apostleship." Hasan, too, received some measure of *imamah* and *wisayah*, as the du'at have stated — certain ranks of these were attained by him — yet the decisive distinction lies in the state of uprising (*qiyam*). Hasan did not attain the state of uprising. The Prophet had said, "Al-Hasan and al-Husayn are two Imams, whether they rise (*qama*) or sit (*qa'ada*)," giving precedence to rising over sitting. There is an Imam held in trust (*mustawda'*) and an Imam who is permanent (*mustaqarr*). In the sequence of succession Hasan is first, but it is Husayn in whom the office becomes permanent.

`Fa-sarat al-arba' 'inda al-Husayn 'alayhi al-salam ghayra kharijatin minh` — "So the four ranks came to be with Husayn, peace be upon him, not departing from him." The four ranks became permanent (*mustaqarr*) in Husayn and did not pass out from him to his own progeny in the manner of an ongoing transfer. As is explained in the Da'wat-e-Ashir — the Tenth Mission — when both Hasan and Husayn came forward, Hasan held precedence in priority (*sabaqiyat*), being the elder and first in sequence; but in sharpness of vision (*hiddat-e-nazar*) Husayn was the superior. It is for this reason that the four ranks settled and became fixed in Husayn. `Hatta tusallima jami'a dhalika Qa'im al-Qiyamah 'alayhi al-salam` — "until the Qa'im al-Qiyamah, peace be upon him, receives all of that." The verb is precise: *sallama* means "to hand over," while *tasallama* means "to receive" — so the sense is that all these ranks are held and carried forward until they are at last received by the Qa'im al-Qiyamah, the Resurrector of the Resurrection.

### The Luminous Haykal of the Qa'im and the Gathering of the Imams

`Wa yasiru jami'u al-maqamat al-ka'inah fi ufuq al-'ashir haykalan nuraniyyan` — "And all the stations that exist in the horizon of the Tenth become a luminous form." Every station gathered in the Tenth Horizon (*ufuq al-'ashir*) comes together into a single luminous spiritual form (*haykal nurani*). `Wa nafsuhu ruhun li-dhalika al-haykal, kama sabaqa bi-l-qawl anifan` — "And his soul is the spirit for that form, as was stated before in the discourse." The soul of the Qa'im al-Qiyamah is the spirit (*ruh*) of that luminous form. `Wa yakunu Amir al-Mu'minin salawatu Allahi 'alayh qalban li-hadha al-haykal al-nurani` — "And Amir al-Mu'minin, peace be upon him, is the heart (*qalb*) for this luminous form." `Wa natiq al-dawr alladhi huwa Muhammad salawatu Allahi 'alayh ra'suhu` — "And the Natiq of the era, who is Muhammad, peace be upon him, is its head (*ra's*)." So in the luminous form of the Qa'im al-Qiyamah, the soul of the Qa'im is its spirit, Amir al-Mu'minin Ali is its heart, and the Natiq Muhammad is its head — the three together composing the living anatomy of that single luminous body.

The gathering of the Imams into this form follows a vast temporal rhythm. The sons of the missionaries (*da'iyyah*) — the Imams who succeed one another — pass away, and their successors are their sons. When those sons pass away, they ascend to the Tenth Horizon, to the horizon of their father, where each has his place; one session out of every hundred sessions is reserved for the son. As Imam follows Imam and each in turn departs, they are all gathered into the horizon of their father. Across the span of a thousand years, the seventh Imam appears, who is called the Qa'im; every seven thousand years one such Qa'im is named. When that Qa'im appears and ascends to the horizon of his father, he attains the *imamah* of the Imams of seven thousand years. Then, as the cycle repeats, seven such sessions are established across seven seven-thousand-year spans; and when forty-nine thousand years are completed, the seventh of these gathers his own era, gathers the Imams of seven thousand years, and gathers the Imams of forty-nine thousand years. This is the method by which the whole succession of Imams is at last drawn up into the horizon of the Tenth Intellect, the soul of the Qa'im al-Qiyamah serving as the spirit of the luminous form into which they are gathered — and he takes up the matters proper to his own era, the things of his time accruing to him.

It is to this reality that Sayyidna Hamiduddin al-Kirmani pointed in his Rahat al-'Aql. `Wa li-dhalika qala Sayyiduna Hamid al-Din fi Rahat al-'Aql: yushriquhum shamsu dar al-ibda' wa qamaru al-inbi'ath, mushiran bi-shams al-ibda' ila al-natiq` — "And for this reason Sayyidna Hamiduddin said in Rahat al-'Aql: 'the sun of the Abode of Origination (*dar al-ibda'*) and the moon of Emission (*inbi'ath*) shine upon them' — pointing by the sun of Origination to the Natiq." The sun of the Abode of Origination and the moon of Emission shine upon the gathered Imams; and Sayyidna Hamiduddin intends by the sun of *dar al-ibda'* the Natiq, the Prophet, and by the moon of *inbi'ath* the Wasi, Ali, peace be upon them. The two great luminaries of the luminous form are thus the Natiq as sun and the Wasi as moon.

`Thumma inna sa'ir al-a'immah min waladayhima — 'Abd al-Muttalib wa Abu Talib wa 'Abdullah wa Fatimah 'alayhim al-salam — sa'iru a'da'i dhalika al-haykal` — "Then, all the rest of the Imams from the children of those two — 'Abd al-Muttalib and Abu Talib and 'Abdullah and Fatimah, peace be upon them — are the remaining limbs of that form." All the Imams descended from the line — the children associated with 'Abd al-Muttalib, Abu Talib, 'Abdullah, and Fatimah — become the limbs (*a'da'*) of that luminous form, the body of the Qa'im. They are the members that complete the form whose head is Muhammad, whose heart is Ali, and whose spirit is the Qa'im.

Yet three among them retain their distinct station and do not enter the luminous form as limbs. `Wa lakinna 'Abd al-Muttalib wa-bnayhi Aba Talib wa 'Abdullah baqu fi dimni al-'aql al-'ashir, wa lam yasiru fi ayyin min al-haykalayn alladhayn huma Muhammad salawatu Allahi 'alayh wa 'Ali 'alayhi al-salam, li-yakunu sufara'a bayna al-'aql al-'ashir wa bayna Muhammad salawatu Allahi 'alayh wa 'Ali 'alayhi al-salam kama sabaqa bihi al-qawl` — "But 'Abd al-Muttalib and his two sons, Abu Talib and 'Abdullah, remained within the fold of the Tenth Intellect and did not pass into either of the two forms, which are Muhammad, peace be upon him, and Ali, peace be upon him, so that they might be emissaries between the Tenth Intellect and Muhammad and Ali, peace be upon them, as the discourse has already established." 'Abd al-Muttalib, Abu Talib, and 'Abdullah did not enter into the form of the Prophet or of Ali; they remained on the horizon of the Tenth Intellect to serve as the ambassadors between it and the two. In the end, they will enter the *haykal* of the Qa'im, peace be upon him — they will go into the luminous form of the Qa'im — while throughout the cycle of Muhammad and Ali they held only their intermediary office between the Intellect and those two.

### Abu Talib at Badr: the Master of Hayzum

The discourse closes with a striking vision of these stations made manifest on the field of battle. `Wa kanu fi jumlat al-mala'ikah al-mutajallina fi yawm Badr wa ghayrih fi nusrat al-natiq salawatu Allahi 'alayh` — "And they were among the company of the angels who appeared on the day of Badr and on other occasions in support of the Natiq, peace be upon him." `Wa al-mala'ikatu hum al-maqamat alladhina kanu 'inda Amir al-Mu'minin 'alayhi al-salam, fa-tashakhkhasu jami'an fi dhalika al-yawm` — "And the angels are the stations that were with Amir al-Mu'minin, peace be upon him, who all became personified on that day." The angels who descended at Badr were none other than the stations that stood with Amir al-Mu'minin; on the day of battle they took on personified, individual forms. `Wa kana Abu Talib sahiba Hayzum al-amira lahu bi-l-qudum` — "And Abu Talib was the master of Hayzum, commanding him to advance." Hayzum is the name of the horse of Jibra'il. The master of Hayzum, the chief among the angelic host who led the horse forward, was Abu Talib; and it was he who commanded Amir al-Mu'minin to advance and to strike in battle. Among all the host of angels present at Badr, it was Abu Talib who, as the master of Hayzum, urged Ali onward, bidding him "advance and attack" — Abu Talib, ranked among the angels, the chief of that host, leading the steed and commanding the charge on behalf of Amir al-Mu'minin.

With this the chapter reaches its close, and the wisdom of the matter is sealed with the Fatiha and with the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.

## Muhammad ibn Isma'il and the Cycle of Concealment

### The Completers of the Cycle and the Seventh Natiq

When Maulana al-Husayn departed and went his way, the rest of the A'immah continued after him, those whom this teaching names the *mutimmin* — the completers. The word carries its meaning openly within it, for *mutimm* is derived from *tamma*, to bring to completion, and the completers are precisely those who bring the cycle to its end. As in the order of creation the first week possesses a special distinction among all the days, gathering and sealing what came before it, so among the Imams there is a distinguished line whose task is to bring the whole *dawr* — the great cosmic cycle — to its consummation. From Maulana 'Ali Zayn al-'Abidin onward, the chain of the Imamate ran through these completers until their command reached its term in Maulana Muhammad ibn Isma'il, the seventh in the count.

He is the one designated in the Arabic of the text as `al-muntahiyatu ilayhi ghāyat al-sharā’i‘` — the one to whom the ultimate goal of the religious laws culminates. The *shari'ah* that had been brought, with all the rulings carried within it, reaches in his person its final end; in his time those rulings reached the entire world, conveyed through the *du'at* across every region, and were brought to their fullness. He is `al-makhtūm` — the one upon whom the seal is set; `al-mushtamil` — the one who encompasses the ranks and their states; and `al-muḥīṭ bi-‘ulūmi al-bāṭin` — the one who encompasses the sciences of the inner reality. The completers do for the cycle of the Imamate what the *khatim al-rusul*, the seal of the messengers, does for the line of prophets: they gather and conclude, and their rank stands very high precisely because they bring an entire order to its term. This is why the rank of the *mutimmin* is exalted above the rest — they are the ones in whom the whole accumulated work of a cycle is summed and sealed.

The reckoning by which Muhammad ibn Isma'il stands as the seventh Natiq follows a fixed law of counting that the teaching lays out with great care. The Natiq who opens a cycle, together with his *wasi* (his appointed executor), is set aside at the head; after the two of them come six completers; and the seventh of these completers is himself a Natiq who opens the next cycle. The point is exact and bears repeating in its discipline: it is not that there were only seven Imams in any given era — between any two Natiqs many Imams and prophets arose — but that within that multitude, seven occupy the distinguished, major stations. Adam and his *wasi* are set aside; after them come six, and the seventh of these is the Natiq Nuh, with whom the *qiyam* of a new cycle takes place. The remaining Imams of that span are minor in this reckoning; the major ones, the load-bearing stations, are these seven. The six who stand between the opening Natiq and the next gather the entire cycle and deliver its total to the seventh, exactly as the days of a week gather and are summed on the day of completion.

The text walks this counting through the whole prophetic sequence so that no link is left in doubt. `wa-idhā ‘addadta Ādam wa-waṣiyyahu wa-mutimmī dawr al-satr sittah kāna sābi‘uhum nāṭiqan wa-huwa Nūḥ ‘alā dhikrihi al-salām` — and when you count Adam and his *wasi* and the six completers of the cycle of concealment, their seventh Natiq is Nuh, peace be upon his mention. `wa-idhā ‘addadta Nūḥ wa-waṣiyyahu wa-mutimmī dawr al-satr sittah kāna sābi‘uhum nāṭiqan wa-huwa Ibrāhīm ‘alayhi al-salām` — and when you count Nuh and his *wasi* and the six completers, their seventh Natiq is Ibrahim, peace be upon him. `wa-idhā ‘addadta Ibrāhīm wa-waṣiyyahu wa-mutimmī dawr al-satr sittah kāna sābi‘uhum nāṭiqan wa-huwa Mūsā ‘alayhi al-salām` — and counting Ibrahim and his *wasi* and the six completers, their seventh Natiq is Musa, peace be upon him. `wa-idhā ‘addadta Mūsā wa-waṣiyyahu wa-mutimmī dawr al-satr sittah kāna sābi‘uhum nāṭiqan wa-huwa ‘Īsā ‘alayhi al-salām` — and counting Musa and his *wasi* and the six completers, their seventh Natiq is 'Isa, peace be upon him. `wa-idhā ‘addadta ‘Īsā wa-waṣiyyahu wa-mutimmī dawr al-satr sittah kāna sābi‘uhum nāṭiqan wa-huwa Muḥammad ‘alayhi al-salām` — and counting 'Isa and his *wasi* and the six completers, their seventh Natiq is Muhammad, peace be upon him. And finally, `wa-idhā ‘addadta Muḥammad ‘alayhi al-salām wa-waṣiyyahu wa-mutimmī dawr al-satr sittah kāna sābi‘uhum nāṭiqan wa-huwa Muḥammad ibn Ismā‘īl salāmun Allāh ‘alayhā wa-‘alayhim` — and when you count Muhammad, peace be upon him, and his *wasi* and the six completers, their seventh Natiq is Muhammad ibn Isma'il, the peace of Allah be upon them all. Thus the law of seven runs unbroken from Adam to Muhammad ibn Isma'il: each Natiq, taken with his *wasi* and his six completers, yields the next Natiq at the seventh place, and the sequence closes upon the seventh Natiq of the prophetic line itself.

### The Two Unveilings: Qa'im bil-Quwwah and Qa'im bil-Fi'l

Within this station Muhammad ibn Isma'il bears a name that places him at the threshold of the great consummation: he is `al-qā’im bi-l-quwwah` — the Qa'im in potency, the potential Qa'im — and he is `ṣāḥib al-kashfat al-ūlā`, the master of the first unveiling. These two titles together describe what is distinctive in his rank. To be the Qa'im *bil-quwwah* is to hold the office of the Resurrector in potentiality rather than in its final act. The rulings of the *shari'ah* that reached the whole world through the *du'at* and were manifested there — these he completed; and the completion of an entire cycle of law is itself the potential form of the resurrection. The Qa'im *bil-fi'l*, by contrast — the kinetic Qa'im, the Resurrector in act — is the one who will appear at the end of the *dawr*, and he is the master of `kashf-i ukhrā`, the later unveiling. The text holds something very deep in this distinction. Muhammad ibn Isma'il opened the inner reality: from him there came an unveiling, a *kashf-i batin*, an opening of what had been hidden in the inward dimension of the religion, disclosed for a specific class among the believers who were ready to receive it. The Qa'im who is yet to come will bring the second and later unveiling, completing in act what the first unveiling opened in potency.

The line of salutation set over the Qa'im in the text marks him out: `Salāmun ‘alā dhikrihi`, peace be upon his mention. The peace of Allah is invoked upon his mention because what he carries is the great resurrection itself; and the Qa'im *bil-fi'l* is also the master of `al-batshat al-kubrā`, the great strike, the one of whom the Holy Qur'an speaks, who will strike with overwhelming force. So the two unveilings divide the office of the Qa'im across the cycle's two ends: the first unveiling opens the inner reality at the close of the prophetic line, and the later unveiling, the great strike, consummates it at the close of the whole *dawr*.

### The Many Resurrections and the Great Resurrection

The reason the salutation `Salāmun ‘alā dhikrihi` is fitting is given immediately: `li-ann al-qiyāmāt kathīratun` — because the resurrections are many. The *qiyamat* is not a single event but a recurring reality, occurring at many scales, and the teaching defines it with precision before tracing its instances. A *qiyamat* is an *ijtima'* — a gathering; more exactly it is an `ijtimā‘-i ṣuwar`, a gathering of forms, where *hashr* and *nashr*, the assembling and the raising up, take place. The word *qiyam* itself derives from `qāma yaqūmu qiyāman`, to stand, to rise; and the standing in question is the standing-up that follows upon a gathering. Wherever forms are gathered and raised, there a *qiyamat* occurs — a small one or a great one, according to the scale of the gathering.

The text enumerates the ranks of the hierarchy and shows how each is itself a *qiyamat* for those connected to it. `wa-awwaluhā al-ma’dhūn al-makfūf` — the first of them is the restrained *ma'dhun*, the one who has been given permission but held back, whose authorization extends only to specific occasions, specific persons, and specific places. The *ma'dhun* is one to whom *idhn*, permission, has been granted for the propagation of the religion; but there is not one kind of *ma'dhun* only — there are many, and the first of the series is this restrained one. After him comes `al-ma’dhūn al-muṭlaq`, the absolute *ma'dhun*, given permission for the general body of the faithful. Then `al-dā‘ī al-muḥarram`, the restricted *da'i*, whose mission is confined to a particular place — and there may be several such *da'is*. Then `al-dā‘ī al-muṭlaq`, the absolute *da'i*, given permission for both the restricted and the general scope. Then `al-dā‘ī al-balāgh`, the *da'i* of conveyance. Then `al-ḥujjat`, the proof. And after these, `al-abwāb`, the gates. Of this whole sequence the text says: `thumma innamā kāna ẓuhūr hādhihi al-ḥudūd qiyāmatun` — then indeed the appearance of these ranks is a resurrection. Each of these *hudud* is itself a *qiyamat*, `li-qiyām kulli wāḥidin minhum bi-mā yataṣallu bihi min al-ṣuwar al-mujarradat al-mufāriqat li-l-ajsām` — by the standing of each one of them with what is joined to him from the abstract forms separated from the bodies. That is to say, when those of a given rank, together with all who stand below them, are gathered and brought to their *hadd* — their proper limit in the hierarchy — a resurrection takes place for them. The one whom they reach gathers their forms to himself, and that gathering is their *qiyamat*.

This is why the *qiyamat* mentioned in the Holy Qur'an, understood at its largest scale, is a resurrection of time and space — a `qiyāmat-i kullī`, a universal resurrection — encompassing the rising of the oppressors and their destruction. But the inner reality of the resurrection is the gathering of forms: not *suwar* in the sense of verses, but *ṣuwar*, forms — the gathering of the *nufus* and the *suwarat*, the souls and the spiritual forms. As the scripture says, "when the one who is raised is raised," the meaning is the gathering of the forms. Wherever they gather, there is the *qiyamat*; a small gathering is a small resurrection, a great gathering a great one. The teaching here connects this directly to what was said earlier of the believer's death: when any believer of good end passes away, his `sūrat nūrāniyyah`, his luminous form, draws his particular self up with it, and the two together reach their `ḥadd-i ‘ālī`, their lofty rank. That ascent and gathering at the lofty rank is itself a *qiyamat* — `qāma qiyāmatun` — a standing-up of the forms with the one who gathers them.

These many minor resurrections are then followed by the resurrection that stands apart from them all: `wa-yatlūhā hādhihi al-qiyāmāt qā’im al-qiyāmat al-kubrā` — and following all these resurrections is the Qa'im of the great resurrection, the *qiyamat al-kubra*, in which the whole world will gather. He is `al-maqām al-ladhī` in which the Imam, upon him be peace, stands; `al-ṣā’irat ilayhi ‘indahu jamī‘ al-ṣuwar` — the one to whom all the forms come, with whom all the forms are. The text adds that it is for the appearance of these ranks that the messengers were appointed: `al-qā’ilat inna hā’ulā’i wa-li-ḍuhūrihim nusibat` — those for whom the *hudud* and the *rusul* were set in place; and at every turn all of them are related back to the Imam, upon him be peace. So the structure of the cosmos resolves toward a single point: the many *hudud* and the many messengers exist for the sake of the gatherings they make possible, and all those gatherings flow, in the end, into the one great gathering at the Qa'im of the great resurrection.

### What Gathers at the Great Resurrection

At the *qiyamat al-kubra* the gathering is total. The Qa'im who follows upon all the lesser resurrections is `al-mushtamil ‘inda jamī‘ al-maqāmāt al-sharīfah` — the one with whom all the noble stations gather. With the Imam the believers gather; the *hudud* gather; and with the Qa'im of the resurrection the Imams themselves gather. The Qa'im of the great resurrection is also, as we have seen, the master of `al-batshat al-kubrā`, the great strike of which the Qur'an speaks, the one who will strike with overwhelming force.

The span of this great gathering is laid out in years. With the Qa'im gather the Imams `min awwal al-thalāthat alāf sanah` — from the first three thousand years, the years of the *dawr al-fitrah*, the cycle of the interval; this is the period that runs to the end of the *dawr al-kashf*, the cycle of unveiling. To these are added the seven thousand years of the *dawr al-satr*, the cycle of concealment — `al-muqannā ‘anhu bi-l-dunyā`, the cycle veiled by the world, so called because in it the truth is hidden behind the world's appearances. Three thousand years of the *dawr al-fitrah* and seven thousand years of the *dawr al-satr* together make ten thousand years, and the one who brings the whole of this span to completion is the Qa'im. All the Imams of these ten thousand years gather in his presence; his *haykal*, his luminous temple, is the gathering-place of everything.

To this the teaching adds the testimony of Maulana Amir al-Mu'minin, peace be upon him, who alluded to exactly this in his saying. `wa-min hīni ramaza mawlānā amīr al-mu’minīn ṣalawāt Allāh ‘alayhi bi-qawlihi: ‘alā ra’s kulli ‘asharat ālāf sanah yaqūmu min al-ṣuwarat` — and Maulana Amir al-Mu'minin, the blessings of Allah be upon him, gestured to this in his saying: at the head of every ten thousand years a form from among the forms stands up. This is a gesture, an indication, toward the Qa'im — `ilā al-qā’im salāmun Allāh ‘alā dhikrihi`, toward the Qa'im, the peace of Allah be upon his mention. At the head of every ten thousand years a form stands up: that is, the three thousand years of the *dawr al-fitrah* and the seven thousand years of the *dawr al-satr* are summed, and at their head the Qa'im rises and creates the *hashr*, the great gathering, in his place after the passing of the present holder of the station from this abode. And of Muhammad ibn Isma'il the text says that he will be the `khalīfah` of the tenth — the successor in his own place, after his passing from this world, of the tenth station.

### The Rank of Divine Oneness That Lies Beyond the Chain

Here the teaching turns to the most subtle point in the station of Muhammad ibn Isma'il, distinguishing carefully between the name he keeps and the rank he in fact occupies. The name `al-nāṭiq al-sābi‘`, the seventh Natiq, remains attached to him — `wa-innamā baqā ‘alayhi ism al-nāṭiq al-sābi‘ li-luṭfihi bi-amr Allāh` — and this name remains upon him because of his subtlety in the affair of God, the affair of God that he conveys when the cycle of the world has run its term. Yet the name is not the whole of his reality. The text continues with his own saying: `wa-qawluhu anā li-annahu ghayr muntazimin fī silk dawr al-satr` — and his saying "I," because he is not arranged in the chain of the cycle of concealment. He is not strung upon the *silk*, the thread, of the *dawr al-satr* in the way the other completers are; he is rather the one who brings that chain to its end.

The distinction is decisive. `wa-laysa huwa bi-mutimm wa-lā rutbah bi-ḥudūdihi wa-lā huwa bi-rasūlihi, bal huwa munfaridun bi-rutbat al-waḥdat al-ilāhiyyah` — and he is not a completer, nor is there a rank among his ranks of the ordinary kind, nor is he a messenger; rather he is unique in the rank of divine oneness. This is the heart of the matter: Muhammad ibn Isma'il has the name of the seventh Natiq, but he has surpassed that very rank to a higher one. The seventh-Natiq station is a rank he attained and then passed beyond, reaching the rank of the *silk* itself — `rutbat al-waḥdat al-ilāhiyyah`, the rank of divine oneness, which is the rank of the Qa'im, upon him be peace, whose station he manifests. The one who stands at the head of the chain is the Imam, the Qa'im, and in his temple gathers everything; Muhammad ibn Isma'il, standing at the chain's terminating point rather than as a link within it, is `munfaridun` — set apart, unique — in this rank of divine oneness.

The text then guards against any confusion of this with the ordinary sense in which the title "seventh Natiq" might be assigned to him. `wa-innamā Mawlānā Muḥammad ibn Ismā‘īl ‘alayhi al-salātu wa-l-salām makhṣūṣun bi-dhālika li-intizāmihi fī silk al-maqāmāt dawr al-satr ṣāḥib al-intiqāl` — and indeed Maulana Muhammad ibn Isma'il, the blessings and peace be upon him, is specific to that rank of the seventh Natiq by virtue of his being arranged in the chain of the stations of the cycle of concealment and its transition. He bears the name in respect of his place in the sequence of the *dawr al-satr*; but in his innermost reality he is the terminator of that sequence and stands in the unique rank of divine oneness beyond it. Name and rank are thus distinguished without contradiction: the name "seventh Natiq" follows from his position in the ordered chain, while his true rank lifts him out of the chain altogether to the singular station that no link in the chain occupies.

### The Three Concealed Completers and Their Gates

The completers of Muhammad ibn Isma'il's own cycle — the *mutimmu dawr al-satr* of his *dawr* — are three. `wa-mutimmū dawr al-satr thalāthah alladhīna al-mastūrīn` — and the completers of the cycle of concealment are three, who are the concealed ones, the *a'immah mastūrin*. They are Maulana 'Abdullah, Maulana Ahmad, and Maulana Husayn: the three concealed Imams who came after Muhammad ibn Isma'il. With each of them there was a *bab*, a gate, so that there are three *abwab* answering to the three Imams. `amarakum imāmikum bi-abwāb al-dunyā alladhīna ‘āhadahum` — your Imam commanded you concerning the gates of the world, those with whom he made covenant. Each Imam among them commanded his own *bab*; and each of these three gates was named as Imam for a particular reason of concealment.

The reason is the discipline of *taqiyyah*, precautionary concealment. The three *abwab* were commanded to conceal the Imam, to keep him in *taqiyyah*, to hold him hidden — and to present themselves outwardly as the Imam and so to manifest in his place. This was not merely a matter of standing in for the hidden Imam; it carried forward toward a definite end. The pattern of the three gates presenting themselves as Imam is completed by a fourth who will manifest: Imam 'Ali, upon him be the blessings and peace, who will be the Natiq, so that with him the count of seven is complete. Three concealed Imams with their three gates, and then the fourth who manifests as Natiq — these together close the reckoning of seven in the cycle of concealment, in just the disciplined manner of counting by which the seventh Natiq is always reached: the Natiq and his *wasi* set aside, the completers gathering the cycle, and the seventh standing forth to open and seal the new turn.

### The Two Kinds of the People of Truth

The teaching now opens a new section, drawn from the third chapter and its third question, treating the path of discourse toward the realities of the faith. The opening sentence sets out the whole scope of what follows: `Raqat sabīl al-qawl wa-l-kalām ilā ḥaqīqat al-tawḥīd wa-tanzīh wa-tajrīd wa-ilā ibtidā’ al-wujūd wa-tasalsul jamī‘ al-mawjūdāt al-jismāniyyāt ilā dār al-kawn wa-l-fasād wa-ma‘ād Allāh ma‘ād al-awliyā’ al-ṣāliḥīn` — the path of speech and discourse rises toward the reality of *tawhid* (divine unity), and *tanzih* (transcendence), and *tajrid* (abstraction); toward the beginning of existence; toward the chain of all bodily existing things down to the abode of being and corruption; and toward the return to Allah, the return of the righteous saints. The discourse, in other words, will trace the whole arc from the origin of existence through the realm of generation and decay to the final return.

It begins with a definition that governs everything afterward: `inna ahl al-ḥaqq ‘alā qismayn` — that the people of truth, the Ahl al-Haqq, are of two kinds. The first kind, `ahl al-ma‘rifat al-ḥaqīqah wa-l-‘ulūm al-ilāhiyyah`, are the people of true gnosis and the divine sciences — those who know the realities and the divine sciences, who joined to that knowledge `al-a‘māl al-ṣāliḥa`, righteous deeds, and `al-muwālāt li-jamī‘ al-a’immah`, loyalty and love toward all the Imams, from the Natiq and the *wasi* down to the Imam of the age and all the *hudud*. They studied the knowledge and they also performed the action, and they fulfilled the right of love that was owed to all the Imams. The second kind, as will appear, are those of pure devotion and righteous deeds without that true gnosis. Both kinds, it must be stressed, perform righteous deeds and hold loyalty to all the Imams; they differ not in devotion or in good works but in whether true knowledge accompanies these. So the People of Truth are not divided into the obedient and the disobedient, but into two grades of the obedient: those whose obedience is illumined by gnosis and the divine sciences, and those whose obedience is genuine and loving but unaccompanied by that inner knowledge.

### Separation from the Dense Body and the Luminous Temple

The teaching now follows the first kind — the people of gnosis — through what befalls them at death. `Fa-hā’ulā’i hum al-a’immah alladhīna yakūnūna ‘inda mufāraqatihim li-kasā’ifihim al-a‘ḍā’ al-ra’īsah min dhālika al-haykal al-nūrānī alladhī huwa haykal al-Imām ‘alayhi afḍal al-salām` — so these are the ones who, upon their separation from their dense bodies, become the chief members of that luminous temple which is the temple of the Imam, upon him be the most excellent peace. The *kasa'if* are the dense, gross bodies; to separate from one's *kasa'if* is to leave the body behind at death. When these possessors of gnosis and good works leave their bodies, they become the `a‘ḍā’ ra’īsah`, the chief members, the principal limbs, of the *haykal nurani*, the luminous temple of the Imam.

The mechanism of this ascent is described in terms of *surat* and *nafs*. What rises to the temple is the spiritual forms that become part of the *haykal* — the *surat* together with the *nufus*. The *surat*, the luminous form, draws the *nafs* with it; specifically it draws the `nafs ḥissiyyah`, the sensory soul. The two, becoming one, reach the *haykal* of the Imam. This is the same movement described earlier of the believer of good end whose luminous form draws his particular self upward to the lofty rank; here it is set out for the Imams of gnosis, who become the principal limbs of the Imam's own luminous temple.

The degree of light a soul attains within the temple is not uniform but graded according to its attainment. `wa-‘alā qadri mablaghi kulli wāḥidin minhum fī al-‘ilmi wa-l-ma‘rifati wa-l-wilāyati yakūnu ‘uluwwuhu fī dhālika al-haykal al-nūrānī` — and according to the measure of the reach of each one of them in knowledge, gnosis, and devotion, so is the level of his loftiness in that luminous temple. The level of luminosity in the temple corresponds exactly to the knowledge (*'ilm*), the gnosis (*ma'rifat*), and the devotion (*wilayat*) that the soul possessed. The teaching is careful to note that the order in which these three are named — knowledge, gnosis, devotion — is not a ranking of priority among them; it is the thing itself that matters, the actual measure each soul has of all three together. The one who has knowledge, who performed the actions, and who held devotion stands at a much higher rank for possessing all three.

Following these come the souls of the second kind, the people of pure devotion without gnosis: `wa-yatlūhum ahl al-wilāyat al-maḥḍ wa-l-a‘māl al-ṣāliḥa fī dhālika al-haykal` — and following them are the people of pure devotion and righteous deeds in that temple. These are they who have pure love and perform righteous deeds but who do not possess true knowledge. For each of them too there is a place: `fa-yakūnu li-kulli wāḥidin minhum yastaḥiqquhu min laṭā’ifihi dūna kasā’ifihi` — so there is for each one of them, in his place, that which he deserves from his subtleties, below his dense forms. For this second kind the text speaks of *lata'if*, subtleties, rather than *kasa'if*; the *jism*, the gross body, is not mentioned for them in this connection, but the *surat* and the *nafs hissiyyah* are. They follow the people of gnosis at ranks below them, possessing the *lata'if* that lie beneath the *kasa'if* — present in the luminous order, but at a lesser station than those whose devotion was joined to knowledge.

### The Preservation and Ascent of the Dense Bodies

Having described what becomes of the soul and its luminous form, the teaching turns to the dense bodies left behind. `wa-ammā kasā’ifuhum fa-takūnu maḥfūẓatan fī a‘azzi ‘izzin wa-aḥrazi ḥirzin` — and as for their dense bodies, they are preserved in the mightiest of might and the most protected of protections. When the body is buried in the grave, it is not abandoned but kept under the highest honor and the most secure guard, and an extended natural process carries it upward by stages.

The first stage is a return to the earth. `wa-awzān dhālika annahā ta‘ūdu ilā al-ṣaḥīḥ` — and the measure of that is that it returns to the *sahih*, to the soil. Here it must be remembered that only the body is being spoken of; the *nafs hissiyyah* and the *nafs natiqah* have already departed with the luminous form. The body returns to the soil — though the teaching notes that how much of the sensory soul departs varies from one person to another, according to his state and how far he followed the *shari'ah*; sometimes the whole of the sensory soul is released, sometimes not. After the body returns to the soil and the subtleties have separated, it enters the *mizaj muntazid*, the tempered mixture. Above the soil, the four elements — fire, air, water, earth — give off their effects; vapors rise, the vapor of air and of water, and these mix together in what is called the *mizaj*, the tempered and compounded mixture. From this mixture the matter returns to the earth as rain. Then it grows ever more subtle, until at last it dissolves into vapor.

In its vaporous subtlety it begins to rise through the spheres. `wa-tas‘adu ilā falak al-burūj bi-shu‘ā‘ihā` — and it ascends to the sphere of the constellations by its rays. The cosmic order through which it rises is set out: first is the *falak al-muhit*, the encompassing sphere; below it is the *falak al-buruj*, the sphere of the constellations; and below the *falak al-buruj* lie the seven planetary spheres. These are, in descending order, the sphere of Saturn, then Jupiter, then Mars, then the sphere of the Sun, then Venus, then Mercury, then the Moon. Seven spheres lie below the *falak al-buruj*; above the sphere of the Moon are these seven planets, and above the seven planets is the *falak al-buruj*. The ascending vapor is carried upward by the rays of these seven planets, little by little, a little after a little, until it reaches the sphere of the constellations.

### Succession in the Constellations and the Descent to a New Human Form

Once the ascending matter reaches the *buruj*, it takes a place there as a successor, and a corresponding descent begins. `wa-takhallafa shay’an min burūj fī amākiniha wa-yanḥadiru dhālika al-burdu ilā al-arḍ` — and it leaves behind something from the constellations in their places, and that garment descends to the earth. The soul's ascending parts go up and take a fixed place in the *buruj*, becoming the *khalifa*, the successor, of what was there before; and the portion of the prior occupant that they displace falls, as a garment, down toward the earth. The teaching is careful about the logic of this exchange: the parts that fall are not the same parts that ascended, but the things that were there before, made ready and now displaced. The *buruj* do not remain empty; rather, as new matter rises and takes its place, the matter already present and ripe falls to the earth below. So the place each soul takes in the constellations is the place vacated by a predecessor, and the matter that falls is its predecessor's portion, released to begin the descent toward a new bodily life.

What falls becomes a fine, pulverized dust. `wa-yaṣīru turāban saḥīqan` — and it becomes a fine, ground dust; and from this the dense body, the *kasa'if* and the *jism*, is reconstituted. `thumma yaltaqī` — then it ascends and progresses by stages until at last `fī ‘ālam al-alfiyyah` — in the thousand-year realm — it becomes a human being. The text specifies what this descended one is made of: `wa-dhālika al-ṣā‘id al-kā’in ‘alā badali al-hāfil huwa qismun min al-laḥm wa-l-jild wa-l-mukhkh wa-l-‘aṣab wa-l-‘urūq wa-l-mā` — and that ascended one, who is the replacement for the one that fell, is a portion of flesh, skin, marrow, nerves, veins, and the like. The one who ascended and took a place in the *buruj* became the successor of the one who fell; and what fell, and was reconstituted below, is precisely a portion of flesh, of skin, of marrow, of nerves, and of veins.

There is a fixed timetable to this circuit. The soul's parts must remain in the *buruj* for a span, and within the larger period of a thousand years, the time comes — after some seven hundred and twenty years — for the prior matter to fall; for as matter ascends from below, matter descends from above. The bones, however, follow a process of their own. The bones are the hard parts, and the text treats them separately from flesh, skin, marrow, nerves, and veins. Because of their hardness and strength, the bones remain upon the earth for a long period. Only after that long time do they dissolve, little by little, becoming vapor, subtle and refined; then they too ascend to the *mizaj muntazid*, the tempered mixture of fire, air, water, and earth. And before they ascend to the planets, their purification has already begun in the form of rain upon the earth. From the parts of the earth this refined matter pours down, and it solidifies into precious things — into sapphire, and into *lulu*, pearls. It rains down toward the seas and the oceans, and into the caves and the favored parts of the earth, where it crystallizes as sapphire and pearl.

### The Honored Destinations of the Purified Bones

The fate of these solidified, precious remains is itself a sign of divine providence. They are divided and distributed — but only for the good people, the two kinds named earlier, both the masters of gnosis and those of the lesser rank, all of them being people of good end. `wa-yanqasimu` — and they are divided — toward the crowns of kings, into the treasures of the great, and into the hands of honored persons. Such matter does not form into ordinary soil or common stone; it forms in such places as it will be valued and honored — set in a king's crown, gathered into treasuries, held in honored hands, and worn `fī a‘nāq al-‘adhārā`, upon the necks of maidens, as a necklace.

All of this, the teaching insists, is from divine providence and its governance — `wa-min tadbīrihi` — and not from any other cause. It is providence, working as a magnet that draws each thing and sets it where it is worthy, that places every portion of that matter in its proper and fitting place. And so these precious remains always remain preserved, honored, and glorified — beloved among the creatures, beloved among all creation, whether by the learned among them or the ignorant, by the friend (*wali*) or the one who denies (*munkir*). For a span of time they abide thus, until the time of their return comes round. Then they become, in the thousand-year realm, a human persona — they return toward the soil, and ascend little by little, until they manifest as `shakhsan insaniyyan`, a human person, in the thousand-year realm.

The text then completes the formation of this new human being. `wa-takhammara minhā lahā wa-munfa‘ala bi-ṭabā’i‘ al-kawākib li-ru’yati al-anwār al-ilāhiyyah wa-naẓara minhā nafsan ka-l-awwala` — and it fermented from it, for it, and was acted upon by the natures of the planets, and by the gaze of the divine lights a soul like the first one arose. The matter is fermented and prepared; acted upon by the natures of the planets and by the gaze of divine providence, it becomes ready, and there arises from it a soul like the first. It becomes a human body, a *jism insani*, and within it the heart and the other organs are constituted from the ascended portions — the flesh, the skin, the marrow, the nerves, and the veins.

### Essences, the Descent of the Intellect, and Our Own Responsibility for the Fall

At this point the teaching reaches back to the very origin of all these forms, to explain what the soul and the body fundamentally are. The question is asked: what did Allah create? And the answer is exact: He created the *zawat*, the essences. `Allah` created the *Dhawat-e-Ruhaniyya*, the spiritual essences, the *Ashbah-e-Nuraniyya*, the luminous forms — these alone are the origin, and all other things come after them. The essence, the *zat*, is the substance of the intellect; all the *uqul*, the intellects, were created, and the form of the *uqul* is the *zawat*. The *zat* was created with the form of intellect, and that form was very high.

But a fall took place, and its cause was not Allah but the essence itself. Through *ta'khir* — delay — the intellect failed to use the powers that Allah had given it. The powers given were *'aql* (intellect), *hayat* (life), and, at the last, *ikhtiyar* (choice); the freedom that was bestowed, to go this way or that, was not used rightly. Because of this delay in using its God-given powers, the *'aql* underwent *tanazzul*, descent: it took on the rank of *nafs*, the soul. It fell, its powers grew weak, and it became *nafs*. And the responsibility for this is the essence's own. The teaching presses the point with great force: who is responsible for the intellect's becoming a soul? Every person himself. The essence itself is responsible; every person to whom Allah gave choice is responsible, because he did not fulfill his responsibility. Allah did not cause this fall — we ourselves caused it.

Nor did the descent stop at the soul. From the *nafs*, by the same delay, the matter fell further and became *jism*, body — and the bodily forms differentiated, some taking the place of the brain, some of the heart, some of the feet, some of the soles. Falling further still, the forms became *haywanat*, animals — snakes, scorpions, and the like. Falling further, they became *nabatat*, plants. And falling further yet, *ma'diniyyat*, minerals. All these — animals, plants, minerals — are progressively fallen forms of the intellect that delayed in using its powers, and as the soul's intentions changed in its fall, becoming corrupt, its forms changed to match, until the hardest and most fallen of all states was reached: the *sakhrah*, the rock, whose condition became the most rigid of any. When the question is raised whether these minerals and stones, having fallen with us, are still in that fallen state to this day, the answer is that the longer the delay a thing made, the deeper it fell, and the longer will be the span until it can rise again — so long, for the hardest, that it may not rise until the entire universe is brought to its end.

This is why, the teaching observes, we make our supplication in the *tahajjud* prayer that Allah carry us from this transient world to the eternal one — for in this world there are all these fallen things, water and fire and stone and the rest, each having taken the form its own intention shaped. The portion of any fallen essence that quickly accepts the truth, that obeys the one whose obedience it is commanded to render, is by the justice of God's power swiftly released from the physical world; but the portion that delays remains bound in matter according to the measure of its delay. This is the justice of Allah — and again the teaching insists upon it: Allah did not do this to us; we did it to ourselves.

### The Soul's Journey and the Formation of the New Heart-Soul

The teaching now retraces the whole journey with the believer's own constitution in view, drawing together the threads of *surat*, *nafs hissiyyah*, and *nafs namiyya*. In this life a human being possesses his *nafs*, which is the essence, the *zat* that Allah created and that we ourselves weakened. The intellect, fallen to the rank of soul, performs *'ilm* and *'amal* — knowledge and action — in the world; through knowledge and action it exercises the choice it was given, whether to become good and use Allah's powers or not. When the soul exercises that choice rightly and acquires what is true, a form is created within it: this is the `surat nūrānī`, the luminous form, the form born of right action. The luminous form stands above the *nafs zat*, yet it is a product of the *nafs* — created within the soul as the soul used its choice rightly. This luminous form is the perfection of existence, the perfections the soul acquired, and these perfections are greater than the soul that bore them, while their source remains the *zat*.

When such a person passes away, the luminous form does not simply detach and abandon the soul. It separates from the body, but it takes the *nafs* along with it to the extent that the *nafs* followed it in life; to that measure it draws the soul upward with itself. Both together, the luminous form and the soul it carries, reach their `ḥadd-i ‘ālī`, their lofty rank, and there they reach the presence of the Imam of the age, the holy Maulana. There a *haykal* is prepared — formed from the luminous form and from the soul; and to the extent that the *Farzand-e-Imam*, the son of the Imam, has commanded, the whole *haykal* becomes connected to the *Farzand-e-Imam*.

What then remains is the body, and its further course is the circuit through the spheres already described — but now its purpose comes clearly into view. The remaining body holds flesh, bones, veins, and fibers; these ascend from below, drawn up by the rays through the *mizaj muntazid* until they reach the Moon and, passing the seven planets, find at last a permanent place in the *buruj*. There they become the successors of those before them, and the prior matter falls. The circuits from one *burj* to the next effect a purification — for in each *burj* the matter must remain thirty years, and there are twelve *buruj*. Twelve *buruj* at thirty years each make three hundred and sixty years to traverse them once; and three hundred and sixty more to return, making seven hundred and twenty years. The further ascent from below and descent from above takes another two hundred and eighty years, until the full thousand years are completed. The bones, hard and strong, remain honored and revered in many places far longer, manifesting only after a great span, when matter from above descends to make them manifest.

The deepest purpose of this whole circuit is now disclosed: it is so that a part of the body may become so perfected that it takes on the form of the *nafs* itself. During this long period, the part of the body that is the heart does precisely this — it takes on the form of the `nafs ḥissiyyah`, the sensory soul, perfecting itself, while the other parts ascend from below to constitute the rest of the body. The heart, in the seven hundred and twenty or the full thousand years, is transformed into the form of the `nafs nāṭiqah`, the rational soul — the luminous form having already gone up into the *haykal*. So when the human being appears again in the world, his new *nafs* is the heart that, in the *buruj*, was purified and transformed into a soul; for his old sensory soul had gone with the Imam, into the *haykal*, and could not return.

### The Inviolability of Rank: Each Returns As He Was

A law of strict constancy governs this reappearance, and the teaching states it with great firmness: a man returns in exactly the rank he held before. If he was a believer (*mu'min*), here too he will be a believer; if a denier (*muqassir*), here too a denier; if he was *ma'dhun*, then here too *ma'dhun*; if a *da'i*, then here too a *da'i*. Whatever his first form and station, in that same form and station he reappears in the world, because that reality has become ingrained in him — he must reach again the very station he reached before, and he cannot change it.

The teaching renders this with the image of the clock that recurs throughout these lectures. When it is half past six, it is still the sixth hour; through every minute — five past, ten past, twelve past, and on until five-and-fifty past — it remains the sixth hour. Only when it becomes seven does a new hour begin; only then do we say it is now seven o'clock and the limit of six is past. In just this way the soul moves within the bounds of its rank: it may advance through the degrees within its own station, but it cannot leap to another station until it has traversed every degree of the one it holds. A *mu'min*, for instance, has ten degrees within the rank of believer; one who returns may advance from the first to the second degree, but he remains a *mu'min*, and only after he has traversed all ten degrees of belief — which may take ten *karrat*, ten cycles — can he set foot in the degree of a *muqassir*. The advance is real, but it is bounded.

This same law is set out in the formal Arabic of the text, rank by rank: `fa-l-bābu ya‘ūdu bāban wa-l-ḥujjatu ya‘ūdu ḥujjatan wa-dā‘ī al-balāgh ya‘ūdu dā‘ī al-balāgh wa-dā‘ī al-muṭlaq ya‘ūdu dā‘ī al-muṭlaqan wa-l-maḥdūd maḥdūdan wa-l-ma’dhūn al-muṭlaq ma’dhūn al-muṭlaqan wa-l-maḥṣūra maḥṣūran wa-l-mu’min al-bāligh mu’minan bālighan wa-l-mustajib mustajiban wa-kullun ‘alā ḥālatihi al-ūlā lā ya‘dūhā wa-in balagha fī al-‘ilm fawqa mā kāna balaghahu khādiman` — so the *bab* returns as a *bab*, the *hujja* as a *hujja*, the *da'i al-balagh* as a *da'i al-balagh*, the *da'i al-mutlaq* as a *da'i al-mutlaq*, the *mahdud* as *mahdud*, the *ma'dhun al-mutlaq* as *ma'dhun al-mutlaq*, the *mahsur* as *mahsur*, the accomplished believer as an accomplished believer, and the respondent (*mustajib*) as a respondent; and each remains upon his first state and does not transgress it, even if he reaches in knowledge above what he had reached before as a servant. And the conclusion follows: `fa-lā ṣu‘ūda lahu ‘an tilka al-rutbati abadan` — so there is no ascent for him beyond that rank ever. Even if in a later *karra* a soul acquires knowledge greater than it had before, it is still established in the very degree it held; the gain in knowledge does not breach the boundary of rank.

The breaching of ranks — *kharq-e-maratib* — does occur, but it is no ordinary transition; the interval between one rank and the next is great, like the passage from the sixth hour to the seventh, which cannot be reached until all sixty of the intervening minutes — all sixty degrees — have been traversed. The teaching recalls here the counsel of Sayyidna Yusuf Najmuddin on the meaning of this clock-image: the seventh hour cannot be established until every minute of the sixth has run. So *kharq-e-maratib* is real but rare, and it never happens in the ordinary recurrence of forms; the law of constancy holds, and only the full traversal of every degree permits the crossing into a higher rank.

### The Sending-Forth of a New Soul and the Single Essence

The law of return is now joined to a further teaching about how, when one soul ascends, another arises to replace it. `wa-lā yazālūna kadhālika kulla waqtin ḥattā yuṣaffā nafsun wa-ṣa‘adat ilā al-muṣṭafā, inba‘atha fī jismihā al-mutakhallif nafsun ukhrā bi-tadbīr al-mudabbirāt wa-naẓarin min al-āyāt al-ilāhiyyāt` — and they cease not to be thus at every moment, until a soul is purified and ascends to the Chosen One, at which point there is sent forth in its remaining body another soul, by the governance of the governors and a glance from the divine signs. When the soul has been purified and has ascended to the *Mustafa*, the Chosen One, a new soul, *munba'itha*, is sent forth within the body that remained — and this is brought about by the *tadbir* of the *mudabbirat*, the governance of the governors, and by a regard from the divine signs.

This new soul is itself a member of the body that has become worthy of ascent. `wa-tilka al-nafs al-munba‘ithatu ‘aḍwun min a‘ḍā’i dhālika al-jism, qad istaḥaqqa al-ṣu‘ūd` — and that soul which is sent forth is a member from the members of that body, which has become worthy of ascent. One of the body's own members, having become worthy to rise, takes the place of the departed soul. `fa-yunẓaru ilayhi wa-yu‘nā bihi ḥattā yustakhraja minhu nafsun ‘alā mithāli dhālika al-jism` — so it is looked upon and cared for until a soul is extracted from it in the likeness of that body, drawn from the spiritual essence appropriate to it; `fa-yakūnāni dhātan wāḥidatan ‘alā mā sabaqa bihi al-qawl` — so they become a single essence, according to what the saying has already established. The member that rose to soul-hood and the soul drawn from it become, in the end, one single essence.

For the martyrs — the *shuhada'* — this process carries a special grace. For them, not the heart only but both the heart and the liver become soul; and this grants them an advance of a degree or two over the ordinary measure. When the heart becomes the *nafs hissiyyah*, the liver takes the heart's place, and then the two eyes take their place in turn — the process unfolding member by member, each *karra* purifying things one by one. Every thousand-year cycle is a *karra*, and in each *karra* the purification advances by stages. But even the martyr's advance of a degree or two is bounded by the law of rank: if he was a *bab* with the Sayyid al-Ida'iyya before, he returns as a *bab*; the advance is within his ten degrees, and he cannot breach them.

And when the Qa'im, upon him be the blessings and peace, at last appears — a vast ocean gathering all the Imams — then the first sensory soul of such a believer, the one that ascended and was kept with the Imam, becomes the *qa'id*, the leader, of all the souls that rose from that one body; it becomes their Imam, just as it was their origin here below. So the whole circuit, from the ascent of the first soul through the sending-forth of its successors to the final gathering at the Qa'im, resolves into a single ordered essence with its own first soul at its head.

### The Successful Believers of the Qur'an and the Pillars of Prayer

These purified, ascending believers are the very ones the Noble Book describes. `wa-hā’ulā’i hum alladhīna ‘arraḍa ‘anhum al-kitāb al-karīm bi-qawlihi ta‘ālā: qad aflaḥa al-mu’minūn, alladhīna hum fī ṣalātihim khāshi‘ūn, wa-alladhīna hum ‘an al-laghwi mu‘riḍūn, wa-alladhīna hum li-l-zakāti fā‘ilūn, wa-alladhīna hum li-furūjihim ḥāfiẓūn, illā ‘alā azwājihim aw mā malakat aymānuhum fa-innahum ghayru malūmīn. fa-man ibtaghā warā’a dhālika fa-ulā’ika hum al-‘ādūn` — and these are they to whom the Noble Book alluded with His saying, Exalted is He: "Successful indeed are the believers, who are humble in their prayers, and who turn away from vain speech, and who are active in giving the *zakat*, and who guard their chastity — except from their wives or what their right hands possess, for then they are not to be blamed; but whoever seeks beyond that, those are the transgressors." The success of which the verse speaks belongs to these believers: those who pray with humility, who turn from idle talk, who give the *zakat*, and who guard their chastity within its lawful bounds — the phrase `mā malakat aymānuhum` referring to the slave-girls lawful to them — while `fa-man ibtaghā warā’a dhālika`, whoever seeks beyond that bound, are the transgressors.

The teaching pauses on the humility of prayer to expound its structure. `buniyat al-ṣalātu ‘alā arba‘ati as-humin` — prayer is founded upon four shares, four pillars. The word `as-hum` is the plural of `sahm`, a share or portion; prayer rests upon four such shares. They are: `al-wuḍū’`, the ablution; `al-rukū‘`, the bowing; `al-sujūd`, the prostration; and `al-khushū‘`, the humility. Of these, *khushu'* receives its own definition, for it is the inward pillar. The meaning of *khushu'* is that when a person prays, at the very least the first degree of humility is that the meaning of what he recites is present in his mind — that he knows what he is reciting and what he is supplicating Allah for. This awareness of the meaning of the recitation is the first degree of *khushu'*; without it, even the outward acts of ablution, bowing, and prostration lack the inward share on which prayer is founded.

### The Mark of Upright Conduct and the Purification over 360 Cycles

These four qualities of the successful believers — humility in prayer, turning from vanity, giving *zakat*, and guarding chastity — are not incidental but are the distinguishing mark of those who reach the noble stations. `wa-innamā balaghū hādhihi al-mabāligh al-sharīfah... fa-lam yakun lahum an yukhālifū shay’an mimmā intaẓama fī hādhihi al-āyah` — and they reached these noble stations only because it was not for them to oppose anything of what is ordered within this verse. Those who reach these noble stations are precisely those who keep what the verse commands and forbids; for them a part of their body becomes a soul, and this teaching belongs to them. As the text declares, `idh hiya simatuhum fī al-ẓāhir wa-l-bāṭin lā simata li-ghayrihim` — for it is their mark, in the outward and the inward alike, and there is no such mark for any other than them. These qualities are the *sima*, the distinguishing sign, of the one who is `mustaqim al-sayr` — of upright conduct — in both his outward and his inward life, and no one else bears it.

The condition of *mustaqim al-sayr* is defined with care: it is the believer from whom no gravely forbidden sin has issued — none of the things the *shari'ah* has strictly prohibited, such as the drinking of wine, or adultery, and the like of these grave evils. The one of upright conduct does not make his standing, his *qiyam*, upon any of the things against which the supplications warn. For such a one a fixed measure of purification is appointed: `lā yazālūna kadhālika kulla waqtin ḥattā yuṣaffā nafsun` — they continue thus at every moment until a soul is purified — and the purification of a *mustaqim al-sayr* is completed over three hundred and sixty *karrat*, three hundred and sixty cycles. In these three hundred and sixty *karrat* his whole body becomes pure. The meaning of the whole body becoming pure is given concretely: the sole of the foot reaches the station of the heart, and a *mustajib*, a respondent, and a *naqid*, a critic, remain at the station of the Prophet's knee — that is, this measure of his parts is purified across those three hundred and sixty cycles. So the law of upright conduct is not only a moral standard but a precise condition of cosmic advancement: only the one whose conduct is free of grave sin undergoes this purification, in which, cycle upon cycle, his body is raised until its lowest member attains the rank of its highest.

### The Calling, the Covenant, and the Indestructible Good Deed

Finally the teaching describes what befalls these descended forms once they reach maturity in the world. `wa-‘inda kamāl hādhihi al-ashkhāṣ wa-bulūghihā al-ḥulm, tud‘ā fa-tujību wa-yu’khadhu ‘alayhā al-‘ahd al-karīm` — and upon the perfection of these persons and their reaching maturity, they are called, and they respond, and the noble covenant is taken from them. The forms in question are those that descended from the heavens and came from the *buruj* into the world. When they reach `bulūgh al-ḥulm`, the maturity of youth, they are summoned; they answer the summons and accept; and from them is taken `al-‘ahd al-karīm`, the noble covenant.

Having taken the covenant, they ascend in the religious ranks without hindrance. `wa-tartaqī fī al-rutab al-dīniyyah wa-ma‘ārif al-ḥaqīqiyyah, shay’an ba‘da shay’in fī qalīlin ba‘da qalīlin, min ghayri an yudākhilahā shakkun aw ta‘tariḍahā... wa-lā ya‘riḍu lahā shubhatun` — and they ascend in the religious ranks and in true gnosis, thing after thing, little by little, without any doubt entering them or any uncertainty presenting itself to them. They progress in the religious ranks and in true knowledge step by step, and no doubt enters them and no uncertainty obstructs them — and the reason for this freedom from doubt is given: they will be upon certainty because their first *karra* was upon certainty. Having once been established in certainty, they return in the second *karra* with that certainty intact, so that doubt cannot be created in them. Rather, `bal takūnu jāriyatan fī miṣ‘ab al-ṣu‘ūd, sālimatan min al-‘awā’iq` — rather they will be flowing in the channel of ascent, safe from all obstacles and hindrances; no one can push them away or stop them, until they reach what is appointed for them — their `mablaghahā al-‘aẓīm wa-l-maqām al-karīm`, their great destination and noble station, as they were in their first manifestation, with no deficiency falling short of that destination and no passing beyond it, each one reaching the rank that was his own.

The certainty of these returning believers rests on a principle about the permanence of the good deed. As the teaching has it, once a point is created — a *nuqta*, an established point of good — that point cannot be destroyed. This is anchored in what Allah has decreed concerning reward: for a good deed, the reward is tenfold; for an evil deed, the recompense is onefold. A good deed, once established, cannot be destroyed; its reward remains for its doer forever, and the deed is to abide. Evil, by contrast, can be brought to nothing — it can be effaced and made non-existent — but the good deed never. A person who has done a good deed keeps its reward for all time; the point of good he created is indestructible, and `bal takūnu jāriyatan`, rather its reward flows on. This is the security on which the certainty of the ascending believers rests: their first establishment in good is a created point that cannot be undone, so that on their return they cannot fall into doubt.

### The Points Held by the Governors of the Cycles

The teaching closes by setting out, on the authority of Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Husayn, where the latent realities of all future believers are held before they manifest. Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Husayn used to teach that all the *nuqta* — all the points — of those who are to become believers in a given *kawr*, a given era, are present with the *Sayyid al-Ida'iyya*. The point is present though it has not yet manifested; the manifestation of every rank in that era is held in latency with the *Sayyid al-Ida'iyya*, who is the *mudabbir*, the governor, of the entire *kawr*. The teaching illustrates this with a homely image: as we have many things stored in our homes but bring out one thing at a time, revealing one in this season and another in the next, so the governor of the *kawr* brings each point to manifestation in the era appointed for it. When the time for a point's manifestation arrives, the governance of the *Sayyid al-Ida'iyya* is enacted, and the point appears in whatever era is appointed for it.

This structure of held points extends across the larger cosmic divisions. Likewise the one who is the *mudabbir* of the *mawr*, the sub-cycle, holds with him the points of his entire *mawr* — all the points of those who are to progress within it are gathered with him. And the first *Sayyid al-Ida'iyya*, together with `Adam al-Ibda'i`, the primordial Adam of origination, holds the points of all who are to progress at all: with Adam al-Ibda'i are present the points of every soul that will ever advance. So the realities of all future believers are nested within the governors of the cycles — the points of an era with the governor of that era, the points of a sub-cycle with its governor, and the points of all who will ever progress with Adam al-Ibda'i at the source — each point waiting in latency until the appointed governance brings it forth into manifestation in its proper time.

So the section ends as it began, with the discipline of the People of Truth and their loyalty to all the Imams. The closing supplication of the text gathers the whole teaching into a prayer: `ja‘alanā Allāhu mimman khuṣṣa bi-hādhihi al-simāt wa-lā ‘adala binā ‘anhum` — may Allah make us among those distinguished by these marks, and may He not turn us away from them. The teaching notes that the word `‘adala` carries more than one meaning in Arabic — both justice and turning away — and here it is the latter that is intended: may Allah not turn us aside from the company of those who bear the distinguishing marks of upright conduct, the believers of *qad aflaha al-mu'minun*, by the right of the Prophet and his pure family, the blessings of Allah be upon them all.
