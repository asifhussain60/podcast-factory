## The Cycle of Unveiling and the House of Ibrahim

### The Ascent of the Souls and the Forming of the Imam's Progeny

We begin where the previous discourse left off: with the journey of the soul at the moment of death, and the way that journey furnishes the very substance from which the household of the Imam is constituted. The Nafse Namiyah — the growth-soul, the vegetative or growing soul of the believers — does not perish at death. When the believer dies, this Nafse Namiyah departs together with the sensory faculties, the hissiyat, and it travels in the form of rihyat: a subtle, fragrant exhalation, a perfumed essence. From the moon, the qamar, it rises toward the sun, the shams. From the qamar it moves upward to the shams, and from there it passes through the various stars and celestial spheres, the kawakib and the aflak. Having traversed those spheres, it descends — and where it descends is precisely upon those things that fire cannot touch and cannot consume. The image is exact: it settles upon what is beyond the reach of flame, as fire that rests upon water never burns the water. This descended subtlety is the rihyat of the entire world, the perfumed essence belonging to the whole realm of creation.

What becomes of this rihyat once it has descended? The Imam and the angelic household around him — the Imam's malak, his retinue — partake of it; they consume it, they drink it in. It is, in its inner reality, a salawat, a blessing offered up and received back. And from this descended rihyat the Imam's offspring are formed. Their very bodies, the takwin, the bodily formation, are constituted out of the rihyat — the gathered, perfumed essence of the believers who have died and ascended. But the body alone does not make the child of the Imam. The bodies take their shape from rihyat, and their Nafse Namiyah — the growth-soul that animates them — is formed from the most excellent subtleties of the sun, the lataif of the shams. So the child of the Imam is doubly noble: his frame is woven from the ascended essences of the faithful, and his living growth-soul is drawn from the finest subtleties of the solar realm. This is the takwin, the engendering, of the Imam's progeny.

### The Gathering at the Babul Abwab and the Making of the Haykal

When the Imam's child reaches four years of age, a momentous gathering takes place, and through it the very form of the cosmos is assembled into a single human shape. With the upbringing and spiritual training, the tarbiyat, of the Imam's child over those four years, the hudud of all the believers — the entire community of the faithful — undergo a separation: their surat, their form, and their nafs, their soul, are drawn apart from one another. Both, form and soul, separate, and they ascend in proper order until they reach the Babul Abwab, the Gate of Gates. They arrive at the Babul Abwab arranged in rank, each at its station. There their forms, taking the shape of a human being, are held in that human configuration.

This continues until the Imam's child has completed his fourth year. At that point the connection is made. Upon the Imam — through the agency of the Babul Abwab — all the gathered forms and souls come and connect with the nafs of the Imam's child. They join to that nafs. And upon that nafs the Imam's own form exerts its effect. The forms and souls connect with the nafs of the child, while the Imam's own form stands distinct from that nafs, for the Imam's form has become a ruh, a spirit; it now occupies the station, the maqam, of the ruh. Through this conjunction the assembled whole becomes a haykal — a luminous temple, a complete human structure. The haykal is the human form thus composed. And the Babul Abwab, which presided over the gathering, becomes the qalbe haykal, the heart of the temple. Within this constituted human form every rank is preserved at its proper place: just as in the living body the hair, the nails, and each part keep their station, so here whatever rank a believer held is retained at its corresponding station within the cosmic human form. In this manner the form of the entire universe is gathered with the Imam into one complete shape.

It is worth drawing a careful distinction here, for the term haykal is not to be used loosely. With the nutaqa', the Speaker-prophets, a gathering does indeed take place, and a great gathering at that — but no haykal is formed in that case. The method of the nutaqa' is not the same as the method of the Imams. With the Imams, the haykal is a thing of its own order. With the nutaqa', what occurs in their assembly is a gathering of forms, an ijtima' of suwar, and nothing more; the haykal as such is not constituted there. When, then, do we properly speak of a haykal? Only when the forms come to the Babul Abwab. That alone is called a haykal — the assembly of all the forms into the complete human form, the form of the alif, the upright human figure. Consider the image of a great mirror: if you shatter it into pieces, each small fragment remains a mirror, and a form can still be seen within each piece. But the original, unbroken mirror — the one in which the complete human form is reflected — corresponds to what gathers at the Babul Abwab, and it is there, and there alone, that the haykal is described. With the nutaqa', then, the people of their eras gather as a gathering of forms, but the form of the haykal is not present; theirs is a gathering, an assembly, but not a haykal. The word haykal has been reserved deliberately for the place where the human form — the form of the alif — is established, which is the Babul Abwab.

### The Grand Gathering at the Coming of the Qaimul Qiyam

What unfolds with each Imam unfolds on an immeasurably vaster scale when the Qaimul Qiyam comes — the Imam of the Imams, the Resurrector of resurrections. With him too a gathering of the same order occurs, but magnified beyond reckoning. There as well a human form is composed, and into that form are gathered all the believers — all who have passed away across the ages are present in it. In this way are formed qawaim of qawaim, resurrectors upon resurrectors, oceans upon oceans: a gathering so great and so grand that it embraces the whole succession of the faithful from the beginning. This is the supreme assembly toward which the entire economy of unveiling has been tending. Bismillah. Allahumma salli ala Sayyidina Muhammadin wa ala aali Sayyidina Muhammad wa sallim — O Allah, send blessings upon our master Muhammad and upon the family of our master Muhammad and grant them peace.

### The Extraction of Each Imam and the Perpetual Da'wat

We now take up the text itself: `Wa istikhraju kulli imamin min da'wati abih li-anna kulla imamin yuqimu da'watahu wa ya'malu fiha ila an yujad min da'watihi man yakhlufuhu fi makanihi ala hadhal-minhaj abadan sarmadan fi Daur al-Kashf wa Daur as-Satr` — "And the extraction of every Imam from the da'wat of his father, because every Imam establishes his da'wat and works within it until there is brought forth from his da'wat one who will succeed him in his place, upon this same path, forever and perpetually, in the Daur al-Kashf and the Daur as-Satr."

The word istikhraj means "to draw out," "to extract"; some have rendered it as akhraja, "he brought forth." The teaching it carries is this: every Imam is drawn out from the da'wat — the summons, the spiritual mission — of his father. This is how it comes to pass. Every Imam establishes his own da'wat and labors within it, working in it and acting upon it, until from that very da'wat there is brought forth the one who will be his successor and stand in his place. And so the process continues, without end and without interruption, forever and always, in the same manner, throughout both the Daur al-Kashf, the Era of Unveiling, and the Daur as-Satr, the Era of Concealment. This extraction of successor from predecessor is the unbroken thread that runs through both great cycles of sacred history; it never ceases.

### The Daur al-Kashf: The Open Reading of the Realities

The text continues: `Wa fi Daur al-Kashf yazharu fihil-ulumul-batina al-haqiqa tuqra' fil-mahafil wa ala ru'usil-manabir wa la 'amala fihi bi-shay'in minal-awda'iz-zahirah` — "And in the Daur al-Kashf there appear within it the esoteric sciences, the realities; they are read aloud in the gatherings and from the heads of the pulpits, and there is no acting within it upon anything of the apparent ordinances."

In the Era of Unveiling, then, the inner sciences come into the open. The batin, the esoteric, and the haqa'iq, the realities or true sciences, manifest plainly. These are read aloud in the assemblies and proclaimed from the pulpits, openly and without concealment. And in that era there is no enforcement of the zahir, the outward ordinances; none of the apparent ritual prescriptions is performed as binding law. The realities themselves take the place that the outward forms held in the era of concealment. The text has already indicated which things will be abrogated in such a time and which will remain.

Here a subtle but indispensable principle must be insisted upon: without Nasut there can be no manifestation of Lahut. The divine realm of Lahut cannot appear, cannot manifest itself, except through the human realm of Nasut. There is no Lahut made manifest without a Nasut to bear it. And this brings us to the Nasut-e-tabi'i, the "natural" or "constitutive" Nasut, whose mention now arises and whose characteristics have been explained. The Nasut-e-tabi'i is that order in which interpersonal relationships subsist — relationships among persons, this connection and that — yet in which the five senses are not present. There the senses do not operate, but relationships nonetheless exist. The khuddam, the servants of the divine economy, and the Imam himself stand in such relationships; and for the relationships of the khuddam and of the Imam to be manifested, the manifestation of the Nasut-e-tabi'i is necessary. Without this constitutive Nasut, in which relation persists though sensation is absent, the whole web of ranks and offices could not be displayed at all. These matters have already been set out in detail.

### The Piety of the People of Unveiling

We arrive at a remarkable claim concerning the people of the Daur al-Kashf, and it is worth dwelling upon, for it overturns any suspicion that the lifting of obligatory law signifies a slackening of devotion. The text moves to the Daur as-Satr — `Wa Daur as-Satr, ad-da'watu fihi ala darbayn`, "And as for the Daur as-Satr, the da'wat within it is of two kinds" — but before passing on, the teacher pauses on the people of unveiling. Although the injunctions of the Shari'ah are not enforced in the Daur al-Kashf, the people of that era are so deeply pious that the supererogatory acts they perform of their own free will — the tatawwu'at — exceed our obligatory duties, our fara'id. Of their own accord, though nothing of it is obligatory upon them, they multiply voluntary worship so abundantly that it surpasses what is incumbent upon us.

Consider the particulars. They offer the prayer of thanksgiving, the shukranah, voluntarily. They fast — and whereas our obligatory fasting is confined to the single month of Ramadan, they keep supererogatory fasts beyond all obligation, and with these they worship. As for zakat: the obligatory system of zakat that governs us will not be in force among them; yet they will give so abundantly in charity, in sadaqat, that their giving far surpasses ours. This is the very quality of their station. And all the disciplines of cleanliness and purity they preserve in full measure, equally and without diminishment. The practice of circumcision, the khatna, they maintain — indeed it is said of some among them that the foreskin removed in circumcision does not even appear upon them, so refined is their constitution. The point stands clearly: the absence of compulsory law in the Era of Unveiling does not lower the people of that age beneath us; on the contrary, their freely chosen devotion towers above our prescribed minimum.

### The Two Da'wats of the Era of Concealment

Now we may take up the Daur as-Satr in earnest. In the Era of Concealment the da'wat is of two kinds: one is an esoteric da'wat that is hidden — da'watun batinatun khafiyyah — and one is a manifest da'wat that is clear — da'watun zahiratun jaliyyah. The text defines the first: `Fad-da'watul-batinatul-khafiyyah qismul-maqamatil-ilahiyyah wal-hayakilin-nuraniyyah wa abwabihim wa hujajihim wa du'atihim wa ma'dhunihim` — "So the esoteric, hidden da'wat is the division of the divine stations, and the luminous structures, and their gates, and their proofs, and their summoners, and their authorized ones."

The hidden, esoteric da'wat, then, is the whole ordered division of the maqamat ilahiyyah, the divine stations, together with the hayakil nuraniyyah, the luminous structures, and their abwab, their gates; their hujaj, their proofs; their du'at, their summoners; and their ma'dhun, those granted licence to call. This is the inner hierarchy of the spiritual world, ranged in its concealed order — the architecture of divine offices that subsists behind the veil throughout the era of concealment.

### Concealment in the Era of Adam: Qabil and Habil

The text then turns to the condition of this hidden da'wat in the earliest era: `Wa kanu fi Daur Adam, salamullahi alayh, fi ghayatin minal-istitari wat-taghatti khawfal-addad wal-fara'inata wa ahlil-'inad li-qillatil-munasabati baynahum wa bu'dihim 'anhum` — "And they were, in the era of Adam, peace of Allah be upon him, at the extreme of concealment and covering, out of fear of the adversaries and the pharaohs and the people of obstinacy, on account of the scarcity of affinity between them and their distance from them."

In the era of Adam, peace be upon him, the people of the esoteric and hidden da'wat were in the utmost degree of istitar — concealment — and taghatti — covering, the drawing of a veil over themselves. These were the people of the era of Imam Hunaid, peace be upon him, and his followers; they reached the very limit of concealment, withdrawn behind the curtain of fear. And the cause of this extreme concealment was fear of opponents — the addad, the adversaries; the fara'in, the pharaohs and tyrants; and the ahl al-'inad, the people of stubborn opposition. Behind this lies a narrative: the progeny of Qabil gained the upper hand and seized dominance, while the progeny of Habil went into concealment. And along with the descendants of Habil, the followers of Imam Hunaid likewise withdrew into hiding. All of this came about because of the scarcity of munasabah — the lack of affinity — between them and the world around them, and because of the great distance that separated them. The distance is a distance of stations: the gulf between the lofty rank of these hidden ones and the world that surrounded them was so wide, and the affinity so slight, that nothing remained for them but to vanish from view.

### The Manifest Summoners and Their Outward Call

The manifest da'wat operates on an opposite principle, and the text describes it: `Ammad-da'watuj-jaliyyah, fa-hum qismu du'atihimuz-zahirah fa-hum mutazahiruna bi-dhalika da'una ilayh... wa dhalika li-qurbil-munasabati baynahum wa bayna sa'iril-'alam` — "As for the clear da'wat, they are the division of their manifest summoners, and they are manifest through it, calling toward it... and that is on account of the closeness of affinity between them and the rest of the world."

The clear and open da'wat is the division of the manifest du'at, the summoners who operate in the open. They make themselves manifest through this da'wat; by it they are named and by it they are known; and they call toward it — da'una ilayh — summoning to the Imam of the time. And the reason they can act openly is precisely the inverse of what drove the others into hiding: there is a closeness of affinity, a qurb al-munasabah, between them and the rest of the world. Because their affinity with the surrounding world is near, they can stand forth in the open and call.

And what is the purpose of their open calling? `Fa-hum yastakhrijuna man anasu rushduhum min ahlid-da'watiz-zahirah ila da'wati mawalihimul-batinah` — "So they extract those in whom they sense sound guidance, from the people of the manifest da'wat, toward the esoteric da'wat of their masters." The manifest du'at draw out, from among the people of the open da'wat, those in whom they perceive rushd, sound and well-directed guidance — those with whom they find a true affinity in their right-mindedness. And they extract such persons and direct them onward toward the hidden, esoteric da'wat of their mawali, their masters. The manifest summoners thus serve as a bridge: standing where affinity with the world is near, they gather the well-guided and convey them inward, toward the concealed da'wat of the masters that the world at large could never reach.

Here a luminous symmetry comes into view, and it is the key to the whole structure. From the text it appears that in the Daur as-Satr the affinity among the momineen — the believers among themselves — is greater, while their affinity with the rest of the world is what governs the work of the manifest du'at. The manifest summoners possess affinity with the realm of the world, and that nearness is what allows them to call openly. The people of the esoteric, by contrast, have no such affinity with the world below; their affinity does not lie here. Theirs is the qillat al-munasabah, the scarcity of affinity, that holds them apart and hidden. The relationship with the Imam does not persist for the common people; they fall away, and what remains is the relationship of the few. In the Daur al-Kashf this inner relationship comes into the open and is manifested; in the Daur as-Satr it must be carried inward, soul by soul, through the bridge of the manifest du'at.

### The Du'at as Instruments of the Imams

The text now describes the inner disposition of these summoners: `Li-annahum li-amrihim mu'tamirun wa 'anhu akhizun wa li-qawlihim qa'ilun wa bi-'uluwwi maqamatihim 'arifun 'alimun wa hum abadan 'ala dhalika fi jami'il-adwar mustamirrun` — "Because they act under their command, and take from them, and speak by their word, and are knowing and aware of the loftiness of their stations; and they are forever, in all the eras, continuing upon this."

The du'at, that is to say, are entirely instruments of the Imams. They act under the command, the amr, of the Imams — having received their orders from them. From the Imams they take the responsibility for every task — 'anhu akhizun — receiving their charge from them. By the word of the Imams they speak — li-qawlihim qa'ilun — uttering nothing of their own but only what their masters give them to utter. And they are knowing and aware of the loftiness of the Imams' stations, recognizing and acknowledging the exalted rank of those they serve. And so they remain, forever, throughout all the eras, the adwar, continuing in this same way without deviation, passing on in this single posture of obedience from age to age.

This complete self-effacement of the summoner before the Imam was given perfect expression by Sayyidna Ali — cited here as Sayyidna Ali bin al-Husayn, al-Jamali — who declared in a khutbah, a sermon, delivered when he came to visit: "I am nothing but like a pen in the hand of the Imam." He is, he says, as a pen in the hand of a scribe — moving only as the writer moves it, forming only the letters the writer intends, possessing of itself no motion and no meaning. So the du'at stand in the hand of the Imams: the qalam in the grasp of the katib.

### The Succession of the Natiqs: From Adam to Nuh to Ibrahim

We now follow the chain of the Speaker-prophets, the nutaqa', as one era completes and another begins. `Wa kadhalika lamma tamma Daur Adam 'alayhis-salam bi-akhiri mutimmihi, qama ba'dahu Nuh 'alayhis-salam natiqan wa wasiyyuhu, thumma a'immatu dawrihi 'alayhis-salam wa du'atuhum bid-da'wataynil-zahirah wal-batinah` — "And likewise, when the era of Adam, peace be upon him, was completed by its last completer, Nuh, peace be upon him, arose after him as a Natiq, and his wasi, then the Imams of his era, peace be upon him, and their summoners with the two da'wats, the manifest and the esoteric."

When the era of Adam, peace be upon him, came to its completion through its last completer, its akhir mutimm, then Nuh, peace be upon him, arose after him as a Natiq, a Speaker. After Nuh, his wasi, his successor, arose; and the wasi of Sayyidna Nuh is Sam, Sayyidna Sam, peace be upon him. Then came the Imams of Nuh's era, and their du'at carrying both da'wats, the manifest and the esoteric, just as in every era. `Ila an tamma Daur Nuh 'alayhis-salam bi-akhiri mutimmihi` — "until the era of Nuh, peace be upon him, was completed by its last completer." And when the era of Nuh too reached its completion through its last completer, `Wa qama ba'dahu Ibrahim 'alayhis-salam natiqan` — "and after him arose Ibrahim, peace be upon him, as a Natiq." So after Nuh, Ibrahim, peace be upon him, arose as a Natiq.

### Ibrahim the Confluence of the Two Preceding Eras

Now we reach the distinguishing mark of Ibrahim, peace be upon him, which sets him apart from the two Natiqs who came before him. `Wa kana majma'an li-suwari ahlid-dawraynis-sabiqayn 'alayh alladhina huma Dawr Adam wa Nuh 'alayhimas-salam` — "And he was a confluence of the forms of the people of the two preceding eras, which are the era of Adam and the era of Nuh, peace be upon them both."

Ibrahim, peace be upon him, was a majma' — a confluence, a place of gathering and convergence — of the suwar, the forms, of the people of the two eras that had gone before him: the era of Adam and the era of Nuh. He was their confluence; in him the forms of both prior eras flowed together and met. (A point of method is worth noting on the word majma': although one might be tempted to derive it from the root jama'a in some other inflection, the rule is that where a clear and simple meaning is available, one should not chase after meanings drawn from secondary derivatives — that is a special principle of sound interpretation, and the plain sense of majma', "confluence," is the meaning intended.)

This is precisely what distinguishes Ibrahim from Adam and from Nuh. Concerning Adam and Nuh, the quality of being a majma', a confluence, is not predicated. They were not themselves the confluence; rather, as the text explains, `Li-annahuma kana da'iyayn wa-stahaqqa martabatan-natiq wa kana Ibrahim bi-khilafihima li-annahu-jtama'a 'indahu suwaru jami'i ahlid-dawrayn wa huwa haykalun nuraniyyun` — "because the two of them were summoners and each deserved the rank of Natiq; and Ibrahim was contrary to them, because there were gathered with him the forms of all the people of the two eras, and he is a luminous structure." Both Adam and Nuh were da'i — they were callers, summoners, toward that confluence which Ibrahim would be; and by virtue of their office each deserved and held the rank of Natiq. But Ibrahim stands in contrast to them both: with him were gathered the forms of all the people of the two eras, and he himself is a haykal nurani, a luminous structure. Adam and Nuh summoned toward the gathering; Ibrahim was the gathering. They were da'i for the confluence; he was the confluence itself.

### The Changing of the Shari'ah and the Constancy of Worship

A question presses itself here, and it must be answered, for it touches the deepest puzzle of sacred law. When a Natiq abrogates his Shari'ah and a new one supersedes it — from the standpoint of the realities, the haqa'iq, what is actually happening? In the visible world one law is repealed and another is enacted; all manner of changes occur. Why should this be?

The answer is that in each such era the emergence of the fudala' — the excellent ones, the superior substances and souls that come forth in that age — makes it necessary that some laws of the Shari'ah be altered. The things that are manifesting from below in a given era manifest in a particular, special manner proper to that era, as has been explained in Zahr al-Ma'ani. An analogy makes the principle plain. Every physician, when he comes, prescribes his own remedy. One goes to a doctor and he gives a certain medicine; though the illness be the same, when one goes to another doctor he will make some changes or offer his own new medicine. The disease is one; the prescriptions differ with the practitioner and the moment. So too with the Shari'ah: the underlying condition is constant, but the prescriptions are adjusted to the age and to the fudala' who have emerged within it.

A crucial line must be drawn, however, between two domains. The changes belong to the sphere of halal and haram — what may be eaten and what may not, what may be done and what may not; the things of the past are abrogated and new things established in their place. But the worship of Allah and the commands of Allah remain everywhere in their place; these cannot be changed. The ground of this is the Quranic word: `Innad-dina 'indallahil-Islam` — "Indeed, the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam." That is the religion, fixed and unchangeable. Yet within it, on account of the emergence of certain fudala', some adjustments must be made, and these adjustments are made through the Shari'ah. In each era, the fudala' that have emerged for that Natiq necessitate that some changes be made in the ordinances of the Shari'ah; and so the changes are made — without ever touching the immovable core, which is the worship and the command of Allah, the din that in His sight is Islam.

### The Three Ranks Gathered in Ibrahim

The text now situates Ibrahim with respect to the two who preceded him in terms of the three great offices of revelation, interpretation, and reality. `Fa-Adam yaqumu maqamat-tanzil wa Nuh yaqumu muqabilat-ta'wil wa Ibrahim muqabilul-haqa'iq wa li-dhalika qala fihil-kitabul-karim, wa Ibrahimalladhi waffa` — "So Adam stands in the station of tanzil, and Nuh stands opposite ta'wil, and Ibrahim opposite the haqa'iq; and for this reason the Noble Book said concerning him, 'And of Ibrahim, who fulfilled.'"

Adam, peace be upon him, stands in the station of tanzil, of revelation in its outward descent. Nuh stands opposite ta'wil, in the place of interpretation, for the sake of ta'wil. And Ibrahim stands opposite the haqa'iq, the realities. And it is for this reason that the Noble Book declared of him, `wa Ibrahimalladhi waffa` — "and Ibrahim, who fulfilled." He fulfilled — meaning that he attained all three of these ranks at once: the rank of tanzil, the rank of ta'wil, and the rank of haqa'iq. The text glosses the verse: `ishratan ila hadhihith-thalathir-rutab allati huwa hiya wa huwa maqamun karim, dhul-haykalin-nuraniyyil-'azim` — this is "a pointing toward these three ranks, of which he is the very possessor; and he is a noble station, the possessor of the grand luminous structure." The verse points toward these three ranks, and Ibrahim is the one who possesses them all; his is a noble station, maqamun karim, and he is the bearer of the grand and luminous haykal, the haykal nurani al-'azim. Because the forms of the two eras were gathered in him and he is a luminous haykal raised above all the stations, he fulfilled — and the Book named him so.

### Ibrahim the Son of Salih, in Whom Four Ranks Convened

The text proceeds to Ibrahim's lineage and the convergence of offices within him. `Wa huwa waladul-imamis-sabiqi 'alayh al-mujidu al-mushiru ilayh, fa-jtama'at 'indahun-nubuwwah war-risalah wal-wasayah wal-imamah` — "And he is the son of the preceding Imam, the one who brought him into being and the one who pointed toward him; so there were gathered with him prophethood, messengership, successorship, and imamate."

Ibrahim is the son of the preceding Imam — and that Imam is Salih. Ibrahim is the son of Salih: the one who brought him into being, al-mujid, and the one who pointed toward him and designated him, al-mushir ilayh. And in Ibrahim there were gathered four ranks together: nubuwwah, prophethood; risalah, messengership; wasayah, successorship; and imamah, imamate. These four offices, which in others are dispersed, convened all at once in the single luminous person of Ibrahim. He is at once the prophet, the messenger, the wasi, and the Imam — the confluence of the eras made also the confluence of the offices.

### The Daur al-Fitrah and the Reckoning of the Eras

We must now situate all of this within the larger architecture of the ages, and clarify a point about the Daur al-Fitrah, the Era of Nature, which is easily misunderstood. Adam belongs to the Daur al-Fitrah, the era of nature. In that era two kinds of people emerged. One kind were the bearers of the sciences of the realities, the haqa'iq — upon which everything is founded. The other kind were the bearers of the apparent sciences, the zahir, and toward those outward sciences they inclined. The bearers of the true and real knowledge — those who were the priests in the highest sense, the carriers of the genuine sciences — had their gathering with Imam Hunaid, peace be upon him; while Sayyidna Adam, peace be upon him, had the gathering of the people of the apparent. Adam's assembly was the assembly of the zahir; Imam Hunaid's was the assembly of the haqa'iq.

Now a careful correction must be registered concerning the placement of these three thousand years, for a misconception had taken root. It had been supposed that the three thousand years of the Daur al-Fitrah stand separate from and additional to the Daur al-Kashf. The conception current in some quarters — and it had lodged itself in the minds in India — was that there are fifty thousand years of the Daur al-Kashf, three thousand years of the Daur al-Fitrah on top of them, and seven thousand years of the Daur as-Satr. But this is not correct. When the matter was examined book by book, the truth emerged otherwise: the last three thousand years within the fifty thousand years of the Daur al-Kashf are themselves the Daur al-Fitrah. They are not a separate addition but the closing portion of the Daur al-Kashf. In those last three thousand years two kinds of qualities manifested: some inclined toward the knowledge of reality, and some toward the knowledge of the apparent. Those final three thousand years are what is called the Daur al-Fitrah.

The reckoning confirms it, and the count must come out exactly. The total is fifty thousand plus seven thousand, which is fifty-seven thousand years. This figure is then multiplied by seven: seven times fifty-seven thousand yields three hundred and ninety-nine thousand years, and with a further thousand the round figure of four hundred thousand years is completed. In this way the four hundred thousand years are fully made up. Were one instead to count the three thousand years of the Daur al-Fitrah as a separate addition, the reckoning would be thrown off and would land somewhere else entirely, with a great discrepancy. So the Daur al-Fitrah must be understood as the last three thousand years of the fifty thousand of the Daur al-Kashf, and the present age is the last part of this first great dawr of four hundred thousand years.

Within that Daur al-Fitrah, the three-thousand-year era of priesthood and astrology stood at its very peak, and the era of sorcery as well. The priesthood, the reading of the stars, and the magical arts flourished to their fullest extent during those three thousand years. And it was Adam, peace be upon him, who began the Daur as-Satr, the Era of Concealment, after the Daur al-Fitrah. Adam is the one who inaugurated the Daur as-Satr. Nor was the Daur al-Fitrah void of prophets: prophets were present within it. During the long span of the Daur al-Kashf proper — the forty-seven thousand years before the final three thousand — there were only those who held to the sciences of the realities, and two factions formed. After those forty-seven thousand years were completed, in the closing three thousand years, two distinct types of people emerged: one type bore the earlier thought of the Daur al-Kashf, the sciences of reality; and the other gave precedence to the developing science of priesthood and the apparent sciences. As their intentions inclined, so their actions followed. Thus the parting of the two ways took place in those final years.

### Imam Hunaid, the Qaim of the Daur al-Kashf and the Highest Veil

A natural question arises: who is the Qaim of the Daur al-Kashf — the Resurrector who presides over the Era of Unveiling, the Qaim of the full fifty thousand years? The one who gathered the people of the Daur al-Kashf, those who bore the realities and the forms, was Imam Hunaid, peace be upon him. And when the question was pressed — where, then, is the station of the Qaim of the Daur al-Kashf, once the three thousand years whose bearers were the people of realities and forms had run their course — the answer given was this: he became the Hijab-e-A'la, the Highest Veil. Imam Hunaid, the Qaim of the Daur al-Kashf, became the Hijab-e-A'la; and one may take out the books and verify it there. He is the Highest Veil. The gatherer of the Daur al-Kashf was Imam Hunaid; the gatherer of the Daur as-Satr was Sayyidna Adam. The last people of the Daur al-Kashf were those who went forward into the Daur as-Satr; and among them, it was the people of the apparent sciences who carried over and advanced into the era of concealment.

### The Ten Ranks and the Station of Divine Speech

To grasp where the nutaqa' stand, we must lay out the ladder of ranks, for the rank of divine speech lies above even the imamate, and this is easily overlooked. The nutaqa' under discussion are not masters of Lahut and Nasut in the manner of the Imams; rather they are bearers of nutq-e-ilahi, divine speech, and the rank of divine speech stands above the imamate. The very word "imamate" does not reach so high. Let us see the ranks in order. There are ten ranks, and they ascend thus. The first, taken at the 'Ahd-e-Sharif, the noble covenant, is the Mustajib, the responder. Then the Momin, the believer. Then the Mukasir. Then the Ma'dhun, the licensed one — first mukasir, then ma'dhun. Then the Da'i, the summoner. Then the Hujjat, the proof. Then the Bab, the gate. And the eighth rank is that of the Imam, peace be upon him.

The rank of the Imam, the eighth, is described in terms of the Nasut-e-tabi'i: the Imam is the one possessed of Nasut-e-tabi'i who has attained the haykal through the Babul Abwab. When the Imam has received the imamate — when, that is, the bearer of Nasut-e-tabi'i has attained the haykal by way of the Babul Abwab — this rank is, in a sense, completed, and his further ranks now begin. For above the imamate there are higher offices still. After imamate comes wasayat, successorship; and above that is nutq-e-ilahi, divine speech. The ranks rise very high. When the imamate has its haykal, then divine speech must have its station above it; the gatherings of the Imams possess divine speech.

And who are the bearers of this divine speech? They are the three Ha's — three figures whose names begin with the letter Ha: Hunaid, Ibrahim, and Hashim. Sayyidna Imam Hunaid, peace be upon him; Sayyidna Ibrahim, peace be upon him; and Sayyidna Hashim. These three Ha's are the bearers of the nutq-e-ilahi. And the Prophet, peace be upon him, stands in the second rank in this regard. This compels a vital distinction: one must differentiate between divine speech, nutq-e-ilahi, and the Natiq as such. There is a Natiq, and there is a divine Natiq, and there is a difference between the two. Ibrahim belongs to the higher order: because the forms of the two eras were gathered in him, and because he is a luminous haykal raised above all the stations, he stands among the bearers of divine speech — the three Ha's — and is more than a Natiq merely; he is a divine Natiq.

### The Creation of the Intellects and the Three Gifts

Now we descend to the very root of the matter — the beginning, the mabda', which is the special opening subject of Mabda' wa Ma'ad. Allah Most High is the Creator, and from non-existence He brought things into existence. What did He first create? Not forms. He created the intellects, the uqul; the luminous specters, the ashbah nuraniyah; and the essences, the zawat. The word used here is zawat, essences — and ashbah nuraniyah, luminous specters; and uqul, intellects. The word nafs, soul, is not yet used at this stage at all. Intellects, luminous specters, essences — these were what He created.

And what did He then do? He bestowed three things upon these created intellects. First, He gave them life — hayat — that is, existence itself. Then, the knowledge, the ilm, of what powers reside within that life: first existence, then the knowledge of the faculties contained in it. Then, third, free will — ikhtiyar — the power of choice, the power to decide whether or not to make use of those faculties. These three things — life, knowledge, and free will — no one can take away. They are a gift from God: neither does the Creator take them back, nor can anyone else strip them away. They are present in every human being. It may be that for a time they lie dormant, unmanifested — that is a separate matter — but these three are within every person without exception: life, knowledge, and the freedom to use them or not.

### The Tenth Intellect, Its Delay, and Its Return

What happened next divides all of subsequent reality. Once endowed with free will, some of the intellects made use of it; and when they used it, they ascended and departed. But some intellects delayed in the use of their free will; they did not employ it. And who was the one who delayed? The tenth — the ashir. In the tenth intellect there arose a corrupt imagination, a wahm-e-fasid, and on account of it he delayed. By delaying, he underwent a transformation: he changed into the form of a nafs, a soul. From intellect he descended into soul. This is anchored in the Quranic address: `Ya ayyatuhan-nafsul-mutma'innah... irji'i` — "O tranquil soul... return." He changed from the form of intellect into the form of a nafs — a descent, a falling, a passage downward from one condition to another. In the realm of origination, the 'alam-e-ibda', no other attained this condition; the rest did not take on the form of a nafs at all. Indeed, the word nufus, souls, is not used for the others. From where, then, did the word nafs first come? From the ashir, the tenth — for it was he who, after his delay and his corrupt imagination, left the form of an intellect.

But the descent was not his final state. When the ashir later left the form of a nafs and re-entered the intellect, he became the 'Aql-e-Kulli, the Universal Intellect — the luminous intellect. He entered the realm and attained perfection: his own perfection, a perfection in which there is no flaw, after which there is no further descent and no further fall. That is finished. The word nafs, and the plural nufus, thus originated solely from the tenth intellect, the ashir. After this, the many forms of the nafs arise: one accepted the call late, another very late, another very late indeed, and according to the degree of their delay the various forms of the nafs were brought into being.

### The Nafse Kulli and the Souls of the Imams

Among these forms of soul, one is the Nafse Kulli, and the Nafse Kulli is the ashir himself — the Nafse Mutma'innah, the tranquil soul. As soon as the ashir realized his error, he immediately turned and changed; he is therefore called the Nafse Kulli, the Nafse Mutma'innah. And in like manner the souls of the Imams are also called Nufuse Kulli. The reason is this: when the call of the ashir went forth in that realm — and with the ashir were his deputies, so that the call of the two, the call of the qawaim, was raised — the souls of the Imams immediately accepted it. Because they answered at once, without delay, a station was established for them there, in the realm of life, the alam-e-hayat. Having gained a station in the realm of life through their immediate acceptance, they too are called Nafse Kulli.

### The Centers of the Three Souls and the Realm Without Dimension

From the Nafse Kulli a further unfolding proceeds, and the three souls must be located in their proper centers. From the Nafse Kulli there comes a soul, the Nafse Hissiyah, the sensory soul — but in the realm of life there are no dimensions; the Nafse Hissiyah does not exist there as such. The dimensions come afterward. It is here, in the dimensional world, that the Nafse Hissiyah is born — and it is born from the sun. For those who are under the sun, the term Nafse Hissiyah is applied; the center of the Nafse Hissiyah is the sun. The center of the Nafse Namiyah is the moon: the moon is the center of the growth-soul. And the Nafse Kulli — where is it? The Nafse Kulli is wholly in the realm of life, which is not in dimensions at all. The sun came into dimensions, entered the dimensional order; but the realm of life is dimensionless. So the Nafse Kulli is entirely in the realm of life: it encompasses all, and its flow and its effects reach down into the dimensional world here below. The Nufuse Kulli are all in the realm of life; here in the dimensional world are the Nufuse Hissiyah, the sensory souls; and below the moon are the Nufuse Namiyah, the growth-souls. So the three are ranged: the Kulli in the dimensionless realm of life, encompassing all and pouring its effects downward; the Hissiyah centered in the sun; the Namiyah centered in the moon.

### The Descent into Body and the Distances Within the One

When the nafs changes its form yet further and descends still more, falling lower, then the body, the jism, is born. And what was the body before? It was a nafs. And what was the nafs before? It was an intellect. The chain of descent has traveled a long way: intellect became soul, and soul became body. Consider the body as it now is, and how vast are the distances within this single thing. Your heart is a physical body; your feet are physical; the urinary and excretory tracts are physical too — all of them parts of the one body. Yet what an immense difference there is among them. On one side is the heart, on another the liver, on another this organ and that. In one and the same body all these things are present, and yet they are separated by an enormous gulf in dignity and station. Just so is the difference between the Nufuse Hissiyah and the Nufuse Kulli: as the parts of one body differ vastly though they belong to one frame, so the sensory souls and the universal souls differ vastly though all derive from the one descent.

### The Nafse Natiqa: The Believer's Own Acquisition

The Nafse Natiqa, the speaking or rational soul, also came from the ashir, but it must be understood with particular care, for it is the believer's own acquisition. The Nafse Natiqa is the spiritual form of knowledge and action. The essence, the zat, that you were given acquired knowledge and action — and this acquired form of knowledge and action is what is called the Nafse Natiqa. Here is the heart of it: the form of knowledge and action — who made this form? Your own essence made it. And whose creation is the essence? It is Allah's creation. But whose creation is the form? It is yours. And so one may say a remarkable thing: the thing made by us has surpassed the thing given to us by Allah. This is no impiety — it is precisely the point of the gift of free will. Allah gave you free will for exactly this reason: that you should create something by your own effort, and that the thing you create by your own effort should be, for you, the real thing, your genuine possession. Who gave the free will? Allah, the Creator. Who gave the underlying life, knowledge, and capacity for action? Allah, the Creator. But the form of knowledge and action raised upon that foundation is your own work, and it is yours.

And the diversity of bodies into which the descent issues is itself an ocean without measure: there is the human body, then the animal body, then those that lack vegetative growth, the nafs-e-numu, and on through countless other kinds of bodies — an ocean, beyond counting.

### The Non-Believer as a Fallen Form

From the order thus laid out, one may draw a sobering inference concerning the non-believer, the ghair-e-momin. A non-believer is a fallen physical form of a believer's body. He is one who did not attain the Nafse Natiqa. He possesses a soul — a nafs he has — but he does not possess a Nafse Natiqa. The meaning of Nafse Natiqa is the form of knowledge and action that is established through the 'Ahd-e-Sharif, the noble covenant, whose very starting-point is the 'Ahd-e-Sharif. That form is not present in the non-believer; lacking it, he is a non-believer — a fallen physical form, possessed of soul but bereft of the speaking soul that the covenant alone establishes.

### The Essence as Pure Intellect and the Reverse Order of Return

We may now answer a question that gathers the whole teaching together: what, in the end, is the essence — is it intellect, soul, or body? It is all intellect. The soul and the body are nothing but changed forms of the intellect. And from this it follows that the return journey reverses the order of descent. Just as the descent fell in sequence — the intellect came into being, then became soul, then became body — so the ascent must climb in the same sequence reversed. Because the body came last in the descent, the body goes first in the return; then, after the body, the soul departs; and so the ascent proceeds, unwinding the descent in reverse. The one who obtained his station first, who went up first, is the hero, the foremost — the one who departed first. He is the one who made the arrangement first and then left this realm; after him the others remained. There is a difference of precedence and succession among them, and nothing more than that: the one who set the order in place departed first, leaving this realm, and the rest followed in their turn.

### The Khameer-e-Nizamat and the Seed Planted a Thousand Years Before

This same principle of precedence and succession illuminates the meaning of the 'Ahd-e-Sharif and the khameer-e-nizamat, the leaven of the spiritual order. At the time of the 'Ahd-e-Sharif one receives the Nafse Natiqa — and this is, in truth, the receiving of one's own form. The khameer-e-nizamat that you yourself performed before is the very thing obtained by you at the moment of the 'Ahd-e-Sharif. The body of yours that appeared a thousand years ago planted a seed at that time; the one who came before has gone, has departed, but in departing he planted a seed — and you are now the one obtaining that very seed. At the noble covenant, the predecessor's leaven, sown a thousand years earlier, is at last gathered in by the successor who stands in his place. The seed planted by the one who left is harvested by the one who comes. This is the inner workmanship of the 'Ahd-e-Sharif. Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim. Allahumma salli ala Sayyidina Muhammad.

### The Four Ranks Convened in Ibrahim and the Bringing Forth of Isma'il

We resume with the text that opens the next chapter: `Al-fasl al-thalith min al-bab al-awwal min al-suradiq al-thalith. Fa-lamma ijtama'at al-arba' al-rutab fi Ibrahim, wa kana 'ala al-halat al-madhkurah min al-fadl wa al-sharaf, aqama da'watahu ila an istakhraja minha waladahu Isma'il 'alayhim al-salam. Wa qad kana wulida lahu Ishaq qabla mawlid Isma'il 'alayhima al-salam` — "And when the four ranks were gathered in Ibrahim, and he was in the aforementioned state of virtue and honor, he established his da'wat until he brought forth from it his son Isma'il, peace be upon them. And Ishaq had been born to him before the birth of Isma'il, peace be upon them both."

When the four ranks — nubuwwah, risalah, wasayah, and imamah — were gathered in Ibrahim, and he stood in that mentioned state of virtue and honor, of fadl and sharaf, he established his da'wat and labored within it until he extracted from it his son Isma'il, peace be upon them both. And it was the case that his son Ishaq had already been born to him before the birth of Isma'il. The text takes care to mark this: though Isma'il is the one drawn out as successor, Ishaq was the elder, born before him.

We may pause to make the meaning of "gathering the eras" concrete. Adam, peace be upon him, was the Natiq of his era; Nuh was the Natiq of his era; and when Ibrahim came, what did he do? He gathered the era of Adam and the era of Nuh together. To gather the eras means this: all the individuals who had been assembled in the era of Adam and the era of Nuh — all who had entered the haykal of the Natiq of their time — Ibrahim gathered all of them into himself. The forms of both eras converged in him.

### The Two Ibrahims

A clarification of names must be entered here to prevent confusion, for the Kalam-e-Pak speaks of more than one Ibrahim. There are, in fact, two Ibrahims. One Ibrahim is the Natiq, the one of whom it is said alladhi waffa, "who fulfilled his covenants." And there is another Ibrahim, concerning whom it is said `kana rajulun yuqalu lahu Ibrahim` — "there was a man called Ibrahim." This second Ibrahim was a prophet, a Nabi, the Nabi of his own time, but he is not the Natiq Ibrahim who fulfilled. The Ibrahim of our discourse, upon whom the four ranks were gathered and who gathered all the forms of the eras of Adam and Nuh, is the Ibrahim alladhi waffa, Ibrahim who fulfilled.

### Ibrahim's Rank Among the Khilafats and the Mutimmin

Within the world of the khilafats — the successorships further detailed in the text — Ibrahim holds a special rank. He stands among the three, the thalathaha: Maulana Hashim, Maulana Hunayn, and Maulana Ibrahim. This is a special khilafah, and among the khilafats of the Qa'im, peace be upon him, these hold a distinguished station. The wazir of the Qa'im is 'Ayn 'Azim; and those who take the khilafah of 'Ayn 'Azimah, the Mim, are Janab 'Abd al-Muttalib, then Janab Abu Talib, then the line of 'Abdullah, with conjunctions, qiran, arising among them. After them come the mutimmin, the completers, who each hold a special rank — the mutimmin of the Dawr-e-Muhammadi, the era of Muhammad, peace be upon him.

Particular distinction attaches to the seventh in each cycle, every seventh one. Who are these sevenths? Janab Maulana Muhammad bin Isma'il; then after him Janab Ma'add al-Mu'izz; and Imam al-Tayyib, peace be upon him; then the twenty-eighth, Maulana Ma'add ibn Husayn; then the twenty-second, Maulana Ahmad; then the forty-second, Maulana 'Ali; and the forty-ninth, Abu al-Qa'im, peace be upon him. The rank of these seven is very high. Yet Hazrat Nasrullah Sahib, may his secret be sanctified, used to teach that although the rank of these seven is high, the rank of the atimmah, the completers, of the dawr of the Prophet rose so high that a conjunction, a qiran, occurred between them and the sevenths — the two became equal, sometimes the one taking the khilafah, sometimes the other. The people of the first week, the first haftah, are of so lofty a rank that a qiran was produced between them and the seventh.

Among the atimmah, the highest rank is that of Maulana Muhammad bin Isma'il, with whom a personality of the order of Imam Husayn is gathered. His rank is very high, and for this reason he does not fall into the ordinary category; his rank is established in the kashf, in the dawr-e-kashf — a very high rank indeed. Concerning these, the words are spoken: `Qul huwa Allahu ahad, Allahu s-samad, lam yalid wa lam yulad` — "Say: He is Allah, the One; Allah, the Eternal Refuge; He neither begets nor is born." Among the seven, four are greater: Maulana Muhammad bin Isma'il, Imam al-Tayyib, Maulana Ahmad, and the coming Abu al-Qa'im. These four are the greatest. Each of the latter three gathered two ranks: Imam al-Tayyib gathered two, Maulana Ahmad gathered two, and the coming Abu al-Qa'im will likewise gather two; whereas Maulana Muhammad bin Isma'il gathered but one rank — and yet even so his rank is the most special among them, the rank of the master of the era of unveiling, sahib-e-dawr-e-kashf. The rank of the dawr-e-kashf is manifest in him. He bears the nutq-e-ilahi and holds the station of the seventh; in name he is Abu 'Ali, and it is by the name of our Qa'im, 'Ali, that he is so called, Abu 'Ali.

A point of doctrine on the ordering of the four ranks deserves mention. In most books the offices are listed as Nabuwwah, Risalah, Imamah, and Wisayah. But the author of al-Anwar — and al-Anwar is a foundational book, from which most of the du'at draw their proofs — has placed Wisayah before Imamah, bringing wasayah first and imamate after. And the question of which rank stands higher is settled accordingly: Wisayah is the higher. Wisayah is higher than Imamah.

### Isma'il the Wasi, Ishaq the Manifest Hujjah and Veil

We return to the text and follow the disposition Ibrahim made of his sons. When the four ranks were gathered in Ibrahim, and he was in that state of virtue and honor, he established his da'wat until he brought forth from it his son Isma'il, peace be upon them both — Ishaq having already been born before Isma'il. Ibrahim then established Maulana Isma'il as Wasi: he entrusted to him the rank of Wisayah and of Imamah, by the command of Allah, the Exalted — both wisayah and imamah, by Allah's command — because Ibrahim himself is a maqam ilahi, a divine station, and a haykal nurani, a luminous temple.

To his son Ishaq, by contrast, Ibrahim entrusted the ranks of Nabuwwah and Risalah, prophethood and messengership. And he made Ishaq a khadim, a servant, in the service of his brother Isma'il, peace be upon them all; he made Ishaq a hijab, a veil, over Isma'il; and he made him a da'i, a summoner, calling toward Isma'il. Moreover, Ibrahim enjoined upon Ishaq that he should charge his own son to know the station of Isma'il and of all his progeny — that the preceding should enjoin the succeeding, one generation charging the next, until the time of the rising of their Qa'im.

Then Ibrahim passed away to the abode of Allah's generosity. And his son Isma'il stood after him as a Wasi in his station, but concealed, mustatiran; while Ishaq stood as his manifest hujjah, his zahir proof, and as a veil, until the passing of Isma'il drew near. Then Isma'il entrusted to his son Qaydar the Imamah and the Wisayah — both ranks together — and this continued in succession in the progeny of Qaydar, transmitted from father to son. And Ishaq entrusted to his son Ya'qub his own rank, the rank of the du'at; and this rank continued to be transferred among them until the Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il at last received it.

### Why Lut Is Not Mentioned: The Two Lines of Istiqrar and Istida'

A pointed question must be addressed: why is Lut not mentioned in this chain? The answer lies in the fundamental division between two lines. Isma'il, peace be upon him, and his progeny are maqamat ilahiyyah, divine stations, possessors of luminous temples, dhu hayakil nuraniyyah, because they are ahl al-istiqrar, the people of permanence, of settled establishment. Ishaq and his progeny, on the other hand, are hujaj and du'at zahirun — proofs and manifest summoners — manifest by way of Isma'il and his progeny, and veils, hujub, upon them, made manifest according to what the confrontations necessitated and what the times and the periods decreed.

So the first of the du'at and the veils was Ishaq, and after him Ya'qub, and after him Yusuf; and the line of entrustment passes through Lawa, and Yahuda ibn Lawa, and Ayyub ibn 'Amus, and Zariq, and Yunus ibn Ayyub, and Shu'ayb ibn Safwan, and Musa ibn 'Imran, and Harun, and Yusha' ibn Nun, and Finhas ibn Harun, and Ilyas, and al-Yasa' ibn Akhtub, and Shamwil ibn Hali, and Dawud ibn 'Asa, and Sulayman, and Ash'iya ibn Anzaya, and 'Uzayr ibn Subaya, and Zakariyya, and his son Yahya, and Zinayal, and Janab 'Isa, peace be upon him. And after 'Isa it passes to Sham'un, and 'Abd al-Masih, and Marwat al-Rahib, and Sarjis — who is Bahira al-Rahib. These are the ahl al-istida', the people of entrustment, who are the du'at and the hujaj; and though they are of various lineages and relationships, all of them trace their lineage back to Ishaq, peace be upon them all — for every mustawda', every one entrusted, is traced back to Ishaq.

This line of nubuwwah ran through them: in the era of 'Isa it had reached Bahira the monk, and then again, after the Prophet Muhammad al-Mustafa, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, was made manifest, this rank of nubuwwah became submerged within the Prophet himself. Before that, nubuwwah was with them; when the Prophet was manifested, a rank of nubuwwah was then taken up by Ubayy ibn Ka'b.

### The Manifestation of the Prophet and the Two Lines United

`Al-fasl al-rabi' min al-bab al-awwal.` The Prophet, peace be upon him, was manifested from Janab 'Abd al-Muttalib, who was the bearer of the ranks of Imamah and Wisayah. From him the Prophet was made manifest. The distinction of the Prophet is great. Janab Hashim — Hashim al-sarid, "he who crumbled bread for the poor" — gathered the ranks and bestowed them in full upon Janab 'Abd al-Muttalib; he gave him all four ranks completely. And what did 'Abd al-Muttalib do? He divided the gathered offices into two parts: one part he gave to Janab 'Abdullah, and the second part to Janab Abu Talib. To Janab 'Abdullah came Nabuwwah, Risalah, Imamah, and Wisayah; and when 'Abdullah passed away, all those ranks were gathered in Janab Abu Talib. When the four ranks were gathered in Abu Talib, he too divided them: he gave the part of Nabuwwah and Risalah to the Prophet, peace be upon him, and the part of Imamah and Wisayah to Janab 'Ali. But because they were young, all the ranks of Imamah and Wisayah were also gathered, in effect, in the Prophet — through the kafalat-e-'uzma, the great guardianship. The blessing of the great guardianship was obtained, and its effect was so powerful that all the mustawda'in, and even the mustaqarrin who were with 'Ali, were overshadowed by him. For at the point where Hashim's da'wat was accepted, when the da'wat of 'Ayn occurred, the very first to affirm it were the Mim; and it is recounted, in a vivid and dramatic manner, how the Mim displayed khuzu' and khushu', humility and submissiveness, at that moment.

The two lines now meet. The chain of the Ambiya and the Rusul runs toward Ishaq. The text says: `hadhihi al-rutbah allati hiya rutbat al-du'at ila an tasallamaha qa'im walad Isma'il` — "this rank, which is the rank of the du'at, until the Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il received it." The Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il is Janab 'Ali, and the Qa'im of the progeny of Ishaq is the Prophet. The succession of the du'at, the line of Ishaq, came to rest with the Prophet; and when the Prophet is gathered with 'Ayn 'Azima, then he will gather them all.

### The Chain of the Divine Stations from Isma'il to Musa

`Al-fasl al-rabi' min al-bab al-awwal min al-suradiq al-thalith.` The first of the A'immah, the divine stations, the maqamat ilahiyyah, after Ibrahim, peace be upon him, was Isma'il ibn Ibrahim. Ibrahim entrusted it to his son Qaydar, and Qaydar to his son Nabat, and so the entrustment descends through a long chain of the divine stations — Sa'i, Ma'dhar, Mahshar, Kumayr, Nazal, Hizan, Dariz, 'Amamah, Badwan, Ra'ib, Mahmud, al-Dada', 'Akhut (said to be 'Unuq), Qaswar, Tamah (said to be Tabikh), Mat'am, Isma'il dhi al-'Awaj, Barat, Isma'il dhi al-Mutani', and on to his son Ibrahim who is Jami' al-Shaml, the gatherer of the assembly. This Ibrahim is yet a third, distinct from the two named before — an Ibrahim who appears among the A'immah, named Jami' al-Shaml. The chain continues through 'Antar, Zurayb, Shahdud, Muhsin, 'Illah, 'Aqba, Ra'imah, Muhtamil (said to be Mutahalhil), 'Awwam, Mahlam, Shahib, Amin, and 'Udad — until it reaches 'Udad ibn 'Udad, who is famous, and whose mutimm, whose completer, is Janab Musa, peace be upon him; the muqim, the one who establishes, is Musa. Between Ibrahim and Musa, then, there are a great many A'immah.

When the time became binding for the manifestation of a Natiq, Janab 'Udad established from among his du'at a Natiq — and that Natiq is Musa, peace be upon him. Then he entrusted it to his son 'Adnan, and 'Adnan to Ma'add, and Ma'add to his son Nizar, and Nizar to Mudar, and Mudar to his son Ilyas, and Ilyas to his son Mudrikah, and Mudrikah to his son Khuzaymah. And again the time became binding for the manifestation of a Natiq, so the Imam commanded his da'i to establish 'Isa, peace be upon him — for the manifestation of 'Isa.

A word should be entered here on the muqims, the establishers, of the great Natiqs, for these are the Imams who stand behind each Speaker. The muqim of Janab Adam is Maulana Hunayn. The muqim of Janab Nuh is Maulana Hud, peace be upon him — and from Hud the lineage runs, as it is said, `Min Hud Muhammadun`, "From Hud is Muhammad." The muqim of Janab Ibrahim is Maulana Salih. The muqim of Janab Musa is Janab 'Udad ibn 'Udad. And the muqim of Janab 'Isa is Janab Khuzaymah. These muqims, Hud and the rest, are themselves A'immah, divine stations standing behind their Natiqs.

Then Khuzaymah entrusted it to his son Kinanah, and Kinanah to Nadr, and Nadr to Malik, and Malik to Fihr, and Fihr to Ghalib, and Ghalib to Lu'ayy, and Lu'ayy to Ka'b, and Ka'b to Murrah, and Murrah to Kilab, and Kilab to Qusayy, and Qusayy to 'Abd Manaf, and 'Abd Manaf to Hashim, and Hashim to his son Maulana 'Abd al-Muttalib. And with 'Abd al-Muttalib the four ranks were gathered — the same four that had been gathered with his ancestor Ibrahim: Wisayah, Imamah, Nabuwwah, and Risalah. The ranks below these belong to the maqamat ilahi and are a different matter; the present account is clarifying the khilafats, the line of the nutq-e-ilahi.

### The Reserved Portions and the Conjunction of the Sevenths with the Qa'im

`Al-fasl al-khamis min al-bab al-awwal min al-suradiq.` It is stated here that for all the Imams of the dawr-e-kashf there is a Qa'im who manifests after a thousand years — every thousand years, on the seventh, one manifests who is equal to one Imam; and after seven thousand years one manifests who gathers all the thousands. The Qa'im, the Imam who manifests after seven thousand years, and the thalathaha sukra, the three intoxicated ones, are in qiran, in conjunction — meaning that the ranks of the dawr-e-sidq are exceedingly high. This conjunction takes place when the coming Qa'im of the era will also gather the dawr-e-kashf. First he will gather his own dawr-e-sidq; then he will gather the dawr-e-kashf as well, the era of unveiling that came before; and when he gathers the dawr-e-kashf, then in that gathering, in his haykal, the two will be peers — the seventh and the Qa'im of the seven thousand years standing as equals.

The text records the disposition of the four ranks among the lines of Isma'il and Ishaq: `Wa lamma ijtama'at hadhihi al-rutub al-arba' 'inda 'Abd al-Muttalib, kanat al-shintan minhunna mudkharatayn li-qa'im walad Isma'il, wa huma al-wisayah wa al-imamah. Wa al-shintan li-qa'im walad Ishaq, wa huma al-nabuwwah wa al-risalah` — "And when these four ranks were gathered with 'Abd al-Muttalib, two portions of them were reserved for the Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il, and they are the Wisayah and the Imamah; and two portions were for the Qa'im of the progeny of Ishaq, and they are the Nabuwwah and the Risalah."

When the four ranks were gathered with Maulana 'Abd al-Muttalib, two of them were reserved — mudkharatayn, stored up and held in joined safekeeping — for the Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il, and those two are Wisayah and Imamah. This carries a doctrine of its own: Imamah and Wisayah will under no circumstances be separated; they remain together, never parted in any place. The remaining two, Nabuwwah and Risalah, were reserved for the Qa'im of the progeny of Ishaq.

### The Luminous Temple and the Condition of the Father

`Fa-kana min 'adl Allahi ta'ala wa fadlihi 'ala Muhammadin wa sharafihi, salawat Allahi 'alayhi, an yakuna maqaman ilahiyyan wa haykalan nuraniyyan. Wa lan yakuna dhalika illa li-man yakunu abuhu kadhalika haykalan nuraniyyan. Wa illa fa-la sabil ila dhalik` — "So it was from the justice of Allah the Exalted and His grace upon Muhammad, peace be upon him, and his honor, the blessings of Allah be upon him, that he should be a divine station and a luminous temple. And that will not be except for one whose father is likewise a luminous temple. Otherwise, there is no way to it."

From the justice of Allah, and from His grace and honoring upon Muhammad, peace be upon him, it was decreed that the Prophet should become a maqam ilahi, a divine station, and a haykal nurani, a luminous temple. And this could not be except for one whose father is likewise a haykal nurani; if it were not so, there would be no path to it at all. The meaning is precise: Maulana Muhammad is a haykal nurani for the very reason that Maulana 'Abdullah is a haykal nurani. By the ordinary reckoning, it was necessary for the Prophet to gather only the ranks of Nabuwwah and Risalah. But because of the great guardianship, the kafalah, that Janab 'Abd al-Muttalib entrusted to him, the Prophet — though the bearer of Nabuwwah and Risalah — achieved superiority over all the ranks that had been gathered with Janab 'Ali in the spiritual world. He became the deputy of 'Ayn; after 'Ayn his rank stood established. And why did he attain that rank? Because he was a haykal nurani — his father being Janab 'Abdullah, and 'Abdullah's father Janab 'Abd al-Muttalib.

This discloses a new perspective, one not set out in this way in other books: that the condition of being a haykal nurani must hold for the father, and indeed for the grandfather. Yet a qualification must be added at once. This condition does not entail that only one son of a father can be an Imam; two children of a single father can both be Imams, both haykal nurani — as from Maulana 'Abd al-Muttalib two children became Imams, luminous temples. In the mustawda' line there is one Imam, and in the mustaqarr line another. After 'Abd al-Muttalib, it is not required of any Imam that only one of his sons be an Imam; both sons may be Imams. And so it follows that Maulana 'Abdullah is himself a haykal nurani — for only then can Maulana Muhammad be called a haykal nurani — and along with Maulana 'Abdullah, Maulana Abu Talib too is a haykal nurani.

The special quality of 'Ayn 'Azima is what crowns this. When the hijab of 'Ayn 'Azima was manifested, 'Ali said: `ana 'abdun min 'abid Muhammad` — "I am a slave from among the slaves of Muhammad." So lofty is the station of the awaited one, the one for whom all wait, that on account of it the Prophet not only obtained all the ranks of Nabuwwah and Risalah and entered upon their ten ranks, but in addition gathered to himself the Imamah and all the Imams and all the Wasis who were gathered with Janab 'Ali — every rank save the rank of 'Ali himself. No rank of the Qa'im remained outside him, save three: `illa thalathah sukra`, except the three intoxicated ones. And even these three intoxicated ones have gone to the Qa'im, with a connection still subsisting — `qaba qawsayn aw adna`, "at a distance of two bow-lengths or nearer." Who is the adna, the nearer? Janab 'Abdullah. And the qaba qawsayn, the two bow-lengths? Janab 'Abd al-Muttalib and Janab Abu Talib, with 'Abdullah as the adna.

This is a new point, not mentioned in other books — that the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, cannot be a haykal nurani unless his father is a haykal nurani. The reason is that here there is no istida'iyyat, no entrustment-relation; here there is fawqiyyat, superiority, over all the Imams — and this by virtue of the kafalah, the guardianship. Were it a matter of mere entrustment, the case would differ. It is a condition set within the madarij, the spiritual degrees: if Janab 'Abdullah is an Imam and a haykal nurani, then the rank of Muhammad cannot be conferred upon any son who is not himself the son of a haykal nurani; it cannot be given to anyone else. The imamates differ in their kind and their rank — as with Hasan and Husayn, and as with Dawud and Sulayman, where among the prophets there is also imamate.

### The Two Gates of 'Abd al-Muttalib and the Order of the Bab al-Abwab

`Fa-kana min 'Abd al-Muttalib, salamu Allahi 'alayhi, an nasaba lahu babayn yatasallamani al-suwar al-muntaqilah al-murtaqiyah min ahl da'watihi, wa ja'ala ahadahuma a'la min al-akhar. Fa-kana al-'ali mujma'an li-suwar al-mu'minin al-bulagha' wa al-hudud al-fudala'. Wa al-thani mujma'an li-suwar al-mustajibin alladhina hadduhum akhdh al-'ahd wa al-wila, al-mustaqimin 'alayhi la ghayr` — "So it was from 'Abd al-Muttalib, peace of Allah be upon him, that he set up for himself two gates to receive the transferred, ascending forms from the people of his da'wat, and he made one of them higher than the other. So the higher was a congregation for the forms of the eloquent believers and the virtuous Hudud, and the second was a congregation for the forms of the respondents whose limit was the taking of the covenant and allegiance, remaining steadfast upon it and nothing more."

Maulana 'Abd al-Muttalib set up for himself two gates, two abwab, to receive the transferred and ascending forms from the people of his da'wat, making one of the two higher than the other. The higher gate was a gathering-place for the forms of the eloquent, accomplished believers and the virtuous hudud; the second was a gathering-place for the forms of the respondents, the mustajibin, whose station went no further than the taking of the covenant and the allegiance, and remaining steadfast upon it.

The arrangement of the bab al-abwab through the generations is precise, and it must be traced with care, distinguishing always the bab al-abwab from the qalbe haykal. For Janab Hashim, the bab al-abwab was his wife, Maulana Fatimah bint 'Amr; the gathered forms became submerged in his son, Janab 'Abd al-Muttalib. Janab 'Abd al-Muttalib then established a bab — his own wife, Fatimah bint 'Amr, as bab al-abwab — and the forms became submerged in his son Maulana Abu Talib. The second bab he established was 'Abdullah ibn Saba; through him the hearing, the istima', took place, and it gathered in Janab 'Abdullah. Janab Abu Talib appointed as his bab al-abwab his wife, Janab Fatimah bint Asad, and she became submerged in the qalbe haykal of Janab 'Ali. Janab 'Ali appointed as his bab al-abwab Janab Salman-e-Farsi, who would become the qalbe haykal for Imam Husayn, peace be upon him.

Here a distinction must be held firmly in mind: the bab al-abwab of the Imam and the qalbe haykal are not the same, and there is a difference between the two that should never be confused. The Prophet, peace be upon him, appointed as bab al-abwab for Janab Fatimah none but Janab Khadijah, who became submerged in Janab Fatimah. And Janab 'Abdullah appointed as bab al-abwab Janab Aminah, who became submerged in Janab Rasulullah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace. This must be understood: the Imam has a bab al-abwab who, by the gathering of the forms, becomes the qalbe haykal for the son who will be the next successor. To become qalbe haykal is one thing; to be bab al-abwab is another. The Imam appoints his wife as bab al-abwab, and she becomes submerged in his son; but through the gathering of the hearing she becomes the qalbe haykal.

### The Four Fatimahs of the Ya Fateru Prayer

This order of the four gates is mirrored, and made an object of devotion, in the Ya Fateru prayer that we recite. There are four invocations of Ya Fateru — four Fateru. One is in the sajdah, the prostration, a hundred times. Then upon the right cheek, a hundred times. Then upon the left cheek, a hundred times. Then again in the sajdah, a hundred and ten times. The first sajdah is for Janab Fatimah al-Zahra. The Prophet has said, "The believer is closest to Allah the Exalted when he is in sajdah." When the haykal is prepared together with the bab al-abwab, at that moment the spiritual realities stand closest to Allah within the da'wat; the worshipper is in sajdah. So the first sajdah belongs to Janab Fatimah al-Zahra, peace be upon her.

The sajdah-station upon the right cheek belongs to Janab Fatimah bint Asad, who was the mother of Maulana 'Ali and the bab al-abwab of Janab Abu Talib — the hujjah of 'Ayn 'Azima, the bab whose submersion is in the Mim; this is the right cheek. The third, upon the left, is Fatimah bint 'Amr, the wife of Janab 'Abd al-Muttalib and the mother of Janab Abu Talib. And the fourth sajdah, whose rank is given as a hundred and ten, belongs to the Umm al-Qa'im, the mother of the Qa'im — also the wife of the Qa'im, the Zawjat al-Qa'im, peace be upon him, whose name too is Fatimah. Her invocation is a hundred and ten times, indicating that she will be superior to the ten ranks. She will be superior to these three Fatimahs of the cheeks — yet Janab Fatimah al-Zahra is superior to all.

### The Seven Limbs of the Sajdah and the Completion of the Order

A final teaching crowns the account, drawn from the discipline of the prostration itself. In the sajdah, seven parts of the body must touch the ground. What are these seven parts? The forehead and the nose, counted together as one face; the two hands; the two knees; and the toes of the two feet. Taking the face as one, the seven are: the face, the two hands, the two knees, and the two feet. If a man is very old and cannot bring all seven to the ground, then the seven do not touch — and the rank is diminished accordingly.

The inner meaning is this. Where all seven limbs touch the ground in the sajdah, there the hudud — the ranks of the da'wat belonging to Janab Fatimah and to this other Fatimah, to Fatimah 'Ayn Ula and Fatimah 'Ayn Ukhra — are complete; the whole order of the da'wat is fulfilled, with seven of their jawarih, their limbs, present and prostrate. But upon the cheek, where only the cheek is touching and not the full seven, the rank is less. The full sajdah, with its seven limbs, marks the complete and highest order; the prostration of the cheek alone marks a lesser station. So the gradations of the four Fatimahs, and through them the whole architecture of the gates and the gathering of forms, are inscribed in the very members of the body bowed in prayer — the completed sajdah crowning the order, the cheek marking the rank that falls below it.
