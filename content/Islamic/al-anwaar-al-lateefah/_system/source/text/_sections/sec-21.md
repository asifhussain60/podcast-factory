## The Hudud, the Rock, and the Sacred House

### The Ascending Return of the Followers

The fifth chapter of the *Futuh* opens with a sentence about a particular class of souls — not the great chiefs of misguidance, but the ordinary followers who stand behind them. The text reads `fa amma'l-atba'u min al-umud wa'l-ra'aya` (as for the followers from the pillars and the subjects). The word `atba'` means simply "followers," those who accept and walk behind another, the people who attach themselves to a cause without themselves originating it. Among these followers are two ranks: the `umud`, the pillars who support a structure, and the `ra'aya`, the common subjects who live beneath it. The Master tells us what becomes of them on the long arc of their return: `fa minhum man ya'udu min ba'di'l-adrak wa minhum man yukmiluha` (and of them are those who return after the perception-levels, and of them are those who complete them). Some of these followers turn back after passing through the levels of perception, while others carry those perceptions through to completion. Their fate is not arbitrary. It is measured precisely: `wa 'ala qadri jiddiyati kulli wahidin minhum li ahli'l-haqq wa qadri a'malihi yakunu nayluhu min al-'adhab` (and according to the measure of each one's earnestness toward the people of truth, and according to the measure of his actions, is his attainment of punishment). The earnestness a follower showed toward the people of truth, and the deeds he performed, together fix the exact share of punishment he must receive. This is the doctrine that governs the entire passage that follows: the soul's journey is a ledger, and what it suffers or escapes is the precise reckoning of how it inclined toward the truth and how it acted.

When that reckoning has run its course and the soul has been emptied of its debt, the return begins to climb. The text describes this turning point: `fa idha istakbala man yustakbalu bihi minhum al-ruju'u sa'idan li yubtada'a bihi fi anwa'i'l-ma'adin al-nafi'ati li'l-bashar` (so when the ascending return is faced by those of them for whom it is faced, it is initiated in the kinds of minerals beneficial to mankind). The phrasing is careful: the return becomes possible only for those whose business of punishment is finished, those for whom it has now been written that they may climb. And the climb does not begin in some lofty station. It begins at the very bottom of the mineral world — but at the *good* bottom, in the kinds of minerals that are beneficial and lawful, the wholesome minerals rather than the harmful ones. From the minerals the ascent continues into the vegetable kingdom: `thumma fi anwa'i'l-nabat alladhi huwa ghayru mahzur` (then in the kinds of plants which are not forbidden). The ascending soul enters only those plants that are not prohibited, the lawful and good growth from which a person may eat without sin.

Here lies the great contrast that defines the whole architecture of return and descent. While *descending* — that is, while falling into punishment — the soul passes through bad bodies, foul and harmful plants and unclean forms. But while *ascending*, the path is paved entirely with good things: the beneficial mineral, the lawful plant, the permitted animal. The two journeys traverse the same kingdoms of nature, but the descending soul touches their corrupt forms and the ascending soul touches their wholesome forms. The way up is a good road, the inverse and the redemption of the bad bodies entered on the way down.

A necessary clarification belongs here, because the reader may wonder whether the ordinary law applies to a soul caught in this machinery of descent and punishment. It does not. Upon those who are falling and undergoing punishment, none of the rulings of the Shari'ah apply. The Shari'ah and its obligations bind only a person of intellect — a responsible, rational human being. They do not bind animals, and they do not bind a soul that is presently inside the period of `qisas`, the period of retribution in which an old debt is being paid out. Such a soul is not in a condition of moral agency where commands and prohibitions could be addressed to it; it is simply discharging a sentence. There is no fresh "grasp" or acquisition for it during this time, no new accumulation of merit or fault. It is in the period of `qisas`, retribution is being taken from it, and only when that punishment is complete will the return begin — `illa ma sha' Allah` (except what Allah wills). What appears, during punishment, to be a further wrong is not a new sin incurring a new penalty; it is the working-out of the old retribution itself.

The ascent then reaches the third kingdom: `thumma fi anwa'i'l-hayawan al-muhallalah` (then in the kinds of permitted animals). The soul now enters the lawful animals, and a precise mechanism is described: `thumma yasta'miluhu man yajibu lahu isti'maluhu min waliyyin wa ziddin fa yakunu lahu ma'baran hatta yakhruja fi'l-khama al-alfiyyah wa yu'radu 'alayhi'l-balaya` (then he is used by whoever must use him, whether a friend or an adversary, so that he becomes a passage for him until he emerges in the fifth millennium, and tribulations are presented to him). The lawful animal is used by whoever is obliged to use it — and this user may be a `wali`, a friend and ally of the truth, or a `zidd`, an adversary. The animal becomes a `ma'bar`, a passage or crossing-place, through which the soul travels until it emerges as a human being in the fifth millennium, the era in which the tribulations of the call will be set before it. The reason the user may be friend or adversary is itself instructive about the soul's standing: a soul brought into the lawful animals on account of some of its good actions, but whose punishments are not yet wholly complete, passes through an adversary; a soul whose punishments are complete passes through a friend. This is a natural process, an orderly grading of the soul's emergence according to how much of its debt remains. The animal is the crossing; the human form in the fifth millennium is the far bank.

And on that far bank, the soul is met by the call. `wa yu'radu 'alayhi'l-balaya` (and tribulations are presented to him): the tribulations are presented by none other than the Imam, peace be upon him, whose invitation is established throughout the universe. He presents his call in his various ways — a great and wondrous invitation. The soul is not lectured on the whole of its metaphysical origin; it does not need to be made to understand the entire doctrine of its beginning and end. It is simply summoned toward the truth, called by some appropriate method to accept what it once denied. This call is precisely for the returning soul, the one who is being given a fresh chance to incline toward the truth. The tribulation it faces is the presentation of the `wilayah` it had once rejected: `li man kana ankara min al-hudud` (for the one who had denied of the hudud) — the soul is now shown the very `wilayah`, the spiritual authority, of the `hadd` whom it had once denied.

What follows is the great fork in the road. `fa in qabila naja wa dakhala fi jumlati ahli'l-haqq` (and if he accepts, he is saved and enters into the company of the people of truth). If the soul accepts that authority, it finds salvation and is admitted into the company of the people of truth. The Master describes this acceptance with a luminous image: it is a clean slate. Once the soul accepts and the matter is fixed, it is as though water has been poured over a written tablet, washing it clean, so that whatever one wishes may now be written upon it. Every prior thing is finished and erased; faith is presented anew, and if the soul accepts, it arrives at its first state — the state of `kamal-e-awwal`, the first perfection, the unblemished original condition in which it first stood before the truth. This is the highest mercy of the whole system: that the returning soul is not merely paroled but restored, given back the spotless tablet of its beginning.

But the fork has another arm. `wa in aba wa'stakbara 'ada saqitan ka ma sabaqa min qawlihi fi asnafi'l-'adhab` (and if he refuses and grows arrogant, he returns falling, as has already been stated concerning the kinds of punishment). If the soul refuses and shows arrogance, it does not stay where it is — it turns back and *falls*, descending once again into the classes of punishment already described. And the Master fixes the duration of this whole alternating process of ascent and fall: `wa 'ala dhalika yakun munha ila qiyami'l-qa'im 'alayhi'l-salam` (and upon that will its course continue until the rising of the Qaim, peace be upon him). This cycle of offered salvation and arrogant refusal continues, soul after soul and turn after turn, until the rising of the Qaim. With his rising, the entire economy of return is brought to its consummation.

### The Universal Gathering Before the Qaim

When the rising of the Qaim draws near, the whole of creation is set in motion toward him. The text marks the moment: `fa idha ana zuhuruhu wa qiyamuhu zaharat bi zuhurihi jamee'u'l-khala'iq min haddi Adama 'alayhi's-salam ila 'ahdi dawrihi 'alayhi's-salam` (so when his appearance and his rising draw near, all of creation appears with his appearance, from the era of Adam, peace be upon him, to the era of his own cycle). The verb `ana` here carries the sense of drawing near, of a thing whose time has come. As the Qaim's appearance approaches, there manifests along with it the whole of creation — every being from the age of Adam, peace be upon him, down to the era of the Qaim's own cycle. No one is left behind. As the text insists: `fa la yabqa ahadun min jamee'i atwari'l-mustafa min muti'in wa 'asin illa zahara ba'da yasiruhu ila'l-ard thumma ila'l-mizaj wa'l-muzdajat thumma fi'l-aghdhiyati wa zuhuruhum nuqafan wa'taraqqihim fi'l-maratib ila an yuzhiru shakhsan 'ala halatihim al-ula al-mad'uwwah ila'l-haqq` (so no one remains from all the stages of the Chosen One, neither obedient nor sinner, except that he appears after his journey to the earth, then to temperament and compounding, then in the foods, and his appearance as seminal drops, and their ascent through the ranks, until he appears as a person upon his first invited state — invited to the truth).

This is the full sequence of every soul's reappearance, traced step by step. There is no one — not from any of the stages of the Chosen One, whether that soul was obedient or a sinner — who does not reappear. Each first journeys to the earth, then enters the realm of temperament (`mizaj`) and compounding (`muzdajat`), then passes into the foods, then appears as the seminal drops (`nutfah`), and so ascends through the ranks until at last it stands forth as a full human person. And the person it becomes is not arbitrary: it appears upon its "first invited state," the precise condition it was in when it was first called to the truth. The phrase `al-mad'uwwah ila'l-haqq` — the one invited to the truth — fixes the reference point. Every believer and non-believer, every soul without exception, will be summoned and made to reappear, and the form of that reappearance is the form it bore at the moment of its first invitation.

The Master makes the exactness of this restoration vivid: `wa hum 'ala silsilah halatihim min shaykhukhatin wa shababiyyatin wa sughurin wa kuburin wa sababin wa inkarin wa dhalika kulluhu 'ala halatihim al-ula allati du'iya fiha` (and they are upon the chain of their states — of old age, youth, smallness, bigness, acceptance, and denial — and all of that is upon their first state in which they were invited). Each soul is restored upon the very condition in which it was first invited: if it was an old man then, it returns as an old man; if a youth, a youth; if a child, a child; whether it had accepted or denied, in that same posture it returns. The text presses the point even into the matter of the body's surface: `la yutraku 'ala halatin saghiran wa la kabiran ... wa la fi shay'in min al-alwan allati hiya abyadu wa zurqatun wa sukhumatun wa sawadun, sawa'un bi sawa'in` (he is not left in a state, small or large ... nor in anything of the colors, whether white, or blue, or dusky-brown, or black — exactly equal to what it was). Not the size of the body, not even the color of the skin, is altered. White, blue, dusky, black — whatever the soul's complexion was at the moment of its first call, in exactly that complexion it stands forth again, equal measure for measure. The restoration is total and faithful to the smallest particular.

Once the souls have all reappeared, each in his original form, the hierarchy of the truth is itself made manifest among them. `thumma yashkhasu kullu nabiyyin wa wasiyyin wa imamin wa haddin li ahli 'asrihi` (then every prophet, successor, imam, and hadd is personified for the people of his era). Every prophet (`nabi`), every successor (`wasi`), every imam, and every `hadd` is made to stand forth in person — individualized and embodied — for the people of his own time, so that each rank of the truth confronts the people who lived under its dispensation. This personification is grounded in scripture. The Master cites the word of the All-Hearing, All-Knowing Master: `wa ji'a bi'l-nabiyyin wa'l-shuhada' wa qudiya baynahum bi'l-haqqi wa hum la yuzlamun` (and the prophets and the witnesses will be brought, and it will be judged between them with truth, and they will not be wronged). The prophets and the witnesses are brought forth, and judgment is rendered among all the gathered with perfect truth, and none is wronged. The crucial point of interpretation lies in the word `shuhada'`, "witnesses." This must not be read in its ordinary, literal sense of someone who merely testifies. Here every `maqam` — every station of the hierarchy — is appointed as a witness over the people of its own time, a witness to their deeds, whether good or evil. The `shahid` is therefore a `salih`, a righteous one of a higher rank, set over the people of an era to witness their actions. It is not a courtroom witness in the common meaning but a station of authority that observes and attests. And the judgment passed "between them" is not a dispute between the prophets and the witnesses — for they are present precisely *as* witnesses, not as litigants — but a judgment rendered, by means of their witnessing, among all those gathered: the prophets, the witnesses, and the whole assembled creation. Allah brings the prophets and witnesses and judges between the gathered souls with truth, and they are not wronged.

A second verse confirms the universality of the gathering. The Master cites it as a narration of their assembling: `qul inna'l-awwalina wa'l-akhirin la majmu'una ila miqati yawmin ma'lum` (Say: indeed the former and the latter will surely be gathered for the appointed time of a known day). The first and the last alike — the whole sweep of creation across all eras — will certainly be gathered at the appointed time, the `miqat`, of a known day. This is the scriptural seal upon the doctrine of total reappearance.

And the gathering is not passive. Each station of the hierarchy actively confronts the people of its time. `thumma inna kulla wahidin min maqamat wa'l-hudud yunafishu ahla 'asrihi'l-qa'im fihim wa'l-mab'uth ilayhim wa yuwafiquhum 'ala ma kana minhu wa minhum min takdhibihim lahu wa 'inadihim wa mu'adatatihim wa 'ala jami'i af'alihim wa aqwalihim qalilatiha wa kathiratiha` (then indeed each one of the stations and the hudud disputes with the people of his era — the Qaim being among them and the one sent to them — and confronts them concerning all that passed between him and them, of their belying him, their stubbornness, and their hostility, and concerning all their actions and sayings, the small and the great of them). Every `maqam` of the imams and every `hadd` enters into `munafashah` — a searching dispute, an admonition and contention — with the people of his age. He brings them to a standstill and makes them face everything that passed between them: the dealings, the persuasion that was offered and refused, their belying of the Imam, their denial, their hostility and enmity, and the whole record of their deeds and words, the trivial alongside the grave. Nothing is omitted from this confrontation; the small and the great of their actions are all laid before them.

### The Plain of Resurrection: Ma'rib and the Released Opposites

Where does this universal gathering physically occur? The Master is precise: `fa yakunu ijtima'uhum fi 'arasati'l-qiyamah wa huwa khatt al-istiwa' al-gharbi alladhi huwa ard al-ma'arib li anna qiyam al-qa'im 'alayhi'l-salam yakunu hunalik` (so their gathering is in the plains of resurrection, which is the western line of the equator, the land of Ma'rib, because the rising of the Qaim, peace be upon him, will be there). The gathering of all the souls takes place in the `'arasat al-qiyamah`, the open plains of the resurrection, and these plains are identified with the western line of the equator — the `khatt al-istiwa al-gharbi` — which is the land of Ma'rib. The reason for this location is that the rising of the Qaim, peace be upon him, will itself be there; the place of his rising is the place of the gathering.

But this raises an obvious difficulty: how could the entire population of all the ages possibly fit into a single region? The text answers by limiting who is gathered. `wa ya'tuna jami'u'l-khala'iq ilayhi min jami'i'l-jaza'ir yahra'un la yabqa minhum saghiratun wa la kabiratun illa yadiqu bihi mawdi'uhu alladhi huwa fihi wa watanuhu alladhi rubbiya fihi` (and all the creatures come to him from all the islands, rushing; none of them, small or great, remains except that his place in which he is becomes constricted for him, and his homeland in which he was raised). The creatures rush toward the Qaim from every island, hastening from all directions, until the place each soul occupies — the very homeland in which it was raised — becomes too narrow to hold the press of the gathering. Yet the Master is careful to clarify that this does *not* mean the literal whole of humanity, or even the whole of the Arab world, physically assembles at Ma'rib, for then the place would be impossibly constricted. Rather, only those creatures whose appointed time for `qiyamah` has come will gather there. And the term `khala'iq`, creatures, is broader than mankind: it includes the animals and all created beings, not humans alone. The ones for whom the dealings of resurrection are due — and only those — rush in from all the islands. The whole world does not come; the world cannot come; only those whose hour has struck are summoned.

The text continues: `wa yakhruju 'inda qiyamihi 'alayhi's-salam kullu wahidin min awla'ika'l-khala'iq mujifan ilayhi musri'an ladayhi ila an yuhdara bayna yadayhi` (and at his rising, peace be upon him, every one of those creatures emerges, hastening toward him, hurrying to him, until he is brought before him). At the rising of the Qaim, each of these designated creatures emerges and rushes toward him, speeding to his presence, until it stands before him.

This rushing-in of the gathered is then read into a passage of scripture concerning the release of Gog and Magog. The Master cites: `hatta idha futihat ya'juju wa ma'juju wa hum min kulli hadabin yansilun` (until, when Ya'juj and Ma'juj are released, and they descend from every elevation). He glosses `al-hadab` as `al-muttafa' min al-ard`, the high or raised ground of the earth, from which they come pouring down. The verse continues: `waqtaraba'l-wa'du'l-haqq fa idha hiya shakhisatun absaru'lladhina kafaru` (and the true promise has drawn near; then behold, the eyes of those who disbelieved will stare). As the true promise draws near and Ya'juj and Ma'juj are made manifest, the eyes of those who disbelieved stare fixedly in stunned amazement, gazing in wonder at what has come to pass and how it has come to pass. The Master then unfolds the inner meaning of the names. Ya'juj and Ma'juj are the `azdad`, the opposites — the antagonists of the truth. Their followers are the disbelievers. And the verse goes on to name their objects of worship: `wa ma'budahum alladhina 'abaduhu min dunillahi ru'asa'uhum al-muqimuna lahum maqama a'immati'l-haqq al-naziluna fi manazilihim` (and their objects of worship, those whom they worshipped besides Allah, are their chiefs who set themselves up for them in the station of the Imams of truth, descending into their ranks). The objects of their worship, worshipped in place of Allah, are the chiefs of the opposites — the leaders who arrogated to themselves the station of the true Imams, planting themselves in the very ranks that belonged to the Imams of truth, so that their followers followed them as though they were the rightful guides. Thus the eschatological imagery of Gog and Magog is decoded as the doctrine of the opposites, their disbelieving followers, and their usurping chiefs.

### The Striking of the Opposites and the Two Knots

With the creatures gathered and the hierarchy of the truth confronting them, the judgment turns to its execution. The text — opening the fourth chapter of the fifth section of the third *Surah* — sets the scene: `fa-idha-jtama'at al-khala'iqu bayna yaday al-Qa'im, salamullahi 'ala dhikrihi, wa-tashakhkhasa kullu wahidin min al-hudud wa-al-a'immah wa-al-awsiya' wa-al-nutaqa', salamullahi 'alayhim, li-ahli zamanihi wa-'asrihi` (so when the creatures are gathered before the Qaim — Allah's peace upon his mention — and every one of the hudud, the imams, the successors, and the speaking-prophets is individualized — Allah's peace upon them all — for the people of his time and his era). When all the creatures are assembled before the Qaim, and each rank of the hierarchy — every `hadd`, every imam, every `wasi`, every `natiq` — is made to stand forth in person for the people of his own age, the confrontation reaches its climax: `wa-waqafahum 'ala jami'i af'alihim wa-a'malihim wa-istaqarra kullu wahidin minhum bima sana'a umira bihi` (and he makes them stand before all their deeds and their actions, and each one of them is settled with what he did, for which he was commanded). Each station makes the people of its time aware of the entirety of their deeds, and every soul is fixed and settled in the judgment of what it had done.

Then comes the sentence of execution: `fa-duribat a'naquhum wa-ursilat 'alayhim narun fa-ahraqathum bi-ajma'ihim fa-istahalu fi al-waqt fa-saru bukharan` (so their necks are struck, and a fire is sent upon them that burns them all entirely, and they are transformed in that moment, and they become vapor). The necks of the opposites are struck — and the careful reader notices that the verb has no explicitly named subject in the immediate clause: whose necks? The pronoun must refer back to the opposites, the `azdad`, of the people of that time, for the preceding sentence had spoken of "the people of his time and his era," and it is their antagonists who are now struck down. Their necks are struck, all of them; a fire is sent upon them; the fire burns them entirely; in that very instant their state is utterly changed; and they are reduced to vapor. The Master pauses to register the strangeness of this annihilation — that a being should in a single moment be wholly undone and turned to vapor. These are matters, he notes, that had not entered our understanding before; yet in our own age we have seen the laser, the atom bomb, and other new inventions that can annihilate an entire region in a single stroke. The image of instantaneous total destruction, once unimaginable, has become conceivable in our time. So the opposites become vapor in a moment.

That vapor does not simply dissipate. It rises with purpose: `thumma yas'adu dhalik al-bukhar ila al-'uqdatayn li-annahuma yajdhibanahu li-al-munasabah al-khabithah allati baynahuma` (then that vapor ascends toward the two knots, because the two knots attract it, on account of the foul affinity that is between them). The vapor of the destroyed opposites ascends toward the `'uqdatayn`, the two knots, because the two knots draw it to themselves. The cause of this attraction is `al-munasabah al-khabithah` — a foul, evil affinity — that exists between the vapor and the two knots. Like is drawn to like; the corrupt vapor is pulled toward the corrupt knots by the sympathy of their shared foulness. This is a doctrine of spiritual magnetism: it is the wicked correspondence between them that does the drawing.

The two knots, however, are not the final destination. `fa-idha saru fi al-'uqdatayn wa-sara taht al-ard jadhabat al-sakhrah allati hiya asfal al-ard dhalik al-bukhar bi-al-munasabah allati baynaha wa-baynahu` (and when they enter the two knots — and the two knots are beneath the earth — the Sakhrah, which is in the lowest part of the earth, draws that vapor, by the affinity that is between it and the vapor). When the vapor has entered the two knots, and those knots lie beneath the earth, the next stage of attraction begins: the Sakhrah — the great Rock that lies in the lowest part of the earth — draws the vapor out of the two knots into itself. Again the mechanism is affinity: there is in the Sakhrah a correspondence to the foul vapor, and by that affinity it pulls all of it inward. Here the Master clarifies a point that recurs throughout this section: the phrase "beneath the earth" (`taht al-ard`) is not to be understood crudely. It does not mean a direction in physical space such as north; it means *opposite the Ka'bah* — the lower face of the earth relative to the Ka'bah, the southern side in the schematic sense, the other surface entirely.

The Master also marks a doctrinal boundary at this point: this whole account describes the `qiyamah` of the *dawr*, the resurrection of the cycle, and not the `qiyamah` of the `kashf`, the resurrection of the unveiling, which is greater still. In the `dawr al-kashf` the two knots themselves do not figure at all, for by then nothing of them remains — all the components of the two knots will have been drawn into the Sakhrah, and so there is nothing left of them to mention. The two knots have existed since the very time of creation, when Allah created them and set them in their place; but in the resurrection of the unveiling their effects are spent. Their satanic energies can no longer operate, for their "arising" has ended, and a thing that does not arise can do no work. So the present passage concerns the cyclical resurrection, in which the knots still serve as a way-station drawing the vapor down toward the Rock.

The vapor, drawn into the Sakhrah, penetrates it to the core: `fa-yatadakhalu fi azda'iha wa-masamiha ila an yasira dhalik al-bukhar fi wasatiha` (and it enters into its parts and its pores until that vapor reaches its center). The vapor permeates the parts and pores of the Sakhrah — into its body, its inner substance, its very pores — until it arrives at the center of the Rock.

### The Re-Forming in the Center of the Sakhrah

At the center, by divine will, a transformation occurs. `thumma yanfa'ilu bi-mashi'at al-mudabbir li-dhalika fa-yu'idu ashkhasan kullu shakhsin 'ala halatihi` (then a reaction takes place by the will of the Director for that purpose, and He restores individuals, each person upon his state). A `yanfa'il` — a reaction, an acting-upon — occurs in the center of the Sakhrah by the will of the `Mudabbir`, the divine Director who governs all things. By this reaction the vapor is re-formed into individual persons; each person is brought back, restored to his own form, exactly as he was. And the precise form to which each is restored is fixed by his moment of opposition: each is re-formed upon the state — the `qamat al-alfiyyah`, the millennial stature, the full human form he bore — in which he had set himself against the `Sahib al-Zaman`, the Master of the Age. There is no question here of the individuals being formed elsewhere and then carried in; they do not "emerge" from somewhere and arrive. They are *there*, in the center of the Sakhrah, and it is there that their individuality is reconstituted. In that very form they had worn in the world — the millennial form in which they opposed the Master of the Age — they take shape again in the heart of the Rock.

The Master now describes the structure of this Rock in remarkable physical detail. `wa-hadhihi al-sakhrah mithalu qidr al-sufr` (and this Sakhrah is like a pot of brass): `allati asfaluha salbun amlas` (whose bottom is hard and smooth), `wa-wasatuha mutajawwif ka-wasat al-qidr` (and whose middle is hollow, like the middle of a pot), `wa-jami'u hijarat dhalik al-wast kibritiyyah` (and all the stones of that middle are sulfuric). The Sakhrah is shaped like a brass cooking-pot. Its bottom is a hard, smooth surface; its middle is a hollow cavity, like the interior of a pot; and all the stones filling that middle cavity are `kibritiyyah`, sulfurous — a kind of sulfuric lava. The roof over this cavity is itself rock: `wa-alladhi huwa laha ka-al-saqf sakhratun saldah` (and that which serves it as a roof is a hard rock). The Master draws out the inverted geometry: it is as though the bottom of a pot were turned to the top and the top to the bottom — the floor is hard rock and the roof is hard rock, and between them lies the sulfuric lava. The whole, he says, mirrors the roundness of the earth, with this difference: that in the middle there is rock, the bottom is rock, and in the cavity between flows the molten sulfur.

The text emphasizes how sealed this structure is: `fa-hiya min jami'i jihatiha mulamlamah` (so it, from all its sides, is sealed solid). The word `mulamlamah` is glossed in the margin as `mushtami'ah`, compacted and gathered together; the Rock is `saldan malasan`, a hard smooth mass, closed off on every boundary. `wa-al-tajwifu fi wasatiha` (and the cavity is in its middle): the hollow, the `jawf` — its very belly — lies at the center. Yet it is not entirely without openings: `wa-innama fi asfaliha masamun wa-manafidh li-yasubb dhalik al-bukhar al-mutasa'id ilayha` (but in its lower part there are pores and vents, so that the ascending vapor may be poured into it). Only in the lower portion of the Sakhrah are there pores and channels, and these exist so that the vapor rising toward the Rock may be admitted and vented through them. In sum, as the Master puts it: `wa-'ala jumlat al-qawl fa-innaha ka-bayt al-hammam` (and to sum up the matter, it is like a bathhouse) — `al-harr` — a *hot* bathhouse, `mulamlam min jami'i jawanibiha`, sealed on every side. The Rock resembles a hot bathhouse closed on all its sides, with pores only below to draw in the heat and vapor.

This Rock is identified by scripture under a particular name: `wa-hiya al-musammat fi al-kitab al-karim bi-sijjin` (and it is the one named in the Noble Book as Sijjin). The Sakhrah is what the Noble Book calls Sijjin. And the wise — `wa-innama wada'at al-hukama' bayt al-hammam 'ala mithaliha` (and indeed the wise ones modeled the bathhouse upon its pattern) — fashioned the bathhouse, the `bayt al-hammam`, in imitation of the Sakhrah's very design: a sealed hot chamber heated from below, an earthly replica of the Rock. The Master interjects the prayer that punctuates this whole grim description: `na'udhu billahi minha` — we seek refuge in Allah from it.

What is the fuel of this furnace? `fa-al-waqid min tahtihi ka-hararat al-shams allati tukhmiha min tahtiha hazwan bi-hazwin` (so the fuel beneath it is like the heat of the sun, which heats it from below, motion for motion). The `waqid`, the fuel that heats the Sakhrah, is the heat of the sun. As the sun passes beneath the earth in its course, it heats the Rock from below — motion answering motion, the sun's passage timed to the Rock's heating. The Sakhrah's own sulfuric lava is already present within; the sun's heat reaching it from below intensifies that heat manifold, exactly as the bottom of a hot pot grows hotter when fire is set beneath it. So the sun is the fire under the cosmic cauldron.

### The Confession of the Punished and the Verdict of Scripture

When the re-formed individuals reach the center of the Rock, an act of recognition takes place. `wa-'inda masir hadhihi al-shukhus hunalik yu'ayinuna biha man qad taqaddamahum min amthalihim fa-yastahhiquna ma hum fihi min al-'iqab` (and upon the arrival of these individuals there, they observe those of their like who preceded them, and they recognize that they deserve the punishment they are in). Upon arriving at the center — these individuals who have been re-formed and brought there — they engage in `mu'ayanah`, direct observation: they see with their own eyes those like themselves who went before them into this torment, and from that sight they recognize that they have indeed earned the punishment in which they now find themselves. They perceive `wa-li-ayyi sababin kanu wa-li-ayyi halatin uridu dhalik al-butah` — for what cause they were placed in the Rock, and in what state they were made to enter that crucible. Here the Master notes another name for the Sakhrah: `al-butah`, the crucible or smelting-pot, the vessel in which metal is melted down — a name fitting for a place of fiery transformation.

And then the confession breaks from them, in the words of scripture: `fa-yaqulu ba'duhum li-ba'din: ya waylatana qad kunna fi ghaflatin min hadha bal kunna zalimin` (so some of them say to others: O woe to us, we were indeed heedless of this; rather, we were wrongdoers). Some cry out to others: woe to us, we were in heedlessness of this very thing — rather, we were wrongdoers. They acknowledge not merely that they were negligent but that they were positively unjust. The reply comes, again in the words of revelation: `fa-yuqalu lahum: innakum wa-ma ta'buduna min dunillah hasabu jahannam antum laha waridun` (then it is said to them: indeed you and what you worship besides Allah are the fuel of Hell; you will come to it). It is said to them that they, together with the false objects they worshipped besides Allah, are the very fuel of Hell, and that they will be brought to it. The "what you worship besides Allah" refers to the chiefs they took as objects of worship; both the worshippers and their false gods become the fuel — and the Master specifies which part of the Sakhrah is meant by "the fuel of Hell": the middle part, Sijjin itself, where the lava flows. They and the leaders they idolized alike feed that molten core.

Then the helpers of those false gods speak — or rather, the verdict is rendered upon the false gods through their followers' own realization: `fa-yaqulu a'wanuhum: law kana ha'ula'i alihatan ma waraduha, ay a'immata haqqin` (and their helpers say: had these been gods, they would not have entered it — that is, had they been true Imams). The helpers say: had these chiefs we followed truly been gods — had they truly been Imams of the truth — they would never have entered the Sakhrah. The very fact of their entry into the Rock is the proof of their falsehood; a true Imam of the truth could not be cast into Sijjin. The leaders' presence in the fire refutes the worship that was offered to them.

Now the Master draws a decisive scriptural and doctrinal distinction concerning the duration of this abiding. The verse declares: `wa-kullun fiha khalidun lahum fiha zafirun wa-hum fiha la yasma'un` (and all of them abide therein; for them therein is sighing, and they do not hear). All abide in the Rock; for them within it there is `zafir`, the heaving sigh — the labored breath, the gasping and crying out, the sound that escapes from one in torment — and they do not hear. The critical point is the word `khalidin`, "abiding." The Master observes with great care that for the disbelievers the Quran says `khalidina fiha` — abiding therein — but does *not* add the word `abadan`, "forever." He marshals the supporting verses: `ula'ika hum sharr al-bariyyah; innalladhina kafaru min ahl al-kitab wa-al-mushrikina fi nari jahannama khalidina fiha` — those are the worst of creatures; indeed those who disbelieved among the People of the Book and the polytheists are in the Fire of Hell, abiding therein — and here, decisively, the word `abadan` does *not* appear. It has been deliberately withheld. By contrast, of the righteous the Quran says: `innalladhina amanu wa-'amilu al-salihat ... ula'ika hum khayr al-bariyyah ... lahum jannatun tajri min tahtiha al-anhar khalidina fiha abadan` — indeed those who believed and did righteous deeds are the best of creatures; for them are gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein *forever* — and here the word `abadan` is present.

From this contrast the doctrine follows: the abiding in Hell is `khulud 'aridi`, a temporary or accidental abiding — an abiding "for a duration," not for eternity — whose very purpose is purification, the cleansing of the soul. The abiding in Paradise is `khulud abad`, an everlasting abiding, eternal without end. The Master is careful to base this not on a single verse but on the consistent withholding of `abadan` from the disbelievers and its consistent attachment to the righteous; the absence of the word for the one and its presence for the other carries the whole weight of the distinction. Hell, then, even at its most terrible, is a furnace of purgation with a term, while Paradise alone is endless. The `zafir`, the sighing of the damned, fills a measured time, not an eternity.

### The Nightly Heating and the Specific Targets of the Sakhrah

The Master returns to the mechanism of the torment, describing its nightly rhythm. `wa-'inda maghib al-shams wa-masiriha taht al-ard kullu laylatin tuhmi al-sakhrah fa-tu'hiju kama yuhmi asfal al-qidr min waqid al-nar az'afan muda'afatan` (and when the sun sets and its course passes beneath the earth, every night it heats the Sakhrah, and it blazes as the bottom of a pot is heated by fire-fuel, manifold multiplied). Each night, when the sun sets and its path runs beneath the earth, the sun heats the Sakhrah, and the Rock blazes up exactly as the bottom of a pot grows incandescent when fire is heaped beneath it — and the heat is multiplied manifold over. The Master clarifies the relation between sun and Rock: the punishments are conveyed through the sun's rays, while the Sakhrah's lava is already present within; when the sun's rays reach that lava, the heat is intensified many times. As a hot pot heated from below by fire grows hotter still, so the sun's heat compounds the Rock's own fire. He adds that this comparison to a pot is given only as an approximation — `wa-ma massalnaha hadha bi-dhalika taqriban` (and we have likened this to that only by way of approximation) — a figure to aid understanding, not an exact equivalence.

`wa-'indama yahmi asfal al-sakhrah ishta'alat tilk al-hijarah al-kibritiyyah ... al-kalsiyyah ... al-zujajiyyah naran` (and when the bottom of the Sakhrah grows hot, those stones ignite — the sulfuric, the calcareous, the glassy — into fire). When the lower part of the Rock is heated, the stones within it catch fire: the `kibritiyyah`, the sulfuric stones; the `kalsiyyah`, the calcareous or lime-stones; and the `zujajiyyah`, the glassy or crystalline stones — all of them blaze up into fire. And in this inferno: `wa-sara ula'ik al-mala'in yataqallabuna fi wastiha wa-hum yastaghithuna fa-la yughathun` (and those accursed ones tumble over in its midst, crying for help, but they are not helped). The accursed ones tumble and are turned over and over in the molten middle of the Rock — like boiling water that heaves up and down — there in the midst of Hell, the midst of Sijjin. They cry out for help, they call for rescue, but no help comes to them; their cries go unanswered.

The Master then specifies, with great precision, exactly which class of beings this Sakhrah-punishment is for. `fa-hadha al-qawl yakhtassu bi-azdad al-nutaqa' wa-al-awsiya' wa-al-a'immah wa-sa'ir hududihim, salamullahi 'alayhim ajma'in` (so this statement is specific to the opposites of the speaking-prophets, the successors, the imams, and the rest of their hudud — Allah's peace upon them all). This punishment in the Rock is not for everyone who erred; it is reserved for the `azdad`, the opposites — the antagonists of the `nutaqa'`, the `awsiya'`, the `a'immah`, and all their `hudud`. The Master here makes a vital observation about the *manner* of this punishment: it is suffered in a fully personal, bodily state. In these re-formed individuals the vegetative faculty, the sensory faculty, and the very forms are all present — even the foul forms that came down from above. Their entire being is present in the torment: their senses, their consciousness, the totality of what they are. Earlier, in the two knots, the components had been separated and dispersed; now the whole being is reconstituted and present, every part of them gathered into the suffering. The punishment is not abstract; it falls upon the complete person, body and faculty and form together.

The list of the condemned is then expanded to name precisely who counts among these antagonists. They are: `al-munasibin lahum`, those who set themselves in hostile opposition to the people of truth, the enemies and antagonists; `al-mudda'in manazilahum`, those who falsely claimed the ranks of the rightful guides for themselves; `wa-kibar atba'ihim al-mu'inin lahum 'ala zulmihim`, their great followers who aided them in their oppression; `al-muta'assibin ma'ahum`, the fanatics who stood bigotedly with them; `al-salikin maslakahum`, those who walked deliberately in their path; `al-muwaswisin li-du'afa' al-'uqul`, those who whispered doubts into the minds of the weak; and `al-mustahzi'in bi-ahl al-haqq`, the mockers who scorned and ridiculed the people of truth. These — the hostile claimants, the fanatical helpers, the doubt-whisperers, and the mockers — are the inhabitants of the Sakhrah's torment.

But there is a class that is explicitly *spared* this severest punishment. `fa-amma al-huhud wa-al-ru'a' al-tabi'un lahum fa-yu'adu bihim min akhiri naw'in min al-idrak` (as for the foolish rabble and the herd who followed them, they are turned back from the last kind of perception). The `huhud` and the `ru'a'` — the foolish masses, the mere herd who merely tagged along behind the opposites — are returned from the last level of perception. They are not cast into the punishment of the peripheries nor into the punishment of the Sakhrah. They are turned back from the second or third level of perception, before that final degree, and so escape the worst. The reason is precisely their lack of knowing intent: these followers acted without understanding what they did, without malicious purpose. Their ignorance, which in the chiefs would be no excuse, here works in their favor — it is better for such people to be without understanding, for they will be released quickly. Their intentions were not evil; they were merely caught in the crowd, following the herd, going along with the multitude. The Master sets the antagonists, who knew and chose, against these followers, who did not know — and the difference in knowledge is the difference in fate. As scripture had already established it, `kama sabaqa bihi al-qawl` — as the statement has already preceded. The Master adds a temporal note on the whole order of reckoning: the chiefs would appear and exercise their rule in the world, and the people of the world would take their worldly benefits and rewards; but when the Qaim appears, that is the time of reckoning, the time of taking account and of retribution. The accounts deferred through the worldly reign are settled at the rising.

### The Geography of the Rock: Pores, Vents, and the Ka'bah

A pressing question now arises about the pores of the Sakhrah: are they, like the pores spread over every part of a body, distributed everywhere, or are they confined to specific locations? The Master answers that the pores of the Sakhrah are located *beneath the Ka'bah*. Entry into the Rock occurs from below the Ka'bah; from there the souls go in. The governing principle is a rule of interpretation: whenever the word `ard`, earth, is used in this context, what is always meant is the Ka'bah. The Ka'bah is the one fixed reference of the "earth," and so the pores are taken from beneath it, and through those pores beneath the Ka'bah the souls enter.

The Master then unfolds the full cosmic geography. He first sets aside the merely scientific account: science says the earth is elliptical, round, and in motion, but its proofs are flawed, and the round shape is a metaphorical figure made for the sake of understanding. The true detail is this: the earth is a plane, and it is flat; and on its other side is the Sakhrah — that is, Sijjin, which is `Jahannam-e-Kubra`, the Great Hell. The center of the earth's surface is the Ka'bah, the House of Allah, `Baytullah`; and on the opposite surface of the earth is the Sakhrah, whose lowest part is the abode of the `Ahl al-Tabut`, the People of the Ark. So the earth is, in this doctrine, both `baydwi`, elliptical, and `musattah`, flat — a flat platform for human beings, with the Ka'bah at the center of one face and the Sakhrah/Sijjin at the center of the opposite face. The Sakhrah's lowest depth, the most wretched part, is the dwelling of the People of the Tabut.

The earth, the Master continues, has four corners, the `atraf al-zamin`, the extremities of the earth. It is upon these extremities that the `'adhab al-atraf`, the punishment of the peripheries, befalls the opponents — a separate punishment from that of the Sakhrah's core. These four corners are adjacent to the Sakhrah, and it is upon them that the Rock's vents, its `manafiz`, are located. At these extremities the cold is so severe that it is impossible for a bird to fly past — a frigidity beyond endurance marking the periphery of the world.

Besides these vents at the extremities, the Master notes, there are many vents in the `rub' al-maskun`, the inhabited quarter of the earth. The greatest of these is called `Durdur`, located in the sea between China and India. Toward this vent flow all the rivers, and it is said that from this opening various kinds of sounds emanate. Again the prayer: `na'udhu billahi minha` — we seek refuge in Allah from it. The Sakhrah, whose shape is like a cauldron, like a pot — its lowest part, the most wretched, is shown in the schematic image as the abode of the People of the Tabut.

The Master then describes the reversed day and night of the Rock's inhabitants. When the sun, in its revolution, comes before the earth, it is daytime for the people of the earth — and at that same moment, on the other side, for the `Ahl al-Sakhrah`, it is night. And when the bright sun rises upon the people of the Sakhrah, it is day for them and night for the people of the earth. But for the Ahl al-Sakhrah, neither day nor night brings relief; both are a calamity. In their day there is intense heat, and in their night there is intense cold, a cold beyond all measure. And when the sun's revolution comes upon them, the state of their punishment increases a hundredfold — the torment multiplied a hundred times over with the sun's turning. `Na'udhu billahi min dhalika` — we seek refuge in Allah from that.

Two further points are to be remembered concerning the earth and the Sakhrah. First, Allah created the Sakhrah *before* the earth. And in the Glorious Word of Allah, the Sakhrah is given a separate individuality: earth, Sakhrah, and sky are mentioned as three distinct individualities together in a single verse, three separate created realities named side by side. Second, around this earth and Sakhrah revolve nine `aflak`, celestial spheres, and seven `kawakib`, planets. The spheres — the `aflak` — have lines that are imaginary, and their revolution is upon a plane. The seven planets, the `kawakib al-saba'`, are circular, and their revolution is of two kinds at once: both around a center and upon their own axes.

The Master then conveys an important point that Sayyidna al-Malik states at this place. Beneath the surface of the earth the Sakhrah is spread out like a cauldron, like a pot — *but* where the Ka'bah stands, and Maqam Ibrahim, and Ghadir, and the places of the holy Rawda, there, on the other side, in that place, there is *no* Sakhrah. Rather, there is a state like a void, an empty space. And in this void is the abode of all the `arwah al-nuraniyyah`, the luminous souls, and the `arwah al-hasanah`, the good souls. Contrary to this, at `amyal`, at `wadi al-shaytan` (the valley of Satan), and at the `jamarat al-thalathah` (the three stoning-pillars), there is a great multitude of `arwah al-khabithah`, the filthy souls — `la'anahumullah`, may Allah curse them. So beneath the holiest places lies a void filled with pure and good souls, while beneath the places associated with the rejection of Satan and the casting of stones gather the foul souls.

To the humble questioner's inquiry about what lies *above* the Ka'bah and these holy places — in the upper part, the part opposite the spheres — Sayyidna al-Malik answered that there is air. And he noted that this matter is not found in the books. As for the pores of these places: their existence is affirmed, but their location must not be conceived as lying within the `aflak`, the physical spheres. Rather — and this is the authoritative clarification of Sayyidna al-Malik — the pores are free from physical limits, free from the `hudud` of materiality; they are all spiritual and imaginary, not physical at all. The map of vents and pores is not a map of matter but of spiritual reality represented imaginatively.

The Master returns to the dual nature of the earth, citing the date of the statement — 1773 — at which it was said that, along with being `baydwi`, the earth is also `musattah`. The maps have all been drawn as elliptical, `baydwi`, because a curved, elliptical projection has been made wherever maps are found; and the books say that the sun is the center of the world, toward which all things are oriented. But in reality the entire revolution happens around the *earth*. The Master reconciles the two statements drawn from the `rasa'il`, the epistles: in one respect the author says that the sun is the center of the entire cosmic system, and in another respect he says that the whole revolution is around the earth and that all the planets are turned toward the earth. The reconciliation lies in the purpose of `istikhraj`, extraction: the revolution is centered on the earth in the sense that the human leaven — human dispositions, the very substance of humanity — can be brought forth, "extracted," from nowhere else but the earth. Nowhere else in the cosmos has the human being been created; the progress and emergence of humanity is only from the earth. So from the perspective of extraction, all is oriented toward the earth; while from the perspective of illumination, it is from the sun and the moon that the earth gains its light. Both statements are true, each in its own place. If one were to travel into space, to the moon, and photograph the earth, it would outwardly appear round; yet it is `musattah` in the sense that its upper layer is round while the real earth — the platform for human beings — is flat. The real earth, in truth, is only the place of the Sakhrah and the Ka'bah and the holy places; the remaining earth spread around is the `kura al-ard`, the globe of earth, but it is not the land of `da'wat`, the land of the call. The land of `da'wat` is `musattah`, flat, platform-like.

The Master reiterates the point about the void: beneath the Sakhrah — that is, beneath the Ka'bah — there is no Sakhrah; and beneath all the other holy places likewise there is no Sakhrah, but rather a void. The Sakhrah is `asfal al-zamin`, the lowest of the earth, meaning the other side of the Ka'bah, the opposite face; and right beneath the Ka'bah itself there is no Sakhrah, but a void. As shown in the schematic picture, the Sakhrah lies on both sides, but in the middle, beneath the Ka'bah, is the void — the place where the fire of the Sakhrah is not lit. This void extends beneath not the Ka'bah alone but also Ghadir Khumm, the noble Rawda, and Maqam Ibrahim. And the progress of the `aflak`, too, proceeds from the `qamat al-alifiyyah`, the millennial form, of the earth.

This whole picture is sealed by a verse: `wa in minkum illa wariduha kana 'ala rabbika hatman maqdiyya` (and there is none of you except he will come to it; this is, upon your Lord, an inevitability decreed). There is no one among you who is not an arriver at it — none who does not "come to" the earth, the Sakhrah included. This arrival is upon your Lord an inevitability decreed, a fixed and binding decree. The Master explains: everyone must arrive in human form upon this earth, and it is only after coming into human form that progress occurs. Even when a soul is found in the Sakhrah, in `asfal`, the lowest, it will be in human form; when it goes into the depth it adopts a human form. Every soul, without exception, must pass through this human arrival on the earth — the Rock not excepted — and from that arrival its onward progress is measured.

### The Order of Creation and the Making of the Rock

The Master now turns back to explain the very origin of the Sakhrah and the order in which the cosmos was made. The priority of the Sakhrah's creation is grounded in its origin: from what was the Sakhrah made? It traces back to the management, the `tadbir`, and to the two rays that were first cast — and the Rock is the place of Iblis, a place for him to dwell. The `kibr`, the primal pride, had produced three classes of souls. The first class was the `nadimin`, the remorseful, those who felt regret. From the `nadimin` were made the `aflak`, the spheres. The second movement produced the `ummahat` — the "mothers," the four elements: fire, air, water, and the ball of earth. And those who were `musirrin`, the insistent, the ones who persisted obstinately without remorse — from them was made the Sakhrah. Then, when fifty-one thousand years had passed in the revolution of the spheres and the elements of fire, air, water, and earth, from that revolution the earth was created — the very earth upon which we perform the prayer. The earth, then, was created last, after the spheres and the elements.

The Master sets out the full sequence with care, working backward and forward through it. First, the `'alam al-'uqul`, the World of Intellects, was made — and it was made in the `'alam al-nurani`, the Luminous World. Then, among those who were delayed (`ta'khir`), the form of the `nafs`, the soul, was created. The tenth intellect, the `'ashir`, was called the `nafs al-mutma'innah`, the contented soul — as in the verse `ya ayyatuha al-nafsu al-mutma'innah`, "O soul at peace" (and the Master refers it to the Quranic address, 89:27). This is a soul which, having attained contentment, is now to enter the domain of the intellect: through its delay, from `'aql` came `nafs`, the form of soul; and when the final tenth became the contented soul, it ascends to take the place of the final intellect in the World of Lights, the `'alam al-anwar`. The delayed tenth, purified and contented, rises to occupy the station of the last intellect.

Then came the command `ihbitu` — descend! fall down! As soon as the command came, some souls immediately confessed, saying that they had erred through a slip; these few who repented at once — from them was made the `'alam al-hayat`, the World of Life. And seeing these, others who had gone astray were also gathered, and from the whole of them the World of Life was constituted. Those who were yet more delayed, in whom — on account of their greater delay — materiality (`maddiyyat`) began to enter, formed the next stage. Among these, what was made first was the `falak al-muhit`, the all-encompassing sphere. The `falak al-muhit` is imaginary, `wahmi`; its delay being greater than that of others, its materiality is only imaginary, and it represents the state of the `nafs`, the soul. It was commanded that the `falak al-muhit` should complete a revolution of the entire system of spheres. Then, after it, was made the `falak al-buruj`, the sphere of the constellations. Here, in the sphere of the constellations, true materiality first appears. The light (`nur`) found there is not like the light of the Luminous World; it is a light close to material light — the light of the `buruj`, the constellations. And within the `falak al-buruj` are the stars: the `thawabit`, the fixed stars.

The Master clarifies the technical distinction between two kinds of heavenly bodies. The `thawabit`, the fixed stars, are those that do not revolve around anything else; they revolve upon their own center, and their center is fixed. The `kawakib` or `sayyare`, the planets, are those that revolve around something else — around some larger star. This, he says, is established by research: the fixed stars are fixed upon their own center and revolve upon it, while the planets revolve around a greater star. This distinction recurs and is fundamental: `thawabit` revolve on a fixed center of their own; `sayyare` revolve around a larger body.

The Master continues the descent through the spheres and correlates each with its class of origin. The state of things is graded: in the Luminous World there are the `'uqul`, the intellects — not souls, for the souls have progressed and become intellect. In the `falak al-muhit` there are the `nufus`, the souls, and the contented soul advances in that station; the term `'uqul` is also used of them in one sense, but only in the sense that they have drawn near to intellect, having attained all purity and cleanliness. After the `falak al-muhit` comes the `falak al-buruj`, in which are the constellations, the fixed stars. After that come the spheres of the planets: the `falak al-zuhal`, the sphere of Saturn; the `falak al-mushtari`, the sphere of Jupiter; then the `falak al-marrikh`, the sphere of Mars; then the `falak al-shams`, the sphere of the Sun. These four are all of materiality, but they derive from the `nadimin` and the `tawabin`, the `ta'ibin`, the repentant — they are parts of those souls who adopted modesty and repented. They were delayed, yet they did repent. After these come the spheres of Venus (`falak al-zuhra`), Mercury (`falak al-'atarud`), and the Moon (`falak al-qamar`). These three, it is said, are all from the `nadimin` — all of the remorseful. The Master qualifies this: it is not said that they are one hundred percent of the `nadimin`, because some bad effects also remain in them. Among them there will also be `mutahayyirin`, the bewildered, when they rise from below. But in essence they are of the `nadimin`.

The Master dwells on this nuance with the case of Saturn (`Zuhal`). Saturn has bad effects, and yet in essence the souls associated with it are of the `nadimin` and `ta'ibin`, the remorseful and repentant. The point is that those who repented were accepted, but among the people who followed them some elements of stubbornness had accumulated — because some had gone astray by following them. Therefore, of the `nadimin` and `ta'ibin` who are in Saturn, it was commanded that the stubborn ones who attached themselves to them would remain *with* them, and that their purification would be the responsibility of these repentant souls. So the sphere is not bad in its essence; essentially it is of the repentant and remorseful, but badness is created in its *effects* on account of the residual stubbornness mixed in with it. Hence the effects of Venus, Mercury, and the Moon — though their souls are of the `nadimin`, the more delayed but repentant — can carry a residual badness.

Then, when the period of the bewildered (`mutahayyirin`) and of the `nadimin` and `ta'ibin` had passed, the next class was taken up: the `shayatin`, the devils, and the `mutahayyirin`. From these were made the elements — fire, air, water — and the earth. And how was the earth made? In the revolution of the world over fifty-one thousand years, as already explained, the existence of the earth came about; over those fifty-one thousand years the earth came into being. The earth, in reality, is earth resting *upon* the Sakhrah: the globe of `ard`, the earth, has the Sakhrah at its middle. The soil of our earth is, in reality, a part of the `ummahat`, the elemental mothers. The real earth, in truth, is only the earth and the Ka'bah; the rest of the soil spread around is the globe of `ard`, a part of the `ummahat`, and upon it is water.

And then, for the Sakhrah: it was made from the `musirrin` and the `mustakbirin`, the insistent and the arrogant. The most insistent and most arrogant of all became the station of Sijjin. And within Sijjin is the `Tabut`, the Ark — the station of the Tabut, the most arrogant of all. It is for this reason that the Prophet said: `Mu'awiyah min al-Tabut` — Mu'awiyah is from the Tabut. By this saying the Prophet placed Mu'awiyah in `asfal`, the very lowest station, among the most arrogant in the Ark of Sijjin. The Master notes that for other obstinate ones no such station is explicitly named; but for Mu'awiyah, as for Yazid, a severe statement was made, placing him in the lowest depth.

### The Great Cycle, the Four Vents, and the Renewed Choice

Returning to the fate of the opposites within the Rock, the Master fixes its immense duration. The text reads: `wa la yazal ha'ula'i al-ad'dad fi jawfi hadhihi al-sakhrah 'ala hadhihi al-halah ila intida' al-kawr al-a'zam alladhi huwa dharb thalath mi'ah alf sanah wa sittin alf sanah fi mithliha` (and these opposites remain in the belly of this Sakhrah in this state until the completion of the Great Cycle, which is three hundred and sixty thousand years multiplied by its like). The opposites remain in the belly of the Sakhrah, in this state of torment, until the completion of the `kawr al-a'zam`, the Great Cycle. This Great Cycle is the product of three hundred and sixty thousand years multiplied by three hundred and sixty thousand years. The text gives the total: `wa huwa mi'at alf alf wa tis'ah wa 'ishrun alf alf wa sitt mi'ah alf alf` — and it is one hundred and twenty-nine thousand six hundred million: that is, 129,600,000,000 years, an almost unimaginable span. The Master works through the magnitude of the figure to impress upon the reader its vastness — the product of the great cyclical number by itself, an abyss of time.

Within that immense cycle, the opposites do not rest even in their punishment. `wa hum fi zimn dhalika yal'anu ba'duhum ba'dan wa yaqtulu ba'duhum ba'dan wa baynahum al-munasabah wa al-muharabah qa'imah abadan sarmadan` (and within that, they curse one another, and kill one another, and between them enmity and warfare stand forever and everlastingly). Throughout the Great Cycle the opposites perpetually curse one another and kill one another; between them enmity and warfare are established forever, eternal and unceasing. And the death of one among them is not an escape but a worsening: `wa man qutila minhum istahala fi al-waqt wa infa'ala shakhsan mushawwahan 'ala aqbahi ma yakun` (and whoever among them is killed is transformed at that moment and reconstituted as a deformed person in the ugliest possible form). Whoever is killed among them is, in that instant, dissolved and re-formed into an uglier, more deformed person than before — the new form yet more hideous than the one it replaced, growing worse with each killing. The Master details the deformities, which mirror the very idols these souls had fashioned and worshipped: `fa minhum man yakunu lahu ra'san wa minhum man yakunu lahu thalath ru'us wa minhum man yakunu wajhuhu ila khalfih wa akharu wajhuhu ila yaminih` (so among them is one with two heads, and among them is one with three heads, and among them is one whose face is toward his back, and another whose face is toward his right). Some have two heads, some three heads; some have their faces turned to their backs, others to the right side, others to the left. These monstrous forms, the Master notes, are precisely the depictions of the gods these souls had made — their deformed idols become their own deformed bodies. `wa hum 'ala aswa'i halatin wa aqbahiha wa hum yataqallabuna fi wahaji tilka al-niran wa yata'ajjajuna` (and they are in the worst and ugliest of states, and they are turned over in the blaze of those fires, and they are set ablaze). They are in the most wretched and hideous condition, tumbled over and over in the flames and embers of Sijjin, burning and feeling the heat — `yata'ajjajun`, glossed as `tawaqqud`, kindling and igniting, so that they become like fuel itself, ceaselessly burning.

When the appointed measure is at last fulfilled — when the people of every cycle have completed this stated duration of the Great Cycle and fully discharged this severe punishment — the cycle of torment ends and the exit begins. `fa 'inda wafa'i ahli kulli dawr hadhihi al-muddah al-madhkurah wa istifa'ihim hadha al-'adhab al-shadid fi hadha al-kawr al-ba'id al-tawil al-ba'id, yaqtulu ba'duhum ba'dan wa yastahiluna wa ya'uduna bukharan wa yas'aduna wa yakhrujuna min a'la al-sakhrah ila zahir al-ard min arba' manafiz` (so upon the completion, for the people of every cycle, of this mentioned duration, and their fulfillment of this severe punishment in this distant, long, far cycle, they kill one another, and are transformed, and return as vapor, and ascend, and exit from the top of the Sakhrah to the surface of the earth from four vents). At the close of the long, distant Great Cycle, the opposites kill one another a final time, are transformed, and return again to vapor; this vapor ascends and exits from the top of the Sakhrah out to the surface of the earth through four vents, `arba' manafiz`, situated at the Rock's upper part.

The Master enumerates the four vents. `ahaduha bi'run fi wadin min awdiyat Hadramawt yuqalu laha Barhut` — the first is a well in one of the valleys of Hadramawt, called Barhut. `wa al-thaniyah bi'run fi Khurasan` — the second is a well in Khurasan, in Iran. `wa al-thalithah bi'run fi al-'arab` — the third is a well in Arabia. `wa al-rabi'ah fi bilad ukhra ghaba 'anni ismuha` — and the fourth is in another land whose name the author says is hidden from him, which he does not remember. The Master then gives the four vents by their directional names: `manafiz al-ard al-arba'` — the four vents of the earth — `wa hiya allati fi al-mashriq tusamma Barhut wa Shafahu` (and the one in the east is called Barhut, and Shafahu — both being its names); `wa allati fi al-maghrib tusamma Buqa wa Han wa Har` (and the one in the west is called Buqa, and Han, and Har); `wa allati fi al-shamal tusamma Saydum wa Jub wa Kharqa` (and the one in the north is called Saydum, and Jub, and Kharqa); `wa allati fi al-janub tusamma Sabr wa Sahiq` (and the one in the south is called Sabr and Sahiq). The Master is careful to distinguish these vents from the four extremities discussed earlier: the `atraf`, the extremities, are a place of *punishment* (the punishment of the peripheries), whereas these four `manafiz` of the Sakhrah are places of *exit*. The two are separate: the extremities are for torment, the vents are for emergence.

The Master pauses over the persistence of devotion even amid such doctrine, reflecting on the spectacle of misguided worship. He marvels at the difficulty of comprehending the devotion of those who fashion a god with their own hands and then worship it — for these are precisely the wretched souls who, in the Rock, curse one another and fight among themselves. He notes that even within the community such cursing has occurred — a Shafi'i would rise against a Hanbali and each would call the other `kafir`, unbeliever. Yet the case of those who carve and shape an idol with their own hands and then bow to it as their god is, he says, beyond human understanding — what could be more foolish than to make a thing oneself and then take it as one's lord? It is to the rejection of this very nature that the Prophet, that Islam, has called. (The Master clarifies that those presently engaged in such worship are still in *this* world; they have not yet gone toward the punishments — they remain in the world, their reckoning still ahead.)

The Master reaffirms the place where the deforming transformation occurs: `infa'ala shakhsan mushawwahan` — the soul is "made into," `munfa'il`, a deformed person — and this deforming, this worsening into uglier and uglier forms, takes place *within the Sakhrah itself*. After one Great Cycle, the souls come back out; they exit from the vents of the earth. The worsening into deformed states happens inside the Rock; the exit happens *as vapor*. The Master clarifies the manner of both motions: when the souls go into Sijjin, they go in the state of vapor; and when they exit, they likewise exit in the state of vapor. Both the entering and the leaving are vaporous. As the killing and transforming and return-to-vapor are described, so the exit through the four vents follows: they leave as vapor.

The text continues with the imagery of ascent: `wa shazza su'uduhum min hadhihi al-shu'ab al-jahannamiyyah ka-su'ud al-sahab wa al-dhabab al-tha'ir min butun al-awdiyah ila ru'us al-jibal` (and their ascent from these valleys of Hell is like the rising of a cloud and the swirling mist that rises from the bellies of the valleys to the peaks of the mountains). Their ascent from the infernal ravines is like a cloud rising, like dust and mist swirling up from the depths of the valleys to the mountain peaks — the vaporous souls billowing upward from the pit. Then begins the renewed ascending return, which exactly mirrors the upward path described at the very opening of this section: `fa idha sa'aru fi sath al-ard 'adu ila al-mizaj wa al-muntadhar thumma ila al-ma'adin al-mahmudah thumma ila al-nabat al-mahmud thumma ila al-hayawan al-muhallal ila an yadh-haru fi al-qamat al-alifiyyah ashkhadan wa yud'awna ila al-haqq wa yu'radu 'alayhim al-walayah wa al-dukhul fi da'wat ahl al-haqq` (so when they move upon the surface of the earth, they return to the temperament and the compounded, then to the praiseworthy minerals, then to the praiseworthy plants, then to the lawful animals, until they appear in the Millennial Form as individuals, and they are called to the truth, and the wilayah is presented to them, and entry into the call of the people of truth). Upon reaching the earth's surface, the souls re-enter the world through `mizaj wal-muntazij`, temperament and compounding — for there is an orderly process by which they pass into temperament and compounding — then through the praiseworthy minerals, then the praiseworthy plants, then the lawful animals, until at last they appear once more in the `qamat al-alifiyyah`, the millennial human form, as full individuals. And there, once again, they are called to the truth: the `walayah` is presented to them, and entry into the call of the people of truth is offered.

And so the great fork recurs exactly as before. `fa in qabilu najaw wa in ankaru wa takhallafu 'ada kullun minhum 'ala qadri munasabatih lahum` (so if they accept, they are saved; and if they deny and turn away, each of them returns according to the measure of his enmity toward them). If they accept, they attain salvation; if they deny and hang back, each returns according to the measure of his hostility. The Master stresses that this is a true clean slate, just as Sayyidna said: the souls come back as a clean slate, and now the choice given to them at the very beginning is given to them again — like the first perfection, `kamal-e-awwal`. They may now use that choice as they wish. The whole machinery of punishment, in the end, returns the soul to the same pristine freedom with which it began.

The Master answers a careful question about the *timing* of this renewed call: is the `da'wat` presented before the soul takes individual human form, or after? The call is given to them as individuals in human form. And those to whom the call comes are not all of one kind. On the platform of the world there are many who are people of the truth, and even among the people of the truth there are many degrees: some are quick to accept, some accept a little, some a great deal — they are not uniform. Then there are some who are bewildered in doubt, uncertain whether they will incline this way or that. And there is a third kind: those to whom the call is being presented for the very first time, whose inclination, this way or that, rests entirely upon their own choice. These re-emerging souls belong to this kind of free choice. So when the call is given, not everyone who receives it responds alike. Some have, as it were, already accepted the call and need only a push, coming at once — as it is said of Salman, `wa law kana fi Thurayya`, that even were he in the Pleiades, he would come running from there to accept the call. As a soul's prior leaven is, so will be its acceptance of the call. And there are some who are `musirr` and `mustakbir`, insistent and arrogant, now in a state of falling, who will never accept the call. All these kinds of people, the Master notes, are mentioned in the Pure Word: there are those upon whom the call will be presented and who will have the choice to accept it or not.

### The Indestructible Three and the Cycle of the Qaim

A profound clarification follows, prompted by the question of whether the soul's original "leaven" — its primordial intention — ever truly changes within the Sakhrah. In the Sakhrah, the Master explains, the original leavens are not changed; the souls have come into the world, but in the Rock their initial intentions are not altered. What they suffer there is the *punishment* corresponding to those intentions; their entire existence, with all its forms and faculties, has reached them, and they undergo the penalty. The "leaven" itself — that primordial disposition — does it never change? Here the Master sets out one of the deepest doctrines of the whole section. There are three things created by Allah that can *never* be destroyed, but only temporarily hidden. The first is `wujud`, existence itself — the existence Allah has bestowed cannot be annihilated. The second is knowledge and intellect — the powers that reside within that existence. And the third is `ikhtiyar`, free choice, freedom. These three — existence, knowledge/intellect, and free choice — that Allah has given can never at any time be changed or destroyed; for a span they may become hidden, that can happen, but they cannot be obliterated.

And the primordial intentions themselves belong to this realm of choice. The intentions a soul made in the `'alam al-azal`, the eternal pre-existence, are matters of `ikhtiyar`, of choice — intention *is* a choice. Therefore those primordial intentions are never destroyed; like existence and knowledge, they may be hidden for a time but they endure. While the souls are undergoing punishment in the Rock, it is not the time for the exercise of choice; but when they exit, freedom is again granted to them — now come toward the right path; you have the choice. If they come, well and good; if not, they return into the Rock once more.

The Master then traces the relation between actions and the primordial intention. The punishments are given according to the soul's actions — and the basis of those actions is the leaven, the intention, made in the `'alam al-azal`. When a soul exits the Sakhrah, that primordial thing has not changed in its essence; rather, its *purification* has taken place. The intention of the eternal pre-existence bore its fruit in the soul's arrangement across the various places and stations of the world. After coming into the world, the soul passed through many cycles, and in each cycle, by its own choice, it performed deeds — oppressions and good works alike. According to those deeds it receives its requital in the next cycle, and the next, and the next, until the manifestation (`zuhur`) of the Qaim, peace be upon him. The full reckoning — the complete punishment and reward for all the deeds heaped up across the cycles — is rendered at this time, at the `qiyamah`, at the `qiyamat al-kubra`, the Great Resurrection, and it is then that the soul went into the Sakhrah.

This raises the question of which resurrection is meant — the cyclical or the universal. The Master distinguishes them sharply. The resurrection here described is the `qiyamah` of the *cycle* (the `dawr`); this is the real resurrection. There is also a cyclical resurrection of a lesser order, but that is a *partial* resurrection. The true, universal resurrection — the `qiyamat-e-kubra` — is the resurrection of the cycle of cycles, signified by the verse `yawm tubaddalu al-ardu ghayra al-ard` (the Day the earth will be changed to another earth — Quran 14:48). On that Day the earth will be changed into another earth; the sky and the earth will be destroyed and a new sky and earth created. This is the true `qiyamat-e-kubra`, distinct from the partial, cyclical resurrection.

The Master then situates our own moment within the vast scheme of cycles. The present `qaim al-muntadhar`, the Awaited Qaim, is cyclical: he is the *first* cycle of the four-hundredth cycle. Three hundred and ninety-nine cyclical resurrections (`qiyamahs`) have already passed; we are at the beginning of the four-hundredth cycle, in its first cycle. The real resurrection is the resurrection of the cycle, and 399 such cyclical resurrections have gone before. Within the present cycle, the Master adds, we have passed seven `kash` and six `satr`, and stand now in the seventh cycle of `satr` — six cycles of `satr` having passed, we are in the seventh.

From this follows a striking claim about the age of the earth. A "cycle" (`dawr`) is four lakh — four hundred thousand — years. At the beginning of this present cycle the first `jussa`, the first manifestation, appeared at Sarandip (Ceylon). The reason a `jussa` manifests at the beginning of a cycle is that before it, the universe is destroyed; and the manifestation of the new `jussa` comes not from the human race but from the earth itself. Because at the end of each cycle everything is destroyed and the earth begins anew, the earth cannot be older than four lakh years. We are in the final part of the first cycle of the four-hundredth great cycle, and this earth is, on this reckoning, only four lakh years old. The Master notes the contrast with the findings of research, which speak of things crores or billions of years old; he allows that, from the standpoint of the things of the *sky*, such great ages may be understandable, but the things of the *earth* — life, animal life, human life, and the effects of the dwellers of that life — cannot be older than four lakh years. The old minerals may remain, for matter cannot be annihilated; when the earth was dissolved at the cycle's end, its substance mixed with air and flew off, but it did not cease to exist. Yet animate life and its traces upon the earth are confined within four lakh years; the earth is new, and so the dwellers of its life cannot exceed that span.

### The Jussas: The Ka'bah, the Holy Places, and the Mawri Succession

The Master closes with the doctrine of the `jussas`, the manifestations, and their hierarchy. The four-hundredth `jussa`, the one now to manifest, will appear in the `Khana-e Ka'bah`, the House of the Ka'bah — and in this there is a great wisdom, for the four-hundredth `jussa` will gather, will be the collector of, the three hundred and ninety-nine cycles that have passed. The first `jussa` of this great cycle manifested at Sarandip; 399 cycles have since passed; and so the `jussa` now to appear is the `jami'-e-arba'`, the gatherer of the four hundred. Its greatness is such that its manifestation will be in the House of the Ka'bah. The Master explains the grading: in the four hundred cycles that have passed, there are forty `jussas` that each manifested over ten cycles — cyclical manifestations whose greatness exceeds the ordinary because each spans ten cycles. But greater still is the `jussa` that manifests at the beginning of a `mawr`, the `mawri jussa`: no other `jussa` in the entire `mawr` can reach it. Across an entire `mawr`, four lakh `jussas` will manifest, and among them the greatest is the first, the one that manifested at the beginning of the `mawr`. The `mawri jussa` holds a very great wisdom.

And greater than all is the one that manifested at the very beginning of the *first* `mawr` — the `jussa-e-ibtida'iyyah`, the initial manifestation at the world's beginning. His greatness is such that none can reach him; and if any is to equal him, it will be only the final `jussa` at the world's very end, whose `zuhur` comes when the world is about to end. None other can attain his rank.

The cyclical `jussas`, by contrast, have manifested at various holy places, and these are precisely the places of `ziyarat`, of visitation. Some manifested at the station of Ghadir, some at the station of the Rawda, some at Najaf, some at Karbala, some at Maqam Ibrahim, some at the Hijr Aswad, some at Bayt al-Muqaddas. The Master mentions Ghadir Khumm, the noble Rawda, Bayt al-Muqaddas, and the stations of visitation in Karbala, Najaf, and Sham — these are their manifestations, and all the `jussas` that have manifested before are revered, and we visit them. But Sarandip is *not* counted among the holy places, even though the manifestation of Janab Adam is mentioned from there — because it belongs to the present time, and so it is not numbered among the holy places. The previous `jussas` that manifested are holy places; Sarandip is not. The reason is that in those earlier eras the manifestations were mostly of Arab manifestation. The Master recalls, in this connection, the victory of Janab Ali in the Masjid of Kufa, in its `mihrab` — the `mihrab` of Kufa, where, he says, the worshippers tremble to pray, so awesome is the place.

Finally, the Master sets out the doctrine of `mawri` succession. The one who comes as the last `jussa` of the second `mawr` will gather his `mawr` — for every manifestation has its sphere — and where will he arrive? He will reach the station of the one of the first `mawr`. The first `mawr` is the beginning of the world, the first `jussa-e-ibtida'iyyah` of the first `mawr`. In the court — the `ufuq`, the horizon — of that first one, the last of the second `mawr` will establish his *second* seating: his first seating was his own, and this, with the first `mawr`, is his second. And gathered with him will be all the `jussas`: as each cyclical `jussa` comes, its seating is established there; and the `jussa` of the `mawr` hands over the two `jussas` gathered with it to the `sahib-e-mawr` of the next coming `mawr`. For who is the true successor of the one of the first `mawr`? Not the cyclical `jussa`, but the `mawri jussa` — the `jussa` of the next coming `mawr` is his true and complete successor.

So the one who will now manifest in the House of the Ka'bah in the coming four-hundredth cycle — whose `khalifah`, whose successor, is he in reality? He is the successor of the one above him, of the previous cycle, but only *partially*. The `sahib` of the initial `jussa` of the second `mawr` is the highest; and the four-hundredth `jussa` will become his successor, but partially, until the four lakh `jussas` are completed. When the four lakh `jussas` are over, the third `mawr` will come, and its initial `jussa` will be the true and complete successor of the second `mawr`. The present `sahib-e-mawr` is preparing for that next one — preparing a `mawri jussa` like himself. This is the abiding law: a `da'i` prepares a `da'i`; a `mad'u` prepares a `mad'u`. The pattern fixed for the succession of the manifestations is that each prepares its like for the one that comes after, so that the chain of guidance — from the first `mawr` to the last — is never broken, and the true successor of each universal cycle is the initial manifestation of the cycle that follows.
