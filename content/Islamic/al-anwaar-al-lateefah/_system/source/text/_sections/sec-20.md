## The Counter-Types and the Poison of Enmity

### The Devilish Forms in the Two Knots and Their Descent into Food and Drink

We take up the text where the previous discourse paused, with a teaching about where the devils come from and how they make their way into the world of human beings. The text reads `man tawaffa hunalika min tilka-s-surah allati qad sarat shayatin` — "the one who comes to his end there, from that form which has become devils." A precise point of recitation is to be observed: the verb is `tawaffa`, with the doubled `fa`, on the pattern of `tafa'ada` and `tafazala`; at its end there stands a written `ya` that is not pronounced — it is inscribed but silent in reading. The meaning is that there is a duration, a span of time, allotted to the one who arrives at that place, and what arrives there arrives from a `surah`, a form, that has already turned into devils, into `shayatin`.

What, then, are these `shayatin`? They are not abstractions but `surat-ha-i-shaytan` — devilish forms, the very shapes of devilry. And they reside in a definite location: the `uqdatain`, the two knots, which together constitute the dark isthmus, the `barzakh al-zulmani`. This is the standing teaching that anchors everything that follows. Those who are holders of `tasawwurat-i-khabithah`, of foul and corrupt conceptions, do not scatter; they gather. And the place of their gathering is precisely the two knots. There the `ijtima'` of the `sharir`, the assembly of the evil ones, takes place; there all of them come together and add to one another's `shararat`, their mischief and malignancy. The two knots are, in this respect, the reservoir and the meeting-house of every devilish form.

From this reservoir the descent begins. The text continues `tanzilu ila-l-ardi bi-mashi'ati-l-mudabbir fa-yasiru fi shai'in min ma'kulat wa mashrubat` — "it descends to the earth by the will of the Director, then enters into some thing of the edibles and the drinkables." These devilish forms fall — they descend toward the earth — and they do so not by their own initiative but `bi-mashi'ati-l-mudabbir`, by the will and design of the Mudabbir, the Director who governs the unfolding of all things. By that will they come down, and when they reach the earth they enter into things: specifically into `ma'kulat` and `mashrubat`, the things that are eaten and the things that are drunk. They become, as it were, lodged within food and drink, awaiting the one who will consume them.

A clarifying question is properly raised here, lest the teaching be misconstrued in a crudely material way. Are the idols that certain peoples worship — figures such as Kali and the like — are these the `majami'`, the gatherings, that have been described, or the `haykal`, the structures? They are indeed `khabith`, foul things, every one of them; that much is plain. But they do not enter into a `haykal` as such. The gathering, the `majma'`, forms in the two knots, and what gathers there is the `suwarat`, the forms — not the `maddah`, the matter. The distinction must be held firmly: there is on the one hand the `maddah`, the gross matter, and on the other the `surat`, the form. The `maddah-i-khabith`, the foul matters, are what manifest in this physical world — these idols and such objects are material things, evil things, things that have not appeared in any good form. But the gathering in the two knots is a gathering of forms, of conceptions, not of physical idols. The two registers must not be confused.

### Why the Respite Is Granted: The Logic of Retribution

A natural and pressing question arises: why is this respite granted at all? Why is the punishment of the wrongdoer postponed, so that these devilish forms are allowed to gather and descend and work their effect, rather than being struck down at once? The answer lies in the very logic of `qisas`, of retribution. The respite is given so that the one from whom retribution is due can have his `zuhur`, his manifestation in the world. Only when he has appeared, only when he stands openly present, can the full `qisas` be exacted from him. The retribution must be complete, and for it to be complete the time must come and both parties must be ready — the one who is owed retribution present, and the one from whom it is owed present as well. When both are present, then the full account can be settled, and once it is settled, everything is cleared. Even the satanic work of tormenting and inflicting pain is folded into this respite: it too serves the eventual completion of the retribution. The postponement, then, is not an oversight or a slackening of justice; it is the precondition of justice being fully done.

### Nourishment Taken by Friend or Foe, and the Food as a Passageway

The text now turns to the one who takes nourishment from these poisoned things: `fa-yagtadhi biha man yastahiqqu an yagtadhi dhalika` — "and there takes nourishment from it the one who is deserving of taking such nourishment." The `ghiza`, the nourishment, is drawn from those foods and drinks — from the `ma'kulat` and `mashrubat`, the very things into which the devilish forms have entered. And here a teaching of great consequence is delivered: the one who takes this nourishment may be a `wali`, a friend of the people of truth, or a `zidd`, an opposite and adversary. Both may eat of it. The friend can partake of these edibles and drinkables, and the adversary can partake of them as well. The poisoned substance does not discriminate at the point of consumption; what differs is what it produces through the one who consumes it.

For the substance becomes, in the text's word, a `ma'bar`: `fa-yakunu li-dhalika ma'baran` — "and it becomes for that a passageway." It is a place of crossing, a passage through which the `surat-i-khabithah`, the evil form, makes its manifestation into the world. And the examples are momentous. From this passageway Qabil had his `zuhur`, his manifestation, from Adam. In the same manner, from various Imams there were born sons who became enemies of the Imam of their time — brothers who turned into adversaries. This addresses the question that would otherwise trouble the reader: does eating the poisoned thing have no effect upon the one who eats it? The point is that the effect comes through him, not necessarily upon him — it passes through him and appears in his children, in his progeny.

The mechanism is then set out in detail. The text reads `thumma yazharu minhu 'inda-l-mulamasati baynahu wa bayna zawjatihi` — "then it appears from him at the time of intimacy between him and his wife." At the moment of `mulamasah`, of intimacy between the man and his wife, the evil form appears from him. Then `fa-yakunu minhu waladun yatarakka fi-r-rutab` — "and there comes from him a son who progresses in the ranks." A son is born from him, and this son ascends through the `rutab`, the ranks, stage by stage. The text continues `ila an yazhara mawludan wa yatarabba fa-yablughu-l-huluma` — "until he appears as a newborn, and is raised, and reaches maturity." He is born as a `mawlud`, a newborn; he is given `tarbiyah`, upbringing and nurture — for the verb is `tarabba`, which means to receive upbringing, an intransitive form that takes a subject but no object, distinct from `rabba` (`rabbahu`), which means to give upbringing and does take an object — and so, having received his nurture, he reaches `bulugh`, the age of maturity. The progression is complete: from the form that appeared at intimacy, to the newborn, to the nurtured child, to the matured man.

### From Devil-in-Potential to Devil-in-Actuality: The Making of the Zidd

What has this matured son become? The text answers: `fa-yakunu ziddan li-dhalika-t-tawr wa wasiyyihi aw Imami-l-'Asr` — "and he becomes the opposite of that cycle, and of its legatee, or of the Imam of the Age." He becomes the `zidd`, the opposite — the opposite of the `natiq` of that `tawr`, that cycle, or the opposite of the natiq's `wasi`, his legatee, or the opposite of the `Imam al-'Asr`, the Imam of the Age. Here we should mark the breadth of the teaching: the son who eats the poisoned thing — whether he is the son of a `wali` or the son of a `zidd` — may himself become a `zidd`. The son of a friend can become an opposite, and the son of an opposite can become an opposite. The line of descent does not fix the outcome; the passageway and what passes through it do.

And with this the decisive transformation is named: `wa huwa-sh-shaytanu bi-l-fi'l` — "and he is the devil in actuality." This son who has matured into a `zidd` has passed from being a `shaytan bi-l-quwwah`, a devil in potentiality, into being a `shaytan bi-l-fi'l`, a devil in actuality. The potentiality that lay latent in the descended form has now been actualized in a living adversary who walks the earth and opposes the holders of the divine stations.

### The Three Acts of the Zidd: Claiming, Concealing, and Denying

How does this actualized devil conduct himself? The text describes three connected acts. First, `wa yadda'i rutbata dhalika-l-maqami-l-'ali` — "and he claims the rank of that high station." He advances a claim; he claims for himself the `rutba`, the rank, of the `maqam al-'ali`, the high station that belongs by right to the holder of truth. Second, `wa yughatti manzilatahu` — "and he conceals his status." He covers over and hides the `manzilah`, the true standing, of the rightful holder, so that it cannot be seen for what it is. Third, `wa yajhadu maqamahu` — "and he denies his station." The verb here is `jahada`, with the soft `ha`, meaning to deny and reject — not `jihada`, to strive — and it signifies that he denies and repudiates the `maqam`, the station, of the rightful one. Claiming the rank, concealing the standing, and denying the station: these three together constitute the characteristic work of the `zidd`.

### Concealment as Spiritual Killing: Al-Harith ibn Murrah and Qabil

The text then introduces a teaching about the gravity of concealment itself: `wa rubbama yakunu huwa-l-mutawalli li-haqiqati-l-hadd alladhi huwa-l-maqamu bi-nafsihi ka-l-Harith ibn Murrah alladhi ghatta manzilata Adam 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and perhaps he is the one who takes charge of [the killing of] the reality of the hadd, which is the station itself, like al-Harith ibn Murrah who concealed the station of Adam, peace be upon him." (A point of reading: the word is `mutawalli`, the one who takes charge or assumes authority, though it had been written `mutawali`.) Sometimes, then, the `zidd` becomes the one who takes charge of the killing of that `hadd`, that rank — a killing aimed at the `maqam`, the station, itself. He intends to kill the Imam. The exemplar given is al-Harith ibn Murrah, who concealed the `manzilah`, the station, of Adam, peace be upon him.

A question of the first importance is raised here: is concealing the `manzilah` itself a form of killing? The answer is yes — it is a `batini qatl`, a spiritual or inward killing. To conceal a `hadd`'s station, to cover over the true standing of one who holds a rank in the divine hierarchy, is itself a kind of murder, an inward murder distinct from the shedding of physical blood. And yet the one who commits this inward killing may also be a `qatil`, an outward killer, as well. The two need not be separate persons. This is illustrated by the next example: `wa ka-Qabil alladhi ghatta manzilata Habil wa-dda'a maqamahu wa qatalahu` — "and like Qabil who concealed the station of Habil, and claimed his station, and killed him." Qabil performed all three acts together: he concealed the `manzilah` of Habil, he claimed his `maqam`, and then he killed him outright. In Qabil the inward concealment and the outward slaying are joined in a single person, just as al-Harith ibn Murrah stood as the enemy of Adam. The discourse is showing us where such manifestations arise — they arise from the two knots — and it is naming the pairs of prophet and adversary across the sacred history.

### The Roll of the Counter-Types: Adversaries of the Prophets and Imams

The text now sets out a long and solemn roll of the counter-types, naming for each holder of truth the `zidd` who opposed him. We preserve each pairing as the text delivers it.

`wa ka-Rasib alladhi kana ziddan li-Nuh 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and like Rasib who was the opposite of Nuh, peace be upon him." Rasib was the `zidd` of Nuh. A question is raised whether Rasib was a son, a `farzand`, of Nuh; the answer given is that he was not necessarily a son — it is not a requirement of being a `zidd` that one be a son, though a `zidd` can indeed be a son. In Rasib's case it is said he was not.

`wa ka-Ham alladhi kana ziddan li-Sam wa qatalahu ba'da dhalik` — "and like Ham who was the opposite of Shem and killed him after that." Ham was the `zidd` of Sam; they were brothers, and Ham killed his brother Sam.

`wa ka-Nimrud ibn Kan'an alladhi kana ziddan li-Ibrahim 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and like Nimrud son of Kan'an who was the opposite of Ibrahim, peace be upon him." Nimrud ibn Kan'an was the `zidd` of Ibrahim, peace be upon him; he was of the line of Ham.

`wa ka-l-Walid ibn Mus'ab alladhi kana ziddan li-Musa 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and like al-Walid ibn Mus'ab who was the opposite of Musa, peace be upon him." Al-Walid ibn Mus'ab was the `zidd` of Musa.

`wa ka-Sahbuta alladhi qatala 'Isa 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and like Sahbuta who killed 'Isa, peace be upon him." Sahbuta was the one who killed 'Isa, peace be upon him.

`wa ka-Abi Lahab wa Abi Jahl alladhayn kana zidayn li-Muhammadin sallallahu 'alayhi wa alihi wa sallam` — "and like Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl, who were the two opposites of Muhammad, may God bless him and his family and grant them peace." Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl were the two `zidd`s of the Prophet Muhammad.

Then, for Ali ibn Abi Talib, the text gives two opposites and a later killer. (A textual matter is noted in passing: in the printed copy there appears a slip in the writing before this point, and the correct reading is established here.) `wa ka-Abi Bakrin wa 'Umar la'natullahi 'alayhima alladhayn kana zidayn li-'Ali ibn Abi Talib salawatullahi 'alayhi` — "and like Abu Bakr and 'Umar, may God's curse be upon them both, who were the two opposites of Ali ibn Abi Talib, may God's blessings be upon him." Abu Bakr and 'Umar were the two `zidd`s of Ali ibn Abi Talib, with the curse of God upon them, and they were his opposites firstly, `awwalan`. Then `wa ka-Ibn Muljam la'nahullah alladhi qatalahu akhiran` — "and like Ibn Muljam, may God's curse be upon him, who killed him lastly." Ibn Muljam was the one who killed Ali ibn Abi Talib at the last, `akhiran`. So the pattern for Ali is: two opposites firstly, and the one who slew him at the end.

`wa ka-Mu'awiyah la'natullah alladhi kana ziddan li-l-Hasan thumma sammahu` — "and like Mu'awiyah, may God's curse be upon him, who was the opposite of al-Hasan, then he poisoned him." Mu'awiyah was the `zidd` of al-Hasan, peace be upon him, and then he gave him poison.

`wa ka-Yazid la'nahullah alladhi kana ziddan li-l-Husayn` — "and like Yazid, may God's curse be upon him, who was the opposite of al-Husayn." Yazid was the `zidd` of al-Husayn. And `wa ka-sh-Shimr alladhi qatalahu` — "and like Shimr who killed him." Shimr was the one who killed al-Husayn.

`wa ka-lladhi samma Mawlana al-Mustansir Billah salawatullahi 'alayhi` — "and like the one who poisoned our master al-Mustansir Billah, may God's blessings be upon him." And `wa ka-l-qatil li-Mawlana al-Amir salawatullahi 'alayhi` — "and like the killer of our master al-Amir, may God's blessings be upon him." Each of these blessed masters, al-Mustansir Billah and al-Amir, was struck down by a poisoner or a killer who was, in his inner reality, a `zidd`.

### The Poisoning of the Prophet at Khaybar and the Testimony of the Goat

The roll then turns to a particular and weighty episode. `wa ka-l-Imra'ati-l-Khaybariyya la'nahallah allati sammat an-nabi sallallahu 'alayhi fi-t-ta'am` — "and like the woman of Khaybar, may God's curse be upon her, who poisoned the Prophet, may God bless him, in the food." The woman of Khaybar, al-Imra'at al-Khaybariyya, poisoned the Prophet in his food. The text continues `wa ma'ahu rajulan` — "and with him were two men." Two men were with the Prophet, and they too partook of the food. `fa-mata ahaduhuma` — "so one of them died." One of the two men passed away. `wa da'a rasulullah sallallahu 'alayhi li-ahadihima fa-salima` — "and the Messenger of God, may God bless him, prayed for one of them, and he was saved." The Prophet prayed for one of them, and he was kept safe. (On the reading of `salima`: the consonants admit `sallama` — "he handed over," or "he said the greeting of peace" — and `saluma` and `salima`, each with its own sense; here the sense is `salima`, "he was kept safe.")

Then comes the gravest part: `wa aqama dhalika-s-summu ma'a-n-nabi sallallahu 'alayhi ila an kana sababa rihlatihi` — "and that poison remained with the Prophet, may God bless him, until it became the cause of his passing." The poison, which had come from the two knots, remained in the Prophet's body from the time of the Battle of Khaybar until his passing, and it became the very cause of his departure from this world. His passing, in this teaching, was from that poison.

And here the discourse pauses upon a sign of the Prophet's surpassing greatness. When the Prophet had eaten of the food, the leg of the goat itself testified to him, saying in effect: "O Prophet of God, stop! I am poisoned" — `masmum`. Consider what manner of thing gave this testimony: not a living animal, but the leg of a goat that had been cut from the body, whose skin had been removed, which had been roasted and cooked over fire, and then set before the Prophet. From that cooked and severed limb came a testimony to the Prophet of God, peace and blessings be upon him, informing him: "I am poisoned." When Ali, may God's blessings be upon him, was once asked how the Prophet's miracle compared with the miracles granted to earlier prophets — for it was said that one prophet had this sign and another that sign — he answered that for the earlier prophets living animals had given testimony, but the Prophet's distinction was that even the cooked leg of a slaughtered goat, severed and skinned and roasted, was brought and gave testimony to him.

A reader naturally asks: why did the goat's leg testify only after he had eaten, and not before? Had it spoken beforehand, the harm could have been averted. The answer returns us to the logic already established: how, then, would the `qisas`, the retribution, be completed? The greatness of the Prophet is precisely what is being discussed, and the completion of the retribution required that the poison take its course. To see the whole pattern, consider the line of enemies once more: the enemy of Adam was al-Harith ibn Murrah, and Qabil; the enemy of Nuh was Rasib; the enemy of Ibrahim was Nimrud ibn Kan'an; the enemy of Sam was Ham; the enemy of Musa was al-Walid ibn Mus'ab; the enemy of 'Isa was Sahbuta; and the two enemies of the Prophet were Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl. This last pairing is confirmed by the hadith `li-kulli nabiyyin shaytanun wa liya shaytanan` — "for every prophet there is one devil, and for me there are two devils." For every prophet there is a single devil; for the Prophet Muhammad there are two, and they are Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl. Thereafter come the opposites of Ali — Abu Bakr and 'Umar, and Ibn Muljam who slew him at the last — and Mu'awiyah, the `zidd` of al-Hasan, and Yazid and Shimr, the enemies of al-Husayn who confronted and killed him, and the adversaries of Mawlana al-Mustansir and Mawlana al-Amir.

### The Vices upon Vegetation and Mineral: Poison and the Sword

The discourse now opens its second great movement, explaining how the vices that gather in the two knots reach the noble stations through the two channels of vegetation and mineral. The text reads `wa qad yakunu min hadhihi-l-qaba'is allati hiya surun mujtami'ah fi 'uqdatain alladhayni huma al-barzakh al-zulmani ma yujra 'ala-n-nabat fa-yakunu summan qatilan minhu talaf al-maqamat al-sharifah ka-s-summi alladhi kana sababan li-talaf al-natiq` — "and there may be from these vices — which are forms gathered in the two knots that are the dark isthmus — that which is made to flow upon the vegetation, so that it becomes a deadly poison from which comes the destruction of the noble stations, like the poison that was a cause for the destruction of the Natiq."

The teaching is this. The `qaba'is`, the vices, are forms — `surun mujtami'ah` — that gather in the two knots, the `uqdatain`, which together are the dark isthmus, the `barzakh al-zulmani`. From these gathered vices, something is made to flow upon the `nabat`, the vegetation. When it descends upon the vegetation, that vegetation becomes a `summ`, a poison — a deadly, killing poison. And by this poison the `maqamat al-sharifah`, the noble stations, are destroyed; `talaf`, destruction and death, befalls them. The exemplar is the poison that was the cause of the destruction of the Natiq himself. The text adds `wa-s-summu alladhi talafa minhu al-Hasan 'alayhi-s-salam` — "and the poison from which al-Hasan, peace be upon him, was destroyed." By this same kind of poison al-Hasan met his destruction; in the Urdu idiom, `talaf hua`, he was destroyed by it. And further, `Mawlana Muslim bin Salwat wa Mawlana al-Shams bin Salwat... jami'an` — by this poison Mawlana Muslim and Mawlana al-Shams, all of them together, were destroyed as well.

Then the text turns to the second channel: `wa minha ma yaqa'u fi-l-ma'adin` — "and from it is that which falls into the minerals." Some of these forms of vices fall not upon vegetation but into the `ma'adin`, the minerals. And from them are made `suyuf wa sakakin wa nisal` — swords, knives, and arrowheads. From these forms, when they enter the minerals, come the swords and the knives and the arrowheads with which the noble stations are killed; with these weapons the assault is carried out upon the noble stations, `al-maqamat al-sharifah al-sabiq zikruha`, those whose mention has already preceded — the very swords with which the Imams were slain, the knives, the arrows. The forms whose origin is the two knots descend, and through their parts they take their revenge, whether lodged in mineral or in vegetation. The text confirms `yuftaku bihi fi-l-maqamat al-sharifah al-sabiq zikruha` — "with which an assault is made upon the noble stations whose mention has preceded."

The reasoning is then made explicit. `Li-anna hadhihi-l-qaba'is idha waqa'a minha 'ala-n-nabat kana dhalika al-nabat sumuman qatilan li-man yasiru ilayhi min al-maqamat al-sharifah al-masmumah` — "because these vices, when some of them fall upon the vegetation, that vegetation becomes a deadly poison for those of the noble stations, the poisoned ones, who go toward it." And `wa in waqa'a 'ala ma'danin kana minhu suyufun wa sakakinu wa nisalun li-n-nabli yuqtalu biha man qutila min al-maqamat al-sharifah` — "and if it falls upon a mineral, from it come swords, knives, and arrowheads for the bow, with which are killed those of the noble stations who are killed." The two channels are thus exactly parallel: vice upon vegetation yields the poison that slays from within, and vice upon mineral yields the blade and the arrow that slay from without.

Why do these poisoned things and weaponized things find their targets? The text answers `li-annaha taqa'u 'ala amthaliha min ma'adin wa-n-nabat al-madhmumah al-mal'unah` — "because they fall upon their counterparts, from the condemned and cursed minerals and vegetation." Like falls upon like; the poisoned vegetation and the weaponized mineral fall upon their own counterparts, which are themselves condemned and cursed. So it was said of the sword of Shimr — Shimr's very sword was described in just this way, as a thing of the cursed mineral that found its cursed counterpart.

### The Preservation of the Weapon: The Dark Atmosphere as Keeper

The text then states a teaching about the safekeeping of these instruments: `fa-kullu shay'in minha mahfuzun li-sahibihi wa-l-hafizu lahu huwa al-jaww al-zulmani` — "so every thing from it is preserved for its owner, and the preserver of it is the dark atmosphere." Every such poisoned or weaponized thing is `mahfuz`, kept and preserved, for its `sahib`, its owner — the one for whom it is destined. And its preserver, its keeper, is `al-jaww al-zulmani`, the dark atmosphere. The management of these things continues unbroken: where to keep each one, at what time to deliver it — these arrangements proceed under the keeping of the dark atmosphere, which is none other than the work of the two knots. The word `mahfuz` carries the sense of a thing kept safe, protected, held in reserve; its protection is carried out so that, when the one who is to be killed arrives, he can be killed with precisely the instrument prepared for him. It is preserved for its owner, and the preserver is the dark atmosphere, which is the two knots.

And this preservation does not operate arbitrarily. `Bi-mashi'at al-mudabbir wa mujib al-hikmah wa mizan al-'adli lillahi ta'ala` — "by the will of the Director, and the requirement of wisdom, and the scale of the justice of God, Most High." It proceeds by the `mashi'at al-mudabbir`, the will of the Director; by `mujib al-hikmah`, what wisdom requires; and by `mizan al-'adl`, the scale of the justice of God. The keeping of every poison and every blade for its appointed owner is, in its inner truth, an operation of divine will, divine wisdom, and divine justice held in perfect balance.

### The Two Eras and the Two Forms of Revenge: Sword-Ascent and Punishment-Descent

The text now distinguishes how the revenge of the slain opposites returns, according to the character of the Imam's era. `Li-anna al-maqama fi dhalika al-'asri in kana sahiba sayfin wa qatlin wa quwwatin wa ghalabatin kanat suwar al-maqtulin min al-azdad al-sa'idat ila-l-'uqdatain al-muqtassah min dhalika al-maqam al-sharif` — "because the station in that era, if he is the owner of the sword and killing and power and dominance, then the forms of the slain among the opposites ascend to the two knots, taking revenge from that noble station." If the Imam in a given era is `sahib sayf` — an owner of the sword, of killing, of power and dominance — then the forms of the opposites who were slain by his hand ascend to the two knots, and from there they take their revenge upon that very noble station that slew them. And the text adds `li-annaha tasiru mu'ajjalatan` — "because it proceeds swiftly": these ascended forms return with speed to the station of that cursed one, becoming devils, and attack — they assault that station with killing, taking revenge from those who had killed them.

But if the era is of a different character, the mode of revenge is correspondingly different. `Wa in kana dawr al-maqami fi dhalika al-'asri dawra awamirin wa nawahin wa iqamati hududin wa jaldin wa habsin wa nafyin wa ma yajri hadha-l-majra kanat suwar al-azdad al-jariyah 'alayhim dhalika 'inda mawtiha wa nuzuliha min dhalika al-barzakh sumuman kama sabaqa fi-l-qawl` — "and if the cycle of the station in that era is a cycle of commands and prohibitions, and the establishing of punishments, and flogging, and imprisonment, and exile, and what runs in this course, then the forms of the opposites upon whom these were executed, at the time of their death and their descent from that isthmus, are poisons, as has preceded in the discourse." If, that is, the Imam's era is one in which he is manifest, issuing `awamir`, commands, and `nawahi`, prohibitions; establishing `hudud`, the prescribed punishments; carrying out `jald`, flogging — `jald` means to whip — and imprisonment (`habs`) and exile (`nafy`) and whatever else runs in that course; then the forms of the opposites upon whom these penalties were executed descend, at their death, from that isthmus, and they descend as poisons, just as was described earlier. So the two eras yield two channels of return: the era of the sword sends the slain opposites' forms upward to the knots to come back as devils attacking, and the era of commands and punishments sends the executed opposites' forms downward from the isthmus as poisons.

This was the case, the discourse reminds us, when the Prophet and Sayyidna Ali, peace be upon him, faced opposition in their various battles — those slain there took their revenge in the manner described.

### The Purification of Retribution and the Promotion of Husayn's Gathering

Why must this entire economy of revenge unfold? The text supplies the reason in terms of the promotions that must be accomplished. The promotions, the `taraqqiyat`, of the gathering of Sayyidna Imam Husayn must take place. But these promotions cannot occur while wrongdoings remain unsettled. In that era there were `ziyadati`, transgressions — wrongs that issued even from the army that was Ali's, against those who opposed him; the killing of women happened, flogging happened, this and that took place. Now the revenge for these must be taken. The `azdad`, the opposites, take upon themselves their respective `jaza'`, their due recompense, for the transgressions that occurred, so that their `qisasat`, their retributions, are completed and their further `taraqqi`, their promotion, can proceed. This is a purification. The text marks it: `qisasat must be completed` so that the transgressions of the wronged opposites are settled and the onward promotion of the gathering of Imam Husayn becomes possible.

And whose `tadbir`, whose planning, is this — that these things should come swiftly to their conclusion? The planning is of the era, but it operates through the `jaww al-zulmani`, the dark atmosphere; the instruments are protected there, in the two knots, and their protection is carried out so that, like everything else, they are subject to the overarching plan. All these plans belong to God, Most High, and to the Imam of the time, that the retributions should be completed. The Imam is the `wasta`, the intermediary, through whom the giving and taking of retribution is brought to completion. This is the purpose of the whole arrangement.

### The Deliverer of the Poison and the Gathered Curses

The text then turns to the human agent who delivers the poison: `wa minha shakhsun yusallimu dhalika-s-summa ila dhalika al-maqam al-sharif 'alayhi afdal al-salam` — "and from them is a person who delivers that poison to that noble station, upon whom be the most excellent of peace." From among these accursed forms there comes a person who hands over the poison to the noble station. And this deliverer is himself, in his inner reality, a collection of curses — a gathering of the cursed form that assembles in the two knots. Just so was Ja'dah, the wife of Imam Hasan, peace and blessings be upon him: she was of this very kind, a gathered collection of curses who delivered the poison to her husband.

The text continues `wa qad yakunu min suwari ha'ula'i-l-mas'ulin minhum la'anatun min al-maqamat wa-l-hudud fa-yakunu aktharuhum fi zulmihim wa akadu li-'adawatihim wa shaytanatihim` — "and there may be from the forms of these accursed ones, curses from the stations and from the punishments, so that they are the most excessive in their oppression and the most confirmed in their enmity and their devilry." From among these forms there gather `la'anat`, curses — curses that issue from the noble stations and from the prescribed punishments. When these curses gather together with the conceptions of the opposites in the two knots, they produce people who are the most extreme in their oppression and the most firmly fixed in their enmity and their devilry, their `shaytanah`. The curse of the wronged station and the curse attached to the executed punishment do not vanish; they accumulate, and they take form in the worst of oppressors.

### Al-Hajjaj and Ibn Mahdi: Curses Made Manifest

Two great historical examples are given. The first is al-Hajjaj. Sayyidna Amir al-Mu'minin Ali, peace and blessings be upon him, had cursed the people of Iraq, the people of Kufa, who committed transgressions against him, abandoned him at his hour of need, and inflicted hardships upon him. He cursed them, foretelling that such a tyrant would be set over them as would visit upon them severe hardships and severe punishments. Those curses, gathered in the two knots, came together and manifested in al-Hajjaj — al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi, the greatest of tyrants. In him the gathered forms came together, and through him every manner of oppression was committed. The text reads `ka-l-Hajjaj alladhi zahara li-ahl al-'iraq` — "like al-Hajjaj who appeared to the people of Iraq" — and notes that he became manifest for the people of Iraq, and the discourse describes how it gathered `fi dhalika al-barzakh al-muzlim`, in that dark isthmus, which is the two knots, `ila an zaharat 'ala ahl al-'iraq`, until it manifested upon the people of Iraq, so that whatever issued from his actions among them came to pass. Al-Hajjaj, it is stressed, did not torment only the followers of Ali; rather, whoever came in his path he gave hardship to, or killed — his calamity was a destroyer for all of creation, `wa li-jami' al-khalq`, and for that reason `that great calamity is a destroyer for the whole world`.

The second example is Ibn Mahdi. `Wa ka-ibni Mahdi alladhi kana min la'anat al-hudud wa tasawwur al-azdad` — "and like Ibn Mahdi, who was from the curses of the punishments and the conception of the opposites." Ibn Mahdi too arose from the `la'anat al-hudud`, the curses attached to the prescribed punishments, and from the `tasawwur al-azdad`, the conception of the opposites. The text continues `tasawwur al-azdad li-ahl al-tihamah fa-jtama'at fi dhalika al-barzakh wa subbat 'ala ula'ika al-mudillat al-mala'in` — "the conception of the opposites for the people of Tihamah, so it gathered in that isthmus and was poured upon those misguided, cursed ones." The conception of the opposites was poured out upon the people of Tihamah; it gathered in that isthmus — in the two knots — and was poured upon those misguided and cursed ones. May Allah keep us in His protection from that, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny, the blessings of Allah be upon them all: `a'adhana Allahu min dhalika bi-haqqi Muhammadin wa alihi-t-tahirin salawatullahi 'alayhim ajma'in`.

So the principle stands clearly: from the conceptions of the opposites that gather in the two knots, and the curses pronounced upon them from the stations and the punishments, all descend together and produce the worst people the world has known — al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi and Ibn Mahdi being the named exemplars. When the curse upon those who oppressed Ali and al-Husayn takes form, it descends upon the people of that later era; al-Hajjaj is the proof.

### The Meaning of the Curse: Distancing from Mercy

A question of ruling arises: is the curse the opposite of prayer? The answer is given precisely. A `la'ana`, a curse, means to seek that God, Most High, distance the person from His mercy — `la'ana Allahu min rahmatihi`, may God curse him by removing him from His mercy. To curse is to seek the distancing of someone from divine `rahmah`, divine mercy. And from this follows a point of practice in supplication: in prayer, seeking `tarahhum`, the showing of mercy, for the opposites is not permissible — but cursing them is permissible. One may not pray that mercy be shown to the `azdad`; one may pray that they be distanced from mercy. As for who accepts the curse: when we supplicate, it is the Lord of the Time who accepts; and the meaning of the curse here is, again, `la'ana Allahu min rahmatihi`, the seeking of distance from God's mercy. Through this very economy the promotion of the `awliya`, the friends of God, is also accomplished, for the giving and taking between them and the opposites must be brought to completion. The opposites, it must be remembered, do not oppress only the believers; they let loose a flood of oppression upon the entire nation — which is precisely why Ali cursed those who had abandoned him, foretelling that one would be set over them who would inflict great oppressions, not upon the believers alone, but upon all who had forsaken him.

### The Death of the Opposites: The Soul Spreading into the Body

We now enter the Fifth Chapter, which concerns the sixth Provider — `al-Bab al-Khamis fi raziq al-sadis`, in five sections — and we take up its first section. `Wa amma ajsamu ha'ula'i-l-azdad wa nufusuhum fa-inna-n-nafsa al-wahid minhum 'inda mawtihi tashri'u fi jismihi wa yasirani shay'an wahidan wa yastahilani ila-t-turab` — "and as for the bodies of these opposites and their souls, the single soul from among them, at its death, spreads throughout its body, and the two become one thing, and they both transform into dust." For the opposites, at the moment of death, the one soul spreads out through its body. And here the contrast with the believer is decisive and must be held in mind: in the believer, the bodily parts become soul — the body is, as it were, raised into the condition of soul; whereas in the opposite, the soul becomes body. The process is exactly reversed. A question is raised — does the soul spread, growing smaller and then diffusing? The answer is that the soul spreads out and takes on a bodily form; the body does not grow, but the soul diffuses through it. And it spreads in such a way that the punishments of the grave are felt by it. As for the future of that soul: no one can destroy it, for it is a thing created by God; whether a soul be evil or good, none can bring its existence into non-existence. So the soul, wherever it goes, will be a cause of torment to itself until it has completed its retributions — after which it will be given an invitation, and it will accept that invitation, and its very nature will change.

The body and the soul become one thing, and the two transform into dust, into `turab`. Then `thumma yas'adani bukharan fa-yanzilani mataran` — "then the two ascend as vapor, then descend as rain." The body and soul, having become dust, ascend together as `bukhar`, vapor, and then descend as `matar`, rain.

### The Disasters of the Opposites: Lightning, Thunderbolt, Flood, and Hail

From this rain of the opposites a train of calamities is produced. `Wa yuhdithu min dhalika al-matari al-buruqat al-muhliqat wa-s-sawa'iq al-mutlifat wa-s-suyul al-mukhribat wa-l-barad al-mudirr` — "and there are produced from that rain the destructive lightnings, and the annihilating thunderbolts, and the ruinous floods, and the harmful hail." From the rain of the opposites come `buruqat`, lightnings that destroy; `sawa'iq`, thunderbolts that annihilate; `suyul`, floods that ruin; and `barad`, hail that harms. The text concludes `wa dhalika min fi'l al-muzaddat fi jami' al-awqat` — "and that is from the action of the opposites in all times." These disasters are the very action of the `muzaddat`, the opposites, and their work continues in all times. The calamities that occur in the world — severe cold and severe heat, the harmful hail, the rain, the floods, the thunderbolts, the thunder and lightning — all bear the imprint of the opposites' bodies and souls, for the conception that reaches the two knots is precisely body and soul, and these things issue from it.

### The Substance Becomes Vegetation and Animal: The Onset of the Nearer Torment

The text continues `wa yansabbani ila-l-ardi fa-yasirani nabatan wa hayawanan wa yastaghriqu bihim al-'azab al-adna` — "and the two are poured upon the earth, and they become vegetation and animal, and the nearer torment overtakes them." The vapors form and rise, then descend upon the whole earth, and there the substance of the opposites becomes `nabat`, vegetation, and `hayawan`, animal. Those who eat of this take their sustenance from it. And by this very means the `'azab al-adna`, the nearer torment, is unleashed upon them and overtakes them.

This nearer torment is then unfolded through a sequence of seven descending levels, the `adrak` — likened to seven dragons of the torment of God, from which Allah has kept us and His friends safe.

### The First Level: Al-Rijs, the Human Tunic

`Wa awwaluha dark al-rijs wa huwa qamis al-bashar` — "and the first of them is the level of al-rijs, and it is the tunic of the human being." The first level of torment is named `al-rijs`. (Our master, it is recalled, disliked the word `qamis`, "shirt"; if one said `qamis` he would correct it to `kurta`, the tunic.) This first level is the `qamis al-bashar`, the human tunic.

The mechanism is set out: `wa yasiru dhalika al-mughtadhi bihi nutfatan` — "and that one who took nourishment from it becomes a sperm-drop." The one who consumed the poisoned vegetation or animal becomes a `nutfah`. Then `wa yartaqi ila rahimin fa-yakhruju min batni ummihi janinan fi qamisin min qumus al-zanj wa-z-zabab wa-l-barbar wa-t-turk wa ghayrihim mina-lladhina la yasluhu mukhatabat ahl al-haqq wa la yanhajuna minhaj ar-rashad` — "and it ascends to a womb and emerges from its mother's belly as a fetus in a tunic from the tunics of the Zanj, the Zabab, the Barbar, the Turk, and others besides them, from among those for whom addressing the people of truth is not suitable, nor do they follow the path of guidance." The `nutfah` ascends to a womb and emerges as a fetus in a `qamis`, a tunic, drawn from peoples such as the Zanj, the Zabab, the Barbar, the Turk, and the like — peoples for whom, in this state, the addressing of the people of truth is unsuitable, and who do not walk the path of `rashad`, of right guidance.

And he does not remain in one tunic. `Wa la yazalu yantaqilu min qamisin ila qamisin ila an yastawfi fi kulli naw'in min hadhihi al-anwa'i sab'ina qamisan` — "and he continues to transfer from one tunic to another until he completes, in every type of these types, seventy tunics." He passes from tunic to tunic, completing seventy `qamis` in each `naw'`, each type. A point of arithmetic and of mercy is added here: he does not necessarily traverse the whole sequence of tunics. It is possible that after entering one tunic he returns from it; not every person must complete all of them. Only a very few, those of the most extreme stubbornness, complete all these tunics.

A question of conscience is raised, which the teacher answers carefully. It does not appear, to the eye, that so many whole nations are in torment — there are Turks and others, and in our day there is civilization and refinement among them. Has God, then, concealed this torment within the nations? The answer turns on a principle established earlier: the ascending and the descending are gathered in one and the same place. The ascending ones have arrived at a single platform together with the descending ones, just as was explained by the example of the animals — all are animals, but there is the lawful animal, which is ascending, and the unlawful animal, which is descending, and both stand upon one platform. In the same way, among these peoples there are the Zanj who are descending, and there are also those who, upon that very path, have turned back — for turning back is possible. The manifestations are not bound to a fixed millennium for these descending souls as they are in other reckonings; rather, in one level, across seventy types, he wears seventy tunics.

### The Second Level: Khums al-'Aks, the Human-Like Creatures

`Thumma kharaja mina-l-azdad wa-l-mumtazid ila-l-khums al-'aks wa huwa al-dark al-thani al-mumathil li-t-tarkib al-bashariyyah wa hum al-qird wa-d-dubb wa-n-nasnas wa-l-ghawl wa-l-'izar wa amthal dhalika` — "then he emerges from the opposites and the blended ones into the khums al-'aks, and it is the second level, similar to the human composition, and they are the ape, the bear, the nasnas, the ghoul, the lizard, and the likes of these." The second level is `khums al-'aks`, the quintessence of reversal, and it is `al-dark al-thani`. It resembles the human composition: where the first tunic was human, this second is human-like. Its creatures are the `qird`, the ape; the `dubb`, the bear; the `nasnas` — a creature said to dwell in the Arab lands, a kind of half-creature; the `ghawl`, the ghoul; the `'izar`, the lizard; and others of their kind, all bearing a likeness to the human form.

`Wa yuslaku bihi fi kulli naw'in min hadhihi al-anwa' sab'ina qamisan ila an yastawfi jami'aha` — "and he is made to pass, in every type of these types, through seventy tunics, until he completes them all." Through seventy `qamis` in each type he is made to pass until all are completed. And `wa huwa fi jami'i hadhihi al-qumus al-waqtiyyah yatahaqqaqu lahu wujud al-'azab wa annahu waqa'a fi dhalika mukhalafat arbab al-haqq` — "and he, in all these temporal tunics, has the existence of torment confirmed for him, and that he fell into that through opposition to the lords of truth." In all these `qumus waqtiyyah`, these temporal tunics, it is confirmed and made real to him that he is in a state of torment, and that he fell into it through `mukhalafah`, opposition, to the `arbab al-haqq`, the lords of truth. And `wa yasiru 'arifan bihim mutahaqqiqan lahum 'arifan li-ahl al-batil alladhina wasala bihim wa waqa'a fi adrak al-'azab` — "and he becomes one who knows them, confirmed for them, knowing the people of falsehood through whom he reached this and fell into the levels of torment." He becomes one who knows the lords of truth and one who knows the `ahl al-batil`, the people of falsehood, through following whom he fell into the levels of torment. It is made plain to him; he himself sees why he has come into this state.

### The Third Level: Khums al-'Ars, the Predators

`Thumma yuslaku bihi fi khums al-'ars wa huwa al-dark al-thalith wa huwa siba' al-barri wa-l-bahri ka-l-usud wa-n-numur wa-d-dhi'ab wa amthalu dhalika min as-siba' al-bahriyyah` — "then he is made to pass into khums al-'ars, and it is the third level, and it is the predators of the land and the sea, like lions and tigers and wolves, and the likes of these from the predators of the sea." The third level is `khums al-'ars`, the predators of land and sea: lions, `usud`; tigers, `numur`; wolves, `dhi'ab`; and their like among the predators of the sea. The text defines the class precisely: `wa 'ala-l-jumlah kullu dhi nabin wa mikhlabin barriyyin wa bahriyyin` — "and in sum, every creature possessing a canine tooth and a claw, of the land and of the sea." Every creature with a `nab`, a canine — the teeth that tear and rend — and a `mikhlab`, a claw, whether of land or of sea. (This is precisely why the unlawful animals are unlawful, for the unlawful animals are those that possess canines and claws, while the lawful animals do not.) `Ila an yastawfi fi kulli naw'in min hadhihi al-anwa' sab'ina qamisan` — through seventy tunics in each type until completed.

And in this level a new degree of awareness is granted. `Wa huwa 'alimun bima huwa fihi min al-'azab mutahaqqiqan lahu 'arifan li-ahl al-haqq wa ahl al-batil` — "and he is aware of the torment he is in, confirmed for him, knowing the people of truth and the people of falsehood." He feels the torment; he is conscious that this is the punishment of God. For seeing human beings while himself in the state of an animal, God has granted his mind enough power to understand that he is in this state for a definite reason — for his prior opposition. It is observed that this awareness was not mentioned as being granted in the first, human tunic; it is in these animal levels that the cognition of the cause becomes explicit.

### The Fourth Level: Khums al-Khars, the Crawling Creatures

`Thumma yuslaku bihi fi khums al-khars wa huwa al-dark al-rabi' wa huwa hawamm al-barri wa-l-bahri ka-l-afa'i wa-l-hayyat wa-l-'aqarib wa ghayriha mina-l-anwa' allati tushbihuhum fa-yatakhammasu fi kulli naw'in fi hadhihi al-anwa' sab'ina qamisan` — "then he is made to pass into khums al-khars, and it is the fourth level, and it is the crawling creatures of land and sea, like vipers and snakes and scorpions and others of the types that resemble them, so he takes on his form in every type of these types for seventy tunics." The fourth level is `khums al-khars`, named for muteness, `khars`, for in it he is mute and cannot speak. It is the `hawamm`, the crawling creatures of land and sea — the whole class that creeps upon the ground: vipers (`afa'i`), snakes (`hayyat`), scorpions (`'aqarib`), and others resembling them. Through seventy tunics in each type he is made to pass. The text continues `ila an yastawfi jami' dhalika wa huwa mutahaqqiqun bima huwa fihi min al-'azab 'arifan li-ahl al-haqq wa ahl al-batil 'aliman annahu sara ila ma sara min al-'iqab bi-mukhalafati arbab al-haqq wa ittiba' ahl al-batil` — "until he completes all of that, confirmed in the torment he is in, knowing the people of truth and the people of falsehood, knowing that he reached the punishment he reached through opposition to the lords of truth and following the people of falsehood." Here the muteness is to be understood with care: in this level there is dumbness in the sense that the specific articulate speech of the human is absent — there are indeed sounds, the hiss of the viper and such, screaming and crying out, but no human speech.

### The Fifth Level: Al-Najis, the Predatory Birds

`Thumma yuslaku bihi fi dark al-khums al-musamma bi-n-najis wa hum tayr al-barri wa-l-bahri 'ala shakilat zawat al-jawarih ila an yastawfi fi kulli naw'in minha sab'ina qamisan` — "then he is made to pass into the fifth level, named al-najis, and they are the birds of the land and the sea, in the manner of the birds of prey, until he completes seventy tunics in each type of them." The fifth level is `al-najis`, and it is the `tayr al-barri wa-l-bahri`, the birds of land and sea, after the pattern of the `zawat al-jawarih`, the predatory birds — those that wound and seize. Through seventy tunics in each type until completed. So the five levels run in order: the first is human; the second human-like; the third the tearing predators; the fourth the crawling creatures; and the fifth the flying birds of prey — one human level and four animal levels, making five.

The text affirms, as in the prior levels, `wa huwa fi jami' dhalika mutahaqqiqan li-l-'azab alladhi laqiyahu 'arifan bihi muta'alliman minhu 'arifan bi-ahl al-haqq wa ahl al-batil 'aliman annahu sara ila ma sara ilayhi mina-l-'iqab bi-mukhalafati arbab al-haqq wa ittiba' ahl al-batil` — "and he, in all of that, is confirmed in the torment which he meets, knowing it, taking pain from it, knowing the people of truth and the people of falsehood, knowing that he reached the punishment he reached through opposition to the lords of truth and following the people of falsehood." In every one of these states he feels the punishment, is pained by it, and knows both the people of truth and the people of falsehood, and knows that he has reached his punishment through his opposition to the masters of truth and his following of the people of falsehood.

### Knowledge as the Precondition of Punishment and Reward

A teaching of profound consequence is now stated. `Wa lawla ma'rifatuhu bi-dhalika la-ma kana li-l-'azab 'alamun wa la li-th-thawab rahatun` — "and were it not for his knowledge of that, there would be no pain in the punishment, nor any comfort in the reward." Were it not for the soul's `ma'rifah`, its knowledge of its condition, there would be no `'alam`, no pain, in the punishment, and no `rahah`, no comfort, in the reward. Knowledge is thus the very precondition of both `'azab` and `thawab`. He feels that the punishment he receives is on account of the specific sins and wrongdoings he committed; these are all levels of sensation, and without the knowing of one's state neither torment nor reward could register at all.

### The Salvation Path of the Lawful Animals

A clarifying question is raised: are the lawful animals separate from this whole descending economy? The answer is firm: yes, they are not in these levels at all; these levels of torment have no connection with the lawful animals. The level of the lawful animals is for ascending. When they ascend, they do not pass from one tunic into another in this manner; they go directly. Suppose a pigeon is slaughtered: it does not enter these tormented forms. Rather, its part goes upward — as much of it as becomes the human body, to that extent its `najat`, its salvation, is accomplished. To the degree that its part becomes the human body, that portion is saved; and the rest of the part, wherever it goes, has its share from that. The lawful animal does not return by this descending path; it returns by the lawful, ascending path. This is the foundation, too, of the ruling that follows in due course.

### The Sixth Level: Al-Naqs, and the Loss of Knowledge

`Fa-idha stawfa jami' hadhihi-l-khumus sulika bihi fi-l-dark al-sadis al-musamma bi-n-naqs` — "so when he has completed all of these five quintessences, he is made to pass into the sixth level, named al-naqs," deficiency. After the five `khums` levels are completed, the soul is made to pass into the sixth level, the `dark al-sadis`, named `al-naqs`. And the defining feature of this level is the loss of knowledge. `Wa lam yabqa ma'ahu 'ilmun bima huwa fihi li-annahu qad faraqa 'alam al-hiss` — "and there remains with him no knowledge of the state he is in, because he has departed from the world of sense." In this sixth level, the `naks`, no knowledge of his condition remains, because he has now left the `'alam al-hiss`, the world of sense; the sense has gone, and with it the awareness that accompanied the earlier levels.

This is given scriptural confirmation. `Wa li-dhalika qala Allahu ta'ala: wa minkum man yuraddu ila ardhal al-'umur likay la ya'lama min ba'di 'ilmin shay'an` — "and for that reason God, Most High, said: 'and among you is he who is returned to the most decrepit of age, so that he knows nothing after having known.'" (Qur'an 16:70.) Just as among human beings there is one who is turned back to `ardhal al-'umur`, the most abject and decrepit old age, so that after possessing knowledge he comes to know nothing — his mind failing by degrees — so too here. The text glosses: `fa-hadha misdaqu ta'miyati-l-amri 'alayhi wa salbihi ma kana ya'lamu` — "so this is the verification of the matter being obscured for him and the snatching away of what he used to know." This is the manifestation of `ta'miyah`, the matter being made blind and obscure to him, and of the stripping away of what he once knew. A question arises whether the remembering and forgetting belong to the body or to the soul; the answer comes with the description of the plant state that now follows.

### The Forbidden Plant: The Faculty of Growth Alone

`Fa-idha balagha hadha-l-hadda lam yabqa ma'ahu ghayru 'alat al-nama'` — "so when he reaches this limit, nothing remains with him except the faculty of growth." When the descending soul reaches this limit, nothing remains to it but the `'alat al-nama'`, the instrument or faculty of growth. He enters the plant state. In this condition, the soul is stripped of all things save the faculty of growth: the growing soul, the `nafs al-namiyah`, remains, but the sensing soul is taken away. The animal had known what to eat and what to refuse, for sense gives rise to knowledge; here that sensing soul is removed, and only the vegetative power of growth persists.

`Alladhi huwa-n-nabat al-mahzur al-qatil al-muhlik li-l-hayawan` — "which is the forbidden plant, the killer, the destroyer of the living being." This sixth-level form is `al-nabat al-mahzur`, the forbidden plant — a plant that is a killer and a destroyer of the `hayawan`, the living being, destructive to both animal and man. (The word is `hayawan`, the living being; in the lexicon a vowel stands upon both the `ha` and the `ya`.) And a principle governing both directions of the soul's journey is stated: while descending, souls descend from bad bodies; while ascending, they ascend from good bodies. It is the same vegetative state through which they pass in either direction — but in ascent it is from good bodies, and in descent from bad bodies.

### The Seventh Level: Al-Daks, the Wicked Stone

`Wa sulika bihi fi-l-dark al-sabi' alladhi yusamma al-daks wa huwa al-ma'din al-sanj wa-l-hajar al-wasikh al-khabith ila an yastawfi fi kulli naw'in min anwa'ihi sab'ina qamisan` — "and he is made to pass into the seventh level, which is named al-daks, and it is the sanj mineral and the filthy, wicked stone, until he completes seventy tunics in every type of its types." The seventh level is `al-daks`, the mineral state: the `ma'din al-sanj`, a hard mineral, and the `hajar al-wasikh al-khabith`, the dirty, filthy, wicked stone. Through seventy tunics in each of its types he is made to pass. An important exclusion is noted: the descending souls do not enter the good stones — such as the stones of a mosque or the stones of the Ka'bah; all of these are excluded. Those good stones are entered when ascending; it is in descending that the soul enters the bad and base things.

### The Arithmetic of the Seven Levels: Seventy Types, Seventy Tunics

`Wa fi jumlat al-qawl inna li-kulli darkin min hadha-l-adrak sab'una naw'an, sab'ina qamisan wa sab'ina naw'an` — "and in summary, for each level of these levels there are seventy types — seventy tunics and seventy types." The reckoning is set out plainly. For every `dark` among these `adrak`, the seven levels, there are seventy `naw'`, seventy types, and within each there are seventy `qamis`, seventy tunics. Seventy multiplied by seventy yields four thousand nine hundred — 4900 — manifestations per level. As the teacher works it through: in one level there are seventy types, and in each type seventy tunics; in every tunic there are seventy kinds. The `qamis` is reckoned as one type, a distinct garment, the passage from one animal to another, a gradation of stages and grades. Thus the level of the birds of land and sea, for instance, is one `dark`, and within it lie its seventy tunics, and the full multiplication gives the 4900 manifestations of that single level. May Allah, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny, keep us and all our brethren safe from that: `wa billahi ta'ala wa bi-jami'i ikhwanina min dhalika bi-haqqi Muhammadin wa alihi-t-tahirin salawatullahi 'alayhim ajma'in`.

### Two Paths to the Mineral State, and the Mercy of Early Return

A teaching about how souls arrive at the mineral state, and about the variability of the descent, is given. The fallen things reach the mineral condition by two paths. The first is that, while falling, their intentions were so utterly bad that they became stones directly. The second is that they first ascended and then fell back. And the descent is not uniform for all: many souls return after only one level, some after two, some after three; some return after all seven levels; and after the seven levels, some reach the punishment of the peripheries and return from there. Not everyone completes the full sequence — the variability is itself a mercy. The arithmetic is restated for clarity: seven times seven is forty-nine, hence the figure of 4900 when extended; in one level there are seventy types, and in each level the 4900 manifestations arise from seventy types each bearing seventy tunics.

### The Shari'ah Ruling: Why Certain Meats Are Forbidden

From this whole doctrine follows a ruling of the sacred law. In the `shari'ah`, the animals whose meat is forbidden are forbidden for precisely this reason: that they are in the descending levels, in the state of punishment. The ascending animals, by contrast, are permissible to eat. This is the inner ground of the dietary law: the forbidden creatures are those traversing the downward `dark`, while the lawful creatures are those upon the ascending path. We seek refuge with Allah, and for all our brethren, from these matters, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny.

### The Four Peripheries: Exile of the Most Stubborn Enemies

We now take up the second section of the Fifth Chapter, concerning the third pavilion — `al-fasl al-thani min al-bab al-khamis fi-s-suradiq al-thalith`. `Thumma innahu yughmadu ba'da dhalika bi-azdadi al-maqamat al-ilahiyyah wa-r-rusul wa-l-anbiya fa-tunfa ila arba' al-ard al-kharijah min hadd al-i'tidal allati hiya ghayr maskunatin li-anna biha ahwalan 'azimatan` — "then after that he is immersed with the opposites of the divine stations, and the messengers, and the prophets, and they are exiled to the four corners of the earth which lie outside the boundary of moderation, which are uninhabited, because in them are great horrors." After the completion of the seven levels, the most stubborn enemies, the `mutakallifin`, are immersed together with the opposites of the divine stations, the messengers, and the prophets. They are then exiled to the four corners of the earth — the four regions that lie outside `hadd al-i'tidal`, the boundary of moderation, where the climate is extreme. These regions are uninhabited, for in them dwell great and dire horrors, `ahwalan 'azimatan`. It is to be stressed that only the `mutakallifin`, the most burdened and stubborn enemies, reach this peripheral punishment; most souls simply return from the seventh level.

### The North: The Frozen Cold of Zamharir

`Ahaduha nahiyat al-shimal... wa huwa madhi'u-l-kawkab al-musamma Jady` — "one of them is the northern region... it is the place of the star named Jady." The first region is the north, the place of the star `Jady`. It is a land frozen, dark, shadowy, frightening, and terrifying, where the sun is absent for six months at a time — for six months the sun does not rise there. `Wa biha yunsabbu bard al-zamharir al-mujmid al-murhiq al-muharriq` — "and in it is poured the cold of the zamharir, the freezing, the exhausting, the burning." There is poured into it the intense cold of `zamharir` — a cold that freezes, that annihilates, and that, in its extremity, burns like fire. Here a definition is supplied: `zamharir` is a mist or wind of intense cold, while `asir` is the mist of intense heat, the sphere of fire. Zamharir is the mist of cold; asir is the mist of heat.

`Wa biha kawnu azdad maqamat 'alayhim al-salam fi hayawanatin ha'ilatin ka-s-sukhur al-jamidah la haraka fiha ghayra harakat al-ra'ash min shiddat al-bard wa-l-yubs` — "and in it is the existence of the opposites of the stations of the Imams, peace be upon them, in terrifying animals like frozen rocks, with no movement in them except the movement of shivering from the intensity of the cold and the dryness." In this northern region exist the opposites of the stations of the Imams, peace be upon them, in the form of terrifying animals resembling frozen mountains — rocks in the shape of animals, in which there is no movement save the trembling of shivering, from the severity of cold and dryness, resembling minerals. And they are punished by the cold of zamharir. A subtle point is made: although those who have passed into the mineral stage had no sensation, here sensation has been re-created in them, for without it the punishment could not be felt — and punishment they certainly undergo. `Wa hiya mu'azzabatun bi-bard al-zamharir muta'allimatun bi-bardihi al-lafi' li-ghayat al-'alam 'alimatun bima hiya fihi min alim al-'azab wa shadid al-'iqab` — "and it is punished by the cold of the zamharir, pained by its piercing cold to the utmost degree of pain, knowing what it is in of painful punishment and severe chastisement." They are pained by the piercing cold to the very limit of suffering, and they know what painful punishment and severe chastisement they endure.

### The South: The Burning Heat of Asir

`Thumma nahiyat al-junub wa hiya madar Suhayl` — "then the southern region, and it is the orbit of Suhayl," the star Canopus. The second region is the south, the orbit of the star Suhayl, where it is day for a full six months. `Wa biha yunsabbu hararat al-asir wa hiya al-har al-muharriqah wa sumum al-muhliqah` — "and in it is poured the heat of the asir, and it is the burning heat and the annihilating poison-winds." There is poured into it the heat of `asir`, which is the sphere of fire: a burning heat and `sumum`, poisonous winds, that annihilate. `Wa biha 'azab azdad al-rusul wa-l-anbiya fi hayawanatin kibarin shawihat al-khalq ka-amthal al-jibal la hawassun laha zahiratun bal tuhissu bima sihiya min 'azim al-'azab wa 'azim al-'iqab` — "and in it is the punishment of the opposites of the messengers and the prophets, in large animals of ugly creation like mountains, with no apparent senses, but rather they sense the great punishment and great chastisement they are in." Here is the punishment of the opposites of the messengers and the prophets, in the form of huge animals of hideous shape, as tall as mountains, which have no apparent outward senses, yet which nonetheless feel the great punishment and great chastisement they are in. So the distinction between the two regions is exact: the north holds the opposites of the stations of the Imams, and the south holds the opposites of the messengers and the prophets.

### The East: The Lofty Mountains and the Assistants of the Prophets' Opposites

`Thumma nahiyat al-sharq min al-ard min haythu tatlu' al-shams wa hiya jibalun shamikhatun wa'iratun 'aliyatun takadu tabulughu dhuraha ila kurrat al-asir` — "then the eastern region of the earth, from where the sun rises, and it has lofty, impassable, high mountains whose peaks almost reach the sphere of the asir." The third region is the east, the place of sunrise, possessed of lofty mountains — impassable and high — whose peaks almost reach the sphere of fire, so elevated are they. `Wa fiha 'azab a'wan azdad al-rusul wa-l-anbiya` — "and in it is the punishment of the assistants of the opposites of the messengers and the prophets." Here is the punishment of the `a'wan`, the assistants and helpers, of the opposites of the messengers and the prophets — together with their learned ones, who were rebellious against the messengers and prophets and spread enmity against them — in the form of terrifying animals, smaller than those of the south, stationed upon those mountains. They do not descend to the earth, nor do they turn from their place; as if shackled and chained, they are pained by the severity of the punishment and chastisement they endure, knowing what they suffer and from what offense.

### The West: The Sea of the Jinn and the Assistants of the Imams' Opposites

`Thumma nahiyat al-dubur wa hiya maghrib al-shams` — "then the western region, and it is the setting place of the sun." The fourth region is the west, the place of sunset. In it lies a vast sea — dark, terrifying, vast, and turbulent — called the Sea of the Jinn, whose waves are like mountains; this is the Pacific Ocean. `Wa fihi 'azab a'wan azdad al-maqamat 'alayhim al-salam wa 'ulama'ihim al-muzakhrifin li-n-nas al-qawl wa-l-balaghah wa qad 'adu hayawanatin mutashakkilatan qara fi qa'r dhalika al-bahr ya'kulu ba'duhum ba'dan wa yasulu ba'duhum 'ala ba'din ashadda al-'azab wa a'zama al-'iqab 'alimin bima hum fihi min dhalika al-'azab` — "and in it is the punishment of the assistants of the opposites of the stations of the Imams, peace be upon them, and their learned ones who embellish speech and rhetoric for the people; and they have returned as misshapen animals settled in the depths of that sea, devouring one another and assaulting one another with the most severe punishment and greatest chastisement, knowing the punishment they are in." Here is the punishment of the assistants of the opposites of the stations of the Imams, and of their learned ones who adorn speech and rhetoric to deceive the people. They have returned as misshapen sea-animals in the depths of that ocean, devouring one another and attacking one another with the severest punishment, knowing fully what torment they endure. The punishment of the assistants is, in this respect, lighter than that of the principals: the symmetry across the four regions is thus complete — north, the opposites of the stations of the Imams; south, the opposites of the messengers and prophets; east, the assistants of the opposites of the messengers and prophets; west, the assistants of the opposites of the stations of the Imams.

### The Rotation Through the Four Regions

`Wa kullama istawfa ahlu kulli nahiyatin min hadhihi al-nawahi al-arba' 'azaba tilka al-'idah ila nahiyatin min hadhihi al-nawahi al-arba' hatta yakulu 'azabuha fi jami' al-nawahi 'azanaAllahu wa jami'a ikhwanina min dhalika bi-haqqi Muhammadin wa alihi-t-tahirin` — "and whenever the people of each of these four regions complete the punishment of that period, they are returned to another of these four regions, until their punishment is fulfilled in all the regions. May Allah protect us and all our brethren from that, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny." The souls do not remain in one periphery alone. When the people of one region have completed the punishment of their term there, they are turned to another of the four regions, and so on, until the punishment has been undergone in all four — the soul rotated from the northern punishment to the southern, then to the eastern, then to the western — so that the `'azab al-atraf`, the punishment of the peripheries, is endured in every direction. May Allah keep us and all our brethren safe from that, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny.

### The Saying of Sayyidna al-Mu'ayyad: Slipping from the Luminous Center

This entire teaching, the text declares, is the very reality of a saying of Sayyidna al-Mu'ayyad. `Wa hadha haqiqatu qawli Sayyidina al-Mu'ayyad, 'ala Allahu qudsahu, fi-s-sab'in min majalisihi al-hikmati min al-mi'at al-ula haythu qala: wa man mada 'ala ghayri hadhihi al-nisbati zallat 'an al-markaz al-nurani qadamuhu wa inqata'at 'an wilayati awliya'illahi ta'ala 'ala qadr 'uluwwi martabatihi wa sumuwwi ismihi` — "and this is the reality of the saying of our master al-Mu'ayyad, may God sanctify his soul, in the seventieth of his Majalis al-Hikmah from the first hundred, where he said: 'and whoever proceeds upon other than this proportion, his foot has slipped from the luminous center, and his connection to the wilayah of the friends of God, Most High, is severed, in measure of the loftiness of his rank and the exaltation of his name.'" This is the reality of what Sayyidna al-Mu'ayyad set down in the seventieth of his Majalis al-Hikmah, from the first hundred: that whoever proceeds upon other than the right proportion, the `nisbah`, has his foot slip from `al-markaz al-nurani`, the luminous center, and is cut off from the `wilayah` of the friends of God — and the severance is measured according to the loftiness of his rank and the exaltation of his name.

`Fa-sara mun'akisan taratan fi muqasati harr al-asir wa taratan bi-bard al-zamharir wa taratan fi-l-ghutati fi qa'r al-buhur` — "and so he became inverted, at times in the afflictions of the heat of the asir, at times in the cold of the zamharir, and at times plunging into the depths of the seas." Such a one becomes `mun'akis`, inverted, turning at one time in the calamities of the heat of asir, at another in the cold of zamharir, and at another plunging into the depths of the seas — precisely the rotation through the peripheries that has been described.

### The Three-Branched Shadow: The Periphery of Three Dimensions

`Intalaqu ila zillin dhi thalathi shu'abin la zalilin wa la yughni min al-lahab` — "they proceed to a shadow with three branches, neither cooling nor availing against the flame." (Qur'an 77:30–31.) They proceed toward a shadow of three branches — a shade that gives no coolness and avails nothing against the flame of the fire. `Wa la yazaluna kadhalika fi hadha-l-'azab ila ayyam al-ba'th fi zillin dhi thalathi shu'ab` — "and they cease not to be thus in this punishment until the days of the resurrection, in a shadow with three branches." They remain in this very punishment, passing from one torment to the three-branched torment, until the days of resurrection. And the three branches are identified: they are the three dimensions — length, breadth, and depth — the threefold periphery already mentioned, the three valleys of the peripheral punishment.

### The Nearer Punishment, the Inhabited Quarter, and the Greater Punishment to Come

`Wa huwa al-'azab al-adna ila an ya'udu ila rub' al-maskun ila an taquma al-qiyamah 'ala dhikrihi al-salam` — "and it is the nearer punishment, until they return to the inhabited quarter, until the Resurrection is established, upon its mention be peace." All that has been described — the seven levels and the punishment of the four peripheries — is `al-'azab al-adna`, the nearer punishment. It endures for a time, until the souls are returned to `rub' al-maskun`, the inhabited quarter of the earth, where they remain until the Resurrection is established, upon whose mention be peace. `Thumma 'uidu ila-r-rub' al-maskun` — "then they are returned to the inhabited quarter." From the four peripheries — the uninhabited extremities — they are taken out and brought back into the inhabited portion of the world, the `rub' al-maskun`, the one-fourth part of the earth where human beings dwell.

`Thumma 'uidu ila-r-rub' al-maskun alladhi huwa lahum fi janbi dhalik jannatun wa jannatun bi-l-mizaj wa-l-muntazij ila an yazharu bi-'alamat al-alfiyyah wa yuhdaruna bayna yadayhi li-l-hisab fa-yustaqbaluna bi-l-'azab al-akbar` — "then they are returned to the inhabited quarter, which for them, in comparison to that, is a garden and a paradise, through the temperament and the mixture, until they appear with the signs of the millennium and are brought before Him for the accounting, and then they are met with the greater punishment." When they are returned to the inhabited quarter, that quarter is for them — in comparison with the four peripheries they have endured — like a garden and a paradise; and they come to it through `mizaj` and `muntazij`, through the temperament and the mixture, as the rain falls and the wind blows. There they remain until they manifest with the `'alamat al-alfiyyah`, the signs of the millennium, and are brought before Him for the `hisab`, the accounting — and then they are met with `al-'azab al-akbar`, the greater punishment. For everything that has been described until now has been the nearer punishment — the seven levels and the punishment of the four peripheries. Now, after the accounting, comes the turn of the greater punishment. May Allah protect us from them, by the right of Muhammad and his pure progeny: `'azanaAllahu minhum bi-haqqi Muhammadin wa alihi-t-tahirin`. By Allah we seek refuge from this matter.
