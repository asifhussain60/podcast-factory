## The Dawn and the Straight Path

The closing of these lectures turns from exposition to recitation. Having traveled through the inner sciences, the gathering returns to the words of the Qur'an itself — first to the oath of the dawn and the summons of the reassured soul, and then to the opening of the Book and its prayer for the straight path. The teaching here is the Qur'an recited in full, and so it is set down here whole, in the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.

### The Oath of the Dawn

The recitation opens with Surat al-Fajr, which swears its first oaths upon the dawn and the sacred nights, calling the soul to reflect on the rise and fall of the mighty:

`Wal-fajr. Wa layalin 'ashr. Wash-shaf'i wal-watr. Wal-layli idha yasr. Hal fi dhalika qasamul li dhi hijr.` — "By the dawn, and by ten nights, and by the even and the odd, and by the night when it passes. Is there not in that an oath for one of understanding?"

The oath sworn, the surah turns to the nations of old, recalling how the Lord dealt with those who transgressed and filled the earth with corruption — 'Aad of Iram of the lofty pillars, the like of which was never created in the land; Thamud, who carved out the rocks in the valley; and Pharaoh, owner of the stakes:

`Alam tara kayfa fa'ala rabbuka bi 'aad. Irama dhatil 'imaad. Allati lam yukhlaq mithluha fil-bilad. Wa thamoodalladhina jab-us-sakhra bil-wad. Wa fir'awna dhil-awtad. Alladhina taghaw fil-bilad. Fa aktharu fihal-fasad. Fa sabba 'alayhim rabbuka sawta 'adhab. Inna rabbaka la bil-mirsad.` — "Have you not considered how your Lord dealt with 'Aad — with Iram of the lofty pillars, the like of which were not created in the land — and with Thamud, who carved out the rocks in the valley, and with Pharaoh, owner of the stakes, all of whom transgressed within the lands and increased therein the corruption? So your Lord poured upon them a scourge of punishment. Indeed, your Lord is ever in observation."

From the fate of nations the surah turns to the soul of every person, exposing how a man misreads both honor and hardship — taking ease as a sign of his worth and want as a sign of his disgrace — while neglecting the orphan, the poor, and just inheritance, and loving wealth with an immense love:

`Fa ammal-insanu idha mab-talahu rabbuhu fa akramahu wa na''amahu fa yaqoolu rabbi akraman. Wa amma idha mab-talahu fa qadara 'alayhi rizqahu fa yaqoolu rabbi ahanan. Kalla bal la tukrimoonal-yateem. Wa la tahaaddoona 'ala ta'amil-miskeen. Wa ta'kuloonat-turatha aklal-lamma. Wa tuhibboonal-mala hubban jamma.` — "And as for man, when his Lord tries him and is generous to him and favors him, he says, 'My Lord has honored me.' But when He tries him and restricts his provision, he says, 'My Lord has humiliated me.' No! But you do not honor the orphan, and you do not encourage one another to feed the poor, and you consume inheritance, devouring it altogether, and you love wealth with immense love."

Then the scene breaks open to the Day itself, when the earth is leveled, pounded and crushed, and the Lord comes with the angels rank upon rank, and Hell is brought into view — the Day on which a man at last remembers, though the remembrance can no longer avail him, and wishes he had sent something forth for his life:

`Kalla idha dukkatil-ardu dakkan dakka. Wa ja'a rabbuka wal-malaku saffan saffa. Wa jee'a yawma'idhim bi jahannam. Yawma'idhiy-yatadhakkarul-insanu wa anna la hudh-dhikra. Yaqoolu ya laytani qaddamtu li hayati. Fa yawma'idhil-la yu'adhdhibu 'adhabahu ahad. Wa la yoothiqu wathaqahu ahad.` — "No! When the earth has been leveled — pounded and crushed — and your Lord has come and the angels, rank upon rank, and Hell is brought into view that Day; on that Day man will remember, but how will the remembrance avail him? He will say, 'Oh, I wish I had sent forth good for my life.' So on that Day none will punish as severely as His punishment, and none will bind as severely as His binding."

And the surah closes — as the whole journey of these lectures closes — upon the reassured soul, al-nafs al-mutma'inna, called home to its Lord, well-pleased and pleasing, gathered among His servants and into His Paradise:

`Ya ayyatuhan-nafsul-mutma'innah. Irji'i ila rabbiki radiyatam-mardiyyah. Fadhkuli fi 'ibadi. Wadhkuli jannati.` — "O reassured soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing to Him. And enter among My righteous servants, and enter My Paradise."

### The Prayer for the Straight Path

After the dawn and the homecoming of the soul, the recitation turns to the opening of the Book, Surat al-Fatiha — the praise of the Lord of the worlds, the Most Gracious and Most Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgment, and the prayer of those who worship Him alone and seek help from Him alone to be guided to the straight path:

`Al-hamdu lillahi Rabb al-'alamin. Al-Rahman al-Rahim. Maliki Yawm al-Din. Iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in. Ihdina al-sirat al-mustaqim. Sirat alladhina an'amta 'alayhim, ghayr al-maghdubi 'alayhim wa la al-dallin.` — "All praise is for Allah, the Lord of the worlds, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgment. It is You we worship and You we ask for help. Guide us to the straight path — the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor, not of those who have earned Your anger nor of those who are astray."

So the lectures end where the believer's every prayer begins: with the asking for guidance, ihdina al-sirat al-mustaqim, upon the path of those whom God has favored, and away from those who earned His anger, al-maghdubi 'alayhim, and those who went astray, al-dallin.
