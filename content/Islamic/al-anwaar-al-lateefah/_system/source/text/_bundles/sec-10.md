- [S276] (doctrine) Jibra'il is part of the Pillar of Light; the Natiq of the sixth era (Muhammad) at the beginning of his affair would faint from the greatness of the angel's light when he saw the form named Jibra'il.  ::terms: Jibra'il, amud al-nur, al-Natiq al-sadis, ghashiya
- [S277] (authority) The opponents and disbelievers mistook the Prophet's fainting (al-ghashyah) for jinn-possession or epilepsy (junna/sara'), while Waraqa bin Nawfal, a scholar and soothsayer awaiting the sixth Natiq, recognized it as prophethood.  ::terms: Waraqa bin Nawfal, junna, sara', al-Natiq al-sadis
- [S278] (authority) Waraqa instructed Khadija to test the visitor: an angel (true Natiq's messenger) turns away from the private parts and would say 'I see nothing,' whereas a jinn would say 'yes' when the head/hair is uncovered.  ::terms: Khadijah, Waraqa bin Nawfal, awrah
- [S279] (ruling) Because angels do not look at private parts, a woman's prayer without covering her hair is not accepted; the same principle explains why Amr ibn al-As escaped death by uncovering his private parts before Ali at Siffin.  ::terms: awrah, Amr ibn al-As, Siffin
- [S280] (doctrine) The Pillar of Light is cut off when private parts appear before it; the Prophet's connection (ittisal) with the Pillar of Light is established through Jibra'il.  ::terms: amud al-nur, ittisal, Jibra'il
- [S281] (scripture) The Quran alludes to the Pillar of Light in 'He sends down the Spirit (al-ruh) by His command upon whom He wills of His servants'; the Spirit (Pillar of Light) is the reality of revelation (haqiqat al-wahy).  ::terms: yunazzil al-ruh min amrihi, al-ruh, haqiqat al-wahy
- [S282] (scripture) Revelation flows in three modes per the verse 'It is not for any human that Allah should speak to him except by wahy, or from behind a veil, or by sending a messenger': these correspond to the ranks of Jadd, Fath, and Khayal.  ::terms: wahy, min wara'i hijab, rasul, Jadd, Fath, Khayal
- [S283] (doctrine) Above every Imam (and Natiq, Prophet, Ayn Azimah, Wali) stand three higher ranks, Jadd, Fath, and Khayal, through which divine commands are conveyed; the rank of Jadd is the highest, flowing without intermediary from the Ruh al-Quds.  ::terms: Jadd, Fath, Khayal, Ruh al-Quds
- [S284] (definition) The rank of Fath is revelation 'from behind a veil' (min wara'i hijab): perception of things through pure thought and struck parables (amthal madrubah), conveying meaning in veiled, non-public form.  ::terms: Fath, amthal madrubah, min wara'i hijab
- [S285] (definition) The rank of Khayal is the angel sent appearing as a well-proportioned man with power flowing from the Abode of Holiness (Dar al-Quds), perceived either as a heard voice or a form seen by the eye.  ::terms: Khayal, Dar al-Quds, basharan sawiyyan
- [S286] (doctrine) The flowing power connecting from Dar al-Quds is the limit flowing from Ruh al-Quds (the First Intellect), forming a luminous chain through which Khayal is heard or seen.  ::terms: Ruh al-Quds, al-mubda' al-awwal, al-hadd al-sari
- [S287] (authority) Sayyidna Ibrahim bin al-Husayn al-Hamidi, the second Da'i, at the passing of Sayyidna Ali bin al-Husayn said 'al-laqab al-sari ta'allaqa bi'l-jari' (the flowing title became attached to the flowing), indicating the deceased was drawn into the Pillar of Light.  ::terms: Ibrahim bin al-Husayn al-Hamidi, Ali bin al-Husayn, al-laqab al-sari
- [S288] (doctrine) When one connects with the flowing grace he appears to the people of bliss in the manifest presence, the gathering place of pure parts; when the number is fulfilled and the term reached, the next paradise (Abbasid dome) appears at the gate of the Imam's veil (Jannat al-Ma'wa).  ::terms: Jannat al-Ma'wa, bab al-hijab, hadirat al-quds
- [S289] (doctrine) The ascending rational soul (nafs natiqah) of one with a good end joins and connects its light/virtues to the Pillar of Light according to its portion.  ::terms: nafs natiqah, amud al-nur
- [S290] (doctrine) The virtuous person (second ibda'iyyah) established his mission until he brought forth a son who deputizes in his place, designated al-inbi'ath (the Emanated), counterpart to himself who is designated al-ibda' (the Origination).  ::terms: al-inbi'ath, al-ibda', munba'ith, na'ib manab
- [S291] (doctrine) In the world of nature the second ibda'iyyah (at the Imam's rank) is the counterpart of the First Originated, and his son the first Emanated (munba'ith al-awwal); every rank thereafter is called ibda' and its extracted son inbi'ath.  ::terms: ibda', inbi'ath, munba'ith al-awwal, mubda' al-awwal
- [S292] (doctrine) After conferring the ruling of Imamate (nass) on his son, the virtuous person passes to the rank of the Tenth Intellect to govern the world of nature, remaining until one like himself succeeds him in that lofty station.  ::terms: imamah, nass, rutbat al-'aql al-'ashir, tadbir

- [S294] (scripture) All rewarded souls, spiritual and physical, ascend by this transition over the passing of cycles and aeons (adwar wa akwar), to which the Quran alludes in 'At the Lote-tree of the utmost boundary, near it is the Garden of Abode.'  ::terms: adwar, akwar, Sidrat al-Muntaha, Jannat al-Ma'wa
- [S295] (scripture) The Prophet said of paradise: 'In paradise there is what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and what has not occurred to the heart of a human,' for the greatness Allah prepared therein for His friends.  ::terms: jannah, la 'aynun ra'at
- [S296] (scripture) The Torah reports Allah saying 'Obey me, O son of Adam, I will make you like me: living, you will not die; mighty, you will not be humiliated; rich, you will not become poor.'  ::terms: Tawrat, ati'ni ya ibn Adam

- [S298] (doctrine) All the lights of the speakers (nutaqa'), foundations (asas/usus), and Imams gather in the Qa'im al-Qiyamah; relative to him every station is a part and he is the whole (kull).  ::terms: nutaqa', usus, a'immah, kull, juz'
- [S299] (doctrine) The son designated by nass establishes the mission, calls to the oneness of Allah for the salvation of souls drowned in the sea of primeval matter (hayula) and the prison of the physical world, then brings forth his successor and passes to the Tenth's horizon, his duties being only governing the physical world and appointing a successor.  ::terms: hayula, asr al-tabi'ah, tawhid, ufuq martabat al-'ashir
- [S300] (scripture) Souls passing from each rank become stopped (mawquf) with their predecessor until their number completes and their period passes; this stopping-place is the barzakh, to which the Quran alludes: 'And behind them is a barzakh until the day they are resurrected.'  ::terms: barzakh, mawquf, wa min wara'ihim barzakhun ila yawmi yub'athun
- [S301] (doctrine) The completion of their number is 124,000 forms (surahs) and their period is 7,000 years (the dawr al-kashf); upon completion their Qa'im, extracted from his father's mission, rises and the stopped stations in the barzakh connect with him.  ::terms: mi'ah wa arba'ah wa 'ishrun alf surah, sab'at alaf sanah, dawr al-kashf, barzakh
- [S302] (doctrine) Each of those stations becomes a limb of the Qa'im's luminous subtle structure (haykal nurani latif), his subtle self being to them like the spirit to the body, so together they form one luminous divine structure and he becomes a holy abstract divine intellect.  ::terms: haykal nurani, ruh, aql qudsani mujarrad
- [S303] (scripture) The Natiq of the cycle said 'Indeed for Allah there are 124,000 prophets,' alluding to this noble great structure, and the Quran alludes in 'Say: indeed the first and the last will surely be gathered for the appointed time of a known day.'  ::terms: mi'ah wa arba'ah wa 'ishrun alf nabiyyin, qul inna al-awwalina wa al-akhirina la-majmu'un, haykal
- [S304] (scripture) The 'known day' is the dawr al-kashf, which is 50,000 years, as the Quran says 'in a day whose measure is fifty thousand years'; at the rising of this Qa'im the reckoning, reward, and punishment occur before him.  ::terms: dawr al-kashf, khamsun alf sanah, fi yawmin kana miqdaruhu khamsina alf sanah, hisab
