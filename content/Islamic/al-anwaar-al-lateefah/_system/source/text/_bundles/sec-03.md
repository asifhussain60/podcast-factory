# SECTION 03: The First Emanation: Origination, the First Intellect, and the Sabiq

Lecture range: [6, 7]


## LEDGER SPINE TEACHINGS YOU MUST PRESERVE (each at full depth)

- [S069] (doctrine) The limit of the intellect ('aql) — and of all three essences/powers (jawahir: hayat, 'ilm, ikhtiyar) given to the created essences — is bewilderment (hayrat), because the intellect cannot attain the gnosis of its God though created by Him.  ::terms: aql, jawahir, hayrat, hayat, ikhtiyar
- [S070] (definition) Two of the luminous forms emanated from the noble First Intellect, called the two First Emanations (munba'ithayn al-awwalayn); though they emanated simultaneously, one preceded the other (sabaqa ahaduhuma al-akhar) — the term munba'ith deriving from alif yielding lam and fa.  ::terms: munba'ithayn al-awwalayn, sabaqa, alif lam fa
- [S071] (doctrine) The First Emanation (munba'ith al-awwal / second intellect) looked at his essence and kind just as the First Intellect did, knew there was an Originator unlike them, negated divinity from himself and his kind, affirmed it for Allah, and glorified/sanctified the First Intellect — acknowledging its precedence, virtue, and nobility.  ::terms: munba'ith al-awwal, ilahiyya, tasbih, taqdis
- [S072] (doctrine) Before their emanation, the First Intellect had two established gazes: the nobler gaze (nazar-e ashraf) born of its tawhid/tanzih/tajrid and resulting luminosity, and the lower gaze (nazar-e adna) of joy at its firstness (awwaliyyat); the First Emanation's gaze was toward the nobler, the Second's toward the lower.  ::terms: nazar-e ashraf, nazar-e adna, awwaliyyat
- [S073] (doctrine) The greatest crime established against the Second/Third Intellect (the 'ashir, the Tenth) was the delusion of equality (wahm-e masawat) — claiming his gaze equaled the First Emanation's because they emanated together — even though he understood the difference of gaze; this halted his rank.  ::terms: wahm-e masawat, ashir, sabaqiyat
- [S074] (doctrine) The First Emanation, having the nobler gaze and acknowledging the First Intellect's precedence, received divine light (nur ilahi) and infinite knowledge from it, reached the second perfection, and became an eternal (azali), perfect, complete, infallible (ma'sum), excellent intellect — differing from the First only by the rank of precedence (rutbat al-sabq).  ::terms: nur ilahi, kamal al-thani, ma'sum, rutbat al-sabq
- [S075] (doctrine) In the second perfection (kamal al-thani) no being can surpass another and no circle can surpass another — the ranks are locked, the seventh intellect can never overtake the sixth; the only ikhtiyar (choice) to surpass one's mufid exists in the fallen first-perfection state of munba'ith and other cycles (karrat), not in the luminous second perfection.  ::terms: kamal al-thani, ikhtiyar, mufid, karrat
- [S076] (authority) The difference between First Intellect and First Emanation is that the First is ibda' (origination — advancing on its own from its essence) and the Second is inbi'ath (emanation — advancing by seeing another); ibda' cannot be like inbi'ath, as Sayyidna Hamiduddin states in Rahat al-'Aql.  ::terms: ibda, inbi'ath, Hamiduddin, Rahat al-Aql
- [S077] (doctrine) The First Intellect rushed upon the tawhid, tanzih, tasbih, and taqdis of its Originator from its essence, for its essence, with its essence (min dhatihi li-dhatihi bi-dhatihi), with no prior or predecessor — thereby deserving all the noble names that belong to no other except him.  ::terms: min dhatihi, tawhid, tanzih, taqdis
- [S078] (definition) The First Intellect is named: al-ibda', al-mubda' al-awwal (first originated), al-wujud and al-mawjud al-awwal, al-haqiqah and al-haqq al-awwal, al-wahdah and al-wahid al-awwal, al-tamam, al-kamal, al-azal, al-'aql, al-'ilm, al-qudrah, al-hayat, al-sabq, al-'ismah, and mudahhir al-duhur (the one who rolls out the ages) — each as the first of its kind.  ::terms: mubda al-awwal, haqq al-awwal, wahid al-awwal, mudahhir al-duhur
- [S079] (doctrine) The name 'mubda'' (originated) applies to the First Intellect and all his kind (the whole cosmos), but the name 'ibda'' is specific to the First Intellect alone, because he rushed upon and initiated the tawhid of his Originator by an act he alone performed.  ::terms: mubda, ibda, ikhtisas
- [S080] (definition) The First Intellect bears the names al-'alim al-awwal (first connected with flowing knowledge), al-qadir al-awwal, al-hayy al-awwal (first connected with the spirit of true life, given eternal life), al-sabiq al-awwal, al-mu'tasim al-awwal (first protected from error by hablillah — the rope of God, the kalimah), and al-dahr al-awwal.  ::terms: alim al-awwal, hayy al-awwal, mu'tasim al-awwal, hablillah
- [S081] (definition) The First Intellect is mudahhir al-duhur, muwahhid al-muwahhidin, and mu'azzil al-mu'azzilin (the one who enables doers, teaches tawhid to unifiers, and grants eternity) because the 'ages' (duhur) are the higher hudud (al-hudud al-'alawiyya), whom he aids with divine light; the physical hudud are named 'years and eras.'  ::terms: mudahhir al-duhur, duhur, hudud al-alawiyya
- [S082] (claim) The First Intellect is in continuous evolution: Allah's treasures (bounties) have no limit, so the First Intellect's splendor, perfection, intellect, and power only increase forever (only rising, never falling); how much he will rise is knowledge of the unseen ('ilm al-ghayb).  ::terms: evolution, ilm al-ghayb, aql al-awwal
- [S083] (scripture) In 'Allahu la ilaha illa Huwa al-Hayy al-Qayyum,' Huwa is the Originator (mabda'), al-Hayy is the First Intellect (station of unity/wahdat), and al-Qayyum is the First Emanation (munba'ith al-awwal), with whom are gathered all the second perfections to be attained in the future.  ::terms: al-Hayy al-Qayyum, mabda, aql al-awwal, munba'ith al-awwal
- [S084] (doctrine) The First Emanation's state was a quality other than the quality of origination: he contemplated that the First Intellect's greatness came from its preceding action and its tawhid/tanzih/tasbih/taqdis of the Originator, then that contemplation emanated in his own essence, so he stood as the First Intellect stood, taking it as his Imam (musta'imman).  ::terms: inbi'ath al-awwal, ibda, musta'imman, Imam
- [S085] (doctrine) The First Emanation is called 'al-munba'ith al-awwal' because what the First originated (ibtada') emanated within him; 'sending forth' (ba'th) requires existence, then effacement (durus), then a second existence — from which is derived the resurrection of the dead (ba'th al-mawta) on the Day of Judgment and the truth that resurrection from graves is real.  ::terms: munba'ith al-awwal, ba'th, durus, ba'th al-mawta
- [S086] (definition) The First Emanation is also called al-tali (the follower, following the First Intellect in action), al-'aql al-thani (the second intellect in tawhid/tanzih/tajrid), al-lawh (the tablet, receptacle for the rays of al-qalam = the First Intellect, the pen), and al-nafs al-kulliyya (the universal soul, noblest existent after the First, a 'whole' to whom all return).  ::terms: tali, aql al-thani, lawh, nafs al-kulliyya, qalam
- [S087] (doctrine) The end (intiha) of all the intellects is toward the First Emanation, not the First Intellect: the Garden of Abode (Jannat al-ma'wa) — everyone's paradise — is the First Emanation, located near Sidrat al-Muntaha (the self of the First Intellect); none can attain Sidrat al-Muntaha except the First Intellect, who affirmed from his own self.  ::terms: Jannat al-ma'wa, Sidrat al-Muntaha, munba'ith al-awwal
- [S088] (doctrine) The Second Emanation (inbi'ath al-thani) emanated and looked just as the First Emanation, negated divinity and affirmed it for the Originator, glorified the First Intellect — but did NOT glorify or sanctify the First Emanation nor acknowledge its virtue and precedence; rather he transgressed (takhatta'ahu) and thought himself equal (musawat/mumathila), which was a slip (zalla) and sin (khati'a).  ::terms: inbi'ath al-thani, takhatta'a, musawat, zalla
- [S089] (doctrine) The principle of precedence: each being must acknowledge the precedence of those before it; one whose vision (nazar) sees the reality/principles (asl) is noble (ashraf), while one who sees only the branches (furu') is lower (adna) — and being noble of vision elevates, while seeing branches lowers.  ::terms: sabqiyyat, nazar ashraf, nazar adna, asl, furu
- [S090] (doctrine) Because of his claim of equality, the bounties, glances, confirmations, and blessings (mawaddat, lahazat, ta'yidat, barakat) of the First Emanation were cut off from the Second Emanation, who did not attain the second perfection — the same action done by both differing only in the reality of vision and intention.  ::terms: mawaddat, ta'yidat, barakat, kamal al-thani
- [S091] (argument) The difference between the two emanations is illustrated by 'two joys' (masarratayn): the higher joy (masarrat al-'ala) of worshipping God (the core reality) versus the lower joy (masarrat al-adna) of superiority over others; the First Emanation's vision was on the higher, the Second's on the lower, yet he still claimed equality.  ::terms: masarrat al-ala, masarrat al-adna, nazar
- [S092] (doctrine) The First Intellect united with the First Emanation, taking it as a veil (hijab) and a gate (bab) to its obedience and the obedience of its Originator; the First Intellect established the da'wah in that world and addressed all through the First Emanation.  ::terms: hijab, bab, da'wah, munba'ith al-awwal
- [S093] (doctrine) In the parallel structure, the Imam corresponds to the First Intellect (advancing on his own through the gnosis of the 'ayn 'azima, who is the Imam of the Imam), the Bab al-inbi'ath corresponds to the First Emanation (advancing by seeing the Imam), and the hidden gates advance through da'wah.  ::terms: Imam, bab al-inbi'ath, ayn azima, da'wah
- [S094] (doctrine) Seven luminous forms (sab'at ashbahin suriyya) responded to the First Intellect's da'wah one after another; each affirmed the unity of his Originator, acknowledged the lordship, precedence, and rank of the First Intellect and the First Emanation, and glorified his own limit (had) at which he stood.  ::terms: sab'at ashbah, da'wah, had, rububiyya
- [S095] (argument) The world ended at seven because Allah placed seven muqatta'at (disconnected letters, where pen/tongue stops) in 'La ilaha illa Allah'; the seven circles were predestined in the kalimah, which is between the Originator and the originated — beings had absolute choice in advancing on their own but not in where the count ended.  ::terms: muqatta'at, La ilaha illa Allah, kalimah, seven circles
- [S096] (doctrine) Each of the seven intellects acknowledged the limit of the one above it in ascending sequence, until the seventh acknowledged the prior six, the First Emanation, the First Intellect, and the Originator; a divine light and endless knowledge flowed from the First Intellect to the second and continued to them, by which the second perfection (kamal al-thani — salvation, infallibility, success, bounty) is attained.  ::terms: nur ilahi, kamal al-thani, najat, isma
- [S097] (authority) Sayyidna Hamiduddin states in Rahat al-'Aql that existence is from the First Intellect, the First Emanation, and seven intellects, and the existence of each is from the last (siwa al-munba'ith al-awwal) ascending to the First Intellect; later authorities counted eight bearers of the Throne (hamalat al-'arsh) from the seven muqatta'at plus 'La ilaha illa Allah' itself.  ::terms: Hamiduddin, Rahat al-Aql, hamalat al-arsh
- [S098] (doctrine) In each da'wah circle there is a center (chief) and twelve chiefs (ru'asa'), because the letters of 'La ilaha illa Allah' number twelve; when a circle is completed (e.g. at 28 limits, predestined in the kalimah), the last individual is commanded to give the da'wah and forms a new circle.  ::terms: ru'asa, twelve, da'ira, markaz
