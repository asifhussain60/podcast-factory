# SECTION 15: The Prophetic Genealogy: from Abd al-Muttalib to the Qa'im

Lecture range: [30]


## LEDGER SPINE TEACHINGS YOU MUST PRESERVE (each at full depth)

- [S489] (doctrine) Ibrahim gathered both the eras of Adam and Nuh — all individuals who had entered the haykal of the Natiq — and the four ranks (Nubuwwah, Risalah, Wisayah, Imamah) gathered in him.  ::terms: ibrahim, nubuwwah, risalah, wisayah, imamah, haykal
- [S490] (scripture) There are two Ibrahims: Ibrahim the Natiq 'alladhi waffa', and a man named Ibrahim who was a Nabi of his time, mentioned in the Quran as 'kana rajulun yuqalu lahu Ibrahim'.  ::terms: ibrahim, natiq, nabi
- [S491] (doctrine) The thalathaha (three) — Maulana Hashim, Maulana Hunayn, Maulana Ibrahim — hold a special khilafah and rank among the khilafats of the Qa'im.  ::terms: thalathaha, hashim, hunayn, ibrahim, khilafah
- [S492] (doctrine) The wazir of the Qa'im is 'Ayn 'Azim, whose khilafah is taken by Mim ('Abd al-Muttalib, then Abu Talib, then 'Abdullah), with two qiran and then Mim Jadd al-Qa'im, and Fatimah as khalifah of Mim.  ::terms: ayn azim, abd al-muttalib, abu talib, abdullah, jadd al-qa'im, fatimah
- [S493] (authority) The mutimmin of the Dawr-e-Muhammadi and every seventh Imam (Muhammad bin Isma'il, Ma'add al-Mu'izz, Imam al-Tayyib, the 28th Maulana Ma'add, 22nd Maulana Ahmad, 42nd Maulana Ali, 49th Abu al-Qa'im) hold a very high rank.  ::terms: mutimmin, muhammad bin isma'il, al-mu'izz, imam al-tayyib, abu al-qa'im
- [S494] (authority) Hazrat Nasrullah Sahib taught that the atimmah of the Prophet's dawr reached so high a rank that a qiran (equality) occurred with the sevenths, making them sometimes equal in taking khilafah.  ::terms: nasrullah, atimmah, qiran, khilafah
- [S495] (authority) Among the atimmah, Maulana Muhammad bin Isma'il holds the highest rank (gathered with a personality like Imam Husayn), established in the dawr-e-kashf, and thus does not fall into the category of the seventh class.  ::terms: muhammad bin isma'il, imam husayn, dawr-e-kashf
- [S496] (scripture) Of the seven, four are greater (Muhammad bin Isma'il, Imam al-Tayyib, Maulana Ahmad, Abu al-Qa'im), three gathering two ranks each, while Muhammad bin Isma'il alone holds the special rank of sahib-e-dawr-e-kashf, anchored in Surat al-Ikhlas.  ::terms: muhammad bin isma'il, imam al-tayyib, abu al-qa'im, qul huwa allahu ahad
- [S497] (authority) The Qa'im's name is 'Ali, and Sayyidna referred to him as Abu 'Ali in his poetic responses.  ::terms: qa'im, ali, abu ali
- [S498] (authority) Because Anwar is a foundational book from which most Du'at take proofs, it places Wisayah before Imamah (unlike most books), and Wisayah is the higher rank.  ::terms: anwar, wisayah, imamah, du'at
- [S499] (ruling) Ibrahim established Isma'il as Wasi entrusting him Wisayah and Imamah by Allah's command because he is a maqam ilahi and haykal nurani, while entrusting Ishaq the ranks of Nubuwwah and Risalah and making him a khadim, hijab, and da'i to Isma'il.  ::terms: isma'il, ishaq, wasi, wisayah, imamah, khadim, hijab
- [S500] (doctrine) Isma'il and his progeny are divine stations (maqamat ilahiyyah) and possessors of luminous temples (the people of permanence, ahl al-istiqrar), while Ishaq and his progeny are proofs and manifest summoners (hujaj and du'at zahirun) and veils upon them.  ::terms: maqamat ilahiyyah, ahl al-istiqrar, hujaj, du'at zahirun, isma'il, ishaq
- [S501] (authority) The chain of Du'at and hujub (manifest summoners) ran from Ishaq through Ya'qub, Yusuf, and the named prophets down to 'Isa, then Sham'un, 'Abd al-Masih, and Bahira al-Rahib — all the ahl al-istida' tracing their lineage back to Ishaq.  ::terms: du'at, ishaq, ya'qub, yusuf, isa, bahira al-rahib, ahl al-istida'
- [S502] (authority) When the Prophet was manifested, the rank of Nabuwwah that had been with Bahira the monk was taken by Ubayy ibn Ka'b.  ::terms: nabuwwah, bahira, ubayy ibn ka'b
- [S503] (doctrine) Hashim (Hasham al-sarid) gathered the four ranks and gave them completely to 'Abd al-Muttalib, who divided them: Nubuwwah, Risalah, Imamah, Wisayah to 'Abdullah, and a second part to Abu Talib; when 'Abdullah died all four gathered in Abu Talib.  ::terms: hashim, abd al-muttalib, abdullah, abu talib, rutab al-arba'
- [S504] (doctrine) Abu Talib gave Nubuwwah and Risalah to the Prophet and Imamah and Wisayah to 'Ali, but since they were young, all ranks gathered in the Prophet through the kafalat-e-'uzma (great guardianship), whose power overshadowed all the mustawda'in and mustaqarrin gathered with 'Ali.  ::terms: abu talib, nubuwwah, risalah, imamah, wisayah, kafalat-e-'uzma
- [S505] (doctrine) The Qa'im of the progeny of Isma'il is 'Ali and the Qa'im of the progeny of Ishaq is the Prophet, but the succession occurred with the Prophet, who gathers them when joined with 'Ayn 'Azima.  ::terms: qa'im, isma'il, ishaq, ayn azima
- [S506] (authority) The first of the A'immah (divine stations) after Ibrahim was Isma'il ibn Ibrahim, entrusting to Qaydar, with a long named chain of fathers-to-sons down to 'Udad bin 'Udad whose muqim was Musa.  ::terms: a'immah, isma'il, qaydar, udad, musa
- [S507] (doctrine) When the time bound the manifestation of a Natiq, 'Udad established Musa from among his Du'at, and the chain continued through 'Adnan, Ma'add, Nizar, Mudar, Ilyas, Mudrikah, Khuzaymah, whose da'i was commanded to establish 'Isa.  ::terms: natiq, udad, musa, adnan, khuzaymah, isa
- [S508] (authority) The muqim of Adam is Maulana Hunayn, of Nuh is Maulana Hud, of Ibrahim is Maulana Salih, of Musa is 'Udad bin 'Udad, and of 'Isa is Khuzaymah.  ::terms: muqim, hunayn, hud, salih, udad, khuzaymah
- [S509] (authority) The chain continued from Khuzaymah through Kinanah, Nadr, Malik, Fihr, Ghalib, Lu'ayy, Ka'b, Murrah, Kilab, Qusayy, 'Abd Manaf, Hashim, to 'Abd al-Muttalib, with whom the four ranks (Wisayah, Imamah, Nabuwwah, Risalah) gathered as they had with Ibrahim.  ::terms: khuzaymah, qusayy, abd manaf, hashim, abd al-muttalib, rutab al-arba'
- [S510] (doctrine) When the four ranks gathered with 'Abd al-Muttalib, two (Wisayah and Imamah) were reserved for the Qa'im of Isma'il's progeny and two (Nubuwwah and Risalah) for the Qa'im of Ishaq's progeny, and Imamah and Wisayah are never separated.  ::terms: abd al-muttalib, wisayah, imamah, nubuwwah, risalah, qa'im
- [S511] (argument) From Allah's justice and grace, Muhammad became a divine station (maqam ilahi) and luminous structure (haykal nurani), which is impossible unless one's father is likewise a haykal nurani; thus 'Abdullah being a haykal nurani is the precondition for Muhammad being one.  ::terms: maqam ilahi, haykal nurani, muhammad, abdullah
- [S512] (doctrine) Because the Prophet received the great guardianship, despite bearing only Nubuwwah and Risalah he achieved superiority over all ranks gathered with 'Ali, becoming the deputy of 'Ayn, with no rank of the Qa'im remaining except the thalathah sukra (the three intoxicated ones).  ::terms: kafalah, ayn, thalathah sukra, qa'im
- [S513] (ruling) After Maulana 'Abd al-Muttalib, the haykal-nurani condition requires that an Imam's father and grandfather be haykal nurani, but two sons of one father can both be Imams (one in the mustawda', one in the mustaqarr), as with 'Abd al-Muttalib's two sons.  ::terms: haykal nurani, abd al-muttalib, mustawda', mustaqarr
- [S514] (authority) 'Ali said 'ana abdun min abid Muhammad' (I am one of the slaves of Muhammad), illustrating the loftiness of the awaited one's station and the three courses (salasa majari) of the relationship: adna (lower), masawat (equality), and a'la (higher).  ::terms: ali, muhammad, salasa majari, adna, masawat, a'la
- [S515] (scripture) Qaba qawsayn aw adna corresponds to the stations: 'Abd al-Muttalib and Abu Talib are 'qaba qawsayn' and 'Abdullah is 'adna'.  ::terms: qaba qawsayn, adna, abd al-muttalib, abu talib, abdullah
- [S516] (doctrine) 'Abd al-Muttalib set up two gates (babayn) to receive ascending forms from his da'wah, one higher than the other: the higher gathering the forms of accomplished believers and virtuous Hudud, the second gathering the forms of steadfast respondents.  ::terms: babayn, bab al-abwab, mu'minin bulagha', hudud fudala', mustajibin
- [S517] (doctrine) Each Imam's bab al-abwab is his wife, who gathers the forms and then becomes the qalbe haykal of his son the next khalifah: Hashim's bab Salma submerged in 'Abd al-Muttalib; 'Abd al-Muttalib's bab Fatimah bint 'Amr in Abu Talib; Abu Talib's bab Fatimah bint Asad in 'Ali; 'Ali's bab Salman-e-Farsi in Imam Husayn.  ::terms: bab al-abwab, qalbe haykal, salma, fatimah bint amr, fatimah bint asad, salman-e-farsi
- [S518] (doctrine) The Prophet's bab al-abwab is Khadijah, submerged in Fatimah; 'Abdullah's bab al-abwab is Aminah, submerged in the Prophet — distinguishing being a bab al-abwab from becoming a qalbe haykal.  ::terms: bab al-abwab, khadijah, fatimah, aminah, qalbe haykal
- [S519] (ruling) The four Ya Fateru sajdahs/recitations correspond to the four Fatimahs: first sajdah (100x) Fatimah al-Zahra, right cheek (100x) Fatimah bint Asad, third Fatimah bint 'Amr, fourth sajdah (110x) Zawjat al-Qa'im (also named Fatimah), with Fatimah al-Zahra superior to all.  ::terms: ya fateru, fatimah al-zahra, fatimah bint asad, fatimah bint amr, zawjat al-qa'im
- [S520] (ruling) In sajdah seven parts of the body must touch the ground (forehead/face, two hands, two knees, two feet), signifying that the complete order of the da'wah requires seven limbs/hudud; the cheek-only position signifies a lesser rank.  ::terms: sajdah, jawarih, hudud
- [S521] (doctrine) The fifth chapter restates that Qa'im min wuld Isma'il is 'Ayn-e-'Azimah and Qa'im min wuld Ishaq is the Prophet; the haykal-nurani requires receptive strength (quwwat) to gather all forms, formable only if the father is a haykal nurani.  ::terms: ayn-e-azimah, qa'im, haykal-nurani, quwwat
- [S522] (doctrine) The bab gathers all the world's forms and, when it is time to install them in the Imam's son (nasb), becomes the son's qalbe haykal; the Imam (and the Imam of Imams, 'Ayn-e-'Azimah) decides which form is ready for inclusion.  ::terms: bab, nasb, qalbe haykal, ayn-e-azimah
- [S523] (authority) 'Abd al-Muttalib made his son Abdullah's matter famous through the sacrifice (dhabh) — sacrificing 100 camels in place of Abdullah — to make known among the ahl al-haqa'iq Abdullah's virtue as one prepared for the station of Nubuwwah and Risalah.  ::terms: abd al-muttalib, abdullah, dhabh, ahl al-haqa'iq
- [S524] (doctrine) The present Imam Maulana Muhammad bin Ali holds the lofty station of jadd-e-qa'im (grandfather of the Qa'im), who will have two sons by custom: one at Abu Talib's station (from whom the Qa'im comes) and one at Abdullah's station (Sayyidna Mahdi), whose daughter Fatimah will marry the Qa'im producing Hasan and Husayn.  ::terms: jadd-e-qa'im, muhammad bin ali, mahdi, fatimah, qa'im
- [S525] (doctrine) 'Abd al-Muttalib handed Wisayah and Imamah to Abu Talib (an imam-e-natiq, like Husayn) and Nubuwwah and Risalah to Abdullah (silent/samit, like al-Hasan); Abdullah fathered Muhammad and Abu Talib fathered 'Ali.  ::terms: abd al-muttalib, abu talib, abdullah, natiq, samit, muhammad, ali
- [S526] (doctrine) When Abdullah's transfer (nuqlatu) came, he entrusted Nubuwwah and Risalah to his father 'Abd al-Muttalib for his son Muhammad, and when 'Abd al-Muttalib transferred he entrusted them to Abu Talib because Muhammad was in childhood (tufuliyyah), Abu Talib becoming custodian (kafil) of all four ranks.  ::terms: nuqlatu, abdullah, abd al-muttalib, abu talib, kafil, tufuliyyah
- [S527] (doctrine) When Muhammad reached maturity Abu Talib handed over the entrusted Nubuwwah and Risalah, and Muhammad ascended through the ranks until reaching the rank of divine speech (nutq-e-ilahi), the final/highest rank above Nubuwwah, Imamah, and Wisayah.  ::terms: abu talib, muhammad, nutq-e-ilahi, rutbah
- [S528] (doctrine) The station of maqam-e-mahmud changes according to the 'ayn — a single-cycle 'ayn versus a seven-cycle 'ayn — and at the khutbat al-tasmeem the station of the 'ayn changes into the form of the Qa'im.  ::terms: maqam-e-mahmud, ayn, khutbat al-tasmeem, qa'im
- [S529] (scripture) The completion of the religion ('I have perfected for you your religion') refers to the completion of the obligations of the din, with the command of wilayah announced last after Hajj, not to a change in station.  ::terms: wilayah, din, hajj
- [S530] (authority) There are three courses (salasa majari) of the Prophet-'Ali relationship: 'Ali's 'ana abdun min abid Muhammad' (majra-e-adna), 'ana wa Aliyyun ka-hatayn' (equality), and the elevation of Hijab-e-'Ain at the conquest of Makkah (a'la).  ::terms: salasa majari, majra-e-adna, masawat, hijab, makkah
- [S531] (authority) Muhammad said 'I am the prayer (da'wat) of my father Ibrahim,' gathering all the lofty stations and luminous structures of all cycles from the first three thousand years (the higher suwarat of the dawr-e-kashf) within his haykal, which became 'Ali.  ::terms: da'wat, ibrahim, maqamat al-'aliyah, hayakil nuraniyah, dawr-e-kashf
- [S532] (authority) Successive du'at (e.g., Sayyidna Sulayman, then Sayyidna Ja'far) explained the du'as of the da'i but each kept some knowledge received from the Imam hidden, a series of concealment continuing from da'i to da'i.  ::terms: du'at, sulayman, ja'far, du'a
