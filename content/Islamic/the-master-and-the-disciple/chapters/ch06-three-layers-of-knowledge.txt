# Three Layers of Knowledge

Of these two men, the scholar continued, the first is one who has been taught the very purpose of knowledge, whose soul the spirit of certainty has quickened, so that he is a scholar by what he knows and a master by what he does. The second is a learner who seeks knowledge for the sake of his own deliverance. As for the rest of the people, they are savages and a rabble who know nothing, followers of every false guide, deceived by his error and seduced by his ignorance. They imagine they are doing well, yet they have been deprived of the true examples that came before them, and they will not escape what Allah has promised, for the disbelievers shall have the like of it.

The boy said: So this is how virtue is, and this is how knowledge and its exposition are. Blessed are the guardians of God, and blessed are the followers of benevolence. Will I attain this great station, which stands above the stations below it?

The scholar said: O my son, this is a station that God has honored above all other stations and degrees, so that you can reach it only by virtuous work. If your soul does its work, and you seek it with honest certainty and a heart that is pure and awake, I hope that you will realize from it what the guardians of God, the chosen and the righteous, have realized.

The boy said: And who may aspire to attain the likeness of this station, to which God has raised His guardians?

The scholar said: Their God is your God. Between God and anyone else there is no kinship; nothing draws the creation near to Him except piety and righteous deeds. The knowledge by which Allah has raised His guardians, you stand at its beginning, and if you act upon it you will reach its end. It is a debt binding upon God that He bring you to the ranks the righteous among them attained, and that He deprive you of nothing of what He gave them.

The boy said: Can you then help me to that, by an inner meaning, as your bounty came to me first?

The scholar said: This is a station in which nothing avails but the work of its own people.

The boy said: How can that be, when you were able to help me before?

The scholar said: The sower is able to make the earth fertile, to sow it and to water it, but he cannot bring forth the plant and the sheath. A man is able to bring forth his seed whenever he wills, but he cannot fashion from it what he wills. So it grieves me, my son, that you should seek my help in a thing and I not help you to it. May Allah expand your breast and light your heart with guidance. Hold fast to the fear of God, and do what is best, for Allah does not waste the reward of the one who does his work well. You are upon the path of salvation, the road of guidance, the way of the people of piety. So walk your road, whose beginning you already stand upon, and cling to the rope you have been called to, until you are guided by a rope from God to the rope of God, for that is the goal of all who seek.

The boy said: Is not the rope of Allah the Imam to whom you have called me?

The scholar said: He is that in what is apparent. He is your rope and the most trustworthy handhold, your hujja, and the door of your Imam.

The boy said: Then what is the rope of Allah, and what is the rope that comes from God?

The scholar said: That is the utmost of your guidance and the furthest reach of all the worlds.

The boy said: There is no power and no strength save in Allah, لا حول ولا قوة إلا بالله. He has drowned in the depths of ignorance who has no guide, just as he has drowned in the seas of this world who has no ship.

The scholar said: And what is the ḥawl and the quwwa which you have said cannot be except by Allah?

The boy said: We mean, I have no device to draw a benefit to myself, and no power to ward off from myself a harm I detest, except by Allah.

The scholar said: Then why do you say, there is no ḥawl except in Allah, and not, there is no ḥīla, no stratagem, except in Allah?

The boy said: We have it narrated in our sciences that the ḥawl is the year, from the saying of Allah, حولين كاملين, two complete years, meaning two years. And we have narrated, as the common people also relate, that the quwwa is mules, from the saying of Allah, وَآتَيْنَاهُ مِنَ الْكُنُوزِ مَا إِنَّ مَفَاتِحَهُ لَتَنُوءُ بِالْعُصْبَةِ أُولِي الْقُوَّةِ, and We gave him of treasures whose very keys would weigh down a band of men endowed with strength. They said the band was forty men and the strength was mules, and that the keys of his treasures were the load of forty mules.

The scholar said: So it is as though, by your saying there is no power and no strength save in Allah, you meant there is no year and no mule save by Allah. Is this, in your view, the interpretation of your words and their meaning?

The boy said: I have no other saying than this, and it is a feeble one that resembles nothing of the speech of wisdom. Indeed I suspect that the meaning within it is other than this, something stronger in the speaking and more firmly fixed in the mind. Is there for these two words an inner interpretation other than this?

The scholar said: The creation has never uttered a word of truth without its having a root in the inner, nor a word of falsehood without its having once had a meaning; for when speech is diverted from its meaning it becomes an absurdity, and the absurdity displaced from its place is altered, and truth, once altered, becomes falsehood. It is rather a speech by which what comes after is grasped through analogy and forms, just as what came before was grasped through likeness and parable.

The boy said: You have spoken truly. So what is the meaning of these two words and their like?

The scholar said: As for the ḥawl, he is the speaking Imam of Allah and His truthful Caliph, the master of the twelve captains, just as the ḥawl, which is the year, is completed only by twelve months. The Imam was named ḥawl because he turns the speech of the Creator, in the subtlety of its essence, into the speech of the children of Adam, until it is understood and becomes apparent speech. The words of wisdom thereby take on a body and a frame, while the speech of the Creator carries within them the spirit of life and the light of salvation. The words of wisdom are ennobled above all other speech by the nobility of the Creator's speech that is their inner. So the Imam was named the ḥawl for this turning of speech, and he turns it only by the leave of Allah. And the quwwa is his hujja and his door, whom God has strengthened to bear the heavy weight of the utterance. He receives it from the Imam in its wholeness and parcels it out to the summoners in detail, each according to his station; so the hujja was named quwwa, and it is strengthened only by God. It is for this that the one who speaks says, there is no ḥawl and no quwwa save in Allah, meaning there is no Imam and no hujja save by Allah, and from Allah come their light and their support. The band, the ones endowed with strength, are the twelve hujjas. So too Joseph (ع) said to his father, يَا أَبَتِ إِنِّي رَأَيْتُ أَحَدَ عَشَرَ كَوْكَبًا وَالشَّمْسَ وَالْقَمَرَ رَأَيْتُهُمْ لِي سَاجِدِينَ, O my father, I saw eleven planets and the sun and the moon; I saw them prostrating to me. By the sun he meant the Imam, by the moon his hujja and his door, and by the planets the completing of the twelve captains. And likewise the brothers of Joseph said, لَيُوسُفُ وَأَخُوهُ أَحَبُّ إِلَى أَبِينَا مِنَّا وَنَحْنُ عُصْبَةٌ, truly Joseph and his brother are dearer to our father than we are, though we are a band; meaning the twelve captains, and asking that a man from among them be chosen over the rest. In this, poverty of judgment concerning Joseph (ع) entered upon them, and the risings of envy toward him appeared in them when God favored him. Yet the command of God was fulfilled in Joseph, and the end of their affair was remorse and repentance before him.

The boy said: Praise be to God, who cleansed them of defilement and returned them to the resort of repentance and the ways of turning back. So what is the meaning of the king's saying, إِنِّي أَرَى سَبْعَ بَقَرَاتٍ سِمَانٍ يَأْكُلُهُنَّ سَبْعٌ عِجَافٌ وَسَبْعَ سُنْبُلَاتٍ خُضْرٍ وَأُخَرَ يَابِسَاتٍ, I see seven fat cows being devoured by seven lean ones, and seven green ears of grain and others withered? What are they, and what is the vision seen in sleep?

The scholar said: As for the vision, it is like the outward work a man performs; it is as the mirage which, when he comes to it, he finds to be nothing. So too is the work of the outward without the inner: nothing of it is accepted, however much it may be. And so is the vision in sleep: nothing is realized from what is seen, however often it is seen. The king said, in the common people's telling, I see seven things turning upon seven. As for the seven fat cows, they are the causes of God that stand between Him and His creation. As for the seven lean ones, they are the seven speakers, driven by need to what comes from God at the hands of His seven causes. The seven green ears are causes between God and the legatees. And the seven withered ones are the legatees, driven to what comes from God at the hands of these seven causes. Blessed then be Allah, Lord of the worlds.

The boy said: And what is the likeness of this among us?

The scholar said: That lies in the foundation of Islam and its offspring, like a grain that put forth seven ears, meaning that from his loins came seven imams.

The boy said: How, then, does God forbid this world, saying, فَلَا تَغُرَّنَّكُمُ الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا, so let not the life of this world deceive you?

The scholar said: God has spoken truly. He did not say, let not this world deceive you; rather He said, let not the life of this world deceive you. Life carries four meanings: a life apparent in this world, whose end is annihilation, and the life of the Hereafter, whose end is permanence. He said: do not work for the perishing life, but work for the abiding life. This is His saying, يَا لَيْتَنِي قَدَّمْتُ لِحَيَاتِي, would that I had sent something ahead for my life, meaning, would that in the perishing life one had sent something ahead for the abiding life. And the two lives in another sense are a life through the outward knowledge and a life through the inward knowledge. The outward knowledge is the life of this world, and it is the lower knowledge; the inward knowledge is the life of the Hereafter. For this reason He said, لَا تَغُرَّنَّكُمُ الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا, let not the life of this world deceive you, meaning, let not the outward of knowledge, and the acting upon it alone, deceive you. For He accepts from you nothing but the inner set up together with the outward, and He does not accept the outward without the inner.

The boy said: Consider one who knows the inward knowledge but does not know the outward and does not act upon it. What is his standing among the people of religion?

The scholar said: A wretched standing. His inner will not rise up nor be made sound if he has abandoned a thing he was commanded to do and made a shield for his inner, like the outward of fruits, for when the rind is peeled from them before their harvest they are spoiled and cannot be set right after; and like the body, for when its limbs are severed the soul is not sustained in it a single hour. Such is the outward of religion: when its obligations are neglected, its inner cannot stand for its owner. Moreover, his leaving of the outward, without leave from the One who imposed it upon him, does not escape one of two things. If he left it out of incapacity, then he is the more incapable in the inner; and if he left it deliberately, out of ability, then he is disobedient to the One who commanded him. The disobedient is a transgressor, and the transgressor does not dwell beside the guardians of God; rather he is their enemy and they are his, because he has severed what God commanded to be joined.

The boy said: And one who knows the outward knowledge and acts upon it but does not know the inner. What is his standing among the people of religion?

The scholar said: An evil standing, for he is like a body that has been formed but into which no soul has been breathed; he is numbered among the dead, and the dead are the abodes of the disbelievers. As one who denies the signs of God, he is an enemy to religion and to its people.

The boy said: So I see that the outward is not made sound except by the inner, and the inner does not stand except by the outward; that this world is lawful only to the one who has known the Hereafter, which is its true life and its inner; and likewise that religion is not made whole for its people until they establish both its outward and its inner.

The scholar said: Yes, this is the meaning, and upon this the work is to be done. For the fulfilling of all that God has commanded is a claim upon His good pleasure, and the abandoning of any of what God has commanded is an exposing of oneself to His wrath.

The boy said: Is not the one who knows the outward together with the inner and establishes them both a believer?

The scholar said: Yes, he is a believer in truth.

The boy said: And is it not so that the one who knows and then works for the Hereafter finds this world made wholesome for him, its good things lawful, and the turning about among its gains permitted?

The scholar said: Yes. This world is made wholesome for none but them; they are the most deserving of it and the most entitled to it.

The boy said: Then why is it that I see the people of this description, who are the scholars and the folk of this affair, having cast off the world and all that is in it, until the ignorant among the people suppose it is forbidden to them? They turn away from this world; they look upon it only with a sidelong glance, and their souls are heavy toward it. And I see their colors gone sallow without illness, their bodies weak, their skins clinging to their bones, as though they do not eat food, even while they eat.

The narrator said: When the scholar heard the mention of his brethren and their description, he was choked with weeping and his eyes overflowed with tears, so that for a time he could not speak from the intensity of it. And the boy wept at his weeping, until the scholar feared that some of their enemies might prevail against them. So he held himself firm and his heart grew settled within him, and then the scholar turned toward the boy.

The scholar said: O my son, the people you have described in this way have looked upon themselves by the light of the true life and busied themselves in their affair with the certainty of salvation, and they have not dealt harshly with themselves. O my son, this world is seen by wealth and children, and the Hereafter is seen by knowledge and certainty. O my son, the faith of these people in the unseen is sound; they held true the promise and the warning, so they yearned toward what they were promised and sought it, and the fear of the threat filled them with dread, so they strove; they feared the stupor of death, so they hastened to good deeds. Their bodies grew weak from the persistence of fasting, and their colors turned sallow from the length of their vigils. They do not sleep when the sleepers sleep, out of dread, nor do they eat their sustenance except grudgingly. The longing of contemplation has turned them from the pleasure of ease, and the sweetness of remembrance has held them back from the respite of sleep. Their souls have been drawn away from the appetites, so this world was not made pleasant for them; they remember death often, so life was not made sweet to them; and they have beheld the bounties of the Hereafter with certainty, so they see this world only as a passing fragment. Thus they cast off the world in preoccupation from it, without holding it forbidden.

The boy said: This is the sound account, and it was in this sense that the scholars disdained this world, while the people are heedless of them, supposing that they have forbidden to themselves what God has made lawful for them. Now describe to me the delusion of the people of the world in their world, and their heedlessness of the Hereafter.

The scholar said: If those enamored of this world would only reflect upon their own creation and the turning of their states, from weakness to strength and from strength to weakness, from ease to ruin, and from life to death; and if they would then look upon those who passed before them of the vanished generations, among whom were the good and the wicked, and how all went down into one plain of earth, and how they saw no reward for the good in this world nor any punishment for the wicked in this world, they would know that they were created for other than this world. But earning weighed heavy upon them, and they would not carry upon themselves the burden of the seeking. So they seized the fleeting thing of this lower life and were content with the ease of this world in place of the Hereafter. This is their habit and this is their meaning. They have no argument before God and no honor before His guardians, and there has already come to them of the tidings that wherein is deterrence.
