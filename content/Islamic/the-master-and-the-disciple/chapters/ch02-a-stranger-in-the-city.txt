# A Stranger in the City

He answered them: "My need has passed me by, and I am at work in seeking it."

They said: "Did anything of what we said please you?"

He said: "All of it is strange, to one who marvels."

They said: "But wonder is of two kinds, one praiseworthy and one blameworthy."

He said: "Yes, I have learned that. And words too are of two kinds, praiseworthy and blameworthy."

They said: "Which is the praiseworthy kind, in your view?"

He said: "That which is correct."

They said: "And what is the correct?"

He said: "That whose origin was from Allah, and which calls to Allah."

They said: "And what is the blameworthy?"

He said: "That whose origin was caprice, and which calls to something other than guidance."

They said: "You have spoken truly. Have you nothing of your own words for us to hear?"

He said: "I have nothing to say of my own. Rather I am a follower of the right saying."

They said: "Then let us hear from your words what may show us your way. Your state has become sweet to us and your brotherhood has grown dear to us, and we hope that you will be a door of mercy that Allah has opened for us."

He said: "Yes." So he settled himself to speak, and the people fell silent for him, and he turned his face toward them.

Then he said: "He who created by His power and made all things even, and who ordained by His generosity and so gave guidance, is the One who brought forth the open ways of the pasture, making its outward as scattered chaff and its inward dark and rich, its paths not confused and its traces not effaced. Were it otherwise, imaginings would be lost in the demand of Him and minds would fail to reach Him. He is not manifest in being so as to be possessed by any but His own, nor so that the aim of His path could be altered. Rather He is generous and veiled: His knowledge is kept hidden for the sake of its worth, and His secrets are guarded for those who are their people. Its honor is the plumage of guidance, and its most complete adornment is the garment of piety. It is marked with the light of lights, a trust deposited in the breasts of the chosen and the righteous, keepers of the Word, honored scribes,

a trust which they discharge and a bound which they do not overstep. It is the law of the Judge and the bounties of the Most Merciful. He ennobled it and set it within, He shielded it and concealed it, He satisfied it with its due rights and crowned it with His covenants. So the aspirations of the thinkers do not reach it, and the schemes of the schemers do not attain it. Its outward is a remembrance and a proof for those who are guided, and its inward is a sure and abundant drink, whose sky is brought low to those who look and whose earth is made level for those who search, and whose falling stars have their appointed places for the seekers. It has watering places for those who journey toward it, and signs whose wonders follow one upon another, pointing by their likenesses to Him. Its course is a granting of favor and its way a setting-out in detail. Its rulings are obligations and its practice is a body of laws. Truthfulness has ennobled its doctrines and justice has made wholesome its gains, so that He has made it for every good a leader and a road, and made it for its own inner meaning a shield and a guide. Then He compelled minds, through ignorance and through need, to seek knowledge and sufficiency. Blessed is He who made the night and the day a succession for whoever would remember or would give thanks. And may Allah send blessings upon the one He chose from among His servants and made a warner to the worlds."

Then he said: "Fear Allah with the fear of one who dreads Him, and dread Him with the dread of one who hopes in Him. Seek knowledge and you will come to know, and act and you will be shown mercy."

Then his eyes overflowed with tears, and he broke off his speech and took leave of the people weeping, and rose and turned back toward his mount. Weeping came over the people at the warmth of his words and the reach of his admonition, and they wished to rise and go with him. But the keeping of good manners held them back from crowding upon him all together, for they did not know whether that would be agreeable to him, and so they turned back to their dwellings. Yet there followed after him, out of the company, a youth who was the youngest of them in years and the most forbearing of them in temper. Discipline had brought his mind to completion and reflection had kindled his heart, and there weighed upon him, along with that, all he had heard.
