# Unity, Justice, and the Living Witness

*Episode five of* The Master and the Disciple. *The disciple-now-master,* Ṣāliḥ *(SAA-lih), and the opposing community's greatest scholar,* Abu Malik *(a-BOO MAA-lik) — known among his people as* Kaʿb al-Aḥbār *(KAB al-ah-BAAR) — sit facing one another. The cup has been emptied. The description of religion has been given —* the rope extending from the heavens to the earth, continuous and without breakage, the firm handhold of those who adhere to Allah (al-LAH), the ark of the divine presence, the ship of salvation, the light of life. *And the most exact possible question is on the table:* what about the reality of what you have described? *Episode five is the answer. It is the climax of the entire book. The unity of Allah beyond names and adjectives; the syllogism of divine justice that requires a living* Ḥujja *(HUJ-jah) in every age; the conspiracy formula by which communities cut themselves off from guidance; the unbroken chain of prophets with no true* fatra *(FAT-rah), no interval; why the Friends of Allah are in concealment; and* Abu Malik*'s repentance and the seal of the work as a* model *— a replicable* daʿwa *(DAH-wa) arc from individual conversion to community-building.*

## Who is Allah? — beyond names and adjectives

Ṣāliḥ opened the final movement of the debate with a sentence that sounded almost familiar.

*I am calling you to Allah's justice and to the assertion of His unity.*

Abu Malik received it as if it were a creed he already shared. He had heard such language all his life. Ṣāliḥ stopped him immediately.

*The school you subscribe to describes Allah's justice without any clear understanding of* who *is being described, and strayed regarding His names without any real understanding of His unity.*

Abu Malik offered his own creed in answer. *I confess that Allah is one. He is just. He commands by giving a choice, and forbids by warning.* It was a clean statement. It was also, in Ṣāliḥ's hearing, exactly the wrong kind of clean — a tidiness of words that hovered above the realities the words were supposed to point to.

*Those are only names and descriptions,* Ṣāliḥ said, *and they are of no benefit without their essential realities. It is like saying* fire is hot *— the words are true, but you cannot cook food with the words, nor brighten darkness without the reality of fire. If nouns and adjectives could take the place of realities, your tongue would burn at the mere mention of fire, and your hunger would be satiated at the mention of food.*

The image is precise, and the doctrine it lands is the spine of the whole movement. A name without its reality is a sound without its referent. *Allah is one* is a true sentence. It is not the unity. *Allah is just* is a true sentence. It is not the justice. Religion that subsists at the level of true sentences about Allah is religion that has mistaken its menu for its meal. The mouth remembers the words. The tongue does not burn.

The Commander of the Faithful, in the opening sermon of *Nahj al-Balāgha*, lays down the same priority Ṣāliḥ has just pressed: *The foremost in religion is the recognition of Him; the perfection of recognizing Him is to testify to Him; the perfection of testifying to Him is to believe in His Oneness; the perfection of believing in His Oneness is to regard Him pure; and the perfection of His purity is to deny Him attributes — because every attribute is a proof that it is different from that to which it is attributed, and everything to which something is attributed is different from the attribute* (Sermon 1, translated by Sayed Ali Reza, Ahlul Bayt Digital Islamic Library Project edition, pp. 39–40). The argument is identical at the joint. Every attribute, by being predicated of a thing, distinguishes itself from that thing. To pile attributes upon Allah is to multiply that distinction, and to multiply distinction is to lose the unity the attributes were supposed to honor. Pure unity is reached by stripping the attributes back to the One they were trying to name.

Then Ṣāliḥ pressed the letters themselves. He took the Arabic word for *one* — ⟪ar:واحد⟫, *wāḥid* (WAA-hid) — written with four letters, *two joined and two separate*. *Which of these letters,* he asked, *is one? Or are they all one together?*

Then he did the same with the word ⟪ar:اللہ⟫ — *Allāh* — also four letters. *Which of these is Allah? Are they Allah together, or are they signs pointing to Allah?*

Abu Malik conceded the only honest answer. *They must be signs pointing to Allah.*

Ṣāliḥ pressed further. *But then which letters point to Him? When you say* Allāh*, you separate the initial* alif *(ah-LIF) and join the remaining three —* ⟪ar:للہ⟫ *—* lillāh*. Which of these points to Allah? The one by itself? Or the three joined? Or both at once?*

Abu Malik did not know what to say.

Ṣāliḥ named the flaw. The obsessive analysis of structure, pronunciation, and presentation — composing beautiful tracks of Allah's names, perfecting the form of *wuḍūʾ* (wu-DOO) and *rukūʿ* (ru-KOO) and *sajda* (SAJ-dah) — while forgetting the essential meaning.

*In the end,* he said, *it all boils down to one simple fact: do you understand who Allah is, the reality behind His unity, or do you only have a textbook definition of that* tawḥīd *(taw-HEED)?*

## From a name or from an attribute?

Ṣāliḥ then narrowed the question. *Where did your understanding of Allah come from — from a name, or from an attribute?*

*From a name,* Abu Malik said. *Allah.*

*Is* Allah *a name, or someone named?*

*Someone named.*

*Then what is his name?*

*Allah.*

*Allah is a name for Allah? Then what is the difference between the name and the one named — and which of the two do you worship?*

Abu Malik, bewildered, fell back on what he had thought was solid ground. He had concluded, he said, from observing that *everything manufactured has a manufacturer*, that this immense universe must have a Creator. A Creator who does not resemble His creations.

Even this, Ṣāliḥ corrected.

*You have taken us away from your original meaning. Your difficulty in comprehending Allah's unity forced you to seek Him through logical assumption, and so you forged a likeness for Him. But Allah is unique and exalted beyond that, for everything is made from a pre-existing substance, while Allah creates from no substance. As for worldly manufacturers, you became aware of them by observing how they constructed things — but Allah did not make you witness His creation, nor does He have a likeness whose creation you could observe.*

The analogy fails at both ends. The universe was not made from prior material the way manufactured things are. The Maker is not a Maker among makers we could compare to.

Ṣāliḥ then exposed the subtler form of the same error. *Your saying* there is nothing like His image *implies He* has *an image — whereas if you truly meant absolute unity, you would have said* there is none like Him*. He is far exalted beyond your implicit analogy. Eyes cannot tolerate the light of the sun, which is only one of this lower world's lights — so how can they perceive the Creator?*

To say *nothing is like His image* is to grant Him an image and specify that nothing matches it. To say *there is none like Him* is to refuse the analogy at the root.

*My words have left me,* Abu Malik confessed. *Intellects are bewildered, imaginations incapable of thinking about Him.*

## The syllogism of divine justice

Ṣāliḥ then walked Abu Malik through a formal argument — a syllogism that is the backbone of the book's *daʿwa*.

*Can the One of unimaginable immensity be perceived by our vision?*

*No,* Abu Malik said. *He is far above that.*

*Can we hear His speech?*

*No, for speech comes from a speaker described by his speech, and Allah is beyond such attributes.*

*Then would this All-Embracing, Exalted One be unjust to His creatures — imposing what they cannot bear, commanding what they cannot perceive, then punishing them?*

*Anyone who imposes such an impossible obligation cannot be just,* Abu Malik admitted. *And Allah is far exalted above that.*

The conclusion is unavoidable, and Ṣāliḥ stated it step by step. *Would the One removed from injustice be pleased with ignorance as a companion for His servants? Did He create them as perfect scholars, or as ignorant? Then how did they receive the knowledge of the gratitude desired from them — so that they could earn His pleasure and amnesty from His wrath?*

*Such knowledge could not be perceived except through* intermediaries *between Him and His creatures. Their existence is His act of justice, for and against His creation. He selected them from His knowledge, purified them above the rest, sent them as messengers and arguments, and made them just witnesses between Him and His servants. Willing obedience to them becomes part of obeying Allah; disobeying them entails His wrath. Otherwise, how else would the divine command extend from Allah to His creatures?*

Set the syllogism out in four lines. One — Allah cannot be perceived directly. Two — Allah is not unjust, and would not punish for failures of obedience to commands that could not have been received. Three — therefore Allah cannot impose obligations without a just witness to communicate them. Four — therefore a living *Ḥujja*, a just intermediary, must exist in every age.

*This,* Abu Malik said, *is the very foundation of justice.*

The syllogism has derived the necessity of an unbroken chain of living intermediaries not from a verse, not from a transmitted report, not from a sectarian commitment — but from the bare meaning of *Allah is just*. The chain is not a doctrine added on to monotheism. It is what monotheism, taken seriously about its own justice, has been requiring all along.

The same syllogism is given canonical statement four centuries earlier by the foundational Imami theologian of Baghdad in his creedal summary: *It is necessary in justice that Allah has not left His creation in any age without a guide and a proof — for if He had, He would have imposed obedience to a command without providing the means of receiving it, and that is the very injustice from which He is exalted* (Shaykh al-Mufīd, *Awāʾil al-Maqālāt fī al-Madhāhib wa al-Mukhtārāt*, section on the necessity of the Imamate, translated by I.K.A. Howard in his introduction to *Kitāb al-Irshād: The Book of Guidance*, Tahrike Tarsile Qur'an edition, 1981, pp. 12–14). The continuity is not coincidence. The reasoning Ṣāliḥ marshals is the reasoning the Imami theological tradition has held to be the rational backbone of the Imamate from its earliest centuries — not as a sectarian doctrine added on, but as the only conclusion the bare premise of divine justice will admit.

## The hierarchy of witnesses

Ṣāliḥ then established the deeper structural point. *Allah's wise judgment is one and unchanging,* he said. *He would not point out His command to one group through a just witness and impose the same command on another without one — for justice is unified by its nature; if a judgment had two meanings, one would be unjust.*

The succession of messengers, therefore, is not an episodic mercy renewed at intervals according to political convenience. It is the continuous discharge of a single justice across the ages. Every age receives a witness. The duty of willing obedience to that witness is imposed wherever the witness is sent — which, by the prior argument, is everywhere and always.

But the witnesses are not interchangeable. They have ranks, *one subordinate to another, yet all equal in reward*. And just as the superior messenger — the *nāṭiq* (NAA-tiq), the speaking prophet — is distinguished by his knowledge of *the unseen in the revealed book*, the subordinate, the *waṣī* (wa-SEE), is distinguished by his knowledge of *the unseen in the esoteric interpretation* of the book. Both the revealed word and its interpretation come from Allah through revealed inspiration. The subordinate is called *subordinate* only because of his obedience to, and need for, his superior — as Allah's inspiration to *Lūṭ* came by means of *Ibrāhīm*, and to *Ismāʿīl* and *Isḥāq*, while the blessing and excellence were assigned to Ibrahim.

The architecture is the architecture episode two had already named — the *nāṭiq* and the *waṣī*, the speaker and his trustee. Here it is being named in the register of justice rather than cosmology. The *waṣī* receives by way of the speaker, and what he carries is the inward of what the speaker has uttered outwardly. The blessing flows down through the chain; the obedience flows up through it.

*In His justice,* Ṣāliḥ closed, *He does not like His commands to be cut off from His servants after His blessing to them, for that would stop their worship and legitimize ignorance.*

A true *fatra* would be Allah legitimizing ignorance. Allah does not legitimize ignorance. Therefore the chain has no true *fatra*.

## The conspiracy formula — how communities went astray

Abu Malik then asked the decisive question. *If Allah obligated obedience to the prophets of resolution and their inspired interpreters, what is the matter with these people who sit in their assemblies pretending to resemble the prophets of resolution, agreeing that they alone uphold Allah's command? How is it that the learned scholars of this community have abandoned inquiry?*

The question is the question every honest scholar of every dominant community must one day put to himself. If the chain is what justice requires, why does the community deny that the chain continues? Why does it insist that *no prophet comes after our prophet*?

Ṣāliḥ answered with a historical and sociological analysis — what we might call the *conspiracy formula*.

*They no longer inquire as they should, because it has been imbued in their hearts that they are the true possessors of Allah's command, and that there will be no prophet after their prophet. This is exactly what was said by those who strayed before them, for every community claims there is no prophet after its own.*

Then he named the mechanism that converts the formula into a sociology.

*Every period has started with three types: a rebellious devil, a willing oppressor, and a hypocritical religious scholar.*

The three together produce the result the formula was designed to produce. The rebellious devil supplies the disposition to lead astray. The willing oppressor supplies the political power to enforce the misleading. The hypocritical religious scholar supplies the legitimacy that lets the political power present itself as religion. The triad is reproducible. It has appeared, the source argues, in every community at the point where that community substituted its own authority for the chain it had inherited.

*By propagating* no prophet after our prophet*, these three cut off the outward traces of prophecy from its living reality so they could exploit those traces as a commodity.* The traces — rituals, recitations, institutional forms — remain. The living reality, the continuing chain, is denied. With the living reality denied, the traces become *property* the three can dispose of, monetize, and use as the instrument of their dominion.

*They insinuated into their communities that no one superior to their own prophet would ever come — so the people abandoned seeking true religion without even realizing it, became attached to their own prophet while denying those who followed, and even imagined this denial drew them closer to him. Thus the devils who lead astray became established, the oppressors gained power to kill the prophets, and the evil scholars made it all attractive.*

The community is taught to *love* its own captivity. Denying the continuing chain is presented as fidelity to the prophet. Once the formula is in place, the community does the conspirators' work for them.

## The same formula across communities

Ṣāliḥ then traced the formula across the four great communities.

The Zoroastrians cling to their fire-temples, *declaring that Allah will never send a messenger after Zoroaster*. The community of the Torah tend to their calf and submit to their judges, *declaring that Allah will never send a messenger after Mūsā*. The community of the Gospels venerate their priests and worship their prophet as God, *declaring that Allah will never send a messenger after ʿĪsā*. *And now your community has followed in the ways of those who preceded them.*

The repetition is the diagnosis. The same formula, the same closure, the same handover of religion to a class that profits from declaring that religion has ended. The communities differ in their rituals and their relics. They do not differ in the *form* of their captivity.

The corruption Ṣāliḥ names of his interlocutor's community is sharp. *They inherited the book from those unworthy of it, followed the seductions of their leaders, established prayers at the wrong times, and paid alms to the undeserving — all while proclaiming that Allah would never send a messenger after their messenger.*

Four charges line up with four pillars of practice. The Book in the wrong hands. The leaders followed for seduction rather than truth. The prayers timed by corrupted calendars. The alms paid to those who should not be receiving them.

*That is how you and those like you followed the great majority,* Ṣāliḥ concluded, *terrified by scholarly consensus and the tyranny of rulers.*

Scholarly consensus. The tyranny of rulers. The faithful are not crushed by argument. They are crushed by the combined weight of what *everyone is saying* and what *the powerful are doing*.

## Turning the consensus argument back

Ṣāliḥ then performed an exquisite move. He took the consensus argument Abu Malik's community uses against him, and turned it back against the community itself.

*If we must acknowledge this community on the basis of its consensus that Allah has cut off His proofs, then the earlier communities are even more deserving of acknowledgment, on three points: they preceded you in adhering to Allah's religion; you confirm the truth of what their prophets brought, but they confirmed it before you; and you all attest that they followed the true messengers, while none of them attest to your prophet. So they hold precedence through your mutual consensus, while you stand alone.*

If consensus is the test, the older communities pass that test with more votes. The consensus argument, taken seriously, places Abu Malik's community at the *bottom* of the line — alone, latest, supported by no community that came before.

Then Ṣāliḥ released the second half of the move, which dissolves the whole edifice.

*However, if the Creator is the One who judges wisely, abrogating and confirming as He wills, then everything these groups say collapses; for He has more right to create and command, and is engaged in a new work every day — so His actions could not be denied even if He sent a warner every day.*

First an internal critique — *on your own terms*, the consensus argument cuts against you. Then the displacement — *the whole logic that put consensus in the position to do this work is the logic that needs to be abandoned*. Consensus is not the test. The Creator is the test, and the Creator does new work every day. To bind Allah's continuing work to the votes of past communities is to put the creature in judgment over the Creator.

## The unbroken chain — there was no *fatra*

Abu Malik, accepting that Allah's argument continues through His just witnesses, asked how to reconcile this with the apparent absence of prophets in their own time. He pointed to what tradition called the *fatra* — the *interval* between Ibrahim and Moses, or between Moses and Jesus.

Ṣāliḥ refuted the very notion of an interval, and he did it by naming the chain.

*What* interval *was there after the community of Ibrahim, when Allah sent after him* ⟪ar:اسماعیل، اسحاق، یعقوب، یونس، شعیب⟫ *— Ismāʿīl, Isḥāq, Yaʿqūb* (YAA-koob), *Yūnus* (YOO-nus), *Shuʿayb* (SHU-ayb) — *leading to Moses? At that time He revealed to them the commands for good action, calling to the community of Ibrahim, upholding prayer and giving charity — until He sent down the Torah upon Moses.*

*And what* interval *was there after Moses, before the Gospel, since after Moses Allah sent* ⟪ar:یوشی بن نون، الیاس، داؤد، سلیمان، طالوط، زکریا⟫ *— Yūshaʿ ibn Nūn* (YOO-shah ibn NOON), *Ilyās* (il-YAAS), *Dāwūd* (DAA-wood), *Sulaymān* (su-lay-MAAN), *Ṭālūt* (TAA-loot), *Zakariyyā* (za-ka-RIY-yaa) — *and* ⟪ar:یحیی⟫ — Yaḥyā *(YAH-yaa) — leading up to* ⟪ar:عیسی⟫ — ʿĪsā*? They all upheld the judgment of the Torah, worshiping by it and reciting its psalms, until He sent down the Gospel upon Jesus.*

The lists are themselves the argument. The *interval* is a verbal artifact, sustained by failing to count the figures who fill it. The chain is unbroken; only the popular memory of it is broken.

The historian of the Ismaili tradition observes that this insistence on the unbroken chain of living witnesses — *the earth is never empty of a proof of Allah, whether manifest or concealed* — is one of the most stable doctrinal markers of the school, surviving every political reverse and every shift of theological vocabulary across a thousand years of texts (Farhad Daftary, *The Ismāʿīlīs: Their History and Doctrines*, second edition, Cambridge University Press, 2007, pp. 84–87 and 105–108). The figures the source recites between Ibrāhīm and Moses, and again between Moses and Jesus, are not an antiquarian flourish. They are the empirical filling of a logical claim — that no period since the first prophet has lacked its living witness, even when popular memory has lost the count.

Then Ṣāliḥ named the verse the tradition has cited for the *interval*, and read it differently.

⟪ar:يَٓأَهْلَ ٱلْكِتَبِ قَدْ جَآءَكُمْ رَسُولُنَا يُبَيِّنُ لَكُمْ عَلَىٰ فَتْرَةٍۢ مِّنَ ٱلرُّسُلِ⟫ — *O People of the Scripture, there has come to you Our Messenger to make clear to you after a* fatra *of messengers* (Q 5:19).

*In this verse,* Ṣāliḥ said, *the* interval *refers to the public manifestation of the divine call after a period of fear, when they were calling people to Allah in secret. The earth is never without a just witness from Allah for the blink of an eye — either publicly and openly, or in fear and concealment.*

The reading is exact. The *fatra* of the verse is not a cessation of witnesses. It is a *concealment* of witnesses — an interval in their *public manifestation*, not in their existence. The witnesses are always there. They are sometimes visible and sometimes hidden. The earth never lacks a *Ḥujja*. The visibility of the *Ḥujja* fluctuates with the conditions the community creates.

The witnesses of their own time are not *absent*. They are *concealed*. The concealment is itself a feature of the chain, not an exception to it.

## Why the Friends of Allah are in concealment

Abu Malik asked the natural next question. What is the meaning of the witnesses' concealment in the present time?

Ṣāliḥ answered with the example of Mūsā (peace be upon him), *who fled and hid from the people of* Firʿawn *(fir-AHN), crossing to the land of* Madyan *(MAD-yan) and the city of the prophet Shuʿayb, because he feared for his life*.

Then he asked Abu Malik a question that cut the moral ground out from under the demand for the witness to appear.

*Have you heard of any of Allah's just witnesses, the descendants of His prophets, who were murdered while persevering in their role?*

*Yes,* Abu Malik said. *A great many.*

*Then which of them would you want to reveal himself to the people of your time? A martyr resurrected after death? Or the one fleeing from you, while you seek to bring him to the death he is fleeing?*

The question forecloses the demand. The demand *let him appear so we may see him* is, in the conditions the questioner himself has created, the demand *let him appear so we may kill him*. The witnesses' concealment is not stubbornness. It is prudence under threat. *So Allah's just witness follows the accustomed way of His prophets — persevering under persecution, and remaining hidden until Allah judges between him and us.*

Then Ṣāliḥ named the precise typology of the three categories of people in relation to the Imam's hiddenness, and the third category is where the moral weight lands.

*There is the relentless murderer, who never tires of hunting them down.*

*There is their deep-seated enemy, who bites his nails in rage and never forgets his hatred.*

*And finally, there are those who dislike the enemies' actions and are sympathetic to the friends of Allah,* even though they abandon them *— who have no spiritual insight regarding faith, and cannot be relied upon to protect the secret of the imams.*

*It is because of this third category,* Ṣāliḥ said, *that the friends of Allah have concealed themselves from you.*

This is the moment in the book at which the conspiracy formula is brought home to the conscience of the listener. The witnesses are not concealed only because of their enemies. They are concealed because of their *sympathizers* — sympathizers who would not protect them, sympathizers whose loyalty is sentiment rather than insight, sympathizers who cannot be trusted with the secret. The first two categories are the active enemies and the implacable haters. The third category is *most of us* — those who would not raise the sword, would even feel something stir in our hearts at the witness's plight, and would also, when the pressure came, fail to keep his location secret, fail to defend him, fail to be the kind of witness *to* the witness that his survival in the open would require. The concealment is not done *to* us. It is done *because of* us.

The shift in moral responsibility is total. The sympathetic abandoners cannot complain of the witnesses' absence. The witnesses' absence is what their sympathy, unprotected by insight and undefended by action, has produced.

The French interpreter of esoteric Shīʿism puts the same point in psychological register. The hiddenness of the just witness is never an absence imposed *on* the community by Allah, but always an absence the community has *produced* by its incapacity to host the witness's presence: *the occultation is a measure of the spiritual condition of those from whom the Imam is hidden, not a withdrawal of the divine economy from history* (Henry Corbin, *Cyclical Time and Ismaili Gnosis*, translated by Ralph Manheim and James Morris, Kegan Paul International for the Islamic Publications Series of the Institute of Ismaili Studies, 1983, pp. 70–73). The reading aligns precisely with Ṣāliḥ's diagnosis of the third category. The witness has not been taken away. The witness has been driven into hiding by the very people who would, if asked, say they loved him.

## The scholars who serve the oppressors

Ṣāliḥ then confronted Abu Malik with the complicity of the scholars who serve the oppressors. *Those who blacken a flag or fill an inkwell for the murderers of Allah's witnesses share in the bloodshed.*

The image is concrete and merciless. The minor service to power — blackening the flag the troops will march under, filling the inkwell the decree will be signed with — is named as participation in the killing those troops and that decree will accomplish. The chain of complicity does not stop at the scribe. The chain of complicity does not stop at the standard-bearer. The chain of complicity, by Ṣāliḥ's reckoning, includes every minor functionary whose pen or banner makes the oppressor's reach longer.

Then he named the deeper complicity. *Indeed, the legal scholar who passes opinions enabling the oppressors is worse than the murderer, for were it not for the opinions of these legal scholars, the oppressors could not maintain their dominion over the foolish.*

The reasoning is structural. The murderer kills a person. The legal scholar who legitimates the murder *enables a system of murder*. The murderer is the instrument; the legal scholar is the engine. Without the *fatwā* (FAT-wah) that names the killing as lawful, the killing cannot be repeated, cannot be normalized, cannot become the policy of a state. The *fatwā* is what turns a murder into a doctrine. The doctrine is what turns the murderer into an institution. By that calculus, the scholar who licenses the killing is the cause of more killings than the killer, and *carries the heavier weight*.

The principle is the explicit doctrine of an early prophetic report preserved in the canonical hadith collection compiled in the third Islamic century: *The scholars are the trustees of the messengers, until they enter the gates of the rulers; once they enter the gates of the rulers, they have betrayed the messengers — so beware of them* (al-Kulaynī, *al-Kāfī*, Book of the Excellence of Knowledge, chapter on warnings against scholars who keep the company of rulers, hadith 5, translated by Muhammad Sarwar, Islamic Seminary Publications edition, vol. 1, pp. 78–79). The hadith and Ṣāliḥ's argument converge at the same structural point. The scholar's seat carries the trust of the messengers *because* it is the seat through which prophetic command reaches the community. To convert that seat into the engine of an oppressive regime is not a minor lapse. It is the betrayal that flips the entire chain into its opposite — the chain of legitimacy now running from the oppressor down through the community, rather than from the messenger down through the witness.

Ṣāliḥ marshaled three verses. ⟪ar:وَلَا تَرْكَنُوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ فَتَمَسَّكُمُ ٱلنَّارُ⟫ — *Do not incline toward those who do wrong, lest the Fire touch you* (Q 11:113). ⟪ar:أَطِيعُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا۟ ٱلرَّسُولَ وَأُو۟لِى ٱلْأَمْرِ مِنكُمْ⟫ — *Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you* (Q 4:59), *warning that the wrongdoers had been falsely treated as* those in authority. ⟪ar:قَتِلُوا۟ ٱلَّذِينَ يَلُونَكُم مِّنَ ٱلْكُفَّارِ⟫ — *Fight those who reject Allah, even if they are close to you* (Q 9:123).

The three verses describe the inversion the community has accepted. The Qurʾan forbids inclining toward the wrongdoers; the community has inclined. The Qurʾan obligates obedience to *those in authority* — meaning the rightful authorities, the chain — and the community has transferred that obedience to the wrongdoers it should have refused. The Qurʾan obligates struggle against those who reject Allah, even when they are close kin; the community has spared the close kin and persecuted the witnesses.

*You willfully disobeyed the one Allah commanded you to obey,* Ṣāliḥ said, *and willingly obeyed those you were commanded to struggle against — all while assuming you were among those doing good.*

The closing clause is the one that lands hardest. *All while assuming you were among those doing good.* The error is not even visible to the ones committing it. The conspiracy formula has done its work. The very people who have made the killing possible imagine themselves to be the most faithful of the community.

## The path of return

Ṣāliḥ then offered the path of return — and the offer is precisely calibrated to the diagnosis.

*If you were all to help the friends of Allah, they would reveal themselves; and if you were to abandon their enemies, they would not be victorious.*

The two clauses are the inverse of the two failures. The friends are hidden *because* the sympathizers have not helped; help them, and the hiding ends. The enemies dominate *because* the community has obeyed them; abandon them, and the domination ends. The repair is in the community's hands. Allah does not need to be persuaded to send the witness back into the open. The witness will return to the open at the moment the conditions that drove him underground are reversed. The reversal is the community's own work.

Abu Malik acknowledged that this was the true description of his community, and asked how to find salvation.

*With Allah,* Ṣāliḥ replied. *Seek protection with Him and He will give you refuge; turn to Him in repentance and He will accept it; ask Him for the path of His friends and He will rescue you through your own righteous actions. For Allah does not do an atom's weight of wrong, and does not hasten to punish even those who hasten to disobey Him. He loves those who turn to Him in repentance.*

The path is the path the syllogism would have predicted. Turn to Allah. Ask for the path of His friends — the path of the chain, which the syllogism has just shown to be the only path divine justice could have laid. Trust the patience of a Lord who does not rush to punish. Trust the love of a Lord who receives the repentant. The salvation is not a transaction. It is a return into the path the chain has been keeping open the whole time, waiting for the returning soul to find it.

Abu Malik turned to Allah in repentance, seeking the means to please Him.

Ṣāliḥ's eyes filled with tears at the mention of repentance, and he concluded his speech. The greatest scholar of the opposing community had been brought, by argument that never raised its voice, to the foot of the chain he had spent his life resisting. The cup that episode four had begun to empty was now empty. The way that episode four had opened had now been walked.

## The model — not a fictitious story

Ṣāliḥ consulted his spiritual father, the scholar, about Abu Malik and his companions. The scholar's advice was the advice of a *daʿī* who knew the seekers come in many grades.

*You know your companions better than I. If you recognize good in them, guide them as you were guided; but if you are apprehensive, test them by ignoring them — not harshly — and order your father to treat them kindly during their period of trial. For the one truly seeking good will not remain hidden.*

The instruction has two halves. Where the disposition is clear, accept and guide directly. Where the disposition is uncertain, *test by withdrawal* — not by hostility, only by absence. Let the apparent seeker discover whether his seeking survives the absence of the sought. The seeker whose seeking is real will press through the silence. The seeker whose seeking was a performance will lose interest when the audience is removed. The test is gentle; the discrimination is exact.

*So Abu Malik and his companions passed through their levels of trial until their affair was complete and they recognized their right guidance; and it was Allah's friend among them who took care of their guidance. They returned to their people, warning them, and through them Allah guided a great many of His servants to His religion.*

The arc is the arc the whole book has been tracing — but now multiplied. One scholar finds one disciple. One disciple meets the Sheikh and meets the Imam. One disciple returns home and converts one father. One father and one son convert the greatest scholar of the opposing community. The scholar returns to his people and converts a *great many*. Each phase is a *daʿī*'s career in miniature. Each new convert becomes the seed of the next phase. The chain that the syllogism derived from divine justice now reveals itself as a chain that propagates — not by institutional appointment, not by political conquest, but by the same one-to-one transmission that started the book.

And then the seal:

*This was not a fictitious story, but rather the confirmation of what Allah has commanded. For in it is the confirmation of the divine messengers, the signs of their trustees, and the proper behavior of those who are seeking.*

The whole work, the source tells us, is to be read as a *model* — not as biography, not as history, but as a *replicable daʿwa arc*. The scholar finds the disciple. The disciple meets the Sheikh. He returns home and converts his father. Father and son convert Abu Malik. Abu Malik and his group convert their community. *Each phase is a stage in the listener's own potential journey.* The narrative is offered as a template the reader is invited to walk. The reader is being asked to recognize, in the disciple's confusion, his own confusion; in the disciple's parting from the Master, his own parting; in the disciple's seven-day rebirth, the rebirth that is available to him; in *Ṣāliḥ*'s patient cup-emptying, the patience required of any *daʿī*; in *Abu Malik*'s painful concession, the concession required of any honest scholar of a corrupted tradition. The model is offered to be inhabited, not admired from outside.

## Closing

*So praise be to Allah, in the beginning and at the end. May Allah's blessings be upon His Messenger, our master Muhammad — Salallahu alayhi wa aalihee wa sallam — sent to His creatures as a bearer of good news and a warner; and upon his trustee, the imam of those who are mindful of Allah; and upon the imams from the people of his house upon whom Allah has bestowed His favors — may He take from them all impurity and purify them entirely. And Allah is sufficient for us, the best of trustees and the best of supporters.*

⟪ar:ولا حول ولا قوۃ الا باللہ العلی العظیم⟫ — *And there is no strategy and no strength except through Allah, the Exalted, the Powerful.*

## What this episode lands

A demolition of the false unity that mistakes the four letters of *Allāh* for the One they point toward. A formal syllogism that derives the necessity of a living *Ḥujja* in every age from the bare meaning of *Allah is just*. A hierarchy of witnesses — *nāṭiq* and *waṣī* — whose superior carries the unseen of the revealed book and whose subordinate carries the unseen of its esoteric interpretation, both by inspiration. A conspiracy formula — *rebellious devil, willing oppressor, hypocritical scholar* — by which every dominant community has cut itself off from the chain it inherited, propagating *no prophet after our prophet* as the very form of its devotion. A walk through the four communities — Zoroastrian, of the Torah, of the Gospels, of the present community — showing the same closure producing the same corruption. A turning of the consensus argument back upon those who wield it, *and* a displacement of the whole logic of consensus by the logic of the Creator who works a new work every day. A refutation of the *fatra* by the simple recitation of the names that fill it. A re-reading of Q 5:19 that locates the *interval* in the public manifestation of the call, not in the existence of the witnesses. A typology of the witnesses' concealment — relentless murderer, deep-seated enemy, sympathetic abandoner — that shifts the moral weight onto the third category, the most of us. A naming of the complicity of scholars who serve oppressors as *worse than the murderer*. Three Qurʾanic verses that describe the inversion the community has accepted. A path of return whose two clauses — *help the friends, abandon the enemies* — invert the two failures and return the witness to the open. A model, sealed at the end of the work, of a *daʿwa* arc that propagates one-to-one from a single Persian seeker to a great many of Allah's servants.

The book closes here. The unity of Allah has been distinguished from the names by which the unity is spoken. The justice of Allah has been shown to require the chain that the conspiracy formula denies. The chain has been shown to be unbroken in fact and concealed only in manifestation. The concealment has been shown to be the community's own work. The path of return has been named. And the whole work has been re-offered as a model the reader is invited to walk — from his own confusion, through his own purification, through his own meeting with the Sheikh and the Imam, through his own return home, through his own patient demolition of the strongest scholar of the corrupted tradition, through his own one-to-one extension of the chain to a great many of Allah's servants. *This was not a fictitious story.* It was the confirmation of what Allah has commanded. The listener has been handed not a doctrine to assent to but a journey to begin.
