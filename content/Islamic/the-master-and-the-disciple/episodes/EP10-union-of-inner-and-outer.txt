# Union Of Inner And Outer

## Opening directive
Open warm: "Welcome. We're with the Ismaili dialogue The Master and the Disciple, and the question that follows once the inner has been opened — does the outer still matter?" Land the spine: Only the union of inner and outer is faith; inner alone rots like fruit peeled before ripening, outer alone is a body without spirit. (Placement 1.) Target a long-form discussion.

## Name discipline
Use these stable English labels every time. First mention takes the epithet; thereafter the bare label.
- the scholar (the master of this chapter)
- the disciple (the seeker)
- the Father of Imams (peace be upon him, first mention only)
- the Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, first mention only)
- the second caliph (the ruler in the thirsting-woman case)
- the true scholars
Never speak Arabic book titles. Do not pair the leadership-title with the personal name of the Father of Imams.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- fasiq: one who has rotted from obedience
- fisq: the rotting
- kafir: one who buries the seed
- mu'min: a person of faith
- shari'a: the outer obligations
- al-fikr: reflection
- dhikr Allah: remembrance of Allah
- al-sunna: the inherited divine way
- zakat al-abdan: the zakat of the body
Cite Quran as "the chapter on the heights, verse one hundred and seventy-nine" — never the Arabic surah name.

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: inner alone, outer alone. The seeker who keeps only the inner is fasiq, fruit peeled before ripening — it rots; he has severed what Allah commanded to be joined. The one who keeps only the outer is kafir in the root sense of a farmer hiding the seed — a body with no spirit, numbered among the dead. Foreground the four meanings of life from the verse "do not let the life of this physical world deceive you."
Beat 2 — Pivot: the union as faith; pallor as vigil. Only the union is the mu'min; for him this world becomes licit — repossessed, not refused. Repeat the spine VERBATIM (placement 2). The disciple asks about pallor: the scholar weeps and answers — pale faces record nights in vigil, distracted from luxury by al-fikr and engrossed in dhikr Allah. Not asceticism; attention.
Beat 3 — Stakes: identical instrument, mercy under necessity. No chest has two hearts; no ignorant person has only one eye. The same instrument of will, intellect, and reasoning sits in every chest — the verse "they have hearts but do not understand with them" judges the use, not the equipment. Archery image: one target, identical bows, unequal hits; the root corrupts when al-sunna is left. Close on zakat al-abdan and the thirsting woman — the second caliph orders her stoned; the Father of Imams frees her by the verse of necessity. Repeat the spine VERBATIM (placement 3) and ask: which obligation have you kept without its inner, or which inner without its obligation, and what would the union ask of you this week?

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, John, scholar) leads; Host B (female, Hannah, seeker) challenges at least three times and concedes once.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — calling the inner-only seeker fasiq is rhetoric, not argument." "That sounds like wordplay — kafir as 'farmer hiding a seed' is etymology dressed as doctrine." "Isn't this just replacing one literalism with another — how is this different from a code?"

## Tone constraints
Use EXACTLY three governing analogies; reuse them every time. No new analogies mid-episode.
Analogy 1: Fruit peeled before ripening — the chapter's image for inner without outer; it rots.
Analogy 2: A body with no spirit — the chapter's image for outer without inner; numbered among the dead.
Analogy 3: Archers at one target, identical bows, unequal hits — the chapter's image for identical instrument, unequal use.
Plain transliteration; no diacritics. Name positionality: the classical Ismaili reading.
