# Homecoming And The Forty Year Syllogism

Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation.

## Opening directive
Open with a brief warm welcome — one or two sentences — naming *The Master and the Disciple* by Jafar ibn Mansur and previewing: a son sent home with a six-line operating manual, who meets his father's two-horn charge with a forty-year syllogism that closes every door. Land the spine verbatim (R-RECURRING-THESIS, 1 of 3): "What has been received must be transmitted." No "welcome back," "in this episode," or "today we'll discuss."

## Stable role-labels
Use these labels every time; never rotate.
- the Sheikh — the master who trained the young man
- the young disciple — the student now sent home
- the scholar — his spiritual father (trust-bearer)
- the father — his biological father at home
- the Imam — the one whose argument the chain upholds
- the Father of Imams (peace be upon him) — first mention only; thereafter "the Father of Imams." His sayings: "the book of eloquence."
Do NOT pair the leadership-title of the Father of Imams with his personal name — forbidden.

## Name discipline
Abraham at first mention — Rotation: Abraham / the prophet who confronted his father / he. The Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family) — first mention only, if invoked. Aristotle — once, naming the constructive-dilemma form.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- hujja: trust-bearer
- Imam: Imam
- Sheikh: Sheikh
- Nahj al-Balagha: the book of eloquence
- Jafar ibn Mansur: Jafar ibn Mansur

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis and release. The Sheikh names three reasons to send the disciple home (travel; the father's sorrow; obligation to transmit) and binds him with four counsels: be mindful of Allah, uphold the oath, safeguard the scholar, conceal identity. Foreground the stakes: what has been received must be transmitted.

Beat 2 — The six-line manual and the three thanks. At the city's edge the scholar gives six counsels: entrust resolution to Allah; speak with wisdom; refuse anger when wronged; refuse upset when called a liar; be patient with responders; be the best example. The disciple thanks him for the Imam whose argument was upheld, the trust-bearer whose pact was kept, the corpse given life. Repeat the spine verbatim (R-RECURRING-THESIS, 2 of 3): "What has been received must be transmitted."

Beat 3 — Homecoming, two-horn charge, pivot, stakes. The father charges betrayal: if you found truth you concealed it; if you were deceived you destroyed yourself. The disciple offers silence or open debate; the father refuses silence. The pivot: across forty years either you were a scholar who withheld, or you were ignorant who should have preceded me — either way you cannot condemn. Name it a constructive dilemma (Aristotle's *Prior Analytics*). The father weeps; he enters the path; through father and son a great number are revived.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least three times and concedes once. Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — doesn't the syllogism just bully a grieving father?"; "That sounds like wordplay — how is constructive dilemma different from rhetorical trapping?"; "Isn't this just replacing one paternal authority with another?" One real concession from Host B when Host A shows the bond inside which the pressing landed.

## Tone constraints
Analogy 1: the six counsels as an operating manual — each line a rule for a moment where temper or vanity could break the call.
Analogy 2: two horns of a charge met by two horns of a reply — every door the charge leaves open, the reply closes.
Analogy 3: roots watered and branches grown tall — why the young man is ready to be sent.
No new analogies mid-episode.

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud.

## Landing
Place the third verbatim repetition (R-RECURRING-THESIS, 3 of 3): "What has been received must be transmitted." Close on a reflective question grounded in this chapter: which piece of what we have received are we still holding back from the parent, friend, or colleague who has been waiting for the argument that finally lets them admit they were waiting? Leave that question with the listener. Do not tidy into resolution.
