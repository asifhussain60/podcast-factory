# Who Is Allah Beyond Names

## Opening directive
Open with a warm welcome — one or two sentences — that names the book *The Master and the Disciple* by its medieval author and previews this episode's specific teaching: that the school's standard creed about Allah's unity does not actually grasp the reality the words point to. Then land the spine verbatim: "Names and descriptions are of no benefit without their essential realities." Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. No "welcome back," no "today we'll discuss," no faux-profundity.

## Name discipline
- The master = the elder teacher in the dialogue. Use "the master" every time.
- The disciple = the younger questioner. Use "the disciple" every time.
- The Commander of the Faithful (peace be upon him) — the fourth caliph quoted from his opening sermon. After first mention: "the Commander of the Faithful."
- Never speak Arabic personal names. Never speak Arabic book titles; say "the book *The Peak of Eloquence*" once, thereafter "that sermon."
- Same label every time. No rotation.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- Allah: Allah
- Quran: Quran
- tawhid: monotheism

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: the disciple recites his four-line creed (Allah is one, just, commands, forbids) and thinks the conversation is over. The master refuses the shared vocabulary. State the spine verbatim again. Make the listener feel the weight: a community can chant the words and have nothing.
Beat 2 — Failed answer A: the school's analysis of the divine name. Steelman it first — the letters are the form scholarship has labored over. Then the master's pressure: which of the four letters is Allah? They must be signs, not containers.
Beat 3 — Failed answer B: the disciple's fallback — the manufacture argument. Steelman it — every made thing has a maker. Let it sound careful and honest.
Beat 4 — Pivot: the master's correction. Analogy from manufactured things forges a hidden likeness; the formula "nothing like His image" smuggles an image back under the table; the Quran says only "there is none like Him." Spine verbatim again.
Beat 5 — Non-bodily correction: everything made comes from a pre-existing substance; Allah creates from no substance. The craftsman model never applied.
Beat 6 — Stakes: the eye cannot bear the sun; the intellect cannot weigh the One. The disciple confesses: "My words have left me." Close on the listener's own question — which creed-words am I reciting while having lost the reality behind them, and what would it cost this week to stop. Spine verbatim a third time. Do not pre-announce any next chapter.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads as the master's voice; Host B (female, seeker) presses as the disciple. Host B challenges at least three times and concedes once at the close.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — if the creed is correct as words, why does saying 'Allah is one' not count?"; "That sounds like wordplay — pressing on individual letters feels like a trick."; "How is this different from any negative theology that just denies attributes?"

## Tone constraints
Analogy 1: fire and food — saying "fire" cooks nothing; the word is not the reality.
Analogy 2: the sun and the eye — the eye cannot bear even the lesser light.
Analogy 3: the craftsman and his thing — analogy from made things forges a hidden likeness.
Use only these three. No model-invented images.

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". Never pair any leadership-title with the personal name of the figure named in doctrinal guidance as the Father of Imams. R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud.
