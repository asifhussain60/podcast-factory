# Jewels Moon And The Description Of Religion

Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. Spine thesis (repeat verbatim three times per R-RECURRING-THESIS — open, pivot, close): "A religion grounded only in transmitted reports is not religion."

## Opening directive
Open warm and brief — one or two sentences. Greet the listener, name the book: *The Master and the Disciple* by Jafar ibn Mansur, and preview the teaching: the young teacher accepts wisdom-as-jewels, then names the condition that dissolves the visiting scholar's religion — real jewels are told from forged glass only by the assayers. Land the spine verbatim.

## Name discipline
Stable English labels — never rotate. Rotation: the young teacher / the young teacher / the young teacher.
- The young teacher — the chapter's protagonist.
- The visiting scholar — the opposing school's greatest debater.
- Commander of the Faithful (peace be upon him) — first mention only.
- The Messenger (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family) — first mention only.
- The trustee — successor figure; the speaker-prophet — prophet of revelation.
Never pair a leadership-title with the personal name of the Father of Imams.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- hadith: hadith
- sharia: sharia
- Ramadan: Ramadan
- Eid: Eid

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: the visiting scholar opens graciously — wisdom is like jewels sparkling in sunlight. The young teacher accepts the image, then names the breaking condition: jewels are useful only where real jewel cannot be confused with forged glass. Stakes: a community whose jewels never reached the assayers. Land the spine verbatim.
Beat 2 — Failed answer A: religion equals sharia equals the school's commands, prohibitions, traditions. State at strongest.
Beat 3 — Failed answer B: moon-sighting as method — community against community, feast against fast. The saying about sighting the moon has become a saying about fighting over it.
Beat 4 — Pivot: the young teacher recovers "follow the moon" as the Messenger's designation of the trustee — moon for trustee, sun for speaker-prophet — with the formula: in all matters of your life, follow the Commander of the Faithful. Quran's witness: chapter 2 verses 183 to 184; sun and moon on precisely calculated paths. Repeat spine.
Beat 5 — Non-bodily correction: the divorce case — a man left not knowing if he is married or divorced. The visiting scholar concedes: sharia is corrupted. The young teacher dissolves religion-equals-sharia, then renders the seven-image description whole — veil, rope from the heavens, firm handhold, ark of the divine presence, ship of salvation, light of life, protected knowledge whose jewels have signs.
Beat 6 — Landing: the visiting scholar accepts the description as the essential meaning religion would have to have, and asks for the reality of which it is the picture. Repeat spine.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) voices the young teacher and leads. Host B (female, seeker) voices the visiting scholar, challenges at least three times, and concedes once.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — reading the moon-saying as coded designation sounds like meaning read in after the fact"; "That sounds like wordplay — who decides which symbol fits which figure?"; "Isn't this just replacing one chain of reports with a smaller one?"; "How is this different from any school claiming its readers alone see the meaning?"

## Tone constraints
Walk the source in its own order; plain transliteration; no diacritics. Quranic citations use the English meaning of the surah.
Analogy 1: jewels in sunlight versus forged glass under the assayers' lamps.
Analogy 2: moon as symbol of the trustee, sun as symbol of the speaker-prophet.
Analogy 3: religion as the seven-image description — veil, rope, handhold, ark, ship, light, protected knowledge.
No other analogies. No modern tech.

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud.

## Landing
End on a question grounded in this chapter: which jewel of inherited practice has the listener been holding without ever bringing it to the assayers — and what would it cost this week to take it there? Place the third spine repetition just before this turn. Leave the question open.
