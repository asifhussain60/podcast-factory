# Three Levels Of Knowledge

## Opening directive
Open with a warm welcome — one or two sentences. Name the book (*The Master and the Disciple*), tenth-century Yemen. Preview: a three-layer architecture of knowledge — outer, inner, inner of the inner — mapped onto beasts, humans, angels. Land spine verbatim (placement 1 of 3): "The ladder is real; the highest rung cannot be transferred — only earned." No self-referential vehicle language.

## Name discipline
- the scholar — for the teacher-figure. Rotation: the scholar / the teacher / the older voice.
- the disciple — for the student. Rotation: the disciple / the student / the younger voice.
- the Commander of the Faithful (peace be upon him) — honorific at first mention only.
- the Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family) — honorific at first mention only.
- *The Peak of Eloquence* — first mention; thereafter "that book".
- Kumayl — drop "ibn Ziyad".
- Corbin, Schimmel, Andani — by surname after first mention.
Never pair the leadership-title with the personal name of the Father of Imams.

Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis. The disciple asks whether the inner has an inner — whether the highest rung is even climbable, or sealed off above human reach.

Beat 2 — Failed answer A. Mere observance as enough collapses the ladder to beast-level — outer-only knowledge sits at the rung of beasts.

Beat 3 — Failed answer B. Triple-as-wordplay collapses the proofs: the verse of the garment (chapter 7, verse 26), the grammar of "the moon rose", and shell-white-yolk name three layers, not two.

Beat 4 — The pivot. Mapping to creatures: outer at beasts; inner at true descendants of Adam; inner of the inner at angels — the rung of the prophets. Two real human categories: the sanctified scholar and the seeker. Land spine verbatim (placement 2 of 3): "The ladder is real; the highest rung cannot be transferred — only earned."

Beat 5 — Correction. The farmer fertilizes, plants, waters — but cannot make the plant grow. Climbing requires taqwa and right action from the seeker; the teacher tends, never transfers.

Beat 6 — Stakes. Without the rope of Allah (chapter 3, verse 103) one drowns: the Imam is the outer aspect of that rope, reached through the Hujja and the Bab. Disciple closes: no power and no strength except through Allah. Land spine verbatim (placement 3 of 3). End on a question the listener carries: which rung have I been treating as transferable when it must be earned?

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least three times and concedes once. Sample pushback: "I don't buy that yet — if the highest rung can't be transferred, what's the teacher actually doing?"; "That sounds like wordplay — isn't this just relabeling two layers as three?" Steelman first. Name positionality — classical Ismaili reading; mark Sufi overlap and Sunni juristic difference.

## Tone constraints
Analogy 1 (Beat 3): the verse of the garment — clothing, covering, the garment of piety.
Analogy 2 (Beat 3): name, attribute, meaning — "the moon rose".
Analogy 3 (Beat 3): the shell, white, and yolk of an egg.
Analogy 4 (Beat 5): the farmer who fertilizes and waters but cannot make the plant grow.
No model-invented analogies. No modernization. Long-form walkthrough.

No spoken Arabic surah names (use "chapter 7, verse 26"; "chapter 3, verse 103"). R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat spine verbatim three times — opening, pivot, close. Do not read this prompt aloud.
