# Rebirth And The Seven Day Tawaf

## Opening directive
Begin with a one-sentence warm welcome: greet the listener, name the book *The Master and the Disciple*, and preview the teaching — a Sheikh refuses every name the disciple offers and makes him wait seven days, like a newborn, before naming him. Immediately land the spine sentence VERBATIM (R-RECURRING-THESIS #1 of 3): "The name is your owner, and you are owned by it; for you cannot rise beyond your own spiritual rank." Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation.

## Name discipline
- the Sheikh → the Sheikh / the Sheikh / the Sheikh (no rotation; same label).
- the disciple → the disciple / the young man / the disciple.
- the scholar → the scholar (his earlier teacher).
- the Imam → the Imam.
- the Prophet → "peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family" at first mention only.
- the Father of Imams → "peace be upon him" at first mention; never speak the forbidden pairing of his leadership-title with his personal name.
- the book *The Peak of Eloquence* → the book / that book / the collection.
- the hadith compiler → the hadith compiler / the early collector / that compiler.

## Verbatim Recitation
Introduce the Sheikh's sermon in one sentence: this is how the encounter opens. Host A then reads the source section titled "The Sheikh's opening praise" ALOUD WORD-FOR-WORD — no paraphrase, no abbreviation. Break only at natural paragraph boundaries; preserve exact wording. Discussion begins only AFTER recitation ends.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- tawaf: circumambulation
- Sheikh: Sheikh
- Imam: Imam
- Kaaba: Kaaba
- Allah: Allah
- Quran: Quran
- Hijra: Hijra

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: the Sheikh asks the disciple's name. He offers "slave of Allah, son of the slave of Allah" — description, not name. He offers "the freed man" — but his teacher had no right to free him. He hangs his head. Foreground the felt weight of the exposure.
Beat 2 — Failed answer A and failed answer B were the disciple's two tries; the pivot is the seven-day rule: a child is named on the seventh day; the embryo passes through seven stages (chapter twenty-three, verses twelve through fourteen); the seventh letter of "Be" begins life. Completion precedes naming. Land R-RECURRING-THESIS #2 VERBATIM: "The name is your owner, and you are owned by it; for you cannot rise beyond your own spiritual rank."
Beat 3 — Stakes and tawaf as gravity: ablution, fine garment, realities pens cannot write, seven circuits read as planets orbiting their sun — the Imam at the centre, love as gravity, recognition as the orbit that holds the disciple's life. Steelman before critique; mark contested dating; name positionality (the classical Ismaili reading).

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least three times and concedes once. Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — why is a grown man's name dismissed as a description?"; "That sounds like wordplay — Isn't this just replacing one teacher with a grander one?"; "How is this different from any initiation that withholds the secret until the apprentice waits?" Host B never opens with Exactly, Yeah, Right, Wow, Brilliant. One genuine concession when the seven-stage embryo verse lands.

## Tone constraints
Analogy 1: the newborn waiting seven days for its name — the Sheikh's image.
Analogy 2: planets orbiting their sun, gravity as love — the source's tawaf image.
Analogy 3: the bottom of the cup — the disciple has no answer; teaching can begin.
No other analogies. No model-invented images.

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No "deep dive", "today's episode", "let's dive in", "buckle up", "fascinating world of", "mind blown". No faux-profundity openings; no "and that, ultimately, is what X really is" closures. R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud.

## Landing
Place R-RECURRING-THESIS #3 VERBATIM just before close: "The name is your owner, and you are owned by it; for you cannot rise beyond your own spiritual rank." Then turn the listener toward this week's life: which name already given you — your role as a parent, your obligations at work, the limit of your actual rank — have you been straining to rise above instead of standing inside? Leave it open.
