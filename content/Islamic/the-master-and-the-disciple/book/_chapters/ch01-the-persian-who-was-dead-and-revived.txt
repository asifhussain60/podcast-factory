# The Persian Who Was Dead and Revived

As for the doctrines of the righteous and the manners of those who seek, it has reached us that there was a man of the people of Persia upon whom the decree had run in an affliction of ignorance. He was not empty of gifts. He had a heart of keen retention, a sound and lofty intellect, and a cultivation of manners he had earned for himself. Yet ignorance had prevailed over him in the very knowledge we have been describing, for it had come to him early and he had grown up within it. So he turned about in the heat of thirst, reckoning the glittering of the mirage to be the whiteness of water, until, when at last he reached it, he found it to be nothing at all, and he found Allah there before him, and his account was settled. Then his Lord honored his resting place and lifted from him the covering that lay over him. He found him astray and guided him, found him in want and enriched him with knowledge, so that by that favor he became one of the scholars of Tur and one of the dwellers of the Bayt al-Ma'mur.

When his guidance was made complete and he had reached the utmost of his hope, thanks became due from him to the One who had made him, and it fell to him to labor toward his Lord. Now he had heard from his father a word that lived in him as the soul lives in the body:

> "The best of good deeds is the reviving of the dead."

So he said within himself: I was dead and He revived me, ignorant and He taught me. I was not the first to be ignorant, that I should be the one who outran all people to knowledge, nor am I the last who will be ignorant, that teaching should come to its end with me. It is a truth binding upon me that I be among those who give thanks for this blessing, delivering this trust to those who come after me just as it was delivered to me by those who came before. For this affair is first of all from Allah, and it reached me only through many intermediaries, the first handing it to the second and the second to the third, until, having descended from the highest assembly to the creatures who are the people of this world, it tore through the veil and the doors gave way before it one after another until it came at last to me.

Am I then its end and its final destination? Never. Those who carried it and kept faith with it have the prior claim upon its gains and its merits. And if their gains are not mine, so that I might lean upon them, then rather I am the one who has been gained for them by their labor. And if their bounty does not excuse me from labor of my own, then I remain as poor before the seeking of knowledge through action as I once was poor before the seeking of knowledge itself. This he said within himself, and he knew that what was owed from him was the very likeness of what had been owed to him, and that the last of his affair, in the reckoning, was as its first.

So he went out from his family and his wealth toward his Lord, answering His call and praying for the good, summoning others to the same obligation, that there might fall due to him of thanksgiving the like of what had fallen due from him. He set out journeying through the lands, passing among the peoples of the non Arabs and the tribes of the Arabs, searching faces and casting his questions before them. Yet he found none to answer and met no fellow seeker, until he came to the farthest reach of the island. There he entered a city of it at a moment when its people were heedless. And as he went recalling the signs of his Lord and examining the marks of them, his eye fell upon a company of the people of that land quarreling over religion without guidance and plunging into the depths of their own caprice. So he sat down a little apart from them, keeping himself aside, listening to their speech and reading their faces to see which among them held to the soundest way.
