# The Persian Who Was Dead and Revived

As for the doctrines of the righteous and the manners of the seekers, it has reached us that a man from the people of Persia was struck by the calamity of ignorance, though he possessed a heart of keen retention, a sound intellect, and a culture he had acquired for himself. Yet ignorance prevailed over him with regard to the knowledge already spoken of, for it had come upon him early and he had grown up within it. So he wavered in the heat of thirst, reckoning the glittering of the mirage to be the whiteness of water, until, when he came to it, he found nothing of it there, and he found Allah waiting for him, and his reckoning was settled. Then Allah honored his resting-place and uncovered him from that which had covered him. He found him straying and guided him, and found him destitute and enriched him with knowledge. By that grace bestowed upon him he became one of the scholars of the Mount (al-Tur) and one of the dwellers of the Frequented House (al-Bayt al-Ma'mur).

When his guidance was completed and he had reached the utmost of his hope, it became incumbent upon him to give thanks to his Maker and to toil his way toward his Lord. He had heard from his father a saying that was to him as the soul is to the body:

"The best of good deeds is the reviving of the dead."

So he said within himself: "I was dead and He revived me, ignorant and He taught me. I was not the first to be ignorant, that I should be the one to precede all people to knowledge, nor am I the last to be ignorant, that teaching should come to an end with me. It is a true obligation upon me that I be one who gives thanks for this blessing, discharging this trust to those who come after me, just as it was discharged to me by those who came before me. For this matter has its beginning with Allah, and it reached me only through many intermediary causes, the first conveying it to the second, and the second to the third, until it descended from the highest assembly to the creatures who are the people of this world. Then the veil was torn and the doors were forced open, one after another, until it came at last to me. Am I, then, its endpoint and its final limit? No. Those who carried it and kept faith with the trust in it have the prior claim to the gains and the merits. And if their gains are not mine to rely upon, then I am only the one who was gained for them through their labor. Their merit does not excuse me from labor of my own; I remain as poor in my need to seek knowledge through action as I once was poor in my need to seek knowledge at all." He said this within himself, and he knew that what was owed by him was the very likeness of what had been owed to him, and that the last of his affair, in the reckoning, was as its first.

So he departed from his family and his wealth toward his Lord, answering His call and praying for the good, calling others to the obligation, that there might become due to him the same thanksgiving that had become due from him. He set to journeying through the lands, passing among the peoples of the non-Arabs and the tribes of the Arabs, searching faces and putting questions, yet finding none to answer and meeting no fellow seeker, until he came to the farthest reaches of the island (al-Jazira). He entered a city of it at a moment when its people were heedless. And as he was recalling the signs and examining the marks, he caught sight of a company of the people of that land quarreling over religion without guidance and plunging into the depths of their own caprice. So he sat down a little apart, in seclusion from them, listening to their words and studying them to see which of them held to the soundest way.
