# A Stranger in the City

He sat there a while, weighing them one against another, until he said within himself: these are the foremost in three qualities for a living debate. The first is that they carry an argument through to its end. The second is that they are of the people of the Umma. And the third is that they are nearer to right awareness, for they observe religion together, and search for it together, and the one who seeks a thing is on the verge of meeting it.

When they had finished the speech they were engaged in, they turned to him and said, "Who are you, and from where, boy?"

He said, "I am a servant of Allah, and one of the dwellers in His sanctuary."

They said, "What is your need, and what is your work?"

He said, "My need has passed me by, and I am at work in seeking it."

They said, "Did anything of what we said please you?"

He said, "All of it is a marvel, to one who marvels."

They said, "But wonder is of two senses, the praiseworthy and the blameworthy."

He said, "Yes, I have learned that. And so too is speech, praiseworthy and blameworthy."

They said, "Then what, in your view, is the praiseworthy kind?"

He said, "That which is correct."

They said, "And what is the correct?"

He said, "That whose origin was from Allah, and which calls to Allah."

They said, "And what is the blameworthy?"

He said, "That whose origin was caprice, and which calls to something other than guidance."

They said, "You have spoken truly. Have you nothing of your own words for us to hear?"

He said, "I have nothing to say. I am only a follower of the right saying."

They said, "Then let us hear from your words what may show us your way, for your condition has grown dear to us and your brotherhood has become sweet to us, and we hope that you may be a door of mercy that Allah has opened to us."

He said, "Yes." So he composed himself for speech, and the people fell silent to listen, and he turned his face toward them.

Then he said: "He who created the creation by His power has made each thing equal to what He has decreed for it. He is the One who has brought forth the highways of the pasture, and made its outward and its inward free of caprice, who is not ambiguous, and is not to be reached by the study of mere traces. Though imaginings are bewildered by the demand of Him, and minds fall short of attaining Him, yet He does not appear in being so as to be possessed by His attributes, or so that the intention of His path might be altered. Rather He is generous. His knowledge is hidden by its very grace, and His secrets are guarded by their own proper people. Its honor is the plumage of guidance, and it is completed by the garment of piety, marked with the light of lights, a deposit in the breasts of the righteous, the keepers of the Word, the honor of the scribes, a trust which they discharge and a limit which they do not exceed. It is the rule of the Judge and the bounties of the Most Merciful. He honored it and set it within, He protected it and concealed it, He gave it the sufficiency of its own rights and crowned it with His covenants. So the resolves of the thinkers do not reach it, and the schemes of the schemers do not arrive at it. Its outward is a remembrance and an evidence for those who are guided, and its inward is a sure drink, whose sky is brought low to the beholders and whose land is paved for the seekers, and whose falling stars are stations for the students. It has watering places for those who journey to Him, and signs whose wonders follow one after another, pointing by their likenesses to Him. Its course is a preferment, and its method a laying-out in detail. Its rulings are obligations and its practice is law. Truthfulness has ennobled its doctrines, and justice has refined its gains, so that He made it a leader and a way to every good, and made it a shield and a guide to its own inner chamber. Then He compelled minds, by ignorance and by want, to seek knowledge and capacity.

"Blessed is He who made the night and the day a succession for whoever would be reminded or would give thanks. And may Allah bless the one He chose from among His servants and made a warner to the worlds."

Then he said, "Fear Allah with the fear of one who dreads Him, and fear Him with the fear of one who hopes in Him. Seek knowledge, and you will come to know. Work, and you will be shown mercy."

Then his eyes overflowed with tears, and he broke off his speech, and took his leave of the people weeping, and rose to go back to his lodging, turning away. Weeping came over the company at the warmth of his words and the reach of his admonition, and they meant to rise and go with him. But the keeping of good manners held them back from thronging after him all together, for they did not know whether that would suit him, and so they returned to their dwellings. Yet one of the company followed after him, a youth who was the youngest of them in years and the most forbearing of them in mind, whose reason discipline had perfected and whose heart thought had kindled, and upon whom there now pressed all that he had heard.
