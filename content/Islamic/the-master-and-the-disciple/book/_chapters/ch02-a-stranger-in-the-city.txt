# A Stranger in the City

As he watched them, he said within himself: these are the foremost among men, for three qualities show in their very disputing. The first is that they carry an argument through to its end. The second is that they are of the people of the Umma. And the third is that they stand nearer to right guidance, for they are awake to religion, observing it together and searching after it together; and the one who seeks a thing is on the verge of finding it.

When they had finished the speech they were engaged in, they turned toward him and asked, "Who are you, and from where, young man?"

He answered, "I am a servant of Allah, and one of the dwellers of His sanctuary."

They said, "What is your need, and what is your work?"

He said, "My need has passed me by, and I am at work in seeking it."

They said, "Did anything in what we said please you?"

He said, "All of it is strange, to one who marvels."

They said, "Wonder is of two senses, one praiseworthy and one blameworthy."

He said, "Yes, I have learned that. And speech likewise is praiseworthy and blameworthy."

They said, "Which is the praiseworthy kind, in your view?"

He said, "That which is correct."

They said, "And what is the correct?"

He said, "That whose origin is from Allah, and which calls to Allah."

They said, "And what is the blameworthy?"

He said, "That whose origin is caprice, and which calls to something other than guidance."

They said, "You speak truly. Have you nothing of your own for us to hear?"

He said, "I have nothing to say of myself; I am only a follower of the true saying."

They said, "Then let us hear from your words what will show us your way. Your state has been sweet to us and your brotherhood pleasant to us, and we hope that you may be a door of mercy that Allah has opened for us."

He said, "Yes." Then he composed himself to speak, and the company fell silent before him, and he turned his face toward them.

Then he said: "He who created the creation by His power has set each thing in due measure to what He decreed for it. He it is who brought forth the open highways of the pasture, and made their outward and their inward free of caprice, a way neither made ambiguous nor a track worn away to nothing. Were it otherwise, imaginings would be lost in the demand for Him and minds would fail to attain Him. Yet He does not stand forth in visible being, that His attributes might be possessed by another, or that the aim of His path might be altered. Rather He is generous and hidden: His knowledge is concealed by its very grace, and His secrets are guarded for their rightful people. Its honor is the plumage of guidance, and its most perfect adornment is the garment of piety, marked with the light of lights, laid up as a trust in the breasts of the chosen and the righteous, the keepers of the Word, the noble scribes,

a trust which they discharge and a bound which they do not overstep. It is the law of the Judge and the bounties of the Most Merciful. He ennobled it and set it within, He guarded it and veiled it, He gave it its due and crowned it with His covenants. So the resolves of the thinkers do not reach it, and the schemes of the schemers do not attain it. Its outward is a remembrance and a proof for the one who is guided; its inward is a clear-flowing drink, whose heaven is brought near to those who look upon it and whose earth is made level for those who search, with marked stations for those who seek. It has watering-places for those who journey toward Him, and signs whose wonders follow one upon another, pointing by their likenesses to Him. Its course is a setting-forth of excellence, its method a laying-out in detail; its rulings are obligations and its practice ordained ways. Truthfulness has ennobled its schools and justice has made wholesome its gains, so that for every good it is a leader and a road, and to its inner sanctum a shield and a guide. Then minds were driven, by ignorance and by want, to seek knowledge and sufficiency.

> فَتَبَارَكَ الَّذِي جَعَلَ اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ خِلْفَةً لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يَذَّكَّرَ أَوْ أَرَادَ شُكُورًا، وَصَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَى مَنِ اخْتَارَهُ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ وَجَعَلَهُ لِلْعَالَمِينَ نَذِيرًا.

"Blessed, then, is He who has made the night and the day a succession for whoever would take heed or would give thanks; and may Allah bless the one He chose from among His servants and made a warner to the worlds."

Then he said: "Have fear of Allah with the fear of one who dreads Him, and dread Him with the dread of one who hopes in Him. Seek knowledge, and you will come to know; and work, and you will be shown mercy."
