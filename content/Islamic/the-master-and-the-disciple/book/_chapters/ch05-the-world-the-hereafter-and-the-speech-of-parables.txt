# The World, the Hereafter, and the Speech of Parables

The boy turned then to a question that had long pressed on him, and said: This world, in which so vast a creation was set, holds a multitude of people, the first of them and the last, and they differ about it. Some have given themselves over to the world and pursued it, and have seen no merit except in gathering more of it. Others reproach it and practice restraint within it, yet do not dispense with it altogether, even though everything of which the abundance is forbidden has its little forbidden too. I have heard from you no mention of it and no report. What do you say of it?

The Master said: As for this world, no one has ever truly reproached it, neither the learned nor the ignorant. The ignorant man knows its worth through his very ignorance of what lies beyond it, and the learned man knows its worth through the worth of its meaning. No one reproaches this world except an ignorant man. How can it be permitted to the creation to reproach the act of the Creator? But this is my word concerning it: the world is set up, for one who has eyes to see, upon seven things that branch from seven origins, which are heaven, earth, darkness, light, vessels, blessing, and hardship. These are seven qualities, and from each of the seven there branch seven more:

(a) The heavens are seven.

(b) The earths are seven.

(c) The light has seven days that turn upon seven nights. Saturday is for repose and rest; Sunday is for the bounds and the year; Monday is for the doublings, in the great meaning; Tuesday has three houses, an apparent, an inward, and an inward of the inward; Wednesday is for the four sacred things; Thursday is for the fifth of the causes of God; and Friday is the greatest law of God.

(d) The darkness is seven nights, a shelter set over the seven days, for each day its night.

(e) The vessels are seven: camels, horses, mules, donkeys, elephants, oxen, and ships. By these seven the empty tracts of the earth are crossed. Camels are for the sands, horses for the plains, mules for the mountains, donkeys for near destinations, elephants for the wastes and the desolate places, oxen for the salt-flats and the thickets, and ships for the seas. Seven mounts for seven domains.

(f) The blessings of this world rest upon seven: air, water, light, darkness, food, clothing, and marriage. The air is the open space between heaven and earth, in which the winds arise and the breaths pass back and forth, and it is the life of the living thing. Water is the life of the animal. Light is the substance of sight; darkness is the increase of what grows; food is the sustenance of bodies; clothing is a covering that hides what should be hidden; and marriage is the seed of reproduction and the cause of offspring. Some of these are keys of the good, and some are mines of blessings. Which of them is blameworthy, when all of them are from the hands of God and His blessings, for which His servants are obliged to give thanks?

(g) Their opposites in hardship are also seven: heat, cold, hunger, thirst, fear, sickness, and death. These are seven that must be borne with patience, once their meaning is known.

The boy said: These are the attributes of the world, and I do not doubt that anything in it is other than the making and composing of the Creator. So what do the learned mean when they censure the world, and what do the ignorant mean when they praise it? I see that the ignorant have struck upon the meaning, even in their ignorance; and if that is so, then the rightness of their case would oblige the learned to be in error, since the learned contradict the people of ignorance in every sense.

The Master said: The learned did not err in censuring this world, but the creation does not know what they mean by it. And the ignorant did not grasp any rightness in what they praised of the world, for they were ignorant of its meaning; and whoever is ignorant of a thing treats it as an enemy.

The boy said: Then make plain to me the truth of what the learned say, so that their truthfulness may be established for me. And set apart the speech of the people of ignorance by what shows their falsehood, since the outward appearance points the other way. My chest has grown narrow from ignorance of this, and I hope for no relief except through its knowledge.

The Master said: This world, and all that I have described to you of it, is an outward for an inward. It has no subsistence except by that inward, for the inward stands to it as the soul stands to the body. Whoever knows the soul, the body is made wholesome for him; and whoever does not know the soul and acknowledges only the body, the body is to him a carcass, and the carcass is forbidden. From here the learned forbade the world, because it is dead. They forbade the dead thing that God has forbidden,

> من حيث يشاء الله. وبان كذب أهل الجهل بمدح الدنيا إذ لم يعرفوا باطنها وما أراد الله بها. وظنوا أن الله خلقها لغير معناها، والله لم يخلق الأشياء عبثاً. ولو كانت الدنيا وما فيها لم يخلق إلا للدنيا، ينشئ فيها خلقاً

until it is taken from the way God wills. And the falsehood of the people of ignorance became plain in their praise of the world, for they did not know its inner meaning nor what God intended by it. They supposed that God created it for some meaning other than its own, yet God did not create things in vain. Had the world and all that is in it been created only for the world, so that He raised up in it a creation that fades away, it would have no consequence and no meaning. Every act without consequence is play, and every speech without meaning is falsehood, and God is far more exalted than that.

The boy said: The truthfulness of the learned is now established, and their excellence is proven, and the people of ignorance are set apart and their falsehood is plain in what has appeared of the argument. There remains for us the knowledge of the inward of the world and what is in it. I see that the narrowness I complained of to you has had its narrowness lifted.

The Master said: What is the reward of one who brings you out of the narrowness of want into the breadth of ease?

He said: Thanks and praise.

The Master said: And what is the reward of one who brings you out of the narrowness of ignorance into the breadth of knowledge?

He said: Obedience and works.

The Master said: This world and all its likes are an outward for the hereafter and what is in it; the hereafter is its soul and its life. Whoever labors in the world for the world, without knowledge of the hereafter, his labor is misguidance, for it has no meaning and no consequence. But whoever labors in the world for the hereafter, believing in it, his labor has meaning and consequence, and his labor is thanked.

The boy said: You have spoken truly. Now make plain to me the features of the world and what is in it, and set the parables against the things they portray from their inward side.

The Master said: God, blessed and exalted, has not created a single thing of the things of the world, no beast upon the earth, no bird that flies with its wings, nothing moist and nothing dry, no fixed and lifeless thing such as the mountains, the stones, the trees, the minerals of gold and silver and gem,

> ، وجميع الأشياء ما كبر منها وما صغر، إلا وهو مثل مضروب. ألا ترى الذرّة على صغرها ولطف معناها لها حدود، أمامها وخلفها ويمينها وشمالها وفوقها وتحتها؟ فذلك سئَّة حدود وهي الحدّ السابع. فكذلك جميع ما خلق الله من الأشياء

Nothing at all, great or small, except that it is a parable struck. Do you not see the atom, for all its smallness and the subtlety of its meaning, that it has limits: before it and behind it, to its right and to its left, above it and below it? Those are six limits, and the seventh is the atom itself. So it is with everything God has created, from the ant and the gnat up to what is above them of the heaven and the earth, and what is between them of things great and small. This is the parable: each of them has six limits, and the seventh of them is the proof of it, pointing to the six origins from which things were created. And the air that surrounds them is the seventh, encompassing them all and holding them. If your chest is wide enough for such questions and for looking into the parables, and for setting each thing against its form among the hidden pairs, then ask about what has occurred to you.

The boy said: What is the likeness of the heaven, in its height above things and the breadth of its regions, so that it has gathered in all things?

The Master said: Its likeness is the likeness of the Speaking Imam (al-Imām al-Nāṭiq), who is raised above all creation by his excellence and the loftiness of his ranks, and who has gathered in all the rulings, the rulings of the Shari'a, by the breadth of his knowledge.

The boy said: What is the likeness of the zodiac and the rest of its stars?

The Master said: They are the likeness of the twelve chiefs (nuqabā'), the chiefs of the Speaking Imam; and their stars are his summoners (du'āt), who call to the good by the leave of their Lord.

The boy said: What is the likeness of the earth and its breadth, since it was made a resting-place and a bed for this creation?

The Master said: It is the likeness of the gate (bāb) of the Speaking Imam in his lifetime, and of his executor (waṣī) after his death. He is a place of return for the people and a resting-place for whoever seeks guidance, being a knower of the parables and of the inner interpretation (ta'wīl).

The boy said: What is the likeness of the twelve islands in the earth?

The Master said: The likenesses of the twelve proofs (ḥujaj), the proofs of the executors.

The boy said: What is the likeness of its rivers and its springs?

The Master said: The likenesses of the summoners to the good (du'āt).

The boy said: What is the likeness of the seven kingdoms of the heavens and their orbits?

The Master said: Those are causes between God and the Speakers (nāṭiq), and they belong to Him. These are the seven and the two orbits, and these are the greatest causes of God.

The boy said: What is the likeness of the seven seas?

The Master said: These are causes between God and the imams of the Speaker, below those first causes.

The boy said: What is the likeness of the salt water and the fresh water, and the difference between them, upon the earth?

The Master said: The salt water is the likeness of the knowledge of the outward, in which there is no summons (da'wa) and no summoners, just as the salt water yields no sowing and nothing else. The fresh water is the likeness of the knowledge of the inward, whether the springs within the earth or the water that runs upon its surface. So too is knowledge: the knowledge of the inward is of two kinds, an outward that the summoners carry along, and an inward that is kept concealed.

The boy said: How did water come to be a likeness of knowledge?

The Master said: Because water is the life of every living thing, and likewise knowledge is the life of every knower.

The boy said: What is the likeness of the air that surrounds all things?

The Master said: The air is too great to be made a likeness of any thing portrayed.

The boy said: Is it greater, or are the heaven and the earth as great as it, that they should be likenesses?

The Master said: Glory be to God, it is greater than both of them. The breadth of the earth within the atmosphere is as a ring set in an open plain; the breadth of the atmosphere within the sky is as a ring set in an open plain; and the breadth of the sky and its regions within the breadth of the air is as an egg set in an open plain. What thing portrayed could grasp this, so as to be a proof of it and a likeness for it?

The boy said: What, then, is its meaning?

The Master said: It is a proof of the One to whom nothing is like, glorified and exalted above what they associate with Him.

The boy said: But did the Creator not say,

> فلا تضربُوا للهِ الأمثالَ

"Do not strike parables for God"?

The Master said: And did He not also say,

> وَللهِ المَثْلُ الأعْلَى

"And to God belongs the highest likeness"?

The boy said: Glory be to Him who set riddles in His Book so that it might be entered by its door, and you are one of its doors. Make plain to us, then, how this is.

The Master said: Everything has a meaning, and the meaning of the parables is this: the outward is an indication of the inward. He, blessed and exalted, forbade that He be measured against anything of His creation, so that one should say,

> هذا مثل الإمام

"This is like the Imam," or

> هذا مثل الحجة

"This is like the proof," or

> هذا مثل الداعي

"This is like the summoner." For their likenesses became indications pointing to them and to the differences of rank among them. It is not permitted, in the matter of His greatness, that the Creator be measured against anything of His creation by word or by deed, so that one should say, "This is like God." Rather the parables are taken according to their meanings, for their meanings carry indications: the small of them points to the great, and the great points to the higher meanings, and the higher points to the Highest of the high. Therefore He said,

> وَللهِ المَثَلُ الأعْلَى

"And to God belongs the highest likeness," meaning the highest indication, for there is no indication of anything higher than the indication of God. And that is the air, highest above the heaven and the earth, encompassing all things while nothing of them encompasses it. It is the highest likeness, and it is the indication of the One to whom nothing is like. He did not say that there is nothing like Him, for indeed there is nothing like Him, but He meant that no indication of Him is like the indications, that to Him belongs the highest likeness, and that no indication among the indications of others, from those of His creation whom we have described, is like the indication of Him. Blessed be God, Lord of the Mighty Throne.

The boy said: The meaning has grown great and the reach has grown wide. Was there, before the air, a thing standing between it and God, given the greatness of what you have described in it?

The Master said: Yes.

The boy said: What is it?

The Master said: It is the time.

The boy said: And what is the time?

The Master said: It is the time of the Will by which He created what He willed and commanded what He wished.

The boy said: You have brought me into the deeps of the seas, down from the highest assembly. Return me now to the sciences of the world and its parables, that I may seek help by them in my affair and be strengthened by them to bear what I am meant to bear. I have grown afraid for myself and anxious for my mind, lest I be led into what I have not the strength to fathom.

The Master said: You rose high by your question, and the answer rose high with you, to the highest of the outward meanings, and there your thought lost its way and your mind was confounded. How would it be, then, if I disclosed its inward to you? You and I would be as Moses and the knowing one.

The boy said: And does this inward have an inward, more inward than itself?

The Master said: By my life, the inward has an inward that is higher in station, wider in power, and more perfect in guidance; it is the utmost of the indications toward the path of deliverance.

The boy said: I see here three layers of knowledge: an outward, an inward, and an inward of the inward. Is there a proof of this?

The Master said: And where would the proof be, if not for this and for what it points to?

The boy said: Then show me a likeness of these three layers.

The Master said: I will show you from the Book of God Almighty, and from the proof of the intellect, and from what the general body of the parables points to.

The boy said: I did not think there was, beyond what I had reached, any doctrine at all. Truly the command of God is greater than the imaginings of the pretentious can rise to.

The Master said: As for the Book of God Almighty, it is His saying,

> يَا بَنِي آدَمَ قَدْ أَنْزَلْنَا عَلَيْكُمْ لِبَاساً يُوَارِي سَوْآتِكِمْ وَرَيْشًا وَلِبَاسُ التَّقْوَى ذَلِكَ خَيرٌ

"O children of Adam, We have sent down upon you a garment that hides your nakedness, and feathers for adornment; and the garment of piety, that is better." These are three: an outward, an inward, and the inward of the inward.

The boy said: You have spoken truly.

The Master said: As for the proof of the intellect, a thing is not known except by a name, or an attribute, or a meaning. The meaning is the very essence of the thing; the name is what it is called; and the attribute is an indication of it. So too is the religion of God: it has an outward, which is a name, and an inward, which is an indication of it, and it stands only by the two together. Nothing indicates a thing except a name or an attribute. Consider the saying of one who says "طلع القمر," "the moon has risen." The meaning is the disk; "القمر," "the moon," is its name; and "طلع," "has risen," is its attribute. Were he to say "قرص," "a disk," and then fall silent, it would carry no meaning. And were he to say "قمر," "a moon," and then fall silent, it would carry no meaning, until he joins the name to the attribute and says "طلع القمر," "the moon has risen," and then the meaning is made clear and the speech is sound. In the same way, if you say "ظاهر," "an outward," it calls for an answer, that one should say, "an outward of what?" And if you say "باطن," "an inward," it calls for an answer, that one should say, "an inward of what?" But if you say "ظاهر الدين وباطنه," "the outward of the religion and its inward," the speech is understood, and it becomes a religion having an outward and an inward, just as a thing has a name and an attribute. So He said,

> وَذَرُوْا ظَاهِرَ الإِثْمِ وَبَاطِنْهُ

"And forsake the outward of sin and its inward." So sin became a thing having an outward and an inward. Such too is the greater religion of God, of which it is said that the laws are its outward and the inward is its inward, and it subsists by itself. And for the knowing of its meaning there are many parables that point to it, such as the guarded egg, which has an outward that shelters it, an inward which is the white within it, and an inward of its inward which is the yellow yolk, and that is its essence and its meaning.

The boy said: Are not the world and the hereafter a pair, the soul and the body a pair, the knowledge of the outward and the inward a pair, and the like of these, as you have described, many pairs, all of them?

The Master said: You have spoken truly. But whose pairs are they, likenesses of what?

The boy said: Pairs that point to the religion of God.

The Master said: You have spoken truly. The pair is an outward that is a name and an inward that is an attribute, pointing to the knowledge of God and to the religion of God, and that is the inward of the inward. Likewise the creation was created upon three stations: the first of them the creation of the angels, the second the creation of the children of Adam, and the third the creation of the beasts. The outward of knowledge is the limit of the beasts, and whoever knows it with no inward is at the station of the beasts. The knowledge of the inward is the knowledge of the children of Adam and their limit, and whoever knows it is a believer and is at the station of the children of Adam. And the knowledge of the inward of the inward is the knowledge of the angels, and whoever knows it is spiritual in his knowledge though bodily in his frame, and is a prophet sent, whom God sets as His deputy in His earth and by whom He establishes His argument over His creation. He is the veil of the angels; he is the cipher of the revelation and its interpreter to the children of Adam. With him are the keys of the gardens, which none enters but the one who obeys him, and with him is the passage from the Fire, which none enters but the one who disobeys him. And the people are only two men: a knower, godly and firm in knowledge,
