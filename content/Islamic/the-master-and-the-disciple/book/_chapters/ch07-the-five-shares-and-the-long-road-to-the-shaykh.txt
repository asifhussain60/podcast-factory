# The Five Shares and the Long Road to the Shaykh

Praise be to God, said the boy, who has guided us to His religion, for we could not have been guided except by His favor. Then tell me about the beginning of this creation and its composition. Is it a single creation or a differing one? For I see so much difference in opinions and in passions.

The scholar said: As for the creation of bodies, it is different, as you see in the variety of tongues and colors. That is a proof of His power, made clear by the signs, so that the created may know one another. The child knows his father and the father knows his son. The man knows his wife and no other, and the woman knows her husband and no other. So God created them. But as for the creation of the instrument, that is, the senses, what is apparent of them and what is hidden, it is a single creation, and what is apparent will tell you of what is hidden. Have you ever seen a scholar with three eyes, so that the increase might be plain in him, or an ignorant man with a single eye, so that the deficiency might be plain in him, such that one might say the scholar saw by way of increase and the ignorant man did not see by way of decrease? Or have you seen any increase in the outward instrument of one man over another? You see their creation as nothing but a single, level creation in the apparent instrument, and the meaning of the composition is one. So we have come to know that the inner senses follow this same pattern. It is justice. God would not be just in what is apparent and unjust in what is hidden. He did not make for the scholar two hearts in one cavity, nor did He deprive the ignorant man of anything. The instrument is one, and the composition belongs to the Gatherer. By this God has argued against them, by the instrument He made for them which they did not use. The Almighty said:

> لَهُمْ قُلُوبٌ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ بِهَا وَلَهُمْ أَعْيُنٌ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ بِهَا وَلَهُمْ آذَانٌ لَا يَسْمَعُونَ بِهَا

They have hearts wherewith they do not understand, and they have eyes wherewith they do not see, and they have ears wherewith they do not hear.

Do you suppose that God, in His bounty and His generosity, charged them with what they are unable to bear and to carry? No. He is fairer than that.

The boy said: I do not see it otherwise, and I do not say it. But I ask you about the difference of passions. From where did it come to them, when the instrument is whole and the origin in the composition is one?

The scholar said: As for the origin, it is one. All of them are from Adam, and Adam is from dust, created by his Lord, who breathed into him of His Spirit. But they deviated from the Sunna in marriage. They were born upon a basis other than the religion and grew up in other than guidance. And I do not doubt that among them are the children of fornication who call upon fathers not their own. When the origins were corrupted, the branches were corrupted and grew strange, and their return to the truth became hard, together with the arrogance that entered them against obedience, the laziness against worship, and the envy of the friends of God, so that they did not hold themselves within the lawful, back from the forbidden.

The boy said: If the origin is one and the instrument is one, from where did the passions differ, when their meaning in seeking is one, for everyone seeks the good for himself?

The scholar said: By my life, their meaning in seeking is one, but they differ in striking the mark, like archers aiming at a target. They are agreed in shooting at it with resolve and with the instrument, yet with all that they differ in hitting it. So too the hearts within the breasts are gathered upon the minds, and the minds are gathered upon the safeguarding of the bodies and their dignity. When the call of truth appeared, they differed. Some of them held that answering the Messengers and humbling themselves before them was a part of their own dignity and a guarding of themselves from folly. Others held that denying the Messengers and lifting themselves above their obedience was a part of their own dignity and a guarding of themselves from the narrowness of worship. The origin was one, the seeking of ease for the bodies and of dignity for the souls. So one people chose what the Messengers promised them of the reward of God, even if in this world it was rare and hard to reach, for the sake of its permanence. And the others chose from the bliss of this world, even if it was perishing, for the sake of its presence, the ease of seeking it, and the swiftness of its reward. The hearts by which the first sought the bliss of the Hereafter were the same by which the others sought the bliss of this world. Each looks to himself, and each supposes himself a sincere adviser to himself. So they came together in the seeking and differed in the striking. That is from the justice of God, His generosity, and the extending of His gift. To every seeker is given from Him what he sought. Whoever desires the reward of this world, We give him of it, and whoever desires the reward of the Hereafter, We give him of it.

The boy said: The people of this world are in classes within their world. Some are rich, above labor, kings by the abundance of their wealth. Others are poor, in need of wealth, and they serve the rich to gain from them. Then the bliss of this world gathers them upon one level, so that they are all alike in the evil of the outcome. Or is there for some of them a station apart from the rest?

The scholar said: It is from the justice of God that He joins them one to another and does not part them from their companions and the people of their affection. They are equal in His punishment, as they were gathered together in His disobedience.

The boy said: What of this religion which God has honored, and so perfected, and was pleased with as a religion for Himself, and made obligatory upon His servants? Will He accept from them a part of it without the rest, so that they stand in their classes like the people of this world? Or will He accept from them only the one who brings it whole?

The scholar said: If the people of this world are in classes within their world, then the people of religion are in degrees within their religion. In justice it is a duty that the Creator be worshipped with the fullness of what He willed. But great is His riches and great is their need of Him. His mercy widened for them, and He knew that among them are the weak, so He accepted from them what was made easy of sincere striving in work and of the effort spent in obedience, whether little of that or much. Then He charged each one with knowledge according to his strength and his deed, and He gave every man of merit his merit, so that they are degrees with their Lord. Then, by His justice, He appointed for them, as the outcome of obedience, that He would join them to the people of the complete religion and the excellent deed among His servants. The Almighty said:

> وَمَنْ يُطِعِ اللهَ وَالرَّسُولَ فَأُولَئِكَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللهُ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنَ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ وَالصَّالِحِينَ وَحَسُنَ أُولَئِكَ رَفِيقًا ذَلِكَ الفَضْلُ مِنَ اللهِ

And whoever obeys God and the Messenger, those are with the ones upon whom God has bestowed favor, of the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous. What excellent companions they are. That is the bounty from God.

This is for their spending of effort in obedience. It is not that their deeds are like the deeds of the righteous, but it is a mercy from God to them, even though they were weak and their works were slight.

The boy said: Praise be to God, who has done this for them. Then is it to be feared for anyone that he be punished for other than disobedience, even if his work is little?

The scholar said: God is more just than that.

The boy said: Is it to be hoped, then, for anyone to enter Paradise without works, like the insane and the young children?

The scholar said: That falls under the grace of God, His generosity, and the breadth of His mercy.

The boy said: Then what is the limit of effort in obedience and of sincere work, such that the acceptance of one's excuse in all else becomes binding upon God?

The scholar said: The reach of one's knowledge and the utmost of one's strength.

The boy said: Then describe to me, of the duty of knowledge and of action, the goal of each, that I may test myself. If I am able to reach the goal in the pleasure of my Lord, I press on. And if not, I halt at the limit of my strength, and God is the more entitled to excuse me for what I fell short of. I am certain of the justice of God and secure from His wronging, yet afraid of the evil of my own soul.

The scholar said: Do not fear from the place where you feel secure, and do not feel secure from the place where you fear. Strive for God in the work with your self and your wealth. For God is not pleased with what He hates, nor does He hate what pleases Him. He has hated unbelief for His creation and is pleased with thankfulness from His servants and with justice in His ruling, and He accepts nothing else. Truthfulness and faithfulness are His two gardens, and none is saved with Him except the people of truthfulness and faithfulness.

The boy said: Then make plain to me the goal of the work in myself, and what God has made binding upon me in it.

The scholar said: Obedience to God ennobles its people, even if their origins are humble, and the good deed is the crown of those who do it. As for what is a duty upon you in your own self, it is the performing of all the obligations and the shunning of all the forbidden things, for that is the sustainer of the religion and the purity of the bodies. And the sum of what makes the pleasure of God binding for you and His acceptance of you is that you bring to another what you love that he should bring to you, and that you hold back from him what you love that he should hold back from you. In that is justice, and God is pleased with justice.

The boy said: Yes, this is the binding truth, and whoever does not do it has no part with God in anything. Then is there, in what you have mentioned, any room in a time of necessity?

The scholar said: God has widened that for His servants, out of tenderness from Him and mercy. He said:

> فَمَنِ اضْطُرَّ غَيْرَ بَاغٍ وَلَا عَادٍ فَلَا إِثْمَ عَلَيْهِ

Whoever is compelled, without willful transgression and without excess, there is no sin upon him.

The boy said: Praise be to God, who has laid upon His servants in the religion no hardship. I am resolved to guard what I have been commanded, and I seek the help of God in what He has ordered me. Then what is the duty regarding my wealth, which you joined to my self when you said to me, Work with your self and your wealth?

The scholar said: As for your wealth, it is what God has granted you of every kind of gain. Take it only from what is lawful, and spend it only in its rightful place.

The boy said: And what is the lawful from which it is taken, and what is the rightful place in which it is spent?

The scholar said: Its lawful source is the wholesomeness of the gains in which you have wronged no one and which you have not mixed with falsehood. As for its rightful claim, it is its zakat, that you look to all that has come into your hand, whether little of it or much, and bring out a fifth of the best of it, the dearest of it to you, and deliver it to the one whose claim upon you is binding. At that, your wealth is purified by the alms, as your body was purified by the work. The work is a shield, and the zakat is a cleansing, and he has prospered who purifies himself.

The boy said: I will do it, and I turn to God in repentance for what has passed. Then what is the work in what remains of the wealth?

The scholar said: You spend it upon yourself and your dependents with kindness. And if its surplus stays with you, comfort from it the people of need among your brothers, and give your kindness to everyone who turns to you desiring it, for the one who has no surplus is in need that another give to him. So be swift to every good and foremost to every act of bounty. This is the goal of the work, if you have patience for it.

The boy said: Yes, I will be patient and then patient again. And how should he who is in great need not be patient toward the one who has no need of his work? Every needy one is patient, and every one given favor gives thanks.

The scholar, when the boy's words gladdened him, said: Praise be to God, who has granted you the right in speech. I ask Him to help you to the completion of the deed, for that is the perfection of merit.

The boy said: I had been seeking the merit of a favor from the hand of another for myself, and when the merit at last became mine from my own hand, I was denying it to myself, and I find no one more treacherous to my soul than myself. So here is all my wealth between your hands, in five shares. One share of it is its zakat. One share is the expiation for what I have wasted. One share is thanksgiving to God for what I have come to know. One share is for the poor among my brothers. And one share you accept by your grace, that it may be a provision for you. Gather them all to yourself, for God will send their like in their place.

The narrator said: The scholar's heart was moved for the boy when he saw that his jewel had come clear, and what the trial had uncovered of praiseworthy inner worth, and that his merit had shown and his deed had grown pure.

The scholar said, while his tears ran down: O my son, for the like of you let the scholars travel the road. For the like of you let the earth be borne up and the sky be held above. For the like of you let the bonds of kinship be made manifest, and for the like of you let the rain come down from the openings of the clouds. So joy to you, O my son, for what God has given you and for the breast He has opened to Islam. Take glad tidings of a light from your Lord and a near opening. Accept the gift of God to you and the good things of His graces upon you, with abundant work and noble thanks. As for my share of this wealth, I have no need of it. Rather I came out from wealth and fled from the trial of wealth, and it would not be of piety, O my son, that you turn me back into what I have hated. As for the share of your brothers, it shall stay with you. If they ask you for anything, pass it to them by your own hand, that they may know your merit and give thanks for your kindness. As for the share of expiation, hold it with you until you come to know your sin. As for the share of thanksgiving, it is a trust with you for God until you reach your maturity and rise to the utmost of what you have come to know. As for the share of zakat, it too is a trust with you for the one to whom it belongs. So take them all to yourself until you meet him, for he is the ruler over you and on your behalf.

The boy said: Then who is this generous one with you, whose judgment you do not overstep and whose command you do not transgress?

The scholar said: He is the one whose right God has made binding upon me and upon all the believers, in whose hand are the keys of the gardens and the landmarks of the kingdom, whose palms are opened

With the light of Sinai he is the cause behind the signs, and the one whose knowledge calls to the loftiest of ends. Through him your light is completed, through him Allah perfects your affair, and at his hand you are blessed.

The boy said: Such kindness as this, to whom does it belong, and who are you yourself?

The scholar said: I am his son and one of his servants in good works, seeking his pleasure and shunning his displeasure.

The boy said: I think I am far from his good pleasure, and yet close enough that, had I not known of him, I would have an excuse. Do I have an excuse, given what I now know?

The scholar said: An excuse is granted only before exposure to such knowledge.

The boy said: Shall I speak, or shall I hold back?

The scholar said: Say what seems best to you.

The boy said: Should you not, at this moment, take me by the hand to the door of his chamber until I enter, and to the house of light until I draw from it? This is part of the full measure of truthfulness which is binding upon you.

The scholar said: This is your duty, but only with his permission and his decision.

Then the scholar took leave of the boy and departed. The boy wished to press more upon him, but thought better of forcing it. The scholar went on until he came to the boy's greater father, and he described the boy to him and assured him that the boy was now of the company of the people of goodness and of the lineage of truthfulness. The father marvelled at it.

He said: Make haste to me with this boy, for I hope that he will be a door of mercy that Allah opens for the people of his age.

The scholar lingered only a little, and then came back to the boy, and found him in a different state of body than the one in which he had left him.

The scholar said: What is this I see in you, different from the state in which I left you?

The boy said: And what is it that you have noticed in me, may my father and mother be your ransom?

The scholar said: Weakness of body and yellowness of color.

The boy said: That is from a desolation that befell me after your departure.

The scholar said: What is this desolation, and what is the cause of this weakness?

The boy said: Your absence made me desolate, and fear of misfortune before meeting you again weakened me.

The scholar said: Allah has known what is in your heart, and so has made the affliction easy for you, and has cast into the heart of the one who has charge of you a mercy toward you. So take up your traveling provisions, for through you I will reach my hope, and I will bring you to the door of your goal. There is no power except by Allah.

At this the boy was so overcome by joy and reverence that his soul almost left his body, until Allah took hold of him, knowing his condition, and returned it to him. When he revived, he said:

Praise be to Allah, who has never ceased to be merciful to the believers.

And he gave great thanks to his father, and came up to his palms to kiss them, and to his feet. He rose at once and took up the traveling kit, and the two of them set out until they reached the greater scholar. They sought permission and were admitted. They entered, gave their greetings, and sat down with his permission. When they were settled in the council, the boy said to his teacher: Teach me how to speak and how to ask for what I need.

The teacher said to him: It is not lawful for me to instruct you in anything while you are in this place, out of reverence for its master. For you are in the hands of one who knows your need and knows what you will.

So the boy held back, and they stayed with the scholar for a time, until he had completed the boy's affair and perfected his guidance, and the boy reached the end of his hope and came to know what he had and what was required of him. Then they took leave and went out, both of them concealed. They had spoken no words in the council save those of peace and good cheer, for the boy had turned yellow from the intensity of his awe and could not speak. The Shaykh, the lord of the house, ordered that the two be treated well and honored, and that whatever they needed be provided. He then went on with them to his house, and when he had brought them in he greeted them and showed his pleasure in them.

He said to the scholar, for the two of them were friends, and between them was brotherhood and an old acquaintance: Brother, is this the boy we used to hear about?

He said: Yes, this is the one whose report has grown sweet in the hearts, so that his remembrance rose upon the tongues of the believers.

The boy said: Has the report of my story truly reached the friends of Allah, until they study my remembrance in their gatherings?

The scholar said: Yes, my brother, you are such.

The boy said: Favors have surrounded me from every side, and bounties have overtaken me from every path. I do not know whom to thank, nor whom to repay.

Then they all sat down and conversed through the night. When morning came, they sought permission to enter upon the Shaykh. When they were admitted and had completed their greetings, the Shaykh gave them permission to sit, and they sat. Then the Shaykh began to speak.

He said: Praise be to Allah, who has opened with His light the hearts that were sealed, and unlocked with His justice what was closed against the seeker, and made remembrance easy by His craft, as a gentleness to His servants and a mercy from Him to those who long for Him. He has not burdened creation with the full measure of what is due to Him, nor has He been content that they disbelieve His blessings. Rather He has charged them, out of His justice, with only what they are not too weak to bear, so that the matter was set level between two extremes, and was therefore called justice. He has spread out His hands with bounty in kindness and grace, and has not disappointed one who calls upon Him, nor deprived one who keeps silent. His blessings are overflowing and His favors abundant, neither reserved to the one who gives thanks for them, for the greatness of His reward, nor withheld from the one who is ungrateful for them, for the greatness of His punishment. He has held His blessings too noble for any stinting, and made His proofs too well established for any denial. He is too vast in His encompassing to be contained in existence, and too high in His evidences to be met with rejection. He is present without being bounded, and known without being described. Glory be to Him. How great in His gentleness to His creation, how merciful in His might to His servants, how just in His ruling despite His power, how near to His people despite His loftiness, how far above the illusions despite His nearness. So worship Him and give thanks to Him. To Him you will return.

Then he said: But to proceed. It is among the honors of minds that they be put to use in seeking, and among the fruits of seeking is finding, and among the marks of finding is the sweetness of what is found. Every sweet, fresh spring has a surface that is drunk and a depth that is veiled. The one who seeks it does not weary of the thought that pursues it, and creation does not reach the end of its purpose. Its features are truth, and the truth of the truth is an obligation, and from this it was named truth, for the establishing of its obligation. Whoever withholds from the truth what is due to it is a wrongdoer. And indeed, among the rights of thought is the purifying of hearts by good manners, and among the rights of good manners is the seeking of knowledge, and among the rights of knowledge is acting upon it, and among the rights of acting upon it is that it be kept pure by obedience to those charged with authority, and among the rights of obedience is that it be perfected in hardship and ease alike, in good fortune and in trial the same.

وما يُلقاها إلا الذين صبروا وما يُلقاها إلا ذو حظ عظيم

And none is granted it except those who are patient, and none is granted it except one of great fortune.

Then he turned his face toward the boy and said: O young man, you have been honored as an intimate who has come to visit, and greeted as a guest who has set his course here. So what is your name?

He said: Ubayd Allah, son of Abd Allah.

The Shaykh said: That is your description, and your report has already reached us.

The boy said: I am free, son of Abd Allah.

The Shaykh said: And who freed you from a master, until you became free?

The boy said: This scholar freed me, and he pointed with his hand to the scholar who had summoned him.

The Shaykh said: Consider now, if this one were owned and possessed nothing of his own, would his freeing of you be valid?

The boy said: It would not be valid.

The Shaykh said: Then what is your name?

Then the boy bowed his head, at a loss for the answer.

The Shaykh said: O young man, how can a thing know that which has no name?

The boy said: Then I am one newly born to you, so name me.

The Shaykh said: That waits until the completion of seven days.

The boy said: And why is that deferred until the completion of seven days?

The Shaykh said: For the honoring of the newborn.

The boy said: And if the newborn dies before the completion of the seven days?

The Shaykh said: Nothing harms him. He is named after that.

The boy said: And this name by which you will name me, is it mine?

The Shaykh said: Would you then become one that is worshipped, even though you were born?

The boy said: How, then, should the matter of it be stated?

The Shaykh said: The name belongs to you, and you belong to the name. So do not enter except within your limit, and take your leave until your term.

So the boy rose in haste, in the goodness of his obedience, and did not answer back, in the goodness of his manners. He went out, and his father went out with him, until they returned to their lodging. The owner of the house greeted them.

He said: What is the state of this brother of ours concerning his need?

The scholar said to him: A promise has been made, and the one promised is thirsty, and the term is near.

So they remained until the seventh day. Then they sought permission of the Shaykh, and when he had granted them leave, he ordered the boy to wash and to put on his purest garments. When the boy set about the preparation, his heart sensed that his need would be granted on that day, and that this preparation he had been ordered to make pointed to a good that would come after it. When he had done what he was ordered, they all went out. The boy went out saying, A day of fortune, and a patron of fortune, repeating it again and again. Nothing drove him to this saying except the abundance of his joy and the intensity of his longing to know what would bring him nearer to the pleasure of Allah. When they came in upon the Shaykh, they found that he had taken up the implements of his work. He greeted them with peace, and they returned the greeting.

Then he ordered the boy to draw near to him, and he drew near, and he brought him close, and he came close. And he began to speak of matters that no fancies had ever encompassed, that no pens had ever traced, and that had never occurred to the heart of any human being; matters that are not to be mentioned in the assemblies of preachers, nor spoken openly in the gatherings of books, for the greatness of their excellence, and that are unveiled only to their own people. And when he had discharged his duty toward him, and brought him out of the wildernesses of his bewilderment to the very end of guidance, his way became pure and his drink became sweet, and he came to know his Lord. So he released his pilgrim state by magnifying the sanctities of Allah, took hold of the firm rope, circled the ancient House, completed his rites with remembrance, and fulfilled his pilgrimage by the greatest sign.

Then he and his father continued to come and go to the Shaykh, studying what he had given him, and the boy was patient in his perseverance. He did not weary of asking, for the nobility of it, nor was he sated with the answer, for the sweetness of it. So they remained in that state for a span of time, until the Shaykh knew that the boy's root had been watered and his branch had risen high.

Then the boy said to the Shaykh, in thanks to Allah and to him: Praise be to Allah, who has made you worthy of this station, so that through you He revives a religion and puts an end to an ignorance, and has made you a place of return for the people and a sanctuary. You are the gate of the people of heaven and the ascent of the people of the earth. From the light of your might the lights appeared, and from the pouring of your palm the rivers flowed. May you remain thus, until the one who is pleased with you is made pleased.

The Shaykh said: As for you, may Allah bless you in what He has given you, and aid you by His enabling grace to guard what has come to you, and employ you rightly in His will, and smooth your way with the light of His guidance, and expand your breast with the keys of wisdom, and ennoble your affair with the path of the call, and complete your excellence with the confirmations of response, and bring you, in obedience to Him, to the best of your hope of His good pleasure. I bid you farewell, my son, and I give you leave to depart. Your parting is grievous to me, but three considerations have driven us to it. The first is that we are setting out on a journey to another land. The second is your separation from your family through all this time, along with the burden you took upon yourself in going out without your father's permission, for in him is a heavy grief and a great anger that nothing will dispel but your return; yet I have hope for your father, if you are gentle with him. And the third is the duty of fulfilling the right of this grace that has reached you, for it is incumbent upon you to serve God by it and to call others to it.

The boy said: Your parting is sorrow upon me, and your loss is a diminishing upon me. But your counsel is more rightly guiding, your command more binding, and your judgment more sound. So enjoin upon me what you see fit, and dismiss me when you will, and be gracious to me by sending my father with me to my land, that I may rely upon him in my work and entrust all my affairs to him.

The Shaykh said: I shall do that, and I enjoin upon you the fear of Allah who created you, and the guarding of the trust you have taken upon yourself, and the safeguarding of your father, who raised you when you were small and toiled long in your affair, with his person and his wealth. So do not clothe him in the garments of disdain, nor set him upon the vehicles of ruin. Do not slight his name, nor turn your speech away from his remembrance, nor betray him in your city. And in the beginning there is room enough for you.

The boy said: May Allah reward you, as a patron of grace, on behalf of every grateful one, with good; and may the Lord of abundance repay you for your shepherding of us, when hands are too weak to repay you. I will keep your commandment. So do not cut off my share from the blessing of your supplication.

Then they stood up, shook hands, and embraced, and each bade farewell to his friend, unable to contain himself under the weight of the parting, able to speak only by gesture. Then they took their separate ways.

So the scholar and the boy went out together, and when they had drawn near to the boy's city, where his father was, the scholar said to the boy: "O my son, I have understood the will of the Shaykh, and you have not. Guidance lies only in his words. This is your city, and we have come to the edge of it. So sit with us here, away from the road, for I wish to remind you of some of my concerns and to charge you with what you should do."

When they had sat down, the scholar said: "O my son, I know your father and his enmity toward the people of this matter. Added to that now is your going out with me and your absence from him without his permission and without his trust. I wish to honor your father's view by keeping some of these affairs concealed, so that you may return to him, and he may be at ease again, and remain near us. And I hope, if you are gentle with him, that he will be closer to what you desire than anyone else. Your absence has worn him down, and his solitude has come to frighten him. Know, my son, that Allah has opened to you the least of your striving what I did not suppose you would reach until a long stretch of time had passed. Allah has been good to you, so be good to yourself, and honor the one through whom Allah has honored you. We have thought well of you, and our hope in you has been great. So keep the trust your father entrusted to you, and stand firm in your affair. Piety and wary devotion are the mainstay of your religion and your work; thanksgiving and patience are an increase to your light. Seek from Allah the opening of your provisions and the goodness of His granting you success."

Then the scholar said: "I commend to you six qualities: that you trust in Allah when you are resolved; that you speak with a ruling when you are called upon; that you do not grow angry when you are wronged; that you do not weary when you are slandered; that whoever answers you toward the good, you bear with him patiently; and that you keep for yourself the best of that to which you call others. For Allah will help you and will open the way before you."

The boy said: "May Allah reward you with three goods: an imam whose case you have upheld and whose trust you have counseled well, and a dead man whose life you graced with kindness. My heart is full, and my breast is packed with light and gladness at the greatness of what you have laid open. I do not know which of your words is the finer: the outward, which you arranged in its sequence, or the inward, which you expounded upon its meanings."

The scholar said: "Praise be to Allah, who has made His way easy for you, and brought its far reaches near to you, and helped you to carry its weight. I am taking my leave of you now and turning away from you. Is there any need still left?"

The boy said: "The pillars of my needs are three: a need already fulfilled, a need I hope for, and a need I ask you to grant of your kindness."

The scholar said: "I have understood your three needs, and I answer you concerning what you have asked. As for the need that has already passed, it is your life in knowledge after death. May Allah bless you with a life in it and grant you the best of what you intended. As for the need for which you hope, it is to act upon what you have known and to accept it when you are brought to account. May Allah bring that about for you by His mercy. As for the need you ask of me, it is as though you have desired an increase upon what you have heard."

The boy said: "There is no increase greater than your knowing what is within me. So grant of your kindness what you see fit."

The scholar said: "As for my knowing what is within you, none knows the unseen but Allah. But the believer sees by the light of Allah:

the believer sees by the light of Allah. As for the increase, I will lay its matter out plainly for you and set you upon the foundations by which you are to work. You are to look at all these things whose outward form I have described to you, and turn them into parables. Whatever among them is praiseworthy is a likeness of the truth and its people, its noblest for their noblest, and its tokens pointing to them. And whatever among the parables is blameworthy, I have turned it upon the enemies of the truth and its people, its greatest for their greatest and its least for their least, and its tokens pointing to them. These are the foundations of the science upon which action turns. If you preserve them, you will have need of none besides them. And I commend to you the fear of the One who created you when you were nothing, and the keeping of His covenant, and the fulfillment of the right He has laid upon you and upon all His creation."

When he had finished his discourse, and brought him to his place of safety, and completed his nurture of him, he said: "O my son, take care of yourself, for I have a work other than you, and this is the parting between me and you."

At the mention of parting the boy gave a gasp so violent that he almost took his own life, his spirit near to taking flight and his soul near to leaving his body. Then he bent to kiss the scholar's feet and wiped their dust upon his cheeks, and bade him farewell and departed. The boy returned to his house, his heart torn from him in dread, unable to hold back any word of his grief over the parting from his elder father, and from the father who had been the cause of his grace, until his family and his neighbors grew afraid for him. He passed his day and his night restless with sorrow and alarm. Then his father came in upon him, in anger.
