# The Boy at the Door — Limits and Conditions

The boy had a father who was a great sheikh among the Arabs, the most honored of them in lineage and the fiercest of them against the people of religion. So the youth kept the company of the Master, with his leave, until the Master came to the end of his journey and reached his lodging. The Master went in, and brought the boy in with him. Then he called for food, and when they had finished and had settled into talk, the boy spoke.

The boy said, "O wise one, you have spoken and made yourself heard, you have preached and reached the mark. You have adorned the religion with the finest ornament, and made it desired with the best of longing and the noblest of provision, and called to it by the most honorable of its trusts. You have made minds yearn to question you by the blows your words struck against the hearts. Such excellence could come only from one whose knowledge has been perfected in the inward, and whose forbearance has widened to embrace the outward, so that he is the wellspring of wisdom rising from a firm root, whose loftiness towers over the worlds, and from whose branches there branch out fruits for the seekers. I hope that you may be one of those blessed branches, for what I see in you of noble judgment, sound core, and perfect intellect. You have said that minds are driven by ignorance to seek knowledge and capacity, and my mind is among the most needy of them and the most destitute. Is there, then, a path for me to life?"

The Master said, "He who created the creatures by His power did not create them in vain, nor did He fashion them as scholars from the start, nor does He abandon them in the wanderings of ignorance to no purpose, nor is He content that stupidity should be their culture or the garments of ignorance their clothing. He has made for them a way to the exit from all of this, by the instrument with which He composed their frame and completed their creation. He made hearing, the eyes, and the hearts full of lights, as an honor from Him to them and as an argument against them. Then He set the treasures of His wisdom and the marks of His will within doctrines that please Him in His elect from among His creation. He entrusted them with religion and called them to it, and imposed upon them the answering of His call by the instrument We have named, so that God might distinguish the corrupt from the good. So the seeker is enabled, by his seeking, to see, and the one heedless of his own backwardness is made known by his lagging. There is no argument left against Allah after His Messengers, and no excuse for going astray after the evidence."

The boy said, "The lack of any excuse has frightened me, and the existence of the evidence has made me long for it. I implore you for a proof, and I will be a lowly servant to you."

The Master said, "God did not create the creation as men, nor as scholars. He created them only as little children and ignorant ones who knew nothing. There is no great except after small, and no scholar except after ignorance. Smallness is the prelude to greatness, and ignorance is a guide to knowledge. In the same way, knowledge cannot be borne except by the introduction of some lesser knowledge before it, a knowledge quicker than itself to reach the hearts and smoother to the understanding, so that its beginning may be steadied in the mind, the limbs may be humbled to its obligations, the breast may shiver at its inmost meaning, and minds may bear its end. This is the way of Allah with His servants, so let us not depart from the religion by our own opinion, nor follow, along with our guidance, anything but the established way."

The boy said, "Then I am too small for what I want, so raise me. I am ignorant of what I have sought, so teach me. I am in need of your bounty, so have mercy on me. You yourself were once in this very state, and God drove to you His blessing at the hands of one whose trial for your sake was great and whose thanks became binding upon you. I know the condition of the one who suffers the narrowness of ignorance and the need of the poor man for a way, and the weakness of his devices."

The Master said, "The state you have described is indeed one I once knew, and I am watchful over you for your sake. But my mercy toward you is not something that would bring your hope nearer to you without your own work. For every speech has a turning, and nothing is accepted except what is truthful. So too, knowledge brings no benefit until it is put to use, and action brings no benefit until it is accepted. The truthfulness of speech is its justice, and the proof of knowledge lies in the traveled ways of works and the covenants of exemplars, in all that the ends of the regions contain, and over which night and day have differed. Do not pursue an argument carelessly, nor cast one aside carelessly, nor accept one without proof, in mere imitation, lest the first doubt that rises against it tear it out of your heart. Do not deny it either, once its proof stands firm, lest you become an advocate for falsehood, and do not abandon the search into it and the deep study of it, lest your judgment be left barren. Think well of me without favoritism, and strive in your questioning without suspicion. Seek the religion as one like you should seek it, and seek God's help in your affair, for God does not let your reward go to waste, nor deprive you of recompense, when He sees you seeking the good."

The boy said, "You have dealt justly with yourself by fair action, and you have pointed to the truth in the finest of speech. I seek God's help, and I ask you about this matter to which you call, and toward which you urge me. What is it, and whose is it, and upon whom does it lie?"

The Master said, "It is the religion of Allah that He has accepted, and it is from Him, so He accepts no other in its place. Then it was delivered first to the elect among His Messengers, and in general to all His creation. Upon the one singled out by the blessing lies thanks for it, action upon it, and the call to it. Upon the one deprived of it lies the seeking after it until he knows, and obedience to the one who has guided him."

The boy said, "This blessing with which Allah has singled out His friends, is it all equal in its meanings, so that they are equal in its excellence, or does some of it surpass the rest?"

The Master said, "Have we not already said in our talk that every knowledge has before it a lesser knowledge, and that every blessing has its bearers, so that they stand at graded ranks before their Lord, each man charged with what he has acquired of his knowledge? Then to every one possessed of excellence is given his excellence, as an act of justice from Him within His creation and a mercy to His servants."

The boy said, "This to which you call me, then, is it that same blessing, or is it something other than it?"

The Master said, "It is neither identical with it in its full reality, nor wholly other than it. Rather it is a part of it, like a single handful of water drawn from the breadth of the Euphrates. I call you to what Allah has accepted for His servants and honored on the day He completed it. It is precious, guarded, generous, hidden. He ennobled it with reward, an honor for the one who upholds it, and He backed it with punishment, a debasement for the one who squanders it. Its outward is figures and its inward is knowledge. Its rule is a light and its justice is plain. This is what I call you to."

The boy said, "What has made you more exclusively entitled to this matter than others, and more deserving of it than any beside you, when you claim that it is from God for creation in general, so that everyone lays claim to it and denies it to the rest? What has made you more deserving of the name of truth than they, when this matter came to you only from where it came to them?"

The Master said, "There is no scholar who speaks a word of truth but that he has taken it from the very origins from which we ourselves have taken it. In this they join with us, by their own acknowledgment and ours. What then divided us from them was their abandoning of those origins while we endured upon them, our obedience to them where they disobeyed, our keeping of what we were entrusted with where they squandered it, and our standing by what we were charged with where they fell short. So they agree with us in acknowledgment and differ from us in deed. Their acknowledgment is a witness for us against them, and their squandering shows their poverty toward us and their need of what is in our hands. They are like brothers who inherited great wealth from their father and divided it equally between them. Some of them squandered it until they fell into poverty, while others kept it, guarded it, and traded with it, and the wealth in their hands multiplied. Then the poor among the brothers turned to the wealthier ones, serving them and seeking their bounty. So too these who have squandered the way of their Messengers are driven by their ignorance to seek knowledge from the people of the noble origins. The proof that we are more deserving of the truth than they is their need of us and our being free of need of them. So our learned one calls their ignorant one, and our clothed one covers their naked one, and our rich one feeds their hungry one. Thus the truth is binding upon the one who would be virtuous, the credit belongs to the one who confers the favor, and thanks are owed to the one who bestows the kindness."

The boy said, "As for your excellence over them, that has become clear. As for your argument by the truth, it has been established against every squanderer among the common people. But what is your argument against me, when I already acknowledge what you have said, stand by what you have described, deny what you have denied, and know the truth and its excellence, and falsehood and its people?"

The Master said, "My argument against you is the same as my argument against them, for you differ from them in description while you agree with them in meaning."

The boy said, "And how is that, when I acknowledge the truth to its owner, and they deny it?"

The Master said, "If you have parted from them by acknowledging where they denied, you have joined them in withholding where they withheld. For acknowledgment without a turning to act brings no benefit, and denial with the turning does no harm."

The boy said, "Then I turn to the people of the truth in their right, once I have come to know them, or to a deputy who may establish it for them. Show me which of the two is possible."

The Master said, "Do you not see that you have been driven to needing knowledge of the one who possesses the truth, or of his deputy, just as the common people have been driven to needing a proof of the truth itself? Deed has divided you from them, and ignorance has joined you with them."

The boy said, "You have spoken truly, so make it plain to me."

The Master said, "Have you not acknowledged that my argument stands firm against you and against the common people?"

He said, "Yes."

The Master said, "Then can anyone establish for himself a truth that belongs to another?"

He said, "No one is able to do that."

The Master said, "Then the argument has been established against you and against the common people. Nothing remains but submission, and turning to the one whom you cannot do without and who can do without you."

The boy said, "As for my poverty, I acknowledge it. As for the One who is free of need of me, I seek Him, so have mercy on me."

The Master said, "Have you seen one whom poverty has driven to a man of bounty attain anything of his bounty except by earning or by grace?"

The boy said, "Yes, he attains it only by earning or by grace."

The Master said, "And does one who receives by grace have any share in the merit of those who earn?"

He said, "No."

The Master said, "Then seek this matter by work, and you will attain it as a thing deserved. Do not seek it by flattery, and so attain it as a mere grace, for then you would be deprived."

The boy said, "You are the key to goodness and its guide, so show me the more meaningful of the two paths and the nearer of them to guidance. The greatness of my need of you has bound my bond fast to you."

The Master said, "O my son, there is nothing beyond your reach, and no reproach against you. What you have hoped for from me will reach you. But it has limits not to be transgressed, and conditions not to be forgotten."

The boy said, "Set whatever limits you love, and lay down whatever conditions you see fit. I hope you will find me patient under your testing and grateful for your kindness."

The Master said, "The first of the limits is to uphold the outward of the Book and its laws, and to act, in that, upon what you know. For:

> فإنه من عمل لله بما يعلم، هداه الله إلى ما لا يعلم

> Whoever acts for God by what he knows, God guides him to what he does not know."

The boy said, "But the Books are many, and all of them are from Allah, and for every Book among them there is a scholar of the creation who holds fast to it, and all of them are agreed on guarding it and on the religion it contains."

The Master said, "We have returned to our first words, and we have come to needing to settle what ought not to need settling."

The boy said, "And how is that?"

The Master said, "By your saying that the Books are many, and all of them are from Allah, and each has its own scholar. Do you then imagine that the words of Allah and His Books cancel one another out, that the first of them gives the lie to the last, and the last to the first?"

The boy said, "I do not imagine that. But what is the proof of it?"

The Master said, "Had the creation acted upon what is in the first Book, it would have pointed them to the second, and had they acted upon the second, it would have pointed them to the third, until the end of the work would be at the last of them as a Book, because it is more fitting than what came before it, even though all of them are from Allah. For the last is nearer in time and clearer in doctrine, since it abrogates what came before it, and nothing came after it to abrogate it."

The boy said, "You have spoken truly, and made plain the knowledge of the truth and the mark of truthfulness. I hold to the limits you have ordered me to keep. So what are your conditions upon me?"

The Master said, "My conditions upon you are five:

1. Do not fail me if I entrust you with something.
2. Do not conceal anything from me if I ask you.
3. Do not seek me out until I answer you.
4. Do not ask me for anything until I begin with you.
5. Do not mention my affair to your father."

The boy said, "I will do all that you have mentioned, and it is easy for me, set against the greatness of what I hope for from you. But how shall I contrive concerning my father? He is among the heaviest of trials upon me, and the sorest to me. Will you permit me to remove myself from him?"

The Master said, "Removing yourself from him would be a sign leading others to you. Rather, stay with him, be dutiful to him, and satisfy him with some outward agreement, and keep your secret from him and conceal your affair. For Allah will suffice us against his harm and the harm of others."

The narrator said: Then the Master and the boy would meet and part for a stretch of their time, and the boy was in bewilderment over his affair. He knew nothing but what the law had brought him to halt at, he did not know where the Master was settled, and he was unable to seek him out because of the condition laid upon him, even as the term lengthened, while the Master was showing him his own patience and endurance under what he had charged him with. At last, on a quiet night, when the creation had grown still and the informers had grown heedless, when the assembly had settled the two of them together, the nearness of his need fell into the boy's soul and the privacy of his companion was granted him. He fell down before him in prostration. And when he raised his head,

the Master said to him, "I saw you prostrate in thanksgiving. Thanksgiving before the deed brings on suspicion, and thanksgiving cut short after the deeds makes the deeds themselves vain."

The boy said, "All your words are truthful, and all your works are acts of mastery. My thanks are only for the bounty of yours that has gone before, and by the thanks I hoped for an increase from you."

The Master said, "Thanksgiving is worthy indeed to make the increase binding for the thankful, and your right has become due. So turn toward me with your heart, make your mind present, and listen to what is laid before you."

The boy said, "I have brought the instrument of understanding and taken up the equipment of hearing. Say what seems good to you."

The Master said, "The religion has a key that makes it lawful and unlawful, like the difference between forbidden union and lawful marriage."

The boy said, "This key you have mentioned is great in the sight of Allah, since it distinguishes between the lawful and the unlawful, and between truth and falsehood. So what is it?"

The Master said, "It is the covenant of Allah, which confirms His rights, gathers His obligations, and is the shield for His friends, the rope of God upon His earth and His safe pledge among His servants. I will recite it to you, and take it upon you."

The boy said, "Yes, take from me whatever you love. I do not refuse your judgment, nor transgress your way."

The narrator said: Then the Master set about reciting the covenant to the boy, conveying it to him slowly and binding him fast by it, while the boy could not master himself for dread, and his tears streamed down from the intensity of his weeping, until he came to the end of the covenant. He praised Allah and glorified Him, and thanked Him for what had reached him, and he became certain that, by accepting their pledge, he had entered the Party of Allah (Hizb Allah) and the company of His friends. Then he fell silent out of his composure, and the Master set about the exposition and the explanation.
