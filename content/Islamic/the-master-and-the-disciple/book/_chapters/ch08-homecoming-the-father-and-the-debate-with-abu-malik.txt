# Homecoming, the Father, and the Debate with Abu Malik

The father said: "O my son, is this the reward of sons to their fathers? I have taught you only the best that a child can be taught by a father, and you have not denied your own self nor renounced your beliefs except from the time I grew old with this strange man. You drew him in upon me, and you crossed over with him in his affair without consulting your parents. If what I knew was true, then you have hindered me in my very self, because you concealed the truth from me. And if it is false, then you have disobeyed me in your own ruin, and whatever has reached him has thereby reached me."

The boy said: "O my father, I have shown you nothing but mercy and compassion. You raised me as a mercy from you, and you nourished me, choosing me over family and wealth. I have no need to be so unkind to you in our day that I should make you the object of one of two strands of gratitude due to you."

The Shaykh said: "What is the difference between the two strands?"

The boy said: "Either you allow me to offer you my courtesies and favors, for I am your son, whom you raised young, and the one who commands us to Allah; or you grant me leave to debate with you and to give you my confidence, so that I am safe from your indignation and may draw upon your mind."

The Shaykh said: "As for your courtesy on the outside, while hearts differ within, this is a reproach between me and you. How long can outward agreement stand when its underpinnings differ? I do not want that. As for your debate and your candor, you have that. So say what seems best to you."

The boy said: "Have you seen my standing with you for forty years to be anything other than one of two strands? Either you are a scholar, and you have withheld from me the teaching I am owed. Do not blame me, then, if you forbid me from seeking my own salvation through another. Or you are ignorant, and you are excused with me, but you needed a man who was a stranger to me. I will precede you to him because of your age and the lateness of your birth, for I have heard his words just as you have heard them, and I have seen what you have seen."

The Shaykh's heart was rent by the boy's words, and he knew there was no escape for him from all of these arguments, and his eyes overflowed with tears. He said: "O my son, the argument that has been made against me is yours. You will share a similar fate with me if you keep your affairs secret from me. Remember it for me. If it is true, I will accept dignity from you for myself; and even if it is false, I have rejected it for your sake, out of compassion for you."

So they debated at intervals and disclosed things to each other at length, until they were guided to the right path, acting upon the religion of Allah, upholding it, holding fast to it, calling others to it, and cooperating in it. Through them, Allah revived many of His creation. And He sent the boy out into the world to preach, with his father's blessing, and to ask others to come to him. The boy came back to them and became a cause for their guidance. Allah blessed the people of that country through them and at their hands. Allah commanded, and slander was disappointed.

The boy's name was Salih, and his father's name was al-Bakhtari. They were honored, raised up before others, and given a high standing because of the excellence of the manners they had acquired. News of them spread through the land, along with news of their religion, until it reached a group of people who held to the old creed. Al-Bakhtari had a long-standing influence among them, and they feared this would be cut off from them by his departure from their religion. So they went out and gathered before a scholar of theirs called Abd al-Jabbar Abu Malik, who was named among them, for his scrupulousness, the abundance of his forbearance, and his defense of his religion, "Ka'b al-Ahbar." He had eminence among them through his knowledge, his understanding of speech, and his discernment of opinions. When they came in to him,

they said: "O Abu Malik, do you not see what the boy has gone over to?"

He said: "Which boy?"

They said: "Salih ibn al-Bakhtari."

He said: "And what has he gone over to?"

They said: "We are telling you that a stranger has come to the country, with fine speech and a doctrine he calls people to, and we do not know what it is. The boy went to him and went out with him, without his father's permission, and was absent for some time. Then he came back to his father, speaking the stranger's words and calling to what he was calling for. We have been told that his father has answered him in his opinion and supported him in it. We are grieved by what they have done, and by our own holding back from them, for three reasons. First: there was a favor of his, running upon us on account of his father, together with what you know of the brotherhood in religion between us; and we fear estrangement after intimacy, and we hate division after brotherhood. Second: if these people are upon the truth, then we have fallen short in following them in it and helping them in it. Third: if they have gone astray in it, then we have fallen short in pushing them away from it and in supporting the truth and its people. You are the head of our religion and one of our standards, and we have come to tell you and to consult you, and we will not overstep your opinion."

Abu Malik said: "Judge gently, for this speech of yours has alarmed me. You know that al-Bakhtari and his son were among those we used to count among the people of forbearance and scrupulousness, and that they have rights over us and the virtue of favors from of old. You have mentioned about them what you have mentioned, and you are, in my regard, of those who do not stray from truth to anything else, so Allah has made you independent of that. This is news that has come upon a people, and its argument has become plain to them, so they have followed it and been satisfied with it. We cannot judge them by error and so wrong them, nor can we judge them by guidance and so wrong ourselves by imitating them; nor can we say they have been deceived and so refute them without knowledge of them, for they are nobler than that with us. But we need to look at this news with a fair eye, with the light of justice, and with the steadfastness of the mind. For it has been said:

> من تثبّت في الأمور لم يزل سلطانه محروزاً، ومن عجّل في أمره لم يأمن زلة قدمه

He who is steadfast in matters keeps his authority secure, and he who is hasty in his affair is not safe from the slipping of his foot. For a breach in all things admits of repair, and what was corrupted in it can be exchanged, like a flaw in food, whose harm is easy and whose cure is near at hand. But a breach in religion is of much harm and grave ruin; its flaw cannot be repaired, nor can another be taken in its place, like a breach in medicine, which does not release its owner nor let him make good his own self. We have been told in this that the best of provisions are the treasures of hearts and the acquisition of manners, whose fruits and outcomes are praiseworthy; and among their fruits is the following of forbearance. Forbearance is the crown of the learned; noble resolves lift them by it to the heights of glories, and their virtue is made plain in every place, together with the honor they win in the ease of their demand. We have stored up for ourselves of that a portion of forbearance we have borrowed, and something of the manners we acquired in the time gone by of our age, amid the narrowness of circumstance and the hardship of the demand. We endured that for the sake of its virtue, and we seek its help against the calamities of affairs and the accidents of the ages. We cannot let it go to waste, and this is the hour of our need for it and of our putting it to use in what has come upon us of this matter that has arisen among our brothers. There is no sense in leaving them and what they are upon without laying it open with them and debating them in it, with the forbearance of the able and the understanding of seekers, free of any doubt in what we hold and free of any accusation against the people, that they have turned from the truth in heedlessness, for they are nobler than that with us, or that they have gone out into error, for such error is beneath them. Falsehood has no eminence for which the clear truth would be left. But reflect with me on the affair of these people, for it cannot be free of one of three states. Either the people are upon what we know of religion, and this new thing among them is only an increase in manners, hadiths, and poetry, and the speech of people is much; or the people have been given an increase in what they hold of the religion, and they have delighted in it, and:

> والعلم ضالة كل مؤمن

Knowledge is the lost property of every believer, and an increase in knowledge is better than a decrease; or the people have been given a guidance other than what they have, from the prophethood of a prophet who has come to them with it and called them to it, and they have followed it upon clear proof. None of this is to be denied of the command of Allah, nor will creation reach the utmost of Allah's decree concerning them and His will from them, even if they strive their utmost, for:

> وإن الله كل يوم هو في شأن

And Allah, every day, is upon some matter."

They said: "O Abu Malik, we came to you with hearts full of rage against these people, of calling them unbelievers and refuting their opinion, to the point that we held their blood and their property lawful. But we have found our hearts humbled by your speech, and by the way you have stood by their argument and defended them."

Abu Malik said: "This argument of mine is not for them; rather my argument is for the truth, that I may not betray it in the unseen. So I have stood by the argument of the truth on its behalf when the one who would argue for it is absent. Nor is it a defense of them either; rather I have defended myself and you against the speaking of falsehood and against pelting into the unseen what we have not encompassed in knowledge. Otherwise we would be, in that, like the tyrants of the nations gone by, when their doctrines pleased them in their forbearance, and they rejoiced in the knowledge they had, until they thought they had encompassed all that Allah had commanded and taught and willed of that from His creation. So they said Allah would not send messengers after their messengers, and they did not suppose that beyond what they had reached of knowledge there was any doctrine. They passed judgment upon Allah that He should cut off His argument from His creation and His deputies in His earth. Then they made the knowledge of Allah in His servants, and that upon which He had sent His prophets and messengers, the measure of their own minds, so that whatever went beyond it was, in their view, error. By that opinion they went astray and led many astray. By their own ruling they held lawful what Allah had forbidden, and by their own opinion they killed the prophets of Allah. I seek refuge for you in Allah, that you should follow the whims of a people who went astray before. For the people of intellects may be bent by whim toward every meaning, and deceived away from every path, save the path of religion, for it is the more forgiving in the seeking, and its virtue is not hidden."

They said: "O Abu Malik, we do not oppose your command, and your counsel to us is not hidden from us. We would not obey you in the first of our affairs and then disagree with you in the last of them. You have pointed to the opinion and hinted at the demand. If you see fit, take us to the people, that we may lay open their doctrine with them and look into it. If it is right guidance, we will follow it; and if it is error, we will shun it."

Abu Malik said: "This is doubt on your part in what you hold, and the doubter knows neither his own right nor the falsehood of another."

They said: "Then we hold firm upon our truth and give the lie to whatever is other than it, whatever it may be."

Abu Malik said: "Not thus is the meaning sought, nor by giving the lie is guidance found."

They said: "How, then, is the demand to be made, if we neither give the lie to falsehood nor affirm the truth?"

Abu Malik said: "If you already know the truth and its acceptance, and know falsehood and the giving of the lie to it, then you are not among the number of seekers; rather you are among the number of the learned in prophethood and guidance, the arbiters by revelation over the people of the world."

They said: "Then we are content with the truth we hold, and we do not doubt it, and we excuse ourselves from the burden of the demand."

Abu Malik said: "Are you not content that they should have virtue over you from one direction, that you would give them virtue over you from many directions? Would you not be content to be, through want, in the stations of the seekers, that you would be, through ignorance, in the stations of the sitters?"

They said: "How has that come to be in us, and what is their virtue over us?"

Abu Malik said: "Their virtue over you is by the knowledge of what you know, and an increase in what you do not know. This is a matter that has come upon a people; if you do not seek them out in it, they will seek you out for it; and if you do not listen to it, they will strive against you in it, and so they will gain the virtue over you by precedence, by demand, and by striving. As for sitting back from the demand, that is ignorance altogether, and neglect, and a fault upon the learned man until he teaches, and upon the ignorant man until he learns."

They said: "O Abu Malik, you have taken us with the argument from every road, and your every word is right. So open for us, from your opinion, a door by which we may act."

Abu Malik said: "Conceit in one's opinion is blindness, and neglect of the demand is error. There is no fault in the demand upon the seekers, but the seeker needs to know the doors of the demand, that he may seek the truth by the knowledge of the demand. Whoever seeks the truth without knowledge of it is the swifter to give it the lie, because falsehood shows its first signs by ostentation and by agreement with opinions, while the truth shows its first signs by affliction and by the crossing of whims. And none forsakes the following of whim and bears patiently the vanguard of affliction save every one who has a sound heart."

They said: "Here a door has opened for us, in which you point to the knowledge of the demand. Make plain to us what that knowledge is, and what the sound heart is."

Abu Malik said: "As for the knowledge of the demand, it is that you know you are in need. And the one in need seeks his need with the humility of want before what is sought. As for the sound heart, it is a heart that does not persist in giving the lie to what comes upon it, whatever it may be, until the truth is that which makes itself plain and shows its own virtue, and falsehood is that which uncovers its own shame. Then it discerns falsehood by its intellect and accepts the truth by its acceptance."

They said: "We have been told that whoever is in this station, who does not affirm the truth nor refute falsehood, is ignorant in his deed, since he does not know the truth so as to follow it, nor falsehood so as to shun it."

Homecoming, the Father, and the Debate with Abu Malik

Abu Malik said: "What you have related, you have believed. But has he not, in your reckoning, passed beyond the bound of the liars and entered the bound of the ignorant?"

They said: "Yes. With us he is ignorant, not a liar."

Abu Malik said: "The ignorant man is the more to be hoped for in the matter of the good, and in its seeking. He seeks knowledge for himself; and if he does not, the learned seek it for him, because ignorance is the root condition of creation, and there is no shame in it for one who seeks knowledge on account of his ignorance. Lying is the worst of wares. The liar is not guided by his lying, and he is never believed in what is written against him."

They said: "You have made us certain of it. We have come to know the defect of lying, and so we will never deny a thing until we know it for what it is. And you have made known to us the door of the request, through reverence born of need for what is sought. Is this reverence and humility toward the sought thing to be shown after it has made its right plain, or before the statement is given?"

Abu Malik said: "Only before the statement, so that by reverence you may attain the statement."

They said: "Then if we revere the sought thing until what it holds is made clear, and its statement turns out to be error, what is the standing of that reverence? Has it not been wasted?"

Abu Malik said: "No. Rather it returns as reverence to God and lowliness before His command, and its reward is yours even if the one before whom you humbled yourself did not deserve it. For the seeker has discharged the duty owed to the request, though the duty owed to the truth was not discharged for him. His likeness in this is the likeness of a petitioner who stands before some king to ask for his gift, and in his asking nothing is open to him but reverence, flattery, and pressing his complaint. If the king gives him some part of what he asked, the king has discharged the duty of the favor, and there is a reward for him in what he gave. And if he gives nothing, the petitioner has still discharged the duty of asking, by the gentleness of his request, and it is withholding that has bound the one who refused. So too the seeker of knowledge: it is upon him to begin with reverence, humility, and patience under trial until he discharges the duty of the request; then, after that, time will bring him whatever it brings."

They said: "This is the plain and manifest truth. Will you give us leave to go out to the people upon what you have counseled us, and not to overstep what you have commanded?"

Abu Malik said: "I would not counsel you to a thing and then be miserly with myself in it. Were it not for the fear of a fault in turning you back from the path of God, I would have counseled you to hold back while I went on ahead of you to the good, as an honor to myself and a preferring of my own soul over every soul. But we go together, and I comfort you with my own presence, as an honor to you and in thanks for the good you have singled me out to share."

They said: "It is as though you had been awaiting this affair before this day."

He said: "As for this very thing, no. But I have never ceased awaiting relief from God from every side, hoping for good by every road, inclining my ear toward every speaker, seeking increase from his words, whether he be in the right or in the wrong."

They said: "As for increase from the words of the one in the right, that we grant. But how do you hope for it from the words of the one in the wrong?"

Abu Malik said: "Increase of truth upon truth is light; and to know falsehood for what it is, from the one in the wrong, is increase in certainty. And the more certainty grows, the greater grows the light of the heart. None are saved with God but the people of certainty. I am rising now to go to the people. If you are resolved to rise with me, then look to yourselves: whoever knows a sin in himself, let him repent to God; whoever bears a debt, let him render it to its owner. Wash yourselves clean of defilement, put on for the request the purest garments of resolve, and bring to the sought thing the intention of one in need, for I myself am doing this. Our going out is a going out to God. If He knows truthfulness in us, He will grant us success to His truth; and if He knows in us mockery of the religion, He will turn us from His signs by the least abasement, in vindication of His friend against the mockers."

They said: "Yes. We will do what you have commanded, within three days."

He said: "No."

They said: "Within two days, then?"

He said: "No, only within this day of ours. If you do it, well; and if not, then I am going on."

They said: "Then we repent to God from every sin, and we discharge every debt, and we do what you have commanded. There is no strength save in God."

So they did this that very day, and they all went out together until they came to the gate of al-Bakhtari. When they had greeted him and were seated, Abu Malik said to al-Bakhtari: "O Abu Salih, how is your son Salih, and where is he?"

Al-Bakhtari said: "Salih today is my father, and I am the son of Salih; and Salih is with his Lord."

Abu Malik, supposing that Salih had died because his father said he was with his Lord, said:

> إنا لله وإنا إليه راجعون

"To God we belong, and to Him we return. Has Salih died?"

Al-Bakhtari said: "Salih has not died and will never die. He abides as long as time abides."

Abu Malik said: "Here are three matters whose meanings have been overturned: the son has become the father; the Lord is present upon the earth, and the Lord has a servant to whom one is brought from Him; and one created for perishing has become everlasting. Alas for seekers, at the confusion of speech. And behind this lies a further wonder."

Al-Bakhtari said: "O Abu Malik, come to your need, and be sure of your asking, for you are at the beginning of the trial."

Abu Malik said: "You have spoken truly. The beginning of the truth is a trial. My need is a meeting with the righteous servant."

Al-Bakhtari said: "There is no way to that. But perhaps you mean my son, Salih?"

He said: "Yes."

He said: "That is by his leave."

So the old man rose in haste until he came in upon his son Salih, and told him of Abu Malik and his companions, and he rejoiced at it, for he knew the excellence of their minds. He said: "O God, open for Your servants the hearing of the hearts, and guide them by Your mercy to the evidences of what is sought, and expand our breasts with the light of Your veiled light." Then he wiped his hand over his face, cleared the sitting place for them, and gave word to his father to bring them in.

When they had entered upon him, greeted him, and were seated, with Abu Malik and those below him taking their places in order of precedence, Salih drew Abu Malik near and seated him close to himself. The first thing Salih said was: "O Abu Malik, you have favored us with your visit and outstripped us to the good by your generosity; yet you had the greater right that we should visit you, to keep faith with your affection and to render the long-standing duty owed to you."

Abu Malik said: "When were you ever wanting in excellence, O Abu al-Khair? You were forbearing with us when we were small, and generous to us when we were grown, and you became among us a caller and a warner to the good. Your root is wholesome, your branch is high, your work is pure; so joy be to you."

Salih said to him: "Have you come to me suspecting, O Ka'b al-Ahbar, or imitating? Where is that complete mind and that excellent forbearance I knew in you?"

Abu Malik said to him: "Your rank is too high for suspicion, and the religion is too high for imitation."

Salih said to him: "Then how do you name me a warner, when the warner is a prophet, and the prophet is a proof from God between Him and His creation, for them and against them? How can he be a proof for you who is not a proof against you?"

Abu Malik said: "You have spoken truly; this is the due. We do not proceed in this upon the ease of speech; but we began with you by that which is fairer. I saw truthfulness as the fitter thing for one who has set himself up as a veil for the religion and a champion for the truth, and has opened its doors to seekers. We have come to know the excellence of the request, so we sought you, and hoped for you as the one sought. Come, then, to our need."

Salih said: "O Abu Malik, is not the one whose excellence you have come to know also one whose right has become binding upon you?"

He said: "Yes, it is binding."

He said: "Then when you came to know the excellence of the request, did you also come to know its due?"

He said: "And what is its due?"

Salih said to him: "To know its faces, and then to seek from among them that in which the truth is binding upon you."

Abu Malik said: "And what are its faces, and how many faces are they?"

Salih said to him: "The request is of three faces: a seeker who knows, a seeker who is coming to know, and a seeker who is only acquainting himself. Which of the three are you?"

Abu Malik said: "I am in need of knowing all three. How should I lay claim to a station I do not know?"

Salih said to him: "Then how did you claim to be a seeker at all, when you do not know the faces of the request?"

Abu Malik said: "I did not know them; so make them plain to me."

Salih said: "Wealth is the treasure of the people of this world, and knowledge is the treasure of the people of the Hereafter. The seeking of wealth is of three kinds: a seeker who seeks what he owns; a seeker who seeks a thing owed to him for a fixed term; and a seeker who seeks the gift of alms. In like manner, the seeking of the religion and the treasures of the Hereafter is of three faces. The first is a seeker who knows, and he is the divine scholar, who seeks out the dead in their ignorance to revive them with his knowledge. The second is a seeker who is coming to know, and he is the learner who has come to know some of the degrees of knowledge, and seeks its farthest reach and its end. The third is a seeker who acquaints himself, and he is the ignorant man made known by his ignorance, who knows nothing beyond his knowing that he is in need; so he seeks the scholars in order to learn. These are the three faces, and the descriptions of the seekers."

Abu Malik said: "Yes. These are the faces of the request, in the religion and in the world. And I am the seeker who acquaints himself, who knows nothing yet has come to know that he is in need. So teach me."

He said: "Did you come to know, or did you come to know by sight?"

Abu Malik said: "And what is the difference between the two?"

Salih said: "Knowledge is report; direct knowing is eye-witness."

Abu Malik said: "This too is among the things one has need to know."

Salih said: "As for report, that is the case of an ignorant man who does not know that he is ignorant until he meets a scholar, and the scholar informs him of his ignorance; then he knows, by the scholar's word, that he is an ignorant man in need of knowledge, and so his knowledge of his own ignorance became report. As for the knowing of need and ignorance by direct sight, that is the man who has come to know in his own mind that he is asked about a thing and does not know the face of its answer; his breast is straitened by ignorance, and he hopes for no relief except by knowledge of what he was ignorant of, and so his ignorance became a knowing through his own mind. Ignorance is want, and want is straitness, and straitness is a severe need that drives creatures to seek breadth; and the breadth is the breadth of knowledge, for knowledge is wide and spacious. By this I have come to know that you are in need."

Abu Malik said: "I find within myself all of this that you have described. I have come to know that I am in need, in severe want. So relieve my want by your excellence."

Salih said: "I see you hastening in the request before making sure."

Abu Malik said: "Want hastened me with its straitness, so I hastened; and I came to know the excellence of breadth, so I sought it."

Salih said to him: "If want has hastened you, and you have come to know the excellence of breadth, then how is it with you as to knowing the one sought?"

Abu Malik said: "Your excellence was made sound to me, so I made for you; and the meaning was hinted at, so I sought you."

Salih said to him: "And from where was it made sound to you that I, and not you, am known for breadth, so that you sought me? And from where did you know that I am the one sought, so that you made for me? No scholar versed in prophecy pointed you to me, that you might learn; nor have you seen in me the confirmations of the evidences, that you might know. Do you not see that you rise in the request only to the ease of imitation, and so wrong wisdom and wrong yourself, like one who measures with the underside of the measure? If he does not level it off, he wrongs his creditor; and if he does level it off, he wrongs himself. So it is with one who takes knowledge by imitation: if he acts upon it, he is not thanked; and if he leaves it, it is called unbelief. And you are, with me, of finer mind than that."

Abu Malik said: "I know indeed that it is so. But I had hoped that the truth is what establishes itself, and that I might spare you the making sure; for disputation breeds contention, and contention breeds rancor, since each adversary seeks the victory over his fellow. And when will the truth ever be attained by one who plunges into the defense of falsehood?"

Salih said to him: "Excellent is what you have said. The truth does establish itself by its own excellence and honor; and when quarreling grows great, it busies the hearts away from that which is worthier than it. But there is no forsaking what the scholars have laid down as their way, for they have said: every seed for which the earth is not first made sweet does not thrive; and every knowledge for which the hearts are not first purified by argument does not grow."

Abu Malik said: "Then bring forward what arguments you will. For the speech of wisdom is like the jewel: if you turn it, its light shines forth; and if you leave it, its excellence is not hidden."

Salih said to him: "You have spoken truly. But that is only when there is not, beside the jewel, a counterfeit of coated glass; for then the jewel is not hidden. Yet when claims grow many, and the tyrants of the world contend over the religion until they clothe it in what is not itself and coat it with the counterfeit before the seekers, it becomes binding upon the seeker then to put the jewel to the test, so that its excellence may be made plain and the coated thing and its people be exposed."

Abu Malik said: "You have dealt fairly and fulfilled the due of the truth. As for the counterfeit of these people, we have come to know it and its defects, and so we rejected it and held fast to this view by which we have come to know one another, and we said: this is the sound jewel of speech. We stood by it, and you agreed with us upon it, and we all clung together to its rope. So if you have found a meaning finer than it in its jewel, then, by my life, some jewels are finer than others. Show me, then, the excellence of your jewel, by a trial by which its excellence is known. As for our jewel, we have come to know it and have made it known; so say of it what appears to you."

Salih said to him: "As for your jewel, it was with us honored, in the rank of the sound jewel, until we submitted it to the jewelers and the people of the mines. They pronounced it false to us and would not accept it from us as a jewel, and said: this is coated glass. They showed us its defects, and it fell from the bounds of the jewel. For the jewel is raised up only by its preciousness, and by the knowledge of the people of insight into its excellence, and by their vying for it, until its price reaches every height. And when the jewelers have cast it down, who is there to know it besides them, or who will accept it thereafter save the people of delusion, who neither sell nor buy? So too is knowledge: it is not knowledge until it is submitted to those who know the knowledge of heaven. If they accept it, it is sound knowledge; and if they do not accept it, it is idle talk. And idle talk is not permitted in wisdom, nor do the wise accept it."

Abu Malik said: "And who are these who know the knowledge of heaven, after the prophets and the messengers? For we know of none of them in this our age, and the people have had no acquaintance with such since revelation by the Books was cut off; and the nations follow nothing now but the reports handed down. What, then, is your word in this?"

Salih answered him: "Any religion that is held to only by way of the reports handed down is not called a religion, for religion is one, upright, belonging to God by His right, and admits no difference within it. The reports, as you already know, are full of disagreement, each of them denying the other, and the religion of God is too precious for that."

Abu Malik said: "But when they differ over a report, they return to the Books. Whatever agrees with the Books they accept, and whatever contradicts them they call a lie."

Salih said to him: "And what is found in the Books only in a general form, who will make it particular for them? And what is found in detail, who will join it back to its principle for them? Who among them is trusted over God's lawful and His forbidden? I heard you on the day of the dispute, when a distressed man came to you and said, 'O Abu Malik, I swore an oath of divorce and then broke it.' You said to him, 'Some people say she has left you and is not lawful to you until she marries another husband, and some say she is still your wife and is not made forbidden to you by a single pronouncement.' The man said to you, 'Then which of the two shall I act upon?' You said to him, 'Act upon whichever of the two you wish.' So he rose from beside you not knowing whether he was going home to what is lawful or to what is forbidden. Is this how judgment is given in the religion of God? Is this how God's lawful and His forbidden are made known?"

Abu Malik said: "The sacred law of my age has been ruined and suspended and thrown into disorder, and we hold to nothing but the name of religion. What, then, is the state of religion with you?"

Salih said to him: "And what is religion, O Abu Malik?"

He said: "Command and prohibition, the lawful and the forbidden, the sunnahs and the obligations."

Salih said to him: "And what is the sacred law, in your view, other than this?"

Abu Malik said: "I know of nothing beyond what I have described to you."

Salih said to him: "Then you have gathered religion and the sacred law onto a single ground?"

He said: "Yes."

Salih said to him: "Then religion has been thrown into disorder by the suspension of the law and ruined by its ruin. What remains with you?"

Abu Malik held back from an answer for a while, his head bowed, pondering the matter of his religion. Then he raised his head and said: "I do not see that anything remains with me. So what is religion with you?"

Salih said to him: "Religion is with me, its veil made fast against the corrupters and its doors opened to the seekers. Noble is the one who reaches it first, and good attends the one who seeks it; through it works are honored, and through its justice the sacred laws come into flower. It is a cause joined from heaven to earth, unbroken and unsevered, the handhold of those who take hold and the rope of those who cling fast, the ark of tranquility and the ship of salvation and the light of life, noble in its content, safeguarded in its sciences, marked in its jewels, its excellence hidden from none who look upon it."

Abu Malik said: "If God has a religion by which He will judge on the Day, one with which He is pleased and for which He rewards and punishes, then in this sense it exists. But now that it has been lost, its description has become plainer to me than the thing described. So how is it described?"

Salih said to him: "The good comes only from the good, O Abu Malik. Set your light in the lamp-niche of your intellect and grasp what is intended for you. The claims of what is decreed have pulled at you, the robes of arrogance have made you forget yourself, and you have taken upon yourself what none but you can carry, together with the affairs of these people who follow you in good faith, who have listened to your word, accepted your guidance, and trusted your judgment. It is your duty to shield yourself and them from a fire you cannot escape passing over; so seek the way to be saved from it. You are the most favored of them in God's grace and the most bound to give Him thanks, for I have known you to recognize the good and to hope for its reward. As for what you asked, the confirming of that to which I have called you, it is my duty to establish it for you by whatever avenues you wish."

Abu Malik said: "All that you have said before this is proven and true. As for the reports of the common people, religion is not made sound by them, and they are not suited to it. There remains for us only the knowledge of what you call us to."

Salih said to him: "I call you to the justice of God and His oneness."

Abu Malik said: "Excellent is that to which you call. Indeed we hold this very saying, and we know its excellence above all doctrines."

Salih said to him: "It is not where your fancy has gone, O Abu Malik. That doctrine you mean is a describing of justice without knowledge of the One described, and a straying in the names of God without knowledge of His oneness. What I call you to is the knowledge of God's justice and the landmarks of His oneness. If you wish, I will halt you before the corruption of that saying, wherever you please."

Abu Malik said: "If the doctrine of the people of justice is corrupt and their affirming of oneness is a straying, then there is no firm truth upon the earth, unless the meaning be other than all the doctrines. Show us the corruption of justice and oneness, and from where it comes."

Salih said to him: "Justice and oneness are among the noblest of all doctrines. Oneness is too great to be corrupted, for oneness has no opposite; and justice is not corrupted, for it is the judgment between God and His creation. But the doctrines are all corrupted by the way men have corrupted the saying of justice and the rest, for it is a claim by which no statement is proved, and they do not encompass the knowledge of what they have said until they defer to the command of God. So defend whichever of the two you wish and take it as your own, until I strip it from you by the proof of the truth."

Abu Malik said: "I would not defend a saying I do not hold, nor one whose author asked this of me, nor have I set myself up as agent for his saying, nor am I a keeper over the author of the doctrines. I defend before you only my own saying. I say that God is One, nothing is like Him (ليس كمثله شيء), just in His ruling, who does not burden His servants with what they have no strength to bear. He commanded them and forbade them, yet He did not command by compulsion nor forbid by force; rather His command is a granting of choice and His prohibition a warning, so the obedient did not obey under coercion and the disobedient did not disobey as one overcome. This is my meaning and what I hold."

Salih said to him: "Did I not tell you that this doctrine is among the noblest? But these are names and attributes that avail nothing unless their meaning is present. It is as when you say, 'fire is hot': you have spoken truly, yet the truth does not avail you, for your saying 'fire is hot' is a name and an attribute whose mention does not avail, when no food is cooked by it and no darkness is lit by it apart from the meaning that fire exists. Were names and attributes to stand in things in the place of meanings, then the mention of fire would burn your tongue and the mention of food would fill you."

Abu Malik said: "As for the saying about these things, you have spoken truly, for their names and attributes avail nothing unless their meaning is present. So show me that my saying of justice and oneness are names and attributes that require the knowledge of a named thing that is described."

Salih said to him: "Your saying that God is One: 'one' (واحد) is four letters, and we see two of them joined and two of them apart. Which of them is 'one,' or are all of them together 'one'? And your saying 'Allah' (الله) is four letters: which of them is Allah, or are all of them together Allah, or are they signs pointing to God, or do you say otherwise?"

Abu Malik bowed his head for a while, then said: "I find no way out of this except that they are signs pointing to God."

Salih said to him: "Which of them points to God? And by what do they point to God, by their being apart or by their being joined? Or by some standing alone and some joined, as in your saying 'Allah' (الله), where the alif (الألف) stands alone and the three letters are connected? Which of them points to God, this one standing alone, or these joined together?"

Abu Malik said: "I do not know what to tell you about this. Take us on to another door."

Salih said to him: "Then the door to the knowledge of oneness has been closed before you by whoever spoke to you in this way, O Abu Malik."

He said: "Yes."

Salih said to him: "Then I ask you about your knowledge of God: from where is it, and from whom did you take it? Was it from an attribute that pointed you to One described?"

He said: "No."

Salih said: "Then from a name pointing to a named one?"

He said: "Yes."

Salih said: "And what is the name that pointed you?"

He said: "Allah."

Salih said: "Is 'Allah' a name, or the named?"

He said: "The named."

Salih said: "Then what is the name?"

He said: "Allah."

Salih said: "So 'Allah' is a name for Allah."

He said: "Yes."

Salih said: "Then what is the difference between the name and the named? Which of the two do you worship, or both?"

Abu Malik was bewildered for a while, pondering his answer, then said: "I find no way in this except that I have seen all the crafts, and for each craft there is a craftsman, and so I judged that this mighty creation has a Creator who resembles nothing of His creation, just as craftsmen do not resemble the things they make."

Salih said to him: "O Abu Malik, you have carried us away from the first meaning, and the narrowness in the meaning of oneness has driven you to seek Him by analogy and to strike likenesses for Him, and God is too precious for that. For everything made is made from some thing, yet God did not create things from any thing existing before them. These craftsmen you know only by having watched some of them with your own eye, how each one makes his work, and so you passed judgment upon a making whose maker was hidden from you by what you had seen of the like of it among the craftsmen you have watched and known, by way of analogy. But when God created, He did not make you witness the creation of what He made, nor was there for Him any like who makes as He makes; yet you have testified by the like that is present to the like that is absent. I have not known you, O Abu Malik, to be content with analogy in your religion; how then are you content to seek the knowledge of your Lord by measurement? Your saying, 'nothing is like Him' (ليس كمثله شيء), proves from your own words that He has a likeness and that nothing is like that likeness. Had you intended oneness, you would have said, 'He is not like anything' (ليس كهو شيء), and you would not have said, 'nothing is like Him' (ليس كمثله شيء). Glory be to the One whom the reach of high resolves cannot grasp by thought, and whom the straining of the pointing hand cannot attain by perception; rather He is exalted high above the description of those who are charged with duty. O Abu Malik, had you aimed this quest at one of His servants, or at a king among His angels, you would have failed to grasp what may be said of him and how he was created; how then the One who created him? The lights of the people of the earth fall short of grasping the light of the sun, and the sun is but one of the lights of this world; so how shall the one who falls short of grasping a servant of His grasp the Creator? Say what seems right to you."

Abu Malik bowed long to the ground in his bewilderment, finding no path to speech.

Salih said to him: "O Abu Malik, raise your head and say what has come to you of speech."

Abu Malik said: "Speech is gone, and there is no speech; the minds are bewildered before the answer, the fancies are struck dumb before thought, and the instrument is too weak to grasp by it anything but seeking and asking."

Salih said: "O Abu Malik, One of such greatness, do the eyes grasp Him?"

He said: "No, He is higher than that."

Salih said: "Is speech heard from Him?"

He said: "There is no speech except from a speaker, and the speaker is described by speech, and God is too great for attributes."

Salih said to him: "This Vast One of greatness, the High One of power, would He be unjust in His creation, charging them with what they cannot bear and commanding them with what they cannot grasp, and then punishing them if they do not do it?"

Abu Malik said: "Whoever charges the obedient one with what he has no strength to bear has charged him with disobedience, and to punish him for it is aggression, and God is too precious for that."

Salih said: "O Abu Malik, One so far above wrongdoing and too great for aggression, would He be content that ignorance should be a companion for His servants?"

Abu Malik said: "Ignorance is a sure denial of thanksgiving and a persisting in disbelief, and God does not love the disbelievers, all of them."

Salih said: "Were they, then, created as knowers, already knowing what is wanted of them?"

He said: "No, but He created them ignorant, knowing nothing."

Salih said: "Then from where do they come to know what He wants, so that it becomes binding upon them to please Him, and they are guarded from His wrath?"

Abu Malik said that they had learned this from God.

Salih said: "If He is higher than sight can reach, wider than perception can grasp, and greater than the limits of speech, then how does the one whom He commands and forbids come to know Him, so as to obey rather than disobey, and to give thanks rather than deny, unless you accept that there are causes for this knowledge set between Him and His creation? Must there not be those who deliver His justice and bear witness to His witnessing, for the servants and against them, whom He has chosen by His knowledge and selected out of all His creation, and made messengers to them and proofs against them, so that they stand as the just ones between Him and His servants, conveying His command and His prohibition, so that obedience to them is obedience to God and His good pleasure, and disobedience to them is His wrath and His punishment? For otherwise, how could the command reach creation from God?"

Abu Malik said: "This, for me, belongs to the foundations of justice. Once creation stands in need of God's justice to be taught what He wants of them, and this comes through the hands of the righteous one He has appointed, then each shall thank his benefactor, be rewarded by Him as He wills, be reproached by Him, and be punished by Him if He wills."

Salih said to him: "Is it permissible to rule with justice in any manner other than this?"

He said: "It is permissible for none but God, and God is the wisest of judges."

Salih said: "Would it be permissible in His judgment that He lay a charge upon some of His creation and guide them to it through one of His just ones, and then lay that very same charge upon others, yet not guide them to it through one of His just ones as He guided the first?"

Abu Malik said: "The judgment can be only one, because justice is one. Were the ruling divided into two meanings, one of the two would inevitably fall into wrong."

Salih said to him: "Has it not been established for you that the judgment of God is one, that there is no difference within it, and that His practice is one, unaltered in His messengers? Were it not for the succession of the messengers and the standing of the command and the prohibition, the Truth would have no growth and creation no meaning."

He said: "Yes, this is the truth."

Salih said: "Is not this succession of theirs from God to His creation appointed so that He may make obedience binding upon them and impose it as a duty, and so that He may set them as an authority between Himself and His creatures, to bear witness for those who obey them, and so accept them, and to bear witness against those who disobey them, and so turn away from them?"

Abu Malik said: "The judgment could not have been otherwise; else He would have belied His messengers and broken His promise, and God is too generous for that. The affair of the messengers has made plain that they are of His justice, and that the servants worship Him by obeying them. But tell me of the ranks of these just ones before God: are they equal, or have some of them a merit above others?"

Salih said to him: "How could they be equal in rank when they are bound to render obedience one to another? Were they equal in rank, none of them would obey another. Rather, by His justice He has preferred some of them above others, so that obedience may pass from the lesser to the greater, and from the greater to one greater still, until obedience and worship reach through them the utmost limit that God has willed."

Abu Malik said: "Is the one who obeys the virtuous one among them, and the virtuous one himself, equal in reward?"

He said: "Yes, this belongs to the justice of God, which makes his due reward binding for every obedient one; and one is not asked about the rank of a virtuous one or a lesser one."

Abu Malik said: "Can anyone resemble the virtuous ones?"

He said: "That cannot be, because they come from the Creator bearing knowledge of the unseen in the Books He has sent down to them, and none is able to bring the like of it except from God."

He said: "Then can anyone imitate the lesser one among them, when no Book is sent down to him in which knowledge of the unseen is revealed?"

He said: "No one is able to lay claim to that station."

He said: "And how is that, when they did not display the seven creations of the unseen as the virtuous one himself displayed them?"

Salih said to him: "If the virtuous one has set himself apart from others by the knowledge of the unseen in the revelation sent down, then the lesser one likewise has set himself apart from others by the knowledge of the unseen in the interpretation. Both its sending down and its interpretation are from God, and what is with God is attained only through inspiration."

Abu Malik said: "And is the lesser one also inspired?"

He said: "Yes, and for that reason obedience to him became obedience to God."

Abu Malik said: "If you set him level with the virtuous one in obedience and in inspiration, why then do you call him the lesser one?"

Salih said: "By reason of his obedience to the virtuous one and his need of him."

Abu Malik said: "And what need has he of the creature, when the inspiration comes to him from the Creator?"

Salih said to him: "Even though the inspiration comes to him, it comes bearing the interpretation of the reports and the Books, and the remainder of what the virtuous ones have left behind; as it was revealed to Lot at the hands of Abraham, so that Abraham's merit was made plain; and as it was revealed to Ishmael and to Isaac, so that the goodness, the blessings, and the merit accrued to Abraham. Thus the inspiration from God is joined to His just ones in His earth, according to the measure of their ranks. He does not will, in His justice, to cut off His command and His prohibition and His worship from His servants after He has favored them with it, for then worship would be severed and ignorance made lawful."

Abu Malik said: "The bearer of the revelation has shown his truthfulness by the knowledge of the unseen that is in his Book. By what, then, does the bearer of the interpretation show that the knowledge of the unseen is with him, so that revelation bears witness for him and makes obedience to him uniquely binding, as it did for the bearer of the revelation?"

Salih said to him: "He comes in his interpretation with knowledge from heaven and a declaration from the highest assembly, and the Book bears witness to it."

Abu Malik said: "Then I bear witness to what the Books have borne witness to: that every interpretation to which the revelation testifies is a revelation from God; and otherwise the Books and the messengers would all have to be belied, since their meaning is one."

Then he said to him: "If some of them give the lie to others, all must be reckoned liars; and if some of them confirm others, all must be confirmed."

Abu Malik said to him: "Thus is justice, and by this the truthfulness is known, and the merit of the one who confirms it. He has made obedience binding to the possessors of firm resolve among the messengers, who are His just ones in His creation, and He charged them with the conveying of His message. If, then, obedience is binding by prophethood, by the revelation, and by the interpretation, what of these who sit in their councils in resemblance of them, yet are not of them? They have all agreed that after their prophet there is no prophet, and no warner in their time, and that they themselves are the upholders of the command of God and of His religion, and that this saying of theirs leads to God's reward or His punishment. Yet you say otherwise. How, then, could the scholars of this community have neglected to consider this age?"

Salih said to him: "They have not left it untouched, but they have not considered it with a consideration that would be sound for them, nor has anyone spoken to them of it, that they might incline to it with their minds; for it has been made to soak into their hearts that they are the worthiest of the command of God and His religion, and that there is no prophet after their prophet, just as those said who came before them among the seducers of the bygone nations. So every nation has claimed that there is no prophet after its own prophet."

Abu Malik said: "And what was the beginning of that, and what did they intend by it?"

Salih said to him: "Its beginning was three in every age: a rebellious devil, a stubborn tyrant, and a dissembling jurist. By this saying they sought to cut off the traces of the prophets from the marks of prophethood, and to make it a dominion passed round among themselves. This they could not accomplish until they had made the hearts of the nations drink in the saying, addressed to every nation, that God has not sent one better than your prophet, and that after him there is no prophet and no warner. So they whispered to them from a quarter they did not perceive, and turned them away from a direction they did not know. Every nation clung to its own prophet by belying whoever came after him, supposing that this drew them nearer to their prophet; and every nation claimed that every prophet after its own was a liar. Thus, by this saying, the mention of prophethood and the traces of the prophets were cut off from all the nations together, so that they neither sought what they had lost, nor would they have believed had they sought it. So the seducing devils gained mastery over the misguided and seduced them; the tyrants gained the power to kill the prophets and killed them; and the doubt-sowers among the corrupt jurists adorned themselves, and were destroyed and destroyed others. All of this because the hearts of creation would not incline toward the prophets when they appeared, nor seek them out when they were lost. So their despair of the prophets reached such a point that when the prophets called them to the truth openly, they were belied, and the prophets were killed among them, and they felt no anger.

"Behold the nation of the Magi, clinging to its tyranny, worshipping its idols, keeping vigil over its fires, saying by their own claim that God will never send a messenger after their messenger. And behold the nation of the Torah, clinging to its scripture, bent over its calf, standing by the bounds of its prophets, humbling itself before its Lord of judgment, saying by their claim that God will never raise a messenger after Moses. And behold the nation of the Gospel, speaking by its likening, claiming that its prophet is its God, drawing near by its crosses, glorifying its rabbis, saying that God will send no messenger after Jesus. And behold your own community: it has been led along the paths of those before it, and whoever went astray has led it astray; so it inherited the Book from other than its rightful people, walked with the religion along other than its own way, followed its great ones in error, obeyed its masters in misguidance, and its jurists humbled themselves before the tyrants, so that they established a religion for the people other than the received practice, performing the prayer at other than its time and giving the alms otherwise than by its right. They are banded together in patience with them, persistent in their need of them, certain of their independence from any other, saying that God will send no messenger and no warner after their Messenger. So you and your like followed the saying of the great multitude, and what won you over was the abundance of consensus and the coercion of the sultans. You accepted it from them, adorned it with the fairness of your doctrines, gave it preference with the fineness of your phrasing, and set up upon it an argument from justice by which no argument can be raised against you. So you held forth, upheld from above by the mighty ones for the sake of prestige, while those beneath you followed you out of desire, all supposing that this was the truth. If, then, this community must be believed in what it has decreed against God, that His proofs have been cut off from His creation by a single strand of consensus, then the bygone nations are the worthier of being believed, for they possess three qualities by which the argument against you is established, and by which the truth is theirs rather than yours."

Abu Malik said: "And what are those qualities in which their merit over us is such that their confirmation becomes binding, if their saying is true, or their belying, if their saying is crooked?"

Salih said to him: "Their saying is like your saying, and your saying is of the same kind as theirs; your hearts have come to resemble one another, and yet the merit in the argument is theirs over you. As for the first quality, they say that they are earlier to the religion of God than you, so theirs is the merit of the foremost. The second, that the consensus, both theirs and yours, is upon confirming what is with them, so theirs is the merit of the truthful. The third, your own testimony for them that their prophets are truthful and that they are the messengers of God, while not one of them has testified for your prophet, neither to prophethood nor to truthfulness. So theirs is the merit over you, by your agreement with them together with their standing apart from you; for they say that what is with you of knowledge is copied from their knowledge, and that theirs is the root of your knowledge. This is their argument against you, clear in speech, plain in its justice. If, then, the ruling of creation is binding upon the Creator, the ruling of these people is the more binding. But if the Creator is the One who rules over His creation, abrogating what He wills and confirming what He wills, with none to overturn His ruling, then, by my life, the saying of all of them falls away, and the Creator is foremost over creation and over the command, and every day He is engaged in some affair:

كل يوم هو في شأن

Every day He is engaged in some affair.

His act is not to be denied, though He were to send a warner every day."

Abu Malik said: "This community has folded itself into a far-reaching misguidance."

Salih said to him: "Most of the ancients went astray before them; for they cast off the friends of God, stripped them of their right, and let the foolish among them prevail over their affair. They made lawful what God had forbidden, and this deliberately, lest any should resemble prophethood, so that people would long for the prophets, and lest, when that longing came, they should be slow to kill them. Instead they diverted the people with sovereignty and largesse, and drew them along in agreement with their own whims. And there helped them in this those who were known outwardly for truthfulness and piety, showing themselves off before the people, until the very one who had commanded the community to the good turned it away from the good, and the very one who had forbidden it the wrong pointed it toward the wrong; and the people of loyalty to the friends of God grew few, so that truth and falsehood came round in turns, like light and darkness in their alternation. Were it not for the remnants of God's friends and His just ones in the earth, and the inspiration that comes to them, the little of truth would never prevail over the much of falsehood. For otherwise, by what would God distinguish the foul from the good, save by His just ones? And how would the argument of God reach all of creation, save through them?"

Abu Malik said: "There is no way, and no escape, for the justice of God and His command, amid the multitude of His adversaries, save through the just ones of God; and by them the argument from God to His creation is made sound."

Salih said to him: "Then, once it is established that the company of the prophets of God are the just ones, is not each single one of them called just?"

He said: "Indeed, he is a just one, as it is said:

شهد فلان وهو عدل من العدول

So-and-so testified, and he was one of the just."

Salih said to him: "It is established for you, then, that the just one is the friend of God, and that this has passed beyond your saying of mere attribution, and he has become one who is himself described?"

He said: "Yes, this is sound as a saying."

Salih said to him: "And whoever does not attain the one who is described, does the description profit him?"

He said: "The description does not profit him except by attaining the one who is described."

him could take no comfort in the description while the one described remained beyond his reach.

Salih said: "Then whoever does not know the just one of Allah and His guardian, will his speech be just?"

The narrator said: Abu Malik smiled and said: "You have brought me out of my chapter on justice and monotheism, and nothing has brought me out of it but the truth itself, in the clearest of arguments and the gentlest of speech. After this, what chapter remains to be proved?"

Salih said to him: "From here, O Abu Malik, the counterfeit glass falls away from those who trade in the colors of jewels. They could not recognize its worth, and when it is set before those who know the gem, it is exposed, and those deceived by them are exposed along with it."

Abu Malik said: "I have believed, and I have acted upon it. But how shall we be safe from the nations that lead astray, and how shall we know the causes of Allah, or even one cause among them, when we do not reach them in their own time, and in our time there are neither prophets nor messengers, as there were in the nations before us?"

Salih said to him: "Glory be to Allah, O Abu Malik. You have not ceased to speak of the justice of Allah through a stretch of your life, yet the moment We reasoned with you to establish it for you where it is binding upon you, you turned back to speak of tyranny toward Allah, and to attribute injustice to Him."

Abu Malik said: "Allah forbid. I do not say that, for whoever says it has transgressed and has forged a lie against Allah. From where have you held me bound to that?"

Salih said: "From your own saying that the age of the prophets has passed you by, and that in this time of ours there is neither prophet nor messenger. Was the sending of those in the first ages a justice from Him toward His servants, or was it in vain?"

He said: "Allah forbid that it be anything but justice from Him."

Salih said: "Then what has taken us out of that justice? Are we not His servants as they were, and a creation like their creation, bound in service as they were bound in service? Has He then dealt unjustly with us after His justice toward them, so that we who come after them are excused? Or has His giving run dry in this time of ours, so that we cling to the bounty of what was given to them, and He is content with that from us? Or has the way of Allah changed in our case after it stood firm in those who came before us, so that we should pronounce upon Allah that He has changed? Or is His justice a right owed to us and His judgment binding upon us, as His way was with those before us, since there is no altering the words of Allah and none to reverse His judgment? And who deserves the name 'the just' except the guardians of Allah? Who among all that Allah created is just, except the one Allah chose, so that by His command he became just? For this reason the ruling is named just, on account of the justness of the one who ruled by it, and the truth is named truthful, on account of the truthfulness of the one who uttered it."

Abu Malik said: "It cannot be other than so, and it is not permitted to us to say of Allah anything but the truth. Yet we followed our speech to our own opinion and ruled by the absence of the righteous from among us. If that is part of the ruling of Allah and His way with us, as was His way with those before us in the interregna between one messenger and the next, like the period that lay between the religion of Abraham and the time of Moses and the Torah, and the period that lay between the Torah and the time of Jesus and the Gospel, then we have been upon that same footing, working through the interregnum, holding fast to the traditions and the way."

Salih said to him: "The religion of Allah is too precious to Him for that. What interregnum was there after the religion of Abraham, when Allah sent after him Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Jonah, and Shu'ayb, leading on to Moses? Then He inspired them with the doing of good deeds, the call to the religion of Abraham, the establishment of the prayer according to its way, and the giving of the zakat according to its obligations, until the ruling of the Torah was sent down upon Moses. And what interregnum was there after the religion of Moses, between it and the Gospel, when Allah sent after Moses Joshua son of Nun, and Elias, Talut, David, Solomon, Zechariah, and John, leading on to Jesus? They upheld the ruling of the Torah, holding to its religion, calling to it and reciting its Psalms, until the ruling of the Gospel was sent down upon Jesus. So it was with those who passed before them and those who came after them, and many generations between, whom none knows but Allah. Their messengers came to them with clear proofs, and to each Allah set forth parables, and each was called to the truth. So where is this interregnum, and when was it? Allah Himself said,

عَلَى فَتْرَةٍ مِنَ الرُّسُل

'after a succession of messengers,' and so He affirmed both the messengers and the interval. The interval fell upon the rising of the call after fear, so they called in secret and concealment. Why then would they be cut off from the earth? The earth is not left, for the blink of an eye, without the just one of Allah, either present and manifest, or fearful and veiled."

Abu Malik said: "This is a statement that cannot be turned back, a word established beyond doubt. As for the establishing of the proof of Allah through justice and the succession of messengers, that has been settled and made clear. What then is the meaning of their absence in this time of ours? What is its cause?"

Salih said to him: "What was the cause of the absence of Moses, when he went out from the city of Pharaoh and passed beyond it in fear and watchfulness, until he reached the land of Shu'ayb?"

Abu Malik said: "He fled from the people of Pharaoh, hiding himself from them, because he feared for his life when they had agreed upon killing him."

Salih said to him: "Have you heard, in this law of yours, of any one of the just, the just of Allah and the sons of His prophets, who was killed for the causes of the religion while enduring it with patience?"

Abu Malik said: "Yes. A great many of them have been killed."

Salih said to him: "Then which of the just of Allah do you want to appear to you and to the people of your time? One who was killed, whom Allah raises up and sends to you after death? Or one fleeing from among you, whom you would drive back to the killing he escaped, when he has followed the way of Allah in His prophets and messengers, patient under fear and in concealment, until Allah judges between him and us, and He is the best of judges?"

Abu Malik said: "There is no blame upon one who follows the way of the righteous, fearing for his life and fleeing from his people. The blame is upon the wicked nations and the helpers of the powers of darkness, for their killing of the prophets of Allah and His just ones, and for their agreeing together upon that."

Salih said: "Then what do you say of one who kills the just of Allah and His proof? Where is his end, and where is the end of the one who helped him?"

Abu Malik said: "To the Fire, and with him whoever blackened an ink for them or dipped a pen for them in their cause."

Salih said to him: "And what do your jurists say of this?"

Abu Malik said: "They call for mercy upon the slain, yet they see no disobedience in the killer."

Salih said to him: "And whoever obeys the killer of his own accord, is he not a partner with him in the blood of the slain?"

Abu Malik said: "Yes, by Allah. He is his partner, and worse than he."

Salih said to him: "His partnership is known by his aiding him. From where, then, did he become worse than him?"

Abu Malik said: "Were it not for the narrations of the jurists, the kingship of the tyrants could never have been established through the foolish."

Salih said to him: "You have spoken the truth, O Abu Malik. Have you then been content for yourself with their kingship as a home, their land as a dwelling, their soldiers as neighbors, and their guardsmen as intimate companions, while you are their just one, judging for them by your knowledge, settling your needs by their kingship? You called upon them in your petition, and thanked them for what you sought. You clothed their tyranny in the crown of your justice, and they grew proud; you dressed their pride in the garment of the sacred law, and they went to excess; you lit up their falsehood with the dimming of your truth. So the weak were misled by your conduct, and those among the scholars who were like you, and they said, 'These are the jurists of the community; in them we have a pattern to follow.' You went astray by your own reasoning, and you led others astray by the reasoning of the tyrants. You supposed the one held to account was someone other than yourselves, and you forgot the word of Allah, glory be to Him:

وَلاَ تَرْكَنُوْا إِلَى الذِيْنَ ظَلَمُوْا فَتَمَسَّكُمُ النَّارُ

'And do not incline toward those who have done wrong, lest the Fire touch you.' So you severed what Allah commanded to be joined, and you saw it as you did so. You spoke of the outward forms of justice while wandering from its path. You came upon His saying,

أَطِيْعُوْا اللهَ وَأَطِيْعُوْا الرَّسُوْلَ وَأَوْلِي الأَمْرِ مِنْكُمْ

'Obey Allah, and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you,' and you claimed that these powers of darkness are those in authority over you, even though they disbelieve. And you forgot the word of Allah, exalted is He,

قَاتِلُوا الذِيْنَ يَلُونَكُمْ مِنَ الكُفَّار

'Fight those of the disbelievers who are near you.' So you disobeyed the one you were commanded to obey, and obeyed the one you were commanded to fight, all the while reckoning that you were doing well. Error carried you far, and opinion led you on, until you said that Allah will not raise up in your age a messenger nor a warner. Is it that the goodness of your deeds has set you beyond need of the guardians of Allah for the proof? Or that the ugliness of your deeds has cut off your hope in Allah for repentance? Or is it justice that you should judge upon Allah without justice, and be content for your Messenger with what you would not be content with for yourselves in his place? Would any one of you accept that his own child be killed, or that he be driven out of his house and his property without right?"

Abu Malik said: "No one is content with that. Were the child of any one of us killed, or he driven out from his property, he would be angered, and his people would be angered for him, and they would not be satisfied until he was satisfied."

Salih said to him: "Then Allah and His Messenger have the greater right that you be angry for their anger, when His vicegerent is killed and His guardian is driven out of the land."

Abu Malik said: "It is grievous to us that the guardian of Allah be killed among us or driven from his land. But they have deliberately kept themselves absent from us, and the tyrants have gained the upper hand over us."

Salih said to him: "Had you supported the guardians of Allah, they would have appeared; had you forsaken His enemies, they would not have prevailed. But the judgment binds you toward the guardians of Allah upon three stations. There is the striker set in power, who does not tire of pursuit and is not satisfied even when he has triumphed. There is the contented one who gloats, biting his fingertips in rage, never slackening in inciting against their enemy, never heedless of them even when they are heedless. And there is the one who hates their deed and holds to love for the guardians of Allah, yet forsakes them, having no clear sight in faith and no trust to be relied upon in keeping the secret. For this reason the guardians of Allah have kept themselves absent from you, by Taqiyya, out of caution before you and before this great mass who have taken their desires as their god, thrown themselves upon the goods of this world, and aided one another in the killing of the prophets. They were ignorant of the fault in their conduct, so they did not hold themselves to account for the injustice they did, nor humble themselves to repent for what they had done. Instead they returned the injustice of their deeds to Allah and ascribed it to Him, and they said, 'Allah will not raise up, after their messenger, a messenger nor a warner.' So they made lawful the killing of the guardians of Allah without right. They wronged in the deed, they swerved in the word, and they transgressed by their denial."

Abu Malik said: "Yes. This is the very description of this nation of ours, and this is their saying. Where then is the salvation from this, and where is the escape from the punishment of Allah?"

Salih said to him: "To Allah. Seek refuge in Him, and He grants you refuge; turn to Him in repentance, and He accepts the repentance from you; ask Him for the way of His guardians, and He delivers you by your deeds from the deeds of those who have done wrong. So too is this mass of people: if they repented to Allah, He would accept their repentance, and what would Allah do then with punishing them? For Allah does not wrong them by the weight of an atom, even when they are unjust and lie, and He does not hasten them to punishment, even when they hasten to disobedience."

Abu Malik said: "Then I seek refuge from the punishment of Allah by repenting to Him and seeking the means to what pleases Him. Show favor to the one who seeks you, and to the one who asks to be led, by guiding him, and your Lord will reward you."

Salih said to him: "If you do that, you will find Allah nearer to pardoning you than you are to repenting to Him, for He loves those who turn to Him in repentance. And you will find me eager in your guidance, watchful over you."

Then the eyes of Salih welled with tears at the mention of repentance, and he broke off his speech in the fullness of his feeling. He gave them heart and bade them go to the house of his father. He went in to his father, the scholar who had first sent him out, and took counsel with him concerning the affair of Abu Malik and his companions.

The scholar said: "You know your companions best. If you know good in them, guide them with your guidance; and when you warn them, let no eagerness move you to impose ta'zir upon them. Test them by leaving them to themselves, without harshness, and bid your father show kindness to them and deal righteously with them in due season. The one who inclines toward good is not hidden, and Allah will not abandon you behind your back. He will open for you, from the light of His enabling grace, that by which the affair of His creation is set in order, and from the gates of guidance that by which He shows you the deeds of those who are rightly guided."

He said: So Abu Malik and his companions went to and fro in their testing until their affair was fulfilled and they knew their own guidance, and the guardian of Allah among them inclined kindly toward their guidance. They thanked Allah for that, and returned to their people as warners, and Allah guided through them a great many of His servants to His religion.

The author said: Whatever in this account took the form of a narrative was no idle telling, but a confirmation of what Allah has commanded, for in it is the steadfastness of the messengers, the signs of the guardians, and the manners of the seekers.

Praise be to Allah, first and last. May Allah bless His Messenger, our master Muhammad, sent forth from Him to His creation as a bearer of good tidings and a warner; and His guardian, the Imam of the God-fearing, the leader of the radiant and honored, the beloved of the Lord of the Worlds; and the Imams from the people of his house, upon whom Allah has bestowed His favor, from whom He has removed all defilement, and whom He has purified completely. Allah is sufficient for us, and how excellent a Trustee, how excellent a Master, and how excellent a Helper. There is no power and no strength except by Allah, the Most High, the Most Great.
