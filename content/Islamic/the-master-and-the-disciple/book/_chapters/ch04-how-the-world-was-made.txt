# How the World Was Made

The first thing the Master said to him by way of explanation, concerning the first of the manifest causes and the beginning of their creation, was this. God is the Creator of things and the One who composes them. He created them and originated them. He is not the first of every first created thing, having taken that as an evidence for what He created; rather He is the First of all firsts and the Inheritor of every existing thing. He began the creation of what He created out of light, and from that light three words branched. The first of them is the will; from the will branched the command; and from the command branched the saying, that He says "Be" to what He wills, and it is. So the first of creation was a will, then a command, then a saying.

> فابتدأ خلق ما خلق من نور تفرّع منه ثلاث كلمات. أولها إرادة، وتفرّع من الإرادة الأمر، وتفرّع من الأمر القول لما يشاء كن فيكون. فكان أول الخلق إرادة أمر بقول.
>
> He began the creation of what He created out of a light from which three words branched. The first of them is the will; from the will branched the command; and from the command branched the saying, that He says "Be" to what He wills, and it is. So the first of creation was a will, a command, by a saying.

These are three words, and from them is derived كن (Kun), which is two letters, and فيكون (fa-yakun), which is five letters. That is seven letters, and from them seven things branch. From the light He created the air, which is the first of them: singular without consort, unique among things, mingling with them and surrounding them, yet not surrounded by any of them. It is the proof of the One concerning whom "there is nothing like Him."

> فذلك ثلاث كلمات تفرع منها كن، حرفين، فيكون، خمسة أحرف، فذلك سبعة أحرف تفرع منها سبعة أشياء. خلق من النور الهواء، وهو أولها، فرد عن الأزواج، فريد عن الأشياء، ممازج لها، محيط بها، ولا محيط به شيء منها. فهو دليل على من ليس كمثله شيء.
>
> These are three words from which كن (Kun) is derived, two letters, and فيكون (fa-yakun), five letters; that is seven letters from which seven things branch. From the light He created the air, which is the first of them: singular without consort, unique among things, mingling with them, surrounding them, yet not surrounded by any of them. It is the proof of the One concerning whom there is nothing like Him.

Then out of these three words He created water, darkness, and light, the light of the heavens and the earth. Then He created smoke out of the water, He created the condensation of heat out of the darkness, and He created fire out of the light. These are three things branching from the three things before them, and each became a consort to its origin because it was created from it. The seventh, the air, holds sway over them all. This was the beginning of creation and the beginning of things.

When creation had been established upon seven principles, and the course of forms had been laid down upon likeness and analogy, He created seven heavens and their signs out of the smoke, and He created seven earths and their sustenances out of the condensation of heat. These are the seven branches from those seven origins.

Then the Master turned to the signs of the heavens and the sustenances of the earth: what they are, and what bears witness to them. He returned in the saying to the three words and their letters from which the seven were created, which are "the will to command by a saying."

> فرجعنا في القول إلى الكلمات الثلاث وحروفهن التي منها خلق السبعة، وهي إرادة أمر بقول.
>
> So we returned in the saying to the three words and their letters from which the seven were created, which are: a will, a command, by a saying.

That is twelve letters. Their signs in the heaven are the twelve constellations, and their witnesses are seven kingdoms; their signs upon the earth are twelve islands, and their witnesses are seventeen seas. Such are the signs of the heavens and the earth, which people pass by while they turn away from them.

Then the heavens and the earth were sealed together in darkness from the black heat, which belongs to the dominion of the earth. Then He rent them apart with His lights, and the light and the darkness, the two contrary realities, were mingled. He set a division between them by the night and the day, as two signs. He made the night, and its sign, a course of darkness, and He apportioned it; and He made the day, and its sign, a course of light, and He apportioned it. The night is dim with its darkness, and the moon and the stars are its sign; the day is radiant with its light, and the seeing of the sun is its sign. From the two of them the days were brought forth, alternating between the night and the day, and they are seven days. Their signs are that each day of them has twelve hours, and each night of them has twelve hours.

When the creation of things was made complete upon these principles, He made them pairs, male and female. He did not mean male and female in the bodily sense, but rather in the sense of the superior and the subordinate. Then the custom went forth of creating in pairs, and out of their substances He created every living thing: humankind, beasts, birds, and crawling creatures. Then this custom extended into speech, and speech too became pairs: attributes and things described, names and meanings. Glory be to Him who created all the pairs and made them the outward showings of His command, the likenesses of His religion, and the proofs of His power. So He pointed by things to their Originator, and by the creation to its Maker.

Then He rent it apart with His light, opened it with His command, created it by His knowledge, and sealed it, out of His names, with the letters of "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."

> بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم
>
> In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.

When He had perfected all of them by His making and joined them into pairs by His measure, He chose for Himself, out of the purest of His will and the depths of His unseen, an abundant knowledge and a manifest light, and made the two of them a religion by which God is served and by which nearness to Him is sought. Then He honored it with the sacred laws and the books, and made it known by the likenesses and the customs. Then He raised up for it, from the choicest of His creation, banners and proofs and rulers, and made them the houses of His prophethood, the treasuries of His knowledge and wisdom, and the interpreters of His revelation. He set them as His successors in His earth, guides to the path of deliverance in what is outward and what is inward. Among them are the imams and the speakers (nutaqa); among them are the proofs (hujaj) and the chiefs (nuqaba); and among them are the summoners to the good and the learned. In their stations they are like the stars of heaven, by whom the works are set aright and in whom the likenesses reach their end. Their imam is like the great sun among the lights, veiled by its own coverings from the sight of eyes, and he is its outward showing and its likeness. The proof and the gate of their imam is like the shining moon, and he is its outward showing and its likeness. Their summoners are like the radiant stars (zawahir), and they are its outward showings and its likenesses.

All the pairs that the Master had described are outward and inward, likeness and thing likened. The outward of the seven heavens are likenesses, and their inward are the seven speakers, the messengers of firm resolve. The seven earths are outwardly likenesses, and their inward are the imams of guidance. For each speaker among them there are twelve chiefs, and their likeness from the seven heavens are the twelve constellations. Likewise, for each imam of the imams of guidance there are twelve proofs by whom God's argument is established upon his people, and their likeness in the earth are the twelve islands.

> حجة يحتجّ بها على قومه، ومثلها من الأرض اثنتا عشرة جزيرة.
>
> Proofs by which the argument is established upon his people, and their likeness in the earth are twelve islands.

The islands are the dwellings of the chiefs, and on every island there stands a proof. For every proof there are summoners, many and few. God increases in creation what He wills, so that the argument may reach its end, the argument of God upon His creation, that people may have no argument against God after the messengers.

Through these causes, the causes of God that the Master had described, the argument of God reaches His creation, and His justice appears among them, outwardly and inwardly. The inward is the religion of God by which the friends of God have been bound; the outward is the sacred laws of the religion and their likenesses. So the religion became to the laws a soul and a spirit, and the laws became to the religion a body and a proof. There is no standing for the body except by the soul, for the soul is its life; and there is no standing for the soul except by the body, for the body is its vessel. Likewise the outward things, the sacred laws and the rest, have no standing except by the inward religion, because it is their light and their meaning, the spirit of life within them; and the inward has no standing except by the outward, because the outward is its body and its proof. The outward is the knowledge of this world, and nothing is seen except by it; the inward is the knowledge of the hereafter, and nothing is seen except by it. There is not a single letter of the letters of the inward, nor a single friend of the friends of God, but that its witness in the outward is many, because of the abundance of the likenesses and the breadth of the sacred laws.

The discourse upon this is long and grows ever greater, and the more its answer multiplies from the words of wisdom, the more its rightness is hidden, and its end makes one forget its beginning, because of its preciousness and the purity of its essence. For the light of wisdom overcasts one part of itself with another, as the light of the sun overcasts the light of the moon and the stars and dims them. So too do the words of wisdom adorn the tongue of the man who is wise.
