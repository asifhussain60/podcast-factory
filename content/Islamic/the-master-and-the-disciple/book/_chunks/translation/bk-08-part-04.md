They learned this from God, said Abu Malik.

Salih answered: If He is higher than sight can reach, wider than perception can grasp, and greater than the limits of speech, then how does the one whom He commands and forbids come to know what is wanted of him, so as to obey Him rather than disobey, and thank Him rather than disbelieve, unless you accept that there are causes for this knowledge set between Him and His creation? Are there those who deliver His justice and bear witness to His witnessing, for them and against them, men whom He has chosen by His knowledge and selected out of all His creation, whom He has made messengers to them and proofs against them, so that they stand as the just ones between Him and His servants, conveying His command and His prohibition? Obedience to them is then obedience to God and the winning of His pleasure, and disobedience to them is His wrath and His punishment. Otherwise, how could the command come from God to His creation at all?

This, for me, said Abu Malik, is among the foundations of justice. Once creation stands in need of God's justice to teach them what He wants of them, and He teaches them through the hand of the righteous one He has appointed for that, then each shall thank his benefactor, be rewarded as God wills, be reproached, and be punished if God wills.

Salih said to him: Is it permissible to rule with justice in any way other than this?

It is permissible for none but God, he said, and God is the wisest of judges.

Would it be permissible for Him, Salih went on, to entrust the command to some of His creatures, setting them up in justice against their own wrongdoing, and then to lay the very same charge upon others, yet not guide these others to it by a just guide as He guided the first?

The judgment can only be one, said Abu Malik, because justice is one. Were the ruling split into two meanings, one of the two could not escape injustice.

Salih said to him: Has it not been established for you, then, that the judgment of God is one, with no difference in it, and that His way is one, unchanged in His messengers? Were it not for the unbroken succession of the messengers and the firm standing of command and prohibition, the truth would have no growth and creation no meaning.

Yes, he said, this is the truth.

Is not their succession from God to His creation, said Salih, so that He may make obedience binding upon them and set them as an authority between Himself and His creatures, to bear witness for whoever obeys them, and so that their obedience is accepted, and to bear witness against whoever disobeys them, and so that such a one is turned away?

The judgment could be nothing else, said Abu Malik, for otherwise He would have given the lie to His messengers and broken His promise, and God is too generous for that. The affair of the messengers has made plain that they are of His justice, and that creation worships Him by obeying them. But tell me of the ranks of these just ones with God. Are they equal, or have some a merit over others?

Salih said to him: How could they stand level in rank when they are bound to obey one another? Were they equal in rank, none of them would obey another. Rather, by His justice He has preferred some above others, so that obedience passes from the lesser to the greater, and from the greater to one greater still, until obedience and worship reach the utmost of what God wills.

Then the one who obeys the greater among them and the greater one himself, asked Abu Malik, are they equal in reward?

Yes, he said. This belongs to the justice of God, which grants every obedient one his reward as a due, and no one is questioned as to the limit of the greater or the lesser.

Is any one of the excellent ones, asked Abu Malik, matched by his opposite?

No, he said, for they come from the Creator bearing knowledge of the unseen in the Books He has revealed to them, and no one can bring the like of these except from God.

Then can anyone match the lesser among them, he asked, if no book is sent down to him carrying revealed knowledge of the unseen?

No one can lay claim to that station, he said.

And how is that, he asked, when they did not distinguish themselves from creation by the unseen, as the excellent one distinguished himself?

Salih said to him: If the excellent one distinguished himself from others by knowledge of the unseen in the revelation, then the lesser likewise distinguished himself from others by knowledge of the unseen in the interpretation. Both the revelation and its interpretation are from God, and what is with God is attained only through inspiration.

And the lesser is also inspired? asked Abu Malik.

Yes, he said, and for that reason obedience to him became obedience to God.

If you set him level with the excellent one in obedience and in inspiration, asked Abu Malik, why do you call him the lesser?

By his obedience to the excellent one, said Salih, and his need of him.

And what need has he of a creature, asked Abu Malik, when the inspiration comes to him from the Creator?

Salih said to him: Though the inspiration comes to him, it comes bearing the interpretation of the reports and the Books and the rest of what the excellent ones left behind, as it was revealed to Lot at the hand of Abraham, so that Abraham's excellence was made plain, and as it was revealed to Ishmael and to Isaac, so that the goods, the blessings, and the merit were set down to Abraham. The inspiration from God is joined to His just ones in His land, each according to his rank. He does not, in His justice, will to cut off His command and His prohibition and His worship from His servants after He has favored them with it, so that worship would be severed and ignorance made lawful.

The bearer of the revelation, said Abu Malik, has shown his truthfulness by the knowledge of the unseen in his book. By what does the bearer of the interpretation show that he too holds knowledge of the unseen, so that the revelation bears witness for him and makes obedience to him binding as it does for the bearer of the revelation?

Salih said to him: In his interpretation he brings knowledge from heaven and a declaration from the highest assembly, and the Book bears witness to it.

Then I bear witness, said Abu Malik, to what the Books bear witness to: that every interpretation the revelation testifies to is a revelation from God. Otherwise all the Books and the messengers would have to be given the lie, since their meaning is one and the same. And he added: If some of them give the lie to others, all must be reckoned liars; and if some confirm others, all must be confirmed.

Such is justice, Abu Malik said, and by it the truthfulness of those who hold to it is known, and their merit. Obedience is binding to those confirmed among the messengers who are His justice within His creation, whom He charged to convey His message. Yet if obedience is binding through prophethood, revelation, and interpretation, then what of those who sit in the seats of the prophets, resembling them though they are not of them? They have all agreed that after their prophet there is no prophet and no warner in their age, that they themselves are the ones who uphold the command of God and His religion, and that what they say leads to God's reward or His punishment. You say otherwise. So how have the scholars of this community failed to look into this matter?

Salih said to him: They have not wholly left it, but they have not looked into it with a sound looking, and no one has spoken to them of it so that they might incline their minds toward it, for it has been made to soak into their hearts that they are the worthiest of God's command and His religion, and that there is no prophet after their prophet, just as the seducers of the nations before them said. So every nation has claimed that there is no prophet after its own prophet.

What was the beginning of that, asked Abu Malik, and what did they intend by it?

Salih said to him: Its beginning was three in every age: a rebellious devil, a stubborn tyrant, and a wrangling jurist. By this saying they sought to cut the marks of the prophets off from the special gifts of prophethood, and to make it a possession passed among themselves. They could not do so until they had steeped the hearts of the nations in the saying, put to every nation, that God sent none better than your prophet, that there is no prophet after him and no warner. So they whispered to them from where they did not perceive, and turned them aside from where they did not know. Each nation clung to its own prophet by giving the lie to whoever came after him, supposing that this drew them nearer to their prophet. Each nation claimed that every prophet after its own was a liar. Thus the mention of prophethood and the marks of the prophets ceased among all the nations by that saying, and they did not seek what they had lost, nor would they have believed had they sought. So the seducing devils gained power over the seduced and seduced them, and the tyrants gained the power to kill the prophets and killed them, and doubt crept in from the evil jurists, who dressed it up finely, and were destroyed and destroyed others. All of this was so that the hearts of creation should not incline to the prophets when they appeared, nor seek them when they were lost. Despair of the prophets grew upon them, until the prophets called to the truth openly and were given the lie, and were slain among them without stirring their anger.

Here is the nation of the Magi, clinging to its tyranny, worshipping its idols, keeping to its fires, saying, by their own claim, that God will send no messenger after their messenger. Here is the nation of the Torah, clinging to its scripture, bent over its calf, standing by the bounds of its prophets, humble before its Judge, saying, by their claim, that God will raise no messenger after Moses. Here is the nation of the Gospel, speaking by its likening, claiming that its prophet is its God, drawing near by its crosses, exalting its rabbis, saying that God will send no messenger after Jesus. And here is your community, walking the paths of those before it, seduced by what seduced them. It inherited the Book from other than its rightful people, walked with the religion outside its true way, followed its leaders in error, obeyed its masters in straying, and its jurists bowed before the tyrants, so that they set up for the people a religion without the established practice, performing the prayer at other than its time and paying the alms into other than its due. They are patient with these masters, persistent in their need of them, certain of their independence from all others, saying that God will send no messenger and no warner after their Messenger. So you and your like have followed the saying of the great multitude, dazzled by the abundance of consensus and the force of the sultans, and you accepted it from them, and sweetened it with the fairness of your schools, and adorned it with the fineness of your words, and by it you set up an argument of justice where no argument had been raised against you. So you followed those above you among the tyrants for the awe of them, and those below you followed you out of desire, all supposing it to be the truth.

Now if this community must be believed in what it has decreed against God, that His proofs are cut off from His creation by a single strand of consensus, then the earlier nations have the better claim to be believed, by three qualities which oblige an argument for them against you, and in which they are more entitled to truth than you.

And what are those qualities, asked Abu Malik, in which they have merit over us, so that they must be believed if their word is true, or given the lie if their word is crooked?

Salih said to him: Their word is like your word, and your word is of the same kind as theirs. Your hearts resemble one another, and theirs is the merit over you in the argument. As for the first quality, they say they are earlier to the religion of God than you, so theirs is the merit of those who came first. The second is that the consensus, both theirs and yours, confirms what is with them, so theirs is the merit of the truthful. The third is your own testimony that their prophets are truthful and are the messengers of God, while none of them has borne witness for your prophet, neither to prophethood nor to truthfulness. So theirs is the merit over you by your agreement with them and their standing apart from you, and they say that the knowledge you hold is copied from their knowledge, and that theirs is the root of yours. This is their argument against you, clear in word and plain in justice. If the ruling of creatures is binding upon the Creator, then the ruling of these is the more binding. But if the Creator is the ruler over His creation, abrogating what He wills and confirming what He wills, with none to overturn His ruling, then by my life the saying of them all falls away, and the Creator has the first claim over creation and over the command. Every day He is engaged in some affair, and His act is not to be denied, even were He to send a warner every day.

This community, said Abu Malik, has folded itself upon a far-reaching error.

Salih said to him: Most of those before them went astray also. They cast the friends of God out of their right and let the foolish overcome them in their affair, and they made lawful what God had forbidden, deliberately, so as not to resemble prophethood, lest people should long for the prophets and then not kill them. But they distracted them with kingship and largesse and led them to follow their whims. In this they were helped by men known outwardly for truthfulness and piety, who made a show of it before the people, until the very ones who had once commanded the community to good turned it from the good, and the ones who had forbidden it evil pointed it toward the evil. The people of faithfulness to the friends of God grew few, and truth and falsehood became alternating fortunes, like light and darkness in their difference. Were it not for the remnants of the friends of God and His just ones in the earth, and the inspiration that comes to them, the little of the truth would not prevail over the much of the falsehood. Otherwise, by what would God distinguish the corrupt from the good, except by His just ones? And how would the argument of God reach all creation, except through them?

There is no way, said Abu Malik, and no escape for the justice of God and His command, amid the multitude of adversaries, except by the just ones of God. By them the argument of God to His creation is made sound.

Salih said to him: If it is established that the company of the prophets of God are the just ones, is not each single one of them called a just one?

Yes, he said. He is a just one, as it is said, so-and-so bore witness, and he is a just one among the just.

Salih said to him: Then it has been established for you that the just one is the friend of God, and this has passed out of what you called merely a description, and become a thing described.

Yes, he said, this is sound as a saying.

Salih said to him: And one who does not reach the thing described, does the description profit him?

The description does not profit him, he said, except by reaching the thing described.
