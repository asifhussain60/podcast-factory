The boy said: "Praise be to God, who has guided us to His religion, for we could not have been guided except by His favor. Tell me now about the beginning of this creation and how it is composed. Is it a single creation or many different ones? For I see so much difference among people in their opinions and their passions."

The scholar said: "As for the creation of bodies, it is various, as you see in the diversity of tongues and colors. That variety is itself a proof of God's power, made plain by His signs, so that His creatures may know one another: the child knows his father and the father his son; the man knows his wife and no other, and the woman knows her husband and no other. So God created them, in justice. But as for the creation of the instrument, by which I mean the senses, both what is apparent of them and what is hidden, that is a single creation, and what is hidden within will tell you the same as what is shown without. Have you ever seen a scholar with three eyes, so that you might point to an increase in him, or an ignorant man with a single eye, so that you might point to a deficiency in him, until someone said that the scholar sees by an increase and the ignorant fails to see by a lack? Have you seen any increase in the outward instrument of one man over another? You see their creation only as one level creation in the apparent instrument, and the meaning of the composition is the same in all. From this we have come to know that the inner senses follow the very same pattern. It is justice. God would not have been just in what is apparent and unjust in what is hidden. He did not make two hearts for the scholar within one cavity, nor did He deprive the ignorant of anything. The instrument is one, and the composition belongs to the One who gathered it together. By this very instrument, which He gave them and which they did not use, God has argued against them. The Almighty said:

'لَهُمْ قُلُوبٌ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ بِهَا وَلَهُمْ أَعْيُنٌ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ بِهَا وَلَهُمْ آذَانٌ لَا يَسْمَعُونَ بِهَا'

'They have hearts wherewith they do not understand, and they have eyes wherewith they do not see, and they have ears wherewith they do not hear.'

Do you suppose, then, that God, in His generosity and bounty, charged them with what they are powerless to bear and carry? Never. He is fairer than that."

The boy said: "I do not see it otherwise, and I do not say otherwise. But I ask you about the difference of passions. From where did it come to them, when the instrument is whole and the origin of the composition is one and the same?"

The scholar said: "As for the origin, it is one. All of them are from Adam, and Adam is from dust, whom his Lord created and into whom He breathed of His Spirit. But they departed from the Sunna in their marrying; they bred upon a way that was not the religion, and they grew up under no guidance. And I do not doubt that among them are children of unlawful union who claim as fathers men not their own. When the roots were corrupted, the branches turned foul and strange, and the way back to the truth became hard for them, together with the arrogance that entered them against obedience, the sloth that kept them from worship, and the envy they bore toward the friends of God. So they did not hold themselves within the bounds of the lawful, apart from the forbidden."

The boy said: "If the origin is one and the instrument is one, from where does the difference of passions arise, when their meaning in seeking is also one, since every man seeks the good for himself?"

The scholar said: "By my life, their meaning in seeking is indeed one, but they differ in striking the mark, like archers aiming at a target. They are agreed in shooting at it with resolve and with the instrument, yet for all that they differ in hitting it. So too the hearts within the breasts are gathered upon the minds, and the minds are gathered upon the safeguarding of the bodies and the securing of their dignity. When the call of truth appeared, they parted ways. Some held that to answer the Messengers and to humble themselves before them was part of their own dignity and the guarding of themselves against folly. Others held that to deny the Messengers and to raise themselves above obedience to them was part of their own dignity and the guarding of themselves against the narrowness of worship. The origin in both was one: to seek ease for the body and dignity for the soul. So one company chose what the Messengers had promised them of the reward of God, though in this world it was precious and scant, choosing it for its permanence; and the others chose from the bliss of this world, though it perishes, choosing it because it is present, because it is easily sought, and because its reward is swift. The very hearts by which the first sought the bliss of the Hereafter were the like of those by which the others sought the bliss of this world. Each man looks to himself, and each supposes himself an adviser to his own soul. So they came together in the seeking and parted in the striking. That is from the justice of God, His generosity, and the breadth of His giving. To every seeker is given what he sought: whoever desires the reward of this world is given from it, and whoever desires the reward of the Hereafter is given from it."

The boy said: "Yet the people of this world are ranked in classes within it. Some are rich enough to have no need to labor, and these are kings by the abundance of their wealth. Others are poor and in need of money, and these serve the first to receive from them. Then the bliss of this world gathers them all on one side. Are they all alike in an evil end, or is there for some of them a portion different from the rest?"

The scholar said: "It is from the justice of God that He joins them one to another and does not part them from their companions and those they loved. They are equal in His punishment, just as they were joined together in disobedience to Him."

The boy said: "And what of this religion, which God has honored and perfected, which He has accepted as a religion for Himself and imposed upon His servants? Will He accept it from some of them without the rest, so that they stand in ranks like the people of this world, or will He accept it only from one who brings it whole?"

The scholar said: "If the people of this world have their classes within it, then the people of religion have their degrees within it. In justice, the Creator is to be worshipped to the full measure of what He wills, according to the greatness of His sovereignty and the greatness of their need of Him. Yet His mercy widened toward them. He knew that among them are the weak, and so He accepted from them what came easily of sincere striving in work and of exertion in obedience, whether little or much. Then He charged each soul with knowledge according to its strength and its capacity to act, and He gave every possessor of merit his due, so that they stand at degrees before their Lord. Then, by His justice, He appointed for their obedience an end: that He should join them to those among His servants who hold the religion whole and whose works are excellent. The Almighty said:

'وَمَنْ يُطِعِ اللهَ وَالرَّسُولَ فَأُولَئِكَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللهُ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنَ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ وَالصَّالِحِينَ وَحَسُنَ أُولَئِكَ رَفِيقًا ذَلِكَ الْفَضْلُ مِنَ اللهِ'

'And whoever obeys God and the Messenger, those are with the ones upon whom God has bestowed favor, of the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous. What excellent companions they are! That is the bounty from God.'

This is by their exertion in obedience, not because their deeds equal the deeds of the righteous. It is the righteous who reach this rank, yet these attain it only as a mercy from God, even though they themselves are weak and their works are slight."

The boy said: "Praise be to God, who has done this for them. Is it then to be feared that anyone should be punished for something other than disobedience, even when his work is little?"

The scholar said: "God is more just than that."

The boy said: "Is there hope, then, that anyone should enter the Garden without works, such as the insane and the little children?"

The scholar said: "That falls under the grace of God, His generosity, and the breadth of His mercy."

The boy said: "What, then, is the limit of exertion in obedience and the measure of sincerity in work, such that God is bound to accept a man's excuse in all that lies beyond it?"

The scholar said: "The full reach of one's knowledge, and the utmost of one's strength."

The boy said: "Then describe to me the duty of knowledge and the duty of action, and the goal of each, so that I may test myself. If I am able to reach the goal in seeking the pleasure of my Lord, I shall press on; and if not, I shall halt at the limit of my strength, and God is the first to excuse me for what I cannot do. I am certain of the justice of God and safe from any wrong on His part, but I fear the evil within my own soul."

The scholar said: "Do not fear where you have made yourself safe, and do not feel safe where you ought still to fear. Strive for the sake of God with your person and your wealth, for God does not accept what He hates, nor does He hate what pleases Him. He has hated disbelief for His creation and has been pleased with thankfulness from His servants and with justice in His judgment; He accepts nothing else. Truthfulness and faithfulness are the two gardens of His pleasure, and none is saved with Him except the people of truthfulness and faithfulness."

The boy said: "Then make plain to me the goal of my work within myself, and what God has made binding upon me in it."

The scholar said: "Obedience to God ennobles its people, even when their origins are humble, and good work is the crown of those who do it. As for what is binding upon you in your own person, it is to perform every obligation and to shun every forbidden thing, for that is the very substance of religion and the purity of the body. The sum of what makes God pleased with you and accepting of you is this: that you bring to others what you would love to have brought to yourself, and withhold from them what you would love to have withheld from yourself. In that is justice, and God is pleased with justice."

The boy said: "Yes, this is the binding truth, and whoever does not act by it has no share with God. Is there, then, any latitude in what you have mentioned when necessity comes?"

The scholar said: "God has made this broad for His servants, out of His compassion and mercy. He has said:

'فَمَنِ اضْطُرَّ غَيْرَ بَاغٍ وَلَا عَادٍ فَلَا إِثْمَ عَلَيْهِ'

'Whoever is compelled, without willful desire and without transgression, there is no sin upon him.'"

The boy said: "Praise be to God, who has laid no hardship upon His servants in religion. I am resolved to hold fast to what He has commanded, and I seek His help in doing as He has ordered me. But what is the duty regarding my wealth, which you joined to my own person when you said, 'Strive with your person and your wealth'?"

The scholar said: "As for your wealth, it is all that God has granted you of every kind of gain. Take it only from what is lawful, and spend it only in its rightful place."

The boy said: "And what is the lawful source from which it is taken, and what is the rightful place in which it is spent?"

The scholar said: "Its lawful source is the wholesome earnings in which you have wronged no one and which you have not tainted with falsehood. As for its rightful due, that is its zakat: look upon all that has come into your hands, whether little or much, and bring out a fifth of it, the finest of it and the dearest to you, and give it to the one whose claim upon you is binding. In this way your wealth is purified by alms, just as your body is purified by work. Work is a shield, and zakat is a purification, and he who purifies himself has prospered."

The boy said: "I shall do so, and I repent to God for what has gone before. Then what shall I do with the remainder of my wealth?"

The scholar said: "You shall spend upon yourself and your household in kindness. If your bounty then exceeds your need, share it with the destitute among your brethren and extend your favor to every one who turns to you in want, for the man who has no bounty of his own stands in need of the favor of another. Be swift to every good and foremost in every act of bounty. This is the goal of the work, if you are patient in it."

The boy said: "Yes, patience, and then more patience. How could a man in great need fail to be patient toward one who has no need of his labor? Every one in want is patient, and every one who has been favored is grateful. Here is all my wealth before you, in five shares: one share is its zakat; one share is expiation for what I have squandered; one share is thanksgiving to God for what I have come to know; one share is for the poor among my brethren; and one share you shall accept by your grace, that it may be provision for you. Take them all to yourself, and God will send their like in their place."

The narrator said: The scholar's heart was stirred for the boy when he saw how the jewel within him had come clear, how the ordeal had brought forth a praiseworthy result, how his merit stood plain and his work was made pure.

The scholar said, while his tears ran down: "O my son, for the like of you let the scholars travel the long road; for the like of you let the earth stand firm and the heaven be held up; for the like of you let the bonds of kinship be made plain, and let the rain come down from the openings of the clouds. So rejoice, my son, in what God has given you and in the breast He has opened to Islam. Take the glad tidings of a light from your Lord and a near opening, and receive the gift of God to you and the favors of His grace upon you with abundant work and noble thanks. As for my own share of this wealth, I have no need of it. I came out from wealth, and it is from the temptation of wealth that I fled. It would not be right, my son, that you should return me to what I have detested. As for your brothers' share, keep it for them; when they ask you for something, pass it to them by your own hand, so that they may know your kindness and give thanks for your goodness. As for the share of expiation, hold it with you until you know your fault. As for the share of thanksgiving, it is a trust kept for God until you reach your maturity and rise to the utmost of what you have come to know. As for the share of zakat, that too is a trust held for the one to whom it belongs. Take them all to yourself until you meet him, for he is the ruler over you and on your behalf."

The boy said: "And who is this noble one with you, whose judgment you do not overstep and whose command you do not transgress?"

The scholar said: "He is the one whose right God has made binding upon me and upon every believer, the one in whose hand are the keys of the gardens and the landmarks of the kingdom, the one whose palms are opened."
