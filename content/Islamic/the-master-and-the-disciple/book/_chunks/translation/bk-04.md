The scholar began with the first of the causes of the manifest order and the beginning of its creation. God, he said, is the Creator of things and the One who composes them. He created them and originated them. He is not the first of every first created thing, as though He had taken some prior creation as an evidence for what came after, but rather He is the First of all firsts and the Inheritor of every thing. He began the creation of what was created out of light, and from that light three words branched. The first of them is the will. From the will branched the command, and from the command branched the saying, that He says to what He wills "Be" and it is. So the first creation is a will, then a command, then a saying.

These are three words, and from them is derived "كن," which is two letters, and "فيكون," which is five letters, so that they are seven letters, and from these seven letters seven things branch. From the light He created air, which is the first of them: singular without consort, unique among things, mingling with them and surrounding them, yet surrounded by none of them. It is therefore the proof of the One concerning whom "ليس كمثله شيء," there is nothing like Him. Then out of those three words He created water, darkness, and light, the light of the heavens and the earth. Then He created smoke out of the water, He created the condensing of the heat out of the darkness, and He created fire out of the light. These are three things that branched from three things before them, and each became a consort to its origin because it was created from it. The seventh, the air, holds sway over them all. This was the beginning of creation and the beginning of things.

When creation had been established upon seven principles, and the path of forms had been laid down according to likeness and analogy, He created out of the smoke seven heavens and their signs, and He created out of the condensing of the heat seven earths and their sustenances. These are the seven branches drawn from those seven origins.

Then we look into the signs of the heaven and the sustenances of the earth, asking what they are and what bears witness to them. We return in speech to the three words and their letters from which the seven were created, namely "a will to command by saying." That is twelve letters. Its signs in the heaven are the twelve constellations, and its witnesses are seven kingdoms. Its signs upon the earth are twelve islands, and its witnesses are seventeen seas. These are the signs of the heavens and the earth, which people pass by while they turn away from them.

Then the heavens and the earth were closed up in darkness from the black heat, which belongs to the authority of the earth. Then He pierced them with His lights, and the light and the darkness, the two opposed realities, were intermingled. He distinguished between them by night and day as two signs. He made the night and its sign a way of darkness and apportioned it, and He made the day and its sign a way of light and apportioned it. So the night grew dim with its darkness, and the moon and the stars are its sign, while the day shone with its light, and the seeing rays of the sun are its sign. Then from the two of them the days were brought forth, passing in turn between night and day, and they are seven days. Their signs are that for each day there are twelve hours, and for each night twelve hours.

When the creation of things was made complete upon these principles, He made them pairs, male and female. I do not mean male and female in the bodily sense, but in the sense of the superior and the subordinate. Then the custom went forth in the creation of pairs, and from their substances He created every living thing, humankind and beasts and birds and creeping things. Then this custom extended into speech, and speech too became pairs: qualities and things qualified, names and meanings. So glory be to Him who created all the pairs and made them outward signs of His command, likenesses of His religion, and proofs of His power, pointing by means of things to their Originator and by means of the creation to its Maker.

Then He opened it with His light, began it with His command, created it by His knowledge, and sealed it, from among His names, with the letters of "بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم." When He had perfected all of it by His making and composed it in pairs by His measuring, He chose for Himself, out of the choicest of His will and the depths of His unseen, a great knowledge and a clear light, and He made the two of them a religion by which God is served and by which one draws near to Him. Then He honored it with the sacred laws and the books, and pointed to it by the parables and the customs. Then He set up for it, from among the best of His creation, banners and proofs and rulers, and He made them the houses of His prophethood, the treasuries of His knowledge and wisdom, the interpreters of His revelation, and His successors in His earth as guides upon the path of salvation in what is outward and what is inward. Among them are the imams and the speakers; among them are the proofs and the chiefs; among them are the summoners to the good and the learned. In their stations they are like the stars of heaven, for by them the works are raised up and in them the likenesses reach their end. Their imam is like the great sun among the lights, veiled by its own coverings from the sight, and it is his outward form and his likeness. The proof of their imam, and his gate, is like the shining moon, which is his outward form and his likeness. Their summoners are like the radiant stars, which are their outward forms and their likenesses.

All the pairs I have described to you are outward and inward, likeness and thing represented. The outward of the seven heavens are likenesses, and their inward are the seven speakers, the messengers endowed with firm resolve. The seven earths are outwardly likenesses, and their inward are the imams of guidance. For each speaker among them there are twelve chiefs, and their likeness from the seven heavens is the twelve constellations. Likewise, each imam of the imams of guidance has twelve proofs by which he is upheld before his people, and their likeness in the earth is the twelve islands, which are the dwellings of the chiefs, on every island a proof. Every proof has summoners, many and few, for He increases in creation what He wills, so that the proof may be conveyed, the proof of God to His creation, that people may have no argument against God after the messengers.

Through these causes, the causes of God that I have described to you, the proof of God reaches His creation, and His justice appears among them outwardly and inwardly. The inward is the religion of God by which the friends of God (رض) have bound themselves, and the outward is the sacred laws of the religion and their likenesses. So the religion became to the laws a soul and a spirit, and the laws became to the religion a body and a guide. There is no standing for the body except by the soul, for the soul is its life, and no standing for the soul except by the body, for the body is its instrument. In the same way the outward things, the sacred laws and the rest, have no standing except by the inward religion, because it is their light and their meaning and the spirit of life within them; and the inward has no standing except by the outward, because the outward is its body and its proof. The outward is the knowledge of this world, and it is seen only through it; the inward is the knowledge of the hereafter, and it is seen only through it. There is not a single letter of the letters of the inward, nor a single friend of the friends, but that its witness in the outward is many, because of the abundance of the likenesses and the breadth of the sacred laws.

Speech upon this grows long and multiplies, and the more its answer increases from the words of wisdom, the more its rightness is hidden, and its end causes its beginning to be forgotten, on account of its preciousness and the purity of its essence. For the light of wisdom overshadows one part of itself with another, just as the light of the sun overshadows the light of the moon and the stars and makes it faint. In the same way the words of wisdom adorn the tongue of the wise man.
