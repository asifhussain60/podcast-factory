Salih said to him: Then whoever does not know the just one of Allah and His guardian, can his own speech be just?

At this the narrator relates that Abu Malik smiled and said: By Allah, you have drawn me out of my whole position on justice and monotheism, and nothing has drawn me out of it but the truth itself, in the clearest of arguments and the gentlest of speech. After this, what position is left for me to defend?

Salih said: From here, O Abu Malik, the coated glass falls away from the hands of those who trade it as the colored gems it is not. Its worth was never truly known; but when it was set before the jewelers it was exposed, and its deceived owners were exposed along with it.

Abu Malik said: You have spoken truly, and the crookedness of these doctrines and the straying of the nations has now become plain to me. But how are we to find safety from them, and to know the means that lead to Allah, or even one such means, when we did not live in the age of the prophets, and there are in our own time neither prophets nor messengers as there were among the nations before us?

Salih said: Glory be to Allah, O Abu Malik. You have spent a long stretch of your life affirming the justice of Allah, yet the moment we press it upon you where it becomes binding upon you to accept it, you turn back and speak as though Allah would deceive you, and you ascribe injustice to Him.

Abu Malik said: God forbid. I say no such thing, for whoever says it has transgressed and lied against Allah. From where did you bind me to that?

Salih said: From your own words, that the age of the prophets has passed you by, and that in our time there is no prophet and no messenger. Tell me: was His sending of the messengers among the former peoples an act of justice from Him toward His servants, or was it done in vain?

He said: God forbid that it be anything but justice from Him.

Salih said: Then what has cut us off from that justice? Are we not His servants as they were? Were they not created as we are created, and are we not bound in service as they were bound? Has He then wronged us after being just to them, so that we who come after are left excused? Or has His giving run dry in our time, so that we cling only to the bounty He gave to them, and He is content with that from us? Or has the way of Allah changed with us after it stood firm with those before us, so that we should pass judgment upon Allah that He has changed? Or is His justice an obligation owed to us and His ruling in force upon us, just as it was His way with those who came before us, so that there is no altering the words of Allah and none to overturn His judgment? And who deserves the name of the just one except the guardians of Allah? Among all that Allah has created, none is just except the one Allah has chosen, who became just by His command. It is for this reason that a ruling is called just, on account of the justice of the one who decreed it, and a word is called true on account of the truthfulness of the one who spoke it.

Abu Malik said: It can only be as you say, and we are not permitted to say of Allah anything but the truth. Yet we walked the road of our own opinion in speech and judged by the absence of the rightly guided from among us. We held that this too was part of Allah's ruling and His way with us, as it had been with those before us in the interregnums between one messenger and the next, like the interval between the religion of Abraham and the time of Moses and the Torah, and the interval between the Torah and the time of Jesus and the Gospel. On that footing we spoke and acted, working our way through an age of interruption, holding fast to the traditions and the established way.

Salih said to him: The religion of Allah is far too precious to Him for that. What interregnum was there after the religion of Abraham, when Allah sent after him Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Jonah, and Shu'ayb, leading on to Moses? He inspired them to do good works, to call to the religion of Abraham, to establish the prayer according to its way and to render the zakat according to its obligations, until He sent down the ruling of the Torah upon Moses. And what interregnum was there after the religion of Moses, between him and the Gospel, when Allah sent after Moses Joshua son of Nun, and Elias, and Talut, and David, and Solomon, and Zechariah, and John, leading on to Jesus? They upheld the ruling of the Torah, holding to its religion, calling to it, and reciting its Psalms, until the ruling of the Gospel was sent down upon Jesus. So it was with those who went before them and those who came after, and with the many generations between, whom none knows but Allah. Their messengers came to them with clear proofs; for each people Allah set forth His parables, and each was called to the truth. Where, then, is this interregnum among them, and when did it ever fall? Allah said only, "after a succession of messengers," عَلَى فَتْرَةٍ مِنَ الرُّسُل, and by these words He affirmed both the messengers and the pause. When the call arose it arose out of fear, so they called in secret and concealment. The earth was never left empty of the justice of Allah for the blink of an eye, but that His just one was in it, either openly present or hidden away in fear.

Abu Malik said: This is a statement that cannot be turned back, a settled word in which there is no doubt. As for the standing of Allah's proof through justice and the succession of the messengers, that is now established and made clear. So point me now to the meaning of their absence in this age of ours. What is its cause?

Salih said to him: What was the cause of the absence of Moses when he went out from Pharaoh's city and passed beyond it in fear, watchful, until he reached the land of Shu'ayb?

Abu Malik said: He fled from the people of Pharaoh, hiding himself from them, because he feared for his life when they had agreed together to kill him.

Salih said to him: Have you heard, in this law of yours, of any one of the just, the just ones of Allah and the sons of His prophets, who was killed while patiently bearing the burdens of the religion?

Abu Malik said: Yes. A great many of them have been killed.

Salih said to him: Then which just one of the just of Allah do you wish would appear to you and to the people of your age? One who was slain, whom Allah would raise up and send to you after death? Or one in hiding from among you, whom you would drive back to the very killing he fled, when he has only followed the way of Allah in His prophets and messengers, patient under fear and in concealment, until Allah judges between him and us, and He is the best of judges?

Abu Malik said: There is no blame upon one who follows the way of the righteous by fearing for himself and fleeing from his people. The blame lies upon the evil nations and the helpers of the oppressors, for their killing of the prophets of Allah and His just ones, and for their agreeing together upon that deed.

Salih said: Then what do you say of one who kills the just one of Allah and His guide? Where is his end? And what of the one who helped him?

Abu Malik said: Both are bound for the Fire, and so is anyone who raised a banner for them or handed them an inkwell.

Salih said to him: And what do your jurists say of this?

Abu Malik said: They ask mercy for the slain, yet they see no sin in the killer.

Salih said to him: And whoever obeys the killer of his own accord, is he not a partner with him in the blood of the slain?

Abu Malik said: Yes, by Allah. He is his partner, and worse than he is.

Salih said to him: His partnership is plain, from his aiding him. But how has he become worse than the killer himself?

Abu Malik said: Were it not for the reports of the jurists, the rule of the tyrants could never have been established through the fools who follow them.

Salih said to him: You have spoken the truth, O Abu Malik. Have you then accepted for yourself their kingdom as your home, their land as your dwelling place, their armies as your neighbors, and their enforcers as your close companions, while you serve as their just man, judging for them by your knowledge and settling their affairs by their authority? You have prayed for them in your petitions and thanked them for what you sought from them. Upon their tyranny you set the crown of your justice, and they grew proud; over their filth you cast the robe of the sacred law, and they overstepped all bounds; and you lit up their falsehood by leaving your own truth to lie idle. So the weak were deceived by your conduct, and every scholar who was like you said: These are the jurists of the community, we have in them a pattern to follow. They went astray through you, and you went astray through the ways of the tyrants, and you supposed that the one held to account would be someone other than yourselves. You forgot the words of Allah, glory be to Him, "وَلاَ تَرْكَنُوا إِلَى الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا فَتَمَسَّكُمُ النَّارُ," And do not incline toward those who have done wrong, lest the Fire touch you. So you severed what Allah commanded to be joined, and you saw what you were doing; you spoke of the outward forms of justice while wandering lost from its path. You turned away from His saying, "أَطِيعُوا اللهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ وَأُولِي الأَمْرِ مِنْكُمْ," Obey Allah, and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you, and you claimed that these oppressors were your rulers, even though they had disbelieved. You forgot the words of Allah, exalted is He, "قَاتِلُوا الَّذِينَ يَلُونَكُمْ مِنَ الكُفَّارِ," Fight those of the disbelievers who are near you. So you disobeyed the one you were commanded to obey, and obeyed the one you were commanded to fight, all the while supposing that you were doing good work. Error carried you away, and your own opinion led you on, until you said that Allah would send no messenger and no warner in your age. Is it that the goodness of your deeds has set you above all need of the guardians of Allah for the proof? Or is it that the ugliness of your deeds has cut you off from all hope in Allah's acceptance of repentance? Or is it justice that you should pass judgment upon Allah without justice, and be content, on behalf of your Messenger, with what none of you would be content with for himself in his place? Would any one of you consent that his own son be killed, or that his house and wealth be taken from him without right?

Abu Malik said: No one consents to that. If a child of one of us were killed, or he were driven from his property, he would rage, and his people would rage with him, and they would not be appeased until he was appeased.

Salih said to him: Then Allah and His Messenger have a greater right that you should rage for their sake, when His vicegerent is killed and His guardian is driven from the earth.

Abu Malik said: It is bitter to us that the guardian of Allah should be killed among us or driven from his land. But they themselves chose to withdraw from us, and the tyrants rose to power over us.

Salih said to him: Had you supported the guardians of Allah, they would have appeared; had you forsaken His enemies, those enemies would never have prevailed. But the case has ranged you, before the guardians of Allah, in three ranks. There is the empowered killer, who never tires of the pursuit and is not satisfied even when he has won. There is the gloating approver, who bites his fingertips in rage, sparing no effort to incite others against them, never heedless of them even when they are heedless of him. And there is the one who loathes what is done, who professes love for the guardians of Allah yet forsakes them, having no clear sight in faith and no trust to be placed in him for keeping a secret. For this cause the guardians of Allah have withdrawn from you, in prudent concealment, for your sake and for the sake of this vast multitude, who have taken their own desires as their god, fallen greedily upon the goods of this world, and joined hands over the killing of the prophets. They knew nothing of the fault in what they did, so they never held themselves to account for the wrong they committed, and never humbled themselves to repent of what they had done. Instead they turned the injustice of their own deeds back upon Allah and ascribed it to Him, and said: Allah will send no messenger after their messenger, and no warner. So they made lawful the killing of the guardians of Allah without right. They wronged in deed, deviated in word, and transgressed by their denial.

Abu Malik said: Yes. This is the very description of this community of ours, and this is exactly what they say. Where, then, is the deliverance from it, and where is the escape from the punishment of Allah?

Salih said to him: To Allah. Seek His protection, and He will protect you. Turn to Him in repentance, and He will accept your repentance. Ask Him for the way of His guardians, and by your works He will save you from the deeds of those who did wrong. So it is with this whole multitude: were they to turn to Allah in repentance, He would accept their repentance, and what then would Allah do with punishing them? Allah does not wrong them by the weight of a single atom, though they have wronged and lied, and He does not hasten the punishment upon them, though they hasten to disobey.

Abu Malik said: Then I seek refuge from the punishment of Allah by turning to Him in repentance and seeking the means to what pleases Him. So show your kindness to the one who seeks it of you, and grant guidance to the one who asks to be guided, and your Lord will reward you.

Salih said to him: If you do that, you will find Allah nearer in His pardon of you than your own repentance is to Him, for He loves those who turn to Him; and you will find me eager in guiding you and watchful over you.

Then the eyes of Salih filled with tears at the mention of repentance, and he broke off his speech. He heartened them, and bade them go to his father's house. He himself went in to his father, the learned man who had first sent him out, and consulted him about the affair of Abu Malik and his companions.

The learned man said: You know your companions better than I. If you find good in them, guide them by your guidance; and if you have cause to warn them, do not let eagerness move you to be quick in punishing them. Test them by leaving them to themselves for a time, yet without harshness, and instruct your father to treat them well and to show them kindness in due season. For the one who longs for good cannot stay hidden, and Allah will not abandon you at your back. He will open for you, from the light of His enabling grace, that by which He orders the affairs of His creation, and from the gates of guidance that which will show you the deeds of the rightly guided.

And so, the account continues, Abu Malik and his companions passed through the working out of their trial until their affair came to completion, and they came to know their own guidance, and the guardian of Allah among them turned toward that guidance with tenderness. They gave thanks to Allah for it, and returned to their people as warners, and through them Allah guided many of His servants to His religion.

The author said: Whatever in this took the shape of a story was no idle invention, but a confirmation of what Allah has commanded; for in it lies the steadfastness of the messengers, the signs of the guardians, and the manners of the seekers.

Praise be to Allah, first and last. May Allah bless His Messenger, our master Muhammad, sent forth from Him to His creation as a bearer of good tidings and a warner, and bless his guardian, the Imam of the God-fearing, the radiant, blaze-marked leader (القائد الغرّ المحجّلين), the beloved of the Lord of the Worlds, and the Imams of the people of his house, upon whom Allah has bestowed His favor, from whom He has removed all defilement, and whom He has purified with a complete purifying. Allah is sufficient for us, and how excellent a Guardian; how excellent a Master, and how excellent a Helper. There is no power and no strength except by Allah, the Most High, the Most Great.
