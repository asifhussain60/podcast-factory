# Show notes — EP16

**Title:** Jewels, the Moon, and the Description of Religion

**Blurb:** Episode sixteen of *The Master and the Disciple* closes the great debate of Part Four. Abu Malik invites Salih to state his arguments under the image of wisdom as jewels in the sunlight; Salih accepts the image with one condition — it holds only where there is no confusion between real jewel and forged glass. The story of the jewelers' verdict on his former faith leads to the principle that spiritual knowledge must be presented to those who receive it from the heavens before it can be deemed genuine. Abu Malik asks who, after the prophets, such people can be; Salih answers by refusing the standing of any religion grounded only in transmitted reports. The example is moon-sighting. The hadith of moon- sighting has turned, Salih says, into the hadith of moon-fighting. The Messenger's instruction "follow the moon" was the designation of the wasi the community was to follow — the moon as the established symbol, the wasi who follows the sun's path — and the community collapsed the symbol into the disc. The Quran's own description of the sun and moon on fixed calculated paths witnesses against the chaos. A divorce case study — a man left not knowing whether he is married or divorced — brings Abu Malik to the confession that sharia has been corrupted. Salih dissolves the religion=sharia equivalence by which Abu Malik had been holding what was left and gives his own description of religion: the veil, the rope from the heavens, the firm handhold, the ark of the divine presence, the ship of salvation, the light of life, the well- protected knowledge, the jewels with their distinctive signs. Abu Malik accepts the description as the essential meaning religion would have to have and asks for the reality of which the description is the picture. The way is opened for the final part of the debate.
