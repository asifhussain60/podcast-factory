# Show notes — EP01

**Title:** Three Thanks and the Persian Awakening

**Blurb:** Episode one of *The Master and the Disciple* opens on a group of the truly faithful and a group of du'at al-din standing in front of one of their scholars with three thanks owed at once — for the call, for the knowledge, for the practice — and asking how on earth they are supposed to repay them. The Master answers first with a metaphysics of stages: every beginning has been given an end, every end a goal, every goal a limit whose full extent cannot be reached. He names the three-stage spiritual path balwa-huda-taqwa, and only then names the three deeds — obedience for the call, action for the knowledge, perseverance for the practice. The episode then enters the story of the Persian seeker, the man whose thirst for knowledge led him through mirage after mirage until he reached the place of nothing and met Allah there. From that meeting he came under the same proverb that had once been his master's: 'The most excellent of deeds is giving life to the dead.' His interior monologue runs the chain backward — did the trust really descend from the heavenly host through every intermediary only to stop with him? The answer requires action. So he sets out across the Arab and non-Arab lands, and the episode lands with him at the far edge of al-jazira, the disciple still unfound.
