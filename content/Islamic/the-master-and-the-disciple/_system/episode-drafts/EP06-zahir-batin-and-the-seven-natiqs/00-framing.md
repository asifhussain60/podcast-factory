# Zahir Batin And The Seven Natiqs

## Opening directive
Open warmly: "Welcome. We're with *The Master and the Disciple* — the moment the scholar names the seven speaker-prophets and shows outer and inner as the body and spirit of religion, each living only through the other." Then land the spine verbatim (placement 1 of 3): "The inner is the true religion; the outer is its revealed paths and symbols — body and spirit, each subsisting only through the other." Target 50 to 60 minutes. No vehicle-talk.

## Name discipline
- the scholar — the elder voice. Rotation: the elder / the scholar / the teacher.
- the disciple — the student. Rotation: the disciple / the student / the seeker.
- the seven speaker-prophets — Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family at first mention), and the awaited one. Rotation: the seven speaker-prophets / the seven messengers / the seven heralds.
- the Commander of the Faithful (peace be upon him at first mention) — from the sermon-collection.
- the chief judge — from the legal treatise *The Pillars of Islam*. Rotation: the chief judge / the Fatimid jurist / the legal master.
- Henry Corbin, Farhad Daftary, Khalil Andani — modern scholars; plain English.
Never pair the leadership-title with the personal name of the Father of Imams. No Arabic book titles spoken. *Peak of Eloquence* on first reference, thereafter "the sermon-collection."

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- Allah: Allah

- ta'wil: inner interpretation
- awwal: first
- Pickthall: Pickthall
- Yusuf Ali: Yusuf Ali

## Six-beat dramatic arc
Beat 1 — Crisis: the disciple cannot read the visible world; he asks why scholars condemn what God made beautiful.
Beat 2 — Failed first answer: pure allegorism, dismissed as wordplay.
Beat 3 — The named lineage. Seven speaker-prophets as the inner aspect of seven heavens; twelves-and-thirties of transmission derive from the sun-month-day calendar.
Beat 4 — Pivot: body and spirit in mutual subsistence. Inner is the true religion; outer its revealed paths and symbols. Repeat the spine verbatim (placement 2 of 3). The chief judge says the same in legal language.
Beat 5 — The world read as a page. Heaven points to the speaker-prophet, zodiac to the chieftains, earth to the gate and trustee, islands to the proofs, rivers and springs to the summoners. Salt water is outer knowledge; sweet water is inner.
Beat 6 — Stakes: without the chain, no completed argument and no demonstrated justice; practice and meaning each die without the other.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads; Host B (female, seeker) delivers genuine pushback at least three times and concedes once. Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — calling the inner the 'true' religion demotes the law to a costume"; "That sounds like wordplay — how is this different from any allegorist's trick?"; "Isn't this just replacing one literalism with a literalism of symbols?" One honest concession near the close.

## Tone constraints
Walk the source in its own order. Use exactly three governing analogies, drawn from the chapter itself:
Analogy 1: body and spirit in mutual subsistence — spirit-without-body is a wraith; body-without-spirit is a corpse.
Analogy 2: the dust mote bound by six sides and itself the seventh — portable cosmology.
Analogy 3: salt sea and sweet spring — both are water, both are life, only one feeds the field.
No model-invented images. Cite verses as "the chapter on the night journey, verse fifteen" — never "Q 17:15."

## Landing
End on a reflective question grounded in this chapter: which element of the listener's own ordinary day — a meal, a walk, a glance at the sky, a cup of water — has been a sentence they have not been reading, and what one habit this week would teach them to read it. Repeat the spine verbatim (placement 3 of 3) just before that reflective turn. Do not pre-announce a next chapter.

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud. No faux-profundity opening, no "and that, ultimately…" close, no "previous episode" or "next episode", no pairing any leadership-title with the personal name of the Father of Imams.
