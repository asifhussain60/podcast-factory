# The Conspiracy Formula

## Opening directive
Open with a warm 1-2 sentence welcome — name the book *The Master and the Disciple* and its tenth-century author, then preview in one specific sentence: tonight the teacher answers why the learned of this community sit in their assemblies as if no living witness existed. Then land the spine VERBATIM (R-RECURRING-THESIS #1): "Every period has started with three types — a rebellious devil, a willing oppressor, and a hypocritical religious scholar." Forbidden hollow openers: "welcome back", "in this episode", "today we'll discuss". No author biography.

## Name discipline
Stable labels — never rotate the figure label; vary descriptor forms per R-NAMEDISCIPLINE.
- the formula's speaker → the teacher / the master in the dialogue / the one answering.
- the questioner → the student / the disciple / the one who asks and concedes.
- Commander of the Faithful (first mention: "peace be upon him").
- the Prophet (first mention: "peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family").
- Zoroaster, Moses, Jesus — English always.
- the historian Corbin; the historian Daftary.
Never speak Arabic book titles.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- Zoroastrians: Zoroastrians
- Quran: Quran
- Allah: Allah
- hadith: hadith
- Bayhaqi: Bayhaqi
- Nahj al-Balagha: the collected sayings
- Shu'ab al-Iman: the hadith collection

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: the student's decisive question — why do the learned sit as if no living witness existed? Let the ache land before any answer.
Beat 2 — Failed answer A: the school's standing ground — community consensus settles the question. Steelman it before any critique.
Beat 3 — Failed answer B: the slogan "no prophet after our prophet" needs no defense because everyone repeats it. State its strongest form.
Beat 4 — The teacher's pivot: the three-actor formula. Spine VERBATIM #2: "Every period has started with three types — a rebellious devil, a willing oppressor, and a hypocritical religious scholar." Their joint product severs the outward traces of prophecy from its living continuation.
Beat 5 — Correction: the historical roll call — fire-temples after Zoroaster, the calf and judges after Moses, the priests after Jesus, this community now. Artifact differs; structure is identical. Then turn consensus back: by their own metric the earlier communities have the greater claim.
Beat 6 — Stakes + open question: collapse via divine sovereignty — He is engaged in a new work every day. The student does not refute; he engages onward (one real concession). Spine VERBATIM #3, then turn to the listener: which slogan repeated in your own circles has made inquiry feel disloyal, and what would it cost this week to ask the question you have been avoiding? Leave it open.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) carries the teacher's voice; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least three times and concedes once at Beat 6.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — three actors sounds neat, but how is this different from any generic elites-conspire story?"; "That sounds like wordplay — calling the slogan an engine just renames repetition." Host B never opens with "Exactly", "Yeah", "Right", "Wow".

## Tone constraints
Analogy 1: a closed system of three functions — temptation, force, legitimation — removing any one collapses the whole (source's own framing).
Analogy 2: the artifact versus the source — books, rituals, names of prophets remain as commodity while the living function has been severed (source's own image).
Analogy 3: two shut doors — consensus and divine decree — leaving only the door to the living witness (source's own image).
No model-invented analogies.

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No "today's episode", "let's dive in", "mind blown", "buckle up". No author biography. No reference to a previous, prior, earlier, or next episode. Do not pair the leadership-title with the personal name of the Father of Imams. R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud.
