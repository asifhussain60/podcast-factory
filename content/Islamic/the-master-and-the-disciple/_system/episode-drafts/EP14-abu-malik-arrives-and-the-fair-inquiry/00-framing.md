# Abu Malik Arrives And The Fair Inquiry

## Opening directive
Open with one warm sentence: "Welcome. We're with *The Master and the Disciple* — Jafar ibn Mansur al-Yaman's dialogue — and the moment the opposing community fields its greatest scholar to handle the new converts Salih and his father." Then land the spine verbatim (placement 1 of 3): "He refused to condemn without hearing; he refused to assume guidance without testing — both would be injustice." Start in the question. No vehicle-talk, no "today's episode."

## Name discipline
- Abu Malik: the visiting Yemeni scholar; first mention add "famous in his community for piety, understanding, and the ability to defeat opponents in debate."
- al-Bakhtari: the convert father.
- Salih: al-Bakhtari's son, the convert who pulled his father in.
- the Commander of the Faithful (peace be upon him): first mention of the *Collected Aphorisms* citation only.
- the Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family): first mention of the Tirmidhi hadith only.
- the sixth Imam in the lineage: at first mention of his sayings.
- Do not pair the leadership-title and the personal name of the Father of Imams.

## Three-part focus
1. The summons and the two refusals. Afraid co-religionists send for the Yemeni scholar; they have avoided the converts out of gratitude, fear of being wrong, and fear of negligence. Abu Malik refuses both bypass routes — condemning without hearing, assuming guidance without testing — and grounds each refusal in justice.
2. The warning and the three readings. He warns his own companions against the satisfaction of past communities who assumed no messenger would come after theirs. Then he gives the conversion three readings — decorative addition, real increase of knowledge ("the lost camel of the believer"), or true prophetic guidance — under each the right move is the same: go and meet. Spine verbatim, placement 2: "He refused to condemn without hearing; he refused to assume guidance without testing — both would be injustice."
3. The inversion of signs and the same-day departure. Falsehood wears the costume of conformity; truth wears the costume of trial. Only the sound heart — the one that does not deny what enters it — perseveres. Humility is the posture BEFORE the truth, not the reward after. They set out the same day, on "there is no power except through Allah."

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least 3 times and concedes once.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — isn't 'go and find out' just polite avoidance dressed up as fairness?"; "That sounds like wordplay — calling the majority's choice 'polished conformity' rigs the verdict before the inquiry starts."; "Isn't this just replacing one certainty with another — Abu Malik's frame for his clients' frame?"

## Tone constraints
Analogy 1: the polished glass versus the jewel from fire — the source's image for the inversion of signs.
Analogy 2: the lost camel of the believer — the source's image for knowledge owed back wherever found.
Analogy 3: the auditor who already knows he is in need — the source's image for humility-before-truth as the posture of search.
No new analogies mid-episode.

## Landing
Place the spine verbatim a third time: "He refused to condemn without hearing; he refused to assume guidance without testing — both would be injustice." Then turn the listener to a modern question grown out of this teaching: which piece of news about a friend, a teaching, or a position have I been refusing to investigate this week — either because condemning from a distance is easier than hearing, or because assuming agreement is easier than testing — and what would it cost to ride out today, not in three days? End on the question. Do not say "ultimately." Do not pre-announce a next chapter.
