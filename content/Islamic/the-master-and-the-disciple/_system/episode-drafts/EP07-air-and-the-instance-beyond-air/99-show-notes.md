# Show notes — EP07

**Title:** Air and the Instance Beyond Air

**Blurb:** Episode seven of *The Master and the Disciple* takes the architecture of creation to its lofty edge. One element of the cosmology has been waiting since the very first concept: Air — the first of the seven, the unpaired king, what mixes with everything and is encompassed by nothing. The disciple asks about it. The scholar refuses to make air a symbol of any creation, then derives — through three nested scale comparisons (a seal-ring on a plain, a seal-ring in a desert, an egg on a plain) — why air points only upward. The disciple raises a Quranic objection that seems to topple the whole teaching: do not strike likenesses for Allah. And the scholar turns it into the doctrine's strongest pillar with a second verse from the same chapter: and Allah's is the loftiest similitude. Both verses are true. Lesser signs are forbidden as peers in a series; the loftiest similitude is licensed because it is the sign that announces the failure of signs. Then the disciple pushes one rung further, and the scholar names what stands behind even Air: al-Awan, the Instance of His divine will, the doorway of irada itself. The disciple pleads to be brought back from the lofty edge. The scholar invokes Moses and the scholar — Khidr — and closes the dialogue at its lofty edge, leaving the inner of the inner unopened.
