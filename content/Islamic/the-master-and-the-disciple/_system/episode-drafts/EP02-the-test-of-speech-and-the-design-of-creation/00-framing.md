# The Test Of Speech And The Design Of Creation

## Opening directive
Open warmly: "Welcome. We're with *The Master and the Disciple* — a Persian scholar sits unseen in a strange city, hears townspeople debating without a guide, and picks them as deserving of life because they were already inquiring." Then land the spine verbatim (placement 1 of 3): "I have no words of my own, for I am following the words of another." Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation.

## Name discipline
- the scholar — the Persian elder. Rotation: the scholar / the master / the teacher.
- the disciple — the youngest townsman. Rotation: the disciple / the young man / the student.
- the father — leader of a rival community that has rejected the Imam.
- the Prophet — peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family at first mention.
- the Commander of the Faithful — peace be upon him at first mention.
- Pickthall — the Quran translator cited.

## Verbatim Recitation
Introduce the sermon in one sentence — the scholar rises, recognized as a gateway. Host A then reads it ALOUD WORD-FOR-WORD from the source section titled "The design-of-creation sermon" — no paraphrase, no shortening. Break at paragraph boundaries if needed but preserve exact wording. Only AFTER recitation, open discussion.

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis. A foreign scholar has crossed continents finding no disciple. He enters a city, sits unseen, listens to townspeople debating without a guide. Knowledge in the wrong heart is not wasted — it does harm.

Beat 2 — Failed answer A. Read inquiry as curiosity and you miss the test: the divine argument has reached them, they belong to the community, they are inquiring. Three conditions, not one.

Beat 3 — Failed answer B. Read the scholar's denial as modesty and the chapter collapses. "I have no words of my own" is the criterion of true speech he just named — he passes his own test before them.

Beat 4 — The pivot. Recognized as a gateway, he delivers the design-of-creation sermon. Outer and inner as cosmic preservation — knowledge entrusted to chosen custodians, the linking-means in everything. Repeat the spine verbatim (placement 2 of 3): "I have no words of my own, for I am following the words of another."

Beat 5 — Correction. The instrument is named: hearing, sight, heart — in four verses, as gift, sealed, covered, accountable. Same instrument, four uses. The error is not lacking it; it is what was done with it.

Beat 6 — Stakes. The hereafter retires the defense of ignorance before the trial. The youngest follows the scholar to his inn. Repeat the spine verbatim (placement 3 of 3) just before the reflective turn.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) lays out the doctrine; Host B (female, seeker) presses and concedes once.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — choosing deserving listeners by eavesdropping sounds like the teacher picking flatterers"; "That sounds like wordplay — how is this different from any preacher claiming he only repeats God's words?" Two genuine challenges, one concession.

## Tone constraints
Walk the source in its own order. Use exactly three governing analogies, all from the chapter:
Analogy 1: tongue behind the heart versus heart dragged behind the tongue.
Analogy 2: the shell of the exterior holding spiritual realities — creation as preservation.
Analogy 3: the tree with firm roots and low fruit — the gateway recognized.
No model-invented images. Cite verses as "the chapter of the Sovereignty, verse twenty-three" — never "Q 67:23."

## Landing
End on a reflective question: of the three openings — hearing, sight, and the heart — which has gone sealed this week through what the listener refused to inquire about, and what would it cost tomorrow to begin asking.
