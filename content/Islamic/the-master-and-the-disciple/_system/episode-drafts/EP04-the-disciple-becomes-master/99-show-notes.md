# Show notes — EP04

**Title:** The Disciple Becomes Master

**Blurb:** Episode four of *The Master and the Disciple* is the book's transformational arc. The disciple is brought to his *wālid al-akbar*, the Sheikh, who praises Allah with *fa-iʿtadala al-amru bayn al-amrayn fa-summiya ʿadlan* — *He harmoniously balanced His command between the two and named it Justice* — and then asks the disciple his name. *ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbd Allāh* is a description, not a name; the disciple has no name yet, because *one cannot name something until it is complete*, and completion requires the seven days that honor a new- born infant. The cosmological reason is named: the universe received its life through *kun fa-yakūn*, whose seventh letter began life, and the embryonic verses (Q 23:12–14) lay out the seven-stage creation of a human being. After the seven days the disciple performs a complete ablution, dresses in his finest, and the Sheikh speaks to him *of realities that pens would fail to write*. He performs the esoteric *Ṭawāf* — *the seven circuits around the house of Allah simulate the motion of the planets orbiting the sun; the sun is the Imam, and the love of the Imam is the gravity that pulls every planet into its orbit* — and finally meets the Imam, the object of his desire. He returns home under the Sheikh's instructions and the scholar's six parting counsels. He converts his own father al-Bakhtarī with a single forty-years syllogism: *either you were a scholar of truth who withheld my share, or you were ignorant and need that stranger more than I do*. The community sends its greatest scholar, Abu Malik — *Kaʿb al-Aḥbār*, the title signifying the highest scholarly reputation — who arrives with striking fairness: he refuses to condemn the converts without hearing them, names the three reasons their conversion could have happened, and gives the chapter's strongest diagnostic of *why truth is hard to recognize* (*falsehood appears attractive and easy; truth appears harsh and demanding*). Ṣāliḥ — the new master — receives him with perfect *ādāb* and applies *kasra*, the cup-emptying that the *makāsir* is formally tasked with. He draws the line between *ʿilm* as report and *maʿrifa* as vision; he names the *ḥadīth* of moon-sighting as the *ḥadīth of moon- fighting* and restores *follow the moon* to its esoteric meaning, *follow Maulā Ali (peace be upon him)*; he marshals *ayyāman maʿdūdātin* and *al-shamsu wa-l-qamaru biḥusbān* as the Qurʾanic counter-evidence; and he gives religion its description as *the rope extending from the heavens to the earth, continuous and without breakage*. Abu Malik concedes — *but what about the reality of what you have described?* — and the way is opened for the final part of the debate.
