# Show notes — EP01

**Title:** The True Sources of Knowledge

**Blurb:** Episode one of *The Master and the Disciple* walks the opening narrative arc that establishes the book's load-bearing doctrine of transmission. A group of *al-muʾminīn* and a group of *duʿāt al-dīn* approach one of their scholars to ask, all at once, how to express gratitude for the call, the knowledge, and the practice — and the Master answers that the only honest gratitude is a different deed in each case: obedience for the call, action for the knowledge, perseverance for the practice. He names the three-stage path of *balwā → hudā → taqwā*, names the constant remembrance of Allah as the tool that carries the seeker through them, and then narrates the story of a Persian seeker who, after his thirst for knowledge led him through mirage after mirage, was found by a teacher and made into one of the great scholars of *Sīnāʾ*. That scholar, in turn, travels across the Arab and non-Arab lands looking for a worthy disciple, and at the far edge of *al-Jazīra* he finds a group discussing religion without guidance — and chooses the youngest of them, best in character, his intellect complete and his heart awakened by reflection. The Master's design-of-creation sermon follows: Allah hid the spiritual realities in the shell of the exterior, *therefore immortalizing them*, and made the chosen ones the trustees of this hidden knowledge. The Qurʾanic instrument is named — hearing, sight, and heart — and the chapter answers the boy's request to be raised with two formulas: *knowledge is of no benefit until it is put into action; action is of no benefit until it is accepted by Allah*. The five conditions follow — *do not neglect what is entrusted to you, do not take it lightly, do not conceal it, do not come looking for him whenever you have a question, do not ask about anything until he himself brings it up, and do not mention him to your father* — and after the boy's period of trial, the *ʿahd sharīf* is taken. *True knowledge belongs to the Imam; accepting his walāyah is like paying the owner his due, while using the knowledge without it is theft.* The episode lands where the boy's tears do, on the night he knew with certainty he had become a member of the *Ḥizb Allāh*.
