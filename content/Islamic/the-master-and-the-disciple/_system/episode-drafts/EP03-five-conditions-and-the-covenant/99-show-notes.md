# Show notes — EP03

**Title:** Five Conditions and the Covenant

**Blurb:** Episode three of *The Master and the Disciple* delivers the structural climax of the opening narrative arc. The young man has begged to be raised; the scholar answers with the infant analogy and the graduated ladder of spiritual learning. Before he agrees to teach a single doctrine, he sets the precondition: knowledge is of no benefit until it has been put into action, and action is of no benefit until it is accepted, and the only thing Allah accepts is sincerity. He then imposes five conditions — and the fifth reveals the political stake: the boy's father is a known leader of a rival community that has rejected the Imam, and premature disclosure of the da'wa could destroy the mission. The conditions are protocols of taqiyya, not pedagogy. After a period of trial, the meeting is arranged. The scholar names the key of religion — the 'ahd Allah — and delivers the analogy: marriage and fornication are the same act, distinguished by the nikah; halal and haram are the same animal, distinguished by the dhabiha; sacred and stolen are the same religious practice, distinguished by walaya. Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq's apple-and-charity teaching locks the doctrine: a stolen apple given to charity propagates the haram and earns ten sins, not forty-five rewards. The episode lands on the title-teaches move — disciple from discipline, discipline from shari'a — and on the night of the oath, the boy's tears, his certainty of belonging to the Hizb Allah.
