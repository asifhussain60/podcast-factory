## [MISSING] — concepts present in source, unexplained without augment

### [MISSING] 1 — Author's rank and authority
- **Passage (¶ frontmatter):** "Author: Sayyidina Ja'far ibn Mansur al-Yaman"
- **What is missing:** The source gives only the name. A listener has no sense of *why* this author's word carries doctrinal weight.
- **Augment fills (01-true-sources, Session 1):** He was a high-ranking *da'i*, a *bab al-abwab* ("door of doors"), and a master of *ta'wil* (esoteric interpretation). This rank is what licenses the doctrinal authority of the dialogue.

### [MISSING] 2 — Genre signal: why a *story* and not a treatise
- **Passage (¶1–¶2):** The book opens with believers asking a Master about thanks, then immediately pivots to a narrative about "a man from the people of Persia."
- **What is missing:** The source never tells the reader this narrative framing is deliberate and unusual for Ismaili scholarly literature.
- **Augment fills (01-true-sources, Session 1):** Syedna Ja'far chose the *story* form on purpose — it acts as a map each seeker reads through their own experience. Different readers will extract different things; this is by design.

### [MISSING] 3 — The Persian seeker as archetype
- **Passage (¶3):** "a man from the people of Persia was struck by the calamity of ignorance…"
- **What is missing:** Why Persian? Why the desert-mirage imagery? The source assumes the listener knows this is a typological figure for the awakened soul.
- **Augment fills (01-true-sources):** The Persian represents the universal seeker; the mirage-then-water sequence is a deliberate symbol of false knowledge collapsing into authentic divine guidance.

### [MISSING] 4 — "Bayt al-Ma'mur" and "Mount Tur"
- **Passage (¶3):** "he became one of the scholars of [Mount] Tur and one of the dwellers of the Bayt al-Ma'mur."
- **What is missing:** Both are Qur'anic loci (Mount Sinai of Moses; the Frequented House circled by angels). The source gives the names with zero gloss.
- **Augment fills (general Ismaili scholarship + Session 1 framing):** These are the spiritual stations of those admitted to the company of the prophets and angels — the seeker has crossed from ignorance into the inner sanctum of knowledge.

### [MISSING] 5 — The boy's father and the social cost
- **Passage (¶27):** "a great sheikh of the Arabs — the most honorable and the most influential of them over the people of religion."
- **What is missing:** The dramatic stakes. The father is a leader of a community that *rejects* the source of true knowledge (the Imam).
- **Augment fills (02-spiritual-symbols, Session 5 [71–74]):** The Master will later impose five conditions including "don't mention me to your father" — the conflict is doctrinal and social, not personal. The boy is being asked to belong to two worlds at once.

### [MISSING] 6 — "Seventh fountain of wisdom from a firm origin"
- **Passage (¶28):** "for he is the seventh fountain of wisdom from a firm origin, his blessings upon the worlds…"
- **What is missing:** A direct cosmological reference Ismaili readers recognize (the cycle of seven prophets/imams, the Natiq–Asas–Imam structure). Opaque to a general listener.
- **Augment fills (Session 5 [84]):** Creation begins from Light through three words — *irada* (will), *amr* (command), *qawl* (utterance) — and unfolds in graded sevens. The "seventh fountain" sits at the apex of this descent.

### [MISSING] 7 — Zahir / batin distinction
- **Passage (¶28):** "the inward [batin] has been integrated as a teacher, and the outward [zahir] has expanded his dream"
- **What is missing:** The source uses these technical terms once, in passing, with no definition — yet the entire book rests on them.
- **Augment fills (03-knowledge-vs-actions, Session 8 [143–157]):** Three graded levels — *zahir* (outer/law/name), *batin* (inner/characteristic), *batin al-batin* (innermost/essential meaning). The egg analogy: shell, white, yolk. This is the spine of the whole dialogue.

### [MISSING] 8 — "Pact of Allah" / *'ahd Allah*
- **Passage (¶25):** "a deposit in the breasts of the righteous, the keepers of [the Word]… a trust which they discharge and a limit which they do not exceed"
- **What is missing:** The source alludes to a covenant without naming it. The boy will later take this oath.
- **Augment fills (Session 5 [81–83]):** The *'ahd Allah* — the formal oath of allegiance — is what distinguishes sacred knowledge from profane. Without it, the inner knowledge of *ta'wil* is forbidden. The augment makes explicit what the source only hints at.

### [MISSING] 9 — "Brothers who inherited wealth" — the umma analogy
- **Passage (¶41):** "they are like brothers who inherited great wealth from their father and divided it between themselves…"
- **What is missing:** The source uses the parable without identifying who the "brothers" are.
- **Augment fills:** The parable describes the post-prophetic split of the umma — some squandered the inheritance of prophetic knowledge, others preserved and grew it. The Master is claiming to belong to the preserving lineage.

### [MISSING] 10 — "Mathematical precision" of creation
- **Passage (¶25):** "made each thing equal to what He has decreed for it… highways of the pasture… outward and inward free of caprices"
- **What is missing:** The source compresses an entire cosmology into one paragraph.
- **Augment fills (Session 5):** Creation unfolds with mathematical precision from Light → Will → Command → Utterance → existence. Every visible thing is a *sign* (signifier) pointing to something prior. This unpacks the dense ¶25 doxology.

## [PARTIAL] — touched but ambiguous

### [PARTIAL] 1 — The three thanks
- **Passage (¶1):** "thanks for that to which you have called us, thanks for the knowledge… thanks for the work…"
- **Ambiguity:** Are these three separate duties, or three faces of one duty?
- **Clarification (Session 1):** They are three obligations on the seeker corresponding to three blessings — call, knowledge, action — and each requires its own form of gratitude: obedience to the Master, action upon the knowledge, patience and onward-calling for the work.

### [PARTIAL] 2 — "Affliction, guidance, piety" sequence
- **Passage (¶2):** "the first of which is affliction, the middle of which is guidance, and the last of which is piety."
- **Ambiguity:** Is this a temporal sequence or three simultaneous states?
- **Clarification:** Read with the Persian-seeker narrative immediately following — it is a stage-by-stage ascent: the trial of ignorance, then the moment of being found, then the settled life of *taqwa*. The structure recurs across the whole book.

### [PARTIAL] 3 — *ghulam* / "boy"
- **Passage (title + ¶7):** "Who are you, and from where, boy?"
- **Ambiguity:** Modern English "boy" carries no doctrinal weight; Urdu *ghulam* means "slave." Which is meant?
- **Clarification (Session 1):** Arabic *ghulam* = young male under guardianship — not slave (*'abd*). The Arabic title literally means "The Master and the Young Disciple under Guardianship." This shapes how the relationship is to be heard.

### [PARTIAL] 4 — "I am one of the dwellers of His sanctuary [Haram]"
- **Passage (¶8):** The Master's self-identification.
- **Ambiguity:** Is this geographical (Mecca) or symbolic (the inner sanctuary)?
- **Clarification:** In the book's idiom, the *haram* of God is the inner circle of those entrusted with the deposit — symbolic and doctrinal, not just geographic.

### [PARTIAL] 5 — "Praiseworthy / reprehensible" pairs
- **Passage (¶13–¶20):** Wonder, words, origins, calls — each split praiseworthy vs. reprehensible.
- **Ambiguity:** Why catalogue these dualities so insistently?
- **Clarification (Session 5):** The Master is establishing the diagnostic logic of the whole book — every act, word, and inclination has a forked origin (from Allah / from caprice) and a forked direction (toward Allah / toward something else). The seeker must learn to read the fork.

### [PARTIAL] 6 — "I have nothing to say — but I am a follower of [the right] saying."
- **Passage (¶22):** The Master's deflection.
- **Ambiguity:** Is this humility, or a doctrinal claim about authority?
- **Clarification:** It is a doctrinal claim — authentic teaching is never original invention; it is faithful transmission down a chain. The Master positions himself in a lineage, not as an originator.

### [PARTIAL] 7 — "He does not appear in being so as to be possessed by His attributes"
- **Passage (¶25):** Theology of divine transcendence.
- **Ambiguity:** Dense, near-impenetrable on first hearing.
- **Clarification (05-unity, Session 18 [430–446]):** Names and attributes are signs pointing to a Reality they do not exhaust — saying "fire is hot" does not cook food. The book later returns explicitly to this; ¶25 plants the seed.

### [PARTIAL] 8 — "Every claim is accepted only when it is truthful"
- **Passage (¶33):** The Master's first refusal to hand over knowledge cheaply.
- **Ambiguity:** Why refuse mercy when the boy is sincere?
- **Clarification:** The Master is enacting the pedagogy he names — knowledge is only useful when *acted* on, and action only counts when *accepted*. Mercy that bypasses the seeker's effort would void the knowledge itself.

### [PARTIAL] 9 — "It is neither identical with it nor wholly other"
- **Passage (¶39):** What the Master calls the boy to, vs. the general favor of religion.
- **Ambiguity:** What kind of subset-relation is this?
- **Clarification (Session 8):** It is the *batin* drawn from the *zahir* — a single chamber drawn from the Euphrates. The same water, but distilled, particular, and entrusted to specific keepers.

### [PARTIAL] 10 — "Their loss is the sign of their poverty toward us"
- **Passage (¶41):** The Master's claim of superiority over rival schools.
- **Ambiguity:** Could read as triumphalism; needs framing.
- **Clarification:** Read with the brothers-inheriting-wealth parable — the claim is *historical and custodial*, not moral. Other schools draw on the same wellspring; this lineage simply did not let it run dry.

## [CLARIFIED] — gloss available for episode framing

### [CLARIFIED] 1 — *da'i*
- **Gloss:** A "caller" — a teacher authorized within the Ismaili tradition to call seekers to the inner truth of religion. Not a missionary in the evangelical sense; closer to a graded scholar-guide.

### [CLARIFIED] 2 — *ta'wil*
- **Gloss:** Esoteric interpretation — drawing the inner spiritual meaning out of the outer text and form. Distinct from *tafsir* (exegesis of the apparent sense).

### [CLARIFIED] 3 — *zahir*
- **Gloss:** The outer, visible, formal layer of religion — the law, the rites, the *names* of things.

### [CLARIFIED] 4 — *batin*
- **Gloss:** The inner spiritual layer beneath the outer — the *characteristics* of religion, what its forms point to.

### [CLARIFIED] 5 — *batin al-batin*
- **Gloss:** The innermost layer beneath the inner — the essential meaning itself, accessible only to a sanctified scholar (*'alim rabbani*).

### [CLARIFIED] 6 — *'ahd Allah*
- **Gloss:** The pact or oath of allegiance to God through his appointed authority. The formal threshold that turns the seeker into an initiate.

### [CLARIFIED] 7 — *ghulam*
- **Gloss:** A young man under a guardian's care. Not "slave" (*'abd*); closer to "ward" or "young disciple."

### [CLARIFIED] 8 — *shari'a* (here: *shara'i' al-zahira*)
- **Gloss:** The revealed paths — the outer obligations and observances of religion.

### [CLARIFIED] 9 — *'alim rabbani*
- **Gloss:** A "sanctified scholar" — one whose knowledge has fused with action and whose spirit is connected to certainty (*yaqin*).

### [CLARIFIED] 10 — *bab al-abwab*
- **Gloss:** "Door of doors" — a senior rank within the Ismaili hierarchy of teachers, indicating one who opens access to deeper inner knowledge.

## [ANALOGY] — modern bridges for esoteric passages

> Each analogy is `[TEACHING-CONTEXT]` — useful for episode framing only, never attributed to Syedna Ja'far or to the source text.

### [ANALOGY] 1 — Zahir / batin / batin al-batin
- **Passage:** ¶39 + Session 8.
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** Like an organization — the *name on the building* is the outer layer; the *org chart* is the inner; the *culture that actually decides things* is the innermost. You cannot understand a company by reading its name on the door.

### [ANALOGY] 2 — Names and attributes vs. essential reality
- **Passage:** Session 18 [435] — "fire is hot."
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** Reading the word "spicy" on a menu does not burn your tongue. Theology that stays at the level of words is a menu, not a meal.

### [ANALOGY] 3 — Customized desires and the fragmenting soul
- **Passage:** ¶1 + Session 1 framing of *adab*.
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** The infinitely customized coffee order — soy milk, two pumps, half-caf, extra foam. Each personal calibration looks like freedom and is actually fragmentation.

### [ANALOGY] 4 — Knowledge requiring preparation
- **Passage:** ¶31 — "no scholar except after ignorance."
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** Like trying to install graduate-level software on a machine without an operating system — the content has nothing to attach to. First the substrate, then the lesson.

### [ANALOGY] 5 — The signpost is not the destination
- **Passage:** ¶25 — signs pointing to Allah.
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** GPS coordinates are not the place. A map of Paris is not Paris. The whole book turns on refusing to confuse the pointer with what it points to.

### [ANALOGY] 6 — Inheritance squandered vs. invested
- **Passage:** ¶41 — brothers and the inheritance.
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** Two siblings inherit a family business. One sells the equipment for cash and burns through it; the other reinvests and compounds. Twenty years later, one is begging from the other. The capital was the same; the stewardship was not.

### [ANALOGY] 7 — Mirage vs. water
- **Passage:** ¶3 — the Persian's mirage.
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** Confusing the glow of a screen with the warmth of a room. From a distance both look like life; only one of them actually sustains you.

### [ANALOGY] 8 — Pact / oath as license to inner knowledge
- **Passage:** Session 5 [79–83].
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** A medical license — anyone can read a textbook, but only the licensed practitioner is *authorized* to act on it. The oath is not the knowledge; it is the standing to use it.

### [ANALOGY] 9 — The egg
- **Passage:** Session 8 [153] — Master's own teaching device.
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** Shell, white, yolk — three layers, one egg. Cracking the shell is not destroying the egg; it is the only way to reach the yolk. Treat the law as the shell and you will starve.

### [ANALOGY] 10 — Following the saying, not authoring it
- **Passage:** ¶22 — "I am a follower of [the right] saying."
- **Analogy [TEACHING-CONTEXT]:** A scientist citing a peer-reviewed result rather than inventing one on the spot. Authority comes from faithful transmission, not from originality. The Master claims a chain, not a chair.
