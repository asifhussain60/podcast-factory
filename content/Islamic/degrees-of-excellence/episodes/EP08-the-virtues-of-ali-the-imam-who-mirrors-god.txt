# The Virtues Of Ali The Imam Who Mirrors God

## Opening directive
Hosts: John (male, scholar) leads; Hannah (female, seeker) questions. Open with a brief, warm welcome — greet the listener, name the book (al-Naysaburi's *Establishing the Imamate*) and its author, and preview in one sentence: its summit is one figure whose dealings mirror God's own. No cold open. Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. Then land the spine verbatim (1 of 3): "The imam treats people as God treats them — giving to the faithful and the faithless alike, seeking nothing in return — and that, not the power of a king, is the mark of God's vicegerent."

## Name discipline
Fixed English label per figure, every time — never speak Arabic names or the book's Arabic title.
- al-Naysaburi → "the author."
- The Prophet → "the Prophet"; at first mention only add "peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family."
- The summit figure → the Commander of the Faithful / the Father of Imams / the first imam; at first mention only add "peace be upon him." Never say "Imam Ali." Never speak the forbidden pairing of the leadership-title and the personal name.
- Reigning imam → "the imam of the age," unnamed. Cited philosopher → "an earlier philosopher of the same tradition." Prophets by English name.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- mutimm: the perfector
- ghulat: the exaggerators
- ahl al-haqq: the people of truth
- ahl al-batil: the people of falsehood

## Three-part focus
Arc: crisis / failed answer / pivot / stakes, following the author's order. Cite the Quran in plain English as (chapter N, verse M) — invent none.
Beat 1 — The summit of summits. Every ordered kind has one peak: gold among metals, the sun among the stars. So the line of imams rises to one point — the Father of Imams, head, spirit, and heart of the body of guidance, the perfector who gathers every prophet before him. The imam of the age continues that summit now.
Beat 2 — The imam who mirrors God (pivot). Philosophy called itself resemblance to the Creator; only the imam achieves it. God gives to pious and impious alike, hides the means of gain, draws near and withdraws — so faith is never bought. The imam does the same, and unlike any king asks nothing back: the worship of the free, not the merchant or slave (chapter 13, verse 26). Spine verbatim (2 of 3).
Beat 3 — The vicegerent and the prayer. Short sight judges him by ordinary rulers; the exaggerators make him a god; the truth runs between — the Universal Vicegerent, a shadow imitating what casts it (chapter 38, verse 26). Close on the author's own supplication of gratitude and service.

## Host dynamic
John (male, scholar) leads; Hannah (female, seeker) challenges at least three times and concedes once. Qualify doctrine as "the classical Ismaili reading." Steelman why impartial giving looks unjust before showing it protects faith.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — treating the faithless as well as the faithful sounds unjust, not praiseworthy"; "How is this different from just divinizing the man?"

## Tone constraints
Exactly three governing images, all from the source:
Analogy 1: nature gathers its excellence to a summit, so the line of imams rises to one peak.
Analogy 2: the heart and the limbs — every limb lives from one central organ, as guidance lives through the Father of Imams.
Analogy 3: the shadow that imitates what casts it — the vicegerent reproduces God's conduct exactly, yet is never God. No invented analogies.

## Landing
Close on the listener's own life: the imam gives without being paid, keeping the soul between fear and hope. Leave a real question tied to action — where do you serve for a wage, and what would it take to give and worship as the free do, this week? Just before it, spine verbatim (3 of 3).

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No modern framings, surprise filler, cross-chapter references, "deep dive," or faux-profound openers. Never name the reigning imam or any Fatimid caliph. R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud.
