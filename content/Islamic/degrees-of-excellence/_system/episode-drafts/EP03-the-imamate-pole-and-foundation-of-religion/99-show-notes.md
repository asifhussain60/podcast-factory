# Show notes — EP03

**Title:** The Imamate, Pole of Religion

**Blurb:** 'The treatise opens, and al-Naysaburi begins with a claim most believers would find backwards: the true pole of religion is not the messenger who brings the law but the imam who guards it — and you cannot affirm the one without first affirming the other. He argues that the world is never for a moment without an imam while the messenger comes and goes; that God Himself called the imam a messenger and the messenger an imam; and that the necessity of the imamate is written into intellect and human nature as surely as the necessity of the Creator. Then, having conceded that people dispute the imam exactly as they disputed God, he lays down the hinge of the whole book: that reality gives two witnesses to the truth — one established by sacred law and one compelled by nature, the horizons and the souls themselves — and that when both testify and the intellect weighs them without bias, the truth of the imamate becomes impossible to miss.
