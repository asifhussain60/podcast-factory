# Show notes — EP06

**Title:** Worship and Law Without the Imam

**Blurb:** 'Having proved the imam''s authority from nature and reason, al-Naysaburi now finds it hidden inside the everyday obligations of Islam. Human beings, he argues, are born knowing nothing and must be taught in a chain that ends in one taught by God alone — so the imam is God''s standing proof over creation, and the perfect circle, the number one, and the indivisible point all image his uniqueness. Then he turns to the rites: the qibla you face and the leader you follow in prayer share the very root of the word imam, and the congregation multiplies your reward because it is performed behind him; the pilgrimage begins with an intention aimed at him, and the Ka''ba in its barren valley teaches that he is reached only through hardship. Alms may be collected only by the imam, holy war waged only under his banner, and the prescribed penalties carried out only by his authority — so that the leaders who claim the office, trusted by their followers with blood and property but not with alms, and bound by their own rule that a sinner cannot judge or testify, refute themselves. What remains, al-Naysaburi concludes, is that the true imamate belongs to the purified progeny of the Prophet''s house.
