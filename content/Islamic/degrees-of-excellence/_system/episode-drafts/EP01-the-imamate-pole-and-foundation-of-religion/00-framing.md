# The Imamate, Pole of Religion

## Opening directive
Two hosts, both unnamed: a male scholar leads, a female seeker questions. Never speak either host's name. Open with a brief, warm welcome — name the book (al-Naysaburi's treatise *Establishing the Imamate*) and its author, and preview: the treatise puts the imam, not the messenger, at the center of religion. No cold open. Target a 30 to 45 minute in-depth conversation. Then state this sentence, word for word: "The imamate is the pole and foundation of religion, the office that never lapses while the messenger comes and goes."

## Naming
Fixed English label per figure, every time — never speak Arabic names or book titles.
- al-Naysaburi → "the author" (named once, then "the author").
- The Prophet → "the Prophet"; first mention only add "peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family."
- First imam → first mention add "peace be upon him"; Rotation: the Father of Imams / the Commander of the Faithful / the first imam. Never pair the leadership-title with the personal name.
- Reigning imam → "the imam of the age," unnamed. Abraham → "Abraham"; Ismail → "Ishmael." Ja'far al-Sadiq → "the sixth imam." Rumi → "a poet."

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE using its phonetic form. Never say the original spelling and the phonetic form back-to-back.
- imamate: IM-uh-mayt — the office; three syllables
- imam: i-MAAM
- al-Naysaburi: an-nay-saa-BOO-ree — the author
- qutb: KOOTB — the pole
- asas al-din: ah-SAAS ad-DEEN — the foundation of religion
- tawhid: tow-HEED
- ta'wil: taa-WEEL
- tashbih: tash-BEEH

## Central tensions to reach
Arc: crisis / failed answer / pivot / stakes. The hosts MUST surface every one of these, by name:
- The imam before the messenger — affirming the imamate already affirms the messengership, because whoever knows the imam knows the messenger, but not the reverse.
- A necessity conceded, then contested — as with the Creator, people grant the imam's necessity, then dispute who he is; the dispute proves the office, not the doubt.
- Two witnesses, one verdict — one set by law, one compelled by nature, the horizons and the souls; the hinge for reading nature as proof.

Repeat that same sentence, word for word, here at the pivot.

## Host dynamic
The scholar leads; the seeker supplies the friction — challenges at least three times with pushback and concedes once. Qualify by school — "the classical Ismaili reading."
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — how can the guardian of the revelation outrank the one who brought it?"; "That sounds like wordplay — every misused title becomes 'proof.'"

## Tone constraints
Follow the author's order: the imam's pole and priority first, then necessity in nature and intellect, then the two creations and two witnesses. Do not import the concrete ladder of excellence. Use only the images below, all drawn from the source, and no others:
- the pole — the pivot a millstone turns on; remove it and everything flies apart.
- the religious stars — imams the stars, prophets the spheres that hold them; spheres alone bear no fruit.
- the two witnesses in court — when legal testimony is tampered with, the witness of nature steps in.
Give Quran references inline in plain English as (chapter N, verse M): Ishmael a messenger (chapter 19, verse 54), Abraham made an imam (chapter 2, verse 124), the two witnesses (chapter 2, verse 282), the signs on the horizons and in the selves (chapter 41, verse 53). Invent no others. Never speak Arabic surah names.

## Landing
Close on the human turn: the reasoning that makes the imam the ever-present pole means a soul without a true guide faces a ruin the body never risks. Just before it, repeat that same sentence word for word: "The imamate is the pole and foundation of religion, the office that never lapses while the messenger comes and goes." End on a question tied to action — which counterfeit are you still building your life on?

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary and framings)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No modern framings, surprise filler, cross-episode or cross-chapter references, "deep dive," or "today we'll discuss." Repeat that sentence word for word at the opening, at the pivot, and at the close.
