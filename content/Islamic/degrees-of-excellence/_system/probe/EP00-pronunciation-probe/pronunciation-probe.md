# Pronunciation walkthrough — degrees-of-excellence

This is a short spoken walkthrough whose ONLY purpose is to say a set of
Arabic-derived terms aloud, in order, so their pronunciation can be checked.
Walk through every numbered item in sequence. For each item, say the term
clearly, read the sentence it appears in, and move on. Do not skip any item.

Every sentence below is taken verbatim from the book, across 6 chapters, so the terms are heard in the same
prose the full episodes will be built from.

## Part 1 — People and scholar names

1. Next, say **an-nay-saa-BOO-ree** — as in: “That is a bold way to begin a book, and it is exactly where al-Naysaburi (النيسابوري) chooses to plant his flag.”
2. Next, say **al-Kirmani** — as in: “Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani (الكرماني), in his Rahat al-Aql, mapped the ranks of religion onto the very structure of the cosmos, so that the imam stands at the human summit as the outermost sphere stands at the summit of the heavens — the point where the whole ladder of being is completed.”
3. Next, say **AHL az-ZAH-hir** — as in: “The bitter, foul, thorned ones stand for the leaders of the literalists, the ahl al-zahir, the people of the surface who hold to the outer husk of religion and never reach its inner sense.”
4. Next, say **MAGH-rib** — as in: “The sun that rises in the west is the imam who rises in the Maghrib — and that interpretation, al-Naysaburi adds, has been borne out, witnessed with his own eyes by the grace of God.”
5. Next, say **TAH-haa** — as in: “If their imamate is false, and the imamate is truly one of the foundations of religion, then it belongs to the pure progeny of Taha and Yasin — the family protected from corruption and falsehood, purified of every blame.”
6. Next, say **YAA-seen** — as in: “If their imamate is false, and the imamate is truly one of the foundations of religion, then it belongs to the pure progeny of Taha and Yasin — the family protected from corruption and falsehood, purified of every blame.”
7. Next, say **ah-SAAS ad-DEEN** — as in: “The second is asas al-din, the foundation of religion — the buried course of stones on which a whole building rests, the part you never see but without which nothing above it can stand.”
8. Next, say **AHL al-HAQQ** — as in: “And he withholds them from the undeserving, drawing the line that must be drawn — distinguishing the people of truth, the ahl al-haqq, from the people of falsehood, the ahl al-batil.”
9. Next, say **DAA-i-rat ad-DEEN** — as in: “The imam is the circle of religion, da'irat al-din, the bounding figure within whose command and prohibition all the ranks of the mission fall and from whom they all draw their benefit.”
10. Next, say **AHL al-BAH-til** — as in: “And he withholds them from the undeserving, drawing the line that must be drawn — distinguishing the people of truth, the ahl al-haqq, from the people of falsehood, the ahl al-batil.”
11. Next, say **NOOR al-i-MAA-ma** — as in: “When he arose, al-Naysaburi says, he commanded that fire be glorified — and if the reports about this are sound, it was a symbolic indication of the glorification of the light of the imamate, the nur al-imama, the radiance that the imam carries and transmits.”
12. Next, say **gha-DEER KHOOM** — as in: “Returning from his farewell pilgrimage, at a pool called Ghadir Khumm, Muhammad halted the caravan, took Ali by the hand, and raised it before the assembled people: > Whoever's master I am, this Ali is his master.”
13. Next, say **Rahat al-Aql** — as in: “Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani (الكرماني), in his Rahat al-Aql, mapped the ranks of religion onto the very structure of the cosmos, so that the imam stands at the human summit as the outermost sphere stands at the summit of the heavens — the point where the whole ladder of being is completed.”

## Part 2 — Places

14. Next, say **BAYT al-MAAL** — as in: “He alone guards the treasury of the Muslims, the bayt al-mal, which is under his guarantee; he alone ransoms captives from enemy hands, protects the border towns, appoints the commanders under whom armies march.”

## Part 3 — Technical and doctrinal terms

15. Next, say **ithbat** — as in: “irming the Imamate, in Arabic the Kitab ithbat al-imama, by Ahmad b. Ibrahim al-Naysab”.
16. Next, say **za-KAAH** — as in: “Begin with alms, the zakat (الزكاة), which he calls among the most emphatic proofs of the imamate.”
17. Next, say **QIB-lah** — as in: “The ritual prayer, al-Naysaburi argues — its arrangement, its standing, its direction toward the qibla (القبلة), the position of the prayer-leader at the head of the rows — is itself a powerful indication of the imamate, and it starts with a piece of language.”
18. Next, say **hu-DOOD** — as in: “Finally the prescribed penalties, the hudud (الحدود) — the amputations, the floggings, the stonings that the sacred law lays down for grave crimes.”
19. Next, say **vice-JEER-uhnt** — as in: “It is therefore necessary, al-Naysaburi says, to believe in the supreme leadership of the imam of the time — to know that he is the head of the world and the vicegerent of God over His creation, the khalifa through whom God's governance reaches what He made.”
20. Next, say **FIE** — as in: “What matters to al-Naysaburi is the word the verse uses for that bestowal — fay'.”
21. Next, say **NAHSS** — as in: “He appointed his son Shem to the legateeship and the imamate by designation, by nass, an explicit naming and handing-on, and by tawqif, a formal appointment.”
22. Next, say **tow-HEED** — as in: “And it is through the imamate, he adds, that three of the greatest things a person can know are actually attained — knowledge of God's absolute unity, which the tradition calls tawhid; knowledge of the revealed Message; and knowledge of the sacred law together with its inner interpretation, its ta'wil, the hidden meaning beneath the plain command.”
23. Next, say **wa-LAA-ya** — as in: “Al-Naysaburi's point is that a believer remains under the care of the mission throughout his prayer for exactly as long as he stays within the allegiance of the imam — the walaya, the bond of loyalty that ties the follower to the one appointed to lead him.”
24. Next, say **KHOOMS** — as in: “And to compensate the family for what was forbidden them, God assigned them instead the fifth, the khums (الخمس), a designated share of the spoils.”
25. Next, say **KOOTB** — as in: “The first is qutb (قطب), the pole — the fixed pivot around which a wheel or a millstone turns, the still point that lets everything else revolve without flying apart.”
26. Next, say **tas-LEEM** — as in: “That is taslim shown from the inside — the recognition that an act which looks like sheer damage from below may be rescue seen from above, and that the whole distance between the two is only the length of one's own sight.”
27. Next, say **SAA-is** — as in: “A world with no head, no leader, no master, no sa'is to steer its worldly and religious affairs, would not limp along in disorder; it would rot.”
28. Next, say **tash-BEEH** — as in: “Others went the opposite way and described Him with attributes that amounted to likening Him to His creation, the error the tradition calls tashbih, anthropomorphism, dressing God in human features until He borders on being just another creature; or else they stripped Him of every attribute at all, emptying Him out until nothing was left to worship.”
29. Next, say **KHUT-bah** — as in: “The Friday prayer, he notes, is not valid without the sermon, the khutba (الخطبة), delivered from the pulpit — and that sermon is invalid without the imam or someone he has appointed to deliver it.”
30. Next, say **ra-EE-ya** — as in: “People everywhere are called subjects, ra'iya — and ra'iya, he notes, means precisely "a flock under a shepherd's care." The very language a society uses for itself confesses that it expects to be shepherded.”
31. Next, say **ar-KAAN** — as in: “Of the four elements the ancients recognized as the raw stuff of the physical world — the arkan, the pillars from which every composite body is assembled — al-Naysaburi puts fire at the top.”
32. Next, say **goo-LAAT** — as in: “On the other side stand those who look at the imam with exaggeration, and, dazzled, claim divinity for him — the ghulat, the extremists who inflate the office past its truth.”
33. Next, say **maf-DOOL** — as in: “The title "the excellent," al-fadil, cannot be laid on the one who is surpassed by another, the mafdul.”
34. Next, say **yaa-SOOB** — as in: “The musk-giving gazelle and the honey-producing bee, he says — and especially the bee's own chief, its ya'sub, the lead bee of the hive — are an indication of the imam.”
35. Next, say **mas-BOOQ** — as in: “The title "the one who precedes," al-sabiq, cannot be laid on the one who is preceded, the masbuq.”
36. Next, say **NOOTQ** — as in: “A human being surpasses the animal by one further spirit still: the spirit of articulate reason, the ruh al-nutq, the power of speech and thought.”
37. Next, say **tir-YAHQ** — as in: “Among plants, al-Naysaburi says, certain mild medicines have been refined and perfected to such a degree that they become antidotes — tiryaq, the healing compounds able to pull a person back from the very brink of death.”
38. Next, say **FAL-tah** — as in: “Ascending the pulpit after Abu Bakr, Umar declared that the oath of allegiance given to Abu Bakr had been a falta — a hasty, unconsidered slip, a decision made in a rush — but that God had averted its evil, and that anyone who attempted the like of it again should be killed.”
39. Next, say **moo-TIMM** — as in: “This is the word he settles on: the Father of Imams is the perfector — in the Arabic, the mutimm, the one who completes and brings to fullness — of every one of them who has passed away.”
