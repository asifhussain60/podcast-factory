# Term glossary — degrees-of-excellence

A glossary of 39 terms used in this book. Each entry gives the term, the
way it is said aloud, and the sentence it appears in.

Every quoted sentence is taken verbatim from the book, across 6 chapters, so each term appears in the
prose it belongs to.

## Part 1 — People and scholar names

### 1. al-Naysaburi

Said aloud: al-Naysaburi

In the book: “That is a bold way to begin a book, and it is exactly where al-Naysaburi (النيسابوري) chooses to plant his flag.”

### 2. al-Kirmani

Said aloud: al-Kirmani

In the book: “Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani (الكرماني), in his Rahat al-Aql, mapped the ranks of religion onto the very structure of the cosmos, so that the imam stands at the human summit as the outermost sphere stands at the summit of the heavens — the point where the whole ladder of being is completed.”

### 3. Maghrib

Said aloud: Maghrib

In the book: “The sun that rises in the west is the imam who rises in the Maghrib — and that interpretation, al-Naysaburi adds, has been borne out, witnessed with his own eyes by the grace of God.”

### 4. Taha

Said aloud: Taha

In the book: “If their imamate is false, and the imamate is truly one of the foundations of religion, then it belongs to the pure progeny of Taha and Yasin — the family protected from corruption and falsehood, purified of every blame.”

### 5. Yasin

Said aloud: Yasin

In the book: “If their imamate is false, and the imamate is truly one of the foundations of religion, then it belongs to the pure progeny of Taha and Yasin — the family protected from corruption and falsehood, purified of every blame.”

### 6. Ghadir Khumm

Said aloud: Ghadir Khumm

In the book: “Returning from his farewell pilgrimage, at a pool called Ghadir Khumm, Muhammad halted the caravan, took Ali by the hand, and raised it before the assembled people: Whoever's master I am, this Ali is his master.”

### 7. Rahat al-Aql

Said aloud: Rahat al-Aql

In the book: “Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani (الكرماني), in his Rahat al-Aql, mapped the ranks of religion onto the very structure of the cosmos, so that the imam stands at the human summit as the outermost sphere stands at the summit of the heavens — the point where the whole ladder of being is completed.”

## Part 2 — Places

### 8. bayt al-mal

Said aloud: bayt al-mal

In the book: “He alone guards the treasury of the Muslims, the bayt al-mal, which is under his guarantee; he alone ransoms captives from enemy hands, protects the border towns, appoints the commanders under whom armies march.”

## Part 3 — Technical and doctrinal terms

### 9. ithbat

Said aloud: ithbat

### 10. zakat

Said aloud: za-KAAT

In the book: “Begin with alms, the zakat (الزكاة), which he calls among the most emphatic proofs of the imamate.”

### 11. qibla

Said aloud: qibla

In the book: “The ritual prayer, al-Naysaburi argues — its arrangement, its standing, its direction toward the qibla (القبلة), the position of the prayer-leader at the head of the rows — is itself a powerful indication of the imamate, and it starts with a piece of language.”

### 12. hudud

Said aloud: hudud

In the book: “Finally the prescribed penalties, the hudud (الحدود) — the amputations, the floggings, the stonings that the sacred law lays down for grave crimes.”

### 13. vicegerent

Said aloud: vicegerent

In the book: “It is therefore necessary, al-Naysaburi says, to believe in the supreme leadership of the imam of the time — to know that he is the head of the world and the vicegerent of God over His creation, the khalifa through whom God's governance reaches what He made.”

### 14. fay

Said aloud: fay

In the book: “What matters to al-Naysaburi is the word the verse uses for that bestowal — fay'.”

### 15. nass

Said aloud: nass

In the book: “He appointed his son Shem to the legateeship and the imamate by designation, by nass, an explicit naming and handing-on, and by tawqif, a formal appointment.”

### 16. tawhid

Said aloud: tow-HEED

In the book: “And it is through the imamate, he adds, that three of the greatest things a person can know are actually attained — knowledge of God's absolute unity, which the tradition calls tawhid; knowledge of the revealed Message; and knowledge of the sacred law together with its inner interpretation, its ta'wil, the hidden meaning beneath the plain command.”

### 17. ahl al-zahir

Said aloud: ahl al-zahir

In the book: “The bitter, foul, thorned ones stand for the leaders of the literalists, the ahl al-zahir, the people of the surface who hold to the outer husk of religion and never reach its inner sense.”

### 18. walaya

Said aloud: walaya

In the book: “Al-Naysaburi's point is that a believer remains under the care of the mission throughout his prayer for exactly as long as he stays within the allegiance of the imam — the walaya, the bond of loyalty that ties the follower to the one appointed to lead him.”

### 19. khums

Said aloud: khums

In the book: “And to compensate the family for what was forbidden them, God assigned them instead the fifth, the khums (الخمس), a designated share of the spoils.”

### 20. qutb

Said aloud: qutb

In the book: “The first is qutb (قطب), the pole — the fixed pivot around which a wheel or a millstone turns, the still point that lets everything else revolve without flying apart.”

### 21. taslim

Said aloud: taslim

In the book: “That is taslim shown from the inside — the recognition that an act which looks like sheer damage from below may be rescue seen from above, and that the whole distance between the two is only the length of one's own sight.”

### 22. sais

Said aloud: sais

In the book: “A world with no head, no leader, no master, no sa'is to steer its worldly and religious affairs, would not limp along in disorder; it would rot.”

### 23. tashbih

Said aloud: tash-BEEH

In the book: “Others went the opposite way and described Him with attributes that amounted to likening Him to His creation, the error the tradition calls tashbih, anthropomorphism, dressing God in human features until He borders on being just another creature; or else they stripped Him of every attribute at all, emptying Him out until nothing was left to worship.”

### 24. khutba

Said aloud: khutba

In the book: “The Friday prayer, he notes, is not valid without the sermon, the khutba (الخطبة), delivered from the pulpit — and that sermon is invalid without the imam or someone he has appointed to deliver it.”

### 25. arkan

Said aloud: arkan

In the book: “Of the four elements the ancients recognized as the raw stuff of the physical world — the arkan, the pillars from which every composite body is assembled — al-Naysaburi puts fire at the top.”

### 26. asas al-din

Said aloud: asas al-din

In the book: “The second is asas al-din, the foundation of religion — the buried course of stones on which a whole building rests, the part you never see but without which nothing above it can stand.”

### 27. ghulat

Said aloud: ghulat

In the book: “On the other side stand those who look at the imam with exaggeration, and, dazzled, claim divinity for him — the ghulat, the extremists who inflate the office past its truth.”

### 28. mafdul

Said aloud: mafdul

In the book: “The title "the excellent," al-fadil, cannot be laid on the one who is surpassed by another, the mafdul.”

### 29. raiya

Said aloud: raiya

In the book: “People everywhere are called subjects, ra'iya — and ra'iya, he notes, means precisely "a flock under a shepherd's care." The very language a society uses for itself confesses that it expects to be shepherded.”

### 30. ya'sub

Said aloud: ya'sub

In the book: “The musk-giving gazelle and the honey-producing bee, he says — and especially the bee's own chief, its ya'sub, the lead bee of the hive — are an indication of the imam.”

### 31. ahl al-haqq

Said aloud: ahl al-haqq

In the book: “And he withholds them from the undeserving, drawing the line that must be drawn — distinguishing the people of truth, the ahl al-haqq, from the people of falsehood, the ahl al-batil.”

### 32. masbuq

Said aloud: masbuq

In the book: “The title "the one who precedes," al-sabiq, cannot be laid on the one who is preceded, the masbuq.”

### 33. nutq

Said aloud: nutq

In the book: “A human being surpasses the animal by one further spirit still: the spirit of articulate reason, the ruh al-nutq, the power of speech and thought.”

### 34. tiryaq

Said aloud: tiryaq

In the book: “Among plants, al-Naysaburi says, certain mild medicines have been refined and perfected to such a degree that they become antidotes — tiryaq, the healing compounds able to pull a person back from the very brink of death.”

### 35. da'irat al-din

Said aloud: da'irat al-din

In the book: “The imam is the circle of religion, da'irat al-din, the bounding figure within whose command and prohibition all the ranks of the mission fall and from whom they all draw their benefit.”

### 36. ahl al-batil

Said aloud: ahl al-batil

In the book: “And he withholds them from the undeserving, drawing the line that must be drawn — distinguishing the people of truth, the ahl al-haqq, from the people of falsehood, the ahl al-batil.”

### 37. falta

Said aloud: falta

In the book: “Ascending the pulpit after Abu Bakr, Umar declared that the oath of allegiance given to Abu Bakr had been a falta — a hasty, unconsidered slip, a decision made in a rush — but that God had averted its evil, and that anyone who attempted the like of it again should be killed.”

### 38. mutimm

Said aloud: mutimm

In the book: “This is the word he settles on: the Father of Imams is the perfector — in the Arabic, the mutimm, the one who completes and brings to fullness — of every one of them who has passed away.”

### 39. nur al-imama

Said aloud: nur al-imama

In the book: “When he arose, al-Naysaburi says, he commanded that fire be glorified — and if the reports about this are sound, it was a symbolic indication of the glorification of the light of the imamate, the nur al-imama, the radiance that the imam carries and transmits.”
