# Degrees of Excellence: The Peak of Every Kind

## The witness of nature

Annotated structure. The imamate rests on two sworn witnesses; this stretch of the book calls the second one to the stand.

Whole: the case for the imamate as pole and foundation of religion.
Parts:
- Witness one, the revealed law. Named in the prior section, let stand.
- Witness two, the natural world. Called to the stand here, examined domain by domain.
Annotation, witness one: scripture speaks for the necessity of a supreme guide.
Annotation, witness two: creation is made to speak the same testimony, specimen by specimen, until the pattern is impossible to miss.

Contrast pair. The blueprint drawn in the introduction against the building raised here.

Column A: The introduction's preview
- Form: an abstract engine, the plain fact that things are not equal refined into a hierarchy of better and best.
- Detail level: a bare note that the ladder climbs from the elements to the stars to gold to a ruby to the human being.
- Concrete specimens: none supplied.

Column B: This section's construction
- Form: al-Naysaburi builds the ladder himself, in his own order.
- Detail level: the concrete cases the preview had to skip.
- Concrete specimens: the fire Abraham honored, the sun that rises in the west, the jewels by color, the seven grains, the date palm, the antidotes and the poisons, the fragrant and the foul plants, the noblest animals, the honeybee and its chief.

Shared row: both describe one ladder of excellence. The preview named it; this section proves it with the world's own contents.

The governing question, held open behind every specimen:

> If even the metals and the grains and the beasts each have a summit, how could the human world be the one ordered thing in creation with no summit at all?

## The peak of every kind

Comparison matrix. Each domain of creation, its summit, the trait that crowns it, and the imamate parallel it teaches.

Rows: Elements | Planets | Metals | Jewels | Grains | Trees
Columns: Summit of the domain | Crowning trait | What the summit does | Imamate parallel

- Elements: summit is fire | crowning trait is that it illumines, moves, lifts, and rises | it quickens the other three and keeps them from inertness | the imam is the highest religious pillar of his age, through whom souls are given light
- Planets: summit is the sun | crowning trait is indispensable light and warmth | without it no eye sees and nothing grows, not a plant, not an animal, not a child | the imam is the sun of religion, through whom insight is enlightened and souls shine with guidance
- Metals: summit is gold | crowning trait is that it fixes value and is not destroyed by fire or water | it is the medium through which people settle every transaction | the imam is the most excellent religious and intellectual substance of his age, the measure of religious worth
- Jewels: summit is the red ruby | crowning trait is incorruptibility under fire and flood | it keeps its essence, radiance, and weight unchanged | the imam is the pinnacle of all creation in his time, uncorrupted by pressure
- Grains: summit is wheat | crowning trait is that it is most balanced and best builds the body | it feeds and nourishes the human frame | the imam is the peak from whom souls draw livelihood, life, and knowledge
- Trees: summit is the date palm | crowning trait is that it most resembles the human being | it comes male and female, must be fertilized, wastes no part | the imam is the human summit the whole catalogue climbs toward

Contrast pair. Why fire earns the top rung over the other three elements.

Column A: Cold, dryness, humidity
- Posture: receptive.
- Action: they take on qualities and are worked upon.
- Fate without fire: they lapse toward inertness.

Column B: Fire
- Posture: active.
- Action: source of luminosity, heat, and upward motion; it stirs the others into life.
- Standing: it gives rather than receives.

Shared row: all four are the raw pillars, the arkan, from which composite bodies are assembled. Only one of them originates and quickens.

Annotated structure. The hidden criterion the whole catalogue applies without ever restating it.

Whole: what makes a member the summit of its kind.
Rule: a domain is crowned not by the member most acted upon but by the one that most gives, that originates, that quickens, that lifts.
Annotation: the summit is the giver, the source, the one on whom the rest depend for the very activity that makes them what they are. Hold this criterion; it is applied to every specimen that follows.

Process flow. Fire read rightly against fire read wrongly.

Start: fire, the summit of the elements, carries a hidden meaning.
  Branch A, the true reading:
    Abraham commands his people to glorify the fire and never let it be extinguished
    The real intent points past the flame to the imamate
    The light that no one is permitted to put out through the injustice of false claimants
  Branch B, the false reading:
    The fire-worshippers take the command literally
    They build a cult around burning an actual fire
    They grasp the symbol and never reach the reality it stood in for
End: the same mistake will recur with the sun, the stars, and the jewels.

Sealing citation, the extinguishers against the light that will not go out:

> They would like to extinguish God's light with their mouths, but God will not allow it until He perfects His light, even if the unbelievers detest it. (chapter 9, verse 32)

Contrast pair. The sun rising in the west, read literally against read esoterically.

Column A: The literal reading
- Claim: the physical sun swerves from its fixed orbit and rises in the west.
- Verdict: impossible astronomy; if the sun left its orbit for the blink of an eye the whole world would be destroyed.
- Fate: ruled out by reason and nature alike.

Column B: The esoteric reading
- Claim: the sun that rises in the west is the imam who rises in the Maghrib.
- Verdict: the only level on which the Prophet's words can be true.
- Fate: borne out, witnessed with al-Naysaburi's own eyes by the grace of God.

Shared row: the astronomy that cannot happen becomes the history that did. The sun-worshippers, like the fire-cult, bowed to the disc and never saw the reality it signified.

Hierarchy tree. The heavens narrowing to a single point, matched by the proofs of the mission.

Level 1: The twelve signs of the zodiac
  Level 2: Four most glorious signs
    Level 3: One sign standing supreme
Parallel ladder:
Level 1: The twelve proofs of the mission
  Level 2 to 3: the one bound to the imamate, highest and noblest of all

Annotated structure. The heavens read onto the map of the earth.

Whole: the earth divided into climes, each aligned to a heaven.
Part: the fourth clime, corresponding to the fourth heaven and aligned with the sun.
Annotation: it is the most temperate and balanced, therefore the most excellent of the earth's regions, as the imam is the most excellent and glorious within the hierarchy, its peak and apex. Every ring drawn, in the stars or on the ground, has a center, and the center always names the same thing.

Comparison matrix. The red ruby against the stones it defeats.

Rows: Red ruby | Other sapphires (white, green, yellow) | Emerald | Badakhshi stone | Carnelian and agate | Diamond | Turquoise | Jade
Columns: Endures fire | Endures water | Keeps essence, radiance, weight
- Red ruby: yes | yes | yes, at all times
- Other sapphires: no | no | no
- Emerald: no | no | no
- Badakhshi stone: no | no | no
- Carnelian and agate: no | no | no
- Diamond: no | no | no
- Turquoise: no | no | no
- Jade: no | no | no

The ruby is crowned not for rarity, not for value, not for dazzle, but for endurance under assault, the excellence of a thing that stays exactly itself when everything around it is trying to alter it. In a book written to defend a living, embattled ruler, that stroke says in the language of nature that pressure does not change what the imam is.

Hierarchy tree. The ranks of the mission the ruby-imam stands above.

Level 1: The imam, at a height none in his age can reach
  Level 2: The grades beneath him
    - The proofs
    - The adjuncts
    - The summoner
    - The wing
    - The licensee
    - The motivator
Annotation: as the ruby stands above every other stone, the imam stands above every one of these grades, unique among every rank of the mission.

Contrast pair. Two readings of the seven jewels, and why al-Naysaburi keeps the stronger one.

Column A: The numerical reading
- Claim: the seven jewels simply stand for the seven proclaimers and the seven imams.
- Force: a neat one-to-one match.
- Allowed: yes, al-Naysaburi permits it.

Column B: The degrees-of-excellence reading
- Claim: the jewels prove that every domain arranges itself around a summit.
- Force: greater, because the natural symbol is present in every age.
- Consequence: the reality it symbolizes must be present in every age too.

Shared row: the stars, the elements, the realms of nature, and the substances never go absent from the world; therefore the summit of the hierarchy must likewise be present in every single age. The permanence of the symbol proves the permanence of the imam. The catalogue is not decoration; it is load-bearing.

Process flow. The date palm as the tree that mirrors the human being.

Start: among fruit-bearing trees the summit is the date palm.
  It comes in male and female
  It must be fertilized, as animals must mate
  Nothing of it is wasted; every part is useful
  Its fruit is eaten in every condition, raw or cooked
  In a dream, plucking dates means benefit received from a certain believer
End: that believer is in truth the imam, the head of all believers.

The Prophet's riddle, which read the palm the same way before al-Naysaburi:

> Among the trees there is one whose leaves never fall, and it is like the Muslim. Tell me which tree it is. (the Prophet, in a hadith recorded by al-Bukhari)

The companions cast about among the trees of the wilderness; the answer none of them ventured, the date palm, was the one the Prophet finally named.

## Antidotes and poisons

Annotated structure. The three portraits the catalogue has stacked, one specimen at a time.

Whole: the accumulating image of the summit.
Parts:
- Fire: the summit is a GIVER, the source that quickens and lifts.
- Red ruby: the summit is INCORRUPTIBLE, unspoiled by the forces that spoil everything lesser.
- Antidote (tiryaq): the summit is a RESTORER, the cure that reaches a soul already given up for lost.
Annotation: it is one thing to nourish the healthy, as wheat does, and another to reach a soul past the point of ordinary help and pull it back. The antidote makes the imam's word not maintenance but rescue, healing from death, illness, doubt, and discord.

The Qur'an had already named revelation a cure:

> And We send down of the Qur'an that which is a healing and a mercy for the believers. (chapter 17, verse 82)

Contrast pair. The medicine that saves against the poison that kills.

Column A: The true imams
- Botanical figure: the mild medicines refined and perfected into antidotes.
- Effect: they reach the dying and rescue them from the very brink of death.
- On the soul: healing and relief from death, illness, doubt, and discord.

Column B: The usurpers
- Botanical figure: the deadly poisons and harmful substances that also grow among the plants; and the useless, worthless stones, the rubble that serves no purpose.
- Effect: fatal to whoever listens to them, keeps their company, or leans toward them.
- On the soul: they destroy the souls of those who trust them, unless the imam reaches them first with the great antidote.

Shared row: the single image of medicine splits cleanly into its two faces, the cure and the counterfeit.

Sealing citations, worthless stone fused with destroying fire:

> A pebble-covered hell to which they will return. (chapter 21, verse 98)

> The Fire whose fuel is people and stones, prepared for those who reject the truth. (chapter 2, verse 24)

Contrast pair. Sweet plants against bitter plants, the true guides against the literalists.

Column A: The imams
- Taste: sweet, delicious, nourishing, a pleasure to eat.
- Figure for: their speech and knowledge, which captivate the soul and delight the eye.
- Scriptural tree: the good word, a good tree whose roots are firmly set and whose branches reach the heavens.

Column B: The leaders of the literalists, the ahl al-zahir
- Taste: bitter, giving neither food nor delight; or fruitless and covered in thorns that wound whoever comes near.
- Figure for: the people of the surface who hold the outer husk of religion and never reach its inner sense.
- Scriptural tree: the bad word, a bad tree torn up from the surface of the earth, with no stability at all.

Shared row: nature itself sorts the true guides from the false. Scripture draws both trees:

> A good word is like a good tree whose roots are firmly set and whose branches reach the heavens, giving its fruit in every season by its Lord's leave; and a bad word is like a bad tree torn up from the surface of the earth, with no stability at all. (chapter 14, verses 24 to 26)

Contrast pair. Fragrant plants against putrid plants, the same sorting run through smell.

Column A: The sweet-smelling plants
- Sense: scent that refreshes people and heals them of their diseases.
- Figure for: the imam and his words, which bring ease and refreshment to whoever receives them.

Column B: The putrid, repellent plants
- Sense: stench that sickens rather than heals.
- Figure for: the imams of error, whose speech harms souls and can even destroy them.

Shared row: taste and smell, the two most immediate senses, both testify. The natural world is coded, top to bottom, to tell the two apart.

Hierarchy tree. The animals, each genus rising to its own summit.

Level 1: The genus of animals
  Level 2: Domestic and ridden animals
    Level 3: summit is the horse, finest in strength, usefulness, and training
  Level 2: Birds
    Level 3: summit is the eagle, master of the air
  Level 2: Beasts of prey
    Level 3: summit is the lion, master of the hunt
Annotation: within the whole genus there is a peak, exactly as within the human species that peak is the imam. Three witnesses that a domain arranges itself around its highest member.

Process flow. The quiet escalation from summits that dominate to summits that give.

Start: the horse, the eagle, the lion, each supreme by a single dominating trait.
  Step: al-Naysaburi does not stop at creatures that merely dominate
  Step: he moves to creatures that are generous, yielding up what the world could not otherwise have
  Step: the gazelle surrenders its musk; the bee surrenders its honey
End: the animal survey bends toward the particular kind of summit the whole book argues for, the one whose excellence overflows into benefit for others, back toward the fire's founding criterion.

Genealogy chain. The lead bee to the Commander of the Faithful.

The lead bee of the hive (ya'sub) → the chief around whom the whole hive orders itself → the natural image of the Father of Imams → Ali b. Abi Talib, the chief around whom the whole community orders itself.

The saying the word carries with it:

> You are the ya'sub, the chief, of the believers. (the Prophet, to Ali b. Abi Talib)

The Qur'an's words on the bee, turning the image toward healing one last time:

> Your Lord inspired the bee, and from within its bellies comes a drink of varying colors in which there is healing for mankind. (chapter 16, verses 68 to 69)

The creature that follows its chief and produces, out of its own body, a cure for human beings: the antidotes, the sweet plants, the fragrant plants, and the bee have all been circling one figure, a guide whose word is medicine against a counterfeit whose word is poison.

## Humankind and its summit

Annotated structure. The human being as the point where all lower creation is gathered and made to appear.

Whole: humankind, the ultimate of the animals and of all the realms of nature.
Works of reason that draw creation's worth out of hiding:
- Harnesses water, drawing it from valleys, rivers, and wells to irrigate the land
- Tills and cultivates the earth and raises buildings on it
- Masters wind and air; discovers fire by striking flint
- Crossbreeds animals never bred before; grafts trees to yield new fruits
- Extracts the jewels from the mineral seams
- Governs the beneficial trees, seeds, and fruits; guards and commands the animals and plants
Annotation: left to themselves, none of these things would show their worth; without the human being to draw it out, the merit of all things would never become apparent, and the order of the world would not hold. Creation needs a creature who can read it and use it, or its excellence stays buried.

The couplet ascribed to the Father of Imams, the cosmos folded into one frame:

> Do you reckon yourself a small thing, when within you the greater world lies enfolded? (the Father of Imams)

Contrast pair. Why the human being is the summit of nature and not just one more animal near the top.

Column A: The finest animals
- The horse is faster than a man; the eagle sees farther.
- They live within nature.
- They cannot take the raw materials of the world and transform them.

Column B: The human being
- Slower and weaker in body.
- He redirects nature, turning rivers, forcing barren ground to bear, coaxing new fruit from grafted trees, drawing jewels from stone.
- Reason is the faculty that makes the hidden worth of creation appear.

Shared row: this is why the merit of the lower creation returns to the human being. It is also the hinge that lets al-Naysaburi claim, a breath later, that the human being in turn needs a summit above himself, because reason ungoverned can turn all that mastery toward ruin.

Visual metaphor. Benefit propagating inward through nested circles to a single center.

Abstract relation: how the worth of creation gathers.
Metaphor: concentric circles, benefit flowing from the rim to the core.
- Outer ring: the elements, minerals, plants, and animals, each perfected in the human being.
- Middle ring: humanity, the quintessence in whom all lower worth is gathered.
- Core: the imams, in whom the benefit of all humanity gathers.
Reading: just as the benefit of all lower creation flows up into the human being, the benefit of all humanity flows up into the imams, the quintessence and perfection of the human race, through whom human affairs are set in order, virtues and knowledge are learned, minds are awakened, and welfare is safeguarded in this world and the next. The human being is the reader of the natural world; the imam is the one who teaches the human being how to read.

Contrast pair. The human being with a summit above him against the human being cut off from it.

Column A: The human being under the imams
- Trained toward virtue, pressed away from vice and animal habit.
- Intellect stirred to life, taught to recognize the Creator and render due gratitude.
- Taught to recognize the messengers and how they ought to be obeyed.

Column B: The human being without the imams
- Refuses the training that would build virtue into the soul.
- Reason, the very faculty that made him the summit, becomes the instrument of a ruin no animal is capable of.
- Sinks below the beast, wholly given over to evil and corruption.

Shared row: were it not for the imams, there would be no difference at all between beasts and human beings; in fact the beasts would be the superior of the two, since an animal is not as given to evil and does not scheme its own corruption.

Sealing citations, the unreasoning human worse than the beast:

> The worst of creatures in God's sight are the deaf and dumb who do not reason. (chapter 8, verse 22)

> They are like cattle, no, more misguided still. (chapter 7, verse 179)

Process flow. Why the whole cascade lands on a duty, not a fact.

Start: without human beings there would be no wisdom in creating the animals and plants, since no one would benefit from them.
  Step: likewise, without the imams there would be no wisdom in creating humanity, since human excellence would never be made to appear.
  Step: human beings were created for religion, the acquiring of virtues and knowledge.
  Step: that religion is precisely what the imams exist to give.
End, the necessary imperative:

> Learn knowledge and religion from the imams, for it is through them that religion is established and perfected.

If human excellence were never made manifest through religion, the worth of the plants and animals would never be known either, and there would be no wisdom in the creation of any of it, no wisdom in the making of all the worlds.

The poet of the same Ismaili world, on knowledge as the faculty that lifts a person above creation:

> Should your tree but bear the fruit of knowledge, you will bring the turning heavens beneath your hand. (Nasir-i Khusraw)

The fruit ripens from a single root: the knowledge that lifts a person above the beasts, and above the very stars, is received from the imams, or it is never received at all.

## What the natural witness proves

Comparison matrix. The full roster of the testimony, domain by domain.

Rows: Elements | Planets | Regions of earth | Metals | Jewels | Grains | Trees | Medicines | Plants by taste and smell | Animals | The bee
Columns: The summit named | The single lesson taught
- Elements: fire | an ordered domain arranges itself around its highest member
- Planets: the sun | the same
- Regions of earth: the fourth clime | the same
- Metals: gold | the same
- Jewels: the red ruby | the same
- Grains: wheat | the same
- Trees: the date palm | the same
- Medicines: the antidote | the same
- Plants by taste and smell: the sweet and fragrant against the bitter and foul | nature codes the true guide against the counterfeit
- Animals: the horse, eagle, lion | the same
- The bee: its chief | the summit that gives, the natural image of the imam

Contrast pair. What the argument has proven against what it has left open.

Column A: What it proved
- That the summit must exist.
- That reality requires it, from the stars down to the grains of the field.
- That humanity is the summit of the whole natural order, in whom the worth of everything below is gathered.
- That the human world, like every ordered domain, must have its summit, the imam, with the sting that a humanity cut off from its imams sinks beneath the beasts.

Column B: What it left open
- Not who holds that summit in any given age.
- Not by what right.
- Not through what line of succession.
- Only that the summit must exist, a necessity woven into the structure of every ordered thing.

Shared row: the catalogue was never really about gold or rubies or bees. It trained the eye to see that a peak is not a luxury a domain might enjoy but a necessity, so that when al-Naysaburi says the human race must have its imam, the reader has already agreed a dozen times over without noticing.

Process flow. The one question the argument opens onto.

Start: the imam is the summit of all humanity.
  Step: the natural next move is to ask what that summit commands.
  Step: how far the imam's authority reaches, and what it means to stand inside it or outside it.
  Step: just as the benefit of every lower creature returns to the human who governs it, and the benefit of all humanity returns to the imams, everything created is finally referred back to the imam and falls under his rule.
End: to reject him is not to win a small argument but to place oneself outside the whole order that his summit governs. The catalogue proved that a summit must exist; what it opens onto is the meaning of living under that summit, or refusing it.
