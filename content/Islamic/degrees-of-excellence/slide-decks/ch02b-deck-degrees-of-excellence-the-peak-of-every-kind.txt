# Degrees of Excellence: The Peak of Every Kind

## The witness of nature

Annotated structure. The three specimens the section opens on, and the single question hidden behind them.

Whole: a naturalist's catalogue that turns out to be an argument about who should lead a religious community.
Parts:
- A flame that must never be let die.
- A red stone that comes out of the fire unchanged.
- A single grain of wheat.
Annotations:
- On the face of it each belongs to a different world, chemistry, mineralogy, the farmer's field, and none has anything to say about human authority.
- Al-Naysaburi marches through all three, and a dozen more like them, as if each were a witness giving sworn testimony in a trial about the imamate.
- The catalogue reads at first like a tour of creation's finest specimens and is in fact a tightly built argument about the one figure the whole cosmos is arranged to point toward.

Genealogy chain. The engine of the whole section, one hierarchy of better and best climbing from the low to the high.

Elements → Stars → Gold → Ruby → The human being

Reading: the ladder runs on the plain fact that things are not equal, refined into a hierarchy of better and best. Al-Naysaburi builds it himself, in his own order, in full concrete detail.

Process flow. What the treatise finished before this section, and what it now owes.

Start: the treatise established the imamate as the pole and foundation of religion, the office without which the whole structure has no center.
  Step: it closed on the idea that this office is testified to by two witnesses, the revealed law on one side and the natural world on the other.
  Step: it named the two witnesses and let them stand.
  Step: now al-Naysaburi calls the second of them to the stand, and owes the testimony in detail, domain by domain, specimen by specimen.
End: he opens the treasuries of creation and begins pulling out one perfect thing after another.

Reading: behind every specimen the same question waits. If even the metals and the grains and the beasts each have a summit, how could the human world be the one ordered thing in creation with no summit at all?

## The peak of every kind

Contrast pair. Why fire is crowned among the four elements and the other three are not.

Column A: Fire, the element that acts
- Standing: it sits at a height no other element and no other nature can reach.
- What it does: gives off light, moves and lifts and rises, keeps the other three quickened.
- Its office: the source of luminosity and heat, the cause of upward motion, the thing that stirs the others into life.
- Why it is crowned: a domain is crowned not by the member most acted upon but by the one that most gives.

Column B: Cold, dryness, humidity, the elements that are worked upon
- Standing: the arkan, the raw pillars from which every composite body is assembled, but the lower three.
- What they do: they are receptive, they take on qualities and are worked upon.
- Their office: sustained through fire, kept from lapsing into inertness by it.
- Why they are not crowned: they originate nothing, they do not quicken or lift.

Shared row: the criterion set here governs every specimen that follows, though al-Naysaburi never restates it. The summit is the giver, the source, the one on whom the rest depend for the very activity that makes them what they are. Just as fire illumines and elevates, the imam is the highest religious pillar of his age, the one through whom souls are given light.

Annotated structure. Abraham's fire, the symbol taken for the reality, and the ruin of that mistake.

Whole: the command to glorify the fire, and what it was really pointing at.
Parts:
- The command: Abraham told his people to honor the fire and never let it be extinguished, if the report is sound.
- The real intent: the imamate, the light no one is permitted to put out through the injustice of those who falsely claim its place.
- The error: the fire-worshippers built a whole cult around the burning of an actual fire.
Annotations:
- The literal reading grasped the symbol and never reached the reality it stood in for.
- It is a mistake al-Naysaburi will watch people make again, with the sun, with the stars, with the jewels.
- The image of a light that cannot be extinguished turns back onto the enemies of the imamate.

Citation. Scripture seals the fire passage.

> They would like to extinguish God's light with their mouths, but God will not allow it until He perfects His light, even if the unbelievers detest it.
> Quran 9:32

Process flow. The sun that rises in the west, from impossible astronomy to witnessed history.

Start: the Prophet spoke of the sun rising from the west as one of the signs of the end.
  Step: read literally, that is astronomy, and impossible astronomy.
  Step: if the physical sun ever swerved from its fixed orbit even for the blink of an eye, the whole world would be destroyed, which reason and nature alike rule out.
  Step: so the Prophet's words can only be true on the level of esoteric interpretation.
  Step: the sun that rises in the west is the imam who rises in the Maghrib.
End: that interpretation has been borne out, al-Naysaburi says, witnessed with his own eyes by the grace of God. The astronomy that cannot happen becomes the history that did.

Comparison matrix. The specimens of creation, each the peak of its own kind, each carrying the same lesson to the imam.

Rows: Fire, Sun, Fourth clime, Gold, Red ruby, Wheat, Date palm
Columns: Its domain | Why it is the peak | What it points to in the imam

- Fire: the four elements | it gives light and lifts and quickens the rest | the highest religious pillar, through whom souls are given light.
- Sun: the wandering lights of the heavens | without its light no eye sees, without its warmth nothing grows | the sun of religion, through whom insight is enlightened and souls made to shine.
- Fourth clime: the regions of the earth | it corresponds to the fourth heaven, aligns with the sun, most temperate and balanced | the most excellent and glorious within the hierarchy, its peak and apex.
- Gold: the metals that melt and can be worked | the medium that fixes the value of everything, unharmed by fire or water, indestructible | the most excellent religious and intellectual substance, the measure of religious worth.
- Red ruby: the precious stones | it keeps essence, purity, radiance, and weight unchanged in fire or flood | the pinnacle of all creation in his time, unique among every rank of the mission.
- Wheat: the seven grains that feed the body | the most balanced in nature, the one that best builds the body | the peak from whom souls draw livelihood, life, and knowledge.
- Date palm: the fruit-bearing trees | male and female, fertilized like animals mate, nothing of it wasted | the tree that most resembles the human summit the catalogue is climbing toward.

Annotated structure. The zodiac and the proofs, one supreme member in each.

Whole: the heavens read as a hierarchy of rank.
Parts:
- Among the twelve signs of the zodiac, four are the most glorious, and among those four one stands supreme.
- Among the twelve proofs of the mission, the one bound to the imamate is the highest and noblest of all.
Annotation: every ring al-Naysaburi draws, whether in the stars or on the ground, has a center, and the center always names the same thing.

## The treasuries of creation

Annotated structure. Why the ruby is crowned, and the stones it defeats.

Whole: the red ruby set above every other jewel, chosen for one particular excellence.
Parts:
- The sapphire is the finest of stones and comes in several colors, white, green, yellow, red.
- Among all of them the red ruby excels.
- The stones it beats: the other sapphires, the emerald, the Badakhshi stone, carnelian, agate, the diamond, turquoise, jade.
Annotations:
- It is not crowned for being the rarest stone, or the most valuable, or the most dazzling to look at.
- It is crowned for endurance under assault, holding its purity, radiance, and weight whether thrown into fire or plunged into water.
- That is the excellence of a thing that stays exactly itself when everything around it is trying to alter it.
- In a book written to defend a living, embattled ruler, that stroke is not decorative. It says, in the only language nature speaks, that pressure does not change what the imam is.

Annotated structure. The method al-Naysaburi pauses to defend, and the hinge it turns on.

Whole: the objection the jewels tempt, and the stronger argument he actually leans on.
Parts:
- The easy reading: the seven jewels simply stand for the seven proclaimers and the seven imams, a neat numerical match.
- What he leans on instead: the argument from degrees of excellence, which carries more force.
- The hinge: the natural symbol is present in every age, so the reality it symbolizes must be present in every age too.
Annotations:
- The stars, the elements, the realms of nature, the substances, none of these ever goes absent from the world.
- Therefore the ranks of the hierarchy, and above all their summit, must likewise be present in every single age.
- The permanence of the symbol proves the permanence of the imam. This is why the natural catalogue is load-bearing, not decoration.

Citation. The Prophet's riddle of the tree, already turning in the tradition.

> Among the trees there is one whose leaves never fall, and it is like the Muslim. Tell me which tree it is. (the Prophet, in a hadith recorded by al-Bukhari)

Reading: the companions cast about among the trees of the wilderness, and the answer none of them ventured, the date palm, was the one the Prophet finally named. The likeness al-Naysaburi rests the palm on was already the Prophet's own figure of the faithful human being. There is even a reading for the dreaming mind, that plucking dates from a palm means benefit received from a certain believer, who in truth is the imam, the head of all believers.

## Antidotes and poisons

Annotated structure. The portrait of the summit, deepened one stroke at a time.

Whole: three natural images, each adding a new stroke to what the summit is.
Parts:
- The fire, at the start of the catalogue.
- The ruby, among the jewels.
- The antidote, among the medicines.
Annotations:
- The fire made the summit a giver, the source that quickens the rest.
- The ruby made it incorruptible, the one thing that stays itself under assault.
- The antidote makes it a restorer, the cure that reaches a soul already given up for lost.
Reading: it is one thing to nourish the healthy, as the wheat does, and quite another to reach a soul past the point of ordinary help and pull it back. The imam's word is not maintenance but rescue, healing from death, from illness, from doubt, from the discord that fractures a community.

Citation. The Qur'an had already named revelation a cure.

> And We send down of the Qur'an that which is a healing and a mercy for the believers.
> Quran 17:82

Contrast pair. The single image of medicine split cleanly into its two faces.

Column A: The antidote, the true imam
- What it is: mild medicines refined and perfected until they become tiryaq, able to pull a person back from the brink of death.
- Its reach: the finest of them reach the dying and rescue them.
- What it heals: souls, from death, from illness, from doubt, from discord.
- Its scriptural stones: the good tree whose roots are firmly set and whose branches reach the heavens, giving fruit in every season.

Column B: The poison, the usurper
- What it is: the deadly poisons and harmful substances that also grow among the plants, those who seize the imams' place by force.
- Its reach: fatal to whoever listens to them, keeps their company, or leans toward them, unless the imam reaches those people first.
- What it destroys: the souls of the people who trust them.
- Its scriptural stones: the worthless rubble, the pebble-covered hell they return to, chapter 21, verse 98, and the Fire whose fuel is people and stones, chapter 2, verse 24.

Shared row: an antidote implies a poison. Where the true imams are the healing medicines, the usurpers are the counterfeits that kill, gathered into both the useless stone and the destroying fire.

Comparison matrix. The plants sorted by taste and by smell, so nature itself tells the true guide from the false.

Rows: Sweet and fragrant plants, Bitter and foul plants, Fruitless and thorned plants
Columns: What the plant is | Who it stands for | What its speech does to the soul

- Sweet and fragrant plants: delicious and nourishing to eat, their scent refreshing and healing | the imams | their speech and knowledge captivate the soul, delight the eye, bring ease and refreshment.
- Bitter and foul plants: bitter to the taste, putrid to the smell, giving neither food nor delight | the leaders of the literalists, the ahl al-zahir, who hold to the outer husk | their speech harms souls and can even destroy them.
- Fruitless and thorned plants: bearing no fruit at all, covered in thorns that wound whoever comes near | the imams of error and the people of the surface | they never reach the inner sense, and they wound rather than heal.

Citation. Scripture draws the two trees.

> A good word is like a good tree whose roots are firmly set and whose branches reach the heavens, giving its fruit in every season by its Lord's leave; and a bad word is like a bad tree torn up from the surface of the earth, with no stability at all.
> Quran 14:24-26

Hierarchy. The animals, each the peak of its own kind.

- Level 1: The genus of animals, which rises to a peak just as the human species does
  - Level 2: The horse, finest of its kind by swiftness and service
  - Level 2: The eagle, finest of the birds by mastery of the air
  - Level 2: The lion, finest of the beasts of prey by mastery of the hunt

Reading: the horse, eagle, and lion are three witnesses that a domain naturally arranges itself around its highest member. They are the summit by a single outstanding trait, and that is all al-Naysaburi needs from them to keep the pattern intact.

Process flow. The quiet escalation across the animal images, from superior to generous.

Start: the horse, the eagle, and the lion, each the summit of its kind by a single trait of dominance.
  Step: al-Naysaburi does not stop at creatures that merely dominate.
  Step: he moves to creatures that are generous, that yield up something the world could not otherwise have.
  Step: the gazelle surrenders its musk, and the bee its honey.
End: the survey bends, at its end, back toward the criterion the fire established, the summit whose excellence overflows into benefit for others.

Genealogy chain. The lead bee and the Father of Imams, one image handed forward by a saying.

The ya'sub, the lead bee of the hive → the chief around whom the whole hive orders itself → Ali b. Abi Talib, the ya'sub of the believers → the Father of Imams, the chief around whom the whole community orders itself

Reading: it was for this reason, al-Naysaburi tells us, that the Prophet said to the Commander of the Faithful, you are the ya'sub, the chief, of the believers. The musk-giving gazelle and the honey-producing bee are the finest emblems because they do not merely sit at the top of their kind, they produce something precious.

Citation. The Qur'an turns the bee toward healing.

> Your Lord inspired the bee, and from within its bellies comes a drink of varying colors in which there is healing for mankind.
> Quran 16:68-69

## Humankind and its summit

Annotated structure. The works of the human being, the mastery that makes him the summit of nature rather than one more animal near the top.

Whole: what man's reason lets him do to every realm below him.
Parts:
- Water: he harnesses it, drawing it out of valleys and rivers and wells to irrigate the land.
- Earth: he tills it, cultivates it, and raises buildings on it.
- Wind, air, and fire: he masters the air, and discovered fire by striking flint.
- Living things: he crossbred animals never bred before and grafted trees to yield fruits the world had never seen.
- Minerals: he extracts the jewels from the seams that would otherwise lie hidden forever.
- Governance: he guards the welfare of the animals and the plants and brings the beasts under his command.
Annotations:
- The horse is faster than a man and the eagle sees farther, but neither can take the raw materials of the world and transform them.
- Reason is the faculty that makes the hidden worth of creation appear.
- Left to themselves, without the human being to draw it out, the merit of all things would never become apparent, and the order of the world itself would not hold.

Citation. The couplet that asserts what al-Naysaburi is here proving.

> Do you reckon yourself a small thing, when within you the greater world lies enfolded? (the Father of Imams)

Hierarchy. How benefit flows upward and gathers at each summit in turn.

- Level 1: The imams, in whom the benefit of all humanity gathers
  - Level 2: The human being, in whom the benefit of all lower creation gathers
    - Level 3: The animals, the plants, the minerals, and every realm of nature, whose worth returns to the human being who reads and uses it

Reading: just as the benefit of all the lower creation flows up and gathers in the human being, the benefit of all humanity flows up and gathers in the imams. They are the quintessence and perfection of the human race, the ones through whom human affairs are set in order, minds are awakened, and welfare is safeguarded in this world and the next.

Contrast pair. The human being with the imams against the human being cut off from them.

Column A: Humanity guided by the imams
- What it receives: virtues and knowledge, minds awakened, understanding acquired.
- What it comes to recognize: the Creator, that gratitude is owed to the one who gave it everything, and the proper way to render it.
- What it learns to obey: the messengers, and how they ought to be obeyed.
- Its standing: the summit of nature, the point at which everything below is perfected and set in order.

Column B: Humanity refusing the imams
- What it loses: the training that would build virtue into the soul, the intellect the imams would stir to life.
- What it becomes: given wholly over to evil and corruption, since without intellect and religion the human being schemes his own ruin.
- Where it lands: below the animal, which has its uses and does not scheme its own corruption.
- Its standing: were it not for the imams, there would be no difference between beasts and human beings, and the beasts would be the superior of the two.

Shared row: the very faculty that makes us the summit of nature, reason, becomes, unguided, the instrument of a ruin no animal is capable of.

Citation. Scripture measures the unguided human below the beast.

> The worst of creatures in God's sight are the deaf and dumb who do not reason.
> Quran 8:22

> Of the heedless, they are like cattle, no, more misguided still.
> Quran 7:179

Process flow. Why the whole cascade lands on a duty rather than a fact.

Start: without human beings there would be no wisdom in creating the animals and plants, because no one would be there to benefit from them.
  Step: likewise, without the imams there would be no wisdom in creating humanity at all, because human excellence would never be made to appear.
  Step: human beings were created for religion, for the acquiring of virtues and knowledge.
  Step: that religion is precisely what the imams exist to give.
End: the closing imperative is not a suggestion but the necessary consequence of the entire natural survey. Learn knowledge and religion from the imams, for it is through them that religion is established and perfected, and without which there would be no wisdom in the making of all the worlds.

Citation. The poet gives the ascent its most memorable voice.

> Should your tree but bear the fruit of knowledge, you will bring the turning heavens beneath your hand. (Nasir-i Khusraw)

Reading: the fruit ripens from a single root. The knowledge that lifts a person above the beasts, and above the very stars, is received from the imams, or it is never received at all.

## What the natural witness proves

Comparison matrix. The full testimony gathered, every specimen delivering the one verdict.

Rows: Fire, Sun, Fourth clime, Gold, Red ruby, Wheat, Date palm, Antidote, Sweet plants against the bitter, Horse and eagle and lion, Bee and its chief
Columns: Its domain | The single lesson it taught

- Fire: the elements | an ordered domain arranges itself around its highest member.
- Sun: the planets | the peak is the one that gives light and warmth to all the rest.
- Fourth clime: the regions of the earth | the center of every ring names the same summit.
- Gold: the metals | the summit is the measure by which everything else is valued.
- Red ruby: the jewels | the summit stays incorruptible under every assault.
- Wheat: the grains | the summit is the one from whom the rest draw nourishment.
- Date palm: the trees | the summit is the member that most resembles the human summit.
- Antidote: the medicines | the summit rescues what was given up for lost.
- Sweet plants against the bitter: the coded plant world | nature itself sorts the true guide from the false.
- Horse and eagle and lion: the animals | every genus rises to a peak.
- Bee and its chief: the generous animals | the truest summit overflows into benefit for others.

Reading: al-Naysaburi did not pile up pretty comparisons. He walked a single argument through the whole of creation, and every specimen taught that a domain arranges itself around its highest member. Then he showed that humanity is the summit of the whole natural order, the creature in whom the worth of everything below is gathered and made to appear.

Contrast pair. What the argument has proven, and what it has carefully left open.

Column A: What the catalogue has proven
- The claim it establishes: that the summit must exist, that reality requires it, from the stars down to the grains of the field.
- How it works on the reader: it trains the eye to see that a peak is not a luxury a domain might happen to enjoy but a necessity woven into the structure of every ordered thing.
- Its result: when al-Naysaburi says the human race must have its imam, we have already agreed, a dozen times over, without noticing.
- Its sting: a humanity cut off from its imams would sink beneath the very beasts it was made to command.

Column B: What the catalogue has not proven
- What it leaves open: who holds that summit in any given age.
- What it does not settle: by what right, or through what line of succession.
- What it was never about: the catalogue was never really about gold or rubies or bees.
- What still waits: the meaning of living under that summit, or refusing it.

Shared row: the catalogue proves that a summit must exist. It does not name the one who fills it.

Process flow. The one open question, and where the treatise turns next.

Start: if the imam is the summit of all humanity, the natural next move is to ask what that summit actually commands.
  Step: how far the imam's authority reaches, and what it means to stand inside it or outside it.
  Step: just as the benefit of every lower creature returns to the human being who governs it, and the benefit of all humanity returns to the imams, so everything created is finally referred back to the imam and falls under his rule.
End: to reject him is not to win a small argument but to place oneself outside the whole order that his summit governs. The catalogue has proved that a summit must exist. What it opens onto is the meaning of living under that summit, or refusing it.
