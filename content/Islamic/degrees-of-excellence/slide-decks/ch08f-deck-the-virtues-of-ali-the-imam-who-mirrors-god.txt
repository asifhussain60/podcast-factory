# The Virtues of Ali, the Imam Who Mirrors God

## Where the argument picks up

Contrast pair. How we ordinarily judge a ruler against the summit the scholar holds up as the highest human excellence.

Column A: The good king we expect
- Toward friends: reward loyalty, make favor legible
- Toward enemies: punish treachery, hold it in check
- Grounds of giving: announced, legible, so loyalty is repaid
- Nearness and distance: withdrawal is a signal, a verdict
- Verdict if a man gave to friend and foe alike, hid his reasons, drew near and withdrew unannounced: capricious, even unjust

Column B: The scholar's summit of excellence
- Toward the faithful: give and withhold in turn
- Toward the faithless: give and withhold in turn, no distinction drawn
- Grounds of giving: concealed, His reasons hidden
- Nearness and distance: draws near and holds back for no announced reason
- Verdict the scholar draws from the same conduct: not a mere monarch but the vicegerent of God on earth, the highest praise a person could receive

Shared row: The same behavior that would scandalize us in a king is, for the scholar, the one thing that proves a man mirrors God. This closing chapter is about why he thought so.

Process flow. What the preceding stretch cleared, and what this final movement raises on the cleared ground.

Start: The thread of the whole treatise, that the world is never for a single moment without a designated guide at its peak, from the first man to the present day
  down to
The preceding movement walked back through sacred history and measured the men the community actually followed against the standard the imamate demands
  down to
Standard applied: knowledge that reaches the inner meaning of revelation, striving in God's cause, piety without compromise
  down to
Finding: the first successors fell short on every count, which cleared the ground
  down to
This movement raises the building: if there must always be a summit, and the ordinary claimants fall short, then who is the summit?
End: The figure in whom knowledge, striving, and nearness to God culminate so completely that nothing higher can be imagined

Annotated structure. The two referents this movement names, held together as one office.

Whole: the summit the whole book was pointing toward
Parts:
- The historical summit: the Commander of the Faithful, Ali b. Abi Talib, the Father of Imams, the furthest limit beyond which there is no further limit
- The living summit: the imam reigning in the scholar's own age, the qa'im and perfector of the whole line, dealing with creation precisely as God deals with it
Annotation: two figures, one office, one ascent. The scholar moves without a break from Ali b. Abi Talib to the reigning imam, and by the end the whole edifice of the book rests on this claim, closing on its author's own prayer.

## The summit of summits

Comparison matrix. The rule written into the structure of everything, that every order of creation gathers its excellence to a single peak.

Rows: Elements, Metals, Stones, Grains, Trees, Heavenly bodies, Imams
Columns: The summit of the kind | What the rule establishes
- Elements: the one element that stands supreme | excellence is not spread evenly
- Metals: gold | nature gathers excellence to a point
- Stones: the ruby that will not burn | perfection fire cannot touch
- Grains: wheat | the apex of its kind
- Trees: the date palm | the summit among its kind
- Heavenly bodies: the outermost sphere and the sun | the supreme body among the heavens
- Imams: their utmost degree, a summit no one before attained | there is no exemption for the highest realm of all

Hierarchy. The three-step ascent the book has laid down, run now one link further than before.

- Level 1: Nature, the whole world of animals and plants
  - Level 2: Humanity, in whom the wisdom of the world comes to full expression
    - Level 3: The imams, through whom the merits of the human race are perfected and wisdom becomes visible
      - Level 4: The Commander of the Faithful, Ali b. Abi Talib, in whom the whole line of imams itself comes to rest
Reading: as nature culminates in humanity and humanity in the imams, the whole line of imams rises to a single point. Through him all the affairs of the prophets, the legatees, and the imams reached their completion, their order set in order, the full wisdom of their laws and inner interpretations made manifest in his person. He is the summit toward which even the summits incline.

Annotated structure. The three bodily images the scholar unfolds in stages, one claim seen three ways.

Whole: the relation of the Father of Imams to all who came before
Parts:
- The head, and all the others are the body: no one disputes that the head governs the body
- The spirit, and they are the body: no body is complete or alive without its spirit
- The heart, and the departed are the limbs, the hands and hair and nails: every limb, down to the smallest, draws its life and nourishment only through the beating of the heart
Annotation: head, spirit, heart, three images for a single claim, that the entire body of guidance lives only because this one figure stands at its center, awakening in every soul the longing to draw near.

Contrast pair. The gathering mechanism, the warrant that licenses the claim, drawn as a parallel.

Column A: The Prophet
- Source of power and merit: gathering into himself the merits of all who lived in the cycles before him
- Range gathered: their knowledge and more besides
- Result: he drew his power from that gathering and surpassed them

Column B: The Commander of the Faithful, Ali b. Abi Talib
- Source of power and merit: gathering into himself the merits of all the prophets and imams and sages who preceded him
- Range gathered: their knowledge and their benefit, all converging in him
- Result: he exceeds them many times over, in matters beyond their reach

Shared row: as the human being absorbed the realms below and surpassed them, and the imams gathered the merits scattered through humanity and exceeded them, so the Father of Imams gathers all prior merit and surpasses it. Had he not arisen to fill this station, the perfection of wisdom in all their arisings would never have been achieved. This is the word the scholar settles on: the mutimm, the perfector, the one who completes and brings to fullness. It is for the sake of such a figure that these worlds were made at all.

Genealogy chain. The intuition was not al-Naysaburi's alone, but a shape his own tradition had already built.

The Greek and Ismaili inheritance of ranked being → Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani, in his Rahat al-Aql, maps the ranks of religion onto the structure of the cosmos → the imam stands at the human summit as the outermost sphere stands at the summit of the heavens → what al-Naysaburi calls the summit of summits is the place where the cosmic order and the order of guidance meet in a single person

Contrast pair. The objection about the final qa'im, and the scholar's quietly radical reply, the hinge on which the chapter turns.

Column A: The objection
- Claim: this towering description, the perfector of all, the one for whose sake the worlds exist, belongs only to the final qa'im
- The final qa'im: the awaited one who arises at the very end to seal all the cycles
- Consequence: the Father of Imams, whatever his rank, is not that terminal figure

Column B: The scholar's reply
- Claim: the imam reigning in his own age is the qa'im of his own time, and the qa'im of all who preceded him, and the perfector of them all
- The office of perfector: not reserved for one distant terminal figure at the end of history
- Consequence: every reigning imam stands, for his own age, in exactly that place, completing the whole line that came before him

Shared row: the summit of summits is not a point fixed in the past. It is a living station, occupied now.

Comparison matrix. The numerical reckonings of completion, presented abstractly, the same idea encoded three ways.

Rows: The sixth, The ninth, The second
Columns: The reckoning | What it grounds
- The sixth: the one through whom the command of a seventh is brought to its perfection, finishing what that seventh required | as the Prophet established everything a later completer would need
- The ninth: the figure through whom the command of an earlier one, now passed away, reaches its fullness | grounded in the body attaining its order through a set of nine, and the human creature perfected over nine months in the womb
- The second: the one who follows, bringing to completion the order set down at the beginning of every cycle | the order laid at the beginning is finished by the one who comes after
Reading: the particular numbers matter less than the single idea they all encode. The reigning imam is not a lesser stand-in awaiting some greater figure. He is himself the living perfector of a line that stretches back through the whole of sacred history.

Contrast pair. The two referents kept clear, deliberately let to flow together.

Column A: Ali b. Abi Talib
- Role: the historical summit
- Standing: the Father of Imams, the head and heart and spirit of the whole body of guidance
- Reach: the perfector of every prophet and imam who preceded him

Column B: The imam reigning in the scholar's age
- Role: the one who continues and completes that summit in the present
- Standing: the qa'im of his own time, gathering the entire inheritance into himself
- Reach: the summit begun in the Father of Imams, carried forward for a new age

Shared row: in this theology the two are not rivals and not really separate. The reigning imam is the summit begun in the Father of Imams, brought to completion for a new age.

## The imam who mirrors God

Contrast pair. The philosophers' highest definition of their own work, and the one figure who alone achieved it.

Column A: The philosophers
- Definition of philosophy: the pursuit of resemblance to the Creator, so far as a human being is able
- Origin of the formula: ascribed across the traditions ultimately to the Greeks, borrowed from the people of the truth
- Outcome: not one of them ever realized its true meaning, they drew the target and never hit it

Column B: The imam
- What resemblance to the Creator means: to deal with mankind the way God deals with them, along the grain of God's own conduct
- What he does: his every dealing with humankind mirrors God's own, in the ordering of the world, the giving of sustenance, all related matters
- Outcome: the imam alone hit the target the philosophers only defined

Shared row: the philosophers drew the target. The imam alone hit it. Everything now depends on making that mirroring concrete.

Comparison matrix. The logic of God's distribution of worldly fortune, and why each direction would corrupt faith.

Rows: Give only to believers, Give only to unbelievers, Give and withhold to each
Columns: The consequence | The verdict
- Give only to believers: faith becomes a business proposition, people believe for the profit | religion turns into a calculation about this world
- Give only to unbelievers: most people's faith collapses into despair and reverts to unbelief | who could hold to a religion that guaranteed him ruin
- Give and withhold to each: obedience is never bought by advantage, never abandoned for its lack | God threads between the two, and the imam follows His conduct
Reading: God gives to some believers and withholds from others, gives to some unbelievers and withholds from others, grants and denies at different times. In exactly this way the reigning imam bestows on Muslim and unbeliever alike and withholds from both, so that obedience to him is neither purchased nor surrendered.

Comparison matrix. The pairing of every human type, to show no category escapes the pattern.

Rows: Pious and impious, Worshipper and non-worshipper, Simpleton and learned, Wrongdoer and just, and the rest
Columns: Why God gives to both | What the imam therefore does
- Pious and impious: so the pious will not do good merely for a worldly return, nor abstain from wrong merely to protect an interest | gives to the righteous and the immoral alike
- Worshipper and non-worshipper: lest worship itself become a means of gain | looks on his people not fixing on their faults but with a merciful eye
- Simpleton and learned: had He given only to the learned, people would pursue knowledge purely as a road to wealth, and the ignorant would despair | gives to the ignorant and the learned alike
- Wrongdoer and just, generous and miser, noble and depraved, full of virtue and empty of it: distributed out of wisdom and mercy so each receives a fair share | treats every one of them the same, following God's own actions and mercy
Reading: type after type, the mirror holds. The whole account rests on a mercy that runs ahead of judgment.

The priority of mercy over reckoning that the imam imitates, preserved verbatim.

> My mercy has overtaken My wrath.
Attribution: the Prophet, transmitting from God

The bare fact of the divine economy scripture states, preserved verbatim.

> God extends provision to whom He wills, and restricts it.
Attribution: Qur'an, chapter 13, verse 26

The danger the withholding guards against, preserved verbatim.

> Man is rebellious, for he thinks himself self-sufficient.
Attribution: God, in the Qur'an

Annotated structure. Why God conceals the workings of the world, and how the imam conceals in the same way.

Whole: the deliberate hiddenness of the means of worldly benefit
Parts:
- God's concealment: He keeps the methods of gain obscure, so one grows wealthy while another has nothing, one prospers in trade while another goes bankrupt, one flourishes in farming while another loses even the seed
- The reason: if a single sure path to fortune were universally known, everyone would pour down that one road, every other path would fall idle, and the ordered variety of the world would be thrown into confusion
- The imam's concealment: he hides the means of gaining benefit through him, so one man seeks his favor by a route and receives it, while another who seeks it by that very route is denied
- The reason: if people ever found a definite method for obtaining his bounty, all other means would become futile, and everyone lacking that one key would despair of his mercy
Annotation: the concealment protects the world from becoming a formula. The hiddenness keeps hope open for everyone and certainty available to no one.

Contrast pair. The purpose of the imam's nearness and his distance, why neither can be fixed.

Column A: If he drew only the sincere close and pushed the hypocrites away
- What people would do: perform belief in order to win his nearness
- What faith becomes: a courtship rather than a conviction

Column B: If he held himself at a distance from all believers at all times
- What people would do: withdraw from him, anxiety creeping in, imagining they had displeased him
- What faith becomes: fear of a coldness they cannot explain

Shared row: so he deals with people as the times and conditions require, arousing longing and alarm, drawing near and pushing away, granting and withholding, punishing and forgiving, in emulation of God, so that souls stay suspended between fear and hope, never so secure they grow bold, never so despairing they collapse. That suspension is the precise spiritual climate in which a soul stays awake.

Contrast pair. The one feature that sets the imam's giving wholly apart from every other monarch's.

Column A: The gifts of ordinary kings
- What every gift is: payment for something, a service rendered, a relationship maintained, an obligation discharged, a debt repaid, a fear of calamity, a hope of advantage, a wish to be praised
- The nature of the act: a transaction, calculated and assessed
- The scholar's judgment: to delay what you owe and pay it late is miserliness, to advance money for a return is investment, not generosity at all

Column B: The gifts of the imam
- What every gift is: given to no one for any of those reasons, seeking no recompense
- The nature of the act: bestowed freely, spontaneously, out of pure generosity, following God who is under no obligation and seeks no reward
- The warrant: as God said of the Commander of the Faithful, Ali b. Abi Talib, "we desire neither reward nor thanks from you"

Shared row: his giving is the one giving in the human world that asks for nothing back.

Comparison matrix. The three kinds of devotion the Father of Imams sorted, and where the imam's giving falls.

Rows: The merchant, The slave, The free
Columns: The motive | What it corresponds to
- The merchant: worships God out of desire for reward | angling for a return
- The slave: worships God out of fear | devotion extracted by fear
- The free: worships God out of gratitude | the open hand of one who owes nothing and asks nothing
Reading: the imam's giving is the giving of the free, moving only out of a fullness that answers to God alone.

The threefold sorting of devotion, preserved verbatim.

> A people worship God out of desire for reward, that is the worship of merchants; and a people worship Him out of fear, that is the worship of slaves; and a people worship Him out of gratitude, that is the worship of the free.
Attribution: the Father of Imams

Quadrant map. The two errors that surround the imam, with the truth passing between them.

Space: how to measure the imam's conduct
- Left pole: short sight, those who judge his actions by the standard of ordinary human beings and rulers, comparing what he does against what they have seen, and falling into perplexity when he gives where a king would withhold
- Right pole: exaggeration, the ghulat, the extremists who are dazzled and claim divinity for him, inflating the office past its truth
- Center, the truth between: the Universal Vicegerent of God over His servants, a shadow that perfectly imitates in every detail the thing that casts it
Reading: he is not a common ruler to be measured by common rulers, and he is not God. The trouble with the first error is that from Adam to the present day no one has acted in this manner, so people have no precedent to reach for. The shadow image holds the whole doctrine: near enough to God to reproduce His conduct exactly, yet, being a shadow, never for a moment God Himself.

## The vicegerent and the closing prayer

Contrast pair. The two halves of the mirror completed, concealing and disclosing across both worlds at once.

Column A: In worldly matters, the imam conceals
- What God does: conceals the reasons for creating the world, hides the means of attaining fortune
- What the imam does: hides the means of gaining fortune through him, gives to deserving and undeserving alike
- The purpose: so no one can reduce his bounty to a formula

Column B: In religious matters, the imam discloses
- What God does: openly explains the means of attaining religion and His good pleasure, gives knowledge and wisdom to those who deserve them and denies the unworthy
- What the imam does: openly teaches the means of attaining religion, knowledge, and wisdom, bestowing them on the deserving, more than anyone in any age could give
- The purpose: to draw the line that must be drawn, distinguishing the ahl al-haqq, the people of truth, from the ahl al-batil, the people of falsehood

Shared row: in all of it he follows the ways of God, governing world and religion together, building up both this present life and the life to come. The vicegerent does over those entrusted to him exactly what the One who appointed him would do.

Comparison matrix. The word khalifa, and the difference between wearing the name and being the reality it was made for.

Rows: Adam, David, All earlier vicegerents, The Father of Imams and the reigning imam
Columns: How the name applies | Extent of the vicegerency
- Adam: called vicegerent because he was the first to take up the vicegerency | the office begun
- David: charged in scripture, "O David, We have made you a vicegerent on the earth, so judge between the people in truth" | to govern God's servants as God would have them governed
- All earlier vicegerents: called by the name because each discharged some part of its duty, and whoever performs part of a charge is known by its name | a small portion of God's manner of dealing
- The Father of Imams and the reigning imam: perform the vicegerency whole, both worldly and religious dimensions, in full reality | the whole of God's manner of dealing, not in part
Reading: when the Prophet said the ruler is the shadow of God on earth, he meant this vicegerent and no other, for no one deals with creation as God does except the one who stands in God's place over God's servants. That is the difference between discharging part of a duty and completing it.

The scriptural charge that fixes the governing sense of the word, preserved verbatim.

> O David, We have made you a vicegerent on the earth, so judge between the people in truth.
Attribution: Qur'an, chapter 38, verse 26

The saying that names the true vicegerent, preserved verbatim.

> The ruler is the shadow of God on earth.
Attribution: the Prophet

Annotated structure. The valedictory supplication, the book turning from proving to asking.

Whole: the scholar's own closing prayer, what the whole austere machinery of the argument was in service of
Parts:
- First petition: gratitude for the blessings God has given, expressed not in words only but in words and deeds and intention together, the whole person turned toward thankfulness
- Second petition: success in obedience to the imam of the age, and the means to strive on that imam's behalf with hand and tongue and heart, the body's action, the mouth's speech, the heart's devotion
- Third petition: that God open the whole world to that imam and, through him, purify the earth of every unbelief and every hypocrisy, by his bounty and grace
Annotation: having shown that the imam stands at the summit, the scholar asks only to be allowed to serve that summit truly, and to be given the gratitude such a station deserves. The prayer gathers the book's argument into a posture of the soul.

The treatise's final words, the argument dissolving into worship, preserved verbatim.

> Praise be to God, the praise of the thankful, the mindful, and the pious, and blessings upon His Messenger, our master Muhammad, and all his progeny.
Attribution: the closing line of the treatise

## What this chapter lands

Hierarchy. The whole ladder the treatise climbed, seen at a glance from its top.

- Level 1: The pole of religion, that guidance is a necessity woven into reality
  - Level 2: The theory of degrees, climbing from the four natures through the stars to humankind, teaching the eye that every ordered domain gathers its excellence to a summit
    - Level 3: Nature rises to humanity, the summit of the created world
      - Level 4: Humanity rises to the imams, the summit of humanity
        - Level 5: The imams rise to Ali b. Abi Talib, the summit of summits, continued in the reigning imam of the age
Reading: the middle stretch turned to worship and the pillars of a religious life, the later chapters read history as one unbroken succession of guides and measured the ordinary claimants against the standard. Every movement was preparing the ground for this recognition, that the imam's whole conduct is a shadow cast by God's own dealing with His creation.

Annotated structure. The boldest and truest thing the scholar says, and the two ways to miss it.

Whole: the highest praise a human being can receive
Parts:
- The claim: not that he is wise, or just, or generous in the ordinary way, but that he treats people the way God treats them, which is the very mark of the vicegerent
- The conduct: gives without being paid, conceals without being cruel, draws near and withdraws to keep the soul awake between fear and hope, asks for nothing in return
- The first way to miss him: to judge him by the measure of ordinary rulers is to miss him entirely
- The second way to miss him: to inflate him into a god is to miss him just as badly
Annotation: he is the shadow that perfectly reproduces what casts it. To recognize him is to recognize the point at which the visible world touches the invisible one.

Comparison matrix. The completed ascent the engine of the book arrived at, one summit per order.

Rows: Nature, Humanity, The imam
Columns: The summit | The relation
- Nature: the human being | everything below culminates in him
- Humanity: the imam | the merits of the human race are perfected in him
- The imam: God's own conduct reflected in his dealings | the visible world touching the invisible
Reading: there is no higher rung to climb. The engine that reached for a lump of gold and a red ruby has run its full course. The ladder has reached its top, and the scholar, having climbed it, lays down his proof and lifts up a prayer, which is exactly where a book like this was always meant to arrive.
