# Slide Deck Framing, EP01-the-fatimid-world-and-al-naysaburi

## Audience
Asif's children, thoughtful young adults who have heard the audio but never opened the source treatise or a Fatimid history.

## Core Principle
The audio carries the narrative and the argument. Each slide exists only to make visible a relationship that words in sequence cannot hold at once. If a slide could be a single spoken sentence, it should not exist.

## Visual Priorities
- The two grounds of the leadership argument, revelation against reason and nature, as side-by-side columns.
- The timeline of the Ismaili rise, from the 870s through the Ismaili century to al-Naysaburi's decade.
- The seven sections of the treatise as one annotated skeleton, the whole book seen at a glance.
- The summit correspondence, sun to the heavens, gold to the metals, imam to humankind, as one parallel structure.

## Prohibited Patterns
- No slides that restate the audio narration.
- No literal illustrations of the source quotes or of scripture.
- No stock-photo imagery of mosques, deserts, or manuscripts.
- No bullet lists dressed up as diagrams; every slide commits to a named structure with its parts placed.

## Steering Phrases
- "Each slide must add visual information not present in the audio."
- "Show the relationships between entities, not a list of them."
- "When two positions are contrasted, show them side by side as columns, attribute by attribute."
- "Avoid stock-photo-style imagery. Every slide is a diagram or it does not exist."
