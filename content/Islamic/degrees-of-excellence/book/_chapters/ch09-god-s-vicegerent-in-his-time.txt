# God's Vicegerent in His Time

We shall now mention some of the merits of the lord of the age and time (walī al-'asr wa zaman), the Commander of the Faithful, though someone of my standing could never attain the degree that would permit a true account of his merits, just as God the Exalted has said:

> "They have not valued God according to His true value."

Yet I will speak according to my limited knowledge and capacity, and the Commander of the Faithful is most worthy to accept the excuse of his servant. God the Exalted, in the fullness of His bounty toward His servants, called upon them to give thanks for His blessings; and when they proved unable to render thanks commensurate with those blessings, He forgave them and was content with their saying "al-hamdulillah" (praise be to God). This was a lesson for His creation: whenever gratitude to a benefactor is obligatory yet one cannot express it in full proportion to the bounty received, one should give thanks to the best of one's ability and not abandon gratitude altogether merely because one cannot give him his due.

We say further that God the Exalted has made the earth radiant with His light, as He promised and declared:

> "And the earth shines by the light of her Lord ..."

This means that the earth of outward reality shines through the justice of the Commander of the Faithful, and the earth of inner meaning shines through knowledge and interpretation.

In every species, genus, root, branch, and law, there is a peak and summit of nobility, merit, rank, degree, and station that nothing else can reach. It is therefore necessary that the imams (عليهم السلام) likewise possess a peak, a summit, and an utmost degree that no one before them has attained. That peak and utmost degree is the Commander of the Faithful, 'Alī. He is the summit of all summits, the furthest limit beyond which there is no other limit. Just as the wisdom of the world, and of the animals and plants within it, came to full expression in the emergence of human beings, and the well-being of all creation was made complete through humanity, and the benefit of all created things returns ultimately to mankind; and just as it is through the imams (عليهم السلام) that the merits of humanity have been perfected and wisdom has become manifest in creation, so that the benefit of all human beings flows back to the imams: in the same way, through the Commander of the Faithful, all the affairs (umūr) of the prophets, legatees, and imams reached their completion, their order (amr) was organized, and perfect wisdom became manifest in their dispensation, their laws, and their esoteric interpretations. He (ع) is like the head, and they are like the body. They are the body, while the Commander of the Faithful is the spirit, and no body is complete without the spirit. The Commander of the Faithful is like the heart, while all those who have passed away are like the limbs. It is through the union of spirit and life in the heart that all the limbs receive life and sustenance. Every nail, every hair, every limb receives its share of nourishment and vitality by means of the heart. This is the sensory knowledge, the recompense of action, that comes to the embryo in its womb (dār 'amalihi) through the six stages of development from sperm to fully formed creation.

Likewise, it is through the Commander of the Faithful that people attain recompense and the fullness of life in the seventh stage. He (ع) is the one who awakens in every soul the desire to draw near to him, and he is the one through whom God has promised creation the fulfilment of its reward on His day. As God said of the Judgement:

> "... And you will be paid only that which you have earned fairly on the Day of Resurrection. Whoever is removed from the Fire and allowed to enter Paradise will indeed be triumphant."

God commands creation to believe in Him and in His Day of Judgement when He says:

> "... They believe in God and the Last Day ..."

Just as all of humanity exists as the final realm of nature (mawalid) in the world, having absorbed everything that existed in the previous realms and surpassing it in ways those realms could not share, and just as the imams gathered within themselves all the merits that existed in humanity, being their peak, their summit, and their quintessence, capable of receiving what lay beyond ordinary human capacity, so that the benefit of all who preceded them flows to the imams: in the same way, the Commander of the Faithful (ع) gathers within himself all the merits of those who came before him, and all their benefit and knowledge converges in him, surpassing what came before many times over. Just as the Prophet's power and merit derived from gathering within himself the merits of those in previous cycles, all their knowledge and more, so too the Commander of the Faithful (ع) gathers within himself the merits of all the prophets, imams, and sages who preceded him in previous cycles, and exceeds them in matters beyond their capacity. Had he not arisen in this station, the perfection of wisdom in their arising (عليهم السلام) would not have been achieved. The Commander of the Faithful (ع) is therefore the totality of everything in the worlds today, spiritual, physical, and conventional (wad'i). It is for his sake that these worlds were created, and all of them are his agents and propagators until His Day of Judgement. He is the one who arose in the world to perfect the Creator's vicegerency among humanity in the universe. We have indeed said that he is the perfector (mutimm) of all those who have passed away.

Some may object that this description applies to the final qā'im, and that the Commander of the Faithful (ع) is not the final one. We reply that the Commander of the Faithful (ع) is the qā'im of his own time and age, and the qā'im of all those who preceded him, the perfector of them all. He is the sixth through whom the command of the seventh will be perfected. He will complete what the seventh requires, just as the Prophet, may God bless him, established all that the qā'im would need in order to become the perfector of what the Prophet had established and arranged. The Commander of the Faithful (ع) is the ninth in a different reckoning. The command of the seventh, al-Mu'izz, who has passed away, is perfected through the ninth, al-Hākim, just as the seven members of the human body reach their perfection, order, and function through the nine, that is, the four natures and the five senses, and just as the perfection of the human creature is achieved in nine months. This reckoning is founded on the number nine. Life in the hereafter is grounded in four spiritual natures and five spiritual senses, as God said:

> "Indeed, the abode of the hereafter, that is life, if only they knew."

The Commander of the Faithful (ع) is the second by a different calculation, for the order at the beginning of each cycle is perfected by the second. He is also the sixth, through whose sword God will conquer the whole world, just as He conquered through the Prophet, may God bless him, the sixth one from Adam. The Commander of the Faithful (ع) arose in the world to perfect the vicegerency (khilafa) of the Creator of the universe among His creatures. He deals with creation in all circumstances in the same way that God, the Exalted, deals with His creatures, with regard to their constitution, their sustenance, and the abundance of His mercy toward them. Each of those who arose before him fulfilled only a small portion of this, but the Commander of the Faithful (ع) fulfilled the whole of it. A vicegerent is one who, over those entrusted to him, carries out everything that the one who appointed him would do.

Indeed, Adam, like all other vicegerents, was called "vicegerent" (khalifa) because he was the first to undertake the vicegerency. Those who came after him fulfilled some of its conditions and so were called by that name, for whoever discharges part of a duty is known by its name. The Commander of the Faithful, however, performs both the worldly and the religious vicegerency in full reality. When the Prophet, may God bless him, said,

> "The ruler is the shadow of God on earth,"

he meant none other than the Commander of the Faithful (ع), for a shadow perfectly imitates what casts it in every detail. No one deals with creation as God, the Exalted, does, except the Commander of the Faithful (ع). The Prophet, may God bless him, also said:

> "Indeed God, the Exalted, establishes (naza'a) proof through the ruler beyond what He establishes through the Qur'an,"

and the Commander of the Faithful (ع) is the one through whom God establishes what He does not establish through the Qur'an.

The philosophers have said that philosophy is the pursuit of resemblance to God (al-tashabbuh bi'l-bāri'), glory be to Him, according to human capacity. To seek resemblance to the Creator, glory be to Him, means dealing with mankind as God, may His mention be exalted, deals with them, interacting with them, and with oneself, in the manner that God acts with both humanity and the individual, and in accordance with their nature. The philosophers have borrowed this idea from the people of the truth (ahl al-haqīqa), yet none of them have been able to realise its true meaning except the Commander of the Faithful. Indeed, everything he does with humankind mirrors God's own dealings with them in the order of the world, in sustenance, and in all related matters. We shall now mention a small portion of what is appropriate to the time in which we live.

Let us begin by saying that the compassion, mercy, bounty, and forgiveness of the Commander of the Faithful, together with his forbearance toward people's failings and disobedience, are beyond my power to describe. All of it follows God's own conduct toward humanity, in obedience to God's command and in emulation of His character.

God in His bounty bestows blessings upon people whether they are pious or impious, believers or unbelievers. Had He granted worldly benefit to believers alone and withheld it from unbelievers, people's faith would have been motivated by worldly gain rather than by God or His religion. Conversely, had He denied it to all believers and given it only to unbelievers, most people's belief (īmān) would surely have reverted to unbelief (kufr). He therefore gave it to some unbelievers and withheld it from others, just as He gave it to some believers and withheld it from others, giving it to each at certain times and withholding it at others. Likewise, the Commander of the Faithful bestows worldly benefit on Muslims and unbelievers alike, and withholds it from them too; he bestows it on believers and hypocrites alike, and withholds it from them too. He gave to all of them at some times and withheld at others. Had he given everything to the believers, their faith would largely have been for the sake of this world; but had he withheld from all believers at all times, they would surely have grown despondent and their good intentions would have weakened. Therefore, the Commander of the Faithful bestowed blessings on the believers for a time such as no one else had ever bestowed, and he also withheld from them for a time, for their own benefit, lest they become like those of whom God said:

> "Nay, indeed man certainly becomes rebellious, considering himself self-sufficient."

God gave worldly goods to the pious and the impious alike, so that the pious would not perform good deeds merely for the sake of this world, nor refrain from immorality merely for worldly reasons, nor hold back from disobedience only for worldly benefit, which would deprive them of their reward in the hereafter. Rather, their conduct should be purely for the sake of religion and God. Likewise, the Commander of the Faithful (ع) gives worldly benefits to the righteous and the immoral alike.

God, the Exalted, has given this temporal world to those who obey and those who disobey, out of His wisdom, so that obedience should not be for the sake of the world, nor restraint from disobedience because of it; rather, both should be purely for the sake of God. Similarly, the Commander of the Faithful gives to the obedient and the disobedient alike, following the actions of God, the Exalted, so that humanity's obedience to him is not for worldly advantage and the reward of the hereafter is not forfeited.

God gives worldly goods to the worshipper and the non-worshipper alike, lest people's worship be motivated by worldly gain. Similarly, the Commander of the Faithful looks upon his people not focusing on their faults and disobedience, but regarding them with a merciful eye. God, the Exalted, has given worldly benefit to the simpleton and the ignorant, and has given it to the learned and the intelligent, while also withholding it from both. Likewise, the Commander of the Faithful gives to the simpleton and the ignorant, the learned and the intelligent alike, out of his wisdom and virtue. Were he to give only to the learned and the intelligent and withhold from all others, people would pursue knowledge solely for the sake of this world, and their use of reason and acquisition of virtues would equally be for this world alone, leaving the ignorant and the simpleton in despair. But God has equally given the world to the simpleton and the ignorant.

He has also given worldly benefit to the wrongdoer and the just, to the generous and the miser, to the noble and the depraved and the lowly, to those possessing every virtue and those lacking it, giving to all of them equally out of His wisdom, mercy, and grace. Likewise, the Commander of the Faithful treats all of them equally, following the actions of God, the Exalted, and His mercy upon His creatures, so that everyone may receive their fair share of His bounty and kindness.

Furthermore, God, the Exalted, has concealed the workings of the world and the means by which His creatures may benefit from it, hiding from mankind both the methods and the actions required to acquire worldly benefits. Thus one person becomes wealthy while another has nothing to eat; one grows rich through trade and commerce while another goes bankrupt; one prospers from agriculture while another loses even his seeds; and one becomes self-sufficient through hunting while another is impoverished. All of this reflects God's surpassing wisdom, lest a single path to worldly gain become universally known while all other paths become futile, bringing disorder upon creation and corrupting the world. The Commander of the Faithful's dealings with mankind in this world follow the same pattern. He conceals the means to worldly goods, so that one person seeks them by a particular means and receives them, while another who seeks them by the very same means is denied. Were people to discover a definite method for obtaining worldly goods from the Commander of the Faithful, all other means would certainly become futile. All those who lacked that one means would despair of his mercy, be left deprived, and there would be no merit in any of it.

The Commander of the Faithful's conduct in drawing people close to him or keeping his distance from them follows the same principle. Were he to push the hypocrites away and draw only the believers near, people would become believers in order to gain his favour, not out of genuine religion. And were he to keep his distance from all believers at all times, they would withdraw from him, anxiety creeping into their hearts, either from a sense of the Commander of the Faithful's displeasure or from a fear that some slip on their part had warranted it. He therefore deals with people according to what the prevailing times and conditions require, arousing desire and alarm, drawing near and pushing away, granting and withholding, punishing and forgiving, all of it following the actions and character of God, the Exalted, so that people remain perpetually in a state of both fear and hope: neither feeling so secure that they grow bold, nor so despairing that they become despondent. In all of this he emulates God's manner with His creation. Just as God, may He be glorified, gives abundantly to all, including those who have no fear of poverty or the exhaustion of wealth, so too the Commander of the Faithful gives to those who have no such fear, surpassing the generosity of all other human beings.

Whoever looks with clear sight at the actions of the Commander of the Faithful in matters of religion and the world will find that everything he does with creation, commanding good, forbidding evil, granting and withholding, runs parallel to the actions of God, the Exalted. Yet people are baffled by this because they judge his actions by the standard of ordinary human beings and of what they have previously heard. They compare the actions of the Commander of the Faithful with those of other people and other rulers, against what they have heard, seen, and understood. Finding it at odds with what they are accustomed to, they fall into perplexity, confusion, and speculation. If they were instead to view his actions in relation to God's dealings with creation, everything would become far clearer. The difficulty arises only because, from the time of Adam to our own day, no one has ever acted in this manner. If anyone did act this way at any time, it was only in a small portion, as is reported in the story of the sage, Khidr, with Moses (ع). Moses was not patient with Khidr's actions because he measured them against his own deeds, knowledge, and understanding. Had he viewed them in relation to God's actions, the outcome would certainly have been different. Another example is the reported judgement of David and God's command in the perplexing and astonishing story of the owner of the vineyard and the owner of the bull. Similarly, God, the Exalted, has given warning in His words:

> "On the day when the summoner calls them to a painful end ... their eyes will be lowered in humiliation,"

and He said:

> "They were called to prostrate while safe and secure."

It is remarkable that if people cannot endure these worldly actions, how can they then endure the far greater demands of religious matters and things more difficult still? Everything found at the two extremes does not exist at the centre, and what exists at the centre does not exist at the extremes; yet people judge these matters by what they observe at the centre while themselves standing at the extremities. That is precisely why they become confused and confounded, unable to bear or endure it.

We also observe that the gifts of other monarchs are perceived by people as being given in return for a service, a relationship, an obligation, love, need, the pursuit of some benefit, or the averting of some harm. Such gifts may also arise from other motives that make them necessary: fear of calamity, hope of good fortune, fear of someone's censure, the desire for praise or exaltation, the wish for gratitude or goodwill, payment to an official, or provision of sustenance. All of this is conducted on the basis of calculation and assessment. The gifts of the Commander of the Faithful, however, transcend all of that, for gifts given for any of the reasons cited above cannot truly be called generosity, virtue, or beneficence. They amount to either the discharge of a past obligation or an advance payment toward a future one. Delaying what one owes and fulfilling rights belatedly is blameworthy and miserly; and advancing money in expectation of future benefit is not generosity or virtue. Nor is whatever is given in connection with indebtedness considered generosity, nobility, or virtue. The gifts of God, glory be to Him, transcend all of that, for God is under no obligation to give anyone anything, and He seeks no reward from any of His creatures. In the same way, the Commander of the Faithful does not give to anyone for any of the reasons cited, nor does he seek recompense for it. God, the Exalted, said regarding the Commander of the Faithful, 'Alī b. Abī Ṭālib:

> "We do not desire either reward or gratitude from them."

Rather, he bestows upon them spontaneously, out of generosity, nobility, and virtue, following the actions of God, the Exalted, with His creation.

Were people to view the actions of the Commander of the Faithful in this light, they would not fall into confusion. Yet some look at him with short-sightedness, judging his actions by the standard of other human beings, while others look at him with exaggeration, claiming divinity for him. The actions of the Commander of the Faithful surpass both of these positions. He is, rather, the Universal Vicegerent of God among His worshippers (al-khalīfat al-kullī lillāh ta'āla 'ala 'ibādihi).

Just as God, the Exalted, has concealed from mankind the reasons for creating the world while explaining the reasons of religion and the means of attaining it, He also explained ways of attaining His pleasure, clarifying it through the words of His Prophet. He gave knowledge and wisdom to those who deserved it and denied it to those who did not, and

> "He made before them a barrier and behind them a barrier,"

establishing signposts of guidance and the summons of the proof and the guide to the bounty of the hereafter. He disclosed ways of attaining to it and opened its gates, so that whoever deserves it attains it and he who does not misses it in utmost wisdom. Likewise, the Commander of the Faithful concealed the means of attaining worldly fortune through him, giving it to both those who deserve and those who do not. He explained the means of attaining religion, knowledge, and wisdom, and bestowed them upon those who deserved it, neither withholding them nor being miserly. He bountifully bestowed favours upon people, giving more than anyone could ever give in any time or age. He also withheld favours from the undeserving, differentiating between the people of truth (ahl al-haqq) and the people of falsehood (ahl al-bāṭil), following the ways of God, the Exalted, in governing the world and religion alike, and in building both this present world and the world to come.

We ask God to bestow upon us gratitude for what He has blessed us with in words, deeds, and intention, and to grant us success in obedience to the lord of the age and time (wali al-'asr wa'l-zaman), (ع). May He provide us with the means of striving on his behalf with our hands, tongues, and hearts. May God open the entire world to him and, through his sword, purify the earth of polytheism (shirk), unbelief (kufr), and hypocrisy (nifāq) through his bounty and generosity.

This worshipper begs and desires the blessings of the Commander of the Faithful (ع), and seeks the supreme command (al-amr al-ālī) to examine what the servant has gathered and compiled, to grant him permission for what is sound and correct, and to draw his attention to any mistakes, so that the servant may be thankful to him for deepening his insight and certainty, and so that this would become a means of guidance and evidence for those who seek to be guided, and a proof against those who deny.

Praise be to God, the praise of the thankful, the mindful, and the pious, and prayers upon His Messenger, our master Muhammad, and all his progeny.
