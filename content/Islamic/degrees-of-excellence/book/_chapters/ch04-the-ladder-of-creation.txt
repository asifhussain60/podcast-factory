# The Ladder of Creation

Section Six

[23] As for fruit-bearing trees, the peak and summit among them is the date palm. It resembles human beings in having male and female, and it requires fertilisation just as animals require mating. Moreover, every part of the date palm is beneficial and nothing goes to waste; its fruit is eaten at all times, whether raw or cooked. When someone dreams while asleep that he is plucking dates from a date palm, the interpretation is that he is going to receive benefit from a certain believer. That believer, in reality, is the imam, who is the head of all believers.

[24] I also maintain that among plants, certain mild medicines have attained such a degree of refinement and excellence that they have become antidotes (tiryāqan) for those on the verge of death. Among these, some cure chronic diseases while others stand lower in degree. The peak and summit among them points to the imam.

The imam and his word become antidotes for souls, a means of healing and relief from death, illness, doubt, and discord. The opponents who forcibly usurp the position of the imams (عليهم السلام) resemble the fatal poisons and harmful substances found among medicines. They are deadly poisons that destroy those who listen to them, accompany them, or incline toward them, unless the imam reaches those people with the great antidote that rescues their souls from destruction. These opponents are likewise comparable to useless and worthless stones. It is for this reason that God, the Exalted, said:

"... pebble-covered hell where you will return." (Qur'an 21:98)

And His words:

"... and guard yourself against the Fire that has been ignited for the people and the stones that have been prepared for the non-believers." (Qur'an 2:24)

[25] I also say that among animals the horse is the finest, among birds the eagle, and among beasts of prey the lion holds first place. The differences among these various species, in strength, usefulness, capacity for training, and the summit each attains within its own kind, point to the fact that there is a peak within the genus of animals, just as within the human species that summit is the imam (ع).

[26] I further say that some plants are sweet, delicious, and appetizing, and people benefit from them. Others are bitter, offering neither nourishment nor pleasure. Still others bear no fruit and are thorned, injuring those who come near them. The sweet and pleasant ones are like the imams, a symbol of their speech and knowledge, which captivate the soul and delight the eye. The bitter, putrid, and thorny ones symbolize the leaders of the literalists (ahl al-zahir). For this reason God, the Exalted, said:

"The example of a good word is like a good tree whose roots are firmly established and whose branches reach the heavens, giving its fruit perpetually in every season by the permission of its Lord. So God sets forth similitudes for mankind in order that they may reflect. And the similitude of a bad word is like that of a bad tree uprooted from the top of the earth, having no stability." (Qur'an 14:24-26)

[27] I also say that some plants are sweet-smelling, their fragrance refreshing and healing people of all diseases and ailments. The summit of their goodness and fragrance is a symbol of the imam and his words, which refresh and bring ease to people, while the putrid, repellent, and offensive plants are an indication of the imams of error, whose speech harms souls and may even destroy them.

[28] I maintain that the musk-giving gazelle and the honey-producing bee, indeed their chief (ya'sub), are an indication of the imam. It is for this reason that the Prophet, may God bless him, said to the Commander of the Faithful, 'Ali:

"You are the chief (ya'sub) of the believers." (al-Majlisi, Bihar al-anwar, vol. 38, p. 144)

And God, the Exalted, said:

"And your Lord inspired the bee to choose its homes in the mountains and on the trees and on that which they thatch; then eat from all the fruits and follow the path of your Lord skilfully, that He has made smooth for you. From within its bellies a drink of varying colours comes forth in which there is healing for mankind ..." (Qur'an 16:68-69)

[29] In the same way, I maintain that humankind is the ultimate among animals and within the realms of nature. The advantage found in everything that preceded the creation of mankind, principles, derivatives, and realms of nature alike, returns ultimately to humankind. The merits of the world, of animals, plants, and all other things, are contained within the human being; their benefit, glory, value, and goodness all become manifest through mankind. Were it not for mankind, the merit of all things would never become apparent, nor would the order of the world hold. Nothing would be complete; no benefit would accrue, nor would anything be preserved, whether from plants or animals. It is the human being who harnesses water and irrigates the land, bringing it under rational control and drawing it from valleys, rivers, and wells. It is man who tills the earth, cultivates it, builds dwellings upon it, and waters plants and fields, bringing the land under his rational governance.

Beyond this, it is the human being who has made use of wind and air; it is man who discovered fire by means of flint and achieved the crossbreeding of animals where none existed before. Man grafted different trees to produce fruits previously unknown in the world. He extracted jewels from minerals and brought beneficial trees, seeds, and fruits under his control and governance. He protected the welfare of animals and plants and their produce, and brought animals under his command. Man is the pinnacle of animals, plants, minerals, and all other realms of nature. Through him, the welfare of animals and everything that came before was perfected and ordered. Likewise, the imam is the pinnacle and summit of all human beings, their quintessence and their perfection. It is through the imam that the affairs of mankind are set in order. Just as the benefit of everything that preceded human beings returns to mankind, so too the benefit of all humanity and their quintessence returns to the imams (عليهم السلام). Human beings learn virtues and knowledge from them, and acquire minds and understanding through them. It is through the imams that human welfare is safeguarded in worldly and religious matters and in the life hereafter. The imams guide people toward recognition of the Creator (ma'rifa al-Sani'), teaching the necessity of gratitude to the benefactor and the proper manner of expressing it. It is through the imams that mankind recognizes the messengers and learns how they ought to be obeyed.

[30] Just as the benefit of all animals and everything that preceded them returns to mankind, and man thereby became their leader, so too the benefit of all human beings returns to the imams, who became their leaders, their commanders, and their restrainers. Were it not for the imams, and the direction and guidance they give humanity, and their protection of human welfare in this life and the next, and their encouragement to acquire virtues and avoid vices and animal habits, there would be no difference between beasts and human beings; indeed, animals would be the superior. If man did not accept the training needed to acquire virtues for his soul, and did not learn to stimulate his intellect through the imams, he would be worse off than animals, since animals are not as prone to evil as humans are, and animals possess numerous benefits. Without intellect and religion, man would be wholly given over to evil and corruption. It is on this account that God, the Exalted and Glorious, said:

"For the worst of beasts in the sight of God are the deaf and dumb, those who understand not." (Qur'an 8:22)

The Exalted also said:

"They are like cattle, nay, more misguided, for they are heedless of warning." (Qur'an 7:179)

Were there no humanity, there would be no wisdom in the creation of animals and plants, for there would be no one to benefit from them. Likewise, were it not for the imams (عليهم السلام), there would be no wisdom in creating humanity, and their superiority would never become manifest. Human beings have been created for religion and for the acquisition of virtues and knowledge. So learn knowledge and religion from the imams (عليهم السلام), for through them religion is established and perfected. If the excellence of man were not made manifest through religion, then the value of plants and animals would not become known either, and there would be no wisdom in the creation of humanity, plants, and animals. And if there were no wisdom in their creation, there would be no wisdom in the creation of all the worlds.

[31] It is therefore true to say that all the worlds, the entirety of natural creation, have been created for humanity, and that humanity has been created for the imams (عليهم السلام): to receive religion from them, to be perfected through them, and to manifest their virtues, their organization, and their foundation. Since all the worlds and all creatures were created for the imams (عليهم السلام), and all the benefits of the worlds belong to them, and the rest of creation depends on them and stands to them as children, whatever they bestow upon creation is given by their favour, generosity, and mercy. If they withhold anything, they are under no obligation to give, nor do they owe gratitude for it. Whoever takes anything from this world and what it contains without the permission and approval of the imam has taken what is haram, forbidden, and has committed an act of usurpation. Thus God said:

"And that which God bestows on the Messenger from the people of the villages, it is for God and His Messenger." (Qur'an 59:7)

The word "bestowal" (fay') means "return," and it is for this reason that a shadow is called fay', because it returns to the place from which it first came. The whole world comes from the fortune and property of the imams, and whatever returns to their property is, in truth, a fay', a return.

[32] I further say that plants surpass the earth by virtue of a single additional spirit, the spirit of growth (ruh al-nama'), and the earth thereby becomes subservient to the plants growing upon it, which draw their strength from it. Animals surpass plants by yet another spirit added to the spirit of growth, the spirit of sensation (ruh al-hiss), and so they subdue the earth, moving across it, and subdue the plants by eating them. Since humans surpass animals by means of the spirit of articulated speech or reason (ruh al-nutq), they take charge of the earth: they build, sow, and plant upon it, bringing it under their wisdom and control. They eat plants and fruits, using what they wish and burning what they will. Man also subdues animals, riding those he needs, slaughtering some, milking others, shearing their wool, and employing them as he sees fit, even against the animals' own inclination.

When the imam assumes his charge, he is endowed with additional spirits, powers, and a purity that no ordinary human being possesses. Thus the pinnacle of purity in the human being is combined in the imam together with the subtlety and refinement of nature. The peak of the sensuous animals, their choicest elements, the furthest felicity of the celestial spheres, the balance of nature, and the subtlest influence of all the celestial spheres and stars are so combined in him that he becomes the quintessence of the whole world. Then the subtleties of the spirit of reason, pure thought, and the entire intellectual spirit unite in the imam, along with the total sanctified spirit (ruh al-quds). Man possesses only a portion of this spirit, no more than what he acquires from the imam, and no more than the imam chooses to bestow by his grace. Connected to this is the creative spirit (ruh al-ibda'), which is the perfect seventh spirit that God has prepared for humanity in the world of reward. It is the spirit of Oneness (ruh al-wahda).

[33] These are some of the merits that God, the Exalted, has bestowed upon him, in addition to what he attains purely through purity of character, generosity, giving, forbearance, courage, and other qualities that the likes of us cannot enumerate, along with his freedom from all impurity, shame, and defect. So when God gathered in the imams all these faculties, merits, and spirits, in addition to what mankind already possesses, He subjected all humanity to them, giving them control over everything that preceded mankind: animals, plants, earth, and minerals, making all of it subservient to the imams. This mirrors the way humans dominate animals and rule over them by virtue of one additional spirit, and the way animals control plants by virtue of one additional spirit, and the way plants dominate the earth by virtue of yet another additional spirit. Moreover, the great number of spirits residing in the imams (عليهم السلام) determines the extent of their control and dominion over humanity, entitling them to rule over people convincingly in accordance with the requirements of those faculties and spirits, in light of what I described earlier concerning human beings. It is therefore not for human beings to remove themselves from the imam's domain and reign, just as it is not for animals to remove themselves from the control of man. Those animals that refuse to submit to human control, such as beasts of prey that offer no benefit or merit, are judged by reason and by law to be lawfully killed, destroyed, and eliminated, because they refuse to obey human beings and remain under the authority of their supremacy.

In the same manner, those who distance themselves from obedience to the imam prove themselves to be like wild beasts, whose killing, destruction, and elimination becomes lawful, on account of their rejection of the imams' rule as required by religious law, intellect, nature, and sound governance.

But whoever obeys the imams (عليهم السلام) and submits to their rulings in this world will escape the sword and punishment here, and will live in peace, well-being, and justice. It is obligatory upon the imam (ع), by the norm of kingship, the demands of nature, sound policy, and law, to protect the welfare of mankind, just as man, in governing animals, protects their well-being, nourishment, and flourishing. Whoever submits to the imams' rule over their own spirit and body will be saturated with knowledge and wisdom, invigorated by it and enabled to stand out among other people, living in the tranquillity of knowledge and enjoying its fragrance. They will depart this abode of deeds for the abode of recompense in an upright form, fully prepared to receive their recompense and eternal life.

Whoever submits to the imams' rule outwardly but not inwardly will fall into the torment of ignorance, perplexity, doubt, discord, and regret, and will arrive at the abode of recompense in a crooked form, unprepared to receive recompense, and will remain in torment and punishment.
