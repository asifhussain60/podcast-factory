# By Nature and by Reason

Section Two

[6] We say, then, that the necessity (wujūb) of the imamate and of the imams (عليهم السلام) is grounded in natural disposition (jibla), innate constitution (fiṭra), nature (ṭabīʿa), intellect (ʿaql), governance (siyāsa), prescription (waḍʿa), and established norms (rusūm). It is obligatory in all revealed law (sharīʿa), in religion, and in the religious communities, just as the existence of the Creator is necessary by innate constitution, by natural disposition, and by intellect in every religious community and every religion. And yet people fell into disagreement about the Creator even after His necessity had been established and after they had accepted His existence. They began to draw conclusions according to their personal whims and opinions, and through blind imitation of their forebears and teachers, claiming that the Creator whose existence innate constitution and natural disposition require is in reality nothing but substance, or the elements, or the stars, or the sun, or something else of that kind. At times they described the Creator with attributes that amount to likening Him to His creation, that is, anthropomorphism, or to something bordering on polytheism, resting all of this upon their own judgement without any authentic knowledge. In the very same way, with regard to the necessity of the imamate, people fell into disagreement after committing themselves to the imam. Some insisted that the imam established by intellect and self-evidence is merely the wise man of every age, a view akin to that of the philosophers, who call their teachers "imams" (aʾimma), "sages" (ḥukamāʾ), "head of the scholars" (raʾs al-ʿulamāʾ), "master of the world" (saʾis), and "the teacher" (al-muʿallim).

Section Three

[7] The followers of the various religions have called their past imams "prophets" and "sages" in every cycle, while certain followers of Islam have applied the title "imam" to usurping, oppressive false pretenders, and have likewise given the name "imam" to their scholars and jurists. Yet if you examine all these cases carefully, you find that every one of them confirms the existence of the imam as a necessity (ḍarūra) and is unable to deny it. Whoever seeks salvation and guidance must set aside his inclinations and desires and come to understand the deficiency in every false claimant to this designation: his want of knowledge, gnosis, and cognition, his lack of piety, and his failure to meet the other conditions of the imamate. Once such a person has recognized the pretender's ignorance and the shallowness of his piety and modesty, and his inability to fulfil a single one of the conditions of the imamate, he will reject that pretender's claim until such time as he attains recognition of the true imam (maʿrifat imām al-ḥaqq).

[8] We shall now examine some of the claims made by each pretender, together with his weaknesses and his limitations. We shall turn his own principles against him and strip the imamate from those who are unfit for it. We shall then establish the imamate of the true imams (عليهم السلام) with proofs and demonstrations, and establish the obligation of obeying them, of entrusting our affairs to their command, and of receiving revelation, interpretation, and the confession of Divine Unity from them, just as we set out in Kitāb al-tawḥīd, the Book on Divine Unity. All those who truly know created beings come to recognize their inability and deficiency and reject any claim of divinity for them, until at last they deny divinity to all created beings. In this way Divine Unity remains pure, free from the ascription of human qualities to God (tashbīh) and free from the denial of all attributes to God (taʿṭīl).

[9] We also say that God, the Exalted, through His subtle wisdom and His boundless power, created for the human body certain principles, causes, and means. He made all the derivatives and realms of nature manifest in them and through their effects, so that these would emerge in a predetermined manner and within a set term, until He brought forth from them a body furnished with senses that correspond to those principles and are in harmony with them. He made those principles and means the body's sustenance and nourishment, through which it survives and by which its corporeal and perceptual reward and punishment are determined. In the same way, He established principles, causes, and means for souls, spirits, and religious noetic forms, from whose effects they become manifest. Through these their sustenance, nourishment, survival, and reward are determined, while their punishment is determined by the disruption of their support and their distancing from their origin.

[10] God, may His name be exalted, made the principles and causes of the body a testimony and a proof for the principles of the soul, as well as for its derivatives and its nourishment, so that the religious creation would mirror the physical creation, corresponding and parallel to it. Just as the body manifests and perfects itself only through the celestial spheres together with the stars (kawākib), for it cannot be perfected through the spheres alone without the stars, even though the stars themselves revolve around the spheres, and even though the course of events is always shaped by the stars, so too the manifestation and perfection of the religious noetic forms and the everlasting souls come about only through the influence of the imams (عليهم السلام). Their nourishment comes from the imams' knowledge, for the imams are the religious stars, as the Prophet, may God bless him and his family, said:

"My companions are like the stars; whomsoever you emulate, you will be guided."

This holds even as the imams act in the place of the prophets, who are like the celestial spheres. The course of affairs for all created beings, and their ultimate recourse, is therefore toward the imams. Were we to elaborate fully upon the parallel between the religious celestial spheres and the natural celestial spheres, this epistle would grow overlong and exceed its intended scope. We shall therefore confine ourselves to certain indications in the introduction of this treatise, for those who wish to pursue further research and to grasp aspects of what we have left unmentioned, if God, the Exalted, wills.

[11] We say, accordingly, that the existence of differences and degrees of excellence in all things, encompassing the elements, the realms of nature, and their derivatives, constitutes the firmest evidence and proof for affirming the imamate and the imams. This is because God, the Exalted, created all things different and disparate in their degree of excellence across all genera and species, and then assigned to each genus and species an upper limit (ghāya), a summit (dhurwa), an end (nihāya), and a distinguishing virtue (faḍīla) that nothing else in that category can attain. Either He made those genera and species foundational to creation, without which humanity cannot manage, nor its bodily existence and function be complete, or else He made them sources of nourishment, remedy, elegance, adornment, and indispensable tools. In all things He placed an advantage (manfaʿa) that exists nowhere else. He powerfully endowed in them a capacity that creation would necessarily need, one it can neither avoid nor sustain its physical affairs without. He also subjected them to a test and impelled them to seek whatever would remedy their deficiency. Thus, when one cannot find the cure for that deficiency in its immediate place, one seeks it elsewhere, or hastens to wherever it may be found.

As long as one remains in the world of the body, one cannot for a single moment do without breathing (tanāsum) or without drawing benefit from the elements of one's world: its derivatives, its realms of nature, its vegetation, and its nourishment. All of this is a wisdom from God, the Exalted, and a mark of His justice, so that these aspects might serve as witnesses and as ephemeral, obligatory symbols (al-mithālāt al-jabriyyāt) for the symbolized realities that are religious, eternal, and chosen. The benefit of all the symbolized realities, encompassing principles, realms of nature, nourishment, and religious remedies, is more deserving, more worthy, and more powerful than the obligatory symbols. The need for them is greater than the need for what nature compels, because the benefit of the natural symbols returns to the body, while the benefit of the symbolized realities returns to the spirit and the soul. The degree to which the intellectual symbolized realities excel the natural symbols corresponds to the degree to which the spirit excels the body. Likewise, the superiority of the symbolized over the symbol corresponds to the superiority of the everlasting over the perishable. The excellence in each of them reflects the deficiency of the other. For whoever is deprived of the physical things he needs will not necessarily be destroyed by that deprivation, and even if such destruction were to occur, it would only be the destruction of his body, which will undoubtedly perish and die in any case. In the absence of the symbolized religious reality, however, the soul itself faces destruction and plunges into perpetual punishment. If people's striving to obtain what the soul needs were equal to their striving and desire to obtain what the body needs, then no two people would doubt, go astray, or fall into anthropomorphism (tashbīh).

[12] We also say that everything God, the Exalted, has created in this order, and humanity's need of it, as we described earlier, is an admonition directing people toward the symbolized religious reality. It is a witness for all who seek such testimony, a proof for all who ask for evidence, and an insight for all who observe and reflect. It is equally a proof against those who deny the symbolized religious reality of the imams and their hierarchies, denying their knowledge and the benefit they bring, while at the same time accepting the benefit derived from the natural elements, the planets, and the celestial spheres.

God, the Exalted, established these elements, means, and natural causes so firmly that no one can deny them, argue against them, alter them, or change them in any way. This stands in stark contrast to what certain people did with the religious law and with the testimony the Prophet had called them to bear: the affirmation of the imamate and the imams, the obligation to follow them, devotion to them, obedience to them, the condemnation of those who disobey them, and the promise of reward for those who obey. They distorted all of this, just as they changed and distorted the Quran. God, the Exalted, has informed us of this in His words:

"…changing words from their context while they forgot a portion of what We have reminded them of." (5:13)

And God, Whose mention is exalted, has also said:

"Indeed, forgetfulness is only an excess of disbelief…" (9:37)

Yet what they committed was not forgetfulness at all. They abandoned the testimony deliberately, as though they had never seen it and never heard it.

[13] Just as the testimony of the outer horizons and the inner selves is compulsory rather than chosen, and therefore impossible for anyone to alter, to replace, or to suppress, so too did the Messenger, may God bless him, make testimony compulsory, allowing the witnesses no choice to change it, alter it, or conceal it. He threatened whoever would change or conceal it with a painful punishment. God, the Exalted, commanded that judgement be rendered upon the testimony of two witnesses, saying:

"If one of them falls into error, the other will remind. And the witnesses must not refuse when they are summoned." (2:282)

By this He meant that true judgement in religion, and in affirming the imamate and the hierarchies, rests with two witnesses: one established by law and the other compelled by nature. When people deny the legally established testimony, the one bound by nature, namely the horizons and our own inner selves, steps in to remind, to enforce, and to verify the conventional witnesses, just as God, the Exalted, says:

"We shall show them Our signs on the horizons and within themselves until it becomes manifest to them that it is the Truth." (41:53)

And just so, when the Prophet was asked, "Who is the witness of your prophethood?" he answered:

"My witness is every stone and every piece of clay."

He was thereby pointing to the testimony of the horizons and the souls. When legal and natural witnesses both testify, and the intellect serves as the criterion for weighing that testimony without preference or partiality, then truth swiftly becomes manifest.

[14] We shall now discuss the differences and the degrees of excellence within each principle and realm of nature, and show that the ultimate limit and highest summit in every genus and species points to the imam in every age and time. And if these genera and species demonstrate the imamate and the imams through a different framework, such as the seven planets serving as testimony for the seven imams, and other heptads besides, that does not preclude a proof being drawn, through the peak and perfection of every genus and species, for the imam in the manner we have just described.
