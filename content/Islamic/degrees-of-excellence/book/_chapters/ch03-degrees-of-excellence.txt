# Degrees of Excellence

The wisest among the philosophers speak of ten categories and hold that nothing can be mentioned or discussed except in relation to these ten: substance (jawhar), quantity (kammiyya), quality (kayfiyya), relation (idāfa), place (makān), time (zamān), possession (jidda), posture (nusba), action (fā'il), and affection (maf'ūl). The highest of the ten is substance, upon which all the others rest and over which all the others it excels, since the remaining nine are established through it. The substance that the philosophers describe is therefore a proof for the permanent, intellectual, luminous, and authentic substance by which all souls are illumined and upon which all nine religious accidents (al-a'rād) depend. Yet this substance has need of no created thing. It draws benefit from nothing and no one in the world; rather, everything benefits from it and stands in need of it. That is the imam (ع). All other ranks stand below him just as the nine accidents stand in relation to substance.

I also say that among the four basic constituents (al-'anāṣir) from which the first composites (tarākib) were formed, two are active and two are passive. The active ones are heat and cold, while the passive ones are dryness and humidity. The highest and noblest among them is heat, which gives rise to luminosity, light, motion, movement, and elevation, while the other three are established through it. Heat thus takes precedence both in origin and in existence, and no foundation or realm of nature is complete without it. In the same way, the imam illumines souls, and from him come intellectual light (al-nūr al-'aqlī), motion (al-ḥaraka), and elevation (al-'ulūw). No principle or religious realm is complete without him.

Among the celestial spheres, the most exalted and supreme is the outermost sphere (falak al-aflāk), within which all the others orbit. In the same way, the imam in his religion is the supreme religious sphere encompassing all the others, and all the heavenly bodies and religious signs of the zodiac move under his command. If someone were to argue that this is rather a symbol for the qā'im, the Resurrector, on the grounds that the highest principle in the world corresponds to the Intellect with respect to the four principles, my reply is that the imam is the qā'im in his own time; he is the proclaimer (nāṭiq) in his age, and he is the one who occupies the position of the qā'im. The imam's position in his own realm is that of the Universal Intellect ('aql al-kullī).

### Section Four

I also say that among the four elements (al-arkān), the highest and noblest is fire, which stands at the summit and peak, a level no other element or nature can reach. In the same way, the imam in his age and time is the highest religious pillar through whom souls are illumined. It is on this basis that Abraham commanded the glorification of fire, if what has been reported from him is accurate, and forbade extinguishing it or putting it out. The real intent, however, pointed toward glorifying the imamate, which no one must extinguish through the injustice of those who falsely claim its position. The fire worshippers fell into confusion, failing to grasp the symbolized reality behind the symbol. God, the Exalted, described the adversaries and the usurpers of the imamate in His words:

> يُرِيدُونَ أَن يُطْفِـُٔوا۟ نُورَ ٱللَّهِ بِأَفْوَهِهِمْ وَيَأْبَى ٱللَّهُ إِلَّآ أَن يُتِمَّ نُورَهُۥ وَلَوْ كَرِهَ ٱلْكَفِرُونَ

They would like to extinguish God's light with their mouths, but God will not allow that to happen until He perfects His light, even if the unbelievers may detest it (9:32).

I also maintain that among the many planets (kawākib) there are differences and gradations in degrees of excellence. Their peak and summit in nobility and merit is the sun, through which physical eyesight draws its light and through which all plants, animals, and newborns grow and reach their perfection. No plant or newborn grows without the influence of the sun. Likewise, the imam is the sun of the religion, through which insight is enlightened and souls are illumined by the light of guidance and wisdom, radiating through the hearts of God's friends. It is because of this that some people have been moved to glorify the sun. Yet sun-worshippers too fell into confusion, grasping only the symbol without recognizing the reality it signified. For this reason, the Prophet spoke of the rising of the sun from the west as a sign of the imam's own rising (ع) in the Maghrib.

Were the physical sun to deviate from its established orbit, from its ascent and descent from its centre, even for the blink of an eye, the entire world would be destroyed. That, as we know, is impossible both rationally and naturally. The Prophet's statement therefore holds true only on the basis of esoteric interpretation. Indeed, that interpretation has proven correct, as we have witnessed with our own eyes by the grace and favour of God.

I also say that among the twelve signs of the zodiac, four are the most glorious and excellent, and among those four one stands at the peak and summit in nobility. Likewise, among the twelve proofs (ḥujaj), the one who is connected to the imamate is the most excellent, the noblest, and the most exalted of them all.

### Section Five

I maintain that the fourth clime (iqlīm), which corresponds to the fourth heaven and aligns with the sun, is the most moderate as well as the most excellent and noble of all. In the same way, the imam is the most excellent and most glorious within the hierarchy, standing as their peak and apex.

I also say that among the melting mineral substances the most excellent is gold. It is the medium through which people conduct their transactions; it establishes the value of all valuables, and it is unaffected by fire or water and cannot be destroyed. Likewise, the imam (ع) is the most excellent religious and intellectual substance in his age and time. Through him the transactions of creation and the well-being of people, in both worldly and religious terms, are brought to completion. Each melting substance has its own peak of nobility.

Among precious stones, the finest is the sapphire, and among all sapphires, white, green, yellow, and red, it is the red ruby that excels above them all. No other jewel endures like the ruby: it preserves its essence unchanged by fire or water, retaining at all times its purity, radiance, and gravity. It draws properties from its source that no other substance can. Not even other sapphires, emeralds, the Badakhshi stone, carnelian, agate, diamonds, turquoise, or jade can retain those qualities. The crown among jewels is therefore the red ruby, just as the imam is the pinnacle of all creation in his age and time, unique among all the ranks of the hierarchy: the proofs (ḥujaj), the adjuncts (lawāḥiq), the summoner (dā'ī), the wing (janāḥ), the licensee (ma'dhūn), and the motivator (mukallib).

The imam is thus the apex whose status, rank, and position no one in his age or time is able to attain. From another angle, the seven jewels serve as proofs for the seven proclaimers and the seven imams. But the argument on which I am relying here, degrees of excellence, carries greater force. That is because the natural symbol is present in every age and time, and the symbolized reality must therefore also be present in every age and time. Since the stars, the natural elements, the realms of nature, and the substances are ever-present, it is equally necessary that the ranks of the hierarchy exist in every age and time, so that they may always exercise their influence upon souls.

I maintain that there are seven grains that provide human beings with food and nourishment, and their peak and summit is wheat, the most balanced in nature and nobility, and the grain that builds the body. In the same way, the imam is the peak and summit through whom souls benefit and are nourished. From him comes their livelihood and their life, and from him they receive knowledge.
