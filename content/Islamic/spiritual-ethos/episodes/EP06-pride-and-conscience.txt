# Pride And Conscience

## Opening directive
Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. Open with a warm one-sentence welcome: greet the listener, name the book — Reza Shah-Kazemi's *Spiritual Ethos* — and its subject: the Imam's warning to a governor that pride is war with God, traced inward to the conscience and outward into mercy. Then land the spine verbatim: "Pride is war with God — and mercy is what justice looks like when a ruler finally remembers Whose he is." Repeat the spine verbatim at the pivot and the close.
Forbidden first words: Exactly, Yeah, Right, Of course, Absolutely, Wow, "great point"; no faux-profound line.

## Name discipline
The letter's author → "the Imam"; first mention "the Imam, peace be upon him", then "the Imam".
The Prophet → first mention "the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family", then "the Prophet".
Others in English roles only → the governor he writes to / his blind brother / his cousin / a deputy governor / the over-strict ascetic / an early Sufi master / the Prophet's own grandsons / the blind Christian beggar / the Aga Khan / Satan / Adam.
Concepts in English: pride, hidden idolatry, pretension, the soul that commands evil, the Real, the public treasury. Never speak Arabic book titles or Arabic names. Never speak the forbidden pairing of the leadership-title and the personal name.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- Iblis: substitute *Satan*
- shirk: substitute *hidden idolatry*

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: how can an inner feeling of superiority be, without exaggeration, war against God? The Imam tells the governor "Do not set your soul up for war with God." Let the listener feel the stakes before any answer appears. Spine verbatim.
Beat 2 — Pride as theft, and its ruin: pride steals a greatness that is God's alone — "Pride is My cloak and greatness is My robe." Satan's refusal to bow is the archetype; pretension is hidden idolatry; punishment can wear the mask of worldly success. Pause to define pride as a lie about reality, not a bad mood.
Beat 3 — Pivot, the war turns inward: the same war is finally waged on the ruler's own conscience — the divine discernment breathed into the soul, the covenant it once made. So justice starts with the self: "If your aspiration is to reform the people, begin with yourself." Spine verbatim here.
Beat 4 — Close, the compassion of justice: self-lowering opens into mercy — "Infuse your heart with mercy for the subjects; they are of two types: either your brother in religion or your like in creation." Steelman the view that mercy softens justice, then show mercy IS justice faithful to the Real — iron toward a governor's misused coin, even toward the Prophet's own grandsons, open-handed to a blind Christian beggar. On the classical Ismaili reading, that undiscriminating mercy is the living thread. Spine a third time, then leave the listener a real question: which voice inside them they have learned not to hear, and what listening would cost this week. End on this chapter.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads and earns each claim from the source; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least 3 times and concedes once — one real concession.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — how can a private feeling of superiority be called war against God?"; "That sounds like wordplay — isn't calling mercy 'justice' just renaming softness?" At least two genuine challenges.

## Tone constraints
Reverent and sober through the diagnosis of pride, opening into humane through conscience and mercy; let the warnings and the mercy passages carry their own weight without moralizing over them. Exactly three governing analogies, source-images only.
Analogy 1 — the cloak and robe a king alone wears, here belonging to God alone (Beats 1-2).
Analogy 2 — Satan converting a mere difference, fire against clay, into a defiance (Beat 2).
Analogy 3 — the door that opens only from within, to "It is Thou" and never "It is I" (Beats 3-4).

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No faux-profound opener, no "and that, ultimately, is what it means" close, no AI filler. Do not reference any other episode or part of the book; end without pre-announcing what comes next. R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud.
