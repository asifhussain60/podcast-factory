The knowledge of God that was declared the foremost of religion at the very beginning is the exact same knowledge that the prophets labored to restore, and it is the exact same knowledge codified in the book. The end of the sermon loops flawlessly back to its first sentence. The negation and the creation are not two distinct subjects. They are one unified teaching. Before a ruler issues a decree, before justice can be enacted in the world, the soul must let go of the idols the mind naturally manufactures. A ruler who serves a bounded picture of God, a God he thinks he can comprehend and contain, will inevitably serve an idol of his own power, his own certainty, or his own tribe. The perfection of sincerity toward him is to divest him of all attributes. The journey ends not in grasping the divine, but in allowing oneself to be unmade by the boundless real. Which idol of the mind, which small bounded picture of the real, does your own worship still serve this week?
