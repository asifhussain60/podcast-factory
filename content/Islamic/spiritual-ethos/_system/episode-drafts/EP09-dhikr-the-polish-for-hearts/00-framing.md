# Dhikr The Polish For Hearts

## Opening directive
Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. Open with a warm one-sentence welcome: greet the listener, name the book — Reza Shah-Kazemi's *Spiritual Ethos* — and its subject, the Imam's teaching on remembrance of God. Then land the spine verbatim: "Remembrance is the polish for the hearts — the one act that clears them until they see God." Preview the teaching: why the simplest worship is ranked the greatest. Repeat the spine verbatim at the pivot and close.
Forbidden first words: Exactly, Yeah, Right, Of course, Absolutely, Wow, "great point"; no faux-profound line.

## Name discipline
The Imam → "the Imam"; first mention "the Imam, peace be upon him", then "the Imam".
The Prophet → "the Prophet"; first mention "the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family", then "the Prophet".
Witnesses in English roles only, never Arabic names. Rotation: the lexicographer / the author of the first Persian Sufi manual / the Persian poet. Also: the reviver-jurist, the modern gnostic.
Never speak Arabic book titles: "the collected sermons", "the collected aphorisms".
Concepts in English: remembrance, sanctity, gnosis, the retreat, the heedless. Never pair the leadership-title with the Imam's personal name.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- dhikr: dhikr

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: state the paradox with weight — the one act that costs nothing and can be done while walking, yet is ranked above fasting, almsgiving, and battle. Let the listener feel the strangeness before any answer. Restate the spine.
Beat 2 — The failed easy reading: that remembrance is a light or minor devotion; steelman it, then show why the Imam sets it at the head of the whole mystical tradition, uniting Sunni Sufism and Shi'i gnosis toward sanctity.
Beat 3 — What remembrance means: the lexicographer's definition — not storing a fact but rendering the known present to consciousness; end and means at once. Pause here.
Beat 4 — Pivot: the polish for the hearts — remembrance burnishes the clouded mirror until it hears after deafness and sees after blindness. Spine verbatim here.
Beat 5 — Non-eye vision: the eye cannot reach God; the polished heart sees Him through the verities of faith — a seeing that is finally God's own, effected through the heart. One reading among the tradition's.
Beat 6 — Stakes and close: hold immanence and transcendence together — the veils that do not veil. End on this chapter's teaching, no pre-announcing what follows; repeat the spine a third time, then leave the listener a question about which remembrance their week is missing.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads and earns each claim from the source; Host B (female, seeker) challenges before conceding — genuine friction, three pushbacks and one real concession.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — how can the easiest act outrank a lifetime of fasting?"; "That sounds like wordplay — isn't 'seeing God' just a metaphor for calm?"
Name positionality (the classical Ismaili reading); mark the thin record on the Imam's initiatic role as contested.

## Tone constraints
Reverent, unhurried, contemplative — never breezy. Exactly three governing analogies, all source-images; no others.
Analogy 1 — the polish and the clouded mirror: remembrance burnishes the heart until it reflects (Beats 4-5).
Analogy 2 — light upon light, a lamp in a niche: the heart's latent light meeting the light from on high (Beat 4).
Analogy 3 — the living and the dead: the one who remembers is alive, the heedless a corpse that walks (Beat 1).

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No faux-profound opener, no "and that, ultimately, is what it means" close, no AI filler. Do not reference any other episode or part of the book; end on this chapter's content without pre-announcing what comes next.
