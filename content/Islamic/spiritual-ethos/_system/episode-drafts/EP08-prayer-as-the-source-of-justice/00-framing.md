# Prayer As The Source Of Justice

## Opening directive
Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. Open with a warm one-sentence welcome: greet the listener, name the book — Reza Shah-Kazemi's *Spiritual Ethos* — and its subject: the letter's close, where the whole edifice of just rule turns out to rest on something done alone, in the dead of night. Then land the spine verbatim: "Know that your every act is dependent upon your prayer." Preview: how drawing near to God overflows into justice. Repeat the spine verbatim at the pivot and close.
Forbidden first words: Exactly, Yeah, Right, Of course, Absolutely, Wow, "great point"; no faux-profound line.

## Name discipline
The subject → "the Imam"; first mention "the Imam, peace be upon him", then "the Imam".
The Prophet → first mention "the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family", then "the Prophet".
Others in English roles only → the governor he counsels / the questioner who pressed the Imam / the early Sufi master / the Aga Khan / Plato / Socrates / Glaucon.
Never speak Arabic book titles: use "the Republic", "his Seventh Letter", "the essay". Concepts in English: drawing-near, remembrance, the Real, the Fashioner. Never speak the forbidden pairing of the leadership-title and the personal name.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- taqarrub: taqarrub
- dhikr: dhikr

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: the whole architecture of just rule — every fair judgment, every honest appointment — rests in the end on something a ruler does unwatched, in the dead of night: prayer. Let the listener feel the stakes before any answer. Spine verbatim.
Beat 2 — Drawing near: to purify one's religion is to purify the soul's own relationship to God; no virtue rises higher than the nearness that feeds it. Pause to define drawing-near.
Beat 3 — The hadith: at the Prophet's first mention, the saying where God becomes "his hearing by which he hears, his sight by which he sees" — to see truly and act on it is justice itself.
Beat 4 — Pivot, the polish: remembrance burnishes the heart like a dulled mirror until it hears after deafness and sees after blindness — recovering what was already inscribed, not learning something new. Spine verbatim here.
Beat 5 — Remembrance and justice: those who belong to the remembrance are steadfast in justice and can teach it; on the classical Ismaili reading, faith and world are one fabric. Justice is at once the cause and the consequence of worship.
Beat 6 — Gazing on the Good: steelman Plato — the ascent to the Sovereign Good stated at its highest — then Socrates confesses it is "beyond my powers", where the Imam answers, "hearts see Him through the verities of faith." End on this chapter, spine a third time, then leave the listener a real question about their own practice.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads and earns each claim from the source; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least 3 times and concedes once — one real concession.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — how can private prayer be the source of public justice?"; "That sounds like wordplay — isn't 'God becomes his hand' just a fancy way of saying he acts well?"

## Tone constraints
Reverent and culminating; let the Plato comparison illuminate, never compete. Exactly three governing analogies, source-images only.
Analogy 1 — prayer as a fountain overflowing into every action (Beats 1-3).
Analogy 2 — the heart as a mirror or blade polished by remembrance until it sees (Beats 4-5).
Analogy 3 — prayer without effort is arrows loosed without a bow (the effort theme).

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No faux-profound opener, no "and that, ultimately, is what it means" close, no AI filler. Do not reference any other episode or part of the book; end without pre-announcing what comes next. R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud.
