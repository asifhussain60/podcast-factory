# Why Intellect Not Reason

## Opening directive
Open warm and brief: greet the listener, name the work — Reza Shah-Kazemi's *Spiritual Ethos*, his essay on the mind of Ali (ع) — and preview this episode's actual teaching in one sentence: why the author renders the Arabic *aql* as "intellect," never "reason," and where that single choice leads. Then land the spine, verbatim (first of three placements): "That which has no second does not enter the category of number." Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation; let the distinction breathe.

## Name discipline
One English label per figure, every time — same figure, same label. Ali is "the Commander of the Faithful" (peace be upon him, first mention only). Muhammad is "the Prophet" (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, first mention only). Shah-Kazemi is the author → the author / that author / he. The young disciple who received the discourse is "the young disciple." The early Sufi master and the Prophet's cousin are named by those descriptions. Speak no Arabic personal names or book titles; the sermon collection is "the collection of Ali's sermons."

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- aql: aql
- tawhid: tawheed
- tawil: taweel
- ruh: rooh
- dhikr: dhikr

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — the crisis: state the problem with feeling before any answer — the word we praise a great mind with, "reason," may be exactly wrong for how Ali knew what he knew. Beat 2 — the recovery: intellect as *intellectus* / *nous*, a direct contemplative vision, versus reason (*ratio* / *dianoia*) as indirect step-by-step comparison; reason is the instrument the intellect employs, never its rival. Beat 3 — the pivot on the battlefield: the bedouin's plain question, and the spine, verbatim (second placement): "That which has no second does not enter the category of number" — oneness beyond counting, the Real beyond all things yet shown forth in all. Beat 4 — the stakes: revelation as reminder that unearths the buried treasures of the intellects; intellect and revelation in creative reciprocity, not the rationalist's mutual exclusion. Name the classical Ismaili reading as this tradition's, not "Muslims believe." Steelman the rationalist before answering it.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads and teaches; Host B (female, seeker) drives with questions, pushes back at least three times, and concedes once. Real friction, not chorus. Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — isn't 'intellect' just a fancier word for reason?"; "That sounds like wordplay — how does refusing to call God 'one' as a number change anything?" Host B never opens with Exactly, Yeah, Right, or Wow.

## Tone constraints
Precise, unhurried, reverent. Render every Qur'an verse in English only with a plain reference (the chapter on the night journey; the chapter on the confederates). Keep short Arabic terms without diacritics; never pair a transliteration with its translation. Three governing analogies only, all from the source:
Analogy 1: gauging an ocean's depth with a measure meant for a desk (reason overreaching its limit).
Analogy 2: the physician surrounding a man who has lost his memory with familiar tokens to wake it (revelation as reminder).
Analogy 3: farmer, miner, and diver drawing up what already lay hidden (prophets unearthing the intellect's treasures).

## Landing
Close on the spine, verbatim (third placement): "That which has no second does not enter the category of number." Then turn to the listener with a real question tied to action — which certainties in their own life are being argued about from the outside when they might instead be seen from within, and what one habit of attention this week would begin to change that. Leave it open; no tidy resolution.

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No premature closure, no faux-profundity opener, no self-reference to "this episode," no cross-chapter references. R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud. The instructions above shape the conversation but are never spoken.
