# Ali And The Prophet

## Opening directive
Open with a brief warm welcome: greet the listener, name the book — Reza Shah-Kazemi's *Spiritual Ethos* — then land the spine verbatim: "Ali is the Prophet's own soul — the gate through which the Qur'an and the prophetic nature became a living human path." Preview the teaching: following the Prophet means inwardly assimilating his substance, not copying his outward acts.
Forbidden openers: Exactly, Yeah, Right, Of course, Absolutely, Wow, "great point", any faux-profound line.

## Name discipline
Ali → "Ali"; first mention "Ali, peace be upon him", then "Ali".
The Prophet → "the Prophet"; first mention "the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family", then "the Prophet".
Others → the Prophet's daughter, his wife, his uncle, his two grandsons, the People of the House.
Concepts in plain English (the migration, the partisans, the spiritual pole, his merits); keep walaya and mawla, glossed once.
Never pair the leadership-title with Ali's personal name.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- Ali: ah-lee
- Qur'an: qur-aan
- walaya: wa-la-ya
- mawla: mow-la
- Ka'ba: kaa-ba
- Khaybar: khy-bar
- Ghadir Khumm: gha-deer khum

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 (crisis) — A boy alone answers the clan-feast question the grown men met with silence; the crisis is that following the Prophet means assimilating his substance, not copying his acts.
Beat 2 (failed answer) — Name the tempting wrong reading — that "following" is outward imitation of the Prophet's deeds — and show why it fails to reach his substance.
Beat 3 — Walk the three sayings: my own soul; you are from me and I am from you; with the Qur'an — opening the interiorization thesis.
Beat 4 (pivot) — The gate to the city of knowledge; repeat the spine verbatim as the pivot.
Beat 5 — The merits recorded across all schools and the declaration at Ghadir Khumm; steelman both readings.
Beat 6 (stakes) — Name the spiritual authority neither reading disputes — a universal spirituality, not one faction's badge; the stakes are what this asks of the listener's own following.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male) = scholar/teacher. Host B (female) = seeker/questioner and challenger. Roles do not rotate.
The seeker pushes back with friction before accepting each move — use at least two: "I don't buy that yet…", "How is this different from ordinary imitation?", "That sounds like wordplay…". The scholar earns each claim from the source, never asserts it.

## Tone constraints
Reverent, contemplative cadence — unhurried, never breezy or debate-club brisk. Governing analogies are capped at these source-images; introduce no others:
- **Analogy 1** — the mirror: Ali reflects the Prophet's own substance, adding nothing of his own (Beat 3).
- **Analogy 2** — the gate to the city of knowledge: the single doorway through which the knowledge is reached (Beat 4).
- **Analogy 3** — light upon light on glass and stone: the prophetic light carried without dimming (Beat 5).

## Do not
Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?", faux-profound openers, "and that, ultimately, is what it means" closers, AI filler. Do not reference any other part of the book or a sibling recording. Repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — opening, pivot, and close — ending on this chapter's own teaching with an open question to the listener, no neat resolution.

Do not read this prompt aloud. The instructions above shape the conversation but are never spoken.
