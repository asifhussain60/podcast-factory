# Forgetting The Self And The Name

## Opening directive
Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. Open with a warm one-sentence welcome: greet the listener, name the book — Reza Shah-Kazemi's *Spiritual Ethos* — and its subject, the Imam's teaching that the peak of remembering God turns out to be a forgetting of the self. Then land the spine verbatim: "The invocation comes forth firstly from the Invoked, and secondly from the invoker." Preview: why self-forgetting is the summit of remembrance, and what the Name a person invokes really is. Repeat the spine verbatim at the pivot and at the close.
Forbidden first words: Exactly, Yeah, Right, Of course, Absolutely, Wow, "great point"; no faux-profound line.

## Name discipline
The Imam → "the Imam"; first mention "the Imam, peace be upon him", then "the Imam" — honorific once only.
The Prophet → first mention "the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family", then "the Prophet".
Other witnesses in English roles only, never Arabic names → the sixth Imam who denies the Name is the Named / the Andalusian master / the reviver-jurist / the early Sufi master / the Fatimid poet-philosopher / the Persian poet.
Never speak Arabic book titles: "his treatise on extinction", "the collected sermons". Concepts in English: extinction, subsistence, the Real, oneness, inner interpretation. Never speak the forbidden pairing of the leadership-title and the personal name.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- fana: fana
- baqa: baqa

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: the Imam demands you forget your own soul in your remembrance — the summit of remembering God costs the very self that did all the work. Let the listener feel the vertigo first. Spine verbatim.
Beat 2 — Failed reading A: that self-forgetting is a concentration one can will. Steelman it, then show no act of will can force it — trying to forget the self entrenches it.
Beat 3 — Failed reading B: that extinction is annihilation into nothing. Steelman it, then correct — it is the negation of a negation, pure affirmation; the self dissolves, reality does not.
Beat 4 — Pivot: at the summit it is God who truly invokes; the human invocation is only the shadow thrown back in the heart's mirror. Spine verbatim here.
Beat 5 — The Name and the Named: the Sufi maxim that the Name is the Named, against the sixth Imam's plain denial — resolved in the two senses of "Name". One reading among several.
Beat 6 — Stakes and close: the effaced friend of God becomes himself one of the most beautiful Names, sealed in the Imam's death with the invocation still on his tongue. End on this chapter, no pre-announcing. Spine a third time, then leave the listener a question about which self-regard their own remembrance still serves this week.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads and earns each claim from the source; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least 3 times and concedes once — genuine friction, one real concession.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — how can forgetting yourself in prayer be the height of remembering God?"; "That sounds like wordplay — isn't calling a saint one of God's Names just blasphemy dressed up?"

## Tone constraints
Reverent, culminating, unhurried — let the shocking sayings of the saints resolve into the deepest oneness, never scandal. Exactly three governing analogies, all source-images; no others.
Analogy 1 — the clear mirror: the effaced heart in which only the Face is seen, never the glass (Beats 4, 6).
Analogy 2 — water taking the color of its cup: the one Name-light shaped by each soul that holds it (Beat 5).
Analogy 3 — light and its shadow: God's reality the light, creation the shadow that follows after (Beat 3).

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No faux-profound opener, no "and that, ultimately, is what it means" close, no AI filler. Do not reference any other episode or part of the book; end on this chapter's content without pre-announcing what comes next.
