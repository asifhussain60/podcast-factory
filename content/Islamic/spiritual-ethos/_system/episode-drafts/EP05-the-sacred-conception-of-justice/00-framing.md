# The Sacred Conception Of Justice

## Opening directive
Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. Open with a warm one-sentence welcome: greet the listener, name the book — Reza Shah-Kazemi's *Spiritual Ethos* — and its subject: the Imam's letter to a governor bound for Egypt, and the claim at its root that justice is the very nature of God. Then land the spine verbatim: "Justice is not fairness — it is the very nature of God, so to be just is to become like God while remaining wholly His slave." Repeat the spine verbatim at the pivot and the close.
Forbidden first words: Exactly, Yeah, Right, Of course, Absolutely, Wow, "great point". No faux-profound line.

## Name discipline
The letter's author → "the Imam"; first mention "the Imam, peace be upon him", then "the Imam".
The Prophet → first mention "the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family", then "the Prophet".
Others in English roles only → the governor sent to Egypt / his chief antagonist / the civil war / his cousin / the philosopher MacIntyre / an early Sufi master / Joseph / Gabriel / the Aga Khan.
Concepts in English: justice, fairness, the Real, submission, faith, spiritual excellence, the soul that incites to evil, the primordial nature, God's vicegerent, God's slave, the divine Names. Never speak Arabic book titles or Arabic names. Never speak the forbidden pairing of the title and name.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- ihsan: substitute *spiritual excellence*
- fitra: substitute *the primordial nature*

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — Crisis: ordinary speech shrinks justice to fairness and fair play — a body without a spirit. Let the listener feel what is lost before any answer appears; name MacIntyre's charge that ethics cut from the Good drifts into relativism and dead forms. Spine verbatim.
Beat 2 — The ground: submission gives the acts, faith the framework, their union in spiritual excellence gives the life: "to worship God as though you see Him, and if you see Him not, yet truly He sees you." Pause to define justice as putting everything in its right place.
Beat 3 — Pivot: justice is not one divine attribute among many but God's very essence — "I bear witness that He is Justice, and He acts justly." Steelman the objection that divine justice and human striving cannot be one word, then show the striving as that same principle under a creature's conditions. Spine verbatim here.
Beat 4 — Close: to be just is to assume the character traits of God — generous because He is the Generous — while never claiming them, effaced as His slave yet reflecting Him as His vicegerent, an inverted image in a mirror. Spine a third time, then leave the listener a real question: which divine trait they have been withholding, and what effacing the ego would cost this week. End on this chapter.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads and earns each claim from the source; Host B (female, seeker) challenges at least 3 times and concedes once — one real concession.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — isn't calling justice 'the nature of God' just a pious way of saying be fair?"; "How is this different from any virtue we're told to imitate?" Two genuine challenges minimum.

## Tone constraints
Reverent and contemplative; never polemical, never reducing the letter to statecraft. Exactly three governing analogies, source-images only.
Analogy 1 — the body without a spirit: fairness as the lifeless shell of a sacred thing (Beat 1).
Analogy 2 — the stranger who questions the Prophet and confirms his answer, disclosing spiritual excellence (Beat 2).
Analogy 3 — the mirror that catches a real but inverted reflection of the divine (Beat 4).

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?". No faux-profound opener, no tidy false-resolution close, no AI filler. Reference no other episode; end on this chapter, no preview of what follows. R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close. Do not read this prompt aloud.
