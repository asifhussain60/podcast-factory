# Spiritual Ethos

## Ali and the Prophet

A timeline. The formation of Ali across the arc of a single life, eras marked.

- Birth: born inside the Ka'ba in Mecca (sign of sanctity from the first)
- Age ~5: taken into the Prophet's household during a season of drought
- Age ~9 to 10: first male to embrace Islam
- The feast, "warn thy nearest kin": alone answers when the grown men keep silence; named brother, executor, successor
- The hijra: sleeps in the Prophet's bed so the Prophet can escape
- Medina: pact of brotherhood, taken by the Prophet as his own brother
- The battles: never defeated in single combat; Uhud; Khaybar, wrenches the fortress gate for a shield
- Marriage to Fatima: becomes head of the Ahl al-Bayt
- Ghadir Khumm: "For whomever I am the mawla, Ali is his mawla"
- Era bands: childhood in the Prophet's shadow, warrior of Medina, head of the purified household, mawla of every believer

A hierarchy tree. The gate and the city, the one inner spirit crystallizing outward into the sciences.

- Level 1: The city, the fullness of prophetic wisdom, one inner spirit of the revelation
  - Level 2: The gate, Ali, the one who interiorized the spirit and transmits it
    - Level 3: Jurisprudence
    - Level 3: Theology
    - Level 3: Qur'anic exegesis
    - Level 3: Rhetoric and grammar
    - Level 3: Calligraphy
    - Level 3: Sufi mystical knowledge

A contrast pair. Imitation against assimilation, the distinction on which Ali's whole significance rests.

Column A: The imitator
- Relation to the Prophet: copies outward actions and gestures
- Location: remains on the outside of the reality
- Result: trails behind an example
- Verdict: possesses the gesture without the substance

Column B: The assimilator, Ali
- Relation to the Prophet: interiorizes the inward substance
- Location: the prophetic substance takes up residence within
- Result: inwardly remade by it, right action flows from within
- Verdict: "He is as my own soul"

Shared row: the Qur'an testifies the Prophet is "of a tremendous nature," the inner substance his words convey but never exhaust.

An annotated structure. Walaya, one reality with two joined faces.

Whole: walaya, the standing of Ali as wali of God
- Face one, loving fidelity: the loyalty, devotion, and love a believer directs toward him
- Face two, spiritual authority: his sanctity, his standing as a source of guidance and grace
Reading: to love him truly is to recognize his sanctity, and to recognize his sanctity is to be drawn to love him. Belongs to no one community; recognized across the whole of Islam, deepest wherever Sufism flourishes, where he is the first qutb after the Prophet.

## Why Intellect, Not Reason

A contrast pair. The two faculties the author refuses to confuse, attribute by attribute.

Column A: Intellect ('aql, intellectus, nous)
- Mode: direct, unmediated
- Act: contemplative vision, a seeing
- Object: transcendent realities, the Absolute
- Reach: can contemplate the Real
- Register: the principle by which consciousness is articulated

Column B: Reason (ratio, dianoia)
- Mode: indirect, discursive
- Act: step by step comparison and inference
- Object: a mental concept, a definition standing in for the thing
- Reach: can only think about the Real
- Register: one mode, one instrument the intellect employs

Shared row: reason is the tool in the intellect's hand, the path by which one climbs, never its rival. It carries the mind to the door but cannot take it through.

An annotated structure. The three load-bearing factors of the whole intellectual perspective.

Whole: the ground on which everything that follows rests
- Factor one, centrality of revelation: the intellect needs a fixed center to revolve around, and that center is the revealed word
- Factor two, harmony of revelation and intellect: not two competing sources of truth but partners in creative reciprocity, each completing the other
- Factor three, intellect as principle over reason as mode: the intellect is the wellspring, reason one of the streams

A comparison matrix. Three images of revelation as retrieval, not manufacture.

Rows: Farmer, Miner, Diver
Columns: What is already there | The work | What it shows about revelation
- Farmer: the seed's life beneath the soil | waters what lies concealed | coaxes into growth what was already there
- Miner: gold and silver locked in the mountain | digs into the rock | brings up the ore, does not create it
- Diver: treasures resting on the sea bed | descends for them | retrieves what already lay hidden

Reading: revelation is dhikr, a reminder. It does not plant new knowledge; it unearths the buried treasures of the intellects, the dafa'in al-'uqul.

## Paradox and the Inner Struggle

A comparison matrix. The three types of people, and no fourth, sorted by their relation to knowledge.

Rows: The lordly knower, The seeker, The common crowd
Columns: Relation to knowledge | Direction | Condition
- The lordly knower: knowing rooted and anchored in his Lord | fixed | established in the truth
- The seeker: pursues knowledge for his own deliverance | ascending, step by step | on the path to salvation
- The common crowd (the moth to any flame): follows anyone who calls | swaying with every current | drifts, illumined by no light

> Truly these hearts are vessels, and the best of them are those which hold the most. So guard well and retain from me that which I say to you. People are divided into three types, and no fourth. Ali, to Kumayl ibn Ziyad.

An annotated structure. The greatest struggle, a war fought for the soul, not against it.

Whole: three figures on a field, the war within
- The intellect (al-'aql): leads the forces of the Compassionate (al-Rahman)
- Caprice (al-hawa): commands the forces of the devil (al-shaytan)
- The soul (al-nafs): stands between, the prize, drawn now to one side and now the other
Reading: the aim is not to destroy the lower soul but to win it over, converting its energy toward the one true object. Its whole outcome is staked in scripture.

> He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who corrupts it. Qur'an, chapter 91, verses 9 to 10.

A process flow. The making of the human being, and where knowing enters.

Start: God gathers earth, hard and soft, sweet and salt
- Works water in until it is pure
- Kneads it until it holds
- Shapes a form with joints and limbs
- Hardens it until it rings like sounding clay
- A full body stands finished: eyes, ears, the organ of thought all in place
- And still it can neither see nor hear nor think
Turning point: God breathes into it of His spirit
End: it stands up a human being, the eyes begin to see, the ears to hear, the mind to reflect. Life and knowing arrive in a single breath.

## Joy, Virtue, and the Hereafter

An annotated structure. The four fruits of the struggle, what the discipline was for.

Whole: the reward of the true intellectual
- Joy: knowledge as the most joyful act, a homecoming from the exile of ignorance
- Beauty: virtue as the inner beauty of a soul
- Detachment: freedom from worldliness, not deprivation
- Foretaste of the Hereafter: eternity already breaking into the present
Reading: not four separate lessons but one fruit of one tree; their common root is a certainty so complete the Hereafter is already, in every good act, begun.

A process flow. Virtue as making beautiful, and how it draws down divine love.

Start: two sayings of the Prophet
- "Truly God is beautiful and He loves beauty"
- "Assume the character traits of God"
- Then: ihsan means "making beautiful," husn means beauty; virtue is what makes the soul beautiful
- Then: to cultivate beauty of character is to make oneself lovable to the One who loves beauty
End: the human love reaching up is met by the divine love reaching down.

> Say: O ye who believe, if ye love God, follow me; God will love you. Qur'an, chapter 3, verse 31.

A contrast pair. Death seen by the ignorant and by the wise, the same event, opposite meanings.

Column A: The ignorant
- What death is: a frightful coming apart
- What is lost: "their" life, the walled-in self
- Response: recoil in horror
- Horizon: the end of their world

Column B: The wise
- What death is: release, encounter, integration, a deeper degree of union
- What is surrendered: the small self, already died to in fana
- Response: intimacy, welcomed as a bridegroom meets the bride
- Horizon: the beginning of true life

Shared row: "The life of this world is nothing but sport and play, and verily the abode of the Hereafter, that is true life" (chapter 29, verse 64).

## The Sacred Conception of Justice

A comparison matrix. MacIntyre's four default positions, what steps in wherever the Good is abandoned.

Rows: Relativism, Nihilism, Utilitarianism, Opportunism
Columns: Core claim | What it grounds morality in
- Relativism: truth and morality are relative to culture and moment | the local, never a standard above it
- Nihilism: rejects all moral principle, life is meaningless | nothing, there is nothing left to be about
- Utilitarianism: an act is right if it benefits a majority | a calculation of greater benefit or loss
- Opportunism: seize whatever benefit circumstances offer | the manipulator's advantage

Reading: none rooted in the divine; even where they mirror faith's values they are, in the essay's image, dead corpses without spirit.

A comparison matrix. The hadith of Gabriel, three questions building the ground of ethics.

Rows: Islam (submission), Iman (faith), Ihsan (virtue)
Columns: What it supplies | The Prophet's answer
- Islam: the outer acts of morality | testimony, prayer, alms, fasting, pilgrimage
- Iman: the framework of ethics | belief in God, angels, books, messengers, the Last Day, the decree
- Ihsan: the living life, the union of the two | "to worship God as though you see Him, and if you see Him not, yet truly He sees you"

Reading: worship without goodness is empty; goodness without the vision of God is baseless. Ihsan is the living link.

A hierarchy tree. Justice as the essence, not one attribute among others.

- Level 1: God is Justice ("I bear witness that He is Justice, and He acts justly")
  - Level 2: His mercy, an expression of His justice
  - Level 2: His anger, an expression of His justice
  - Level 2: His love, an expression of His justice
  - Level 2: His creating and His destroying, expressions of His justice
Reading: every other name is a facet of this one reality, so to conform to God at all is to conform to justice.

A contrast pair. The double truth of the human being, held together.

Column A: Khalifat Allah, God's vicegerent
- Task: actively embody the divine qualities
- Guards against: passivity, neglect of duty in the name of humility
- Manner: reflect Him, as a mirror holds a real but inverted image

Column B: Abd Allah, God's slave
- Task: efface the self before the Real
- Guards against: competing with God in grandeur, the ruler's cardinal sin
- Manner: remain destitute before the only source of what is reflected

Shared row: "O mankind, you are the destitute before God, and God is the Self-sufficient" (chapter 35, verse 15). Drop either half and the whole collapses.

## Pride and Conscience

A contrast pair. The same disposition, perfection in God and corruption in man.

Column A: Takabbur in God (al-Mutakabbir)
- Standing: pure praise, a name of God
- Ground: greatness that is underived and absolute
- Truth: His greatness is simply the truth of what He is

Column B: Takabbur in the creature
- Standing: the worst of vices
- Ground: a greatness he does not and cannot possess, everything received
- Truth: a lie about reality, mistaking a loan for a possession

Shared row: the divine word, "Pride is My cloak and greatness is My robe; whoever contends with Me over either, I shall cast him into the Fire." To take up pride is to reach for God's own regalia.

A process flow. The fall of Iblis, the anatomy of every pride.

Start: God fashions Adam from clay and breathes into him of His spirit
- Command: "fall down before him prostrate"
- The angels prostrate, all of them together
- Iblis reasons: "I am better than he; You created me from fire and created him from clay"
- He converts a difference (fire against clay) into a claim of superiority
- The claim becomes defiance of God's command
End: disgrace. "Do you not see how God humiliated him through his pride?" The self-exaltation is itself the fall.

A process flow. The soul's inscription, why conscience comes from above the individual.

Start: the primordial covenant. "Am I not your Lord?" "Yes, verily, we testify" (chapter 7, verse 172)
- The soul is perfected and inspired with knowledge of its wickedness and righteousness (chapter 91, verses 7 to 8)
- Divine justice above becomes conscience below, breathed in, not the soul's own achievement
- To override conscience is to war against the trace of God in the soul
End: the tyrant's first act of violence is against his own conscience; the war with God has an inward face.

A contrast pair. Two austerities that look identical and mean opposite things.

Column A: Asim the ascetic
- Denies himself: the permitted good things
- Direction: turned inward toward the self
- Verdict: a private indulgence of a different kind

Column B: The leader of truth (imam al-haqq)
- Denies himself: comfort, to match the weakest of the people
- Direction: turned outward toward the people, so the poor feel no sting
- Verdict: a duty of those who lead in the name of the Real

Shared row: "O mankind, ye are the poor before God" (chapter 35, verse 15). Same rough clothing, opposite spirit.

## The Classes of Society and the Poor

A visual metaphor. The wheel of society and its hidden axle.

Abstract relation: how the classes of a state depend on one another and on God
Metaphor: a wheel
- The hub: the governor, at the center of every human relationship, on the horizontal plane
- The spokes: the army, scribes, officers, judges, tax collectors, merchants, artisans, the needy
- The rim: the ordered society they hold together
- The axle: the vertical connection to the Real, invisible
Reading: the hub is the most important part and the most helpless; without the axle it turns nothing. All the governor's efforts are dead weight without his connection to the Absolute.

A comparison matrix. The classes and the due assigned to each.

Rows: Army, Judges, Officers, Tillers, Scribes, Merchants
Columns: What they are | What is owed to them
- Army: the fortresses of the subjects | a commander marked by forbearance, gentle to the weak, firm with the strong
- Judges: those who settle disputes | independence, generous pay, safety from flattery and pressure
- Officers: handlers of funds | testing before appointment, then close and constant watching
- Tillers: the workers of the land | concern for cultivation set above the collection of tax
- Scribes: keepers of records | appointment by proven record, not by a polished face
- Merchants: the veins of a society's goods | free trade, watched, with hoarding kept in check

A comparison matrix. Three postures of worship, sorted by motive.

Rows: The merchant, The slave, The free
Columns: Motive | What it serves for
- The merchant: desire | a reward not yet in hand
- The slave: fear | to avoid a punishment
- The free: gratitude | thanks for what is already given

> There is a group who worship God out of desire, and this is the worship of the merchants; and a group who worship out of fear, and this is the worship of the slaves; and a group who worship out of gratitude, and this is the worship of the free. Ali.

Reading: the free man is beyond the reach of both discouragement and bribery, for his service never rested on a gain that could be lost.

A contrast pair. The two competences a just ruler needs.

Column A: The scholars (al-ulama)
- Know: the formal rules, the letter of the law
- Source: the Book and the Sunna as fixed
- Danger alone: correct rules misapplied

Column B: The sages (al-hukama)
- Know: how to lay the rule against a living situation
- Source: the spirit of wisdom, knowledge put into practice
- Danger alone: none named; this is the harder to acquire

Shared row: a just society needs both the letter and the spirit; study long with the first, converse long with the second.

## Prayer as the Source of Justice

A process flow. The hadith of drawing near, from obligatory act to divine seeing.

Start: the slave draws near to God through what He has made obligatory, which God loves most
- Then: he never ceases to draw near through supererogatory acts
- Then: until God loves him
End state: "I am his hearing by which he hears, his sight by which he sees, his hand by which he grasps, his foot by which he walks." To see and act through God is to see things as they are and place each rightly, which is justice.

> Prostrate, and draw near. Qur'an, chapter 96, verse 19.

A contrast pair. What Plato reached and what he left unspoken, set beside the Imam.

Column A: Plato in the Republic
- Ascends: through mathematics, geometry, astronomy, harmonics, dialectic to the Good
- At the summit: "lift up the eye of the soul to gaze on that which sheds light on all things"
- The question, "what is the Good?": "I am afraid it is beyond my powers"
- Names the summit but never says how it is climbed

Column B: The Imam, answering Dhi'lib
- Ascends: through prayer and the remembrance at its heart
- At the summit: a real seeing, "eyes see Him not, but hearts see Him through the verities of faith"
- The question, "how did you see Him?": answered directly, without hesitation
- Supplies the method Plato left dark

Shared row: both point to a highest reality on which all value depends; Socrates knew there was a summit, the Imam had climbed it.

A process flow, a closed circle. Justice as at once the cause and the consequence of worship.

- Worship gives God His due, and justice requires that everyone be given his due, God above all
- So justice demands worship (its cause)
- And from nearness in worship, justice overflows into action (its consequence)
- Which returns to demand still deeper worship
Reading: the circle turns without end; the one steadfast in remembrance is the one steadfast in justice, and able to teach it.

## Dhikr: The Polish for Hearts

A genealogy chain. Ali at the head of the mystical tradition.

Prophet Muhammad
  → Ali ibn Abi Talib, the friend of God, first qutb after the Prophet
    → the chains of affiliation of the Sufi orders (Arab, Persian, Turk, Malay; Sunni and Shi'i)
    → Shi'i gnosis, the esoteric core poured into the structure of Shi'ism

Reading: the two streams, Sunni Sufism and Shi'i gnosis, converge in one figure. For Sayyid Haydar Amuli the outward differences dissolve in the fire of walaya, the sanctity that is the goal of both.

A hierarchy tree. Al-Raghib's anatomy of dhikr.

- Level 1: Dhikr, that by which knowledge already attained is rendered present
  - Level 2: Dhikr of the heart
    - Level 3: following forgetfulness
    - Level 3: following no forgetfulness, unbroken presence
  - Level 2: Dhikr of the tongue
    - Level 3: following forgetfulness
    - Level 3: following no forgetfulness, unbroken presence
Reading: to remember is not to store a fact but to make a reality present. When the reality is God, remembrance becomes the quintessence of religion, the one work commanded without a ceiling.

A process flow. The polish, and what the cleared heart recovers.

Start: the heart, a mirror or blade dulled and clouded by neglect
- The friction of remembrance burnishes it
- It hears after suffering from deafness
- It sees after being blind
- Light within meets light from on high: "light upon light"
End: two works at once. The cleared mirror reflects truths from above, and discloses its own buried treasures within.

> For everything there is a polish, and the polish of hearts is the remembrance of God. The Prophet.

A contrast pair. Two orders of seeing.

Column A: The physical eye (basara)
- Organ: the eye, bound to surfaces
- Reach: the sensible world only
- Verdict on God: "eyes see Him not through sight's observation"

Column B: The heart (basira)
- Organ: the innermost faculty of consciousness, lit by faith
- Reach: the reality concealed beneath the surface
- Verdict on God: "hearts see Him through the verities of faith"

Shared row: "It is not the eyes that are blind, but the hearts within the breasts" (chapter 22, verse 46). At its depth the seeing is less man's sight of God than God's own sight, through the heart He has purified.

## The Veils That Do Not Veil

An annotated structure. The veil that reveals, read as a word reveals its speaker.

Whole: creation as the veil between God and His creatures
- The veil is: the very createdness of the world, its being-a-distinct-thing
- It conceals: as a word is not the mind that speaks it, set apart from the speaker
- It reveals: as a word carries the speaker's meaning and would not exist unless he spoke
- The name it wears: al-Zahir, the Apparent, the one who shows Himself, projected from al-Batin, the Hidden
Reading: "the veils do not veil Him." The world hides God only in the way a word both is and is not the one who utters it.

> He is the First and the Last, the Outward and the Inward. Qur'an, chapter 57, verse 3.

A quadrant map. The balance, and the two errors that break it.

Space: how to hold God's presence in the world together with His transcendence
- The right pole, tanzih (incomparability): God is other than all things, "nothing like unto Him"
- The left pole, tashbih (similarity, immanence): God is with all things, "closer than the jugular vein"
- The error of collapse: press immanence alone, the world is divinized, the idolater's mistake
- The error of exile: press transcendence alone, God is sealed off, the world goes mute
- The center: both poles affirmed at full strength, each true only in the light of the other

> He is with everything, but not through association, and other than everything, but not through separation. The Imam.

A process flow, a turning circle. Reflection, remembrance, and love drive one wheel.

- Ponder the signs of God, taste their beauty, and love for Him deepens
- Love brings His Name to the lips, so one invokes
- Invocation polishes the heart, which then reads the signs more keenly
- Which returns to deeper reflection
Reading: reflection intensifies remembrance, remembrance deepens reflection, and love drives the wheel; the practice can be carried out standing, sitting, reclining, at every hour.

A contrast pair. Formal prayer and the remembrance that is greater.

Column A: Formal prayer (salat)
- Described as: a preventative, it restrains from indecency and wrongdoing
- Character: bounded, fixed words, postures, times
- Role: the fence, the necessary discipline

Column B: Remembrance (dhikr)
- Described as: the positive, enlivening substance of prayer
- Character: unbounded, at all times, in all postures
- Role: the living presence the fence was built to protect

Shared row: "the remembrance of God is greater" (chapter 29, verse 45). The greater does not cancel the lesser; it comprises and completes it.

## Forgetting the Self and the Name

A process flow, a descent inward. The graded degrees of remembrance.

Start: the remembrance of the tongue
  ↓ the remembrance of the heart
  ↓ the remembrance of the innermost secret
  ↓ the remembrance of the spirit
  ↓ the remembrance of the hidden
End: the remembrance of the Essence, essential vision, attained only through the removal of duality altogether.
Reading: a progressive descent from the lips toward the ground of the soul, until the rememberer, as a separate self, is no longer there.

A process flow. The drink of the friends, a chain from intoxication to oneness.

Start: God has a drink for His friends
- they drink it and are intoxicated
- intoxicated, they are enraptured
- enraptured, they are blessed
- blessed, they dissolve
- dissolved, they are free
- free, they devote themselves purely
- purified, they seek
- seeking, they find
- finding, they arrive
End: arriving, they are at one, and there is no difference between them and their Beloved. Not two things made one, but the disclosure that there was never a genuine second.

A contrast pair. The Name in its two senses, resolving the apparent conflict.

Column A: The Name as identical with the Essence
- Sense: the Name is the very Reality that is named
- Count: in this respect there is only One
- Maxim it grounds: "the Name is the Named"

Column B: The Name as a distinct meaning
- Sense: that by which one Name is told from another, the Merciful from the All-Conquering
- Count: in this respect there are ninety-nine
- Maxim it grounds: Imam Ja'far, "the Name of God is other than Him"

Shared row: the Names of God are not human inventions but self-disclosures, penetrated by the reality they denote, unlike the idols' names, "but names that you have named" (chapter 53, verse 23).

A visual metaphor. The effaced friend as the mirror.

Abstract relation: how a creature can become "one of the most beautiful Names" without deifying itself
Metaphor: a mirror holding a face
- The mirror: the friend of God, nothing on his own account
- The polishing: remembrance, rubbing away the rust of egoity
- The face reflected: the divine Names and qualities, belonging to God, not to him
- Forgetting the mirror: the effacement of the self; fixing on the image: the remembrance of God
Reading: the friend is the mirror in which the Face is seen, precisely because he has ceased to draw attention to the mirror. "Down from me torrents pour."

## The First Sermon of Nahj al-Balagha

An annotated structure. The overture of negation, three works of speech each defeated.

Whole: the sermon's opening, praise that confesses praise fails
- Laudation: "whose laudation those who speak cannot deliver," speech defeated in praising
- Counting: "whose graces those who count cannot number," counting defeated in reckoning
- Striving: "whose rightful due those who strive cannot render," striving defeated in requital
Reading: words, measure, and effort, the whole of what a creature can offer, all fall short at once. A precise map of the creature's poverty, drawn in the first breath.

A hierarchy tree, an ascending ladder. From the knowledge of God to the divesting of every attribute.

- Rung 1: the foremost of religion is the knowledge of Him
  - Rung 2: the perfection of knowing Him is to believe in Him
    - Rung 3: the perfection of belief is to affirm His oneness
      - Rung 4: the perfection of oneness is sincerity toward Him
        - Rung 5: the perfection of sincerity is to divest Him of all attributes
Reading: the climb ends not in a fuller description of God but in the stripping of description; every determination the mind adds is a boundary drawn around the boundless.

A process flow, two parallel chains. Negation working link by link.

Chain one: whoever ascribes an attribute
  → has joined another to Him
  → has made Him two
  → has divided Him into parts
  → is ignorant of Him

Chain two: whoever points to Him
  → has confined Him
  → has counted Him
  → has enclosed Him ("in what is He?")
  → has set Him apart ("upon what is He?")

Reading: yet the negation is the near side of an affirmation, the rhythm of the testimony itself, "there is no god" (nafy) then "except God" (ithbat); no mercy is denied, only the mercy shrunk to a bounded thing.

A process flow, the great descent and ascent. The single arc of the sermon.

Start: the Essence no word can reach, divested of every attribute
  ↓ God cleaves the atmospheres, sends water surging on a violent wind
  ↓ a barren wind churns the sea until it throws up a froth
  ↓ from the froth He raises the seven heavens, hangs the sun, moon, and stars
  ↓ He fills the heavens with ranks of angels who never picture their Lord
  ↓ He gathers clay, shapes it, and breathes into it of His spirit: the human being
  ↓ the command to prostrate before Adam; all bow save Iblis, seized by pride
  ↓ the long succession of prophets, each naming the one to come
End: Muhammad, and the Book left in human hands, "its lawful and its forbidden, its clear and its obscure."
Reading: negation and creation are one teaching; only a God bound by nothing could give being as sheer gift, ordered toward the creature's return to the knowledge of the One.

## The Letter of Ali to Malik al-Ashtar

An annotated structure. The four charges of the mandate, and the reversal folded into their order.

Whole: what Malik is sent to Egypt to do
- Charge one: gather Egypt's revenues
- Charge two: fight its enemies
- Charge three: seek the welfare of its people
- Charge four: bring prosperity to its lands
Reading: the taxes and the war, which any ruler would list first, are bracketed and subordinated to the welfare and the flourishing. Read in the Imam's order, the letter describes a gardener who happens to command an army.

A hierarchy tree. The vertical ladder of authority, the reckoning that never stops climbing.

- The subjects, either "your brother in religion or your equal in creation," no third kind
  - The governor, above them
    - The one in authority over the governor, above him
      - God, above the one who appointed him
Reading: every rung is answerable to a higher rung; to forget the top is to imagine standing on the ground while suspended in mid air.

> Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for his flock. The Prophet.

A comparison matrix. The pillars of rule and the two instruments that hold each honest.

Rows: The commander, The judge, The officer
Columns: The character sought | Generous provision | Close watching
- The commander: forbearance, gentle to the weak, firm with the strong | benevolence to his men so want never drives them | supervised as parents supervise a child
- The judge: unbent by complexity, greed, flattery, or pressure | pay so generous his need of others disappears | verdicts examined carefully and often
- The officer: proven, modest, from a household of service | a livelihood that removes the temptation to steal | supervisors of known reliability set over him

Reading: the same two instruments recur, provision and watching, a state built to make honesty easier than corruption.

An annotated structure. The ruler's open door, and the one door he keeps shut.

Whole: the sharpest test of justice, the class that can never repay him
- The poor: a fixed share of the treasury, and an officer charged to seek out the need that hides itself
- The assembly: sitting with them stripped of guards, "so they may speak without inhibition"
- The prayer: paced to the weakest body in the row, "be merciful to the believers"
- The one seclusion: "the finest portion of your time for what is between you and God," the axle the hub turns on
Reading: a secluded ruler goes blind; only one door stays shut, and it opens toward God.

A contrast pair. The two summits on which the letter weighs a ruler.

Column A: The covenant kept
- Command: keep the pledge with complete trustworthiness, even to an enemy
- Image: "make your very life a shield for what you have promised"
- Ground: God has made His own pledge a sanctuary; even the pagan Arabs kept faith

Column B: The blood unspilled
- Command: beware of shedding blood unlawfully, seek no strength through it
