# The First Sermon of the Nahj al-Balagha

The opening sermon of ⟪ar:نھج البلاغہ⟫ Maulana Ali (ع) delivered as a ⟪ar:خطبۃ⟫ proclaiming the oneness of God, a oneness that cannot fall into the category of numbers. It is built upon paradox, for the loftier the truth he means to reveal, the deeper the paradox he forms to reveal it: by negating the reach of human words and concepts he affirms the Reality that no word and no concept can bound. Such is the manner of his sermons. The eye cannot see Him, yet the mind cannot deny Him; He is farther than the flights of imagination can reach, yet nearer to us than our own jugular vein. Nowhere is this plainer than in these opening lines.

Praise be to God, الحَمْدُ للهِ الَّذَي لاَ يَبْلُغُ مِدْحَتَهُ القَائِلُونَ, whose laudation those who speak cannot deliver. Here is the very paradox of speech, whose office is to convey a concept, turned to demonstrate that God is so lofty and sublime that words can deliver nothing of Him. Whose graces those who count cannot number, وَلاِ يُحْصِي نَعْمَاءَهُ العَادُّونَ, for counting is suited to enumeration, but not to His bounties. Whose rightful due those who strive cannot render, ولاَ يُؤَدِّي حَقَّهُ الُمجْتَهِدُونَ, for however hard they strive, they can never render Him the praise and gratitude He is due. He who cannot be reached by far-reaching aspirations, الَّذِي لاَ يُدْركُهُ بُعْدُ الهِمَمِ, nor fathomed by profound intuitions, وَلاَ يَنَالُهُ غَوْصُ الفِطَنِ. He whose attribute has no binding limitation, no existing description, no time appointed, no term extended, الَّذِي لَيْسَ لِصِفَتِهِ حَدٌّ مَحْدُودٌ، وَلاَ نَعْتٌ مَوْجُودٌ، وَلا وَقْتٌ مَعْدُودٌ, so that His attribute cannot be confined to a definition and yet is affirmed as real, described by the very words of revelation even though no description can capture Him. Through His power He originated all created things, and through His mercy He diffused the comforting winds; and He entrenched with rocky pillars His swaying earth.

Foremost in religion is knowledge of Him; the perfection of this knowledge is believing in Him; the perfection of this belief is affirming His oneness; and the perfection of this affirmation is to purify one's devotion to Him, the perfection of sincerity, أخلاص, which is to render one's devotion and one's very conception of God pure. The perfection of this purification is to divest Him of all attributes (نفی الصفۃ), because every attribute bears witness that it is other than the object of attribution, and every such object bears witness that it is other than the attribute. So whoever ascribes an attribute to God, glorified be He, has conjoined Him with something else; whoever conjoins Him has made Him twofold; whoever makes Him twofold has fragmented Him; and whoever fragments Him is ignorant of Him. Whoever points to Him confines Him; whoever confines Him counts Him; whoever asks 'in what?' encloses Him; and whoever asks 'upon what?' isolates Him. For the very attributes by which He is named in revelation, that He is the All-Compassionate (رحمن), the All-Merciful (رحیم), the Almighty (عزیز), are affirmed as real and yet cannot be defined, bounded, or described: He is merciful, but not as any mercy we can picture; powerful, but not as any storm or flood or force we have known. Reason itself 'de-fines,' drawing a boundary, a ⟪ar:حد⟫, around whatever it grasps, and by breaking that boundary the sermon opens an awareness beyond the reach of speculative thought, so that in negating the limits of human words it affirms the Absolute that has no limit. Such is the very movement of the testimony of faith: لا الہ, 'no deity,' which is the negation (نفی), and الا اللہ, 'except God,' which is the affirmation (اثبات). Each attribute is negated only insofar as the limited mind conceives it, and is thereby affirmed as nothing but God Himself: La Rahmana illa'Llah, there is no All-Compassionate but God; La Raheema illa'Llah, there is no All-Merciful but God. Thus is God glorified above all that the mind would attribute to Him: "‏سُبْحَنَ ٱللَّهِ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ", 'Glorified is God above all that they attribute to Him!' (Surat al-Mu'minun, 23:91).

Being, but not by way of any becoming; existing, but not from having been non-existent; with every thing, but not through association; and other than every thing, but not through separation; acting, but not through movements and instruments; seeing, even when nothing of His creation was to be seen; solitary, even when there was none whose intimacy might be sought or whose absence might be missed.

He brought forth creation, originating it from the very beginning, without pondering over any deliberation or benefiting from any previous experience; without initiating any movement or engaging in any unsettling exertion. He assigned to all things their span of time and harmonized their differences; He instilled within them their natural dispositions, impressing upon them their respective forms; knowing them before their genesis, encompassing their boundaries and their finalities; aware of their configurations and their contours.

Then He, glorified be He, created a fissure in the atmospheres, cleaving apart directions and the uppermost levels of air; then He caused water to flow therein, a current of surging waves swelling and swirling in overflowing abundance. Then He mounted it upon a tempestuous wind, a thunderous gale; then He made the wind prevent the return of the water, enabling it to dominate the violence of its flow and aligning it with the boundaries of the water; the air beneath the wind was spread wide open, while the water above it flowed copiously.

Then He raised up a wind, making it sterile, fixing its course, intensifying its current, and distancing it from its source. Then He ordered it to agitate the swelling waters and to stir up oceanic waves. The wind churned up the waters, as one churns milk in a milkbag, imparting to the water its own violence in the atmosphere, turning its foremost back upon its hindmost and its stillness into motion, so that its torrents surged and its swelling waves threw up foam. Then He elevated that foam within a cleft air and a vast open atmosphere and formed from it the seven heavens, making the lowest of them a suspended wave and the highest a protected shelter and an elevated ceiling, without any pillars to support them or any clinch to transfix them. Then He adorned the heavens with the ornaments of the stars and the effulgence of the firmament, and made a radiant lamp, the sun, and an illuminating moon to follow an orbit in a revolving sphere, beneath a roof in motion and a firmament in flux. Then He split up the highest heavens and filled them with angels of different dispositions: some prostrating and not bowing; others bowing and not standing upright; some arrayed in ranks, not separating from one another; others glorifying, never wearying, not overcome by sleepiness in the eyes, distraction in the mind, fatigue of the body, or the negligence of forgetfulness. Among them are custodians of His Revelation, and conveyors of His Words to His Messengers, and those who come and go with His Decree and His Command. Among them are the guardians of His slaves, and the wardens of the gates of His Gardens. And among them are those whose feet stand firmly on the lowest worlds, and whose necks surpass the highest heaven, and whose sides exceed all bounds, and whose shoulders are proportioned to the pillars of the Throne, their eyes cast down before it, enwrapping themselves beneath it in their wings. Between them and all others there is a barrier: veils of Glory and screens of Might. They do not imagine their Lord through formal representation, nor ascribe to Him created attributes; they do not confine Him within places, nor do they allude to Him through similitudes.

Then He, glorified be He, gathered up a quantity of dust, from some hard earth and some soft, some sweet and some salty, and poured water onto it until it was pure, then kneaded into it moisture until it held together. From this He fashioned a form with curves and joints, limbs and parts, compacting it until it held fast, solidifying it until it became as sounding clay, and assigning to it a measured duration and a known end. Then He breathed into it of His spirit, and it rose up as a human being, endowed with intellectual powers for reflection, thoughts by which to conduct himself, limbs to put to use, instruments at his disposal, and knowledge with which to discern between true and false, between different tastes and fragrances, and between different colours and kinds: a constitution compounded of different hues, unified forms, contrasting oppositions, and distinct admixtures of heat and cold, moisture and dryness.

Then God, glorified be He, demanded of the angels the fulfilment of what He had charged them with, and their compliance with His injunction, by prostrating in submission before Adam and humbling themselves before him. So He said, "Prostrate to Adam," and they prostrated, all except Iblis. Passionate scorn deranged him and wretchedness overwhelmed him; he took pride in his constitution of fire and despised the creature made of clay. Then God granted him a respite, so that His wrath would be fully warranted, the affliction consummated, and the promise fulfilled. So He said, "You are among those given respite until a day whose time is appointed."

Then He, glorified be He, settled Adam in an abode where He made his life comfortable and his domain secure, and He warned him of Iblis and his enmity. But his enemy beguiled him, driven by envious desire for Adam's immortal dwelling and his intimacy with the righteous. So Adam traded certainty for doubt and resolution for weakness, and replaced joy with dread and self-esteem with remorse. Then God accepted his repentance, enveloped him in a word of His mercy, and promised him a return to His Garden, before sending him down to the abode of tribulations and the begetting of progeny.

From Adam's offspring God chose prophets, taking from them a pledge concerning the Revelation and entrusting them with the conveyance of His message, at a time when most of His creatures had strayed from their covenant with God, ignoring His due, setting up rivals alongside Him, while the demons distracted them from knowledge of Him and severed them from worship of Him. So He sent among them His messengers and dispatched prophets in succession, to demand of His creatures the fulfilment of their covenant, to remind them of His forgotten graces, to remonstrate with them through the communication of His Revelation, to unearth for them the buried treasures of the intellect, and to show them signs of His omnipotence: the outspread earth laid low beneath their feet, provisions that give them life, endings that mete out death, ailments hastening their decline, and a succession of events that flows over them.

God never left His creation without a prophet sent forth, a revealed scripture, a compelling proof, or a clear path. These messengers were not weakened by the scarcity of their numbers or by the multitude of those who denied them. Those who came earlier were told the names of those who would follow, and those who came later were informed of those who had gone before. In this way generations were born and ages elapsed, fathers passing away and sons succeeding them, until God sent forth Muhammad, Messenger of God, in fulfilment of His promise and in completion of the cycle of prophecy, he concerning whom a pledge had been taken from the prophets, known through his signs, of noble birth.

At that time the people of the earth were divided into different religious communities; caprice was rampant and pathways were in disorder, among them the path of those who likened God to His creation, distorted His name, or pointed to other than Him. So God guided them through His Prophet out of their error and, through his presence, delivered them from their ignorance.

Then God chose for Muhammad the meeting with Him, and desired for him what was with Himself. He removed him with dignity from the abode of this lower world and drew him away from the realm of trial, taking him to Himself in honour. The Prophet left behind among you what the prophets leave for their communities, so that they would not be abandoned to wander without a clear path or a visible beacon: the scripture of your Lord, elucidating what is lawful and unlawful, what is obligatory and what is recommended, the abrogating verses and the abrogated, the conditionally permissible and the unconditionally compulsory, the specific and the general, His admonitions and His analogies, the undelimited and the delimited, the unambiguous and the equivocal, making plain what is condensed and clarifying what is obscure.

In it are matters whose knowledge is binding upon God's servants, and others whose ignorance is overlooked. There are injunctions affirmed as obligatory in the scripture yet known to have been abrogated by the Sunna, and actions made obligatory by the Sunna from which the scripture has nonetheless permitted abstention. There are duties incumbent upon a person at their prescribed time that cease to be incumbent once that time has passed. The scripture also draws a distinction between forbidden acts: the major sins, with which He has threatened punishment by His fire, and the minor ones, for which He has prepared His forgiveness. And it describes acts that are acceptable when performed to a modest degree and equally acceptable when performed to a higher degree, without either being obligatory.
