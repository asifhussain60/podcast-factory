# The People of the House and the Prophet's Witness

Ali (ع)'s role as a warrior is only one of many factors that account for his unique place in the Islamic tradition. Far more significant is his closeness, in every respect, to the Prophet, and the wisdom and sanctity that flowed from that proximity. Ali (ع) was accorded the immense honour of marrying the Prophet's daughter Fatima, considered, alongside her mother Khadija, the Prophet's first wife, to be the most saintly woman in Islam. One of the epithets the tradition bestows on Fatima is Majma' al-Nurayn, 'Confluence of the Two Lights', the two lights of nubuwwa (prophecy) and walaya (sanctity). After the Prophet himself, Ali (ع) was thus the head of what became known as the Ahl al-Bayt, 'the People of the House', that is, the Prophet's family.

The Ahl al-Bayt are addressed in an important Qur'anic verse as having been purified of all defilement:

وَقَرْنَ فِى بُيُوتِكُنَّ وَلَا تَبَرَّجْنَ تَبَرُّجَ ٱلْجَهِلِيَّةِ ٱلْأُولَىٰۖ وَأَقِمْنَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَآتِينَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَأَطِعْنَ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۚۥٓ إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ ٱلرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ ٱلْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًۭا

'God only wisheth to remove from you all impurity, O People of the House, and to purify you with a complete purification' (33:33).

According to several of the most authoritative early sources, the Prophet called for Fatima, Ali (ع), and their two sons Hasan and Husayn, and declared, 'This is my Ahl al-Bayt.' On one well-attested occasion the Prophet gathered these members of his family beneath his cloak and recited the words of this verse. The event took place when a Christian delegation from Najran was engaged in religious debate with the Prophet, and a revelation was received calling for a challenge of mutual imprecation (mubahila) against the Christians. The Prophet brought forward the four persons named above for this mubahila, but the Christians declined the challenge.

The importance of the Ahl al-Bayt is further expressed in a verse in which the Prophet is instructed:

ذَلِكَ ٱلَّذِى يُبَشِّرُ ٱللَّهُ عِبَادَهُ ٱلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّلِحَتِ ۗ قُل لَّآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًا إِلَّا ٱلْمَوَدَّةَ فِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ ۗ وَمَن يَقْتَرِفْ حَسَنَةًۭ نَّزِدْ لَهُۥ فِيهَا حُسْنًا ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورٌۭ شَكُورٌ

'Say: I ask you for no reward, save love of the near of kin' (42:23).

Several of the most significant Qur'anic commentators identify 'the near of kin' in this verse as the Prophet's Ahl al-Bayt. Love of the Prophet's family is thus explicitly demanded by the revelation, without this excluding the verse's broader import, which encompasses love of all those bound to one by close kinship. The degree to which the Prophet's family is to be revered by all Muslims is further indicated by the fact that blessings are to be invoked upon his al, or progeny, as part of the blessings invoked upon him. The Qur'an instructs the believers:

إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ وَمَلَٓئِكَتَهُۥ يُصَلُّونَ عَلَى ٱلنَّبِىِّۚ يَٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا۟ صَلُّوا۟ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلِّمُوا۟ تَسْلِيمًا

'Truly, God and His angels bless the Prophet. O ye who believe, bless him and greet him with peace' (33:56).

When this verse was revealed, the Prophet was asked how one should perform this blessing, and he replied that blessings were to be invoked upon him and his al as follows: 'O God, bless Muhammad and the progeny of Muhammad, as Thou hast blessed Abraham and the progeny of Abraham. Truly, Thou art the Praised, the Glorious ...' The importance of this blessing, to be recited during the canonical prayer, together with the obligation to love the Prophet's family, is beautifully expressed in the following lines by Imam al-Shafi'i, founder of one of the four schools of Sunni jurisprudence:

Loving you, O family of the household of the Prophet of God, is an obligation (fard) from God in the Qur'an which He revealed; Sufficient to show the grandeur of your dignity is that one who blesses you not, no prayer has he.

There is also a prophetic saying that underscores the spiritual significance of the Ahl al-Bayt:

Truly, I am leaving behind amongst you the two weighty things (al-thaqalayn): the Book of God and my Ahl al-Bayt. They will not be parted from each other until they return to me at the [paradisal pool] al-hawd.

Beyond these sayings concerning Ali (ع) as the most important member of the Prophet's Ahl al-Bayt, there are sayings of the Prophet concerning Ali (ع) specifically that deserve to be cited in full, for it is only on the basis of these sayings that one can begin to appreciate the immensely important role of Ali (ع) in the spiritual dimension of the Islamic faith. These traditions (ahadith, sing. hadith) are presented here as a list with minimal commentary, following the manner in which they appear in most standard hadith collections. Anyone who takes prophetic guidance seriously cannot but take Ali (ع) seriously, and it is largely for this reason that countless Muslims across the centuries have meditated on these sayings, drawing from them diverse interpretations and incalculable inspiration. In a certain sense, these sayings form the prism through which the tradition views Ali (ع), and they deepen one's spiritual receptivity to his teachings. The crucial importance of Ali (ع) in the spiritual firmament of nascent Islam emerges with greater clarity in the light of these prophetic descriptions, and they should therefore be regarded as a significant aspect of the 'prophetic background' to Ali (ع)'s seminal contribution to Islamic spiritual and intellectual life, whether in Shi'i, Sunni, or Sufi context.

The traditions given below can be found in any standard Shi'i hadith collection, but to show that they, together with their implications and the devotion they have inspired, are not confined to Shi'i Islam, they are cited here from two of the most authoritative Sunni traditionists: Ahmad al-Nasa'i, author of the Sunan, one of the six collections deemed 'canonical' in the Sunni corpus (al-kutub al-sitta); and al-Hakim al-Nisaburi, author of al-Mustadrak 'ala'l-sahihayn (Supplement to the Two Sound Collections), which gathers those prophetic traditions that meet the exacting criteria of authenticity established by Bukhari and Muslim, authors of the sahihayn, but were not included in their own collections. The sayings pertaining to Ali (ع)'s distinctions, virtues, and merits, all conveyed by the Arabic word fada'il (sing. fadila), explain why Ahmad b. Hanbal, another leading Sunni traditionist and founder of one of the four Sunni schools of law, made the following statement: 'No companion of the Prophet has had such fada'il ascribed to him as those which have been ascribed to Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع).'

The sayings are these:

'Truly, Ali (ع) is from me and I am from him (inna 'Ali minni wa ana minhu), and he is the wali (patron/spiritual master) of every believer after me.'

'Ali (ع) is with the Qur'an and the Qur'an is with Ali (ع), إِنَّ عَلِيًّا مَعَ الْقُرْآنِ وَالْقُرْآنُ مَعَ عَلِيٍّ، لَنْ يَفْتَرِقَا حَتَّى يَرِدَا عَلَيَّ الْحَوْضَ. They will not separate from each other until they return to me at the [paradisal pool] (al-hawd).'

'Three things were revealed to me regarding Ali (ع): he is the leader of the Muslims, the guide of the pious, and chief of the radiantly devout (sayyidu'l-muslimin, imamu'l-muttaqin, wa qa'idu'l-ghurra'l-muhajjalin).'

'Gazing upon Ali (ع) is an act of worship (al-nazar ila 'Ali ibada).'

'May God have mercy on Ali (ع). O God, make the truth revolve around Ali (ع) wherever he turns (adiri'l-haqq ma'ahu haythu dara).'

'I am the city of knowledge and Ali (ع) is its gate; so whoever desires knowledge, let him enter the gate.'

'O Ali (ع), you are a leader (sayyid) in this world and the Hereafter. Your beloved is my beloved, and my beloved is the beloved of God; your enemy is my enemy, and my enemy is the enemy of God. Woe to those who hate you after me [after I have passed away].'

'Whoever desires to live my life and to die my death and to take his rest in the eternal Garden my Lord has promised me, let him orient himself towards Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), for truly he will never lead you away from right guidance, nor lead you into error.'

The Prophet said that Ali (ع) was 'as my own soul' (ka-nafsi).

He said to Ali (ع), 'You are from me and I am from you (anta minni wa ana minka), أنْتَ مِنِّی وَ أِنَا مِنْکَ.'

'... whoever obeys Ali (ع) obeys me, and whoever disobeys him disobeys me.'

'You will clarify for my community that over which they will differ after me (anta tubayyinu li-ummati ma'khtalafu fihi ba'di).'

'There is one amongst you who will fight for the ta'wil [spiritual interpretation] of the Qur'an as I have fought for its tanzil [literal revelation], عَلِیٌّ یُقَاتِلُ عَلیٰ تَاوِیلِہِ کَما قَاتَلْتُ عَلیٰ تَنزِیلِہِ.' Abu Bakr asked, 'Is it I?' The Prophet said, 'No.' 'Umar asked, 'Is it I?' The Prophet said, 'No, it is the one who is mending the sandal.' The Prophet had given Ali (ع) his sandal to mend.

'O Ali (ع), there is in you something akin to Jesus, on whom be peace and blessings. The Jews hated him so intensely that they slandered his mother; and the Christians loved him so intensely that they ascribed to him a rank he did not possess.' 'O Ali (ع), whoever separates himself from me separates himself from God, and whoever separates himself from you, O Ali (ع), separates himself from me.'

'Whoever curses Ali (ع) curses me, and whoever curses me curses God.'

The Prophet said to his wife 'A'isha, 'Call unto me the leader (sayyid) of the Arabs.' She asked, 'O Prophet of God, are you not the leader of the Arabs?' He said, 'I am the leader of the children of Adam, and Ali (ع) is the leader of the Arabs.' When Fatima was asked by some women why the Prophet had given her in marriage to a man as poor as Ali (ع), she related this to the Prophet. He said: 'Are you not pleased that God, exalted and glorified be He, surveyed the people of the world and chose two men from among them: one is your father, and the other your husband?'

'The first of you to reach the [paradisal] pool (al-hawd) is the first of you who entered Islam, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع).'

'Ali (ع) is from me and I am from him ('Ali minni wa ana minhu), and nobody can fulfil my duty but myself and Ali (ع).'

Ali (ع) himself relates that the Prophet told him that none but a believer will love him, and none but a hypocrite will hate him.

When the Prophet was about to depart on an expedition to Tabuk, he left Ali (ع) as his deputy in Medina. Ali (ع) was saddened not to be going with him. The Prophet said, 'Are you not content to stand in relation to me as Aaron stood in relation to Moses, except that there is no prophet after me?'

The Prophet prayed to God to bring 'the most beloved of Thy creatures' to share with him a meal of fowl. Only when Ali (ع) arrived did the Prophet invite him to join him.

Among the several Qur'anic verses on which the Prophet commented with reference to Ali (ع) is 13:7: 'Verily thou art a warner, and for every people there is a guide.' The Prophet said, 'I am the warner ... you are the guide, O Ali (ع). After me, the rightly-guided shall be guided by you.'

Regarding the revelation of verse 55 of Sura 5 (al-Ma'ida), 'Verily your wali is only God and His Messenger and those who believe, establish the prayer and give alms while bowing in prayer,' the commentators relate this to the occasion when Ali (ع), while bowing in prayer, extended his ring to a beggar who had asked for alms. The Prophet recited this verse upon being told of the incident, and then added, in what are perhaps the most celebrated words ever spoken about Ali (ع): 'For whomever I am the mawla [guardian, master, close friend], Ali (ع) is his mawla (man kuntu mawlahu fa-'Ali mawlahu).'

This last saying was expressed on a number of occasions, the most famous of which was after the Prophet's final pilgrimage to Mecca, in the year 10/632, at a pool midway between Mecca and Medina known as Ghadir Khumm. The Prophet assembled all the pilgrims, had a pulpit erected, and delivered an address to the thousands gathered there. The address culminated in the declaration: 'For whomever I am the mawla, Ali (ع) is his mawla.' For Shi'is, this is regarded as an unambiguous designation (nass) of Ali (ع) as successor to the Prophet; for Sunnis it indicates the special closeness of Ali (ع) to the Prophet, but not his nomination as successor in the political domain. That the reference to Ali (ع) as mawla, in some versions as wali, is of the highest spiritual significance, however, is not seriously disputed. 'Umar is reported to have approached Ali (ع) and said, 'Bakh bakh', an expression of congratulation, 'you have become my mawla and the mawla of every Muslim.' The polemical debate that has exercised countless minds throughout history, and that continues to this day, turns on the political implications of Ali (ع)'s spiritual authority, his walaya.

Although the place of Ali (ع) within Shi'i Islam is not the subject here, his charisma was such that, even during the Prophet's own lifetime, a group came to be known as the shi'a, or 'partisans,' of Ali (ع). Al-Tabari, author of the first great commentary on the Qur'an, reports that the Prophet interpreted the verse:

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّلِحَتِ أُو۟لَٓئِكَ هُمْ خَيْرُ ٱلْبَرِيَّةِ

'Those who have faith and do righteous deeds, they are the best of created beings' (98:7), as referring to Ali (ع) and his 'shi'a.'

Four individuals in particular were renowned for their devotion to Ali (ع) during the Prophet's lifetime and are regarded as the prototypes of early Shi'ism: Salman al-Farisi, Abu Dharr al-Ghifari, 'Ammar b. Yasir, and Miqdad b. 'Amr. This early manifestation of love for Ali (ع) should not be identified with the theological doctrines that were later elaborated in the Shi'i branch of Islam in subsequent generations. It is nonetheless worth emphasising that, at the core of Shi'ism in all its different expressions, lies the notion of fidelity to Ali (ع). This fidelity, loyalty, or love constitutes one aspect of walaya; the other, complementary aspect is the spiritual authority or sanctity of Ali (ع). It is equally important to stress that neither devotion to Ali (ع) nor the spiritual orientation toward the walaya he embodies is the exclusive province of Shi'ism. Rather, it extends across the whole of the Islamic faith, for devotion to Ali (ع) as head of the Prophet's Ahl al-Bayt is found throughout the Muslim world and is especially deep wherever Sufism flourishes. Ali (ع) is regarded as the first spiritual 'pole' (qutb) of Sufism after the Prophet himself, and he stands at the summit of all the spiritual chains (salasil, sing. silsila) through which the Sufi orders trace their lineage back to the Prophet. To speak of Ali (ع) is to speak not of exclusivist sectarianism but of universal spirituality.
