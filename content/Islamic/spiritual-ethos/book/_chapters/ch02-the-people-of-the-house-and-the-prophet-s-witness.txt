# The People of the House and the Prophet's Witness

Ali (ع)'s standing as a warrior is only one of many things that explain his unique place in the Islamic tradition. Far more important is how close he was, in every respect, to the Prophet, and the wisdom and sanctity that flowed from that closeness. Ali received the immense honor of marrying the Prophet's daughter Fatima. She and her mother Khadija, the Prophet's first wife, are regarded as the most saintly women in Islam. One of the epithets the tradition bestows on Fatima is Majma' al-Nurayn, 'Confluence of the Two Lights', the two lights of nubuwwa (prophecy) and وَلَايَة (sanctity). After the Prophet himself, Ali was thus the head of what became known as the Ahl al-Bayt, 'the People of the House', that is, the Prophet's family.

The Ahl al-Bayt are addressed in an important Quranic verse as having been purified of all defilement:

> وَقَرْنَ فِى بُيُوتِكُنَّ وَلَا تَبَرَّجْنَ تَبَرُّجَ ٱلْجَهِلِيَّةِ ٱلْأُولَىٰۖ وَأَقِمْنَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَآتِينَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَأَطِعْنَ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۚۥٓ إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ ٱلرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ ٱلْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًۭا
>
> 'God only wisheth to remove from you all impurity, O People of the House, and to purify you with a complete purification' (Al-Ahzab: 33).

According to several of the most authoritative early sources, the Prophet called for Fatima, Ali, and their two sons Hasan and Husayn, and declared, 'This is my Ahl al-Bayt.' On one well-attested occasion the Prophet gathered these members of his family beneath his cloak and recited the words of this verse. This happened when a Christian delegation from Najran was engaged in religious debate with the Prophet, and a revelation came calling for a challenge of mutual imprecation (مُبَاهَلَة) against the Christians. The Prophet brought forward the four persons named above for this مُبَاهَلَة, but the Christians declined the challenge.

The importance of the Ahl al-Bayt is further expressed in a verse in which the Prophet is instructed:

> ذَلِكَ ٱلَّذِى يُبَشِّرُ ٱللَّهُ عِبَادَهُ ٱلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّلِحَتِ ۗ قُل لَّآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًا إِلَّا ٱلْمَوَدَّةَ فِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ ۗ وَمَن يَقْتَرِفْ حَسَنَةًۭ نَّزِدْ لَهُۥ فِيهَا حُسْنًا ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورٌۭ شَكُورٌ
>
> 'Say: I ask you for no reward, save love of the near of kin' (Ash-Shura: 23).

Several of the most significant Quranic commentators identify 'the near of kin' in this verse as the Prophet's Ahl al-Bayt. The revelation thus explicitly demands love of the Prophet's family. This does not exclude the verse's wider meaning, which takes in love for all those bound to a person by close kinship. How deeply all Muslims are to revere the Prophet's family is shown further by another practice: blessings are to be called down upon his al, or progeny, as part of the blessings called down upon him. The Quran instructs the believers:

> إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ وَمَلَٓئِكَتَهُۥ يُصَلُّونَ عَلَى ٱلنَّبِىِّۚ يَٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا۟ صَلُّوا۟ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلِّمُوا۟ تَسْلِيمًا
>
> 'Truly, God and His angels bless the Prophet. O ye who believe, bless him and greet him with peace' (Al-Ahzab: 56).

When this verse was revealed, the Prophet was asked how one should perform this blessing, and he replied that blessings were to be invoked upon him and his al as follows: 'O God, bless Muhammad and the progeny of Muhammad, as Thou hast blessed Abraham and the progeny of Abraham. Truly, Thou art the Praised, the Glorious ...' This blessing is recited during the canonical prayer. Its importance, together with the obligation to love the Prophet's family, is beautifully expressed in the following lines by Imam al-Shafii, founder of one of the four schools of Sunni jurisprudence:

Loving you, O family of the household of the Prophet of God, is an obligation (فَرْضٌ) from God in the Quran which He revealed; Sufficient to show the grandeur of your dignity is that one who blesses you not, no prayer has he.

There is also a prophetic saying that underscores the spiritual significance of the Ahl al-Bayt:

Truly, I am leaving behind among you the two weighty things (اَلثَّقَلَيْنِ): the Book of God and my Ahl al-Bayt. They will not be parted from each other until they return to me at the [paradisal pool] الْحَوْضَ.

Beyond these sayings, which concern Ali as the most important member of the Prophet's Ahl al-Bayt, there are sayings of the Prophet that concern Ali specifically, and these deserve to be cited in full. Only on the basis of these sayings can one begin to appreciate the immensely important role of Ali in the spiritual dimension of the Islamic faith. These traditions (ahadith, sing. hadith) are presented here as a list with minimal commentary, following the manner in which they appear in most standard hadith collections. Anyone who takes prophetic guidance seriously cannot but take Ali seriously, and it is largely for this reason that countless Muslims across the centuries have meditated on these sayings, drawing from them diverse interpretations and incalculable inspiration. In a certain sense, these sayings form the prism through which the tradition views Ali, and they deepen one's spiritual receptivity to his teachings. The crucial importance of Ali in the spiritual firmament of nascent Islam emerges more clearly in the light of these prophetic descriptions. They should therefore be regarded as a significant part of the 'prophetic background' to Ali 's seminal contribution to Islamic spiritual and intellectual life, whether in a Shii, Sunni, or Sufi context.

The traditions given below can be found in any standard Shii hadith collection. To show that they — together with their implications and the devotion they have inspired — are not confined to Shii Islam, they are cited here from two of the most authoritative Sunni traditionists. The first is Ahmad al-Nasai, author of the Sunan, one of the six collections deemed 'canonical' in the Sunni corpus (اَلْكُتُبُ السِّتَّةُ). The second is al-Hakim al-Nisaburi, author of al-Mustadrak 'alal-sahihayn (Supplement to the Two Sound Collections). This work gathers those prophetic traditions that meet the exacting criteria of authenticity established by Bukhari and Muslim, authors of the sahihayn, but were not included in their own collections. These sayings speak of Ali s distinctions, virtues, and merits, all conveyed by the Arabic word fadail (sing. fadila). They explain why Ahmad b. Hanbal, another leading Sunni traditionist and founder of one of the four Sunni schools of law, made the following statement: No companion of the Prophet has had such fadail ascribed to him as those which have been ascribed to Ali ibn Abi Talib.'

The sayings are these:

'Truly, Ali is from me and I am from him (إِنَّ عَلِيًّا مِنِّي وَأَنَا مِنْهُ), and he is the wali (patron/spiritual master) of every believer after me.'

'Ali is with the Quran and the Quran is with Ali, إِنَّ عَلِيًّا مَعَ الْقُرْآنِ وَالْقُرْآنُ مَعَ عَلِيٍّ، لَنْ يَفْتَرِقَا حَتَّى يَرِدَا عَلَيَّ الْحَوْضَ. They will not separate from each other until they return to me at the [paradisal pool] (الْحَوْضَ).'

'Three things were revealed to me regarding Ali: he is the leader of the Muslims, the guide of the pious, and chief of the radiantly devout (سَيِّدُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ، إِمَامُ الْمُتَّقِينَ، وَقَائِدُ الْغُرِّ الْمُحَجَّلِينَ).'

'Gazing upon Ali is an act of worship (النَّظَرُ إِلَى عَلِيٍّ عِبَادَةٌ).'

'May God have mercy on Ali. O God, make the truth revolve around Ali wherever he turns (أَدِرِ الْحَقَّ مَعَهُ حَيْثُ دَارَ).'

'I am the city of knowledge and Ali is its gate; so whoever desires knowledge, let him enter the gate.'

'O Ali, you are a leader (سَيِّدٌ) in this world and the Hereafter. Your beloved is my beloved, and my beloved is the beloved of God; your enemy is my enemy, and my enemy is the enemy of God. Woe to those who hate you after me [after I have passed away].'

'Whoever desires to live my life and to die my death and to take his rest in the eternal Garden my Lord has promised me, let him orient himself toward Ali ibn Abi Talib, for truly he will never lead you away from right guidance, nor lead you into error.'

The Prophet said that Ali was 'as my own soul' (عليٌّ مِنِّي بِمَنْزِلَةِ نَفْسِي).

He said to Ali, 'You are from me and I am from you, أنْتَ مِنِّی وَ أِنَا مِنْکَ.'

... whoever obeys Ali obeys me, and whoever disobeys him disobeys me.

'You will clarify for my community that over which they will differ after me (أَنْتَ تُبَيِّنُ لِأُمَّتِي مَا اخْتَلَفُوا فِيهِ مِنْ بَعْدِي).'

'There is one among you who will fight for the تَأْوِيل [spiritual interpretation] of the Quran as I have fought for its tanzil [literal revelation], عَلِیٌّ یُقَاتِلُ عَلیٰ تَاوِیلِہِ کَما قَاتَلْتُ عَلیٰ تَنزِیلِہِ.' Abu Bakr asked, 'Is it I?' The Prophet said, 'No.' 'Umar asked, Is it I?' The Prophet said, 'No, it is the one who is mending the sandal.' The Prophet had given Ali his sandal to mend.

'O Ali, there is in you something akin to Jesus, on whom be peace and blessings. The Jews hated him so intensely that they slandered his mother; and the Christians loved him so intensely that they ascribed to him a rank he did not possess.' 'O Ali, whoever separates himself from me separates himself from God, and whoever separates himself from you, O Ali, separates himself from me.'

'Whoever curses Ali curses me, and whoever curses me curses God.'

The Prophet said to his wife 'Aisha, Call unto me the leader (سَيِّدٌ) of the Arabs.' She asked, 'O Prophet of God, are you not the leader of the Arabs?' He said, 'I am the leader of the children of Adam, and Ali is the leader of the Arabs.'

When Fatima was asked by some women why the Prophet had given her in marriage to a man as poor as Ali, she related this to the Prophet. He said: 'Are you not pleased that God, exalted and glorified be He, surveyed the people of the world and chose two men from among them: one is your father, and the other your husband?'

'The first of you to reach the [paradisal] pool (الْحَوْضَ) is the first of you who entered Islam, Ali ibn Abi Talib.'

'Ali is from me and I am from him (عَلِيٌّ مِنِّي وَأَنَا مِنْهُ), and nobody can fulfill my duty but myself and Ali.'

Ali himself relates that the Prophet told him that none but a believer will love him, and none but a hypocrite will hate him.

When the Prophet was about to depart on an expedition to Tabuk, he left Ali as his deputy in Medina. Ali was saddened not to be going with him. The Prophet said, 'Are you not content to stand in relation to me as Aaron stood in relation to Moses, except that there is no prophet after me?'

The Prophet prayed to God to bring 'the most beloved of Thy creatures' to share with him a meal of fowl. Only when Ali arrived did the Prophet invite him to join him.

Among the several Quranic verses on which the Prophet commented with reference to Ali is 13:7: 'Verily thou art a warner, and for every people there is a guide.' The Prophet said, 'I am the warner... you are the guide, O Ali. After me, the rightly-guided shall be guided by you.'

Verse 55 of Sura 5 (al-Maida (اَلْمَائِدَةُ)) reads, 'Verily your wali is only God and His Messenger and those who believe, establish the prayer and give alms while bowing in prayer.' The commentators tie its revelation to the occasion when Ali, while bowing in prayer, extended his ring to a beggar who had asked for alms. On being told of the incident, the Prophet recited this verse, and then added what are perhaps the most celebrated words ever spoken about Ali: 'For whomever I am the mawla [guardian, master, close friend], Ali is his mawla (مَنْ كُنْتُ مَوْلَاهُ فَعَلِيٌّ مَوْلَاهُ).'

This last saying was expressed on a number of occasions, the most famous of which came after the Prophet's final pilgrimage to Mecca, in the year 10/632, at a pool midway between Mecca and Medina known as Ghadir Khumm. The Prophet assembled all the pilgrims, had a pulpit erected, and delivered an address to the thousands gathered there. The address culminated in the declaration: 'For whomever I am the mawla, Ali is his mawla.' For Shiis, this is an unambiguous designation (نَصّ) of Ali as the Prophet's successor; for Sunnis it marks the special closeness of Ali to the Prophet, but not his nomination as successor in the political domain. That the reference to Ali as mawla — in some versions as wali — carries the highest spiritual significance is, however, not seriously disputed. 'Umar is reported to have approached Ali and said, Bakh bakh', an expression of congratulation, 'you have become my mawla and the mawla of every Muslim.' The heated debate that has occupied countless minds throughout history, and continues to this day, turns on the political implications of Ali 's spiritual authority, his وَلَايَة.

Although the place of Ali within Shii Islam is not the subject here, his charisma was such that, even during the Prophet's own lifetime, a group came to be known as the shia, or 'partisans,' of Ali. Al-Tabari, author of the first great commentary on the Quran, reports that the Prophet interpreted the verse:

> إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّلِحَتِ أُو۟لَٓئِكَ هُمْ خَيْرُ ٱلْبَرِيَّةِ

'Those who have faith and do righteous deeds, they are the best of created beings' (Al-Bayyinah: 7), as referring to Ali and his 'shia.'

Four individuals in particular were renowned for their devotion to Ali during the Prophet's lifetime and are regarded as the prototypes of early Shiism: Salman al-Farisi, Abu Dharr al-Ghifari, 'Ammar b. Yasir, and Miqdad b. Amr. This early love for Ali should not be equated with the theological doctrines later elaborated in the Shii branch of Islam in the generations that followed. It is nonetheless worth emphasizing that, at the core of Shiism in all its different expressions, lies the notion of fidelity to Ali. This fidelity, loyalty, or love is one aspect of وَلَايَة; the other, complementary aspect is the spiritual authority or sanctity of Ali. It is equally important to stress that neither devotion to Ali nor the spiritual orientation toward the وَلَايَة he embodies belongs to Shiism alone. Rather, it extends across the whole of the Islamic faith, for devotion to Ali as head of the Prophet's Ahl al-Bayt is found throughout the Muslim world and is especially deep wherever Sufism flourishes. Ali is regarded as the first spiritual pole' (قُطْبٌ) of Sufism after the Prophet himself, and he stands at the summit of all the spiritual chains (salasil, sing. silsila) through which the Sufi orders trace their lineage back to the Prophet. To speak of Ali is to speak not of exclusivist sectarianism but of universal spirituality.
