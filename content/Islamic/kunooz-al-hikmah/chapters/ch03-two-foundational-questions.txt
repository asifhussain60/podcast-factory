# The Two Foundational Questions

## Where this episode opens

What if the boldest question a believer can ask — the question of what was there *before* the world began — turns out to be a question the intellect is forbidden to answer? Not difficult to answer. Not yet to be answered. Forbidden. And what if the second-boldest question — the question of how the believer himself, in his own substance, came to be where he is — turns out to receive an answer so detailed that it traces a stream of luminous matter from the Sacred House through the moon, through the believer's morning meal, through the very drops of water that touch his skin during ablution? The first question is met with a wall; the second, with a map. Both come from the same Dai. Both stand in the opening pages of *Kunooz al-Hikmah*. And the contrast between the wall and the map is itself the first teaching of this episode.

This episode walks the two questions that open the body of the book. The first asks what was before the World of Origination — and the answer, delivered as much by what is forbidden as by what is permitted, lays down a frame for every cosmological statement the book will make afterward. The second asks about the highest sphere of the cosmos, the *falak al-mustaqim* — and unfolds, by its long answer, into the central teaching of the book about the Imam's soul, the believer's substance, and the streams of luminous matter that circulate between them. Three concepts run through what follows. The forbidden inquiry and the Originator's act. The fall of the penitents and the cartography held by the Tenth Intellect. And the encompassing sphere whose ascent opens onto the doctrine of the Imam's dissolved heart and the believer's daily intake of light.

## The forbidden inquiry and the Originator's act

The first question is bold from the start. The questioner declares that he has searched — in the books of the *awliya Allah*, the friends of Allah, and in the teaching of his masters — and has found nothing that points him to what was *before* the existence of the *alam al-ibda*, the World of Origination, or to what the state of affairs was there. He has also picked up, from his reading, the claim that the Imam's *hijab al-khas*, his special veil, is from the *riyadh al-hadrah*, the gardens of the Presence; he wants to know whether this is correct. And he asks, in passing, how the heavens and the earth came to be, and from what remnants the first *jussa* — the first particle or corporeal foundation, in the lower part of the earth — came into being.

Before the Dai's answer is read, a clarifying note from the teaching tradition needs to be placed in the listener's hand. Al-Walid Abdullah Sahib used always to say: do not rely on the questioner's framing; rely on what the Dai answers in response. The questioner may state his concept clumsily, or even wrongly. We do not learn doctrine from his concept. We learn it from the correction the Dai gives. In this case the questioner has come carrying a tripartite scheme of the Imam's veils that he has built out of the *tasbih*, the prayer-bead recitation. When one says *Allahu Akbar*, that points, he supposes, to the *hijab al-adna*, also called *hijab al-tabii*, the natural veil, or *hijab al-tanasuli*, the procreative veil. When one says *Subhanallah*, that points to the *hijab al-khas*, the special veil. The third portion points to the *dhat-i Imam*, the very essence of the Imam. And inside this scheme he has assumed that the special veil is made from the *riyadh* of the *hadarat*, the dignitaries of the Presence, while the natural veil is made from the *riyadh* of the *hujaj al-jazair*, the Proofs of the Islands. He has further imagined that the natural veil is what marries some of the women of the outward veil, leading to ordinary birth.

The Dai answers in three movements. The first concerns the question that should not have been asked at all.

What was before the World of Origination? The answer, in the words of the text: the existence of the World of Origination is not from a thing, and not in a thing, and inquiring about its modality is not permitted. The *Mubdi*, the Originator, brought the World of Origination into being not from any prior material, and not within any prior space. The ordinary picture we carry — a world inside of which intellects were placed, like furniture brought into a room — is mistaken. The World of Origination *is* the Intellects themselves. There is no surrounding container distinct from the contents. Mawlana Iddan Sahib used always to teach precisely this point. The Intellects, in their luminous being, are themselves what is called *alam al-ibda*. No "there" lies beyond them. No "before" precedes their emanation. The two questions that the questioner imagined could be asked — what was there *before*, and what was the state of affairs *there* — collapse on inspection. There was no there. There was no before.

And then the strict prohibition. One may not even ask, because the intellect has no scope in it. To speculate about the *kayfiyyah*, the modality of the *sudur*, the emanation of the *ibda*, is misguidance and association of partners with Allah and a plunging into the sea of destruction. We seek refuge in Allah from that. The originated Intellects themselves are at a loss to know how they came to be; they cannot peer back behind their own origin. How then could the lower forms below them, our forms, pretend to know it? The *awham*, the estimations of the mind, and the *damair*, the inner thoughts, cannot encompass it. The only thing the gnostic needs to know is that the emanation of the World of Origination is from the Exalted Identity, not from any thing. To go further is forbidden. This is one of those rare doctrinal moments in which the answer is itself a refusal. The wall is the doctrine. To walk up to the wall and read the sign on it is the most a creature can do.

The second movement of the answer corrects the questioner on the veils. His location of the *hijab al-tabii* in the *hujaj al-jazair* is, the Dai says plainly, an error — from you, a mistake — and whoever believes that is rejected and has fallen. To find the truth of the *nasut khas* and the *nasut tabii al-dani*, the listener is sent back to the opening preface that the previous episodes have already walked. Allah manifested the special form from the gardens of the people of specialty, and made the lower form from the gardens of those of lesser rank. The principle that governs both is one: in every *nasut*, the *riyadat* of all those traveling to the *lahut* are gathered, high and low. Sayyidna Sarimuddin — Sayyidna Ibrahim ibn Muhammad ibn Fahd — has set this out in his lofty books, and other duat in our line have pointed to it in the luminous collections: when the *suwarat* and the *riyadat* ascend, the *riyadat* ascend within the *nasut* and the *suwarat* ascend within the *lahut*, and they ascend in parallel. Whichever place the *riyadat* go in the *nasut*, in that very place the *suwarat* go in the *lahut*. They do not split between unrelated organs of the Imam's being. The two columns rise as one column with two faces.

The special veil is now defined precisely. It is built in the Sacred House. It is not confined by the proximities of loins and wombs. It is not contained by any courtyard, by any house, by any structure. It does not enter into the bones of any grave. It is not born of a mother and a father. Its construction takes place in the Sacred House of God. It is not so contained because it is so high. By contrast, the natural veil is born of a mother and a father in the ordinary way. Both veils are real. Both belong to the Imam. But only the lower one moves through the world of birth and death.

And so the great principle that will recur throughout the book is laid down here at the start. The one upon whom death and killing and passing away occur is the lowest of the veils — so that the soul may discharge what is owed and obligatory. The killing that befell Imam Husayn at Karbala did not touch his real self; it touched his lowest veil. The teacher is honest about the limit of the record: we do not know, from the texts, exactly which of the Imam's veils came forward at that moment to be killed. The matter is not specified. Hadrat Gyani Sahib *muqaddas* used to inquire whether anyone had read it anywhere. No one had. But for the Messenger of Allah, the structure of the veils at his passing is on record. The veil that received the ritual washing was Abdullah ibn Masud. The veil that was buried was Abdullah ibn Rawahah. The veil present for *ziyarat*, for the visitation of the tomb, is Ubayy ibn Kab. And these *are* their own veils, not borrowed garments. Ubayy ibn Kab's station is itself enormous. The Messenger himself called him "my spiritual mother." Ubayy was the first teacher who taught the Prophet, before the call, the laws and principles of Adam. Five teachers in succession taught the Prophet in those years; each of them, once their teaching was complete, became *mamnun* — under obligation — to the Prophet, because the very content they had taught had been received from him in another order of being. The final pre-call teacher was Janab-i Khadijah. She became *mamnun* to the Prophet, and she in turn became the teacher of the Father of Imams. For this reason it is said that the son of Mary is the Father of Imams; Mary is Khadijah. And for this reason Mary herself was exempted from the five prayers — her station as teacher made the obligation work in a different mode.

These veils themselves, of course, had to know who they were. Without gnosis, the work of being a veil cannot be carried. They were people of such station that, if they were to manifest, they would themselves manifest as prophets and messengers. The transmission from the Prophet to them was heart to heart. You will not find scenes in any of the books where the Prophet sits and teaches them their lessons in the way a master teaches a student in a school. In the thirteen years of Mecca the opposition was too great; in the ten years of Medina, the wars consumed the time. But the lessons came across. There is the famous incident with Ubayy ibn Kab himself. The Prophet was sitting in the prayer-niche and Ubayy was performing a supererogatory prayer. The Prophet called out, "Ya Ubayy!" Ubayy continued his prayer, and only after the second or third call cut the prayer short to come. The Prophet said, "I called you, and you did not come." Ubayy said, "O Messenger of Allah, I was praying." The Prophet said, "Who taught you this prayer?" — making the point that the call of the Messenger is greater than the prayer itself, since the prayer is itself from him. From this incident the verse came: that if Allah and His Messenger call you, you should answer the Messenger. The phrasing — answer the Messenger, rather than the doubled answer Allah and His Messenger — is itself the proof of the matter. The Prophet was also asked once by Ubayy which is the most excellent verse of the Quran — Ubayy being a great reciter. Ubayy answered, "Allah and His Messenger know best." The Prophet said: it is the Verse of the Throne.

So the first question has been answered by a refusal that becomes a doctrine, and by a correction that becomes a structure. What was before the World of Origination cannot be asked; the World of Origination is the Intellects themselves; the special veil of the Imam is built in the Sacred House and is not subject to ordinary birth, and the death that befalls the lowest veil is what discharges what is owed.

## The fall of the penitents and the cartography of the Tenth Intellect

The third movement of the first answer turns to how the heavens and the earth came to be, and from what the first *jussa* came into being. The answer opens onto a story that is also our story.

What was there before the heavens and the earth, the Dai says, was the *al-dhawat al-nadimah*, the penitent essences. From them what was derived was derived, and from them the creation of the *aflak* — the celestial spheres — was arranged. The spheres are the categories of the penitents. There were fifteen categories of these *nadimin*. From the first category the spheres were arranged; from this came the heavens and the earth. The cosmos, in this reading, is not a backdrop into which souls have been placed. The cosmos is the architecture of a regret.

And the story of the penitents is the story of how we, the believers, came to be where we are. The *Ashir*, the Tenth Intellect, commanded the souls in the order above him: I have made a mistake; you should also accept your mistake and repent. The first time, all of them refused. We do not hear, they said, and we do not obey. A second command went out, urging them once more to repent. Some of them — the *musirrin*, the insistent — held to their refusal. Others, the *mutahayyirin*, in confusion, did not know what to do. The Ashir removed both groups from the upper world. As they fell, the *nadimin* repented. They were all falling together; but among them, fifteen categories were formed. We believers are in the *fifteenth* category. Of that fifteenth, we are in the *tenth* division. Each category has ten divisions. The believers of the *sitr*, the era of concealment, occupy the very last of the last. From this one may gauge the descent: from such a height we have fallen.

And so a difficult and central question arises here. What is the reason for our falling? In what does our falling end? How low does it go? In one of our supplications, the daily prayer recited on the third — *dua yazul qadr* — we name the *darkat*, the lowest levels of hell, that we seek refuge from. All the way to the garment of *damar*, the last garment, the last seven *darkat*, from which the lava itself bursts. That is the floor. The supplication is not abstract. It is naming the actual basement of the fall and asking refuge from it.

It is in connection with this *Ashir*-knowledge that the Quranic verse "that is the determination of the Mighty, the Knowing" (chapter 36) is read. By "the Mighty, the Knowing" in that verse is meant the *Ashir*, the Tenth Intellect. It is the Ashir who knows how many *mawrs* — cycles — each troop of the fallen requires for its purification, and within each *mawr* how many *dawrs* — periods — and within each *dawr* how many Imams will come. The whole map is in his mind. This is why his station, even after his own descent, is great and vast. And here the bitterness of our condition arrives. The Ashir who now holds the map of every troop's purification was once our companion at the higher station from which we fell. The cartographer of our long road home was once one of us. He is reading us back to a place where he stood beside us — before any of this began.

The penitents in their original error did not stop at one step. The *Ashir*, who later became the Tenth, was originally the *third* Intellect. He fell because of *wahm al-musawat*, the illusion of equality. He thought he stood at the same rank as the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*, the First Emanator. But the gaze of the First Emanator was upon the *surah ilmiyyah*, the intellectual form, of the First Intellect; the gaze of the Ashir was upon the *surah amaliyyah*, the practical form. A difference in *nazar* — gaze — separates them. The nobler gaze and the lower gaze. This he did not see. The deep principle here is one to hold: no two persons can sit at the same rank. Not even two Imams; not even two duat. Two true Imams, whether they stand or sit — but their ranks differ. Hasan and Husayn were both Imams, but their ranks differed. Sayyidna Ghulam Husayn Sahab and Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad were both Dais at one time, but their ranks differed. Two *madhuns* together — their ranks will differ. To imagine equality where in truth there is differentiation, that is *wahm al-musawat*, the illusion of equality, and that is the deepest curse. The fall began there.

This is also why, when later in the believer's path he sees someone advancing very rapidly, the explanation is often that they had been established at that rank in a previous *karrah*, a previous turn, and now their progress only takes them from that point further. We see people leap ahead in our community, and at first it does not match our reckoning. But the elder used to say: we do not give opportunity on the basis of merit alone; sometimes we give it so that the believer may complete his good deeds — because the believer was already established at that rank in the previous turn, and it is our duty to bring him back to it. The leap is a return.

The disbeliever is in a different case. Associating partners with Allah is a great wrong, and Allah forgives all sins but does not forgive that association until the association itself is given up. Until they accept Islam, the disbelievers do not ascend through the categories at all. There is, however, a peculiar case treated by Sayyidna Muhammad bin Tahir in *Anwar al-Latifah*: the *uyafa*, noble people who love the family of the Prophet and who abide by the sacred law and are mindful of what is lawful and what is forbidden, whether they are non-Muslims or Muslims who have not yet entered our circle. They gradually ascend into faith. Salman al-Farsi was already from the ranks in his deeper reality, even when his outer life passed through other religions; his case is a sealed one and does not fit the general rule.

The first originated particle takes the vicegerency of the Ashir in a partial capacity. The vicegerency of the universal belongs to the *akhir mawr*, the last person of the cycle. And the noble remnants do not exist *before* the first particle. A remnant is properly that which remains of a body after the spirit and the soul have emanated from it. Before the first body, there is no remnant. The cosmos has an order, and what comes after cannot be projected backward into what came before.

The questioner closes the first question with thanks, and the prayer of the gathering is recited. The wall has been read and the map has been sketched. The fall has been named, and we have been told exactly where in the fall we stand.

## The encompassing sphere and the believer's daily intake of light

The second question, brought from Imam Ahmad bin Yusuf, takes the cosmology upward. It concerns *falak al-mustaqim* — the straight sphere, also called *falak al-muhit*, the encompassing sphere — the highest sphere, which is invisible. After this sphere the *alam al-ruhani*, the spiritual world, is to begin. The questioner asks: is the light of the sun from the great light of *falak al-mustaqim*? And what becomes of *falak al-mustaqim* at the end of the *kawr*, the cycle, when the *rubaataat*, the bonds that hold the celestial order in place, terminate? Where does it go? Where is its ascent? And what do the Imams attain from it?

The Dai sets out a principle first. The principle that governs every other ascending entity is that it must descend to the earth and then ascend. The encompassing sphere is unique in not following this principle. It ascends from its own place. Your concept, the Dai tells the questioner, is correct. At the end of the cycle, the encompassing sphere ascends straight from where it is. It enters into the tenth throne that is then prepared, and it becomes an intellect existing in actuality. Until then it had been existing in potentiality. From its potential station it had been giving help to others, particularly to the light of the sun, which was connected to it; from there the Imams also received their helps. But in the new dispensation it stands as Universal Intellect, in actuality, in its own right.

A note made by the audience at the lesson captured the underlying principle from the Quran: "they pass through; you do not pass through except with an authority" (chapter 55, verse 33). Whatever ascends waits at the *barzakh* of the Ashir until the authority comes. Though the text does not say so explicitly, the same waiting applies to the encompassing sphere. It waits, and then ascends.

The other part of the questioner's intuition is also confirmed. The light of the sun does draw from the great light of the encompassing sphere. But there is a refinement. The light of the sun also draws lights from the heart. By "heart" here is meant the House of Allah that stands opposite the *taamur*, the heavenly counterpart — the *qalb haqiqi*, the true heart, which lies beneath the outer House. The pronoun in the text is feminine, referring back to the lights of the sun. The point is that in all paths, at every moment, the sun's light receives a stream of lights from this hidden heart.

Now the answer moves to the believer's case, and to the deeper doctrine of the Imam's soul — which becomes the great teaching of the book.

When the Imam is to be transferred to the Eternal Abode, he dissolves in the heart, that is, in the House of Allah. His ascent takes place from there. He does not rise and depart; he dissolves and is taken up from that dissolution.

When a believer dies, the same House is the conduit, though the order of events differs. His luminous form goes to the higher ranks; his *riyat* go into the spheres — the sun, Jupiter, and so on. His *fuzala*, his excellences, ascend; while ascending they are called *zuubat*, and then become *zakhair*, treasures. Their ascent passes through twenty-eight stations. They pause at the prayer-niche where in life the believer used to stand to pray — ascending to that point of his prayer in the mosque. From the prayer-niche they pass to the center of his island — which, for us of these regions, is the tomb of Sayyidna Sulayman. From there the ascent continues all the way to the House of Allah, where the four pillars stand. The pillars are the *atqiyah*, the pious ones, in physical form. The believer's excellences travel up to the Black Stone corner, and from there their ascent into the upper world is completed.

Now the deeper teaching. When the Imam is about to pass, he dissolves in the House, and becomes substance and strength for the hearts of the learned ones from the ranks. The strength of speech, the strength of benefiting others, the right decisions, the safety from sin — all that the ranks receive after the Imam's passing is this substance from the dissolved heart of the Imam. This is the meaning of the verse of Joseph: "had he not seen the proof of his Lord" (chapter 12, verse 24). The proof of the Lord that protected Joseph from the wife of his master is precisely this substance received from the Imam. When the ranks themselves come to pass, that substance reconnects with the pure heart, free of impurities, and returns to its origin. The substance given them in life was a trust for their service; at their passing, the trust is returned. They are masters of those states, but only temporarily; the substance does not stay with them but flows back to the heart.

And from the very same heart, the next Imam's soul is formed. The soul of the Imam of the East and the West is supplied from what comes to him out of the heart, from the water around it, from a portion of the marshes adjacent, and from what was stored for him out of what dissolved at the beginning of creation. A stream from the heart, a stream from the water around it, a stream from the marshes, and a stream from what has been stored from the first interval — the era of creation onwards. Sayyidna Hibatullah bin Sayyidna Ibrahim has set all this out in his treatise *Saqilat al-Quloob min al-Fikr* — also called *al-Munawwirah lil-Basair* and *al-Musfiyah lil-Suwar* — in the answer to its twelfth question, with the clearest of explanations and the most evident of proofs.

The Dai now sets out a second mode of explaining the same teaching, in comparison to the *shahadah*. In the Imam are *nasut* and *lahut* — the two poles parallel the negation and affirmation of the *shahadah*. The *nasut* is the collection of *riyat*. When a believer dies, the *riyat* that emerge immediately, before burial, are the higher *riyat*; from these are formed the shadow-forms of the successors. The *riyat* that emerge in the three days after burial — and only for three days — form the natural form. After three days, what remain are vapors only.

Here a practical guidance follows. A relative of the deceased should give charity, conduct *fatiha-khwani*, *sadaqallah*, *beeswa khwani*, hire someone to read Quran, give meals, within those three days. The *riyat* are giving notice to the family in a subtle sense: whatever you intend to do for me, do in these three days; after that, I am gone for a thousand years. Whatever you do later will be for the noble form and for the excellences, not for the *riyat*. The Prophet's guidance to bury the dead quickly is wise on its own; but the three-day window for the family's offerings is a window not to be wasted.

The purified *riyat* that are ascending do not stop in the sky. They have a long process. They go first to the moon, then to Jupiter, then back to the sun, then back again to Jupiter; their main station is the moon. The bright shining on the thirteenth, fourteenth, and fifteenth nights is said to be from the great congregation of the *riyat* that gathers there. Then, like drops of dew, the purified *riyat* descend on uncooked fruits — apples and things like them, things that are not cooked. The Imam and his wife eat these, and from this nourishment the child of the Imam is conceived and is born. And when the child is born, his nourishment is also *riyat* — though he appears outwardly to drink milk and later to eat ordinary food, in inner reality he is being nourished on *riyat*. Imam Jafar al-Sadiq alluded to this to his bab al-abwab, Mufaddal bin Umar. When Mufaddal came to him and the Imam was eating, the Imam said, "Join me." Mufaddal said, "Mawlana, I have just eaten." The Imam said: what you ate, we do not eat. And what we eat, you have not eaten. He meant: your food is external food; ours is *riyat*. Join me in *riyat*.

The believer too receives *riyat*. He receives them in three places. First, during ablution: when he says the *Bismillah*, when he takes a breath, when water touches his body, when water enters the pores — these moments transmit excellences and *riyat* to him from the purified *riyat* gathered in the moon. This is why completing the ablution thoroughly is of high merit. The atonements for major sins are several, and performing the ablution in full while the temperament is cold is among them. The coldness meant is not only external cold; it includes the coldness of disposition, the laziness that makes the limbs unwilling. Performing the ablution thoroughly in *that* condition is what is meant. The believer is also instructed, after the ablution, to shake the water off his hands — from each drop shaken, an angel is created who prays for his forgiveness. Reciting blessings on the Prophet, and then *Inna anzalna* (chapter 97) after ablution, makes the prayer to follow be one that is accepted.

The second place is *sahri*, the pre-dawn meal. The third is *iftar*, the breaking of the fast. The *riyat* received at these are of a high order; this is why *sahri* should be taken at the very last moment — not at two in the night — and *iftar* should be hurried, not delayed for prayers or other affairs. Hurry to receive what is being given. The *riyat* the believer receives in these stations are not his own — he has not yet passed away. They are the purified *riyat* of the believers gathered in the moon, and from there they reach him.

It was at this point in the lesson that the well-known story of Avicenna and his student arose. The student used always to say to him — known here as Sayyidna Ali ibn Sina, a great *dai* and *hujjat* — that "if you were to claim prophethood, no one would deny you." Avicenna would smile and not answer. Once they were traveling in Iran during snowfall, and at the time of sunset prayer they came upon a man in the distance who was breaking the snow with his hands to gather water for his ablution. Avicenna turned to the student: "You always ask me why I do not claim prophethood. Here is the answer. Three hundred years have passed since the Messenger of Allah, and in his sacred law, in his sacred self, what effect is still in this man — who without caring for his own life is breaking snow to perform ablution?" And the night before, in a traveler's inn, Avicenna had been thirsty and had asked his student to bring him water from the pot in the veranda, and the student had said, "Mawlana, you can drink in the morning; it is cold now." So Avicenna closed: "My state is such that I could not get my own dear student to bring me water; and three hundred years after the Messenger this man breaks snow to perform ablution. That is the answer to your question." A small story; an enormous measurement of authority.

The Dai now gives the comparison with the *shahadah* more strictly. The *nasut* and the *lahut* are negation and affirmation. The phrase "to the Possessor of Might and Power" is what the *shahadah* affirms. The Imam has three veils as fundamental veils, though other veils take work as needed, and these three veils are the example of the three jewels of the *shahadah*: the natural form, the shadow form, and the special form. Then comes a teaching that organizes our understanding of the Imams. Every Imam from our master Husayn has combined four ranks. With Hasan, there were two ranks — of Imamate and succession — and with Husayn the other two. In Hasan's time the higher two were with Hasan, and he was thus the Imam; Husayn had the two lower, and he was thus under Hasan. The same pattern had run in the past: in the progeny of Abraham, Ishmael had the two higher ranks and Isaac had the two lower, and so Ishmael was the Imam of his time and Isaac the prophet.

After Husayn the four ranks came together permanently with the Imams. But strictly speaking, the first Imam in whom the four ranks were combined in the full sense was Ali Zayn al-Abidin — with Husayn the two lower ranks had still dominated over the higher, and even being Imam, he was dominated. So Ali Zayn al-Abidin is called the Adam of the family of the Prophet. Just as Adam was the first man, Ali Zayn al-Abidin is the first man of the family of the Prophet in the fully combined sense. The Prophet, of course, had all four ranks himself. With him, however, the two higher were preserved as a trust. After Mawlana Abd al-Muttalib, the four ranks with him were divided: he gave the two higher to Abu Talib and the two lower — since Abdullah had passed — were preserved for the Messenger as a grandson's inheritance. Abu Talib, in turn, gave all four to the Messenger; the two higher he gave were a trust for the Father of Imams. "Of whomsoever I am master, this Ali is his master" — the meaning is: the ranks Allah made me master of, the Father of Imams is now master. The transmission was the fulfillment of a trust.

The four ranks parallel the four sections of the *shahadah*. And the seventh of the Imams is the station of rest. In him the matters of the previous six are gathered, and so the seventh has nothing more to do. He is the gatherer. Six Imams' work comes to rest in him. The parallel in the *shahadah* is the seven *fusul*, the seven places where the writing pen lifts off the paper as one writes the *shahadah* in the Arabic style. At those seven places the pen lifts — and so the seventh Imam is the rest of the writing. Finally, the Imam is the gatherer of the forms of the twelve islands in the end. Twelve islands, twelve duat, twelve noble hearts — every island itself being a heart. The parallel is the twelve letters of the great *shahadah*. And when we sum: two (Hasan and Husayn) plus three (the three veils) plus four (the four ranks) plus seven (the seven Imams) plus twelve (the twelve islands) equals twenty-eight. This is the eternal order of the higher world. The number 28 returns at every level of the doctrine — the believer's excellences pass through twenty-eight stations on ascent.

A side-question is raised here about whether the encompassing sphere, which alone ascends without descending to earth, goes through the *barzakh* of the Ashir before its ascent. The text does not say so explicitly, but the verse already cited deduces it. It must wait, like all ascending things, for the authority of the Ashir before passing.

## What this episode lands

Two questions; two answers; one architecture. The first answer was almost entirely a refusal — the wall before the Originator's act, the forbidden inquiry into the modality of emanation, the warning that even the First Intellects cannot peer back behind themselves. The second answer was almost entirely a map — the encompassing sphere's unique upward path, the Imam's dissolution into the House, the substance flowing into the ranks and back again, the believer's daily intake of *riyat* at his ablution and his meals, the number twenty-eight returning at the close as the order of the higher world. Between the wall and the map, two further teachings have been set down. The illusion of equality is the deepest curse — no two persons sit at the same rank — and this is the original wound of the Tenth Intellect's fall. And the killing that befalls the Imam touches only the lowest of his veils, while the highest is built in the Sacred House and is not subject to ordinary birth or death. The book has now finished laying down its frame. The questions that follow will draw on every line of what these two questions established.
