# Doctrinal Synthesis, Supplementary Expositions, and the Last Word

## Where the chapter opens

After the lectures have opened the family of light, named the duat (دُعَاة, the summoners, missionaries), traced the cycle, set the disciplines, and distinguished the two Mehdis, one work remains: to gather the structure whole and draw out the doctrines that need final precision.

The remaining work is not to add a new doctrine. The remaining work is, first, to see what has been built — to step back from the lectures and let their structure show itself as one. The doctrine of the cycle, the doctrine of concealment, the doctrine of the heart's three cavities, the doctrine of the numerical lattice, the doctrine of the karrah (كَرَّة, the return) and the mawr (مَوْر, the passing) — these were given as the lectures permitted. The synthesis takes the lectures together and shows what they together establish. That is the first movement.

Then comes the second movement: a concise closing orientation toward the Dai (دَاعِي, summoner, missionary), the Imam, and the line.

And then a third movement: the supplementary expositions, the doctrines that bear further setting out. The five tones of the shahadah (شَهَادَة, testimony of faith) and the place of Jonah. The dark valley before Ghadir and the prolonged lightning. The two brothers of Imam Husayn and the structural priority of Ali Zayn al-Abidin. The five teachers of the Prophet before the call. Salman as the double gate. The noble death and the bad death. The steam from the pot. The mansub (مَنْسُوب, appointed officer) of Yemen. The three jazair (جَزَائِر, the islands). The closing movement returns all of this to the believer's lived orientation.

Three movements: synthesis, closing orientation, last setting-out. We take them in order.

## The synthesis

The synthesis is not a recapitulation. It does not number the teachings and move on. It does something subtler: it names six principles that the lectures together establish, and lets the principles do the seeing-together. The principles are not new content; they are the structural shape that the content has been taking all along. The believer who has followed the lectures with attention finds, when the principles are named, that he had been carrying them implicitly.

The first principle is the principle of the two hierarchies. The teaching has continually held two parallel hierarchies in view. The first is the hierarchy of the Aimmah (أَئِمَّة, the Imams): the Pure Imams in whom complete protection from error is realized, descending from Husayn through the Fatimid Imams, culminating in the present Imam and, in the future, in the Qaim (قَائِم, the Riser). The second is the hierarchy of the the summoners, missionaries: the summoners in whom partial protection is realized, connected (مَوْصُول) through Hasan, carrying the call across the eras of concealment, culminating in the summoner, missionary of the present time. The two hierarchies are not in competition. They are complementary. The the Imams carry the Imamate. The the summoners, missionaries carry the summoning. The the Imams are veiled in the era of concealment. The the summoners, missionaries are visible. The believer's straight path runs through the the summoners, missionaries to the the Imams; the the summoners, missionaries are the visible portion of that chain; the the Imams are the veiled portion. Stored wisdom is for the the summoners, missionaries because they are the ones in partial protection who need treasures to draw from; the the Imams do not need stored treasures because their substance is direct.

The second principle is the discipline of boundaries. Five boundaries hold the doctrine in place. The boundary of speculation: no inquiry into the *how* of the originating act, because the intellect has no scope there; speculation in that region is assigning a partner and is destruction. The boundary of broadcasting: no carrying of the inner doctrine beyond those prepared to receive it, on pain of the seat in fire. The boundary of giving: no transfer of the *aqiqa (عَقِيقَة, birth-rite sacrifice)* meat or the Eid al-Adha meat to those outside the structural channel. The boundary of slaughter: no consumption of animals that died of themselves in the wild, with the exception of the pigeons of the Sacred House and Medina. The boundary of imitation: no taking of the Prophet's total charity as personal model — the middle path is for us, the extremes belong to the protected. These are not arbitrary restrictions. They are the structural conditions under which the doctrine can be carried forward across the cycles. Without them, the doctrine dilutes; with them, it is preserved at the depth where it continues to feed the line.

The third principle is the pattern of the structural numbers. Twenty-eight and twenty-nine have been traced at length; the synthesis adds the smaller numbers that run through the body (جسّة) of the teaching. Three: three veils of the Imam, three jewels of the shahada, three cavities of the heart, three days of the offerings after burial, three days of the Qaim's manifestation, three intermediary ancestors of the Prophet. Four: four ranks of prophethood, mission, Imamate, succession; four sections of the shahada; four pillars of the House; four doors of the journey. Seven: seven Imams of the station of rest, seven sections of the shahada's writing, seven seekings-of-forgiveness after the Throne Verse, seven cycles of concealment, seven verses of the Opening, seven great intellects, seven tests of the Prophet before the mission and seven after. Twelve: twelve islands, twelve the summoners, missionaries, twelve hearts, twelve letters of the word of testimony, twelve the summoners, missionaries of the presences, twelve the summoners, missionaries of the islands, twelve consecutive prayer-cycles of the night-prayer. Twenty-five: twenty-five words of the Opening, twenty-five prophets named in the Quran. Twenty-eight: two plus three plus four plus seven plus twelve equals twenty-eight, the eternal order; twenty-eight letters of the word of testimony; twenty-eight stations of the believer's ascent; twenty-eight letters of the Quranic alphabet; the twenty-eighth in any sequence as gatherer; twenty-eight titles of the first designation (نصّ) of Dawud bin Ajabshah. Twenty-nine: twenty-nine of the abbreviated letter-openings; the twenty-ninth in any sequence as breaker; the phrase *Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah (اللّٰه)* in the twenty-ninth verse of the chapter of the Opening Victory. Three hundred and sixty: three hundred and sixty manifestations of the integrated child per cycle; three hundred and sixty subtle bodies in the human form. Forty hundred thousand multiplied by forty hundred thousand: that is one great cycle. Fifty thousand: the years of this present era of the the Riser. These numbers are not numerology in the loose sense. They are the structural countings of the doctrine's elements. When the believer encounters one of them, he can usually find the structural element it indexes. The believer who has internalized the lattice has internalized the doctrine at the structural level.

The fourth principle is the triad of disciplines that organizes the believer's life: faith, action, and manners. Faith is the inner orientation toward the chain — the heart set on the Imam, the summoner, missionary, the structural ranks. It is not abstract belief; it is structural orientation. Action is the embodied expression of that orientation — the prayer, the fast, the alms, the pilgrimage, the giving of charity, the attending of the gathering, the taking of the covenant (عَهْد). Manners are the small disciplines that maintain the connection — how one stands in the presence of the summoner, missionary, how one greets a fellow believer, how one handles the *birth-rite sacrifice* meat, how one performs the ablution, how one closes the invocation of the Name. The three together constitute the believer's life. Faith without action is empty; action without manners is graceless; manners without faith are theatrical. The triad must hold. This is also why so many practical rulings stand alongside the cosmological doctrines. The doctrines are not for cognition alone; they are for the action and the manners. The believer who receives the doctrine of the Imam's three veils is also receiving the doctrine of how to perform the praising of the Lord with attention to *God is greater*, *Glory be to God*, and the essence of the Imam. The cognition and the practice run together.

The fifth principle is the continuity of the line. The Imam's soul dissolves, the substance enters the ranks, the ranks return the substance, the next Imam is formed. The mechanism runs across generations. The line, structurally, is never broken. The the summoners, missionaries are named in the praise — Sulayman, Jafar the seventh, Muhammad bin Fuad as the Mim (مِيم, the letter Mim), Ali bin Sulayman as the Ayn (عَيْن, the letter Ayn), Ibrahim the traveler, Muhammad bin Ismail the emigrant — and the names together carry the line. The believer connects through his junior summoner, his breaker, his summoner, missionary; the connection is his straight path; the connection is preserved across his life and, through the cyclic return, into the next. The continuity is therefore at every level: in the formation of the Imam's soul, in the naming of the the summoners, missionaries, in the believer's chain, in the return. The cycle and the line work together. The standard reference history of the call confirms the structural reading from the outside: across the era of partial protection, the line preserved itself by the mechanism the doctrine describes, with the the summoners, missionaries carrying the visible portion and the Imams the veiled portion, and the believer's allegiance to his own rung the active means of preservation. [Farhad Daftary, *The Ismailis: Their History and Doctrines*, 2nd ed. (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2007), pp. 128–131.] This is the structural answer to whether the line can be lost. It cannot. The mechanism is too deeply embedded. The the summoners, missionaries may face the Pharaoh of the age; the believers may face persecution; the discipline of concealment may be severe — but the line itself, by structural necessity, continues. The believer's role is to participate in the continuity, not to worry about it. The continuity does not depend on his worry; it depends on his participation. His daily prayer, his manners, his action, his faith — these are his participation. The line takes care of itself through the participation of its members.

The sixth and final principle is the doctrine of the end and the return. The end is the great cycle, of which nine remain; at the end of the ninth, all are absorbed into the First Emanator, and only the domain of the First Intellect and the First Emanator remains. The return is the cycle of the thousand years; each believer returns in the same rank, in the same place; the cycle is structurally fair. The two together orient the believer's life. He is not in an endless drift; the great cycle has its end. He is not in a single life; the return gives him return. His effort in this cyclic visit shapes his rank in the next; his ranks across the visits compound; the compound brings him, over the great cycles, toward the final absorption. The believer who has internalized this orientation is the believer whose decisions are made on the right scale. He is not deciding for this lifetime alone; he is deciding for the cyclic returns and the great cycles. The disciplines that feel difficult in the lifetime are easy at the scale of the great cycle. The luxuries that feel necessary in the lifetime are nothing at the scale of the great cycle. The orientation transforms the felt weights. The modern reader of Ismaili cosmology has described this scaling of action to the great cycle as the doctrine in which the believer's measure of his own life is taken not from the bounded interval of one lifetime but from the unbounded horizon of the cyclic return. [Henry Corbin, *Cyclical Time and Ismaili Gnosis*, trans. Ralph Manheim and James W. Morris (London: Kegan Paul International, 1983), pp. 96–99.] The stored wisdom is scaled to the great cycle. The the summoners, missionaries draw from it at the scale at which they must serve. The believers receive what the the summoners, missionaries distribute at the scale appropriate to their position. The whole is scaled to the cycle that contains it.

Six principles. Two hierarchies, the discipline of boundaries, the lattice of numbers, the triad of life, the continuity, the end and the return. This is the synthesis: not a list of doctrines but a structural shape of the whole. The believer who carries the six can read any of the doctrines anew through them.

## The closing orientation

The closing orientation is brief and direct: the stored wisdom has been displayed within the limits of what may be displayed; the line has been named; the cosmology, the heart, the House, the letters, the cyclic return, the thousand-year cycle, the great cycle, the awaited figures, and the daily disciplines have been set out. The believer closes by praying for the summoner, missionary, for the Imam, for the protection and elevation of both, and for the People of the House. The prayer returns the doctrine to the chain.

## The supplementary expositions

The supplementary mode sets out more fully a handful of teachings the lectures had touched in passing. This is itself a mode of teaching: returning to what has been said and giving it the small additional turn that lets it settle properly.

Consider the five tones of the testimony. The word of testimony has appeared in five tones across the Quran: *there is no god but Allah (اللّٰه)*, *there is no god but He*, *there is no god but I*, *there is no god but the One who*, and *there is no god but You*. Of these, *there is no god but You* has come only once — in the supplication of Jonah, peace be upon him, when he was in the depth of the sea, in the belly of the great fish, in a darkness within darkness within darkness. The verse runs, in the Yusuf (يُوسُف) Ali rendering at verse eighty-seven of the chapter on the Prophets, *He cried through the depths of darkness: 'There is no god but Thou: glory to Thee: I was indeed wrong!'* [The Holy Quran, ch. 21 verse 87, trans. Abdullah Yusuf (يُوسُف) Ali (Beltsville: Amana Publications, 1989), p. 240.] *There is no god but You; glory be to You; indeed I was among the wrongdoers.* The teacher would say: this is the formula of the soul in the deepest enclosure. The other tones keep the divine in the third or the first person; *but You* addresses the divine directly, in the second person, in vocative directness. That directness is the mark of the depth. The formula has come once because the situation calling it forth is rare. The believer in his own depths — the darknesses within darknesses — may reach for it. It is not for the ordinary moment; it is for the moment in the belly of the fish. And the deliverance of Jonah from the fish is the consequence: the depth was opened by the wrongs; the depth was closed by the formula; the deliverance was given because the formula was rightly spoken. The depth is not opened by mere endurance; it is opened by the formula spoken from the depth.

Consider the dark valley before Ghadir. A companion of the prophet, in her closeness, was told the secret about the succession of Ali; she did not keep it; the secret reached the hypocrites; they decided to prevent the declaration by killing the Prophet in the dark valley on the road to Ghadir. Allah (اللّٰه) intervened directly. The lightning that flashed in the valley was not ordinary lightning. It lasted longer than the natural lightning lasts. It lit the dark valley as if it were noon. The Prophet and Hudhayfa al-Yamani saw together, and in the seeing they recognized the faces of those who had gathered. The recognition was the preservation. The conspirators, once their faces were seen, withdrew. The killing did not proceed. The Prophet passed through the valley and arrived at Ghadir; the declaration was made. Hudhayfa's later oath — that he had been among them — is the witness preserved. The structural meaning is precise: at the moment when the line is most threatened, the line is most directly preserved. The believer who finds himself, in his own life, at a moment of crisis around the chain may pray for a similar intervention. It may not come as lightning; it may come in another form. But the principle holds: at the moment of greatest threat, the line is most directly preserved by the divine attention.

Consider the two brothers, Hasan and Husayn. In every Imam, all four ranks are present — prophethood, mission, Imamate, succession. The two higher are Imamate and succession; the two lower are prophethood and mission. In Hasan's time, the two higher were manifest with Hasan as the Imam; the two lower were manifest with Husayn under his brother's authority. In Husayn's time, after Hasan's passing, all four came together with Husayn — but the two lower dominated over the two higher in his own being. Husayn was the Imam, but his outward office — the bearing of the public responsibility, the standing against Yazid, the procession to Karbala, the martyrdom — was a manifestation of the two lower. The two higher were present but veiled by the two lower. The first Imam in whom all four were combined with the two higher *dominating* was Ali Zayn al-Abidin. He is therefore called the Adam of the Family of the Cloak — the first in the family of the Prophet in the fully-combined-with-higher-dominating sense. The foundational Ismaili compendium gathers the hadith of the cloak and the verse of purification as the structural anchor of the household's identification, preserving the corroborating chains across both witnesses. [the book *The Pillars of Islam*, trans. Asaf A. A. Fyzee, rev. Ismail K. Poonawala (New Delhi: Oxford University Press, 2002), vol. 1, pp. 22–25.] This is a precise resolution of what might otherwise be a tension. Husayn is, doctrinally, the Imam; the line of the the Imams passes through him; but the internal structure of his Imamate had the two lower dominating, which is part of the meaning of his being the one who manifested the occultation through the martyrdom. The martyrdom was the work of the two lower ranks; the two higher remained protected within the two lower. From Ali Zayn al-Abidin forward, the structure is the dominance of the higher over the lower. The pattern thereafter is settled.

Consider the five teachers of the Prophet before the call. He had five teachers in succession. The first was Ubayy ibn Kab, who taught him Adam's laws. The last was Khadijah. The three between taught the laws of Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus — the five speakers of the previous era. The Prophet's full inheritance of the line is therefore constituted by these five. Each teacher, after teaching, became indebted-in-return to the Prophet, because what he taught had been received from the speaker in another order of being; the indebted one is not a debtor in the ordinary sense but an inheritor of the connection. Khadijah, the last teacher before the call, became indebted-in-return to the Prophet and then became the teacher of Ali. This double-relation is what makes her place so unusual. She is teacher and indebted-in-return simultaneously; she carries Ali's instruction; she is the Mary of the inner reading, with Ali as the son-of-Mary in our doctrine. Her exemption from the five prayers follows from this teacher-relation: a teacher of the speaker and of the deputy operates in a different mode from the ordinary ritual obligation. The doctrine of the five teachers also explains why the call was not an interruption from outside the Prophet's preparation but the manifestation of what had been gradually built. The teachers prepared the vessel. The call announced what the vessel already held.

Consider Salman the Persian. He is the only person in the era of concealment who became the supreme gate for both the Prophet and his deputy. There is normally one supreme gate per office. Salman is the exception. The doctrine of the exception is that his inner reality was already constituted in a way that allowed the double office. His outward biography passed through other religions, but his inner reality was already from the ranks of the line. By the time he came to the Prophet, he was prepared to serve at an office that ordinarily would have required a different individual. This is also why, in our community, he is honored as he is. *Salman is from us, the People of the House.* The Prophet's statement is not a courtesy; it is a doctrinal recognition. Salman was already from the People of the House in the inner reading; the Prophet's statement makes the inner reading visible. The lesson is that the line of the ranks extends, in its inner reality, into figures whose outward biographies pass through other communities. The believer should be cautious about drawing the line too tightly around the persons of his own immediate community. Salman, Aristotle, the confidants of Jesus — these are reminders that the essential beings of the line are wider than the individual persons we can see in the present.

Consider the discipline of the body (جسّة) in death. The speaking soul absorbs the sensitive soul through life. The absorption is gradual. By the time of death, the absorption is more or less complete depending on the discipline of the life. If complete, the speaking soul departs with the sensitive soul gathered within it; the body (جسّة) is left without sensation; the corpse does not linger as a sentient thing. This is the noble death. If incomplete, the sensitive soul remains in the body (جسّة) at death; the corpse retains some residue of sensation; the death is not the clean separation it should have been. This is the bad death. The remedy across the lifetime is the discipline of praising — *God is greater*, *All praise belongs to God*, *Glory be to God*, *There is no god but Allah (اللّٰه)*. These phrases, especially *All praise belongs to God*, work on the sensitive soul; they gradually orient the sensations toward praise; they gradually carry the sensitive soul into the speaking soul's frame. By the end of life, the believer who has practiced the praising has gathered the sensitive soul substantially. This is also why our funeral disciplines pay attention to what is done in the three days. The opening-recitations and the other offerings work on what remains; the family takes up what the deceased could not complete. The doctrine does not condemn; it organizes our response. And there is a kitchen-discipline of the same shape: the steam from the cooked meat must be allowed to escape. The slaughter has separated the sensitive substance from the animal by the *God is greater*, but the separation is not always complete at the moment of slaughter. The cooking opens the body (جسّة) further; the heat releases the remainder; the remainder departs through the steam. If the pot is sealed tightly, the substance may be trapped in the food and carried into the eater. The discipline therefore requires that some opening be left. A small rule of the kitchen carrying the same structural weight as the slaughter itself.

Consider the place of Yemen. The summit-officer of Yemen, of Najran — the one ranked below the summoner, missionary but above the breaker and the junior summoner — is uniquely elevated. He is the Dai's successor in potentiality. When the summoner, missionary passes, the noble designation (نصّ) may fall on him, elevating him to the summoner, missionary. The structural potentiality confers a present elevation: his station today is above the station of the other officers because his future, if the designation (نصّ) falls on him, is to be summoner, missionary. The future office, in our doctrine, casts a present shadow. The believers of Najran, in particular, honor him with the depth this dual position requires. And this connects to the three islands — Yemen, India, and Sindh — in which the summoner, missionary of Yemen leads prayer; he does not lead prayer in the other islands. The three are connected (مَوْصُول) in a special way through his office; the other islands have their own structures. For the believer in India or Sindh, the connection to Yemen is therefore not merely sentimental. It is structural. He is in the Dai's prayer-circle in a way the believer in another island is not. The exception of the summoner, missionary leading prayer outside the three was given only to Sayyidna (سَيِّدُنَا, our master) Muhammad bin Ismail, for Habasha, for the loftiness of his station. The rule is precise.

The doctrines do not yield to a single pass. The structural numbers come back at different levels. The same teaching is opened from one direction in an early section and from another direction in a later one. The Imam's three veils in the second question return as the three jewels of the testimony, return again as the three cavities of the heart, return again as the three layers of the human realm. The Commander of the Faithful had taught the same posture toward the deep word — that the words have a limit but the meanings have none — and the fifth-century compiler of his aphorisms preserves it among the sayings the rank-holders place behind every commentary on durable speech. [the book *A Treasury of Virtues: Sayings, Sermons, and Teachings of Ali*, ed. and trans. Tahera Qutbuddin (New York: NYU Press, 2013), §28, p. 16.] This is the nature of the truths. They are not propositions to be memorized; they are structures to be inhabited. The doctrine is not in the words; it is in what the words point to. The pointing requires attention. The disciplines of ablution and the call and the pre-dawn meal and the breaking of the fast and slaughter and the seventh-day offering and visitation shape the believer's days. The cosmology of the cyclic return and the great cycle shapes the believer's sense of time. The doctrine of the straight path as one's own chain shapes the believer's relation to those above him in the hierarchy.

The opening praise itself is doctrine: *All praise is for Allah (اللّٰه), the Great, who manifested a special human form from the gardens of the people of specialty.* We are the gardens. The Prophet ascended through what we, in our small way, contributed to. The line that runs from the Prophet down through the the Imams and the the summoners, missionaries passes also through us. We are not bystanders to the line; we are constituents of its substance. To know this is to receive the teaching at the right level.

There is one more thing the supplementary mode does that is worth naming. By returning to teachings that were touched earlier, it teaches the *manner* of return — that one comes back to a teaching with a small new turn each time, that one does not hear the testimony of Jonah the same way after carrying the doctrine of the noble death, that one does not hear the dark valley the same way after carrying the doctrine of structural continuity. The supplementary expositions are not afterthoughts but instruction in how durable teachings are carried.

## What the chapter lands

The chapter lands three things. First, the structural shape of the teaching in six principles: two hierarchies, the discipline of boundaries, the lattice of numbers, the triad of faith and action and manners, the continuity of the line, and the end and the return. Second, the closing orientation toward the summoner, missionary, the Imam, and the line. Third, the supplementary mode itself: the doctrines that bear further setting out, from the singular tone of Jonah to the dark valley before Ghadir to the structural priority of Ali Zayn al-Abidin to the five teachers to Salman to the noble death to Yemen — and a final reflection on how durable teachings are returned to and carried.

## Closing

The treasures will yield what they will yield. The discipline of concealment will hold what it must hold. The line will continue across the cyclic returns and the great cycles. The believer participates in the line by his prayer, his action, his manners, his faith. The Mehdi will come at his time; the the Riser will manifest in the three days of his office; the believers will perform the cyclic return in the thousand-year cycle; at the end of the ninth great cycle remaining, all will be absorbed into the First Emanator. Until then, the believers participate. The the summoners, missionaries draw from the treasures. The chain runs through us, and we through it, and the chain — preserved — will, at its end, bring us to where the chain itself ends.
