# Lectures Twelve, Fourteen, Fifteen (continued)

## Where this chapter picks up

What if every motion the pilgrim makes during his pilgrimage, the standing at Arafat, the move at sunset to Muzdalifah, the throwing of stones at Mina, is not, on the inside, a sequence of separate rites at separate sites, but a walk through three figures of the line, in the very order in which the inner core of the era of concealment was constituted? And what if the final accounting that the believer has been told to expect at the end of time, the gathering, the slaying of the opposers, the dispersal, is not a long event spread across centuries, but a compressed three days, the work of one figure to whom one office is handed for the duration? The teaching now opens the geography of the Hajj, the three days of the Riser, and the rolling up of the heaven at the end of every cycle, and lets the architecture of the line stand at its horizon.

Three bodies of doctrine are gathered here. The first reads the geography of the Hajj as the geography of the inner core of the era of concealment, Arafat to the Messenger, Muzdalifah to Fatimah, Mina to the Commander Of Believers, and follows the consequences of that mapping into the luminous forms on Arafat, the plot against the Messenger's life before the pool of Khumm, the hidden treasures at Arafat that the discipline of not cutting trees protects, the verse against the broadcasting of the secrets of the call, the doctrine of the designation as a structural recapitulation of the testimony of faith, the doubly-entrusted title of one of the great dais of the line's middle generations, the gathering at the lesson that includes the departed, the brief invisible visits of an earlier dai to the gathering of a later one, and the doctrine that today's Imam, the thirty-fifth, gathers fourteen Imams within himself. The second names the three days of the Riser as the compressed work of the final accounting and reads the daily prayer of the believer as the chain of the part-duat in which he stands, then gives the precise rate of spiritual progress, three hundred and sixty souls in the body, one part per great cycle, and the two types of spiritual progress, the type that is striven for and the type that is obtained by sitting in the lap of a teacher. The third gives the rolling up of the heaven at the end of each great cycle, the answer the fourth Imam withheld from the questioner, one great round has passed, nine remain, and the final absorption of all that is into the First Sender.

## The geography of the Hajj

The teacher's most striking teaching in Lecture 12 is the correspondence of the figures of the era of concealment to the geography of the pilgrimage. The Messenger corresponds to the mountain of Arafat. Fatimah corresponds to Muzdalifah. The Commander Of Believers corresponds to Mina. The three sites of the pilgrimage are the three figures of the inner core of the era of concealment. The inner core of the era of concealment is constituted by these three. The teacher allows the mapping to land. The pilgrim, in his pilgrimage, is moving through the inner geography of our line. He stands at Arafat: he is standing at the Messenger. He moves to Muzdalifah: he is moving to Fatimah. He moves to Mina: he is moving to the Commander Of Believers. The motions of the pilgrimage are the motions of the inner pilgrimage through the line.

This is also why the rituals at each site are weighted with the inner reality. At Arafat, the camel of the Messenger would sit down under the weight of the descending revelation. The revelation would come heavy on Arafat. The pilgrim who stands at Arafat is standing where the revelation came; he is opening himself to receive what the place still carries. On the mountain of Arafat, luminous forms manifested that had never occurred to a human heart. The intensity was the more profound because of what was unfolding regarding the declaration at the pool of Khumm. The forms were of an order that the prophets before had not seen. The intensity, the teacher said, was the intensity of the imminent declaration: the Messenger was, on this Arafat, about to issue the declaration that would set the line for centuries. The luminous forms gathered around the imminent declaration.

The plot against the Messenger's life before the pool of Khumm is part of the same geography. The secret of the designation of the Commander Of Believers had reached, through one of the wives, into the hands of those who would oppose it. A group of the hypocrites had planned to kill the Messenger in a dark valley before the pool of Khumm. The plot was real; the threat was real. At the dark valley, lightning flashed, prolonged. The flashes lasted longer than ordinary lightning. The Messenger and the companion who saw the prolonged lightning, riding together, saw the faces of the would-be killers in the prolonged light. The seeing was the preservation; the killing did not succeed. The would-be killers withdrew, knowing they had been seen. This is the moment of providence that the doctrine remembers. The companion's later oath to one of the figures who would press him for the names, that he had been among the conspirators, is the witness to the moment. The conspirators' faces were preserved in the companion's memory; the oath sealed the memory in history.

The doctrine of the hidden treasures at Arafat, the deposits that the place itself holds, explains the prohibition on cutting trees in Arafat. The trees neighbor the treasures. To cut a tree near a treasure is to disturb the treasure's neighborhood. The discipline preserves the treasures by preserving their neighbors. The pilgrim who walks at Arafat under the standing trees is walking past treasures whose presence the trees themselves are protecting. The discipline of the pilgrimage is not exhausted by its visible motions; it is, at every step, the honoring of an inner geography that the outer eye does not see.

The verse *whoever broadcasts the secrets of the call has prepared his seat in the fire and is disavowed by the Mighty and the Forgiving* is the verse of the discipline of concealment. The seat in the fire is the structural consequence of the broadcasting; the disavowal by the Mighty and the Forgiving is the second consequence. The Mighty and the Forgiving is the divine attribute that combines the strict and the merciful. To be disavowed by the combined attribute is to be cut off from both the strict and the merciful. There is no remaining channel. The discipline of the line is therefore, for the carrier of the secrets, a discipline of survival as much as a discipline of fidelity. The carrier who broadcasts is not corrected by another channel; the channels are closed by the same verse.

The doctrine of the first designation by one of the dais of the middle line on his successor, the designation written by the scribe of the time, using twenty-eight titles that correspond to the twenty-eight letters of the testimony of faith, is the doctrine of the designation as a structural recapitulation. The twenty-eight titles are not decorative; they enact the twenty-eight letters; the designation is, in its very form, the testimony's own form. Whoever recites the testimony after this designation, the masters of the line established, must also bear testimony for the recipient of the designation. The structural recapitulation has made the testimony itself widen to include the designation-recipient. This is itself a precise doctrine of how the designation operates. The designation is not an external naming. It is a structural enactment that, when properly performed, draws the testimony itself into a wider form. The believer's daily testimony, after such a designation, includes the recipient by structural necessity.

The dai of the middle line received the title *trustee of the doubly-entrusted*. The trust of the Imam is one; the trust of the believers is another; he is trustee of both. The believers' trust in him is itself part of his being trustee before the Imam. The doctrine is recursive: he is trustee of both directions simultaneously. He was at the station of humanity for his Imam and at the station of divinity for his fellow believers. The two stations are simultaneous and structural. They are not contradictions; they are directions.

The dai of Yemen, the teacher notes, is superior to all duat of other islands and does not lead prayer outside the three islands of his domain. The discipline is geographical: the dai of Yemen serves these three; his prayer-leadership is confined to these. The exception of one early figure leading prayer in Habasha is precisely the exception; he was given the dispensation for the loftiness of his station, and the dispensation was not given to others. And the chief officer of Yemen, of Najran itself, is superior to all chief officers. The reason is that he is the dai's successor in potentiality. His potential succession to the dai elevates his actual station among the officers. He is, in the structural ordering, the dai-in-waiting; the office before its time confers a station.

The phenomenon of the gathering at the lesson at which the souls of the departed believers attend is, the teacher said, a phenomenon that every dai's gathering exemplifies. The questioner asks through the tongue of a higher rank-holder. The asker may be living; the asking is also being voiced by the rank-holder whose tongue gathers the questions of his lower ranks. The teaching responds to both: the visible asker and the gathered askings of the chain. One of the early teachers' sending of a possessed woman of the community to another teacher, with the instruction to *conduct lessons in the mosque so that the departed forms could hear*, is the witness to the larger gathering. The possessed woman was the vehicle of the message; the message was that the lessons in the mosque must continue, because the departed forms were present and were continuing to learn. This teaches us that the lesson is not for the present audience alone. Whoever teaches in the gathering-house of our community is teaching to a gathering that includes the present and the departed.

The invisible visits of an earlier dai to the gathering of a later dai are of the same order. The dai of an earlier era visits the dai of a later era. The visit is invisible; the receiving dai stands and greets him as one greets a present visitor. The believers in the room may not see the visitor; the receiving dai sees, and the standing-up signals to those in the room that the unseen visitor is being received. The vision of an early teacher at the circumambulation, of the souls of three of the great dais performing the circumambulation at very high speed, is the vision of the body-less circumambulation. The souls have shed the drag of the limbs; their motion is at the speed of the soul's natural movement.

A small further teaching about the office: one of the great middle dais had, in addition to his island form, a second form in another city, under a different name. He was present in two places, in two forms, simultaneously. The two were not in conflict; they were complementary. The double-presence is, the teacher said, unprecedented among duat. The exception itself is part of the doctrine: when the office requires it, the exception is made. And the dai under house arrest in the city of his confinement would come to the House in the inner ascent and perform seven circumambulations daily. The body in confinement and the circumambulation at the House are not in contradiction. The inner ascent is real. The believer who is constrained outwardly may still travel inwardly. Today's Imam, the thirty-fifth, is the seventh of his septet and combines two septets. He possesses fourteen Imams within himself. The believer of today is therefore a believer in an extraordinarily gathered moment of the line. The accumulation is the gift of the era.

The teacher pauses to gather what the mapping has done. The pilgrim, on the surface, has performed a sequence of rituals fixed by the law: he has stood at Arafat, he has spent the night at Muzdalifah, he has thrown the stones at Mina, he has sacrificed his offering. On the inside, he has performed an act of recognition, in the inner geography of the line, at three figures of the inner core of the era of concealment. The outer rite and the inner act are not two acts; they are one act with two readings. The pilgrim who has only the outer reading has performed the rite and earned its outer reward. The pilgrim who has the inner reading also has performed an act of recognition at the three figures and earned its inner reward. The doctrine does not subtract the outer reading; it adds the inner. This is the pattern: not the replacement of the outer by the inner, but the placing of the inner beside the outer, so that the believer who has both has the full reading the doctrine intends.

A second small teaching belongs at the close of this lecture. The doctrine of the dai's invisible visits to a later dai's gathering and the doctrine of the soul's body-less circumambulation are both witnesses to the same broader doctrine: the limbs of the body are a drag on the soul. The soul, when shed of the drag, moves at its own speed. The duat of an earlier era are not bound by the spaces and times of the duat of a later era; the soul at the circumambulation is not bound by the speed at which the body can move. The doctrine is not an invitation to fantasize about superhuman feats. It is a precise teaching about what the soul is, beneath the body, and what the soul will do when, in the absorption of the rational soul into its higher reaches, the body's drag falls away. The longing of the early teacher who watched the body-less circumambulation and cried out is the proper longing of every believer in this body: the longing for the lightness that the soul, properly absorbed, will move with. The longing is not unhealthy; it is the healthy longing of one who knows what is ahead.

The teacher's geography rests on a broader heritage. The verse *they ask you about the new moons; say, they are appointed times for the people and for the pilgrimage* (chapter two, verse one hundred and eighty-nine, in the translation by A.J. Arberry, *The Koran Interpreted*, page thirty) frames the appointed times of the rite as a public discipline. The well-known prophetic teaching that the believer who stands at the mountain of standing receives a forgiveness the place itself confers indicates that the station is not neutral ground but carries what the rite places upon it. The Neoplatonic philosophical heritage on which the inner reading rests speaks of the soul not merely traversing space but participating in what each station holds (Plotinus, *The Enneads*, treatise four, section eight, in the English translation by Stephen MacKenna, page three hundred sixty). The teacher's specific identification of each pilgrim's station with each figure of the inner core is a further determination within this broader pattern: the believer is doing more than walking; he is participating, by his standing and moving, in the office each site carries.

## The three days of the Riser

The doctrine of the Riser's three days is, the teacher said, easily understated. The Riser takes the vicegerency of the Tenth Intellect upon his departure from this abode. The vicegerency is the office of standing-in; the Tenth Intellect is the one whose vicegerency is being transferred. The Riser, in taking the vicegerency, is taking the office that the Tenth had held in this dispensation. He remains in this office for only three days. The duration is short. Why? Because the final accounting is concentrated. The work of the final accounting is the work of the gathering of the believers, the slaying of the opposers at Mina, the dispersal of the stench through the four doors of the diffusion, the elevation of the gathered. The work is severe; the work is intense; the work does not require extension. Three days suffices.

The believer who imagines the final accounting as a long event is misunderstanding the doctrine. The final accounting is three days. The work compressed into those three days is enormous. The Riser, in those days, performs the work that the cycle has prepared for him. After the three days, the new dispensation begins. The fifty thousand years that follow are the fifty thousand years of the new era; the Riser's role in them is, in the standing doctrine, in another mode. The three days are the final accounting proper; what follows is the new cycle.

A small further teaching belongs here that the teacher does not pass over. At the final accounting, the opposers will be dragged in human form to Mina and killed by the believers. The names are listed in the doctrine, including the late opponents of the era of concealment and the early opponents of the era of disclosure. They are all gathered. The stench of their bodies will be carried by the wind through four doors of the diffusion. One of the doors is in Hadramaut. The geography is precise: there are four doors, and one is in Hadramaut. The stench is dispersed through the doors; the dispersal is the final purification of the place. The teacher allows that the doctrine of the final accounting is severe. It is severe because the work being done is the final accounting of the line's history of opposition. The opposers are not allowed to slip out unaccounted for. The justice is concrete.

The straight path, in the inner reading, refers to the line of the part-duat in which one stands. The believer's straight path is the chain from the believer to the Imam through the lower and higher rank-holders to the great gathering. The teacher elaborates. The believer has a permitted one who is immediately above him, the person who has taken his covenant, who teaches him, who corrects him. Above the permitted one is the breaker; above the breaker is the dai; and so on up through the ranks. Each believer's chain is constituted by the specific persons at each level. The believer who stands at the bottom of his chain is also standing on the straight path. The straight path is the chain. The believer who walks the straight path is the believer who walks the chain; he obeys his permitted one, he honors the rank above, he holds the line. The verse *guide us to the straight path* (chapter one, verse six) is his daily prayer that this chain be preserved.

Spiritual progress, the teacher continues, is of two types. One is striven for; the other is obtained by sitting in someone's lap. The first type is the type of effort. The believer reads, prays, fasts, gives charity, attends the gatherings, takes the covenant, renews the covenant. By these efforts, his progress is earned. The progress is his to celebrate; the progress is also his to be humble about, because the effort itself is also a gift. The second type is the type of transfer. The believer who sits in the lap of a teacher receives what the teacher transmits; the teacher's substance becomes the believer's substance; the progress is by transfer, not by effort. This is the progress of the child who learns by being in the presence of the elder. The child's heart is moved by the elder's example; the child's understanding is shaped by the elder's words; the child becomes what the elder makes.

The two types are not mutually exclusive. The same believer can have both. The same lifetime can carry both. The believer who has the wisdom to seek both is the believer who is making the fullest use of the means of progress. The discipline of effort without the discipline of presence is exhausting; the discipline of presence without the discipline of effort is incomplete. The two together are the discipline the doctrine names.

In a great cycle there are three hundred and sixty manifestations of the integration of the inner child of the believer. The human body contains three hundred and sixty souls; one part progresses per great cycle. The doctrine is precise: the body is three hundred and sixty souls; the rate is one per great cycle. The believer's progress across his many rounds is the slow integration of these three hundred and sixty. This is also why the believer's progress can feel small in one round. He has worked hard; he sees small movement. But the doctrine says: one part per great cycle. The small movement is the proper rate. The believer who knows this is not discouraged by the rate; he knows the rate; the rate will compound across the rounds into the full integration of the three hundred and sixty.

The verse *I am on a straight path* (chapter thirty-six, verse four) refers, in the inner reading, to the grandfather of the line, the grandfather through whom the chain runs to the Commander Of Believers. *I am on a straight path*, the speaker is, on the face of the verse, the speaker of the chapter; in the inner reading, the *my Lord* whom the speaker addresses is also being identified. *My Lord* is the grandfather of the line; the grandfather is on the straight path of the part-duat; the verse, on the inside, says: my Lord the grandfather is on the straight path of the chain. The teacher notes that this reading does not contradict the outer reading; it deepens it. On the outside, the verse is the speaker's affirmation. On the inside, the verse names the line that the speaker's affirmation honors. Both are true; the inside is the deeper layer of the outside.

The teacher closes Lecture 14 with a brief gathering of the two lectures together. The geography of the Hajj and the geography of the daily prayer are continuous. The pilgrim who walks Arafat to Muzdalifah to Mina once in a lifetime is walking the inner core of the era of concealment on the ground; the believer who, every day, recites *guide us to the straight path* is walking the chain of the part-duat in his own house. The two acts are at different scales and at different frequencies; they are the same one act of recognition. And the rate of three hundred and sixty souls progressing one per great cycle is the rate that ties the daily walk to the cosmic walk. The believer who does not see his daily walk producing visible progress is not failing the walk; he is walking at the rate the doctrine sets. The visible progress will come; it will come at the rate the round of rounds can sustain; it will not come faster because of the believer's impatience. The doctrine of progress is, in this respect, a doctrine of patience.

The doctrine that the final accounting is compressed into three days reflects a wider teaching about the soul's economy of time. The verse *they ask you to hasten the chastisement; God will not fail in His promise; surely a day with your Lord is as a thousand years of your reckoning* (chapter twenty-two, verse forty-seven, in the translation by Marmaduke Pickthall, *The Meaning of the Glorious Koran*, page two hundred forty-five) names the compression directly: divine duration and human duration are at different scales. The philosophical heritage of the line, in its treatise on the orders of intellect, teaches that the higher orders move in durations that compress what the lower orders experience as long stretches (in the English account by Paul Walker, chapter four of his study of the eleventh-century philosophical heritage of the line). And the well-known supplication recorded in the canonical book of devotions of the fourth Imam, that time in the divine presence is not as time in human presence, names the same teaching from the side of prayer (in the translation by William Chittick, *The Psalms of Islam*, supplication twenty-two, page one hundred two). Three days at the scale of the final accounting are not three days at the scale of an ordinary week. The rate is set by the higher scale.

## The end of the cycles

The doctrine of the end of the cycles turns on the Quranic *the day We will roll up the heaven as the rolling up of a scroll* (chapter twenty-one, verse one hundred and four). This is the verse-form of the end of the great cycle. The bonds that hold the celestial order in place terminate at this point. The heaven is rolled up like a scroll. The ordering of the spheres dissolves. After each end-of-the-great-cycle, the earth is changed. Length becomes width; north becomes south. *As We began the first creation, We will repeat it* (chapter twenty-one, verse one hundred and four) applies in a literal sense. The cardinal directions reorganize. What was the length becomes the width; what was the width becomes the length. The reorganization is total.

This is itself a teaching about the impermanence of the orientations we take for granted. The directions of our compasses are not eternal. The great cycle's end will redo them. The believer who has invested too heavily in the directions of his moment will find his investment recast at the end-of-the-great-cycle. The believer who has invested in the chain through which he is connected upward will find his investment preserved. The investment in the chain is the only investment that survives the recasting.

The era of concealment and the era of disclosure succeed each other like night and day until the round of rounds is completed. The pattern is the pattern of night and day; concealment is followed by openness; openness is followed by concealment. The pattern runs across the great cycles until the round of rounds is at its end. One round of rounds contains four hundred thousand great cycles multiplied by four hundred thousand great cycles. The product is enormous; the number is past the human imagination. One round of rounds has passed; nine remain.

This number is the answer that the fourth Imam would not give to the questioner. The questioner had asked: how much has passed, how much remains? The Imam gave her the first answer: one round of rounds has passed. The second answer he withheld: she did not have the standing for it. The fuller answer is this: one has passed; nine remain. The nine are the future. The future is structurally bounded. The believer who is given the number is, the teacher said, given a gift. The gift is the comprehension of where in the cosmos he stands. He stands in the second round of rounds. Eight more rounds will follow this one. At the end of the ninth that remains, the round of rounds will be at its end; the absorption into the First Sender will be complete.

At each end of the round of rounds, the place of the Tenth Intellect is taken by the ninth intellect. The Tenth, who has stood as Tenth in each round of rounds, is succeeded by the ninth at the end-of-the-round-of-rounds. All ascending forms gather in the domain of the First Sender. The gathering is the consolidation; the First Sender's domain is the receiving place. In the end, all are absorbed into the First Sender. Only the domain of the First Intellect and the First Sender remain. The horizon is at this absorption.

The believer who is given this horizon is the believer whose life can be rightly oriented. He is not investing in the local, in the temporary, in the surface; he is investing in the chain that runs through the rank-holders to the First Intellect and the First Sender. The chain is what carries him forward. The chain is what survives the absorptions. The believer who has held this horizon together with the calendar discipline of the twenty-first, the slaughter that frees the meat, the daily prayer as the believer's own offering, the geography of the Hajj as the geography of the line, and the three days of the Riser as the compressed work of the final accounting, has held one whole architecture in one mind. The horizon at the end is the horizon the chain has been pointing toward all along.

The teacher closed: these are the treasures of wisdom. The treasures are stored for the holders of the part-share of preservation. The duat are to draw from the treasures across the eras. The believers are to receive what the duat distribute. The chain is to be preserved across the rounds and across the great cycles and across the rounds of rounds. The end is the absorption into the First Sender. Until then, the work is the work of the line. May the blessings of Allah be upon our master Muhammad and upon the family of our master Muhammad, and may He grant peace and salutation. Peace be upon them all, for ever and ever.

A small further teaching belongs to the closing of this lecture. The investment in the chain is not the same as the investment in the persons of the chain. The persons of the chain change across the rounds and across the great cycles; the chain itself does not. The believer who has invested in a particular rank-holder, and grieves at his passing, is invited by this doctrine to understand that the rank-holder's office persists in the next person who takes it; the chain has not changed; only the holder of one of its links has changed. The grief is real; the chain is real; the two are not in conflict. And the absorption at the end is the absorption of the rank-holders into the First Sender, not the abolition of the rank-holders by the First Sender. The rank-holders are gathered into the source from which they came; they are not erased. The believer who has feared that the absorption is a loss has misread the doctrine. The absorption is the consummation. The chain is preserved in the absorption, not lost in it.

The doctrine of the rolling up of the heaven and the absorption into the First Sender stands in a long line of teaching on cosmic return. The verse *as We began the first creation We will repeat it; a promise binding upon Us; surely We shall do it* (chapter twenty-one, verse one hundred and four, in the translation by A.J. Arberry, *The Koran Interpreted*, page three hundred twenty-six) is the verse the teacher has named, but the inner reading of *We will repeat it* is given its philosophical determination in the heritage of the line as the literal recasting of orientations rather than as a moral metaphor (in the English account by Paul Walker, chapter four of his study of the eleventh-century philosophical heritage of the line). The Neoplatonic root of the absorption is the doctrine of return to the One (Plotinus, *The Enneads*, treatise six, section nine, division eleven, in the English translation by Stephen MacKenna, page six hundred twenty-four): the procession from the One is matched, at the end, by the return to the One. The teacher's doctrine that all ascending forms gather in the domain of the First Sender is the specification of this return within the precise hierarchy the line has named, the rank-holders are gathered into the source from which they came, not erased by it. The believer who hears the closing horizon is hearing a teaching that the heritage has been pointing toward across centuries.

## What this chapter lands

Three teachings have been opened and landed, and together they fix the horizon of the chain. The outer Hajj is the inner pilgrimage through the line: Arafat is the Messenger, Muzdalifah is Fatimah, Mina is the Commander Of Believers; the pilgrim moves through the inner core of the era of concealment as he moves through the sites. The plot before the pool of Khumm was foiled by prolonged lightning and preserved in an oath; the trees of Arafat are not cut because they neighbor hidden deposits; the verse against the broadcasting of the secrets seals the carrier of the secrets into a discipline of survival; the designation is the structural recapitulation of the testimony, widening the believer's daily testimony to include the recipient; the gathering at the lesson includes the departed; today's Imam, the thirty-fifth, gathers fourteen Imams within himself.

The final accounting is three days. The Riser takes the vicegerency of the Tenth Intellect, performs the compressed work of the gathering and the slaying and the dispersal, and then the fifty thousand years of the new dispensation begin. The straight path of the daily prayer is the chain of the part-duat in which the believer stands. Spiritual progress is of two types, effort and lap-transfer, and the rate is one part of three hundred and sixty per great cycle. The small movement is the proper rate. The believer who knows this is not discouraged by it.

The heaven is rolled up at the end of the great cycle; length becomes width and north becomes south; concealment and disclosure succeed each other like night and day; one round of rounds has passed and nine remain; at the end of the ninth, the absorption into the First Sender is complete. The horizon is given. The chain is what survives the absorptions. The investment in the chain is the only investment that survives the recasting.

A last small movement closes the third lecture. The doctrine of the absorption into the First Sender is, in its precision, also a doctrine about the believer's right scale of attention. The believer who attends only to the day in front of him is investing at the wrong scale; the believer who attends only to the absorption at the end is investing at the wrong scale in the opposite direction; the believer who attends to the chain that runs from the day in front of him through all the rounds to the absorption is the one investing at the proper scale. The proper scale is the scale of the chain. The chain is daily and the chain is cosmic; it carries the believer's small actions of the morning into the absorption at the end. The believer who has heard the doctrine is being asked to live at that scale, to do the small things daily that connect him to the chain, to refuse the broadcasting of the secrets that would sever him from it, to honor the rank above him and the rank below him, to keep the calendar discipline of the twenty-first, to keep the slaughter discipline at the cooking pot, to keep the daily offering of himself at the opening of the prayer.

## Closing

The full horizon is now set. The heart beneath the House, the eternal numbers, the missing tranquility in the cave, the doctrine of return, the inner mechanism of slaughter, the believer at the summit of beings, the daily prayer as the believer's own offering, the geography of the Hajj as the geography of the line, the three days of the Riser, the rolling up of the heaven, the answer the fourth Imam withheld, all of these now stand together. The architecture is one architecture. The chain is one chain. The horizon is one horizon. The treasures of wisdom are stored. The chain is the means of access. The believer who has walked the chain across these teachings has walked, in one evening, from the calendar of his month to the rolling up of the heaven, and the same one chain has carried him through all of it.
