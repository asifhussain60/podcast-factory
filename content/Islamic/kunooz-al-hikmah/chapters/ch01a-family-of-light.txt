# Family of Light

## Where this chapter opens

What if the body in which the Prophet ﷺ ascended on the ليلة الإسراء was not, in the deepest sense, his own? What if the very substance of that ascending form was woven out of the inner essences of the believers, of the ranks of the summoners in the regions, of the dignitaries gathered around them, of the small struggling souls who had just barely entered the circle of faith? The opening teaching begins with that astonishment: the highest ascent is bound to the inner substance of the believers, and the ranks that carry the call are not outside the mystery they serve.

Three teachings stand at the threshold. The first concerns the special human form and the gardens from which it is composed. The second concerns a bewilderment so radical that the highest existing thing and the newest believer stand on the same ground before it. The third concerns the chain of veils that runs from the Commander Of Believers (peace be upon him) through the Prophet's daughter, through her two sons, through the line of the Pure Imams (peace be upon them), and finally arrives at the Imam who is hidden in our own time. None of these is a side-doctrine. Each is a frame for the teachings that follow.

## Partial preservation and the special form

The opening distinction is between two orders of preservation from error.

In our tradition there are two orders of preservation from error. The first is total infallibility. This belongs to the Pure Imams, the family of the Prophet. For them, sin is impossible. Error is impossible. The preservation is complete. The second order is partial infallibility. This belongs to the summoners who carry the call after the Imams have entered concealment, and especially to the summoners who stand for the era of the awaited one. They are preserved enough to bear the trust. They are not, like the Imams, beyond every possibility of slip. One of the great summoners of the line wrote an entire treatise to fix the distinction precisely. The Imams are masters of total infallibility. The summoners are masters of partial infallibility. It is not an interchangeable phrase.

It is to *these* second masters, the summoners, that the stored wisdom belongs in practice. They draw new things out of what has been handed down, so that the call may continue to bear fruit in their hands. The point is not literary ownership but the structure of service: the hidden Imam is the source of complete preservation, while the summoners carry a partial preservation sufficient for the trust they bear.

Pause for a moment on the architecture of that distinction. The summoners are not lesser Imams. They are not approximations. They are a different kind of office altogether, an office that exists precisely because the Imams have entered concealment, and because the call must continue to be carried in the world while the Imam is hidden. The Imam is the master of total preservation because the Imam is the one through whom the inner reality flows without interruption. The summoner is the master of partial preservation because the summoner stands in the world, takes counsel in the world, faces the contingencies of the world, and yet must carry the trust without breaking it. Total infallibility belongs to those who are above contingency. Partial infallibility belongs to those who walk through contingency without letting the trust fall. The architecture of the title is the architecture of the call itself.

A small mark of style on the very first leaf is worth pausing on. Above the opening invocation on the original page is written the الاسم الأعظم. A later scholar of our line considered, at length, whether the light of the الاسم الأعظم is greater than the light of the opening invocation. After protracted argument he established that the light of the الاسم الأعظم is the more excellent. For this reason we write the الاسم الأعظم above the opening invocation whenever the occasion calls for the full inscription. The sign itself is rendered in a sacred calligraphic script: the first dot above is a seal-ring, alluding to the noble family. The elongation mark above points to the assistance received from the higher ranks of the hierarchy. The Commander of the Faithful himself composed verses about the الاسم الأعظم, verses preserved in the collected sermons and aphorisms of his line, the book *The Peak of Eloquence*.

The praise is also a doctrine. All praise is for Allah, the Great, who manifested a special human form from the gardens of the people of specialty. Two technical terms organize the teaching: the special human form, and the gardens, the spiritual reservoirs of refined souls from which that form is composed.

There are three distinct kinds of human form or vessel. The most basic is the الصورة الطبيعية, which refers to the physical body that is conceived and born through natural biological processes, from parents. Above this is the الصورة الظلية, the shadowy form, which is a more subtle, spiritual body. And finally, there is the الصورة الخاصة, the special form, which is of a completely different and elevated order, distinct from the other two.

The الصورة الخاصة is made from the gardens of the people of specialty. The gardens are the spiritual gardens of particular souls: the ranks of the summoning hierarchy in the regions; the believers themselves; and the dignitaries gathered in those regions. From the spiritual essences of these is the الصورة الخاصة composed. The lower form is by contrast made from the gardens of those who are at a further distance, lesser in station, the wider community of souls.

This is the substance behind the astonishment. When the Prophet ascended on the Night Journey, through which form did he ascend? Through the الصورة الخاصة, formed from the gardens of the people of specialty. That is, the very souls of the believers and the ranks of the regions, in their inner essences, were the substance from which the Prophet's ascending form was woven. The Prophet did not ascend in any other body. He ascended in this one. And we, in our small ways, are gardens that contribute to such a form. This is why two parallel hierarchies are honored: the Imams, in whom the الصورة الخاصة is fully realized, and the summoners, who serve as the bearers of the work in the era of concealment. The praise is not ornamental piety. The praise *is* the doctrine, and what is being praised is also what is being said about us.

## The great bewilderment

The testimony of faith then unveils a teaching that had not been framed before by any summoner: the first of existing things and the last of His creation are equal in the inability to comprehend the exalted essence.

To feel the force of this we have to fix the two ends of the hierarchy. The first existent thing in the order of creation is the First Intellect, the highest creature in the chain of being, the radiant source from which the orders below it proceed. The last existent thing is the believer, and not the believer who has spent half a lifetime in study and practice, but the soul that has just newly entered the circle of faith. The just-arrived believer. These are the highest and the lowest poles of the order of creation, separated by what one would suppose is an unbridgeable distance of station.

The claim made here is breathtaking. In the matter of gnosis of the Exalted Essence, these two are *equal*. The First Intellect, despite its lofty station, has not advanced in the gnosis of the Originator beyond the point where this last believer stands. The believer, low in the order of being, has not fallen behind by any measurable distance. Both are equal in the inability. The First Intellect is perplexed before the Originator in exactly the same way that the newest believer is perplexed. The gnosis of the Originator is simply impossible to either. It is not that one has a smaller portion than the other; it is that the very thing called gnosis of His essence lies altogether outside the reach of any creature, however high it may stand in the chain of being.

This teaching reorganizes the picture we carry of where we are. We are used to imagining a long ladder up which different creatures stand at different rungs, with the closeness to God measured by the rung you have reached. The praise reverses this. The ladder is real. The rungs are real. But on the question of the essence, the ladder does not climb at all. The highest rung looks up; the lowest rung looks up; both see the same impossibility. In matters of being, the order matters; in the matter of the gnosis of the essence, the order falls away.

The Quranic word translated as "thing" must be re-read in this light. The Quran says, in many places, that Allah has power over every thing, knows every thing, encompasses every thing, verses such as "Verily, Allah has power over all things" (Quran 2:20, Pickthall trans., Knopf ed., 1992). The "thing" of those verses, on the doctrinal reading, does not mean every random object in the world. It means that which is real, that whose value endures. Its opposite is the mirage, what looks like water in the distance but when you reach it there is no water there. The mirage has no real value. So the real thing points to the believer, who has value; and at the highest reach, the real thing points to the Imam of the time, in whom all value is gathered. When the Quran says Allah has power over every thing, knows every thing, encompasses every thing, it is the believer above all who is being kept in view. The verses are spoken about the universe; they are addressed, in their inmost layer, to the believer.

This is not a doctrine that stayed in books. The summoners who held this teaching lived it outwardly. One of the great summoners of the line, when believers came to meet him, would stand up to receive them. He would rise from his seat. When asked why a summoner should rise for a believer who had merely come to greet him, he answered: because in the apportioning of the noble family, the believer is my companion. Just as an Imam takes his share in the noble family, so the believer takes his share. In the matter of belonging to the noble family, the believer is my fellow. And so out of honor, I stand. The gesture is small. The doctrine behind it is total.

Likewise another summoner wrote of two figures of his generation that one of them was "the most excellent of existing things." People who heard the phrase puzzled at it. The most excellent of *all* existing things? More excellent than the angels, the celestial spheres, the First Intellect? The phrase was not careless. "The most excellent of existing things" meant: most excellent among the believers present in the call at that moment. The believer is not a peripheral figure in this universe. The believer is the most excellent of existing things, and the Quran's "every thing" over which the Lord has power has the believer, above all, as its hidden referent.

There is one more consequence to register before moving on. If the gnosis of the essence lies altogether outside the reach of any creature, then the language of "approaching" Allah, which the supplications of the tradition use so freely, has to be heard correctly. It cannot mean a movement toward the essence. The essence does not admit of distances closing. What the language of approach refers to is the closing of the believer's distance from the ranks above, from the figures through which the substance descends to him. The believer climbs the hierarchy of the call. He does not climb the essence. He cannot. No one can. To approach is to draw nearer to the Imam who is above us in the chain of being, who is veiled in his own way, and who is the highest place toward which the creature can be oriented at all. The Originator remains where He has always been, at a distance no creature has begun to measure. The believer's whole movement is, properly speaking, a movement within creation toward the highest reach of creation, and there it ends. This is why the equality of bewilderment is good news rather than disappointment. It removes the false picture in which someone, somewhere, is "ahead" of us in the gnosis of the essence. No one is. The First Intellect is not ahead. We are not behind. The journey is all on the side of the ladder, and the ladder is where the journey is supposed to happen.

If you sit with these three readings together, the equality in bewilderment, the believer as the real thing of the Quran, and the believer's movement as a movement within creation rather than toward the essence, you start to see the strongest statement of the believer's *dignity* that the tradition will allow. The newest believer stands on the same ground as the First Intellect before the Originator. The Quran's most exalted verses about Allah's power and knowledge keep that very believer in view. The summoner stands up for him in his own house. The doctrine assumes this dignity as already established. We are being addressed as the real thing the Quran has been speaking of all along.

## The veiling chain

From the opening praises the book turns to the line of the Imams and to the secret of their veiling, and what unfolds is not a sequence of independent biographies but a single doctrine of veil-and-veiled that runs from the Prophet's lifetime to our own era.

After the declaration in which the Prophet said, "of whomsoever I am master, this Commander Of Believers is his master", a tradition preserved in the early canonical Shia hadith compilation *The Sufficient* ("Book of the Proof," hadith 4; English ed., World Organization for Islamic Services, Tehran, 1978, vol. 1, p. 287), the Prophet lived his remaining months in the veil of the Commander Of Believers, and the Commander Of Believers, for the rest of his own life, lived in the veil of the Prophet. The two did not simply exist as separate persons after this declaration. They had become veil and veiled, each interchangeable with the other in the inner reality. The Prophet himself had declared, "My flesh is your flesh, my blood is your blood", a saying recorded across the canonical compilations of Prophetic tradition (cf. the encyclopedic Sunni hadith compendium *The Treasury of the Workers Tradition*, vol. 11, hadith 32913; Beirut critical edition, 1985), and the two were of a single soul. One of the summoners of the line, in his writings, drew out the structural consequence of this. The successors pass away before the speaker-prophet in the inner reality. What this means is that during the Prophet's remaining time after the declaration, it was the Commander Of Believers who, in the inner ordering, had already given his form to be the veil of the Prophet; the Prophet, while his body still walked the earth, was already living in the inner veil of the Commander Of Believers. When his body departed, what departed was a veil. The inner Commander Of Believers was already in continuity.

The proof of how deeply the "our selves and your selves" of the Quran ran is in the famous matter concerning the Prophet's youngest wife. The Prophet had said in the presence of the Commander Of Believers that he gave him the right that, after his own passing, he could divorce her. The companions of the Prophet found this hard. How could this be? But the proof is from the Quran itself. In the verse of the mutual trial, "If anyone disputes in this matter with you, after the knowledge that has come to you, say: come, let us gather our sons and your sons, our women and your women, ourselves and yourselves" (Quran 3:61, Pickthall trans., Knopf ed., 1992), the Prophet was commanded to call our sons and your sons, our women and your women, our selves and your selves. For "our sons" he brought the two grandsons. For "our selves" he brought the Commander Of Believers. So the Commander Of Believers became the self of the Prophet, and what the Prophet may decide, the self of the Prophet may decide. The divorce-authority over the Prophet's wife is a direct corollary. The "selves" of that verse is not a poetic flourish. It carries legal consequence.

In the same vein the praise speaks of the Tenth, who is veiled by the Prophet. This refers in the broadest sense to the Tenth Intellect. But more precisely, as our teacher used to insist, the Tenth here does not point to that Tenth alone. It points to the relative part of the master of the cycle in this cycle of ours. The Tenth has many "relative parts" running through history; what is veiled by the Prophet in this praise is the particular relative part of *this* cycle's master of the cycle, the summoner who carries the line of summons through our era.

The praise next turns to the Prophet's daughter, and what it says of her is among the most quietly extraordinary teachings of the entire book. The Prophet, before his passing, said of her: successor of her father, and proof of her husband. And in the inner reckoning, the most excellent Proof of all is the Prophet's daughter herself. She is the Greatest Proof. Because of this, the first night of the seventh sacred month, the month of supreme station attributed to the Commander Of Believers, has its own supreme station as well: the first night of that month alludes to the Prophet's daughter, the Greatest Proof.

The consequence for our doctrine is decisive. The successor whom the Prophet appointed over the community at the inner level was the Prophet's daughter. For this reason her passing came so quickly: within six months of her father, she departed. And while the Prophet was alive, whenever she arrived in his presence he would stand up and seat her in his own place. He seated her in his place. This was no mere gesture of paternal love. It was a continual public sign of who would sit, in the inner reality, in the Prophet's seat after him. We are conditioned to read "the Prophet rose for his daughter" as a charming detail of his tenderness. Read it again with the praise in mind. It is a designation, made repeatedly, in plain view.

This is also why, in this cycle, those who came after the second of her two sons, the Imams from his progeny, are called by her name. One of the senior summoners of the line, in his book *The Treasury of Sciences*, has written that the station of the Prophet's daughter is such that even the Pure Imams could not attain its gnosis; the Imams who follow her in the line are bewildered before her station, and for this reason they bear her name. Those Imams who descend from her second son and bear her name carry that title on the awe in which they stand before her. Another senior summoner has set this out clearly in the same *Treasury of Sciences*, and a later summoner wrote a parallel work, *The Treasury for the Son*, that takes the same vein further.

The hostility of the early caliphs was not pure ignorance of all this. Some of the matters of the realities were known to those near the family. The two senior companions of the Prophet who took up the leadership after his death had been around long enough, from the time of the Prophet's grandfather, to have caught hints of some of these things. There are even traditions that point to it. The second of those caliphs once, when a young one of the Prophet's two grandsons climbed onto the pulpit and said, "This pulpit belongs to my father," himself answered, "Yes, this pulpit belongs to your father," and stepped down. He was acknowledging publicly that the pulpit, in its inner significance, was not his. Another well-known saying among the common people goes that the same caliph admitted of one of these grandsons, "These hairs were not grown by you", meaning, the hairs on your head were not grown through any work of mine, but through yours and your father's. The rank was known. That was the bitterness of the rejection.

After the Prophet's daughter, the praise turns to the elder of her two sons, the trustee of the secret of the leadership. He is the entrusted one who carries the secret of the leadership forward in a particular manner. Even while bearing the trusteeship he has, like any Imam, his human form and his divine form; he rises from the human form to the divine, as the masters of leadership do. From him a particular line proceeds: all the summoners are connected to the elder grandson. From the younger grandson, by contrast, the line of the Imams descends; the Imams are connected to the younger grandson. The doctrine, then, is a forked inheritance. One branch runs through the elder to the summoners who carry the call through the eras of concealment. The other branch runs through the younger to the Imams themselves. To recognize the elder grandson is to recognize the source of every summoner.

The Quran itself points to these two branches at the very end of its recitation. "Say, I take refuge with the Lord of mankind" (Quran 114:1, Pickthall trans.). The "mankind" here are those intimate with the call, who have affection for it. Their Lord is the elder grandson. "Say, I take refuge with the Lord of the daybreak" (Quran 113:1, Pickthall trans.), the chapter on the splitting of dawn. From there, from the splitting, the line of leadership proceeds. The two final chapters of the Quran encode the two branches of the inheritance. The Quran closes by sending us into both refuges at once, into the Lord of those who love the call, and into the Lord of the splitting from which the line of Imams descends.

The younger grandson himself, as the praise notes, is the one who made manifest the occultation through death at the hands of the people of doubt. The killing in the desert was the manifestation of an occultation. What the people of doubt saw killed was a veil. The real self was not the thing that was struck. This is the theme: the younger grandson's body was killed, his real self was protected, and the occultation of the inner Imam was, by that very killing, made publicly visible to those who could read what was happening.

The praise next embraces the Imams from his progeny, the assemblages of lights. The distinction between secrets and lights is important. Secrets are intentions; lights are luminous forms. A great summoner of a later generation is the assembler of the secrets of the summoners of his region who came after the master from whom his line proceeded. He gathered their secrets; he assembled their luminous forms in himself as one assembles lights. The Imams from the younger grandson's progeny are, in the same fashion, assemblages of luminous forms.

The praise then reaches the Imam of the present time, the hidden one: the Imam of the Time, who is concealed by the veil, hidden by the secret from all of creation. Whatever has appeared of him outwardly is the veil; the original self is hidden. And all the Imams who will come, even unto the Day of Resurrection, are veiled by the Commander of the Faithful: they are *his* veils, and he is their veiled essence. The familiar phrase "there is no power and no strength except with Allah, the High, the Great" carries this teaching inside it. The "High, the Great" here is the essence of the Imams. All the Imams are veils of the High and the Great. Every prayer-formula we say that names the High and the Great is, in its inner orientation, an acknowledgment of this essence veiled by every Imam.

Notice the texture of what is being said. The veiling chain is not a sequence of independent events. It is a single doctrine repeating itself in different registers. The Prophet veils the Commander Of Believers during the months after the declaration of mastery. The Commander Of Believers veils the Prophet for the remainder of his own life. The Prophet's daughter is veiled by her father in the role of inner successor, covered by him while he lives, revealed by him in the gesture of rising to seat her in his own place. The Imams of the line are veiled by the Commander of the Faithful, who is their veiled essence. The summoners are veiled by the Imams. And the Imam of the present time is veiled by every appearance, every face that might be taken for him is the veil, every secret that might disclose him is precisely what conceals. There is one doctrine and many enactments. To read the praise correctly is to hear it as variations on a single chord.

The praise also brings in the gates, whose lights shine forth in the forms, and their Proofs, and the summoners of this auspicious era that will last fifty thousand years. The number is striking. The era of concealment in our line ended with the summoner who closed its seventh cycle, seven eras, all completed. Numerically, by the reckoning, seven cycles of concealment are finished. This present era of ours, our particular awaited-one era, is included within the great era of the awaited one: fifty thousand years of what you count. When the awaited one comes, fifty thousand years will begin from after him. So when the praise says "the summoners of this auspicious era that will last for fifty thousand years," it is gathering our own summoners into the company of those who carry the awaited one's era. We are not late arrivals to a finished story. We are early arrivals to a story whose horizon is fifty thousand years long.

## What the opening lays down

The opening has done three things before the first question is even asked. It has distinguished complete preservation from partial preservation, and located the summoners within the second order. It has told us what the special human form is made of: the gardens of the believers and the ranks in the regions, the very substance from which the Prophet's ascending form was woven. And it has told us how the line of veils runs: the Commander Of Believers in the Prophet and the Prophet in the Commander Of Believers, the Prophet's daughter as the inner successor to whom the Prophet rose, the elder grandson as the trustee from whom every summoner descends and the younger grandson as the source of the Imams, the Imams as assemblages of lights, and the Imam of our own time veiled in concealment beneath the masters that hide him.

What ties these three together is a single doctrine of dignity. The newest believer is the real thing the Quran speaks of. The believer's inner essence is the garden from which the Prophet's own human form was composed. The summoners who carry the call across the eras are the masters to whom the treasures of wisdom are addressed. The believer is not outside the doctrine; the believer is inside its address.

It is worth noticing how rare this combination is. The highest figures of the tradition are named, and in the very same breath the believer is not left outside. The First Intellect and the newest believer are made equal in bewilderment before the Originator. The Prophet's ascending body is woven of the believer's substance. The summoner rises from his own seat for the believer who has come to greet him. The believer is not gathered in at the end as an afterthought; the believer is held at the center of the doctrine from the first sentence.

And so the threshold is crossed. The praise has placed the believer where the doctrine wants him: not at the door looking in, but already inside the room, already counted among the gardens whose essences compose the الصورة الخاصة, already addressed as the real thing the Quran has been speaking of all along.
