# The Middle Way and the Heart Beneath the House

The verse forbids two extremes. It forbids that the hand be kept shut, tied to the neck, so that nothing at all is given; and it forbids that it be opened utterly, since the one who opens it wholly is left blamed and regretful, asking himself afterward, "What have I done?" That is the meaning carried by the image of the hand upon the neck. The hands are not to be bound to the neck, nor spread open without measure.

A parallel verse stands in Surah al-Furqan, in the passage concerning the `'Ibad al-Rahman` (the servants of the Most Merciful):

> *Walladhina idha anfaqu lam yusrifu wa lam yaqturu wa kana bayna dhalika qawama*

"And those who, when they spend, are neither extravagant nor miserly, and there is ever a just middle way between them."

The meaning is the same as in Surah Bani Isra'il: one is not to become so miserly that the hand never reaches into the pocket, nor so lavish in generosity that nothing remains for family or self, so that blame and regret follow. The `'Ibad al-Rahman` are praised precisely because when they spend they neither waste nor withhold. Their conduct is the middle path. *Khayr al-umuri awsatuha* — the best of matters are those that occupy the middle.

A tension seems to arise here, since the quality of the Prophet and of the noble Imams was to give everything in charity. The well-known incident is recalled of the Prophet returning from a journey, having distributed all his provisions along the way, so that when he arrived home nothing was left. That state belongs to the `Awliya' Allah` (Friends of God), to Rasulullah, and to the `ma'sumin` (the infallible ones).

What once occurred with Amir al-Mu'minin bears on the matter. A wealthy man came to him with his two sons. The elder son spent freely, upon others and upon himself, and lived well. The younger son took no pleasure in life, citing the example of Amir al-Mu'minin himself, whose clothing was coarse and whose food was spare and harsh. Amir al-Mu'minin replied that they should not take his example for their own lives, but should rather benefit from the blessings Allah had given them. He affirmed that what the elder son was doing was correct: when Allah has granted blessings, those blessings are to be used.

This is an established narration. There are many incidents of Mawlana 'Ali performing manual labour and giving away the earnings along the road, arriving home with nothing. The same was the state of the Prophet. In the incident recalled here, Sayyida Khadijah had already passed away. The Prophet would slaughter sheep often and in great numbers. He left his house with a goat, slaughtered it, and distributed it. When he returned, only one leg remained. Sayyida 'A'ishah said:

> *Ma baqiya illa hadha*

"Nothing is left but this. You have given everything in charity and alms."

He replied in the same register, and his reply was most eloquent:

> *Kullun baqiya illa hadha*

"Everything remains, except this."

What is given away endures; only what is kept is truly lost.

As for 'A'ishah and Hafsah and the troubles they caused the Prophet, this reached such a degree that a Quranic verse was revealed concerning them, in Surah al-Tahrim:

> *Laqad saghat qulubuhuma*

"Your hearts have indeed inclined away from the right."

Their hearts had become crooked. Allah declared that He would not accept their repentance, even though Allah is *Tawwab al-Rahim*, the Acceptor of repentance, the Most Merciful. And if they were to come in opposition, then Allah was sufficient, and Jibril was sufficient, and the *Salih al-Mu'minin*, the righteous believers, and by this is meant 'Ali. *La tuzara hidayatuhu*.

The reading now advances. *Wa fi wajhin akhar* — "And in another aspect." In this second reason, *yuniru min ahl al-basa'ir al-suwar* — it illuminates the forms of the people of insight. *Fa-llahu ta'ala alima ma hadhihi sabila min hadha al-sirr al-ghamiz wa-l-ma'na* — "So Allah the Exalted knows what this path is of this obscure secret and its hidden meaning." And there is the Quranic verse:

> *Wa ja'alna al-bayta mathabatan li-l-nasi wa amna*

"And We made the House a place of resort for the people and a place of security."

Two qualities are thus assigned to the Baytullah: it is a place of reward and `thawab` (divine recompense), and it is a place of `amn` (security).

*Wa fi al-qalb mithlu ma fi al-shahadah al-mu'addiyah li-qa'iliha ila dar al-sa'adah* — "And in the heart is the like of what is in the testimony of faith, which leads its speaker to the abode of felicity." In the heart resides that which is in the `shahadah` (testimony of faith), the very thing that leads the one who says *La ilaha ill-Allah* toward eternal felicity. *Wa dhalika anna al-qalb al-haqiqi al-mutala'li' bi-l-nur huwa tahta hadha al-bayt al-ma'mur* — "And that is because the true heart, gleaming with light, lies beneath this Frequented House." The `qalb al-haqiqi` (true heart) lies beneath the outer house that is circumambulated. *Alladhi hadha lahu ka-l-hijab wa huwa hakhadha ka-l-muhtajib* — "For which this outer house is like a veil, and that which lies beneath is like the veiled one." The outer house of the circumambulation is the veil; what lies beneath it is the veiled reality. *Fi jami' al-asbab, mithlu al-nafi wa-l-ithbat* — "In all the means, like the negation and the affirmation." Just as the `kalimat al-shahadah` (word of testimony) contains negation and affirmation, so too the Bait al-Ma'mur and the `bayt al-haqiqi` (true heart) exemplify that same pairing of negation and affirmation. In another passage it is stated that opposite the Bait al-Ma'mur there is a `qalb`, a Baytullah facing it, the outer house being the veil and that inner reality being the veiled one.

*Al-shahadah allati man qalaha mukhlisan dakhala al-jannah* — "The testimony of faith, which whoever says with sincerity enters Paradise." An occasion during Hajj, during 'Umrah, illustrates a related point: there was no space upstairs in the Masjid al-Haram, so prayer was offered in the lower level. Just as prayer is valid above the level of the Ka'bah, it is valid below it as well. The place of prayer is not within the Ka'bah itself, nor in a basement of the Ka'bah as such, but below its surface level, and this is permissible on either side, above or below.

*Allati u'iddat li-lladhina ahsanu al-husna wa ziyadah* — "Which is prepared for those who do good; for them is the best reward and an increase." Such is the Paradise prepared for those who did good deeds and increased in them. As to what constitutes a good deed, Amir al-Mu'minin says:

> *Al-hasanatu hubbuna wa-l-sayyi'atu bughduna*

"The good deed is our love, and the evil deed is hatred of us."

That is, love and hatred of the Ahl al-Bayt (People of the House). The Quranic verse *li-lladhina ahsanu al-husna wa ziyadah* thus points to those who, in this `karrah` (cycle of existence), took the `'ahd al-sharif` (noble covenant) and thereafter increased in their faith. In the next `karrah`, whatever rank they held, they will be granted a rank beyond it. For those who did good is the best reward and an increase. Their ranks will rise from one `karrah` to the next.
