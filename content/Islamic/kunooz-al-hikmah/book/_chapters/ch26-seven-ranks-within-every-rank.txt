# Seven Ranks Within Every Rank

The discussion had turned to prayer in the *ghayr jazirah*, and specifically to Habashah, so its mention came up. There is a *da'wah* in Habashah, but its people do not give allegiance to the Da'i. It continued up to the time of Sayyidna Husayn bin Isma'il, up to Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan. Ali bin Ali related that Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan had sent him to Habashah to establish a *qiyam* there, and that after returning he had established the *qiyam*. There was a Sajjad who related that Syedi sahib had said that his father, in the last phase of his life, would be the Da'i of Habashah. The Prophet had not given permission for that in the *ghayr jazirah*, and permission for prayer was given in the *ghayr jazirah* of Makkah al-Mukarramah.

The text before them read: *wa lahu sha'nun 'adhim wa maqamun jasim*, and he has a great station and a lofty rank. His station is very great and his rank very high. Then: *wa ma hajar min ardihi wa baldatihi illa li-'adhm shanihi*, and he did not migrate from his land and his city except for the greatness of his station. The *hijrah* from the land of his birth, from that land and from his city, was made for nothing other than his greatness and his station. The people of Satan were what they were, and the *hijrah* that was performed was performed because of that great station.

Long afterward, Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan went to those places where Sayyidna Mahmud bin Isma'il had performed *hijrah*, and stayed there for several months. He built a *masjid* there, and he built a *Dar al-Hijrah* there as well. Later, in the present age, Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan built a magnificent *Dar al-Hijrah* in that place, and a very splendid *masjid* with it.

Of a visit to that place the account runs in the speaker's own words: "So then when we went there, we were sitting in the *masjid*. I said, 'Malik, if you permit, I will recite about Sayyidna Sulayman sahib.' He said, 'Yes.' I recited the *qasidah*. It was a very long *majlis*, and Sayyidna sahib kept listening with concentration."

Next: *wa li-ihya'i marasim al-da'wah*, and for the revival of the ceremonies of the Da'wah. The *marasim al-da'wah* are those written documents and the *nusus* that existed; he reissued them and brought them to notice. Everything he did in the Da'wah, he did. *Wa fi'iluhu bi-al-wa'il*, and his action by the earlier ones, who were lying in the abyss until he brought them into action and made people aware that these things had been written in the past, that these documents had been made, that the *du'at* had ordained these writings apart from the books. All of that he manifested.

Then: *wa li-istikhraj al-sawa' al-kamil min bilad al-Yaman*, and for extracting the complete secrets from the lands of Yemen. The things that lay hidden in the lands of Yemen he brought to light. *Min al-sadis*, from the sixth, *shurur al-sharifah*, the noble secrets, and those noble secrets he brought to light.

Then: *wa law tulina al-kalam*, and if we were to prolong the speech. To speak on this subject would carry it on very long. The ranks of the *du'at* are *rutab al-'aliyah*, high ranks, and the ranks of the *du'at* and the other ranks besides them differ among themselves. *Fi 'uluww al-darajat*, in the loftiness of degrees. Rank is not of one type, because the degrees themselves are lofty.

*Li-anna kull al-martabah fi dhatiha sab' rutab*, because every rank in itself has seven ranks. *Yu-khafi min al-asrar al-thaniyah*, hidden from the second secrets. Every rank is divided into seven ranks, according to the high secrets that belong to it. If there is a *da'i*, then one *da'i* is divided into seven ranks, and among them is one who stands in the highest rank of the seven. Sometimes it happens that a *da'i* is in the highest rank among the seven ranks.

*Inda dhawi al-adhan al-saqiyah*, with those of piercing intellect. *Wa minhum man yakunu fi awsatihi*, and among them is one who is in its middle. Some *du'at* stand in the middle of these ranks. *Tilka al-rutab al-madhkurah*, those mentioned ranks, whose mention has been made. And among them is one who is in the lowest rank; some *du'at* stand at its lowest.

Asked who decides which rank a man occupies, he answered that it is the Imam of the time who decides which *da'i* is in which rank, and that its declaration comes later. The succeeding *du'at* say that so-and-so was in such a rank, and that his rank was this. Its mention is in the books, and some *du'at* have also expressed their ranks, saying that this one was in such-and-such rank and that one in such-and-such rank.

Then it is said that among them there are also such *du'at* who, because of their past *sawabit* and their *hasanat*, the good deeds that were theirs, come *ila adna rutab al-hujjiyah*, to the lowest rank of the *rutbah* of *hujjiyah*. They leave the rank of *da'i* and come to the lowest rank of *hujjat*. *Ma fawqaha min rutab al-'aliyah*, what is above it from the high ranks, and they may go to a rank even higher than that, since the rank of *hujjat* likewise contains several ranks, and they enter into those as well.

A *hujjat* is of two types. One is the rank above the *du'at*, and that is the rank of *hujjat*. And the son of the Imam is also called a *hujjat*.

Asked about the *da'i*s of the present day, he answered that all the *du'at* who came after Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan, until the *Qiyamah*, were established in the rank of *hujjat*. Their ranks were elevated and they were established in the rank of *hujjat*. This is their *shan*. These are the *qa'imiyyat*, the *du'at* of the era of the *qa'im*, for there are three eras: the *dawr al-nabawi*, the *dawr al-tayyibi*, and the *dawr al-qa'imi*. The *dawr al-nabawi* lasted until Mawlana Ali al-Tayyib. After Mawlana Ali al-Tayyib came the *dawr al-tayyibi*, until Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan, and then the *dawr al-qa'imi* began. When one thousand years were completed, the *dawr al-qa'imi* began.

*Wa minhum man yakunu mustawda'an*, and among them is one who is a temporary trustee. This is a special point. Among those *du'at* there is also this, that some are of the rank of *mustawda'*. And *wa minhum man yakunu mustaqarran fi al-istida'*, and some are *mustaqarr*, permanent, within the *istida'*. The *istida'* is the rank of *du'at*, and it is a *mustawda'* rank, while the rank of the *a'immah* is *mustaqarr*. So within the *istida'*, some are *mustawda'in* and some *du'at* become *mustaqarrin*.

Asked to point to an instance, he said that Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan, despite being so learned and despite the greatness he possessed, such that an example of it was set before them, was in the rank of *mustawda'in*, while Sayyidna Ja'far bin Sulayman was *mustaqarr*. Other *du'at* came after him. Not all the *du'at* have been mentioned, but some *du'at* became *mustaqarr*, and among them the first was Sayyidna Ja'far bin Sulayman, who was in the rank of *mustaqarr*, meaning that within the *istida'iyyat* he became *mustaqarr*. They are all *mustawda'in*, but within the *istida'iyyat* he became *mustaqarr*. So the rank of Sayyidna Ja'far became higher still, and he reached the station of *hujjiyah*.

When it was compared to the station of the Nabi, he said that the station of the Nabi is something else. When asked for the definition of *mustaqarr*, and whether it meant the succession of son after father, he answered that it is not like that. That belongs only to the case of the Imams; the lineage of son from father is among the Imams. But *mustawda'* and *mustaqarr* mean this, and their full explanation has not come down, that their rank moves from *istida'* toward *istiqrar*, permanence, and it is possible that they become masters of special *majami'*. Allah knows best. When it was put to him that from *mustawda'* to *mustaqarr* is higher, and that this is what the passage yields, he agreed, and said it could be that they become masters of *majami'*, special ones, and that special support is obtained by them from the Imam.

Just so did Sayyidna Ja'far receive it. When Sayyidna Ja'far went to Janad, a place in Yemen, and entered the *masjid* there, he was on a journey. With him were Sayyidna Ibrahim, his brother, the son of Sayyidna Muhammad bin Fawz, and Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad, his sister's son, and a Sindhi brother travelling with them. Right at the time of *Maghrib*, when its hour was very near, Sayyidna Ja'far said, "Look, at this moment the *tajalli* of the Imam is upon me. And at this moment, whatever *du'a* you make, it will be accepted." So Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad bin Fawz made this *du'a*, that his son should be a Da'i and that the lineage of *du'at* should continue in his progeny until the *Qiyamah*. And Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad asked that he obtain such knowledge as neither the first ones had received nor the last ones. He then wrote such magnificent books, *Badi'at al-Bayan*, and the *Hashiyah* on the *Anwar*, and others, in which the *du'at* are astonished and perplexed. The *du'at mustaqarrin* are astonished and perplexed by them. When they mention his name they call him Sayyidna Husam, though he was not *mustaqarr* and remained *ghayr munfarid*; yet they mention his name with great respect and call him Sayyidna Husam. And the Sindhi brother who was there had very poor handwriting, so he asked that his handwriting become very good. As the capacity of each was, so were the supplications, and his handwriting also became very good.

This narration used to be read in the *hijrat*, in the time of Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan. Between *Maghrib* and *'Isha* the book of *Sirah* of the *du'at* was read, and incidents from the *sirah* of the *du'at* would come up. One time this incident passed, and Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan looked at Ghulam and said, "Ghulam, you people are always worried about your handwriting." At that time Syedi 'Abdullah had arrived from Hindustan, Hadrat 'Abdullah sahib had arrived. And Sayyidna sahib said, "Mawlana, he was not a Hindi, he was a Sindhi."

Asked whether the man spoken of was Syedna Husayn bin Ahmad Dhul-Jaddayn, he said yes, Dhul-Jaddayn. But the one who is in Baroda, he said, is not Dhul-Jaddayn. He is Ali bin Husayn bin Ahmad bin Husayn bin Dhul-Jaddayn. Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad whose story is told with Sayyidna Ja'far is his grandfather, and the one in Baroda is his grandson. He was sent in the time of Sayyidna Hibatullah, Ali bin Husayn. So it is a misunderstanding to say that the son of Dhul-Jaddayn lies here; the one here is not his son but his grandson. He passed away at forty-five years of age, in Baroda, and he was a great scholar, that person who is buried there, a very great scholar.

It was said that this is why Sayyidna Sulayman sahib always referred to Sayyidna Ja'far as "my mawla." He answered: *Al-Mawla*. "Mawla Ja'far, Mawla Ja'far." How great a station it must be, that the father, in the *abyat* that Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan wrote describing the *shan* of Sayyidna Ja'far bin Sulayman, used the words "Mawla Ja'far, Mawla Ja'far." Then Sayyidna Ja'far, *qaddas Allahu ruhahu*, after his father's passing, transcribed those verses. His handwriting was very strange and unusual, completely unique. He transcribed them, and he removed the word "Mawla." When Sayyidna Sulayman sahib passed away, Sayyidna Ja'far was eleven years old. Had he been much older when he was called "Mawla," the matter would be understandable; but at such a young age it was said, "my Mawla Ja'far."

As for Ali bin Husayn just mentioned, his *rawdah* is the one called the *rawdah* of Dhul-Jaddayn, and in it Hadrat Shakir sahib Muqaddas is also buried, and 'Azizah Bu as well. On one side is Hadrat Shakir sahib Muqaddas, and on the other side is 'Azizah Bu. It was observed that the day before had been his day, and he confirmed it.

When Sayyidna Ali bin Husayn al-Makrami came to Hindustan, Hadrat Shakir sahib Muqaddas was alive. In Baroda he made this request: "Mawlana, my age has advanced, and *kullu nafsin dha'iqat al-mawt*, every soul will taste death, so I want to select the place of my burial, where I will be buried." Syedi sahib looked at all four sides and then said, "No, your place should be here," meaning where he was standing. There are four corners; in one corner he is buried, and in one corner is 'Azizah Bu, who was already there. He said, "This is your place." Asked whether Hadrat Shakir sahib had chosen another spot, he said yes, he had thought of being near Dhul-Jaddayn, and that Syedi sahib said, "This is the place."

Concerning Sayyidna Sulayman sahib and Sayyidna Ja'far, it was asked whether what happened at the birth was not an indication that a Da'i greater than himself had been born, since at that time Sayyidna Sulayman sahib had not yet become the Da'i. He said yes. It was then the era of Sayyidna Da'ud bin 'Ajab Shah, who passed away in 999 Hijri. The matter belongs to a time before that: when Khadijah bint Nuh was pregnant and Sayyidna Ja'far bin Sulayman was in the womb, Sayyidna Sulayman sahib said to Janab Khadijah bint Nuh, "Your son is very blessed." She said, "Mawla, who knows whether it will be a boy or a girl?" He said, "It will be a boy, and he will be the Da'i of his time." And he said also, "You will serve his *da'wah* greatly." The believer sees with the light of Allah. The second thing that happened was that of Khadijah bint Nuh he used the term *rijal al-da'wah*, saying that she wears an earthly dress but in reality she is from the *rijal al-da'wah*, one of the *rijal al-da'wah*.

Sayyidna Muhammad bin Fawz, *'ala al-usul*, mentioned to Sayyidna Ja'far, after the *intiqal* had happened, "I think that for the prosperity and well-being of the *da'wah*, I should marry the wife of Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan." Sayyidna Ja'far related this to his mother, and she said, "I will ponder this matter." After some time she agreed. And so blessed a wife was she that from Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan, Sayyidna Yusuf bin Sulayman was born; from Sayyidna Ja'far bin Sulayman came Sayyidna 'Abd al-Tayyib and Sayyidna Ali. All four became *du'at*. Sayyidna Yusuf and Sayyidna 'Abd al-Tayyib were *du'at ghayr munfarid*, and Sayyidna Ja'far and Sayyidna Ali sahib were *munfarid du'at*. Sayyidna 'Abd al-Tayyib wrote many books; his handwriting was very fast and very clear, and he wrote several works.

Then, when he married Mawlana Khadijah bint Nuh, Sayyidna Ibrahim sahib was born, and other children as well. Sayyidna Ibrahim sahib was imprisoned in Yemen by the Zaidi king, and remained in prison for fourteen years. Of those fourteen years Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan used to say, "Ghulam, the love of you Hindustanis for the *du'at* is astonishing and strange. Our grandfather, when he was in prison, a Hindustani brother was there who would perform *Hajj* for him." In those days the performance of *Hajj* was a very difficult task, accomplished with great hardship. "For our grandfather, a Hindustani brother would perform *Hajj* every year." What a great matter of distinction this is.

It was observed that the lineage of *du'at* continued through Sayyidna Ibrahim, and not through any of his brothers. He confirmed it: none of his brothers. Sayyidna Ibrahim sahib had appointed his son Isma'il, but he passed away in his lifetime, and after that he appointed Sayyidna Muhammad bin Isma'il, his grandson. Upon his grandson he made the appointment.

*Kama kana sayakun*, as it was, so it will be. *Wa ma 'ulima sayu'lam*, and what was known will be known; just as it has been known, so it will certainly be known. *Wa ma taht al-shams shay'un bi-jadid*, and there is nothing new under the sun.

Then the covenant: I am taking the covenant of Allah, which is very firm. *Wa mithaquhu al-shadid*, and His covenant is strong, very strong. *Alayka ayyuha al-akh al-sa'id*, upon you, O fortunate brother, in protecting the secrets of *al-'Aliy al-Majid*. This noble covenant is that he will protect the secrets of *al-'Aliy al-Majid*.

*Wa man nadhara ila hadha al-kitab bi-ghayr idhn al-da'i al-majid*, and whoever looks at this book without the permission of the glorious Da'i, *aw mimman lahu 'adw al-'ilm*, or of one who holds the reins of knowledge, that is, the one in whose hand is the rein of knowledge and who makes decisions in the *da'wah*, *wa al-nadhr al-sadid*, and who possesses sound and special insight, being high in rank; whoever looks without such permission, *fa-qad bari'a min al-Mubdi' al-Mu'id*, then he is free of the Originator, the Restorer.

And his saying: *man adha'a sirrana adhaqahu Allah bard al-hadid*, whoever spreads our secret, Allah will make him taste the coldness of iron. Whoever broadcasts the secret, Allah will make him taste the punishment of cold iron. The punishment of cold iron places him at the northern or the southern pole. The punishments are three: *'adhab al-adna*, *'adhab al-awsat*, and *'adhab al-akbar*. He goes into the *'adhab al-awsat*. Asked whether this applies to one who reads without permission, he confirmed that it does.

And with that the book is concluded, alhamdulillah. *Al-'Aliy al-Wahhab*, the High, the Bestower, with His praise, *wa al-salat wa al-salam 'ala sayyidina Muhammad*, and blessings and peace upon our master Muhammad, *al-muntaqil min al-aslab al-tahirah ila al-arham al-mutahharah*, the one who transferred from the pure loins to the purified wombs. He kept transferring from the pure loins of the pure forefathers into the pure and holy wombs. None of his ancestors committed *shirk*; they were all pure and clean, because they were the *a'immah* of their time. How could they commit *shirk*? All of them were righteous, the very essence of the universe, the *a'immah* of the time.

*Wa 'ala akhihi wa wasiyyihi*, and upon his brother and his legatee. Janab Ibrahim had two sons, Isma'il and Ishaq. The line here descends from Janab Isma'il, not from Janab Ishaq. From the progeny of Janab Isma'il came the *a'immah*, *mustaqarr*, and from the progeny of Janab Ishaq came the *anbiya'*. The station of Janab Isma'il is very high: he was present with Janab Ibrahim in the building of the Ka'bah and in the placing of the Hajar al-Aswad, and he was with Janab Ibrahim in raising the walls, and the genesis of Zamzam happened through his feet. Everything is there. Yet his rank is less than his son's, and his son Qaydar's rank is less than his, because Qaydar is the seventh Imam.

*Wa sallallahu 'alayhi wa 'ala akhihi wa wasiyyihi*, and may Allah send blessings upon him and upon his brother and his legatee, *al-mu'in al-a'immah al-ghurr*, the helper of the brilliant Imams, *bi-wasitat al-pak*, through the medium of the Pure one, *fi al-sirr al-mustasirr*, in the hidden secret. *Wa 'ala al-a'immah al-akhyar, majami' al-anwar*, and upon the righteous Imams, the collections of lights. *Wa 'ala Imam al-zaman, al-natiq, al-mubdi'... fi hal al-wiladah bi-'alaniyah*, and upon the Imam of the time, the speaker, the originator, who in the state of birth spoke openly. *Wa al-radi' fi hadd al-tufuliyyah ila awan al-zuhur bi-nuraniyyah*, and the infant at the stage of childhood until the time of manifestation with luminosity. He progressed from the stage of childhood toward manifestation, until his manifestation happened with *nuraniyyah*. *Wa sallam 'alayhim*, and (عليهم السلام).

Asked of whom this speaks, he answered that it speaks of the Imam of the time, whose rank is such that *Mubdi' Ta'ala* spoke with him openly in the state of *wiladah*, and who progressed from the state of childhood toward manifestation, until his manifestation happened with *nuraniyyah*. And (عليهم السلام), and upon their forefathers, and upon their pure progeny, and upon their *du'at*, who are *al-mayamin*, noble and honorable, *al-na'ibina manabahum ila qiyam yawm al-din*, their representatives until the establishment of the Day of Judgment.

It was objected that the mention of *Mubdi' Ta'ala* here seems too elevated, and that 'Aql al-Awwal or 'Aql al-Thani would make sense but *Mubdi' Ta'ala* would not, and that nowhere else does such a mention occur. He answered that it is possible the term *Mubdi'* has been used while the reference is to the ranks below it. By *Mubdi' Ta'ala* the intent is not the one who brought existence from non-existence. That is not the intent, because what he did was to bring existence from non-existence, and it was finished. After that he has no other action. He has no other action at all. It was asked whether he would perform that action again, bringing existence from non-existence, and it was said that he will never do such a thing, that he will not do it again. It was asked, "What? Does he not have the power?" It was answered, "No, despite having the power, he will not do this." And concerning him, since when he is, what he is, what he is doing, and why he did it, the *du'at* have no answer. There is only this: that he did it, and it was his *tafaddul* and his *mihrabani* that he did such a thing. That is the whole of the answer.

Now this is 'Aql al-Awwal. From the *ma'rifah* he had of *Mubdi' Ta'ala*, 'Aql al-Awwal became a *hijab* and a *bab*, and a continuous and flowing knowledge was established upon him which can never stop. For this reason it is said, *la ta'khudhuhu sinatun wa la nawm*, neither slumber nor sleep overtakes him. He neither feels drowsiness nor is overcome by sleep, this 'Aql al-Awwal. In drowsiness a person becomes partially heedless, and in sleep completely heedless. 'Aql al-Awwal is neither partially heedless nor completely heedless. And *al-Hayy al-Qayyum*, the Living, the Self-Sustaining, he has become: alive and life-giving. It has been said that *al-Hayy al-Qayyum* is one of Allah's names and among the high ones, and that in *al-Hayy al-Qayyum* the *Ism al-A'dham* is present. There are certain such words, as it is said of *Dhu al-Jalal wa al-Ikram*, *Ya Dha al-Jalal wa al-Ikram*, that in it the *Ism al-A'dham* is present; or *al-Hayy al-Qayyum*, which is the *tasbih* for Sunday. So *al-Hayy al-Qayyum* points toward 'Aql al-Awwal, that he is alive and life-giving.

And then what did he do? The substance and support that 'Aql al-Awwal received from *Mubdi' Ta'ala* he gave to 'Aql al-Thani, that is, to al-Munba'ith al-Awwal, only two and a half percent of it. He gave two and a half percent of it, as it is in *zakat*. Of all the things gathered with him he gave a portion of two and a half percent, because had he given the whole, his existence would have been consumed. In the same way al-Munba'ith al-Awwal gave to the ranks below him two and a half percent of his own. And so it went on.
