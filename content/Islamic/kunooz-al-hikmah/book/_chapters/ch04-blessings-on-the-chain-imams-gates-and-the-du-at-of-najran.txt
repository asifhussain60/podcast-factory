# Blessings on the Chain: Imams, Gates, and the Du'at of Najran

*Wa 'alal a'immati min dhurriyyatihi majami' al-anwar*, and upon the Imams from his progeny, the assemblages of lights. The phrase *majami' al-anwar* carries a precise sense. *Anwar* (lights) and *asrar* (secrets) are two different things. *Asrar* is used for *niyyat* (intention). Concerning *anwar*, Sayyidna Sulayman has written in this very book that he is the *jami' al-asrar*, the assembler of secrets, of the *du'at* of Yemen who became *du'at* after Sayyidna Dhu'ayb bin Musa. There the word used is *asrar*, not *anwar*. And *anwar* refers to the *suwarat*, the luminous forms, of which he is the assembler.

*Wa asma' al-'ayn al-'azimah fil-'ali min al-asrar*, and the names of the great essence in the high secrets. These *A'immah Tahirin* are the names of the great essence, dwelling in the great and high secrets, for the great essence, and all the *A'immah* who come until the Day of Resurrection, are veiled by him, and he is their *hijab* (veil). These Imams are a *hijab*, and their veiled one is Amir al-Mu'minin.

*Wa 'ala Imam al-zaman al-mutawari bil-hijabi*, and upon the Imam of the Time, who is concealed by the veil. *Al-mustatir bil-sirr 'an jami' al-bara'a*, the one who is hidden by the secret from all of creation. The original self remains hidden, and what has appeared is a *hijab*.

*Wa sallama 'alayhim wa 'ala abwabihim al-mutalali' anwaruhum fis-suwar*, and may (عليهم السلام) and upon their *abwab* (gates), whose lights shine forth in the forms.

*Wa 'ala hujajihim wa 'ala du'at hadha al-dawr al-maymun al-qa'in khamsina alfa sanah mimma ta'uddun*, and upon their *hujjahs*, and upon the *du'at* of this auspicious *dawr* (era), which will last for fifty thousand years of what you count.

The blessing falls upon the *du'at* of this *dawr*, that is, the *dawr* of the *qa'im*, the era of the Qa'im: upon its *du'at* also, may God's (عليهم السلام). This *dawr* will last for fifty thousand years. The number has its source in this, that when the Qa'im comes, from after him fifty thousand years will begin. The present era is therefore included within it. The *dawr al-satr* (era of concealment) ended with Sayyidna Sulayman Sahib; the seven eras were finished, as confirmed by calculation as well, and this *dawr* is included in the *dawr* of the Qa'im. Hence the words *al-qa'in khamsina alfa sanah mimma ta'uddun*.

*Wa Sulayman bin Hasan al-mujtama' ladayhi asraru du'at al-Yaman*, and Sulayman bin Hasan, with whom are gathered the secrets of the *du'at* of Yemen. In whose possession were the secrets gathered? In his. How great a station is his.

*Wa Ja'far al-sabi' fis-sirr al-ma'mun*, and Ja'far, the seventh in the protected secret. Ja'far is the seventh, that is, being the twenty-eighth, he became the seventh, in the manifest, protected secret.

*Wa mimun min la'la*, and a Mim from above. Mim refers to Muhammad bin Fu'ad, that intermediate figure who came in between, non-independent though he was.

*Al-qa'im bi-fard al-jihad fi mala'in*, the one who established the duty of jihad amongst the assembly. He is the one who establishes the duty of jihad in the midst of the assembly, that is, among the people. His jihad was tremendous. Had the position of Sayyidna Muhammad bin Fu'ad been ruined, as happened in the case of Abd al-Muttalib and others, the *da'wah* itself would have stood in peril, and its history would have changed entirely. Yet the jihad he carried out was tremendous.

*Wa al-'ayn al-mutafajjir 'ala lisanihi yanabi' al-asrar*, and 'Ayn, upon whose tongue gushed forth the springs of secrets. 'Ayn means Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman, so that gushing forth from his tongue are the springs of secrets. The various secrets of the doctrines, their springs and fountains, were made manifest from his tongue. In his *haqa'iq* and his other books he presented sublime matters of doctrine, things that had not been presented before, and he made them manifest. Of *Shu'al al-Anwar*, which is the first book, he himself wrote that this *Shu'al al-Anwar* is in the *haqa'iq* (realities) and the *daqa'iq* (subtleties): *hadha al-kitab fil haqa'iq wad-daqa'iq*. From there the books of subtleties began.

Sayyidna Ja'far did not write a single book on *haqa'iq*. All his books are on *hujjat* (proof) or *ta'wil* (interpretation), although his own station is very lofty. Mim and 'Ayn are often mentioned to refer to the *muhtajib*, but here they are used to refer to the *du'at*.

*Al-'ati bi-ma la yakhtur 'alal afkar*, bringing forth that which had never occurred to the thoughts. He brought forth such things as had never crossed people's thoughts and had never been made manifest in them. The books that stand in his name, *Anwar al-Shashaniyyah* and others, and the lofty realities within them, are very special things that he presented.

*Wa saribuhu alladhi ghada ghidha' al-nufus bi-asrar al-malakut al-quddus*, and his drinking companion, who became the nourishment of souls with the secrets of the holy *malakut*. His *sarib*, his drinking companion, means Sayyidna Ibrahim. He became the nourishment of souls with the secrets of the holy *malakut*, from the secrets of Allah, the Holy.

*Wa Muhammaduhu al-muhajir min baldatihi li-'uluwwi shanih wa rutbatihi*, and his Muhammad, the emigrant from his town for the loftiness of his station and his rank. Muhammad here means Muhammad bin Isma'il. He is the first *Da'i* who emigrated from his town, to Najran. Until then the *da'wah* had been in Yemen: in Sana'a, in Haraz, and in Sa'fan. He is the first *Da'i* who left Yemen and came to Najran, for the loftiness of his station and rank. He is also the first and only *Da'i* who was given permission to lead prayers in *ghayr jazirah*, outside the Arabian peninsula, and so he led prayers in Habasha.

After him the *du'at* did not simply return to Yemen. A few remained where they were, and then they too moved. It was in the time of Sayyidna Hasan Shihab and Sayyidna Ali bin 'Ibad that they were here, and in the time of Sayyidna Ghulam Hussain Sahib they came fully to Najran, though in fact this was somewhat earlier.

The emigration was towards Najran, and there was also the going to Abuna, to Sayyidna Muhammad bin Isma'il. Sayyidna Sulayman Sahib, in his time, toured Abuna and stayed there for quite some time. The coming from Yemen to India was a journey of great hardship that took one year, passing through various cities of Syria and elsewhere. There was a stay in Abuna as well, and a mosque was built there which still exists today. Sayyidna Hussain bin Hasan later enlarged that mosque, and in the time of Sayyidna Hussain bin Isma'il it was further expanded and rooms were built for those sitting in *i'tikaf* (seclusion). It is a very magnificent mosque.

Abuna is not a port. It is a mountainous area, very far from the coast, about two hundred kilometers from Najran, remote and mountainous. Sayyidna Sahib went there, and it has been visited in later times as well. The grave of Sayyidna Rashid, Rashid bin 'Isa, is also there, and the grave was visited and the *ziyarat* performed. Sayyidna Rashid is the one who, after becoming a *Da'i*, stood for six years in his prayer niche making up his missed prayers. It is possible that in the early period of the *da'wah* some prayers were missed, and he made up six years of them standing in the prayer niche.

After the passing of Sayyidna Hussain bin Hasan, Sayyidna Hussain bin Isma'il gave one of their companions, Ahmad by name, a boy from Yemen, very intelligent and an exceptionally good young man, the prayers of Sayyidna Hussain bin Hasan, the prayers of four years that had been missed, for in those last days he had become like an angel and could not keep track of his prayers. So four years of prayers were made up. And when Sayyidna Hussain bin Hasan passed away, the walk to his side was made barefoot from the house, a considerable distance, of the order of the distance from here to Grand Road station, and it was covered barefoot.

*Sallam 'alayhim ajma'in abad al-abadin*, (عليهم السلام), forever and ever. May peace be upon all of them, for all time.
