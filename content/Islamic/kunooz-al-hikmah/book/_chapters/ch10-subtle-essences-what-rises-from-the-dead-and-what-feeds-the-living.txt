# Subtle Essences: What Rises From the Dead and What Feeds the Living

When the time of his passing comes, he finds dissolution in the *qalb* (heart). *Thumma sara mawaddan wa qiwaman li quloob al-ulama min al-hudood* (Then he becomes substance and strength for the hearts of the learned ones from the *hudood*). After dissolving, they become substance and strength for the hearts of the learned ones who are the *hudood*, the great *hudood* of the *da'wah* (mission). Substance here means that the *hudood* of the *da'wah*, the great ones, receive help in the strength they possess: the strength of speech, the strength of benefiting others. Through them it comes. So it was with Janab Yusuf, of whom it is said *lawla an ra'a burhana rabbihi* (had he not seen the proof of his Lord): had he not seen the proof of the Lord, he would have erred in the matter that occurred with Zuleikha. And the proof of the Lord is this *mawadi* (substance) obtained from the Imam. *Alladheena bi hidayatihim 'urifat ta'at al-ma'bood* (Those through whose guidance the worship of the Worshipped One is known), such that through their guidance the worship of the Worshipped One can be performed.

*Fa idha haanat nuqlatuhum* (So when their time of transfer comes). When the passing of these *hudood* occurs, then *ila 'alam al-anwar... ittasala dhalika al-maddah bil-qalb al-saafi min al-akdar* (to the world of lights... that substance connects with the pure heart, free of impurities). When their passing occurs to the world of lights, *yarji'u ila aslihi* (it returns to its origin). The substance by which their hearts were given strength, received from what dissolved in the *qalb* of the Imam, is returned. It returns to its origin at the time of passing. These were trusts they had held, so that in their decisions, in their benefiting of others, and in the matters of their lives, the *hudood* should not make mistakes and should remain safe from evils and sins. They become masters of all those states. But they receive this temporarily, not permanently. At the time of their passing it connects and is returned to the origin, to the pure one, free of impurities.

*Wa yakoonu min ma'ooneen nafsu imam al-sharqi wal-gharbi ma madda lahu min al-qalb* (And from those helpers, the soul of the Imam of the East and the West is that which is supplied to him from the heart). From that the soul of the Imam is formed. Through the dissolution that happened in the Baytullah, the soul of the next Imam takes shape, the Imam of the East and the West. *Ma madda lahu min al-qalb*, that which is obtained for him from the heart. *Wa min al-ma' alladhi hawlahu* (And from the water that is around it). From the water around it, it is also obtained. *Wa shay'in min al-bata'ih allati talyihi* (And a thing from the marshes that are adjacent to it). All these things are gathered for that Imam whose soul is in the process of origination.

*Wa mimma zukhira lahu mimma inhallah min awwal al-fitrah... ba'da awwal al-fatrah, ba'da tamam al-kawr al-mashhoor* (And from what was stored for him of what dissolved from the beginning of creation... after the first interval, after the completion of the well-known *kawr*). The thing that has been stored for him, dissolving from the first creation, from the first interval, after the completion of the *kawr* which is well known: from that the Imam's soul receives additions. From the moment the era was created, whatever treasures have been accumulating, from those the Imam's soul is enriched.

*Yajdharu minhu fa'idah... 'an idrees al-shurafa'... al-muwasilah min da'ihi... mishkat al-noor* (A benefit stems from it... for the noble ones... which is connected from his *da'i*... the niche of light). From it arises a benefit for the noble ones, connected through his *da'i*, who is the niche of light.

*Wa qad awdaha hadha al-ma'na... al-da'i... al-ajall al-'aleem hibatullah bin sayyidna ibrahim fi risalatihi al-mawsumah bi saqilat al-quloob min al-fikr, wa al-munawwirah lil-basa'ir, wa al-musfiyah lil-suwar* (And this meaning has been clarified by the *da'i*, the most glorious, the most learned, Hibatullah bin Sayyidna Ibrahim, in his treatise named *Saqilat al-Quloob min al-Fikr*, The Polisher of Hearts from Thought, and *al-Munawwirah lil-Basa'ir*, The Illuminator of Insights, and *al-Musfiyah lil-Suwar*, The Purifier of Forms). This has been clarified by Sayyidna al-Da'i al-Ajall al-'Aleem Hibatullah bin Sayyidna Ibrahim in his treatise known as *Saqilat al-Quloob*, which illuminates insights and purifies forms.

*Fi jawab su'al al-thani 'ashar* (In the answer to the twelfth question). Sayyidna Hibatullah, in that book, in the answer to the twelfth question, has written *bi qawlin 'aleem al-ma'ani wa al-gharad* (with a speech of profound meanings and purpose), authored with great and grand meanings. *Wa ma fihi abyan al-bayan* (And what is in it is the clearest of explanations), in which there is the clearest explanation. *Wa awdah al-burhan* (And the most evident proof), a clear proof.

*Wa mazkuruhu fi hadhihi al-mas'alah wajhayn fil-haqeeqah* (And its mention in this matter has two aspects in reality). The matter is now explained in two ways. Sayyidna Ali (ع) is saying: I will explain to you the state of the Imam's soul. *Fi haqeeqah... fi wajhayn* (In reality... in two aspects). I will explain it in two ways. *Fi muqabalat al-shahadah* (In comparison to the *shahadah*), in comparison to the word of the *shahadah*.

*Wa huwa anna al-asrar... allati la yattali'u 'alayha illa al-ahad min 'ibadihi* (And that is that the secrets... upon which none are informed except the unique ones from His servants). It is from the secrets which no one can know except those who have reached the rank of *ahad* (unique) from among the servants. *Wa dhalika anna fil-maqam 'alayhi al-salam... ma yuqabilu qutbayn al-shahadah fil-hukm* (And that is that in the station, upon him be peace, there is what corresponds to the two poles of the *shahadah* in its ruling). If the Imam (ع) is compared with the word of the *shahadah*, then *li qawli asdaq al-qa'ileen* (according to the saying of the most truthful of speakers), and the most truthful of speakers says:

> إِنَّا نَحْنُ نُحْىِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ وَنَكْتُبُ مَا قَدَّمُوا۟ وَآثَرَهُمْۚ وَكُلَّ شَىْءٍ أَحْصَيْنَهُ فِىٓ إِمَامٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ

It is We who give life to the dead, and We record what they have sent ahead and the traces they left behind, and every thing We have enumerated in a clear Imam. This verse appears in Surah Yasin.

In this, two things merit reflection. The first is the word *kullu shay'in* (every thing). What is meant by *shay'*? This fan, this light, this carpet are also *shay'*, things, and they too fall under the category of *shay'*. But there, by *shay'* is meant the form of the *mu'min* (believer). Wherever the word *shay'* has been used by Allah Ta'ala, it refers to the *mu'min* and the form of the noble *mu'min*. So with *kullu shay'in ahsaynahu fi imamim mubeen*, and with *innallaha 'ala kulli shay'in qadeer* (Indeed, Allah has power over all things), and *wa innallaha bi kulli shay'in muheet* (and indeed, Allah encompasses all things), and *innallaha bi kulli shay'in 'aleem* (indeed, Allah is knowledgeable of all things). These usages convey the same meaning. By *shay'* is meant the *mu'min*, or the form of the *mu'min*.

The second point concerns *ahsayna* (We have enumerated). What is significant in *ahsayna* is that *jama'na* (We have gathered) was not said. In gathering, things become jumbled. In *ahsayna*, each form retains its separate nature and its state remains distinct, meaning that despite being gathered they retain their own individuality and can be separated out. So the Imams did: with their forms, with a single form, they made a manifestation. The reason lies here. Had they been gathered, *jama'*, they could not have been separated. But they were *ahsa*, enumerated in such a nature that the individuality of each remains in its own place.

A question was raised: in this, the Imam cannot be encompassed except by the Imam. So does that mean the Imam can also do it through his own power? That enclosure, that everything is as it is, except by the power of the Imam, what does that mean?

The answer: this is it. *Kullu shay'in ahsaynahu fi imamim mubeen*.

It was remarked in reply: this is, of course, the Imam, but the Imam also does not come within its scope.

Everything will come into it.

It was then said: I will check it and then say.

*Wa dhalika anna al-maqam fih nasut wa lahut* (And that is because the station has in it humanity and divinity). Here the comparison is drawn with the word of the *shahadah*. In the Imam there are *nasut* (humanity) and *lahut* (divinity). *Nasut* is the collection of *riyat* (subtle essences). When a *mu'min* passes away, the *riyat* that emerge immediately after his passing are the higher *riyat*. The *riyat* that emerge after burial are the lower *riyat*. From the higher *riyat*, those discharged after the passing but before burial, the *nasut-e-zilli* (shadowy human forms) of the *nawasi* (successors) are made. And from the *riyat* that emerge after burial, which emerge only for three days, after which what remains are vapors, the *nasut-e-tabi'i* (natural human forms) are made.

A question was raised: the ones that come out first are more excellent?

Yes, the ones that come out first are more excellent. There is also a tradition that the one who passes away should be buried as soon as possible. This is the guidance of the Messenger, that the deceased should not be kept for too long but buried promptly.

So these *riyat* correspond to *nasut* and *lahut*, and correspond to the negation and affirmation of the word of the *shahadah*. The *riyat* do not immediately become *nasut*; they must go through a process. Those *riyat* travel to the light of the moon, which draws them; then they go to Jupiter; then from Jupiter they go to the sun; then from there they return to Jupiter again; and their principal station is the moon. The shining that occurs in the sky on the 13th, 14th, and 15th nights is said to happen because of these *riyat*, for their congregation becomes very large during those three nights, and that is why that luminosity occurs.

For the one who has passed away, the *riyat* that emerge over three days are giving a notice in a spiritual sense to the relatives: whatever you have to do in these three days, do it, whether giving charity, performing good deeds, hosting meals, reciting *sadaqallah*, holding *fatiha khwani* or *beeswa khwani*, arranging for Quran recitation, or giving in charity. Do it within these three days. After that, whatever you do will be for the noble form and for the *fuzala'*. I, the departed, will have already left and will not return for a thousand years. Such is the state of the *riyat*.

The purified *riyat* then descend like drops of dew and settle on certain fruits, uncooked fruits such as apples and similar things. The Imam and the Imam's wife eat those fruits, and from that conception takes place, and from that the *Farzand-e-Imam* (Child of the Imam) is born. As soon as the *Farzand-e-Imam* is born, the nourishment he takes is entirely *riyat*. He appears to be drinking milk or taking ordinary nourishment, but in reality he is receiving *riyat*.

One incident occurred in this regard. Mufaddal bin 'Umar, who was the *bab al-abwab* (gate of gates) of Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq, came to Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq's presence while the Imam was eating. The Imam said, "Come, join us in the meal." Mufaddal said, "Mawlana, I have just eaten." The Imam replied, "What you ate, and what we are eating: you have not eaten what we are eating. And what you ate, we do not eat." The indication was that what you eat are the external nourishments that Allah has created, while what we eat are *riyat*, and in that you should join us.

The *mu'min* also receives *riyat*. He receives them during his *wudu* (ablution). As soon as he says Bismillah, the breath he draws, and the water that touches his body and enters through the pores, these carry the *fuzala'* and *riyat* that the *mu'min* receives. This is one way. That is why it is said that *isbagh al-wudu* (performing *wudu* completely) is of great status. As has been stated in the traditions, some things become atonements for major sins, and among them is *isbagh al-wudu fil-burudat*, or *sabarat* as it is recorded in one place, meaning that he performs *wudu* with piety in extreme cold. And *burudat* does not mean only that the weather is very cold. It also refers to the coldness of temperament, the inner reluctance when one's disposition is not inclined toward *wudu*, when there is laziness within. Performing *wudu* with completeness in such inner coldness is the inner meaning. In the outward sense it also means the time of cold weather, and in the inner sense, when there is coldness of temperament, doing it completely even then.

A question was raised: the *riyat* that are received, are they for the common *mu'min* also?

Yes.

For the *mu'min* also?

Yes.

Whose *riyat* are these?

The *mu'min*'s own *riyat* are received by him. The purified *riyat* that are ascending come into the *mu'min*'s share. And those *riyat* are in the moon. After emerging, they are gathered in the moon, and from there they are connected to the one who performs *wudu* and so on.

That is why it has been said in a tradition that after *wudu*, whatever water remains on the hand should be shaken off, for from every drop that is shaken off an angel is created who will pray for one's forgiveness. And it has also been said that after *wudu* one should recite *salawat* (blessings) and Surah al-Qadr, *inna anzalna*, and then pray, and that by doing so one's prayer will not be rejected. This too has been mentioned among the blessings of *wudu*.

The second occasion on which the *mu'min* receives nourishment from the *riyat* is at the time of *iftar* (breaking fast) and *sahri* (pre-dawn meal). That is why it has been said that *sahri* should be taken at the last possible moment, not that one wakes at two in the night and eats, but that *sahri* is had as late as possible. And that the fast should be broken promptly, without waiting to first pray and then break the fast, but hurrying to break it. What is being received at those moments is of a very high nature, and one should hasten to obtain it.

A question was raised: the *riyat* that are received, do they become part of the body?

Yes, they become part of the body. They ascend. The precise details of how they become part of the body, whether they go to the heart or elsewhere in the body, I have not read. But they enter the body, as water enters through the pores or through respiration. As soon as one says Bismillah, the breath one draws after that carries nourishment from the *riyat*.

A question was raised: are they his own *riyat*?

No, not his own *riyat*; he has not passed away yet. These are the *riyat* of the believers. The purified *riyat* of the believers that are gathered in the moon are the source from which these things are obtained by him.

Then came the matter of the one visited in Iran. His name was recalled: Avicenna.

Ali ibn Sina was a great *da'i*, a very great *hujjat* (proof). With him was a student who was very dear to him. That student used to say to him: if you were to claim prophethood, people would not deny you. You are so great, and you have mastery over everything. What field of knowledge is there that you do not command? You have mastery over medicine, over everything. He would smile and give no answer. Once he was traveling with this student in Iran during a time of snowfall. While traveling they reached a certain place and saw that it was the time of *maghrib* (the evening prayer). From a distance they saw a person, the snow having settled, breaking through the snow to make water, so that he could perform *wudu* with it. Both of them watched this scene. Then Avicenna said to that student: you always used to ask me why I do not claim prophethood, that if I were to claim it people would not deny me. Today I give you the answer. Three hundred years have passed since the passing of the Messenger, may Allah's blessings be upon him, and yet in his *shari'ah* (sacred law) there is such an effect, upon his very soul, that today this person, without caring for his own life, is breaking the snow and performing *wudu*.

Before that, the night prior, these two were staying at a traveler's inn. When they went to sleep that night, Avicenna became thirsty. He said to the student, "Please give me some water." The water pot was kept in the veranda, and one had to go outside to fetch it. The student said, "Mawlana, it is very cold. You can drink the water in the morning." So the next day Avicenna mentioned this: you were saying that I should make such a claim, yet my own state is such that I could not get my dear student to rise and fetch me water. And this person, three hundred years later, following the *shari'ah* of the Messenger, is breaking the snow and performing *wudu*. That was the state of it.
