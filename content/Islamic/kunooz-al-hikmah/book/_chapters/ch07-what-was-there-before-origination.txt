# What Was There Before Origination

In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. And in Him we seek help. May Allah's blessings and peace be upon His Messenger, Sayyidna Muhammad, and upon the family of Muhammad.

The book is *Kunooz al-Hikmah al-madhkura li-arbab al-'ismah* (The Treasures of Wisdom Mentioned for the People of 'Ismah), a work of Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Hibatullah al-Mukarrami. What follows is the first question and its answer.

*Al-su'al al-awwal* (The first question). In the copy at hand the heading reads *awwalan al-su'al al-awwal* (Firstly, the first question).

The questioner said:

"*Inni basartu wa ta'ammaltu fima tafaddaltum bihi min kutub awliya' Allah wa ba'd al-mufidin, lam ajid ma dallani 'ala ma kana qabla huduth 'alam al-ibda'.*"

"Indeed I have seen and contemplated that which you have graciously provided from the books of the friends of God and from some of the benefactors, and I did not find that which would guide me to what existed before the origination of the World of Origination."

He had reflected and pondered upon the matter with which he had been graciously favored, from the books of the *awliya' Allah* (friends of God) and from the benefactors with whom he had studied. Even after that study he had not found what would indicate what existed before the *'Alam al-Ibda'* (World of Origination).

He continued: *wa kayfa kana al-hal wa ma al-majal wa al-minwal?* (And what was the state of affairs, and what was the scope and the manner?) What matters were occurring before the *'Alam al-Ibda'*, what things were taking place, and what were the conditions there? *Wa mimma hadathat 'uqul 'alam al-ibda'?* (And from what did the Intellects of the World of Origination come into being?) As for the Intellects of the *'Alam al-Ibda'*, what pertained to them?

Then he offered what he had already gathered: *fa-qad ra'ayna fi kutub awliya' Allah wa afadna anna al-Imam hijabuhu al-khass min riyad al-hadrah* (For we have seen in the books of the friends of God, and we have benefited from the fact that the Imam, his special veil, is from the gardens of the Presence). That is, the Imam's *hijab al-khass* (special veil) is fashioned from the *hadarat* (presences); it is made from the *riyad* (gardens) of the *hadarat*.

Al-Walid 'Abdullah Sahib used always to say that one should not rely upon the question but upon the answer, upon what the Da'i is answering. The questioner here has asked about the Imam's *hijab al-khass*, and in a broad reckoning there are three types of *hijab*: *tabi'i* (natural), *dhilli* (shadowy), and *khass* (special). Beyond these is the *dhat-i Imam* (essence of the Imam). His further question is how this has been set out in the *tasbih* (prayer beads). In the *tasbih*, the first utterance is *Allahu Akbar* (God is the greatest), and this points toward the *hijab al-adna* (lowest veil), which is also called the *hijab al-tabi'i* (natural veil) or the *hijab al-tanasuli* (procreative veil). The second portion of the thirty-three is the *hijab al-dhilli* (shadowy veil). The third portion, of *Subhanallah* (Glory be to God), is the *hijab al-khass* (special veil). At the end comes the Imam, who is the *dhat-i Imam*. Thus these three states are passed through.

The questioner has therefore posed two matters: first, what existed before the *'Alam al-Ibda'*; and second, what were the circumstances of the *'uqul* (intellects) of the *'Alam al-Ibda'* when they came, and what befell them. Alongside this he presents his own conception, that the *hijab al-khass* is made from the *riyad al-hadrah* (gardens of the presence).

He went on: *wa hijab al-la'iq al-tabi'i min riyad hujaj al-jaza'ir* (and the fitting natural veil is from the gardens of the Hujaj of the Islands), meaning that the veil which comes after it and is joined to it, the *tabi'i*, is made from the *riyad* of the *hujaj al-jaza'ir* (Proofs of the Islands). *Ta'wi ila ba'd nisa' al-hijab al-zawahir 'ala hadhihi al-kayfiyyah wujuduhu* (resorting to some of the women of the outward veil upon this modality of its existence). Its existence is in this modality. His point is that the one who performs the act of marriage with wives is the *hijab al-tabi'i* of the Imam. *Wa yadharu bi-khalqihi wa min khalqihi fi khalqihi* (And he appears through his creation, from his creation, and in his creation). That veil appears from creation, from the side of creation, and within creation, in that it is made from *riyad*, and the *riyad* are from the *makhluqat* (creatures). Once the veils are formed, they come into human form; those veils, those *riyad*, take human form.

He asked further: *wa kayfa kana qabla wujud al-samawat wa al-ard* (And how was it before the existence of the heavens and the earth), that is, before the existence of the skies and the earth, what things were there? *Wa qad 'alimtu mimma wujudha* (and I have known from what its existence is): the manner in which the existence of the heavens and the earth came to be is known to him. *Wa min ayyi fudulatin wujud al-awwal al-jussa' fi safil al-ard* (and from what remnants is the existence of the first particle in the low part of the earth). From what remnants did the *awwal jussa'*, the first particle of the *karr*, come into existence? It was created from the clod in the earth. The one that comes at the beginning of the *karr* is created from the clod in the earth, and so it holds a great station. From what remnants, then, did it come, given that there was nothing at that time? *Fa-tafaddal bi al-jawab* (So graciously provide the answer).

*Al-jawab 'ala dhalika al-su'al* (The answer to that question). The Da'i, Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Hibatullah, responds:

*Naqulu bi-'awnillah al-kabir al-muta'al* (We say with the help of Allah, the Great, the Exalted). *Al-Kabir al-Muta'al* is also a *tasbih* recited on Thursdays. *Wa minnati waliyy al-'asr wa al-zaman, 'alayhi salawat al-rahim al-rahman* (and from the favor of the master of the age and time, upon him be the blessings of the Merciful, the Compassionate). Here *rahim* has come first. In the Quran *rahim* has never come first; *rahman* has always preceded it. In *Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim*, among the various interpretations, *al-rahman* in one interpretation points to Mawlana 'Abd al-Muttalib, and *rahim* refers to Mawlana Abu Talib. Mawlana 'Abd al-Muttalib was certainly the greater of the two: he is the *jadd al-qa'im* (grandfather of the Qa'im), the *jadd al-'ayn* (grandfather of the 'ayn), and the *jadd al-'ayn al-akhirah* (grandfather of the final 'ayn) as well. *Wa ta'yidi mawlay al-'alam wa sirrihi al-bahir al-mu'azzam* (and with the support of my master of the world and his manifest and magnified secret): from this support the answer is given.

*Anna al-jawab 'ala dhalika qad sabaqa min mawla al-malik 'alam al-iman fi kitabihi al-sharif al-mawsum bi-kanz al-'ulum* (the answer to that has already been given by the master, the king, the flag of faith, in his noble book named *Kanz al-'Ulum*, The Treasure of Knowledge). The answer is already present in the book of Sayyidna Hibatullah, in *Hibat al-Rahman*, in his noble *Kanz al-'Ulum*. It is a very special book. In *Kanz al-'Ulum* there is a passage concerning Bibi Fatima al-Zahra to the effect that her station and greatness are such that even the A'immah Tahirin (the pure Imams) could not attain her gnosis. For this very reason the later Imams are called *A'immah Fatimiyyin* (the Fatimid Imams). The Imams up to the twenty-first are called *A'immah Fatimiyyin* because they are perplexed and bewildered by the station of Bibi Fatima al-Zahra.

The answer proceeds: *'alayka ya ayyuha al-arshad bi al-jawab al-awwala 'ala al-awwal bi al-qawl al-'ali al-mumajjad, ma fihi kifayah kafiyah wa ni'ma al-shafiyah* (Upon you, O rightly guided one, is the first answer to the first question, with the high and glorified saying, in which there is sufficient sufficiency, and what an excellent cure). The saying is high and great, in it there is sufficiency, and it is a cure.

That saying is: *anna wujud 'alam al-ibda' la min shay'in wa-la fi shay'in wa-la yajibu al-bahth 'an kayfiyyatihi* (the existence of the World of Origination is not from a thing, and not in a thing, and it is not required to inquire about its modality). The *Mubdi' Ta'ala* (Originator, the Exalted) created the *'Alam al-Ibda'*, and the natural assumption would be that there was a world within which the intellects were created. It is not so. Mawlana 'Iddan Sahib also used to say that the world and the intellects are not two separate things. Rather, *'alam al-ibda' hum al-'uqul* (the World of Origination is the Intellects themselves). The *'Alam al-Ibda'* is those very intellects whom Allah Ta'ala created. It is without any thing and not within any thing. It is therefore not obligatory, nor even permissible, to inquire into the modality of the *sudur al-ibda'* (emanation of the origination), how the *ibda'* came into existence. *Wa-la su'al idh laysa li al-'aql fihi majal* (and no question, since the intellect has no scope in it). Human intellects cannot reach that question of how the *ibda'* emanated, how it was created, how it appeared. The matter lies far beyond them. *Wa fi al-ajwibah 'an dhalika dalal wa ishrak* (and in the answers to that is misguidance and polytheism). Whoever attempts an answer, saying that it happened in this way or that, falls into misguidance and *shirk* (polytheism). *Wa tahawwutun fi bahr al-halak* (and a plunge into the sea of destruction). Whoever speaks of it falls into the sea of destruction, since it is not even known how the *'Alam al-Ibda'* came into existence. *Na'udhu billahi min dhalika wa nas'aluhu al-nijah min al-mahalik* (We seek refuge in Allah from that, and we ask Him for salvation from the places of destruction).

*Li-anna dhalika mu'allal fi-man la tajasar nahwahu al-khawatir* (because that is substantiated for one towards whom thoughts do not dare to venture). That matter is proven by evidence of a kind that the *khawatir*, the passing thoughts of the heart, cannot dare approach: how the *Mubdi' Ta'ala* created the *'Alam al-Ibda'*, and what that modality was. Of its modality there can be no knowledge. *Wa-la tuhitu bihi al-awham wa al-dama'ir* (and estimations and inner thoughts do not encompass it). The *awham* (estimations) and *dama'ir* (inner thoughts) cannot encompass that modality. *Wa yahtaj al-'arif li-dhalika 'ala an yakuna ma'ahu surah aw 'aql aw wahm qabla huduth al-ibda'* (And the gnostic needs for that knowledge that he should have with him a form, an intellect, or an estimation from before the origination of the ibda'). For the gnostic to know what was there before the *ibda'*, he would need some form, intellect, or estimation belonging to what came before it. *Aw fikrah bal suduruhu 'an al-huwiyyah al-muta'aliyah, la min shay'in* (or a thought; rather its emanation is from the Exalted Identity, not from a thing). This much alone is to be known: that the emanation of the *'Alam al-Ibda'* came about through the *huwiyyah al-muta'aliyah* (Exalted Identity), that is, the *Mubdi' Ta'ala*, by His own means, and not from any pre-existing thing.

*Wa qad awdaha al-du'at sababu qillati al-wusul ila ma'rifati dhalika* (And the du'at have clarified the reason for the scarcity of access to the gnosis of that). The *du'at* have explained that access to its gnosis is all but non-existent. *Dhalika li-'ajz al-'uqul al-ibda'iyyah* (that is due to the inability of the originated intellects). The *'uqul ibda'iyyah* whom the *Mubdi' Ta'ala* created, the First Intellect, the Second Intellect, and those that follow, are themselves at a loss to know how the *Mubdi' Ta'ala* brought this about. *Fa-kayfa bi-man dunahum min al-suwar al-daniyah* (So how about those below them from the lowly forms?). Human forms are very lowly beside the intellects of the *'Alam al-Ibda'*; how then could that modality be known through them?

The answer then turns to the veils. *Wa amma al-hijab al-'ali alladhi huwa al-nasut al-khass al-hawi li al-sharaf wa al-ikhtisas wa al-nasut al-tabi'i al-dani, fa-khudh haqiqata ma'rifatiha min awwal al-tamhid* (As for the high veil, which is the special human form containing honor and specialty, and the natural, lowly human form, take the reality of its gnosis from the beginning of the preface). The *hijab al-'ali* (high veil) is the *nasut al-khass* (special human form), which holds honor and specialty and is therefore *khass*; the *nasut al-tabi'i* (natural human form) is the lowest *nasut*. The reality of both is to be taken from the preface at the very opening of this book, where it is written: "All praise is for Allah, who made the *nasut al-khass* from the *riyadat ahl al-ikhtisas* (gardens of the people of specialty)." The *nasut al-khass* is made from the *ahl al-ikhtisas* (people of specialty), that is, from the *riyadat* (spiritual gardens) of the *hadarat* (presences) and the *hudud* (ranks) of the da'wah; this is the term used for them. *Wa ja'ala al-nasut al-dani min riyadat man dunahum min al-mawani* (And He made the lowly human form from the gardens of those below them in station). The lowly *nasut*, the *tabi'i*, is made from those of lesser station. The questioner's view that the *hujaj al-jaza'ir* are the source of the *nasut al-tabi'i* is thereby corrected as a wrong conception, and its reality is to be understood from that first preface. *Tataddahu laka ma'ani* (its meanings will become clear to you). *Wa sun dhalika yasunka Allah fa-huwa min al-sirr al-rabbani* (And guard that, may Allah guard you, for it is from the Lordly secret). These matters, concerning the veils and the *nasut*, how they originated and from what they are made, constitute a great *sirr al-rabbani* (Lordly secret).

*Wa amma qawluka inna al-nasut al-tabi'i al-zahir min hujaj al-jaza'ir fa-huwa minka ghalat* (As for your saying that the manifest natural human form is from the Hujaj of the Islands, that is a mistake on your part). The conception that the *nasut al-tabi'i*, the most manifest one, is made from the *hujaj al-jaza'ir* is wrong. *Wa man i'taqada dhalika fa-qad taradda wa saqat* (And whoever believes that has been rejected and has fallen). *Bal al-awwal fi dhalika ayyuha al-sa'il innahu yajtami'u fi kulli nasutin riyadatu jami' al-sa'irin ila al-lahut min 'alin wa danin* (Rather, the first principle in that, O questioner, is that in every human form gather the spiritual gardens of all the travelers to the Divine Realm, from the high and the low). In every *nasut* there gather the *riyadat* of all who are traveling *ila al-lahut* (to the Divine Realm), meaning all believers, whether of high station or low.

*Hasbama amma 'anhu sayyiduna al-da'i sarimuddin ba'd kutubihi al-'aliyah wa ghayruhu min mawalinna al-du'at 'ala al-lawh al-sharif al-altaf fi al-majam' al-nuraniyyah* (according to what our master the Da'i Sarimuddin implied in some of his high books, and others from our masters the du'at, upon the noble and subtle tablet, in the luminous collections). Sarimuddin here is Sayyidna Ibrahim ibn Muhammad ibn Fahd, and besides him other *du'at* have also pointed to this matter. The *riyadat* ascend within the *nasut*, and the *surat* (forms) ascend within the *lahut*. The point of the statement is that the *nasut* of the Imam, within which the *riyadat* ascend, and the *lahut* of the Imam, within which the *surat* ascend, stand in perfect correspondence. It is not that the *riyadat* which go to the place of the ear in the Imam's *nasut* arrive at some different place in the *lahut*, such as the heart or a lower station. Wherever the *riyadat* go in the *nasut*, there the *surat* go in the *lahut*.

*Murad al-hijab al-khass huwa al-mubtana fi al-bayt al-haram* (The intended meaning of the special veil is that which is built in the Sacred House). The *nasut-i khass* has its foundation laid in the *Bayt al-Haram* (Sacred House of God). *Huwa alladhi la ta'zibuhu qara'ib al-aslab wa al-arham* (it is that which the proximities of loins and wombs do not confine). This *nasut-i khass* is not bound by loins and wombs; it is not born of a mother and father. Its construction and making take place in the Sacred House of God. The *tabi'i*, by contrast, is born of a mother and father. *Wa-la tahwih afniyah wa-la dur* (and neither courtyards nor houses contain it). No courtyard, no ground, no house can contain the *nasut-i khass*; it is so exalted that it lies beyond all these. *Wa-la yaliju fi 'idam al-qubur* (And it does not enter into the bones of the graves). At the time of death the *nasut-i khass* does not descend into the grave. That is the Imam's affair. *Wa-lladhi yaqa'u 'alayhi al-mawt wa al-qatl wa al-fawt huwa adna min al-hujub* (And the one upon whom death, killing, and passing away occur, it is the lowest of the veils). *Li-yaqdiya ma 'alayhi wa yajib* (so that it may fulfill what is upon it and is obligatory). The states that befall fall upon this lowest veil, not upon the *nasut-i khass*. So it was with Imam Husayn: the killing and all that took place befell the veils; nothing befell the real self. The real self is protected.

As for which specific *hijab* was in use at a given time, no mention of it is found anywhere. Hadrat Gyani Sahib muqaddas also used to ask whether anyone had read anywhere which veil came into use and when, but it has not been found in any source. Concerning Rasulullah, salawatullah 'alayh, however, it is known that the veil used for his *ghusl* (ritual bath) was other than the one that was buried. The *ghusl* veil was 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, and the one buried was 'Abdullah ibn Rawahah. The one present for *ziyarat* (visitation) is Ubayy ibn Ka'b. These are their own *hijabs* that are buried. Ubayy ibn Ka'b holds a great station; he is also among the teachers of Rasulullah. As it is said, Rasulullah himself said that Ubayy is his spiritual mother, *ummi al-ruhaniyyah*, his first teacher, who taught him all the laws and principles of Adam, alayhis salam. After that teaching Rasulullah became *mamnun* (indebted). In the same way five teachers taught him, and after their teaching they became *mamnun*. The final teacher was Janab-i Khadijah, before the *ba'thah*. Janab-i Khadijah's position is that she became *mamnun* to Rasulullah, and she became teacher not only of Sayyidna Rasulullah but of Janab-i 'Ali as well. For this reason it is said, *Ibn Maryam* (son of Mary): *'Isa Ibn Maryam* (Jesus son of Mary) is Janab-i 'Ali, and Maryam is Janab-i Khadijah. For this very reason Janab-i Maryam was exempted from the five prayers. The five prayers were exempted because, after the five prayers, Janab-i 'Ali joined, on account of her being his teacher.
