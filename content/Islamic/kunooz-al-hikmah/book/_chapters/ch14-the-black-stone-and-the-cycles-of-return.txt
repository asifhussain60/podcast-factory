# The Black Stone and the Cycles of Return

In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. And in Him we seek help, and may Allah's blessings and peace be upon His Messenger, our Master Muhammad, and the family of Muhammad.

These two are the counterparts of the Kalima-e-Shahadat already mentioned. Sayyidna Ali bin Hibatullah said:

"fa-sun hadha al-sirr al-masun, yasunaka Allah, wa-hfaz ma ulqiya ilayka, yahfazaka Allah, wa hadha min al-asrar al-'izam allati la yakad yastur bi'l-aqlam, wa dhalika mimma ajrahu."

"Guard this preserved secret, and Allah will guard you. Preserve what has been cast upon you, and Allah will preserve you. This is among the great secrets that pens can scarcely set down, and it is from what He has caused to flow."

These are matters of the most magnificent secrets, of a nature that cannot be committed to writing. Whatever has been written here flowed upon the tongue by the grace of the Mawla. Sayyidna Hibatullah said of it:

"wa lam yakun qabla dhalika tara fi qalbi wa janani, wa hiya nuqtah min bahri 'ulumihi wa hibatihi, wa tuhfatun min tuhafihi, wa sadaqa min sadaqatihi, wa qad saqatha ayyuha al-... ilayka hadiyah mubaradah bi-ghayri ta'abin 'alayka, wa mathaluha la yahsul illa bi'l-ibtila wa'l-hijr wa'l-hijrat wa'l-firaq li'l-ahl wa'l-awtan, fa-ahmad mawlaka fi ghuduwwika wa masaka."

"Before this, these matters had not arisen in my heart or my soul. It is a single drop from the ocean of his knowledges and his gifts, a gift from among his gifts, a charity from his charities. These things have reached you, O brother, as a cooling gift that brings peace, without any toil on your part. Their like is not attained except through trial, through separation and emigrations, through leaving one's family, one's cities, and one's homelands. So praise your Master in your morning and in your evening."

No effort was made, and yet this came; while seated in one place, what others obtain only through exile was obtained.

Then the third question: "qad 'alimtu mimma jawwaba Zain al-'Abidin 'ala Umm al-Nada min qabli... 'umr al-dunya" — "I have already taken note of what Mawlana Zain al-'Abidin answered to Umm al-Nada concerning the age of the world." Umm al-Nada was a woman of great standing, among the Hudud al-Da'wat and a woman of deep learning. She came to Imam Zain al-'Abidin (ع) and said, "Mawlana, tell me how much time has passed over the world, and how much remains." He replied, "I will tell you how much has passed, but I will not tell you how much remains. You do not have the istihqaq (merit) for that." He then told her that one mawr had passed, and that they were living within the kawr, the dawr, of the second mawr.

The questioner continued: "wa qad qala Sayyidna al-Qadi Sarim al-Din qaddas Allahu nafsahu wa razaqna shafa'atahu bi-ridaka 'anna, anna al-rukn min fudulat Amir al-Mu'minin al-qadimah fi al-akwar al-ula" — that Sayyidna al-Qadi Sarim al-Din, may Allah sanctify his soul and grant us the provision of his intercession and be pleased with us, has said that the Rukn is from the ancient remnants of the Commander of the Faithful in the first cycles. Sarim al-Din is Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad Faad, the questioner's grandfather. The Rukn al-As'ad, spelled alif, sin, 'ayn, dal, is the most blessed corner of the Baytullah, the corner in which the Hajar al-Aswad is set. It is said that this Rukn al-As'ad is composed of the fudulat (remnants) of Amir al-Mu'minin from the first akwar (cycles). This was set down "fi waraqatayni bi-khatti Sayyidi Safi al-Din Ahmad bin Isma'il" — "in two pages in the hand of Safi al-Din Ahmad bin Isma'il," who was among the Hudud of that era, "fi awwal al-jussah al-mudhamah," in the first great corporeal form. In those first akwar, Amir al-Mu'minin underwent the raj'at (return) repeatedly, born and then passing away, and through successive cycles their accumulated fudulat formed the Rukn al-As'ad.

From this arose the further questions: "wa fi ayyi kawr zahara amruhu, wa kayfa kana zuhuruhu, wa hal yaqi'u 'alayhi al-subhaniyyah... wa kam min hadha 'umr al-dunya" — "In which cycle did his affair appear, and in what manner did it manifest? And does the term Subhaniyyah apply to him? And how much of the age of the world has this encompassed?" That is, when one says "Subhan Allah," is it permissible to apply the word "Subhan" to Amir al-Mu'minin as well?

A questioner raised a difficulty concerning the fudulat of Amir al-Mu'minin: the A'immah are all ri'yat, and from the perspective of the purity of their din, fudulat are not generated in their case at all.

The answer given was that the question is a good one. By fudulat here is not meant what fudulah signifies in the case of an ordinary Mumin. In the case of the A'immah, all such matter becomes ri'yat and departs; within three days what remains is confined to the grave, and the rest becomes flowing, becoming ri'yat. What is meant here by fudulat is their remaining parts. As was explained in a previous lesson, their ri'yat, which are stored, are housed in the Baytullah and divided into five portions. One portion goes to the Hudud al-Da'wat, through which they arrive at correct decisions, are preserved from evils, and their standard in taqwa (piety) is elevated. That one-fifth portion of their ri'yat is also called fudulat in one sense, and it is returned when the Imam passes away, those things returning to the Baytullah. So by fudulat here is meant their ri'yat in a progressed state, and those progressed ri'yat come to the Baytullah.

The questioner objected that this has taken a material form: the Rukn al-As'ad has been formed, a stone has been formed. The answer was that it is not material in that sense. The nature of the Rukn al-As'ad itself is lofty; the blessings within the stone are what is being pointed to.

The questioner then said that the teaching regarding the blessed stones, 'aqiq (carnelian) and yaqut (ruby), which are the fudulat of the Du'at Kiram, is readily understandable, but that the fudulat of Mawla Ali and the A'immah Kiram are all ri'yat. The answer was that fudulat in the ordinary sense are not generated in their case; in one sense alone are they called fudulat, being the remaining matter left from their bodies, and sometimes ri'yat are also called fudulat. Those fudulat progress through the moon and ascend to Jupiter; one part of them descends and forms the body of the coming Imam, and some part comes to the Baytullah, where one-fifth is received by the Hudud al-Da'wat. And it also occurs that the fudulat of Amir al-Mu'minin, which have progressed and exist in a purified state, come to the Rukn al-As'ad of the Baytullah. The word fudulat in this passage raised the same question when it was first read; what is meant by it here is ri'yat.

The question closed: "wa qad tabaddala al-mashriq maghriban wa'l-shimal junuban. tafaddalu bi'l-jawab al-shafi. tawwala Allahu subhanahu bi-a'marikum."

"And the east has become the west, and the north has become the south. Do us the favor of the sufficient answer. May Allah, glory be to Him, lengthen your lives."

The answer: "al-jawab 'ala dhalika al-su'al, naqulu bi-'awn Allah al-Kabir al-Muta'al wa minnat Wali al-'Asr wa'l-Zaman, 'alayhi salawat al-Rahim al-Rahman" — "The answer to that question: we say, with the help of Allah, the Great, the Most High, and with the grace of the Master of the Age and the Time, upon whom be the blessings of the Merciful, the Compassionate" — and with the support of Mawla al-A'lam and his brilliant and magnificent secret, that is, the support of Hibatullah Sahab. "awrada Sayyidna Sarim al-Din... bi-anna al-Hajar al-Aswad min kathif Amir al-Mu'minin al-muwajjad" — "Sayyidna Sarim al-Din has presented the matter thus: that the Hajar al-Aswad is from the kathif of Amir al-Mu'minin al-muwajjad," may Allah sanctify him in 'Illiyyin. By kathif (dense substance) is meant those ri'yat which are purified and which find a place in various noble stations. One such noble station is that they become the very body of the Imam, the body of the Imam who is to come. Throughout his life, the food consumed by the present Imam, and the food consumed by the future child, all of it is ri'yat. And because the Mu'minin are connected to those Imams and profess their walayat (allegiance), they receive a share of that sustenance.

So it is in Surah al-Furqan, in the verses concerning 'ibad al-Rahman (servants of the Merciful):

"wa-j'alna li'l-muttaqin imama"

"And make us an Imam for the righteous."

A man was once circumambulating the Baytullah reciting this verse, for it appears among the verses of tawaf (circumambulation). Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq, or Mawlana Ali Zain al-'Abidin, said to that man: "You have asked Allah for something far beyond your station. You cannot become the Imam of the righteous; you do not have that standing." Though it is a Quranic verse, in du'a (supplication) one should instead say "wa-j'alna min al-muttaqin," make us from among the righteous.

"fa-dhalika huwa al-haqq alladhi la shakka ya'tarihi wa'l-sirr al-khafi alladhi la maryata fihi."

"That is the truth which no doubt can befall, and the hidden secret in which there is no ambiguity."

Then: "wa amma qawluka, wa fi ayyi kawr zahara sallallahu 'alayhi wa 'alihi al-ghur" — "As for your question about in which kawr he appeared, blessings upon him and his luminous family" — "faqad qala al-Nabi 'alayhi al-salam, ana ibn al-karrat wa mumtahin al-awqat," the Prophet (ع) said:

"ana ibn al-karrat wa mumtahin al-awqat."

"I am the son of the recurring cycles and the one tried by the times."

Birth has come across various recurring cycles, and testing across various ages. In one narration he said: "Allah tested me seven times before the risalat (prophethood), and seven times after." Amir al-Mu'minin likewise says: "Rasulullah tested me seven times before making me his wasi (successor), and seven times after." He recited the full list of all fourteen tests in a single gathering, asking, "Did this not happen?" and the entire gathering testified: "Yes, you speak the truth; it happened just so." The Awliya Allah (friends of Allah) all pass through such tests.

Testing is intrinsic to the system of the da'wat (mission). Until a person is made to pass through a test, he is not established in a rank. Sayyidna Yusuf bin Sulayman, the first Da'i in Hindustan and the grandfather of Sayyidna Sulayman Sahab, was a man of great standing and magnificence. The last Da'i of the Bani Anf was Sayyidna Da'i Muhammad Husayn, after whom no further du'at came from that line, and he put Yusuf bin Sulayman through a most severe test. Yusuf bin Sulayman lived in a distant village and would come early each morning to study with Sayyidna Sahab. One day he arrived and the hajib (chamberlain), the guard of Sayyidna Muhammad bin Husayn, told him to go back, saying the Sayyidna did not wish to receive him. For forty days he came continuously at his usual hour, and each time he was turned away. On the fortieth day the hajib said, "Go away." But at that very moment the Da'i called out from his veranda: "Let Yusuf come to me." He came, and the Da'i embraced him. This was the test. In this way, through various such trials, he was made ready.

Sayyidna Husayn bin Isma'il once remarked that there were two periods in the da'wat when the Hindustani muhajirin (emigrants) outnumbered the muhajirin of Yemen and Najran: the time of Sayyidna Yusuf bin Sulayman, and the time of Sayyidna Hibatullah Sahab.

"wa qad su'ila Mawlana al-Sadiq haythu qala, kunna fi'l-adwar al-madiyyah wa'l-huqub al-khaliyah."

"Mawlana al-Sadiq was asked, and he said: We were in the past cycles and the bygone eras."

The cycles that have passed, in them there was presence. Of Aristotle he said:

"Kana Aba Muhammad Aristatalis minna Ahl al-Bayt."

"Aba Muhammad Aristotle was from us, the People of the House."

Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq even mentioned his kunyah (patronymic): Aristatalis, whose title was Aba Muhammad, was from the Ahl al-Bayt. So too he said of Salman al-Farsi:

"Salman minna Ahl al-Bayt, yusalla wa yusallam 'alayhi."

"Salman is from us, the People of the House; blessings and peace are to be sent upon him."

The blessings sent upon them are sent upon him. The station of Janab Salman is therefore very lofty, for he is the only person in the entire da'wat, throughout the entire Dawr al-Sitr, who became the Bab al-Abwab (Gate of Gates) for both the Nabi and the Wasi. He became the Bab al-Abwab for the Prophet, the Master of the Universe. Janab Khadijah had also become a Bab al-Abwab, and Salman al-Farsi (ع) similarly held that rank, and he likewise became the Bab al-Abwab of Janab Ali (ع).

"fa-qala lahu al-sa'il, ya Mawlai, kuntum bi-ashkhasiukum am bi-a'yanikum. fa-qala lahu bal bi-ashkhasiina wa a'yanina."

"So the questioner said to him: O my Master, were you there in your persons or in your representatives? And he said to him: Rather, in our persons and our representatives."

That is, were you present with your real selves or with your hijabs (veils)? The answer: with the real selves and with the hijabs both.

"wa amma al-Hajar al-Aswad wa'l-Rukn al-As'ad fa-qad yakun wallahu a'lam anna qawl Sayyidna Sarim al-Din innahu ittasala bihi shay'un latif mimma dakhkhiratuhu al-'inayat al-ilahiyyah min hujub Amir al-Mu'minin al-mukanna 'anhu bi'l-'ayn al-'azimah al-nuraniyyah."

"As for the Black Stone and the Most Fortunate Corner, it may be, and Allah knows best, that the statement of Sayyidna Sarim al-Din is that a subtle substance was connected with it from what the divine providence stored from the veils of the Commander of the Faithful, who is alluded to as the Great, Luminous 'Ayn."

That is, a subtle substance, from what was stored by the divine providence, namely the jussah-e-ibtidaiyyah of 'Ashir, drawn from the hijabs of Amir al-Mu'minin, who is called the 'Ayn al-'Azimah, the luminous one, was connected to his real self.

"wa amma qawluka wa hal yaqi'u 'alayhi al-subhaniyyah, faqad sabbahatuhu al-mala'ikah al-qudsaniyyah haythu tashawwaqat ila tasawwur shakhsihi al-gharr, fa-awjada Allah ta'ala subhanahu fi'l-samawati mithaluhu, fa-kanat tasbihuhu wa taqdisuhu laylan wa naharan 'ashiyyatan wa abkara, fa-kana hadha mithal 'azim."

"As for your statement, does the term Subhaniyyah apply to him: the holy angels have glorified him, when they yearned for the perception of his brilliant person. So Allah the Exalted, glory be to Him, brought into existence his likeness in the heavens, and they glorified and sanctified him night and day, evening and morning. And this was a great example."

The angels yearned to perceive his brilliant person, to behold him. In the spiritual realm they sought from Allah the didar (vision) of Amir al-Mu'minin, for though he is corporeally present on earth, it was to behold him in the spiritual world and in the heavens that they longed. So Allah created a likeness of him in the heavens, and the angels glorified and sanctified him morning and evening, night and day.

"fa-kayfa bi-khalifat al-'Ashir al-Hakim."

"So how is it with the Caliph of the 'Ashir, the Wise?"

As the Quranic verse says:

"fi'l-sama'i ilahun wa fi'l-ardi ilah."

"In the heaven is a god, and on the earth is a god."

This points to the state of Amir al-Mu'minin: in heaven there is an Ali and his likeness, and on earth there is an Ali whose likeness is in heaven.

"fa-kayfa bi-khalifat al-'Ashir al-Hakim wa ma-ndimamuhu fi dimn al-Qa'im al-hashir al-nashir."

"So how is it with the Caliph of the 'Ashir, the Wise, and his inclusion within the Qa'im, the Gatherer, the Resurrector?"

His inclusion took place with the Qa'im; he became the Bab al-Abwab for the Qa'im. The A'immah who came before, from Janab Hunayn to Amir al-Mu'minin, and the thalathah sufarah (the three emissaries), namely Mawlana 'Abd al-Muttalib, Mawlana 'Abdullah, and Mawlana Abu Talib, did not have their inclusion through Amir al-Mu'minin. Rather, they remained in the barzakh (intermediate state) of the Janab-e-Qa'im, and the Qa'im absorbed them directly, not through Amir al-Mu'minin. They hold a very high status. Up to Janab Hashim, Amir al-Mu'minin gathered the hayakil (structures) of the sufarah-e-nayyirah (luminous emissaries). And after that, he gathered the hayakil from Janab Hasan to Abul-Qa'im, who became the Bab al-Abwab of Qa'im (ع). It has never happened that an Imam gathered the A'immah who came after him; yet this is among the extraordinary miracles of Amir al-Mu'minin, that he gathered the hayakil of the Imams who came later as well. His nafs-e-hissiyah (sensory soul) was so powerful and spiritual that it was greater than the hayakil of others, for only one whose nafs-e-hissiyah surpasses another's haykal can gather it.

His hashr (gathering) was therefore with the Qa'im. The point is that he will become the caliph of the 'Ashir, the Mudabbir. By 'Ashir here is meant not that 'Ashir, but the jussah (body) of this kawr; yawman ma (one day), through a progression in the line that has been formed, he will become the khalifah (caliph) within it, the Caliph of the 'Ashir. "wa su'uduhu ila rutbat al-'Ashir," and his ascent to the rank of the 'Ashir: his ascent will be as the 'Ashir, and he will be the jussah-e-ibtidaiyyah in which he will come, yawman ma.

"sabbahathu al-mala'ikah al-ruhaniyyah bi-a'la min dhalika al-shan... li-man fatana al-ma'ani al-rabbaniyyah."

"The spiritual angels glorified him with a station higher than that, for one who comprehends the Lordly meanings."

They glorified him with a station higher than any he had held before, higher than what he had attained in the various recurring cycles. In the seventh dawr (period), in the seventh zuhur (manifestation), this manifestation of his was the seventh, higher than all previous manifestations. It was because it was his seventh manifestation that he performed those extraordinary miracles. The angels glorified him with a glorification surpassing all previous manifestations, for the one who reflects deeply upon the Lordly meanings.

"wa amma qawluka kam hatta tabaddala al-mashriq maghriban wa'l-shimal junuban, fa-yakunu dhalika 'inda tarakhi al-rubatat, wa takunu al-tabdilat wa'l-istihalat."

"As for your statement, how long until the east has changed into the west and the north into the south: that will be at the loosening of the ties, and the changes and transformations will occur."

The tarakhi al-rubatat is the end of the kawr. Very great changes will occur then, and major events will take place, such that things will be entirely transformed.

"wa amma 'iddat dhalika fa-qad dhakarahu Sayyidna Humam."

"As for the period of that, Sayyidna Humam has mentioned it."

The duration has been mentioned by Sayyidna Humam al-Din, Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan, and those matters are to be consulted with the permission of the Wali al-Amr.

"wa fi aqwal dhil-jaddayn ma yujli al-sada wa'l-rayn."

"And in the sayings of Dhul-Jaddayn is that which clears away the rust and the stain."

Dhul-Jaddayn is Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad, a figure of immense stature. He wrote the tafsir (exegesis) of the Quran in seventy days and nights, on the command of Sayyidna Ali Sahab, who commanded him because he was the son of Sayyidna Ali Sahab's sister. He was thirty-three years old when the command was given. He also performed Hajj on behalf of Sayyidna Ja'far, for Sayyidna Ja'far, despite his great status as the twenty-eighth Dai and the first among the shumukh du'at (lofty du'at), did not himself perform Hajj; he had once set out for it but had to turn back due to certain difficulties. Sayyidna Ali Sahab therefore commanded him: "You perform the Hajj of Sayyidna Sahab as a qada' (make-up) and complete it." He did so. And when Sayyidna Ali Sahab commanded, "Husayn, write the tafsir of the Quran," he wrote it in seventy days and nights, even while traveling throughout that period. When he had completed it, a very swift scribe from Yemen took eight months to copy it. It fills three large volumes. This is a magnificent state that Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad achieved. Although he was not a munfarid (solitary) dai, he holds immense standing; the du'at take tawassul (intercession) through him, and Sayyidna Isma'il bin Hibatullah Sahab refers to him as "Sayyidna Usama" in several places within the Kawakeb al-Durriyah, that great and magnificent book. Help is therefore to be sought from the sayings of Dhul-Jaddayn, which remove the rust that has settled upon hearts.
