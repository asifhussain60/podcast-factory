# The Mahdi, the Qa'im, and the Straight Path

The first question concerns *Mawlana Mehdi*, *sallallahu alayhi*. Two figures bear the name *Mehdi*. One is the eleventh Imam, who is *Mehdi*, and the other is the fiftieth Imam, who is likewise a *Mehdi*.

This fiftieth one is, in a sense, of the same order as *Rasulullah*, in that he does not fall within the circle of *Imamat*. So *Mehdi Akhir al-Zaman*, the last *Mehdi*, also stands outside that circle. He is neither an Imam nor the son of an Imam, yet his station (*shaan*) is immense. He is also called *Mim-e-Akhirah*. *Mim-e-Ula* is *Rasulullah*, *'ala dhikrihis salaam*, and *Mim-e-Akhirah* is *Mawlana Mehdi*, the fiftieth Imam. The parallel carries this significance: just as *Rasulullah* broke the chain (*silk*) of *Imamat*, the twenty-ninth person breaking that chain, so too did this *Mawlana Mehdi* break the chain of *Imamat*, because after the forty-ninth Imam the *Qa'im* was due to come, yet he came in between. Today's Imam is *Abul Qa'im*, and after *Abul Qa'im* the *Qa'im* should have followed; but *Mawlana Mehdi* came in between, owing to his great precedents (*sawabiqat*) and his exalted station.

The forefathers (*jadd*) of *Janab-e-Mehdi* were nonetheless Imams, just as the forefathers of *Rasulullah* were, though his immediate father was not.

A question was then raised, drawing upon a Qur'anic verse. God the Exalted said:

> *Ta'ruj al-mala'ikatu wa al-ruh ilayhi fi yawmin kana miqdaruhu khamsina alfa sanah*

The angels and the Spirit ascend to Him in a day whose measure is fifty thousand years.

*Fa ma'na al-yawm 'alayhi sallallahu alayhi wa al-dawr al-'adhim, dawr al-kashf*: the meaning of "the day" for him, (ع), is the great cycle, the cycle of unveiling (*kashf*). From *Janab-e-Mehdi* the cycle of *kashf* began; his cycle is called the cycle of *kashf*.

It is recalled that while studying with *Sayyidna Husayn bin Isma'il*, the notion arose that *Mehdi* and *Qa'im* are distinct. He said:

> *Al-Mehdi huwal Qa'im, wal Qa'im huwal Mehdi*

The Mehdi is the Qa'im, and the Qa'im is the Mehdi.

And he cautioned against treating them as two separate figures.

The question continued: *Fa al-ma'na 'alayhi sallallahu alayhi wa al-dawr al-'adhim, dawr al-kashf, wa lahu mutimmoona min waladihi, wa huwa ya'khudh zakaa*. The meaning for him, (ع), is the great cycle, the cycle of unveiling; and for him are completers (*mutimmin*) from his progeny; and he will receive the *khilafah* of *zakaa*, meaning the sun. The word *zakaa* appears in a *munajat* recited in *Sadaqallah*, composed by *Khassa 'Amir bin Sa'b*, where it is written: *Antum al-zakaa wa al-nasu*, "You are the sun, and the people are...", meaning that *Janab-e-Ali* holds the position of the sun while others hold the position of stars.

As for the term *mutimmin*: just as there were *mutimmin* at the close of the cycle of 'Isa, those who come at the end of a *dawr*, or at the beginning of a *dawr*, being called *mutimmin*, so in the progeny of *Mawlana Mehdi* there is a *mutim*.

*Wa kam takunu khidmatuhu? Wa ya'khudh khilafat al-'ashir*: And what will his service be? He will receive the *khilafah* of the *'Ashir*. *Tafaddal 'alayna ya mawda' al-sirri wa al-sara'ir*: Bestow upon us, O repository of secrets and hidden matters. *Jazakallahu afdal al-jaza'*: May Allah reward you with the best of rewards. *Bi-haqqi Sayyidina Muhammadin wa aalihi al-tahirin*: By the right of our master Muhammad and his pure family.

The answer follows. *Bi-'awnillahi subhanahu wa mannihi wa yusrihi wa ta'yidi mawlana al-Malik, mahbat ash'at al-mala'ik*: with the help of Allah, Glorified is He, and His grace and His ease, and the support of our master the King, namely *Sayyidna Hibatullah*, the place where the rays of the angels descend. *Anna mawlana al-Mehdi al-'adhim, alladhi huwa qa'im dawr al-sitr*: our master, the great *Mehdi*, is in truth the *Qa'im* of the cycle of concealment (*dawr al-sitr*).

*Wa lahu sha'nun 'adhim*, his station is very great; *wa fadlun jasim*, his virtue is immense. The author describes him not as a confluence (*jami'*) but as the *Qa'im* of the *dawr al-sitr*. *Wa huwa majma' thaman suwar, suwar 'idham samiyah*: he is the confluence of eight surahs, surahs of great and lofty rank. This rests upon the verse:

> *Wa yahmilu 'arsha rabbika fawqahum yawma'idhin thamaniyah*

And on that Day, eight will carry the Throne of your Lord above them.

That is, eight angels will bear the Divine Throne on the Day of Resurrection. *Wa sab'u suwarin fi dawr al-kashf al-madi li-hadha al-shakhs*: and seven surahs belong to the past cycle of unveiling for this person, a *surah* having come every seven thousand years in the past *dawr al-kashf*. On this matter *Sayyidna 'Ali bin Hanzala*, may Allah sanctify his soul, is cited: *Wa hiya yas'adu fi asna'ihi min suwar, sab'un min al-qism alladhi, 'azimah fi thalathat alaf fi dawr al-fatrah*, and it ascends in its midst from the surahs, seven from the portion which is great, in three thousand years in the period of interruption (*dawr al-fatrah*). These are three surahs, each representing one thousand years, yielding three in all.

*Wa hadha dawr al-sitr al-mutaqallibina fihi ahl al-sharra'*: this is the cycle of concealment, in which evil people have prevailed. *Wa yawmuhu huwa dawr al-asna'*: his day is the lofty cycle. *Wa al-imamah fihi mustamirrah*: the *Imamat* within it is continuous, as it was throughout the *dawr al-sitr*. *Min dhurriyyatihi, alladhina hum ma'ani al-asma' al-husna*: from his progeny, who are the meanings of the Most Beautiful Names.

*Wa dhakarta annahu ya'khudh zakaa, fa-laysa dhalika kadhalik. Bal alladhi ya'khudhuha ba'da intiha' al-rub'atati huwa al-rukn al-asham*: the questioner mentioned that he will receive the *khilafah* of the sun, but that is not so. Rather, the one who will receive it after the end of the four (*rub'atat*) is the lofty pillar (*rukn al-asham*), meaning the *rukn al-asad*, who will receive the *khilafah* of the sun.

*Fa-'rif dhalika wa ta'aqqal ma hunalika. Wa 'alayka bi-al-tasawwur al-mustaqim. Li-takuna min sukkan jannat al-na'im*: so understand this matter and comprehend what is there, and hold fast to straight understanding, so as to be among the dwellers of the Garden of Bliss.

*Wa amma al-Qa'im, 'alayhi salamu, wa tahiyyatuhu wa ikramuhu, fa-innahu 'inda firaqi min hadhihi al-dar, yakhluf al-'ashir*: as for the *Qa'im*, may peace and greetings and honor be upon him, upon departure from this abode, at the time of passing away, he will succeed the *'Ashir*.

A question arises concerning how long the *Qa'im* will remain in the world. Only three days. The *qiyam* of the *Qa'im* will last three days.

Whether the *Qa'im* and *Mehdi Akhir al-Zaman* are two distinct personalities: they are indeed two distinct personalities. The *Qa'im* of the *dawr al-sitr*, however, has been called *Mehdi*, and the station of *Mawlana Qa'im* is certainly greater than that of *Mehdi*. That *Qa'im*, peace be upon his mention, will receive the *khilafah* of the *'Ashir 'Aziz al-Jabbar*. The *'Ashir* here refers to the second *juz'i da'i* of this *kawr*. When he progresses beyond one *kawr*, he will become the *juz'i da'i* of the next *kawr*, and that will be the *Qa'im*, and that too in a *juz'i* capacity, a partial one, not in a *kulli*, total, capacity, for many others stand in the line ahead.

The opening of Surah Ya-Sin illumines this:

> *Yasin. Wal-Qur'an al-hakim, innaka la-mina al-mursalin, 'ala siratin mustaqim*

Ya-Sin. By the wise Qur'an, indeed you are from among the messengers, on a straight path.

Allah grants such greatness as to say that he is from among the messengers, and then adds that he is on the straight path. At first this seems incongruous, like introducing a man as a PhD professor holding degrees from ten universities and then adding that he has passed his matriculation examination. Yet the meaning becomes clear: *'ala siratin mustaqim* refers to that line, the line of *juz's*, and among those standing in it the Prophet, (ع), is one. Before him in that line stand the *Thalathat Kubara'*, the Three Great Ones, and after them it is he.

There is a further verse in which Allah says:

> *Inna rabbi 'ala siratin mustaqim*

Indeed, my Lord is on a straight path.

This is also reported as a saying of the Prophet, that his Lord is on the straight path, referring to Abu Talib, the one who raised him, affirming that he too is on that path. From all of this, *sirat mustaqim* signifies this very path of *juz's*; it is the straight path. Yet for the *A'immah al-Tahirin*, the Pure Imams, and for *Rasulullah*, the *sirat mustaqim* carries a different and higher meaning.

The recitation of *Ihdina al-sirat al-mustaqim*, "Guide us to the straight path," is a magnificent prayer. As *Haddrat al-Muqaddas* once explained, it does not merely mean "place us on the straight path" but rather "make us walk upon it," cause us to move forward along it. The chain extending from a *mu'min* to the Imam passes through both the lower ranks (*hudud daniyah*) and the higher ranks (*hudud 'aliyah*). To walk that path is to move swiftly toward the *Bab al-Abwab*, which is the destination of every *mu'min*'s spiritual form (*surah*). When that station of *Bab al-Abwab* is reached and its magnificent assembly, *fasl al-khitab*, the decisive speech, is attained, and when the Imam receives that assembly and conveys it to the *Farzand-e-Imam*, the son of the Imam, it becomes as it were a fixed deposit: wherever the *Farzand-e-Imam* goes, it accompanies them.

There are, therefore, two kinds of spiritual progress (*taraqqiyat*): one in which a person strives and advances through their own effort, and another in which one advances by sitting in the lap of another, resting in that nearness. Both are genuine forms of progress. The pace of advancement depends on each person's capacity, their *taqwa*, piety, and their *taharat*, purity. Furthermore, the *sadaqat*, charities, and good deeds performed after a *mu'min*'s passing, when children recite on his behalf and perform righteous acts, their benefits reach him as well, causing him to progress more swiftly from whatever station he has attained.

A human's *surah* is not divided among the Imams; all the units gather in one place. A *surah sharif* that is progressing keeps on progressing. Whatever comes into the world, as a person passes away and after a thousand years returns, comes back in the same way, but minus the vegetative soul (*namiyah*), minus the sensory soul (*hissiyah*), minus the rational soul (*natiqah*). Sometimes it even rises above his *moththalan ikhtira'i*, his archetypal form. Its progress is very slow, yet it keeps progressing. And in one *kawr* its manifestations (*zuhurat*) occur three hundred and sixty times, through the integration of the *walad*. For this reason *Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman* said that in the human body there are three hundred and sixty *nufus*. This means that one of his *nafs* progresses in one *kawr*, in one *dawr*. When his integration through grace takes place, then one part out of three hundred and sixty progresses.

In the *du'as* of *Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq*, which are to be recited daily, there occurs in the twenty-seventh *tariqah* the phrase *Innaka 'ala siratin mustaqim*, "Indeed, you are on a straight path." Its address appears to be to Allah, as though saying, "O Allah, you are on the straight path." The answer is the same as before. "Allah" there does not mean *Mubdi' Ta'ala*, the Originator, the Exalted, but the *juz'i da'i*, the partial missionary, of that *kawr*. The indication is toward him, that he too is on the *sirat mustaqim*, meaning that he stands in the line to take the *khilafah* of the *'Ashir*.

Everyone has to progress. *Allati ma yuhitu biha illa man safat suratuhu min al-rayb*: which cannot be encompassed except by one whose form has been purified from doubt. These matters can be understood, and encompassed, only by one whose *surah* has been completely purified from doubts and suspicions. *Wa tawalla amra 'alam al-tabi'ah*: and he took charge of the affair of the world of nature. Such ones become masters of the affairs of the natural world. *Wa yadharu fi afwaniha surur al-badi'ah… Fa-hafiz hadha al-jawab al-mukhtasar wa sunhu fa-innahu min al-sirr al-akbar*: and in its horizons appear wondrous joys; so preserve this concise answer and guard it, for it is from the greatest of secrets. This concise answer is to be known and guarded, for it is among the loftiest of secrets.

Here the matter rests for today, the need having been met.
