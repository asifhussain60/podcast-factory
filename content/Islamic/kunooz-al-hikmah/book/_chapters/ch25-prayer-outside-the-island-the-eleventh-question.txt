# Prayer Outside the Island: The Eleventh Question

The eleventh question comes from Sayyidna Amin al-Mu'taman Sulayman ibn Hasan. The title Amin al-Mu'taman was conferred upon him by Sayyidna Da'ud ibn 'Ajabshah, who had two *nussus* (texts of appointment) written for him. The first *nass* is very long, written by the hand of Da'ud ibn Qutb, and at its conclusion he wrote *ana turabu na'layhi*, that he, Da'ud ibn Qutb, was the dust of the feet of Sayyidna Sulayman ibn Hasan. The second *nass* was dictated to Sayyidi Raj three days before his passing, in the year 970 Hijri. In the first *nass* Sayyidna Da'ud ibn 'Ajabshah employed twenty-eight distinct titles. Sayyidna Muhammad ibn Fa'd states that these twenty-eight titles point to the *darajat* (ranks) of Sayyidna Sulayman, that he was the possessor of twenty-eight exalted titles corresponding to the twenty-eight *huruf* (letters) of the *Kalimah al-Shahadah*. After this *nass*, whoever recites "La ilaha illa Allah, Muhammadun Rasulullah, 'Aliyyun Waliyullah" is required to bear an additional *shahadah*, a testimony for Sulayman ibn Hasan; otherwise he does not remain within the circle of Imamah. This was established by Sayyidna Muhammad ibn Fa'd al-'Alawi. When Sayyidna Da'ud passed away, his two sons Ibrahim and Ahmad were present with him, along with his wife.

Sayyidna Muhammad ibn Fa'd offered magnificent praise for Sayyidna Sulayman. In one passage he states that Sayyidna Sulayman ibn Hasan stood, in relation to the Imam of his time, at the *maqam-i nasut* (station of humanity), while in relation to his *abna'-i jins* (fellow believers), the circle of believers beneath him, he stood at the *maqam-i lahut* (station of divinity). This is a most profound declaration.

*Wa-iqamatuhu li-waladihi fi ardhi bi'rayn*: and his establishing of his son in the land of the two wells. Sayyidna Sulayman ibn Hasan established his son Ja'far at the place of two wells while he was traveling. The precise details of this incident are not fully known, since some accounts were abbreviated in transmission.

Then the matter of Sayyidna Muhammad ibn Isma'il, *qaddasa Allahu ruhahu*, the one who appointed Sayyidna Hibatullah as Da'i and served as his support. *Fa-qad sami'tu annahu salla jama'atan fi tarfi bilad al-habashah*: it is reported that he led a congregation in prayer in the lands of Habashah. *Fa-la-'amri la-qad faqa man mada*: by Allah's oath, he surpassed all who had come before him in this regard, for the *du'at* had never previously led prayer outside their designated three islands. The questioner then says: *fa-haqqiq li-'abdika al-faqir fi hadhihi al-mas'alah, bi-haqqi fulan, bi-haqqi fulan*, so investigate this matter for your humble servant, by the right of such-and-such, by the right of such-and-such. *Wa-lakin akhafu an tatla'a 'ala da'fi 'aqli*, but I fear that you will perceive the weakness of my intellect. *Wa-qad 'udtu bi-dhambi wa-dhilli wa-dhalili fa-ya harrun*, I come burdened with my sins, my abasement, and my lowliness, so O my protector.

As for the three islands: the Da'i of Iran would lead prayer within Iran, but the Da'i of Yemen was appointed over three specific islands and would not lead prayer outside them. Habashah and other regions each had their own Da'is. The island of Sham, for instance, is a distinct island, and no Da'i of Yemen prays there. The Da'i of Yemen is superior in rank to the *du'at* of all other islands, yet even so he confines himself to the three islands, Hind, Sindh, and Yemen. When Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Husayn al-Makrami traveled to London, he did not lead prayer there, as it lay outside his island. Likewise no Da'i may lead prayer outside his own island or within another's island. The *mansub* (appointee) stationed in Najran holds a *mansub* superior to all other *manasib* (appointments), even if appointed at a later time, because his nobility and station are elevated: he is, *bi-l-quwwah* (by potential), the successor of the Da'i, possessing the eligibility to become the Da'i's khalifah regardless of how many other *manasib* there may be.

*Wa-qad ahattat bi dhambi wa-yuhallu bi qarbi*: my sin has encompassed me, yet it is resolved by my closeness to you. Pardon my sins and resolve the debt that weighs upon me. *Wa-lakin aqulu ya Allahu ya man azhara al-jamila wa-satara al-qabiha*, but I say: O Allah, O You who make the beautiful manifest and conceal the ugly. This is in fact the supplication of Husayn ibn 'Ali. *Fa-mnun 'alayya bi-ihsanika wa-fadlika*, so favor me with Your grace and Your bounty. *Fa-kayfa akhafu wa-ihsanuka awsa'u wa-fadluka 'indi ajma'u*, how then should I fear, when Your favor is most vast and Your grace is most complete with me, and blessings have been continuously bestowed upon me? *Wa-la ziltu fi hifzi al-da'i wa-husni ri'ayatihi*, and I have never ceased to be in the protection and good care of the Da'i, meaning in Allah's protection. By the right of Sayyidna Muhammad and his pure and noble family.

The answer to that question follows, with the help of Allah the Great and Exalted, by the bounty of the Wali of the Age, may the blessings of the Merciful be upon him, and with the support of Mawla, the Most Luminous Secret and the Great Ocean, and with the support of Mawla Hibatullah and his shining proof.

When *ifadah* and *istifadah* (imparting and receiving benefit) take place, when the one who imparts knowledge and the one who receives it sit together, the *suwar* (souls) of believers who have passed away, who carried within them a longing for knowledge, find that longing still alive, though they can no longer act upon it. They have no body, and thus cannot pray, fast, perform Hajj, engage in Jihad, or carry out any deed pertaining to bodily existence. Yet the quest for knowledge persists even after death. When such *majalis* (gatherings) are held, gatherings in which the words of the Awliya' Allah are spoken and discussed, in which *ifadah* and *istifadah* take place, those souls gather there. And when a questioner puts forth a question, in reality it is not the questioner alone who asks; through his tongue, those souls ask their question. It is as though the *hadd-i 'ali* (high-ranking representative) causes the question to be asked through him.

An incident bears upon this. Dhul-Ma'ali, in the old Ziyarah Muqaddasah, had been giving *asbaq* (lessons) in his house rather than in the mosque after the Fajr prayer, on account of pain in his leg. Then a matter arose: a spirit possessed the mother of Hadrat Ziyarah Sahib Muqaddas, casting a shadow over her. When Hadrat Ziyarah Sahib Muqaddas questioned the spirit through his knowledge, it emerged that she was the soul of a Bohra woman. He rebuked her, but she said she had come with no ill intention, that she wished to enter the noble community. He then administered the *'ahd al-sharif* (noble covenant) to her, and she departed. After some time she returned. He rebuked her sharply, reminding her of her promise to leave and not come back. She replied that she had not come of her own accord but had been sent, sent by his Mawla and hers, Fardali Sahib Muqaddas, who had already passed away. She had been sent with three messages. The first was that the *asbaq* being conducted at the Ziyah Manzil should be moved to the mosque, because the *suwarat* (souls) who attend have a longing to hear them and gather there for that purpose. The second was that Ja'far 'Ali Sahib, the forefather of Maqsood bhai and Safdar bhai now in Hyderabad, was building a house and was sitting beneath a wall for shade while supervising the workers, and that he should be warned that the wall was about to fall and he must not sit there. A third matter was also conveyed, though its precise details have not been retained in memory.

Such is the manner in which these things come about, and the manner in which the spiritual dimension enters into worldly affairs. Similarly it is recounted that Sayyidna Sulayman ibn Hasan was once delivering a sermon in a *majlis* with the *ikhwan* present, when Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Sulayman stood up in the midst of it and said, "Mawla, you have graced my *majlis* with your presence, and it is a great honor that you have come. Please pray for me." Then he sat down. The gathering was astonished. This happened on several occasions, and when people asked what was occurring, he said that Sayyidna Sulayman ibn Hasan had come, that he had perceived his presence there.

When the Da'udis filed a case in the time of Sayyidna 'Ali, Sayyidna 'Ali and Hasan Khan ibn 'Abd al-Malik were required to appear in court. The night before their appearance, Sayyidna Ja'far would come to Sayyidna 'Ali Sahib in a dream and say: "Tomorrow the judge will ask you this question. Give this answer." The next day they would appear, the judge would ask the very question foretold, and they would give the prescribed answer. On another occasion, when they were greatly troubled by a government matter, a person appeared before both Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Sulayman and Sayyidna Hasan Khan ibn 'Abd al-Malik and recited a verse of the Quran to them:

> *La takhafa, inna ana ma'akuma*

Do not fear, for I am with you both. With this they were consoled and their hearts steadied.

Another incident: Sayyidna Husayn ibn Ahmad had been placed under a form of house arrest in Ta'if. From there he would come to Baitullah, and during his stays in Makkah he would perform seven *tawaf* every day without fail. On one occasion, while performing *tawaf*, he raised both hands and cried out, "*Wa shauqah, wa shauqah*," O my longing, O my longing. The companions and *hudud* who were with him had never witnessed such a state in him, and afterward they asked him about it. He said that he had been seeing three *suwar*, the souls of Sayyidi Fardali Sahib Muqaddas, Sayyidi Ziyali Sahib Muqaddas, and Sayyidna Ghulam Husayn Sahib Muqaddas, performing *tawaf* before him. They were circling with great speed, but he, encumbered by his body, could not match their pace. The sight of them intensified his longing, his *shauq*, because he could not perform *tawaf* as they did.

On yet another occasion, a person in Najran recounted that he was sitting beside Sayyidna Husayn ibn Isma'il, both of them reciting, for Sayyidna Husayn ibn Isma'il was known to recite the Quran abundantly and with great speed. As they were reciting, a figure appeared and extended his hand toward Sayyidna Husayn ibn Isma'il. The moment Sayyidna Husayn ibn Isma'il saw him, he kissed the figure's hand and immediately rose to his feet, and at that moment the figure vanished. It is possible that this was Imam 'Ali (ع) himself.

So, with the support of Mawla-i 'Alam and his magnificent secret, the affair of Sayyidna Sulayman ibn Hasan is taken up, Sulayman-i Humam, Sulayman al-Imam. The account of Sulayman-i Humam is that in the *jazirah* (island) he is Sulayman, and one of his forms is established in Hadramawt, whose name is Humam. This has not occurred with anyone else. For the Imam of the time is the thirty-fifth Imam, and he is the seventh (*sabi'*) of his cycle (*haftah*). Nor is that all: he is included among those four Imams, Sayyidna Ahmad ibn Muhammad among them, whose greatness and station are very high. Two cycles are thus combined in him; he is the possessor of fourteen Imams. On account of these circumstances his station is very high, and when the Imam's station is high, the Da'i's station also becomes high.

*Wa-iqamatuhu li-waladihi Sayyidna Ja'far*, and his establishing of his son, Sayyidna Ja'far: he established his son in Bi'rayn, among the people. *Wa-dhalika kama fa'ala asluhu al-'azim fi al-dawr al-qadim*, and that is as his great forefather did in the ancient period. So this matter came about just as it did in the ancient period, when Sulayman ibn Hasan and Ja'far ibn Sulayman appeared: it was done then, and so it has been done again now. What that incident was is not known. *Allahu yahdi man yasha'u ila siratin mustaqim*, Allah guides whom He wills to a straight path. *Wa-aydhan lamma ittasalat maddatu imamatihi ilayhi*, and also, when the substance of his Imamah connected to him, that is, when the Imam's confirmations reached him, *dhakara qurbanahu wa-mathubatahu*, he mentioned his sacrifice and his offering, that the sacrifice should be presented.

*Wa-amma sallatu Sayyidna Badruddin*, and as for the prayer of Sayyidna Badruddin, *'ala qudsuhu fi lina kama dhakarta fi ghayri hadhihi al-jazirah al-yamaniyyah*, may his soul be sanctified, in a land as mentioned, in other than this Yemeni island. The prayer led by Sayyidna Badruddin, that is, Sayyidna Muhammad ibn Isma'il, his leading of the prayer, as the questioner mentioned, in other than the island of Yemen: that island was not Yemen's. The prayer he led in Habashah was thus, *fa-dhalika li-'izzati rutbatihi al-bari'ah*, on account of his sublime and great rank. That matter came to pass because his rank is very sublime and great among the people. *Wa-li-ihtijabi imamihi 'anhu bi-suratihi al-mu'azzamah*, and because his Imam became veiled from him with his magnificent form, the Imam becoming veiled from him with his magnificent form in the island. *Wal-idhnu lahu mu'adhdhinan lahu bi-l-salah fi ghayri jazirah al-mukkaramah*, and the permission for him to lead the prayer in other than the honored island: the Imam gave him permission to lead the prayer outside the honored island, that is, outside the island of Yemen. *Wa-lahu sha'nun 'azimun*, and his is a great station. *Wa-khatbun jasimun*, and a weighty matter. *Wa-ma hajara min ardhihi wa-baladihi illa li-'adhami sha'nihi*, and he did not migrate from his land and his country except for the greatness of his station. He did not migrate from his land and his country but on account of his great and magnificent state. He was in Sa'fan, in Yemen, but he migrated to Najran and went to Habuna, and did so because of that greatness and magnificence. *Wa-quwwatihi al-ruhaniyyah wa-li-ihya'i marasim al-da'wah*, and for the strength of his spiritual power and to revive the rituals of the Da'wah, that is, on account of his spiritual powers and in order to revive the rituals and secrets of the Da'wah.

Was there a personal meeting with the Imam? No. The Imam's luminous form became veiled from him. How that occurs is not known. There is a treatise by Kamaluddin, Sayyidna Husayn ibn Ahmad himself, *Al-Hijab wal-Muhtajib*, The Veil and the Veiled. It is a small treatise, yet very difficult; what he intends to say is not easily grasped. These states lie beyond comprehension, how a veiled one is veiled by a veil, because the veil is in reality...

Here the lesson was brought to a close, and the Fatihah was presented as a gift and a connection, pure and pleasing, to the soul of Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Hibatullah; may Allah sanctify his soul and illuminate his resting place, and may his blessings, lights, and supports be multiplied, and may He accept this imparting and receiving of benefit in the best possible way. By the right of Sayyidna Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon them.

> Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim. Al-hamdu lillahi Rabb al-'alamin, al-Rahman al-Rahim, Maliki Yawm al-Din. Iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in. Ihdina al-sirat al-mustaqim. Sirat al-ladhina an'amta 'alayhim, ghayr al-maghdubi 'alayhim wa-la al-dallin. Amin.

And with the intention:

> *Wa-bi-niyyati Sayyidi Mawlaya al-Mansub al-Mutlaq fi al-Jazirah al-Hindiyyah al-Sharifah ila al-Faqih al-Nabil, al-'Alim al-Jalil al-Hajj al-Sharafi Usamauddin Ghulam Husayn ibn Sayyid al-Mansub al-Muqaddas Nasrullah ibn Hibatullah ibn al-Mansub al-Muqaddas Fathullah. Tawwala wa-abqa'ahu Allahu Ta'ala, yahfazuhu wa-yahmihi wa-yubqihi bi-tayyibi 'ayshihi min sharri jami' al-amrad wal-asqam, min sharri al-awja' wal-a'lam, min sharri al-ghilal wal-hijam, min sharri al-nufus al-khabisat wal-fusufat, min al-taqdidat wal-tathrisat, wa-bi'ati al-munazarat, wa-yudda wa-yazid fi nasrihi wa-yahfazuhu fi ma'uni 'amrihi min 'aybi al-nawafis wal-nuqsan, wa-yahmihi fi al-hifazat al-rabbaniyyah al-shahaniyyah, wa-tawala Allahu fi tuli hayatihi, wa-af'ali wa-kalamihi wa-darajatihi, wa-yashkurana iyyakum fi sa'ati da'awatihi, wa-yadhkuruna iyyakum fi anfa'i hadaratihi, bi-jahi Sayyid al-Mursalin, Sayyidina Mawlana al-Mubayyin wa-Ahl Baytihi al-Tayyibin al-Tahirin, salawatullahi wa-salamuhu 'alayhim ajma'in. Al-Fatihah.*
