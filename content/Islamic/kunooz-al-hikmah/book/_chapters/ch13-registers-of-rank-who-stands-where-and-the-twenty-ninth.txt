# Registers of Rank: Who Stands Where, and the Twenty-Ninth

In another context it is said that a believer, at whatever rank he departs this life, it is incumbent upon the power of the Creator, *Bari' zubanuhu qudratuhu*, to bring him back to that rank in the next `karrah`, by one means or another. This accounts for the fact that some people progress rapidly and leap forward, apparently from far behind. In their previous `karrah` they were already established at that rank, and their journey now proceeds from that rank onward.

An elder once had occasion to grant `fursat` (an appointment) to someone, and one of the `hudad al-da'wah` (ranks of the mission) remarked: "Mawlana, we did not think you would give him `fursat`." The reply was: "We do not grant `fursat` on the basis of present merit alone, but on the basis that the recipient may complete his `hasanat` (good deeds) and virtues." Such persons were already established at that rank from a previous `karrah`, and it was therefore incumbent to bring them to that place. Whatever progress follows thereafter is achieved through their own knowledge and action.

> *Wa fi al-qalb thalatha tajwifat, mithlu al-thalathah al-jawahir*

"And in the heart are three cavities, like the three jewels."

A person often does not himself perceive at what rank he is established. It is said that among the ranks surrounding the Da'i are many `hudad` of very high station who are not yet in a condition to be brought from potentiality to actuality.

An illustration lies among those appointed who have the first grave in Baroda, the small mausoleum of Mulla Karam 'Ali Sahab. In the time of Sayyidna Hibatullah Sahab, to whom these were appointed, the affair of Da'ud ibn Fi had occurred. Sayyidna Sahab wrote a treatise in which he clarified the rank-list of the `hudad`, and produced a formal register. Da'ud ibn Fi had claimed to be superior in knowledge and rank to the Da'i. He later repented, travelled to Najran, was forgiven by Sayyidna Sahab, and died as a believer, yet his rank was held at the level of a believer. Sayyidna Hibatullah bin Ibrahim Sahab compiled his register and placed Sayyidna Mulla Karamullah Sahab, who was the Da'i, or `mansub`, of two `jazirah` (islands), that is, Hind and Sindh, at the twenty-eighth position. Twenty-seven persons were superior to him in that list. The eighth position was held by Sayyidna Isma'il bin Hibatullah Sahab. The very first position was Sayyidna 'Ali bin Hiba's, the author of the very book being read here, upon whom `nass sharif` (noble designation) had been performed. But he passed away. So Sayyidna Sahab, passing over the seven above him, performed `nass sharif` upon the eighth, Sayyidna Isma'il bin Hibatullah Sahab.

Such things occur. In the time of Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan, *qaddasa Allahu ruhahu*, it happened that Sayyidna 'Ali bin Husayn al-Mukarrami, who was the `sahib al-waqt` (master of the time), was unable to attend the Friday gathering for some reason. The mosque was full. He sent his Abyssinian servant, Rayhan, with the message that Husayn bin Hasan would lead the prayer. But there were fifty men named Husayn bin Hasan in attendance. People asked Rayhan which one was meant. Rayhan returned to Sayyidna Sahab, who replied in a loud voice with the full lineage: "Husayn bin Hasan bin 'Abdullah bin Ahmad al-Mukarrami," and recited it for ten generations. Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan was seated at the eighth or tenth position, with five `manasib` (high-ranking officials) before him, including the `mansub` of Yemen and Najran, Sayyidna Muhammad bin Jamali and the father of Sayyidna Ahmad bin Jamali. All the `manasib` were present. Yet Sayyidna 'Ali bin Husayn chose Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan to lead the prayer.

When Sayyidna 'Ali bin Husayn later came and brought Sayyidna 'Abdullah ibn Hamid with him, announcing to all who were being taught by the `hudad` that he was departing for Hindustan and that Husayn bin Hasan would look after all affairs in his absence, the community understood this to be a `nass sharif`. It also became clear that Sayyidna 'Abdullah bin Hamid, who was being taken along, would not become the Da'i afterward, because it is a principle of the mission that the Da'i of the three `jazirah` cannot leave the `jazirah` of Yemen, which is the `qalb` (heart), without leaving his `na'ib al-maqam` (deputy) there. Since Sayyidna 'Ali bin Husayn left that station and took Sayyidna 'Abdullah bin Hamid with him, and appointed Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan in that place, the meaning was plain.

On the matter of what lies beneath the Baytullah: there came a mention in another passage that beneath the basement of the Baytullah is the `sakhrah` (the sacred rock), which is a distinct and separate matter, not directly in the basement. In the `sakhrah` there are ten `abrar` (righteous ones). The last among these ten is Nimrud, he who claimed divinity, saying *Ana Rabbukum al-A'la*, "I am your Lord, the Most High."

> *Wa fi al-ithna 'ashar al-jazirah, ithna 'ashar qalban sharifan, mithlu al-ithna 'ashar harfan fi al-shahadah*

"And in the twelve islands are twelve noble hearts, like the twelve letters in the testimony of faith."

The twelve `jazirah` each correspond to a `qalb` (heart); every `jazirah` is itself a `qalb`. And this mirrors the twelve letters of *La ilaha ill-Allah*.

> *Min qawlihi al-jamil, jami' dhalika thamaniah wa 'ishruna 'addan. Li-l-diyanah wa-l-huda. Fa-sun hadha al-sirr al-masun*

"From his beautiful saying, the total of all this is twenty-eight in number, for the religion and the guidance. So preserve this guarded secret."

The number twenty-eight is always maintained. Among the `hudad`, the twenty-eighth position, such as that of Sayyidna Ja'far bin Sulayman, is of very high station. Similarly, the twenty-eighth Imam, Sayyidna Ma'add, Mawlana Ma'add, is of very high station. Yet the twenty-ninth is also of extraordinary station.

What pertains to the Prophet bears this out. The `kalimat al-shahadah` appears in five forms in the Quran: *La ilaha ill-Allah*, *La ilaha illa hu*, *La ilaha illa ana*, *La ilaha illa alladhi*, and *La ilaha illa anta*. The last form, *La ilaha illa anta*, is rare, appearing only once in the Quran, in the glorification of Yunus (ع). The rest are repeated. And *Muhammadun Rasulullah* likewise appears only once in the Quran, unrepeated, in Surah al-Fath, in its twenty-ninth verse. In the sequence of messengers he was the twenty-ninth. Janab 'Ali (ع) was the twenty-eighth, and the twenty-eighth holds a very high station. The twenty-ninth, whether among the Imams or among the `du'at` (missionaries), is rare; he enters into the sequence, disrupting its ordinary flow. Rasulullah, *sallallahu alayhi wa sallam*, entered the line of Imams, not as an Imam in the ordinary sense, for after Imam Janab Abu Talib it should have been Janab 'Ali. But he entered, and 'Ali became his follower, because in him all four ranks were combined: `nubuwwah` (prophethood), `risalah` (messengership), `imamah` (leadership), and `wasayat` (succession). He thus became the master even of 'Ali. This was a station established for Janab Rasul in the `alam al-ruhani` (spiritual world).

The same pattern holds among the `du'at`. Sayyidna Ja'far bin Sulayman is the twenty-eighth, and the twenty-ninth is Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj. Although Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj was a `da'i ghayr munfarid` (non-absolute missionary), he became `munfarid` (absolute), having been established in the rank of the `munfaridin`. Sayyidna Ja'far passed away during his lifetime, yet Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj was established in the rank of a `hujjah` (proof), one who is the equal of thirty `du'at`. After Sayyidna Ja'far, every Da'i who has come has been established in the rank of `hujjah`, without exception, until today. This is the status of the `qa'im al-du'at` (established missionaries). When Sayyidna Ja'far bin Sulayman was established in the rank of `hujjah`, the `da'i al-balagh` (missionary of communication) of the three `jazirah`, that rank, became vacant, and `nass sharif` was performed upon it. He is therefore retained in the sequence.

This sequence will be found in the old `sahifah` (books), those of Sayyidna Ghulam Sahab, Sayyidna Farhat Ali Sahab, and Sayyidna Ziyayi Sahab Muqaddas, *Zul Ma'ani*, where no mention of Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj appears. When the new `sahifah` was compiled, his mention was likewise omitted in accordance with the older books. But when Sayyidna 'Ali bin Husayn reviewed the manuscripts, he studied them carefully, declared that everything was correct, and raised two or three points. One of those points concerned Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj.

The status of Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj is that he is the twenty-ninth. And the twenty-ninth, wherever he appears, enters into the sequence, just as Rasulullah entered the sequence as the twenty-ninth. Surah al-Fath embodies the same principle: nowhere in the Quran did Allah Most High mention Rasulullah by name in his capacity as Rasul except in Surah al-Fath, and there in the twenty-ninth verse. The twenty-ninth verse of Surah al-Fath opens with *Muhammadun Rasulullah*, "Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah."

The `muqatta'at` (disjoined opening letters of the Quran) are twenty-nine in total. Of the 6,666 verses of the Quran, the letters may be classified in more than one way. One classification distinguishes dotted from undotted letters. Another distinguishes `nurani` (luminous) from `zulmani` (tenebrous) letters. The twenty-eight letters of the Arabic alphabet are arranged in a scheme of four groups of seven:

1. the first seven are `nurani khafifah` (lightly luminous);
2. the second are `nurani thaqilah` (heavily luminous);
3. the third are `zulmani khafifah` (lightly tenebrous);
4. the fourth are `zulmani thaqilah` (heavily tenebrous).

In Surah al-Fatihah, the summary of the entire Quran, one `zulmani khafifah` letter does appear. But there exists one location in the Quran where no `zulmani khafifah` or `zulmani thaqilah` letter appears at all: the `muqatta'at`. Not a single `zulmani` letter, whether lightly or heavily tenebrous, occurs in the `muqatta'at`. This is why their status is very high, and why the `du'at` have used them as a `wasilah` (means of supplication) in `ta'wizat` (amulets) and in certain prayers. In the brief daily supplications of Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq, the second supplication employs *Kaf-Ha-Ya-'Ayn-Sad* as a `wasilah`, and this entire supplication has been translated. The `muqatta'at` function as a `wasilah`, and there are twenty-nine of them in all.

Among the `muqatta'at`, some are repeated and some are unique. *Alif-Lam-Mim* appears many times, whereas *Nun* appears only once, and *Ya-Sin* only once. From the non-repeated `muqatta'at`, a single sentence can be formed, and only one: *Siratu 'Aliyyin haqq, numsikuhu*, "The path of 'Ali is truth; we hold to it." No other sentence can be formed from them.

In translations of the Quran produced for general audiences, the `muqatta'at` are either passed over, or accompanied by a note that only Allah knows their meaning, or at most that His Prophet knows. This is because those translators did not remain attached to the Ahl al-Bayt, and so the knowledge did not reach them. Even the Shi'a, who profess love for the Ahl al-Bayt, express their `walayat` (devotion) fervently, and genuinely love them, do not possess the knowledge held here. The realities of `lahut`, `na'ut`, `nasut`, the `nafs-i-imam`, and matters of that nature are entirely absent from their understanding. This reveals that with Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq two dimensions were always present: an outward following, whose adherents were his Shi'a in the common sense, and a `batini` (inner) following, which was separate and distinct. The `batini` Shi'a moved forward gradually, steadfast in their faith, firm in their silence and constancy.
