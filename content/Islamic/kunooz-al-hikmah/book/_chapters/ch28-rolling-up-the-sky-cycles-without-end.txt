# Rolling Up the Sky: Cycles Without End

At the close of the book two further questions stand appended. They were not part of the original composition. One man, Ibrahim bin 'Ali, put them to the author after the work was otherwise finished, and they were written down at the end. The first has already been answered. What follows is the second and last.

The question concerns the saying of God Almighty:

> *Yawma natwi al-sama'a katayyi al-sijilli lil-kutub.*
>
> "The Day when We will roll up the heaven like the rolling up of a scroll for the books."

Just as the pages of a book are rolled shut, so the heaven will be rolled up. The word in the verse is *lil-kutub*, in the plural; where a copy reads *lil-kitab*, that is a slip of the pen, and the verse itself has *kutub*.

The questioner asks: *wa hal dhalika lahu haddun ma'ruf* — is there a known limit for this, a fixed term at which the matter comes to pass? Or is it that it will not happen until after the mountains and the stones have crumbled, *wa ya'udu ila al-ma'adin*, and what remains returns to the mineral realm, so that these heavens must first enter the *ma'dan* in order to rise? And then pass on to *al-nabat*, plant life, and move forward from there? Will the process continue until what is held is freed from its captivity, and until the ascent of spirits to the celestial spheres is accomplished and the bonds of the spheres are broken?

Then he adds: *ya Maulai, ju'iltu fidak* — O my master, may I be your ransom — will the great era of unveiling follow the era of concealment, like this era in which we now stand? Or will the eras succeed one another forever and ever? And he closes with the words of Scripture, *ya'lamu kha'inata al-a'yun wa ma tukhfi al-sudur wa ila Allahi tasir al-umur*: He knows the treachery of the eyes and what the breasts conceal, and to God all affairs return. So ends the question.

### The Answer

The answer opens with the help of God, Glorified be He, and His favor, and with the support given by Maulai al-Malik, the landing place of the rays of the angels, meaning Sayyidna Hibatullah.

As for His saying, *yawma natwi al-sama'a katayyi al-sijilli* — that will be at the ending of the bonds, *fa dhalika yakunu 'inda intiha' al-ribatat*. In other words, it comes at the end of the *kawr*, the minor cycle. *Bila shakk wa la rayb wa la irtiyab*: without doubt, without skepticism, without uncertainty, it will be so.

On that day *tubaddalu al-ardu ghayra al-ard*, the earth is exchanged for another earth. Length is created upon width, so that what was extended in one direction is turned into the other. The north becomes the south. *Wa dhalika ma taqtadihi 'inayat 'allam al-ghuyub*: that is what the providence of the Knower of all hidden things requires, and as He decrees, so it comes to be. *Yawma tamuru al-ardu mawra*, the day the earth sways in its great cycling, *wa tasiru al-jibalu sayra*, and the mountains set out and travel, quitting their places and fleeing.

As the Presence of the Best of Beings has said:

> *Kama bada'na awwala khalqin nu'iduhu, wa'dan 'alayna.*
>
> "Just as We began the first creation, We shall repeat it. A promise binding upon Us."

A promise binding upon Him, which He will assuredly fulfill. After one *kawr*, another *kawr* comes. The creatures that issue from the wombs, and the bodies that are composed, are composed only at the opening of a *kawr*; and one *kawr* is followed without fail by the next.

As for the question, will the era of unveiling follow the era of concealment, like the one in which we now stand, the answer is yes. *Yata'aqubu dawr al-sitr wal-kashf ka ta'aqub al-layli wa al-nahar*: the era of concealment and the era of unveiling succeed one another as night succeeds day, *bila tahwil wa la tabdil*, with no shifting and no alteration, and so it continues until the Greatest Cycle is completed, *ila an yatimmu al-mawr al-a'zam*.

### The Measure of the Cycles

The reckoning proceeds by multiplication, not by simple addition.

1. Fifty thousand years make one *dawr*.
2. Four hundred thousand *dawr* multiplied by four hundred thousand *dawr* make one *kawr*.
3. Four hundred thousand *kawr* multiplied by four hundred thousand *kawr* make one *mawr*.

The duration is immense beyond ordinary reckoning. One *mawr* has already passed over the world. Nine remain.

The length of what remains was raised as a difficulty: nine *mawr* is a period past measuring, and how is it to be traversed? The *da'i* who gave the answer replied that just as one *mawr* passed over without being perceived at all, so the nine will pass in the same way.

The manner of it is this. When a *mawr* has passed, the *'ashir*, the Tenth Intellect, who stands in that station, yields his place to the ninth *'aql*, and the offspring of his rank moves up accordingly. So the station of the intellect keeps changing, which is to say the *tadbirat*, the dispensations, keep changing. The *'ashir* ascends one degree with each *mawr*, so that across ten *mawr* he mounts ten steps and arrives at the first rank, the first station. Yet he does not pass onward into the second station, into the domain of the *'Aql al-Awwal*. He is absorbed instead into the *Mumba'ith al-Awwal*, the First Emitted Being.

And where do the ascending forms go, all the forms of the *mawr*, and the *'ashir* who vacates his place? To the domain of the *Mumba'ith al-Awwal*. The domain of the *'Aql al-Awwal* is the domain of *wahdat*, of unity: it admits no partner, it is one and has no companion. Just as forms gather at the *Bab al-Abwab*, so every gathering of ascending forms, those that rise as intellects and the countless others that climb alongside each one, assembles in the domain of the *Mumba'ith al-Awwal*. At the end, two domains alone remain: the domain of the *'Aql al-Awwal* and the domain of the *Mumba'ith al-Awwal*. All the rest are absorbed, and after that no other being remains. This absorption belongs to the last *mawr*, after the ninth, while the process itself runs continuously throughout.

Concerning the two stations named in Scripture: *Sidrat al-Muntaha* stands higher than *Jannat al-Ma'wa*. In one respect *Jannat al-Ma'wa* corresponds to the *bab al-zahir*, the manifest gate, and *Sidrat al-Muntaha* to the *bab al-batin*, the esoteric gate.

By the arrangement of nature, God did not grant human beings knowledge of their former lives. He did not give it. The point is not remote: we have come from the era of unveiling, yet we who stand in the era of concealment retain no knowledge of the era of unveiling. God Almighty empties it from the mind and causes it to be forgotten. Every advance the world has made began again from an entirely new beginning. The unveiling is the work of the A'immah.

And the judgment on that day belongs to God. He decides and He decrees.

### The Repetition Within the Great Cycle

Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad, may God sanctify his soul and illuminate his shrine, gave an illustration of this: *anna dawr al-kashf wa al-sitr yatakarraru fi hadha al-kawr al-a'zam al-mashhur wahid wa 'ishruna lakh marra* — that the era of unveiling and the era of concealment repeat, within this well-known great cycle, twenty-one lakh times, that is, two million one hundred thousand times, together with its fractions. The word *lakh* stands there in transliteration, taken into the Arabic. One *mawr* has already elapsed; and within the *kawr* of the second *mawr*, in which we now sit, two million one hundred thousand alternations of concealment and unveiling have already transpired.

Then comes the exhortation:

> *Fa atruku al-ghaflata wal-nisyan. Wa Allahu yuwaffiquna wa iyyakum lima yurid al-Rahim al-Rahman, bihaqqi safwat Mudar wa 'Adnan, 'alayhim al-salawatu wal-salam ma ta'aqaba al-malawan.*
>
> "So abandon heedlessness and forgetfulness. And may God grant us and you success in what the Merciful, the Compassionate desires, by the right of the elect of Mudar and 'Adnan. Upon them be blessings and peace as long as the two alternate."

The elect of Mudar and 'Adnan are the Messenger of God and the Imams, the pure essences of those two ancestral lines. And *al-malawan*, the two that alternate, are the night and the day.

### The Gathering of Forms

In our own age likewise, the noble forms of all believers that are ascending, whether of lower rank or higher, are going to the *Bab al-Abwab*. A lifetime is spent in *nazar-maqam*, taking the name of the Imam of the Time at every moment, calling out, "O Imam, help us." That belief stands firm in its own place. Yet the *surat-i sharif*, the noble form, travels to the *Bab al-Abwab*: passing through the manifest gate it arrives at the esoteric gate.

And for this reason it has been said: *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah al-'ali al-'azim* — there is no power and no strength except in God, the Most High, the Most Great. Neither the manifest gate nor the esoteric gate possesses power sufficient to compose the *haykal*, the spiritual body. That lies beyond them. The one who composes it is God, meaning the Imam of the Time, and He alone can compose the *haykal*. He will grant to His *fard*, the high dignitary, that vast and magnificent collection of *suwarat*, taking it from the *Bab al-Abwab* and delivering it to His *fard*. The *Bab al-Abwab* then passes away: once the *haykal* has been set down, he departs within three days. And the recompense granted to the *Bab al-Abwab* for his service is that he becomes the *qalb*, the heart, of the *fard*.

Of the same order is the service performed from ancient times by Maulai al-Ka'inat, Sayyidna 'Ali. The *suwarat al-anbiyya'*, the forms of the prophets, and the *hayakil* from Maulana Hunaid onward, from Maulana Hunaid to Maulana Hashim, were arriving, since the amalgamation of the *thalath suwar* had not yet taken place with him. And from Janab Hasan to Abul-Qasim, down to the Imam of today, the one who gathered all their *hayakil* is Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع). He gathered them for the Qa'im. The whole collection accumulated with him he will entrust to the Qa'im. He became, as it were, the *Bab al-Abwab* for the Qa'im, and he will become the *qalb*, the heart, of the Qa'im.

### The Scribe

The closing lines are the writing of the *faqir*, the one poor and needy before God: 'Ali bin Qutbuddin, who sets himself down as a *faqir* before his *wali* and his *da'i*, *al-awwab*, the ever-returning. He asks that whoever comes upon the book pray for him and seek forgiveness for him in this world and the next. He was among the great elders of this community, buried in Baroda beneath the neem tree, and this was his humility.

He composed other works as well, among them a *sirat* of Sayyidna Isma'il bin Hibatullah written in the manner of *haqa'iq*, the first book of the Da'wah in which a life is set down in that manner, a *sirat* ordinarily being a record of historical events. It is a magnificent book, *Al-Bahirat al-Adillah*. In Najran it is said of it that it is not a book but *halwa*: there is the same pleasure in reading it as in eating sweets. Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan, may God sanctify his soul, once asked where in Hind 'Ali bin Qutbuddin lay buried, and whether he was the author of *Al-Bahirat al-Adillah*. On being told that he was, he said, "What a magnificent person he was."

He held no designated rank. He lived in the time of the first *mansub* of Baroda, Karamullah Sahab. In his day the Sunnis of the Sadikpuri sect complained to the king, a Hindu ruler of that time, that the Bohras and the 'Alawis held false beliefs and were misguided. The king summoned the Bohras, and they answered, "On our behalf, 'Ali bin Qutbuddin will reply." The Sulaymanis agreed to this, and the 'Alawis said the same. The Sunni scholars present put several questions to him, and he answered every one of them out of their own books, naming the page: in your book, on this page, is the answer. The king was astonished and dismissed them with honor. Then 'Ali bin Qutbuddin said, "Now I will put three questions to you, and I know you will not be able to answer them." He asked his three questions, and they could not reply. Much effort has been spent in trying to recover those three questions and their answers, without success. He was a magnificent elder of this community.

With that the book is complete. *Tammat hadha.* Praise belongs to God.

Al-Fatiha.*
>
> O God, we ask You for the Opening as a gift, an offering raised, pure and pleasing, to the soul of Sayyidna 'Ali bin Hibatullah, may God sanctify his soul and illuminate his shrine, and may He pour upon us of his blessings, his breaths, and his support. Accept from us, O God, the giving and the receiving in the best manner there is, by the standing of the Master of the Messengers and of our master Maulana the Commander of the Faithful, and by the mighty Quran and the people of his house, the good and the pure. The blessings of God and His (عليهم السلام).
