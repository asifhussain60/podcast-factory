# The Knife and the Name: Sacrifice at Mina

From *Kunooz al-Hikmah* comes the tenth question, *as-su'āl al-'āshir*: *Dhibh al-hadī fī Minā*, the slaughter of the *hadī* at Mina, *mā ḥaqīqatuhu*, what is its reality?

One meaning of *hadiyyah* is a gift, and *al-hadī* is the name given to the sacrificial animal, that which is offered up. As for the place, it is called both *Minā* and *Munā*, and *Munā* is the more evocative form, since *munā* means the ultimate desire, the deepest aspiration. The questioner asks concerning the animal slaughtered at Mina by a pilgrim (*ḥājj*): what is its true reality? *Wa ammā al-mathal wa'l-mawṣūl*, and as for the metaphor and its referent, *faqad 'arraftuhu min tafaḍḍulukum*, these he has already come to know through the favor shown him. So too with what is at Mount 'Arafat, the highest point of that plain: *faqad 'araftu al-mathal*, the apparent sense of it he has grasped. What he asks for now is *al-jawāb al-shāfī wa'l-manwāl al-kāfī*, the healing answer and the sufficient provision, a full and complete exposition.

*Al-jawāb 'alā dhālika as-su'āl, naqūlu bi-'awni'llāh al-kabīr al-muta'āl.* The answer to that question is given with the help of Allah, the Great, the Exalted, *wa minnat walī al-'aṣr wa'l-zamān*, and by the favor of the *Walī al-'Aṣr wa'l-Zamān*, *'alayhi aṣ-ṣalawāt al-raḥīm al-raḥmān*, *wa ta'yīd mawlāya al-a'lam wa sirruhu al-bāhir al-mu'aẓẓam*, from the support of Mawlāya al-A'lam, namely Hibatullah Saheb, and from the effulgence of his magnificent secret.

The whole plain of 'Arafat is a *mawqūf*, a place of standing. The Sarwar-e-Kainat ascended the hill there and offered *du'ās*, and Muslims generally make their way to that hill as well.

The meaning of the sacrifice is this. When the *Mudabbir al-Ḥakīm*, the Wise Planner, intends *min irqā'ihā wa is'ādihā 'alā an-nasl al-qawīm*, its elevation and its ascension upon the upright lineage, so that the flesh of the sacrifice may rise to the pure lineage, the slaughter is performed. As for its soul, that which is called the *nafs ḥissiyyah*, the sensory soul, it is punished through the act of slaughter. By the slaughtering of the animal, its *nafs ḥissiyyah* is expelled from it.

If an animal dies a natural death, its *nafs ḥissiyyah* retreats inward into the body, and that is a bad form of death, for the sensory soul has turned inward. But when the slaughter is performed with the invocation of the name of Allah, the *nafs ḥissiyyah* separates from the animal, and for that reason its flesh may be eaten and used. The flesh is elevated and made to ascend, while the *rūḥ*, that is, the *nafs ḥissiyyah*, is punished and driven out.

There is the question of whether the slaughter is valid when something other than *Allāhu Akbar* is pronounced. The Jews who perform slaughter do not say *Allāhu Akbar* but invoke in their own tongue, and their slaughter is nonetheless permissible (*jā'iz*). The slaughter of a Jew and the slaughter of a Christian are both *ḥalāl*. A Muslim will say *Allāhu Akbar*; in the case of others, whatever they say in their own language for that purpose is accepted.

As for what is done in Saudi Arabia, a butcher who performed slaughter there explained that a tape recording of *Allāhu Akbar* is played in the slaughterhouse while the machine operates. That is a practice about which one may have reservations. Their contention is a practical one: how many times can the invocation be repeated over a mechanical process?

Immense are the blessings that flow from saying *Allāhu Akbar*. There is a hadith of Rasulullah, Sarwar-e-Kainat:

> *aṣ-ṣalātu qurbānu kulli taqiyyin*

Prayer is the sacrifice of every God-fearing person.

The word *qurbān*, sacrifice, is used here deliberately. At a slaughter *Allāhu Akbar* is said, and the animal becomes *ḥalāl* so that its flesh may be eaten. In prayer, after *wuḍū'* and after coming to the masjid in a state of worship, the worshipper says *Allāhu Akbar* over himself. His own *nafs ḥissiyyah* is thereby being sacrificed, struck and expelled continuously. In a two-*rak'at* prayer alone, *Allāhu Akbar* is uttered eleven times. If a single utterance renders an animal *ḥalāl*, what a magnificent difference it must make when a believer in a state of *īmān* pronounces it over himself. For the *nafs nāṭiqah*, the rational soul, of a believer is so veiled in its inner workings that it does not disclose to the *nafs ḥissiyyah*, the soul nearest to it, the secrets, the blessings, and the divine effulgence it receives from *'Alī 'Illiyyīn*. It informs neither the *nafs nāmiyyah*, the vegetative soul, nor the *nafs ḥissiyyah* in any way. Such is the nature of the *nafs nāṭiqah*.

The Messenger named the most excellent *waẓīfah*, the finest invocation, as four phrases:

> *Subḥānallāh, wa'l-ḥamdu lillāh, wa lā ilāha illallāh, wallāhu Akbar*

Glory be to Allah, and all praise is for Allah, and there is no god but Allah, and Allah is the greatest.

*Allāhu Akbar* is among them. A fifth phrase is also included:

> *Wa lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāhi'l-'aliyyi'l-'aẓīm*

And there is no might and no power except with Allah, the Most High, the Most Great.

It is related in a narration that the Prophet counselled a person to recite these four phrases before sleeping, for doing so brings the reward of one *Ḥajj*. Such are the blessings contained within them.

As was noted in the discussion of the *tasbīḥ*, *Allāhu Akbar* corresponds to the *nafs nāmiyyah*, *al-ḥamdu lillāh* to the *nafs ḥissiyyah*, and *Subḥānallāh* to the *nafs nāṭiqah*. The final phrase, *Lā ilāha illallāh*, its form pertains to the *nayyirah*, the luminous form. At the time of death the three souls unite, and they are transformed into the noble form and the form of the *nayyirah*.

A question was raised about the *nafs ḥissiyyah* escaping the *jism*, the body: does escaping mean that it ascends? It does not ascend. The *nafs nāṭiqah* absorbs the *nafs ḥissiyyah*, and it absorbs the *nafs nāmiyyah*. The sensory soul spreading into the body is a very bad death, a bad state; the sensory soul being absorbed into the rational soul is a very noble thing. The *nafs ḥissiyyah* becomes part of the *nafs nāṭiqah*.

Nor does this belong to the hour of death alone. Throughout life the process continues, the *nafs nāṭiqah* drawing the *nafs ḥissiyyah* into itself, and the more obedient the sensory soul is, the more completely it is absorbed.

This is why it is said that when the *awliyā' Allāh* pray, their state changes. The person he was before is not the person he is after. Examples of this are given. The Messenger, on the night of *Mi'rāj*, went to meet Allah and obtained those excellences and that nearness to God; on his return, he was not the same person who had departed. It is very far from reason that the one whom the Lord of the worlds had summoned for a meeting would be sent back in the same state.

There is also the case of Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad bin Fahd. Sayyidna 'Ali bin Sulayman said of him: "Had it not been decided in pre-eternity that I would precede Ibrahim, I could never have preceded him." A momentous statement. Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad bin Fahd remained in Yemen, for there is a principle of the *da'wah* that a *Dā'ī* cannot leave the island of Yemen unless his successor is present there. At that time the Zaidi Imam who held power imprisoned him, and he remained in jail for fourteen years. During those fourteen years, Sayyidna 'Ali bin Sulayman performed approximately eleven *nuṣūṣ*, appointments, upon him. One of the *ḥudūd* of the *da'wah* asked him: "Master, you have already performed *naṣṣ* on Sayyidna Ibrahim; why was there a need to perform it again?" He replied: "The Ibrahim upon whom I had performed *naṣṣ* has changed. Ibrahim is no longer Ibrahim." How great a statement that is. That Ibrahim is not the same Ibrahim; he has changed, and the *naṣṣ* was performed upon a different Ibrahim.

Ibrahim was the son of Sayyidna Muhammad bin Fahd, and their mother was the same, Khadija bint Nuh. He was perhaps born after the passing of Sayyidna Sulayman, so the difference in their ages may not have been great.

*Wa'l-qatlu fī jamī'i ḥālātihā wa lā taqtaṣṣu lahā al-'ināyāt al-ilāhiyyah bi's-ṣu'ūd*: the divine providence did not take *qiṣāṣ*, retribution, from the *ṣu'ūd*, the ascension, of the *dhabīḥah*, *li-kawnihim min azdād al-ḥudūd*, because it was from the opposites of the ranks. What is meant is its *nafs ḥissiyyah*. The meat of the slaughtered animal did ascend and entered into the food of the believers; but its sensory soul, being from the opposites of the ranks, was not elevated through retribution. *Wa yufāriquhu bi-wāsiṭat at-tasmiyat al-mu'aẓẓamah 'inda arbāb al-hudā*: it separates from it by means of the naming of God, magnificent in the view of the masters of guidance. The *nafs ḥissiyyah* separates by means of the *tasmiyah*, that is, by the saying of *Bismillāh al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*.

Where it goes is to desolate deserts. The *nafs ḥissiyyah* goes to *Ra's al-Zanab*, which is its abode. The sensory souls that gather there, whether they are *azdād*, opposites, or those of the slaughtered, being evil, cause harm to the believers. The *azdād* of the *awliyā' Allāh* in particular are given a respite of forty days during which they roam between the heavens and the earth, harming the believers. After forty days they are drawn into *Ra's al-Zanab*, their permanent abode. Their powers are also greatly increased during that time, for the opponent of the *da'wah* has performed good deeds in his life; the reward for those deeds will not come to him in the hereafter, but in this very world he is given free rein, and that is the reward he receives.

As for the *dhabīḥah*, its meat is at the level of *yawm an-na* ascending in human form. Yet many eat of it, fifty people separately. How nature keeps the account of what belongs to a single unit, say the goat of a particular individual, when it has been divided among fifty, only God knows. But it has entered the state of ascending, and though divided among many, it remains in that state.

Great is the importance of *Bismillāh*. The Prophet says in one of his prayers:

> *Bismillāhilladhī lā yaḍurru ma'a ismihi shay'un fi'l-arḍi wa lā fi's-samā'i wa huwa as-samī'u'l-'alīm*

In the name of Allah, with whose name nothing in the earth or the heavens can cause harm, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing.

The *Bismillāh* carries such power and strength that nothing can harm the one who says it, neither in the earth nor in the sky. This prayer is well established and known even among the general people. Sayyidna Muhammad bin Fahd has also mentioned it in his collection of prayers, with mention of reciting it three times in the morning and three times at night, and it is a promise from Allah conveyed through the tongue of the Messenger that no harm will come to the one who does so.

The matter of the *dhabīḥah*, that which is made *ḥalāl*, is thus understood. As for the animals that are not made *ḥalāl*, their matter goes into the *darakāt*, the lower levels. It goes there absolutely. How subtle a matter this is in the world. The greatness of making something *ḥalāl* is a great virtue: one utterance of *Allāhu Akbar* makes a creature *ḥalāl*. Those who eat meat that comes from outside ought to exercise great caution regarding how it has been prepared.

The meat of the *dhabīḥah* of Eid al-Adha should not be given to a non-Muslim, and generally it is not given; nor to those who are paid wages. The meat of the religious offering is a different matter. As for people who take their Eid al-Adha *dhabīḥah* home and distribute it themselves, what they do is their own responsibility. But the meat of the *dhabīḥah* of Eid, and likewise the meat of the *'aqīqah*, should not be given to a non-Muslim. And the mother whose child's *'aqīqah* has been performed should not eat that meat either, since the child is still a nursing infant. If the mother eats it, its nutrition will enter her body, form her strength, and form her milk. The child will drink that milk, and for him that milk is not *ḥalāl*. This is generally not known to people. She should not eat it, not even the soup made from it.

How many dimensions there are within what is *ḥalāl*. If two animals are brought, and one remains alive while the second shivers from the cold and dies, the dead one is simply a carcass to be discarded, while the living one is slaughtered with *Allāhu Akbar*. An animal that dies its own death dies a bad death, with the exception of the pigeons of the Baitullah. If the pigeons of the Baitullah die their own death, that death is *ḥalāl*; they are not to be slaughtered, nor eaten by way of slaughter, but their natural death is *ḥalāl*. This question was put to the *du'āt*: since there are pigeons at the Baitullah and also pigeons in Madinah al-Munawwarah, are the pigeons of Madinah like those of the Baitullah? The *Dā'ī* Sayyidna Isma'il bin Hibatullah replied: "No, the pigeons of Madinah are not like the pigeons of Baitullah, because the station of the pigeons of Baitullah is very high."

The *riyāt*, the subtle essence, of a buried believer rises within three days, ascends to Jupiter, then to the moon, and at the level of *yawm an-na* descends as completely pure and transparent matter to form the body of the Imam. The *fuḍalā'*, the remains, go into the *burūj*, the constellations, to be refined. Many things, the hard bones, the teeth, the skull, take a very long time to be cleansed, while the soft tissue ascends more readily. After refinement, these remaining things emerge in three forms.

The first: they become *jawāhir*, jewels. For those who are the *fuḍalā'* of the *ḥudūd al-da'wah* who performed great service in their lives, even their remains are honored, as *fa'llāhu khayrun ḥāfiẓan wa huwa arḥamu'r-rāḥimīn*, for Allah is the best of guardians and He is the most merciful of the merciful, and they become jewels set in the crowns of kings or preserved in their treasuries.

The second: they become pigeons, those very pigeons whose natural death is *ḥalāl* and who need not be slaughtered. Remarkably, they do not perform *bawl-o-barāz*, urination and defecation. The animal that ordinarily produces the most *bawl-o-barāz* is the pigeon, a nuisance in any house it inhabits. Yet the pigeons of the Baitullah do not make a mess, nor do they fly over the Baitullah itself, and the pigeons of Madinah also do not make a mess. This nobility is theirs because they are formed from the *fuḍalā'* of the *ḥudūd al-da'wah*.

The third: they become the salt of which it is said:

> *al-milḥu qabla kulli ṭa'āmin wa ba'dahu yadfa'u sab'īna dā'an ahwanuhā al-judhām wa'l-junūn*

Salt before and after every meal wards off seventy diseases, the least of which are leprosy and insanity.

Such is the nature of that salt. It is not the ordinary salt used for cooking and other purposes, but the salt that nature refines and brings to the table, tasted at the beginning and the end of a meal. It carries such blessing that it wards off diseases, and it is formed from the bones of the *ḥudūd al-da'wah*.

As for wild animals that die their own death, they are not slaughtered; they simply die, and they remain in a fallen form. Yet in *yawm an-na* their resurrection must also occur. As for the disbelievers and the polytheists, because *shirk* is a great wrong, progress will not happen for them until they recite the *kalimah* of *Lā ilāha illallāh*.

The *da'wah* is presented to them at every death, and when they change bodies (*qālib*), Islam is presented to them as well. This continues until they fall so far as to enter the body of tar (*dammār*). At that point the *da'wah* is no longer presented to them. There is a prayer in the *munājāt*: *Wa libās al-qaṭirān lā tulinnī*, do not make me wear the garment of tar, meaning, may I never fall so far as to reach that state. In present times that garment is worn in the form of nylon and whatever else is now in use, for nylon is made from petroleum, and the final residue of petroleum is tar.

There is a thing made of thread and a thing made of air, of which there is neither emptiness nor fullness. In the air no thing is empty; rather, all things are filled. It is the eye that lacks the capacity to see this. This is why it is said that one should not become naked, for the things present in the air exert their influence upon a person.

*Wa lidhālika mathaltu 'alā qatl al-mukhālifīna 'inda qiyām qayām yawm ad-dīn*: and for this reason, when the *Qayām* rises on the Day of the Hereafter, these opponents will be killed. The opponents meant are the *azdād*, the opposites. And the *nafs ḥissiyyah* was itself from the opposites of the ranks. Those opposites of the ranks will be given a human garment and will come forward near the time of *Qiyāmah*. They will appear dressed in human form because this *Qayām* who is coming occupies a very high station, the station of the seventh *kashf* and the seventh *sidr*. For such a *Qayām*, what kind of assembly will there be? From ancient times, those who were the opposites, who had died and been destroyed, will be presented before the *Qayām* in physical form. Yazid, Mu'awiyah, Marwan bin Hakam, Nimrud, Fir'awn, all of them will be brought in human form to the plain of Mina. Nature will drag them, as a flock is driven, and they will be brought to that plain. The *Qayām 'alā dhikrihi as-salām* will command the believers to kill them. They will do so, and the plain of Mina will be entirely covered with their corpses, until such a stench arises that the believers declare they cannot remain in that condition. They will complain to the Imam, who will command the wind, and that wind will blow and carry the corpses toward *ṣafrah*. There are four doors to that *ṣafrah*, one of which is in Hadramaut. The mention of all four doors is recorded in a book by Sayyidna 'Ali bin Sulayman. The corpses will be taken inside, and their duration there is one *qawr-e-a'ẓam*, one great epoch, *illā mā raḥima rabbī*, except for those upon whom my Lord has shown mercy. Those who had performed some good deeds will, on account of those deeds, be released before the full *qawr-e-a'ẓam* has elapsed. That release comes through volcanoes: they emerge as lava.

The example is 'Umar bin 'Abd al-'Aziz, a caliph of the Muslims from the line of Mu'awiyah, from the Banu Umayyah, who did two things of consequence. The *Bāgh-e-Fadak*, which had been usurped by 'Umar ibn al-Khattab and thereafter remained in the possession of successive governments, he returned to the Ahl al-Bayt. That was one. The second was that from seventy thousand pulpits the cursing and reviling of Sayyidna 'Ali and his progeny had been carried out, and even the keeping of the name 'Ali had been forbidden; he put a stop to that practice, declaring it an injustice upon the Ahl al-Bayt. Because of these two deeds his duration in that state was shortened, and he was released through a volcano before the full *qawr-e-a'ẓam*. A gate in that courtyard bears the name 'Abd al-'Aziz, though the 'Abd al-'Aziz of that name is theirs, from the Saudis.

The lava that comes out is a good form for them, for by it they emerge. That lava then becomes soil, and from that soil vegetation grows, and from the vegetation comes food, and so it ascends and is refined.

And then there is *Jabal 'Arafat*.

Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim. Al-Fatihah.
