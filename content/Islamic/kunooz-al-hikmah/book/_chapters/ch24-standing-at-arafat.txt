# Standing at 'Arafat

And as for the noble Mount 'Arafat: beneath it is a subtle secret, and on the Day of 'Arafah there shines in that place, from the forms, what has never occurred to the heart of any human. So the passage reads: *Wa amma Jabal 'Arafat al-sharif, fa-tahtahu sirrun latifun, wa fi Yawm 'Arafah tashruqu fi dhalika al-mahall, min al-suwar, ma la yakhturu 'ala qalbi basharin*.

Jabal 'Arafat is the *maqam* (station) of 'Arafat, the *maydan* (plain) of 'Arafat, and it is obligatory for the *haji* (pilgrim) to set foot there. Among the six days of Hajj, 'Arafat becomes the *ruh* (spirit) of those days. If a person goes for Hajj and does not have enough time to perform the 'Umrah of *Tamattu'*, but reaches 'Arafat directly on the ninth day of the month, his Hajj is considered complete. Even if he did not reach Mina, by reaching 'Arafat his Hajj is considered done. Such is the *'azmah* (greatness) of 'Arafat.

The three *maqamat* (stations), Mina, 'Arafat and Muzdalifah, are each associated with one of three great souls. 'Arafat is associated with Mawla-i Ka'inat, Mawla Rasulullah. The *maqam* of Mina is associated with Janab-i 'Ali (ع). And Muzdalifah is associated with Janab-i Bibi Fatimah. In the *Dawr al-Satr* (Period of Concealment), these three are personalities without equal, the *lubab* (essence), the *lubb al-lubab* (very essence), of the *Dawr al-Satr*.

Regarding 'Arafat, one must arrive before *ghurub al-shams* (sunset). One must set foot there before sunset, because then the next day begins; the tenth date begins. On the ninth date, one must reach 'Arafat somehow, and before sunset one's feet must touch the ground. Jabal 'Arafat has its own *sharafah* (nobility) when one reaches it, for there the *khuddam* appeared. Jabal 'Arafat is the most noble place.

*Wa li-hadha, fa-tahtahu sirrun latifun*: beneath it is a *sirrun latifun*, a subtle secret. And on Yawm 'Arafah, *wahi* (revelation) was continuously descending upon Rasulullah. When he was reaching the plain of 'Arafat, what must his state have been? So much revelation was descending, so many times, that his she-camel would sit down from its weight. It is related that from the weight of the revelation the she-camel would repeatedly lower herself, and people would try to raise her, but Rasulullah would say, "Do not lift her; she sits down from the weight of the revelation." The purity and luminosity of Rasulullah's blessed heart is measured by this, that an animal could not bear what descended upon it. The Quran and its *anwar* (lights) descended upon that heart.

On Yawm 'Arafah, *ishraq* (illumination) occurs in that place from *suwar* (forms) such that *ma la yakhturu 'ala qalbi basharin*, what has never occurred to the heart of any human. Such *suwar-i nayyirah* (luminous forms) manifest and shine upon it. And the most intense *ishraq* that occurred was connected to Ghadeer-i Khumm, that is, because of the imminent arrival at Ghadeer. Rasulullah was heading toward Ghadeer afterward. One reason was the exalted station itself and its association with Rasulullah. Another was that the noble Kalam was nearly complete; only the final *surah* relating to the succession remained. Whatever *wahi* was descending, whatever *anwar* or *ahkam* (commands) were coming from Allah's side, it was on account of this that the she-camel was in that condition.

Immediately after Hajj, Rasulullah set out for Medina. On the thirteenth of the month he arrived in Makkah, and there he informed Janab-i 'A'ishah that Allah had commanded him to appoint 'Ali as his *wasi* (successor) and *khalifah* (caliph), and asked her to keep this matter secret. But she went and told her father Abu Bakr. Abu Bakr then told 'Umar, and their scheming began.

When Rasulullah reached the approach to Ghadeer-i Khumm, the company was passing through an extremely dark valley at night. Certain persons, *munafiqun* (hypocrites), drew their daggers with the intention of attacking Rasulullah. But by Allah's power, lightning flashed so brightly, so forcefully, and for so long that Rasulullah saw all of their faces clearly. Among those who witnessed this was Hudhayfah al-Yamani. Rasulullah said, "Hudhayfah, did you see what I saw?" He said, "Yes, O Rasulullah, I also saw." Rasulullah then said, "I am instructing you to keep this a secret and not tell anyone." Hudhayfah al-Yamani kept that trust for as long as Rasulullah lived, telling no one. Afterward, 'Umar ibn al-Khattab would persistently ask Hudhayfah al-Yamani, "Was I among those who attacked the Prophet?" Hudhayfah would reply, "Rasulullah made me swear not to say this to anyone." But 'Umar would say, "By Allah, I was among them." These are great spectacles of history. As for the route, Rasulullah came to Makkah, and from Makkah he went directly to Ghadeer-i Khumm; he did not return to Medina first.

*Wa li-hadha tunfara fihi al-dhunub*: and for this reason, sins are dispersed therein. Due to the shining of these *suwar*, sins are forgiven, faults are pardoned, and *mawa'iz* and *balaya* (trials and tribulations) are removed and finished. *Wa fi al-mawaqifi quma'irun tahirah wa asrarun zahirah*: and in the stopping places are pure hidden treasures and manifest secrets. In the *mawaqif*, meaning Mina, Muzdalifah and 'Arafat, at these stations of standing, there are *khama'ir* (hidden essences) that are very pure, and secrets that are manifest; things like *tayy al-ard* (the folding of the earth) are among them.

*Wa li-hadha hurrima 'ala al-anam qatl al-ashjar*: and for this reason it has been forbidden for mankind to cut down the trees and shrubs there. *Fi tilka al-jiha al-lati nahat fiha al-asrar*: in those regions in which the secrets are contained. *Fa-hiya mujawiratun li-tilka al-khama'ir al-kaminah*: for the trees are neighbours to those hidden *khama'ir*, mingled with the *dhubat al-batinah* (hidden essences).

*Wa hadhihi qatratun min bihar 'ilm Mawla-i al-'Alam*: and this is a drop from the oceans of the knowledge of the Master of the World. *Wa sadaqatun min jumlati sadaqatihi al-mursalah 'ala 'abdihi al-mawjud al-mujid lahu min al-'adam*: and a charity from his many charities, sent upon his servant whom He brought into existence from nothingness. The servant was nothing at all, and from nothingness He made him, and made him capable of giving answers and clarifying realities. This is said about his *walid* (father).

*Jazahu Allahu 'an imami wa sayyidi afdala ma jaza al-du'at*: may Allah reward him on behalf of my Imam and my master with the best of what the *du'at* (supplicants and propagators) are rewarded. *Wa razaqna minhu maqbul al-shafa'at*: and grant us sustenance from his accepted intercessions. *Wa sunna 'an al-bayni al-ladhi ulqiya 'alayka bi-haqqi mawlaya*: and may we be protected from that veil which has been cast upon you, by the right of my Mawla, which is established upon you. *Wa man adha'a shay'an min hadhihi al-asrar fa-qad tabawwa'a maq'adahu min al-nar*: and whoever broadcasts anything of these secrets has certainly prepared his seat in the Fire. *Wa bari'un min al-'Aziz al-Ghaffar*: and he is disavowed by the Mighty, the Forgiving. Disavowed means that he will be disassociated, that Allah will be free of him. So too in Surah al-Bara'ah, *bara'atun min Allahi*: displeasure is declared, that the displeasure of Allah and His Messenger rests upon the polytheists. That is the meaning of it: to become independent of, to be free from.
