# The Cold Edges of the World: Where the Opponents Are Cast

The day was the twenty-first of Rabi' al-Awwal. When the twenty-first of any month falls, it stands among those seven dates in which inauspiciousness (*nahusat*) is gathered. Yet the du'a appointed for it is a rare (*nadir*) du'a. Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq composed such du'as with great subtlety, and the tone of this one is unmistakable: fulfill my needs, forgive my sins, and I will not rise from this spot until You do this for me. It is a strange and wondrous tone, like a child insistent with his father, saying, "Get me this thing," and bringing his father to agree one way or another. Do this for me, do that for me, and until You do it I will not rise from this spot. That is the tone of the du'a recited on the twenty-first. The day also marks the passing of the *awsiya'*.

Several errors commonly enter the recitation of Ayat al-Kursi. At the beginning there is *bi-'urwatillahi al-wuthqa* (by the firmest handle of Allah), and mistakes gather there. The second place is *li wa lakum wa li-walidayya* (for me and for you and for my parents), which is often recited as *li wa-likum*; it is not *wa-likum* but *wa-lakum*, with a *zabar*. Then there is the passage:

> *shahida Allahu annahu la ilaha illa Huwa wa al-mala'ikatu wa ulu al-'ilm... la ilaha illa Huwa al-'aziz al-hakim. Inna al-dina 'inda Allahi al-islam... wa man ya'mal... su'an*

Allah witnesses that there is no god but He, and the angels and those of knowledge; there is no god but He, the Almighty, the Wise. Indeed, the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam, and whoever does evil.

The reading is *wa-man ya'mal*, though many say *wa-min ya'mal*. The tone of the Quran Majid is *wa-man ya'mal*. It occurs in two places, written differently in each: in the shorter form it differs, in the longer form it differs. In the Quran Karim it is *wa-man ya'mal*, while the one that comes later is *fa-man ya'mal*. The one at the beginning belongs to the du'a of the *wazifa*, not to the Quran Majid itself; the one that comes later is the Quranic verse.

Likewise, *wa yastaghfirullah* is incorrect; it is *yastaghfirillah*. In both instances further down it is *yastaghfirun*.

The gravest error concerns *'Aliyyun al-'Azim*, where the reading should be *'Aliyya al-'Azim*. The word comes in three forms in the Quran: *'Aliyyun al-'Azim*, *'Aliyya al-'Azim*, and *'Aliyyi al-'Azim*, with *zabar*, *zer*, and *pesh*. All three have come, each in a different context and place. Here, at the end of Ayat al-Kursi, when *istighfar* is performed seven times, it is *al-'Aliyya al-'Azim*, with a *zabar*. In the *ad'iyah*, *'Aliyyan* is recited at the beginning.

The sevenfold recitation at the close, *astaghfirullah al-'aliyya al-'azim* (I seek forgiveness from Allah, the Most High, the Most Great), has several reasons behind it. Among them is that in the seven *dawr al-sitr* (cycles of concealment), this present existence is the seventh existence, the seventh *zuhur* (manifestation). *Istighfar* is therefore performed for the six manifestations that have passed, and then for the seventh as *al-'aliyya al-'azim*. These are the seven acts of *istighfar*.

In a previous *majlis* it was said that the name of Mawla 'Ali stands within the Kalima Sharif. It appears in many places: *'Aliyyan waliyyu Allah* (Ali is the friend of Allah). It was said that *'Aliyyun* occurs in Surah Maryam, where the words *'ala siraatin mustaqeema* (upon a straight path) are recited as *'Aliyyan mustaqeema*. The *zer* and *zabar* were changed, but the intention was directed toward Janab 'Ali. The verse is:

> *Inna hadha siraatu 'aliyyin mustaqeem*

Verily, this is the path of 'Ali, a straight one.

On that point it was said that this is Mawla 'Ali's own testimony that he was *sadiq* (truthful). Yet the word *sadiq* is not used there. The other verse cited is:

> *Wa'j'al li lisana sidqin 'aliyya*

And grant me a tongue of truthfulness, an exalted one.

That is a different verse, and the point was made upon *sidqin 'aliyya*. The first remains *inna hadha siraatu 'aliyyin mustaqeem*.

### The Seventh Question

*Al-Su'al al-Sabi'.* *Qala ba'du al-mufidina, jazahum Allahu 'anna afdhala al-jaza'* (Some of the benefactors said, may Allah reward them with the best of rewards on our behalf), *wa kadhalika fi kitab Kalilah wa Dimnah* (and likewise in the book Kalilah wa Dimnah). Kalilah wa Dimnah became widely famous. It is an Arabic rendering drawn from the Panchatantra, and Ibn al-Muqaffa' composed it. Ibn al-Muqaffa' was a great scholar who wrote several books, among them *Al-Adab al-Kabir* and *Al-Adab al-Saghir*, and this one as well. Since he lived after the time of the Prophet, he also wrote certain matters concerning the Hudud.

The report is that *yawma sa'ala al-natiq* (on the day the Natiq asked), whose mention comes in what follows, the north (*al-shimal*) is the path (*masir*) and destination (*ma'ad*) for the opponents (*azdad*) of the A'immah, and their followers are in the west (*fi al-maghrib*). The north here means the peripheries. There is the northern pole and the southern pole, those parts of the earth buried under extreme snow. In the extreme north and the extreme south lies the path and place of return for the opponents of the A'immah (*azdad al-a'immah*) and for the followers of those opponents. Their destination is there, at the pole. *Wa fi al-maghrib wa azdad al-anbiya' fi al-junub* (and in the west, and the opponents of the prophets in the south): the followers of the opponents of the A'immah have their destination in the west, and the opponents of the Anbiya' are in the south (*al-junub*). *Wa al-tabi'ina bihim fi al-mashriq* (and their followers in the east): the followers of the opponents of the Anbiya' are in the east (*al-mashriq*).

There is also another explanation. The opponents, those who are opponents of the A'immah and of the Hujjat and of the Hijabs of the A'immah, are gathered in *ra's wa dhanab* (head and tail). The *dhanab* (tail) is a more evil place than the *ra's* (head). These are *wahmi* (imaginal) realities, lying beyond the celestial sphere (*nitaq al-qamar*). *Ra's wa dhanab* are also called Rahu and Ketu. In the *dhanab*, the tail, lies the destination of the opponents of the A'immah and their Hijabs; in the *ra's*, the head, that of the opponents of the Anbiya' and the *Du'at*. That is their destination. This is what is said, and it is why in the polar regions there is no namaz.

The *mashriq* and *maghrib* intended here are places, but places of severe cold. The north and south are bitterly cold, and the extreme east and extreme west are in a similar state, with snow there as well. They fall within the map of the world. Within the world, then, the north, the south, the east, and the west, taken as the extreme boundary points, are all described as bad. It is a widespread misconception to read this otherwise and to suppose it does not concern one's own region, since broadly speaking every land falls within east and west. But that east and that west mean the end corners, places such as Tibet and Alaska, one at the far edge and the other far up beyond. The intent is the extreme, faraway places, where the east ends, or the west, or the north, or the south. These are the places always covered in snow, where there are six months of day and six months of night, a condition found up in the north and south. As for the extreme east and west, there are many islands in the Pacific around the dateline, on both sides, on the Japan side and the America side. Those are extreme positions. The volcanic islands of Hawaii and others lie there, all in the middle of the Pacific Ocean.

The questioner then asks: *Fa-kayfa yunsakhu ma yunsakhu ila hadhihi al-mawadhi' al-nufus wa'l-ajsad?* (So how is that which is transmigrated transmigrated to these places, the souls and the bodies?). How do the transmigrating things reach those places, and is it the souls (*nufus*) of the opponents of the A'immah or of the Anbiya' that arrive there, or their bodies (*ajsad*)? Sayyidna Abdullah Sahab always said that one should rely on the answer, not on the question.

The question continues. *A'udhu bika min masiri hadhihi al-hussad* (I seek refuge in You from the destiny of these envious ones): refuge is sought in Allah from the destination and place of these envious ones. *Wa a'tasimu bika min fi'li ahl al-fasad* (And I take refuge in You from the actions of the people of corruption): *'ismah*, protection, is sought that one may remain free of the deeds of these people of corruption. *Wa 'ala kam mudadin yulhaquna bi-tilka al-diyar?* (And for how long a period are they attached to those lands?): for how long do the opponents of the A'immah and the opponents of the Anbiya' go and remain there? *Wa hal al-riyah yuwassiluhum bi-shay'in wa al-riyah shimal wa junub wa dabur wa saba?* (And do the winds connect them with something, and the winds are north, south, west, and east?): do winds blow that reach them, and are the winds named north, south, *dabur*, and *saba* available to them? *Fa-tawallaw 'alayya* (So they turned away from me): have mercy upon me. *Wa a'udhu bika wa bi-Sayyidina Muhammad ibn Isma'il, wa bi-waladika Sayyidina 'Ali ibn Hibatullah min amrin taqulu 'anni atasawwaru ma laysa yu'dhanu li* (And I seek refuge in You and in our master Muhammad ibn Isma'il, and in your son our master 'Ali ibn Hibatullah, from a matter about which you would say of me that I imagine what is not permitted for me): that no such conception be formed for which permission has not been given. *Wa as'aluka bi-man ya'lu 'alayka ila babika al-'azim wa khalifatika al-karim, an tasturani wa tamhunani ridaka wa sitraka wa 'awnaka wa safhaka* (And I ask You by the one who is exalted before You to Your great door and Your noble successor, that You conceal me and grant me Your pleasure, Your covering, Your help, and Your forgiveness): that faults be kept concealed, that the honor of His pleasure be attained, that affairs be kept covered, that His help accompany, and that He forgive. *Wa anta al-mukhyyar fi al-'ata'i aw al-man'* (And You are the one with choice in giving or withholding): the choice to grant or to refuse the answer belongs to Him.

### The Answer

*Al-jawab 'ala dhalika al-su'al, naqulu bi-'awnillahi al-kabir al-muta'al wa minnati waliyyi al-'asri wa'l-zaman* (The answer to that question: we say with the help of Allah, the Great, the Exalted, and the grace of the Master of the era and time), *'alayhi salawat al-rahim al-rahman, wa ta'idi mawlaya al-'alam al-anwar wa sirrihi al-sari ila lata'if al-suwar* (Upon him be the blessings of the Merciful, the Compassionate, and the support of my master, the luminous banner, and his secret which flows to the subtleties of the forms). Upon him be the blessings of the *Rahim* and the *Rahman*, and with the support of the master who is the banner, Hibatullah Sahab, most luminous, whose secret flows toward the subtleties of the forms.

The answer is this: *inna intiqaala azdad al-a'immati wa al-nutaqa', wa al-tabi'ina bihim min al-'awam, awan al-ayyam, ila tilka al-atraf al-mash'umah wa al-jihat al-mal'umah, tarmihim ilayha al-'inayat al-ilahiyyah* (Verily, the transmigration of the opponents of the Imams and the Natiqs, and their followers from among the common folk, at the time of the days, to those inauspicious peripheries and the cursed directions: the divine providence throws them there). The opponents of the A'immah and the Nutaqa', who are themselves very great, and likewise their followers among the lowly, are carried in their transmigration to those peripheral places which are utterly bad and those directions which are cursed. The divine providence (*'inayat ilahiyyah*), meaning the divine arrangements through him who has become the viceroy of the Tenth upon the earth, that is, the *hudud siddiqiyyah*, casts them to these places. *Wa al-qudrat al-rabbaniyyah* (and the lordly power): the lordly power carries out this act. *Fa-yakununa fiha 'ala mithali hayawan wa'l-jibal* (So they are in it in the likeness of animals and mountains). There they exist in the likeness of animals and mountains. As stated in another book, there are vast chunks of ice, and beneath such a chunk lies an immense animal that the ice has wholly covered. Or there are great mountains within which their filthy refuse accumulates.

*Wa al-hissu mawjudun fihim* (And sense perception is present in them). In those upon whom these things fall, sense perception is present; they feel it. So too it has been said that the person who does not take the *'ahd sharif* (noble covenant) has his *nafs al-hissiyyah* (sensory soul) pervading his body, on account of which he remains in torment after death and continues to feel that torment. This same *nafs al-hissiyyah* is at work in *dhabihah* (slaughter): with *Allahu Akbar* the *nafs al-hissiyyah* of the goat is separated from it, and by that separation it is elevated, and one day it will come into human form. Because of that separation its meat becomes lawful (*halal*). It has also been said that the meat should not be eaten raw even after the *nafs al-hissiyyah* has been separated; it should be cooked. And even after cooking, the Rasoolullah says that when the steam rises it should be allowed to escape and the meat not eaten while hot, because whatever remainder of the *nafs al-hissiyyah* is left departs with the steam.

The text continues: *fihim, bila intiqal, muqabbalina bi'l-salasil* (in them, without transmigration, shackled with chains), *wa'l-aghlal* (and shackles), fetters upon them and handcuffs as well, *mu'adhdhabina bi'l-'adhab al-ha'il al-muhin* (tormented with the terrifying, humiliating punishment), a punishment vast, dreadful, and horrifying, *bi'l-harri wa'l-bard* (with heat and cold), with extreme heat and extreme cold. It is said likewise that those who reach *saqar* (hellfire) are given the light of the moon by day as punishment and the light of the sun by night.

*al-mu'lim al-nakil. Wa amma qawluka wa hal hiya ajsadun am al-nufus al-marmiyyah fi al-bilad wa al-bu's* (the painful, exemplary punishment. As for your statement: "Are they bodies or the souls cast into the lands and misery?"). The question was whether it is their bodies or their souls that reach these places, cast there amid calamity and hardship. *Fa'lam, fa-inna-ma yufariqu ahl hadhihi al-tabaqat illa al-tasawwur al-khabith al-qa'im min nasb li-awliya' Allah wa'l-adawat* (So know, for nothing separates the people of these classes except the vile conception arising from enmity toward the friends of Allah and the enmities). What separates them from these classes is their vile conception. One who harbors a wrong conception sends that vile conception to those places. *Al-qa'im min nasb li-awliya' Allah wa'l-adawat*: that which is done in enmity with the friends of Allah. *Wa amm al-nufus fa-hiya tashrif fi tilka al-ajsad al-radi'ah* (As for the souls, they are ennobled in those base bodies): the souls pervade within their bodies, which are base. *Wa yakunu minha fiha al-wayl wa al-thubur* (And from it, in it, is woe and destruction): from this comes ruination, destruction, and affliction for them. *Wa takunu akthafa min al-ajsad li-'adhami ma a'taw* (And they become denser than the bodies because of the greatness of what they were given). They become denser than bodies, because what the Qudrat had given advances and, after a period of time, makes his soul out of his bodies. Then the man, out of his own foolishness, puts his soul back into his body. The favor the Qudrat bestowed, he did not accept. As it is said:

> *al-shirku zulmun 'azim*

Associating partners with God is a great injustice.

Allah ta'ala will forgive every sin, from one magnitude to another, except this. As it is said:

> *inna Allaha yaghfiru al-dhunuba jami'an*

Verily, Allah forgives all sins.

Allah has the power to forgive every sin, but He will not forgive the ascribing of partners to Him.

The state there is *la yahya wa la yamut* (neither living nor dead). When they enter that punishment, it is *la yahya wa la yamut*. So too in Surah al-A'la:

> *la yamutu fiha wa la yahya*

He will neither die therein nor live.

They neither live nor die. It has been said of them that in their punishments they are killed a thousand times each day and revived a thousand times. Such is the state, and immense is the torment and the pain within it.

The scribe of this text was not a great scholar, and many mistakes stand in it. The correct reading is *wa al-wayl wa al-thubur*, yet here *shol* is written. Many such errors have entered through the transcription of the copyists.

*Wa amma qawluka 'ala kam mudad yulhaquna...* (As for your statement, for how long a period are they attached...). Here *kam madad* is written. The word is *mudadd*, the plural of *muddat*; *madad* is something else entirely. For how many ages are they attached to those peripheries, and when do they arrive there? *Fa-ba'da an yaktatifu adhraw al-silsilah fi kulli nawmin minha sab'ina qamisan bila khilaf* (So after they have snatched the arms of the chain, in each sleep of it, seventy shirts, without disagreement). What has been learned of this stands in the books of the *awliya' Allah* and the *hudat*, upon them be the salutations of their Imam, and blessings be upon them.

As for the statement concerning the winds, they differ in all their states. Among them are ascending, noble ones, and some winds ascend. *Ittasalat bi-kulli latif* (connected with every subtle thing): they meet with certain subtleties. *Wa ma kana min al-habitin ittasala bi-ma yula'imuhu min al-zalimin* (And what was of the descending ones connected with what suits it from among the oppressors): among them are descending winds also, whose connection is with the oppressors. *Wa qad yattasilu al-salihu bi'l-talih* (And the righteous may connect with the corrupt), the meaning of which is not disclosed. *Wa yakhussu bi'l-qawl al-wadih li-mujibat qadhat ba'da asbabin* (and he specifies with the clear statement the requirements that some causes have decreed): for what reason it occurs, Allah knows best. *Min ahl al-jahl wa al-iktisab. Fa-khudh dhalika bi'l-wajiz min al-qawl* (from the people of ignorance and acquisition. So take that with the concise statement). *Wa lillahi subhanahu al-quwwah wa al-hawl* (And to Allah, the Glorified, belongs the power and the might). To Allah alone belong the power and the might.

### There Is No Power and No Strength

The words *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billahi al-'aliyyi al-'azim* (there is no power and no strength except in Allah, the Most High, the Most Great) are not a verse of the Quran Majid. The verse of the Quran Majid, which appears perhaps in Surah Bani Isra'il, is *la quwwata illa ma sha' Allah* (there is no power except what Allah wills). But when the Master of the Universe began to use *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billahi al-'aliyyi al-'azim* in his du'as, this saying became so renowned, and Allah ta'ala so loved it, that He made it common in du'as. It appears very often in du'as, especially at their conclusion.

The realities explained of it are these. One meaning of *la hawla wa la quwwata* is that a man has no strength to refrain from evil and to do good except what Allah ta'ala grants. This is the outward meaning. The other meaning is that there is no power and no might except that which is through Allah, *al-'Aliyy al-'Azim*. And within that it has been said that there is no power in the external realm (*bab-i zahir*), nor in the internal realm (*bab-i batin*), by which a *haykal* (spiritual form) might be formed. That is, the *suwarat al-nayyirah* (luminous forms) do not reach the Imam.

The form of the deceased, one day, through the *hudud daniyyah* and the *hudud 'aliyyah*, gathers with the *da'i*, and rises upward until it reaches the *Bab al-Abwab* (Gate of Gates), who is the *bahr al-zakhar* (overflowing ocean), also called *fasl al-khitab* (the decisive speech). There all the forms of the believers gather with the *Bab al-Abwab*. It is a vast gathering. Yet within that gathering, for all its immensity, there is not the form of a *haykal*. A *haykal* is that which is in human form. When this gathering is prepared, the present Imam (ع) receives it and grants it to his son. The son, who until that moment was Imam *bi'l-quwwah* (potentially), becomes through this gathering's connection to him Imam *bi'l-fi'l* (actually). The present Imam then passes away, and his *Bab al-Abwab* also passes away. The *qalb al-haykal* (heart of the *haykal*) of the son, who has now become the Imam, is formed by the *Bab al-Abwab*. The highest form within the *haykal* that the son of the Imam now holds is that of the *Bab al-Abwab*, who rendered a great service, being the greatest servant of the Imam in gathering so immense an assembly through his arrangements.

What is said here, then, is *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah*. There is no power and no strength except in Allah, by His grace. And who is meant by Allah? He is the Imam of the era and time. And who is *'Aliyy al-'Azim*? The *ayn al-a'immah* (essence of the Imams), who is the manifestation of 'Ali (ع). All the A'immah are therefore the *hijabs* (veils) of *'Aliyy al-'Azim*, the *hijabs* of the *ayn al-a'immah*. In this the entire picture emerges, from the believer up to the *ayn al-a'immah*, revealing the process that is unfolding.

*Ya Allahumma, ashrah al-fatihah hadiyyatan wasilatan suniyyatan zakiyyatan mardiyyatan ila ruhi Sayyidina 'Ali ibn Hibatullah, qaddasa Allahu ruhahu wa anwara darihahu wa da'afa 'alayna min barakatihi wa ta'idatihi wa 'inayatihi wa taqabbal minna al-ifadah wa al-istifadah bi-haqqi Sayyidina Muhammadin wa 'alihi wa barik wa sallim. Al-Fatihah.*
