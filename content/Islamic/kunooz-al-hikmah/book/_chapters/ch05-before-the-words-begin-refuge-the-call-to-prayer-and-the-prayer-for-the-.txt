# Before the Words Begin: Refuge, the Call to Prayer, and the Prayer for the Da'i

*Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim.* By His name, the Exalted, the High. *Wa bihi nasta'in, wa sallallahu 'ala rasulih, Sayyidna Muhammadin wa 'ala aali Muhammadin wa barik wa sallim.*

The opening rests on the statement of Allah Ta'ala:

> *Wa idha qara'ta al-Qur'an fasta'idh billahi min al-shaytan al-rajim.*

And when you recite the Quran, seek refuge in Allah from Satan the accursed.

A question stands at the threshold. This is the Book of Allah itself, so why was the command given to seek refuge, `ta'awwudh`? Why should the reciter of the Quran be told to take shelter before he begins? The Book is the word of Allah and the source of guidance, yet people have been led astray by this very thing. A `da'i` once held up this `Kitabullah` (Book of Allah) and said that it is this very book that has led communities astray, meaning that those who misinterpreted it and drew wrong meanings from it caused communities to go astray. For this reason, upon the command of Allah, `ta'awwudh` is adopted at the beginning of the `da'wah`, both when the Quran is recited and when other `du'as` containing Quranic verses are read.

The adhan is the exception. `Ta'awwudh` is not to be said while giving the adhan. There, only `Bismillah` is said. And within the adhan, the two final utterances of *La ilaha illallah* are not alike: the very last one is not recited in the same emphatic manner as the first, but in a soft tone. A difference must be kept between them. That closing utterance is bound to the `ihtizaz` of a particular being, a `dhat` for whom there are special prayers, in that he is the last `mushir`, the last person who will depart from his entire circle in the blink of an eye. That matter will happen in the blink of an eye, and so the utterance is not to be elongated.

Concerning the verse *wa qulil hamdu lillah*, and say, "All praise is for Allah," the `salawat` is recited after it. Since the verse mentions only the saying of the `takbira`, an impression can arise that one should begin directly with "Allahu Akbar," even without `Bismillah`, as though between the words of the adhan and the words of the verse no other words may stand.

It is not like that. After *wa kabbirhu takbira*, and magnify Him with great magnification, one may recite the `salawat` first, and then *wa qulil hamdu lillah... wa kabbirhu takbira*, "Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar." There is no hard and fast rule. `Bismillah` should be said before the adhan. There is no strict rule that the adhan must begin immediately upon *wa kabbirhu takbira*; before it we read from our own side, and there is no harm in doing so.

As for the order, the `tartib`, Imam-ul-Ma'mool explained in a `tashrih` that `hamd`, praise, should come first, and that is why the verse says *qulil hamd*. The `salawat` is recited afterward, and so it comes later. `Hamd` is a higher thing: recite `hamd` first and place nothing before it, then the `salawat`. In this way he explained the order.

A further point attaches to this verse. It is the last verse of Surah Bani Isra'il, and among the names that verses carry, it is called `Ayat al-'Izz`, the Verse of Honor.

### The Prayer for the Da'i

*Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim.*

> *Allahumma wahfaz dhata al-da'i al-jami'a li-asrarihim al-mujtama'a ladayhi anwaruhum.*

O Allah, protect the being of the `da'i`, who is the collector of their secrets and with whom their lights are gathered.

This speaks of the future. In life, `asrar` means `riyah`, and `anwar` means `surah`, forms. During the `da'i`'s lifetime the `riyah` of the `hudud al-da'wah` or of the `mu'minin`, and the `anwar` in the sense of `surah`, are not yet fully formed. The `riyah` emerge after death: after burial or after passing away, the `riyah` begin to emerge, for three days. That is a matter for later. But later, they will be gathered with him, their `asrar`, meaning their `riyah`, and gathered with him their `anwar`, meaning their `surah`.

`Wahmi hawzatahu`, and protect his `hawzah`. A `hawzah` is a place around which a boundary has been drawn, a walled enclosure. What is meant here is the center of the `da'wah`. Protect the center of the `da'wah` `min fawaran fir'awni al-zaman`, from the aggression of the Pharaoh of the age, for `li-kulli da'in fir'awn`: for every `da'i` there is a Pharaoh, an opponent of the `da'wah`.

The matter was raised in Hujjat that during the time of Sayyidna Husayn ibn Ahmad there did not seem to be any such Pharaoh openly opposing the `da'i`. It was a magnificent era, a time of complete peace; the disturbances came later. To this the answer was given that the `da'i` of the Dawoodis is also a kind of Pharaoh, in a passive sense. He did not openly confront, but had he found the opportunity, he would have done as Dawood ibn Qutub did and made a claim, becoming a `mudda'i`. Here, however, the opposition is greater. There it was not so; here there is a Pharaoh.

`wa ballighhu jami'a ma'moolihi ya Rahim ya Rahman`, and may Allah convey to him all his aspirations and desires, and fulfill the `da'i`'s desires. `Ya Rahim ya Rahman` is itself a `tasbih`, the `tasbih` of the Pir.

A question of order arises here as well: should it not be `ya Rahman ya Rahim`? In the Quran, `Rahman` always precedes `Rahim`. But in books this order is not always observed, because of rhyme schemes, `qafiyah-payma'i`. Since `Fir'awn al-zaman` came before it, `ya Rahim ya Rahman` follows for the sake of the rhyme. In the other divine attributes as well, `Rahim` always comes second: `Ra'uf-ur-Rahim`, `Aziz-ur-Rahim`. It has never come first in the Quran. In the `du'as` and `sahifas`, however, `Rahim` precedes `Rahman` in several places. And `Rahman` has also appeared alone in the Quran, as in the verse:

> *Qulid'ullaha awid'ur-Rahman.*

Say, "Call upon Allah or call upon the Most Merciful."

No other name of Allah has appeared alone in this way.

In Surah al-Hajj, at one place, it reads `Ya Dhal-Jalali wal-Ikram`, O Possessor of Majesty and Honor. The verses of Surah al-Hajj from 58 to 65 are very blessed verses. Among them are eight verses called `Mafatih al-Jinan`, the Keys to the Gardens, corresponding to the eight heavens, whose keys these verses are. There is great virtue in reciting them. One verse alone contains fifteen of the `Asma' al-Husna`, the Beautiful Names of Allah, and `Ya Dhal-Jalali wal-Ikram` is counted as one among them. `Rahman` appears there as well.

The prayer continues. `wa anir masabih da'watihi`, and illuminate the lamps of his `da'wah`, meaning the `hudud al-da'wah`; illuminate the entire `da'wah`. `wa kaththir 'adada shi'atihi`, and increase the number of his followers. `wa asri ilayna mawaddahu`, and cause to flow toward us his substance, his `mawad`, and his confirmations, his `ta'yidat`. `wa 'allimna bi-lata'ifi barakatihi`, and teach us through the subtleties of his blessings, directing the subtle dimensions of his blessings toward us. `warzuqna tahannunahu wa shafaqatahu`, and grant us the sustenance of this `da'i`'s kindness, his `tahannun`, and his compassion, his `shafaqah`. `wa 'afwahu wa rahmatuhu`, and his forgiveness, his `'afw`, and his mercy, his `rahmah`, that he may forgive our sins.

`Ya 'Aliyyu ya 'Azim, ya 'Aliyyu ya 'Azim.` This too is a `tasbih`, one performed on Wednesdays.
