# The Reluctant Answerer: How These Questions Came to Be Written

And after this, the treatise opens with an account of its own occasion: *Wa ba'du, fa-inna-ha waradat masa'il 'aliyatun*, "And after this, there came lofty issues." Certain lofty questions were presented, coming forward *ila al-hadrat al-samiyah*, to the elevated presence, that is, to the venerable *da'i*, *min ba'd al-awlad*, from some of the children, the *awlad*. The presence intended is Sayyidna Hibtullah al-Mu'ayyad, and the questions are those which the *abna' al-da'wah*, the children of the *da'wah*, bring forward. *Fa-sata al-amru al-'alami*: the term *al-'alami* belongs to Sayyidna Hibtullah, one of whose titles is *Alamuddin*, the banner of religion, the high banner of the faith. "So the command of *al-'alami*," of the *da'i*, "flowed *ila 'abdihi akhar*," to his other servant. Hamza b. al-Walid is the speaker, and Sayyidna Ali records that the command flowed upon him, *al-haqir*, *bi-an yujibu 'ala su'alatihi*, that he should answer those questions, *wa ma lahu bi-aqlamihi tatr*, "And with his pens, to set it down in writing as well." The answer was to be not merely verbal but committed to writing.

*Wa kuntu aradtu an a'tadhira ila mawlayya li-qusuri 'ilmi wa bu'di fitrati wa fahmi*, "And I had wanted to make an excuse to my master on account of the deficiency of my knowledge and the distance of my nature and understanding." The wish was to present the excuse to the master that his knowledge is deficient, that he is greatly lacking, and that his intellect and understanding are weak. It is the *da'i*, Sayyidna Ali, who says this. *fa-'aradtu mawani'a ba'd al-bilad*, "And I presented the obstacles posed by distant lands." People from various lands had brought questions; how could they all be answered? This was the excuse. *fa-asra'tu al-ta'ata*, "But in the end, I hastened to obey." He obeyed and accepted the command. *wa asdaqtu al-murad*, "And I found the desired object," that is, he attained the goal. *wa tadharra'tu ila suratihi al-nuraniyah*, "And I supplicated through his luminous form," his *surah nuraniyah*. *wa awwaltu ila mawaddihi al-qudsaniyah*, "And I relied upon his holy substance," his *mawad qudsaniyah*.

A remarkable thing has been said here. He says, "I supplicated through your luminous form." The *da'i*'s form, his *surah*, has not yet been constituted, for the *da'i* is still *natiqah*, *hissiyah*, and *namiyah*. The *surah* is formed only after passing away. Yet the *da'i*'s station is such, and his personality so magnificent, that his very *natiqah* must be understood as the blessed *surah*, the one that will be fully realized as a *surah* on the Day of Resurrection.

*wa wassamtu hadhihi al-risalah bi-kunuz al-hikmah*, "And I named this treatise *Kunooz al-Hikmah*," the Treasures of Wisdom. These are treasures stored for the People of *Ismah*, *arbab al-'ismah*. By *arbab al-'ismah* here the Imams are not intended, but rather the masters of partial *ismah*, *juzwi 'ismah*, that is, the *du'at*. For them these things have been stored, so that they may draw substance, *mawad*, from them. As was stated in the introduction to this *risalah*, this matter had not been articulated by any *da'i* before: that the *aql al-awwal*, the first intellect, and the *akhir al-mawjud*, the last existent being, meaning the *mustajib* who has just entered the *hadrat sharifah* and stepped into the circle of faith, are equal. Equal in what? In the *ma'rifah*, the gnosis, of the *Mubda' Ta'ala*, the Exalted Originator. The *aql al-awwal*, despite its proximity, has no advantage in the *ma'rifah* of the *Mubda' Ta'ala*; it is perplexed and bewildered in the very same way that we are perplexed and bewildered. No *da'i* had said this before.

*fa-ma kana fiha min sawab*, "And whatever is correct in it," whatever correctness is present in this book, *fa-mansubun ilayya ila man ajra lisani*, "then it is attributed to the one who caused my tongue to flow," the one who gave such strength to his tongue that he could express the matter and set it down. It is attributed to him, to the *da'i*. This *sawab*, from *sad-waw-alif-ba*, means correctness, not reward. Common usage runs, "Jazakallah bil-khairi was-sawab," and people write it with the sense of reward, *thawab*. Here, however, *sawab* means correctness: "What you have said is correct." It is a subtle point, and everyone tends to read *sawab* as *thawab*.

*ila man ajra lisani*, to the one who caused my tongue to flow. *wa ma kana fiha min khata', wa billahi al-'iyadh*, "And whatever mistake is in it, and in Allah I seek refuge," *fa-mansubun ilayya*, "then it is attributed to me." The error is his own. *idh ana alaqtu al-jani*, "As I am a sinner," a *jani*. People sometimes call their beloved or their children *jani*, but in Arabic *jani* means sinner.

*wa ana akhidhu hadh al-'ahd al-mu'akkad*, "And I am taking this confirmed covenant," this *'ahd*. *wa mithaquhu al-mughalladh al-mushaddad*, "And its pact, which is severe and strong." *'ala man waqa'a hadha fi yadayhi*, "Upon whosoever this falls into his hands," whoever's hands this book reaches and who becomes aware of its secret, *an la yaqra'a fihi*, "that he should not read in it," *wa la yata'ammala ma'anihi*, "and should not ponder its meanings," *illa bi-fushin min huwa al-naqid*, "except with the permission of one who is the *naqid*." A *naqid* is one who can discern the good from the bad, who understands what is right and what is wrong, one who criticizes with insight. *wal-basir*, "and the insightful one," who possesses inner sight. *man lahu al-taqdim wal-ta'khir*, "the one who has the authority of *taqdim* and *ta'khir*," who can bring someone forward within the *hudud* or put them back, who holds full authority in appointments. Only with his permission may one read it. *wa illa, fa-huwa bari'un min al-'Aliyy al-ma'bud*, "Otherwise, he is absolved from the Exalted, the Worshipped," meaning that Allah, the Most High, Whom we worship, will know his matter. *wa min kaffat al-mahdudin wal-hudud*, "And from all the *mahdudin* and *hudud*," from their obedience, he is absolved.

*wa hadha qawl al-sa'il hujjatuhu*, "And this is the statement of the questioner, his argument." The one who is writing this introduction has set down the entire question within it. *awradtuhu bi-fassih ru'yatihi*, "I have presented it with the clarity of his vision," in the very manner in which the question was brought to the *da'i*. *hafidhallahu al-maqam al-'azim*, "May Allah protect the great station," the *maqam al-'azim*. *wal-'alam al-rabbani al-'alim*, "And the lordly, knowing banner," the one who is a lordly scholar, deeply learned: *Alamuddin Hibtullah b. Ibrahim al-Muqrami*. *hafidhahullahu wa dafa'a 'anhu al-aswa'*, "May Allah protect him and ward off evils from him."

The word *aswa'* raises a question: does it mean the *da'i* has evils, and that we are praying for them to be warded off? Sayyidna al-Sijilmasi says, "My sins are like mountains, *idhama intalat al-jibali*," they have risen so high. Were his sins truly so great? We do not regard even great sins of our own in such terms. The meaning of such expressions lies elsewhere. The *da'i*'s sins, in reality, are these: when they intend to perform a good deed, to write a book, to record a noble thought or concept, to benefit someone, but because of some obstacle they are unable to carry it through. The good deed they intended but could not complete, this they regard as their sin, that they had the intention but could not translate it into action. They count those as grave sins.

Allah also says to the Prophet that his former and latter sins are forgiven. God forbid that the Prophet should have sins. On this point there is what Sayyidna Husayn b. Ismail, Qaddas Allahu Ruhahu, said. He recounted that he and his brother were studying when Muhsin came, the brother of Sayyidna Ali b. Husayn, a thin and frail man who was not himself a great scholar but was always in the presence of the *da'i*, his brother, and used to ask many questions. He was both Muhsin's son-in-law and his father-in-law, Muhsin b. Ali. He came and asked, "May I put a question?" The question concerned the first verse of Surah al-Fath: *Inna fatahna laka fathan mubina*, "Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory," *li-yaghfira laka Allahu ma taqaddama min dhanbika wa ma ta'akhkhara*, "so that Allah may forgive you your former and latter sins." What were the Prophet's former and latter sins?

Sayyidna looked and asked, "Do you know?" The answer, drawing from the *da'i*'s substance, his *mawad*, is this: the verse is not referring to the Prophet's own being, which is pure, *bari*. Rather, the *hujab*, those who rise from the *hudud al-da'wah* and later become deputies, do not initially possess the substance of *ismah*; *ismah* comes to them later. In the meantime they are unable to fully govern themselves and sometimes err. The allusion is to them. The former and latter sins refer to the *hujab* of the Prophet, those inclined towards Abu Bakr. That inclination was the sin, and the second was the yielding of a portion of Ali's *wilayah* to him, so that the system of the *da'wah* might be preserved. Allah was displeased with this, and a verse came: *Wa in ashrakta layahbatanna 'amaluka*, "And if you associate partners, your work will surely become worthless." The address is to the very Prophet from whom we learned the lesson of faith. It does not refer to outward shirk. It means: if you include even an iota of anyone else in the *da'wah* of Ali, in the *wilayah*, We will nullify your work. That is the meaning. And here Allah says that all his matters have been completely forgiven.

*wa hadha qawl al-sa'il hujjatuhu awradtuhu bi-fassihi wa hay'atihi*. *wa dafa'a 'anhu al-aswa'*. The *aswa'*, the evils, are in reality the *mahdudin* and the *hudud al-da'wah*. They are what is truly meant by the evils.

*wa ballighhu kulla ma yarumu wa yahwa*, "And convey to him everything that he intends and desires." May Allah fulfill whatever you intend and whatever you desire. *wa 'alayhi min 'abdihi afdhal al-salam*, "And upon him from his servant, the best of peace." *wal-matlub min tafaddulihi jawab ma sa'altu 'anhu*, "And what is requested from his grace is the answer to what I have asked about." It is requested that, from your grace, you answer what I have asked. These are the words of the questioner. *bi-hadhb al-manni 'alayya wal-tafadduli wal-tatawwuli*, "By the favor of grace upon me, and grace and bounty," the grace that you wish to bestow upon me. *wa uqaddimu bayna yadayhi ma as'alu 'anhu ilayhi*, "And I present before him what I ask about to him," of the *wilayah* of the *hudud* of your religion. *wa al-dallina 'alayhi*, "And those who guide towards him." *wa abra'u mimman ankara addanihum*, "And I absolve myself from those who deny their lowest ones," meaning the *hudud al-din*. *wa atadharra'u ilayhi bi-wusuli 'alam al-ibda' al-kiram*, "And I supplicate to him through the connection of the noble world of creation," who are the honorable ones. *wa bi-haqqi kulli wasiyyin 'azimin wa imamin*, "And by the right of every great successor and Imam," every *wasi* and Imam. *wa bi-kulli da'iratin tadawwarat*, "And by every circle that revolved," the various circles, of Imamate and of *qa'imiyat*. *wa nuktatin sharifatin takawwanat*, "And a noble point that came into being."

*tahjibuni*, that you may not set me aside, given the weakness of my master. *fa-mathaluka man yasturu 'ala misli*, "For your example is one who covers the faults of someone like me." *wa idha tafaddalta an la tukhayyibni mimma ammaltu*, "And when you show grace, that you may not disappoint me in what I have hoped for." *bi-haqqika al-'azim la tahmalu haqqi*, "By your great right, do not neglect my right," meaning, do not abandon me by withholding an answer. *wa an tarhamani birrika wa 'ammika*, your piety, *bir*, and your beneficence, *'am*, your *sirrika*, your forgiveness, *'afw*, your help, *awn*, and your kindness, *ghawth*, in the two worlds, the *darayn*, meaning this world and the hereafter. *wal-nazar ila ma afuh bihi nafsan aw tafawwuhan*, and what I will say and what my soul is filled with, or has uttered. *la yujidu lahu ma'na*, "Which has no meaning," meaning, I am asking questions, and among them there will also come matters in which my soul is implicated, or which it has uttered. So then, *fa-albisni sawb al-sitr wal-ghina'*, "Then clothe me with the garment of concealment and contentment," the garment of *sitr* and *ghina'*: place the veils upon me. *wa umnun 'alayya bi-tatabu'i ihsanika*, "And favor me with the continuity of your benevolence." *razaqani Allahu ridaka*, "May Allah grant me the sustenance of your pleasure," your *rida*. *wa qad lakam a'udhu billahi wa bil-'ali*, "And indeed, I seek refuge in Allah and in the Exalted." *an la yadhunna al-dhannu anni ma as'alu illa mutaddi'an fasaahat lisan wa ghadharat jinan*, "That the one who thinks may not think that I am asking only as a claimant of eloquence of tongue or sharpness of mind." No, by Allah, never. *wa innama qasartu 'an al-shurbi min ra's al-ma'in*, "But rather, I fell short of drinking from the head of the spring," meaning, from the *da'i* himself, from benefiting directly from him, he was deprived. *wa qad 'alima al-hatm al-'azim al-barr al-rahim bi-aqdari 'abdihi*, and the great, pious, merciful decree knew what the *qa'id* has stated, *kaifa wa sadqa min sadaqatihi*, that whatever you bestow upon me will be a charity, a *sadaqah*, from your charities. *wa nafkhatan min nafahatihi*, "And a breath from his breaths." It will be a forgiveness from your forgiveness. *wa la akhira qurbanan illa ma qaddamtu min masturihi*, "And I do not seek any other nearness except what I have presented from his written work." It has been presented to you. That is all.

This is the introduction. The questions have not yet begun. The questioner did not frame his questions with great precision, pointing things out directly; the framing is not of that kind.
