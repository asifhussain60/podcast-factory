# The Fifth Intellect and the Verdict on the Caliphs

And Maulana Ismail was seen in the veil of his father, and it became a mutual exchange. *Fi sirrin jalilin*, in a great secret. *Wa lam yaqif 'ala ahl al-haqa'iq tilka al-asrar*, the people of realities were not unaware of those secrets. *Wa kullu shay'in 'indahu... 'inda rabbika bi-miqdar*, and everything with your Lord is in a decreed measure. *Wa qawluhu fi hadha al-du'a' al-madhkur fi sha'n Musa al-mashkur*, and this supplication is in the station of the thankful Musa, that is, the supplication of Maulana Zayd. *Bi-quwwat al-da'irah al-rabi'ah*, by means of the power of the fourth circle, *al-mutasha'shi'ah anwaruha al-sati'ah*, whose brilliant and radiant lights spread all around. This is because the honorable Musa was the fourth among the *nutuqa'*, and the fourth is granted a special power. Among the Imams, and among the *nutuqa'*, the one who is second and the one who is fourth is granted a particular power. Thus among the Imams, Imam Husayn ibn Ali was granted such power that he waged the greatest *jihad*. And the fourth Imam, the honorable Baqir (ع), was granted that power by virtue of which he caused all the sayings of the honorable Prophet to be made manifest, explaining their interpretations and inner meanings. "O Baqir, split open knowledge," is what was said to him. "O Muhammad, split open knowledge." Because of this he became known as Baqir. He conveyed this message to Jabir ibn 'Abdullah al-Ansari, the Prophet having said to him: my son's son, when you meet him, convey my peace to him. And conveying peace means conveying the *ta'yid* and *mawaddah* that one carries; the bearer is the medium for it, and delivers it to them. Those were the circumstances.

Sayyidna Jabir ibn 'Abdullah al-Ansari was an elder of the very highest rank. He became a companion of the honorable Prophet and held great love for the honorable Ali. His joining was with the honorable Imam Baqir, and he lived an extraordinarily long life. On one occasion the Prophet said to him, "Go to that place." Jabir submitted, "O Mawla, I do not know that place; how should I go?" So the Prophet gestured, pointing his finger, and from that finger a light emerged. He said, "Follow this light. Wherever it is visible to you, go that way." By the guidance of that light Jabir set out on his journey, which was very far. He reached that place and accomplished what he had been sent to do, delivering the Rasul's message and completing his task. Afterward a thought came to him: in the past, when he had once lost his way and had valuable goods with him, he had buried those goods beneath a bush somewhere nearby. He would see whether that place was still there and whether the goods remained. He went and found the place, dug the ground, and found the goods still there. He took them with him, brought them home, and then came before the Prophet. The Prophet said, "I sent you for one task, and you have returned having done two." Such was the honorable Jabir ibn 'Abdullah al-Ansari. His grave is in Alexandria, and a visitation was made there as well. He passed away in the time of the honorable Maulana Baqir. A very long life was his, and he is visited to this day.

As for his son, 'Abdullah ibn Jabir, he is in Iraq, in the mausoleum of Maulana Salman Farsi. Two are buried there. Maulana Tahir is also there, Tahir who was the son of Maulana Baqir and the brother of Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq. He too is there, in Salman Pak, alongside Maulana Hudhayfah al-Yamani.

Hudhayfah al-Yamani holds a station of great significance. He was a companion of the Master and was present when, after the day of Ghadir, after the eighteenth, the day of Ghadir, Rasulullah undertook the journey toward Madinah. The hypocrites, the *munafiqin*, were burning with rage over the appointment of the honorable Ali, and they resolved to eliminate the Master himself. They formed a group and made their plan. It happened to be night, and as they were traveling through a valley the conspirators had drawn their daggers in accordance with their plan. But by the power of God, Allah commanded the lightning to flash, and it flashed so continuously and so brightly that the Master was able to see every one of their faces. Hudhayfah al-Yamani was with the honorable Prophet at that moment. The Prophet saw all their faces and said, "O Hudhayfah, did you see what I saw?" Hudhayfah replied, "O Rasulullah, what you saw, I saw." The Prophet said, "Keep this a secret; do not reveal it." And so he revealed it to no one. But the honorable 'Umar ibn al-Khattab would constantly ask Hudhayfah, "Was I among that group that intended to kill the Prophet?" And Hudhayfah would remain silent, until finally 'Umar himself said, *Wallahi, ana fihim*, "By God, I was among them." The question stands on its own: people of that kind, who conspired to kill the Prophet, are they worthy of becoming caliph?

There is a treatise by Nasir al-Din al-Tusi that is worth reading. The manner in which he has dismantled the caliphs, and then Mu'awiyah and Yazid after them, makes it essential reading. It is a short treatise, fifteen to twenty pages, but the titles he applies to Abu Bakr are such that they make one's hair stand on end. Likewise for 'Umar and for 'Uthman.

As for whether Abu Bakr is counted among the *da'is*, it is said that he was in the *da'wah*. The reality with Abu Bakr is that from ancient times, from the earlier *da'wah*, he had been a participant in the *da'wah* of Abu Talib. He continued in that *da'wah* and harbored a wish that he himself might be the awaited Prophet. But when the Prophet (ع) received prophethood and proclaimed the call of Islam, Abu Bakr accepted him. Yet hypocrisy remained in his heart, and its expressions surfaced many times. One expression was that the Master did not invite him along on the migration from Makkah to Madinah; the Prophet had intended to make that journey alone. But Abu Bakr came along under the pretext of buying camels. He had come to know of the plan because the Master, though he had kept the matter of the migration secret, needed a fast camel and asked Abu Bakr for one. Abu Bakr agreed, but charged the Rasulullah ten times the going rate for the camel.

There are various such incidents. On one occasion Rasulullah was standing in the market and had struck a deal with a camel seller, someone who had come from another city. The Prophet paid the agreed price and took the reins, saying, "Hand it over." The man said, "I will not hand it over. This camel is mine." A strange situation had arisen: the man had received the money but would not give up the camel. At this point, according to the narrations, Abu Bakr and 'Umar were passing by. They asked, "O Rasulullah, what is the matter?" The Prophet explained: "This man came to sell a camel. I liked it, I paid the price, and now he will not hand it over." Abu Bakr and 'Umar then asked the Rasul, "Are you quite sure you paid the price?" And at that the Prophet fell silent. By coincidence the honorable Ali also passed by and asked what was happening. The Master explained the same thing to Ali. Without another word Ali seized the man by the collar and said, "Return this camel." Afterward the Master asked Ali, "Why did you not do as the two Shaykhs did, and treat me the way they treated me?" Ali replied, "O Rasulullah, we have believed in you without seeing. We have not seen paradise, we have not seen hell, we have not seen the angels, we have not seen the hereafter, yet we have believed in all of it solely upon your word. Would we then not believe you in so small a matter as this?"

There are many such incidents. There is, for example, the incident of the cave. A great deal is made of the fact that the honorable Rasul was with Abu Bakr in the cave and that Abu Bakr cradled the Prophet on his lap, and all manner of things are said that do not hold up. The Qur'anic verse in Surah al-Tawbah says *idh huma fi al-ghar*, that they both were in the cave. This is acknowledged by Allah. And then: *fa-anzalallahu sakinatahu 'alayh*, Allah sent down His tranquility upon him. There were two people there, and tranquility descended upon one. No Muslim would dare to say that the tranquility, that special form of divine assistance, descended upon Abu Bakr while the honorable Prophet was left restless and helpless. Tranquility, then, descended upon the Rasul.

One might object that the Rasul is the Rasul and Abu Bakr is Abu Bakr, and that they are different. But elsewhere, in Surah al-Fath and indeed in Surah al-Tawbah itself, Allah says in another verse, *anzalallahu sakinatahu 'alayhi wa 'ala al-mu'minin*, Allah sent down His tranquility upon him and upon the believers. So tranquility descends upon the believers as well. If Abu Bakr had been among the believers, tranquility would have descended upon him too. A *da'i* by the name of Shahriyar has demonstrated this conclusively: since tranquility descends upon the believers, as another verse makes plain, and no tranquility descended upon Abu Bakr in the cave, he was not even within the circle of faith. How, then, could such a person become caliph?

And then, when Rasulullah passed away, a gathering was convened at Saqifah Bani Sa'idah while the body of the Rasul lay unburied and without shroud. As Shaykh Sa'di writes in his couplet: the dogs of the world, in their greed for worldly power, left the Prophet without his due.

There is also this point: as long as Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr was still in his father's loins, certain things were as they were. As for Abu Bakr's other son, 'Abd al-Rahman, according to the narrations both were absent in those moments. The narration specifically concerns Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr.

There is also the matter of the gap between the event of Ghadir and the Prophet's passing, two, three, perhaps four months, during which period history records that the Prophet dispatched Usamah ibn Zayd on a military expedition, and these very men were placed under his command. They objected to being sent under his leadership, even though Usamah, the son of Harithah, was young, some saying seventeen, though the correct figure is twenty-eight and a half years old. They did not go. And Rasulullah, in his illness, kept asking by name: "Has Abu Bakr gone? Has 'Uthman gone? Has 'Umar ibn al-Khattab gone?"

And the oath of allegiance was taken at a place called the Saqifah Bani Sa'idah, a place where thieves and robbers, after looting goods, would gather to divide their spoils among themselves. At that place they decided the matter of the caliphate. While the discussion was still underway, 'Umar ibn al-Khattab immediately came forward and gave his oath of allegiance, declaring, "Whoever does not give the oath, my sword is on his head." This is how the oath was taken. God forbid. Today that place has been turned into a public bathhouse.

It has been said that if this was a democracy, then in all three successions the principle of democracy was destroyed, in one case through nomination, in another through a council of six. It was all done in enmity toward Ali. As for the *da'is* of high rank, they were not unaware of these matters. The ones the Prophet spoke of were indeed aware of all the sciences, Abu Bakr especially. But the greed of the world and the lust for rank led them to do all of this.

What happened to Muhsin bin Ali bears reflection. He was so learned, so well read, that words fail to describe it. In the time of Sayyidna Husayn ibn Hasan, Sayyid 'Abdullah ibn Muhammad had been appointed, and Muhsin bin Ali stood behind Sayyidna Husayn ibn Hasan in prayer. Sayyid 'Abdullah ibn Muhammad would stand in such a way that he would draw Muhsin close, half the space for himself and half for Muhsin. Such was their closeness. Sayyid 'Abdullah ibn Muhammad had such great friendship with Muhsin bin Ali that he would always seat him at his side.

On Fridays Sayyid Muhammad al-Jamali would come, for he came only on Fridays, praying in his own mosques the rest of the time. Being senior, he would sit to the left of Sayyid Husayn bin Ismail, behind the Imam.

That was the time of Sayyidna Husayn. On a later occasion Muhsin was sitting behind him, and after Muhsin was Sayyid 'Abdullah. Once, when the summers began, the prayer was held in the courtyard. Sayyid Husayn bin Ismail led the Maghrib prayer. Then, in the interval between the two evening prayers, he sat awhile, rose, and went into the prayer niche, and the congregation followed. He called Sayyid 'Abdullah Muhammad and said something to him, to which Sayyid 'Abdullah replied, "You pray your prayer, I will pray mine." At that point Muhsin led the prayer.

The affairs deteriorated considerably after that. Yet such was the friendship between Sayyid 'Abdullah Muhammad and Muhsin. When the clarification came, and Sayyid sahib forgave Sayyid 'Abdullah Muhammad after testing him and Sayyid Ahmad al-Jamali for many years, he performed the *nas* (appointment) and forgave him. So when Sayyid 'Abdullah ibn Muhammad became a *da'i*, Muhsin came to him on the third day. He did not offer the full *musafahah* (handshake of recognition); he merely shook hands and said, "You and I were at the same station." Sayyid 'Abdullah ibn Muhammad replied, "Yes, it is correct that you and I were at the same station. But from my station I have built a building. And you, you have remained on that same station." That is, you stayed where you were, while from my station I have built a great edifice of faith.

Abu Bakr was not among the confidants of the era of 'Isa. They are other individuals, outside the list of the righteous. Among the six names there are also common names. The teachers of the Rasul, and among them the confidants of the era of 'Isa, the six confidants who existed between the Rasul and 'Isa, were those who gathered the precious treasures of the teachings of 'Isa. Their joining occurred with the Rasul. Although they were the confidants of the era of 'Isa, they are also among the chosen ones of the *dawr* and hold a great station.

*Fa-hwa yushiru 'ala anna kulla 'aqlin min al-'uqul al-sab' al-'izam yanzuru ila man qabalahu min al-rutab al-nutuqa' al-sab'a fi al-sarb wa al-nizam*, which indicates that every intellect from the seven great intellects looks toward the one opposite it from the seven ranks of the *nutuqa'*, in their series and order. As for the seven intellects: setting aside the First Intellect and the tenth, eight remain in between. But in that count the first emanated one, the one who became unique, is also excluded. So after the first emanated one, and before the tenth, there are seven intellects. Each of those seven intellects gives material support to the one opposite it among the *nutuqa'*. The third intellect gives support to the first among the *nutuqa'*, that is, Adam (ع). The fourth intellect, the eternal one, gives support to the honorable Nuh. And so it proceeds.

*Wa amma qawl al-natiq li-Kalilah, thumma istaqbili al-'aql al-khamis, dhu al-fadl wa al-fadilah, sahib al-nur al-a'zam al-anwar, mufid al-khayrat wa mu'ti al-fada'il, ila an qala 'alayhissalam wa al-salam minhu zaharat al-qudar al-Fatimiyyah*, which is the saying of the Natiq to Kalilah: "Then come forward to the fifth intellect, the possessor of grace and virtue, the possessor of the greatest and most luminous light, the bestower of good things and the giver of virtues," until he said (ع): "from him the Fatimi powers appeared."

*Fa-l-'aql al-khamis 'arrafna fi hadha al-wajh wa al-ma'na huwa al-Husayn ibn 'Ali Amir al-Mu'minin, al-khamis min al-Nabi*, that is, the fifth intellect, understood in this aspect and meaning, is al-Husayn ibn 'Ali Amir al-Mu'minin, the fifth from the Prophet. Within the *Panjatan-i Pak* (the Holy Five), the honorable Prophet, the honorable Ali, the honorable Fatimah al-Zahra, Hasan, and Husayn, Husayn was the fifth. It is a reference to this.

*Wa al-qudar al-Fatimiyyah, hum al-a'immah min awladihi*, and the Fatimi powers are the *a'immah* from his progeny, that is, from the progeny of the honorable Imam Husayn. They are called the Fatimi powers.

*Wa awlad Maulana Fatim al-huda*, the children of our master Fatim, who guides.

*Wa minhum ashraqat badayi' al-suwar al-musawwarat*, and from them, meaning from the *a'immah*, new and wondrous forms shone forth, and their advent radiated light.

*Wa bihim taharrakat al-mutaharrikat*, and by them the movers moved. These are all spiritual matters and joinings to which reference is made here.

This passage is found in *Kanz al-'Ulum*, the book written by Sayyidna Ibrahim ibn Muhammad al-Fad. There is also *Kanz al-Walad*, which is a different work. *Kanz al-Walad* was written by Sayyidna Ibrahim ibn Husayn al-Hamidi, the second *da'i* after the first *da'i* Sayyidna Dhu'ayb, and he wrote it for his son Sayyidna Hatim, to instruct a son who reaches the station of a *da'i* in the things he must know. The *Kanz al-'Ulum*, however, was written by Sayyidna Ibrahim ibn Muhammad al-Fad, who explained therein that the *a'immah* who appeared in succession from the progeny of the honorable Husayn ibn Ali are in one sense also called *A'immah Fatimiyyin*, because they stand in awe and reverence of the station of the honorable Bibi Fatimah al-Zahra.

It is also said that the honorable Rasulullah, whenever the honorable Fatimah would come to him, would often rise and seat her in his own place. The reason was the gathering she carried with her, a gathering of such station that when the Master of the Universe perceived it, he would rise out of joy and to honor that gathering. And that gathering was the gathering of the *muhtajibin* of the *awsiya'*, a most magnificent gathering. The honorable Fatimah had assembled the *muhtajibin* of all the *awsiya'*, which caused the station of the *hijab* to increase. The states that belonged to Fatimah bint Muhammad were the states of the *hijab*, and when the station of the *muhtajib* increases, the station of the *hijab* increases accordingly.

Wherever the footsteps of the Rasul fall, there the footsteps of Fatimah will fall. The *khilafah* was established. And then the *du'at* stand in awe and reverence regarding who should be appointed in the station of the *'ayn*: Bibi Fatimah al-Zahra or Ibrahim Khalilullah. Among them, who is the successor in the *khilafah* of the *'ayn* after the Rasul? They have not clarified this matter. One of the *da'in*, Sayyidna 'Abdullah al-'Ali, has written a book in which, as the brother Mukarram Saifullah sahib had transcribed, he clarified that the successions of all the Imams until the Day of Judgment have been explained: who will become the successor of the *'ayn-i 'azimah* after whom. A most magnificent book, in which every single point has been made clear.

*Wa bihim tajalla al-'ayn al-'azimah, 'ilmuhu, 'ulumuhu, wa biyutuhu, wa maqamatuhu al-fahimah*, and through them, through the Imams of purity, the Great *'Ayn* manifested: His knowledge, His sciences, His houses, and His exalted stations. The honorable Ali became the *muhtajib* for all the Imams, and they, the Imams, are His *hijab*. Such is the state, and such is the station of the Master of the Universe.

This matter, the station of the Master of the Universe and the station of the Imams, has been clarified in Surah al-Fatihah in the following way. Sayyidna Hasan Khan bin 'Abd al-Malik observes that Allah began His word, after Bismillah, with *Al-hamdu lillahi rabb il-'alamin*, All praise is due to Allah, the Lord of the worlds. *Al-hamd* is the *ma'rifah* (definite) form; its *nakirah* (indefinite) form is *hamd*. The word *hamd* is composed of the letters *ha*, *mim*, and *dal*. Their numerical values are: *ha* is eight, *mim* is forty, and *dal* is four, giving a total of fifty-two. Fifty-two signifies the three, Muhammad, Ali, and Fatimah, together with the forty-nine Imams who extend to Abu al-A'immah. After Abu al-A'immah, the era of manifestation (*dawr-i kashf*) begins: the Mahdi of the end of time will come, and the manifestation will occur. The honorable Muhammad, (ع), the honorable Ali, and the honorable Fatimah al-Zahra, these three, together with the forty-nine Imams, make fifty-two. It is as though in reciting *Al-hamdu lillahi rabb il-'alamin* they are recognized and affirmed.

*Wa-'bud rabbaka hatta ya'tiyak al-yaqin*, and worship your Lord until there comes to you the certainty. Where the word *yaqin* (certainty) is used, it also carries a reference to Amir al-Mu'minin. In the literal sense, *yaqin* means death: worship Allah until your death. But it also means until you meet Ali, for after passing away the meeting will be with Ali, and he will ask questions.

As it is in the Quran, in Surah al-Zumar, the thirty-ninth surah, when the group is taken toward the heavens they will be stopped and questioned. The commentators, both Sunni and Shia, hold that this question is the question of the love and *walayah* of Ali. That is established by proof. In one's life, did one hold the correct *walayah* for Ali or not? That is the question that will be asked.

As the Master has said: if a person spends his entire life in worship in the blessed gap between the Rukn al-Asad and Maqam Ibrahim, such that his very joints become loose from the exertion, yet has no love for Ali in his heart, then Allah will cast him into hellfire headlong. For the essence of the matter is this: once, Rasulullah asked his companions, "What is the essence of faith?" Someone said prayer. The Rasul said, "No, prayer is not the essence of faith." Another said fasting, another said Hajj, another said jihad. For each the Rasul said, "Not this." Then the Rasul declared, "It is *walayah* and love," meaning the *walayah* for Ali is the essence of faith.
