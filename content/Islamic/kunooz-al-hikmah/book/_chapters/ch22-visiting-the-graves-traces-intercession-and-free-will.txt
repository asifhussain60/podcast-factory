# Visiting the Graves: Traces, Intercession, and Free Will

*Al-su'al al-tasi' 'an ziyarat al-qubur*, the ninth question, concerns the visitation performed at graves: *kayfa dhalika*, how is that, and *wa ma yahduru'l-qasidina li'l-ajsman*, what attends those who visit the bodies? When visitors present themselves at the graves of those buried therein, *kayfa shu'uru'l-maqsud*, how is the feeling of the one intended? Do the people whose graves are being visited perceive that a visitor has come to them?

The question also concerns Sayyidna 'Abdullah ibn Thamir. He was a very high-ranking elder, a *mubashshir al-nabi*, one who gave glad tidings of the prophet. He lived before the Prophet ('ع), his birth and his death both preceding the Prophet, and he gave glad tidings of the coming of the Huzur. He died in Najran, at the place of *Ukhdud*, which is mentioned in Surah al-Buruj. At that place his death occurred. In that era his grave was seen, and it was left in a very poor condition, merely a mound of earth with no care given to it. Later, in pictures, it appeared beautifully restored. When journeys for *da'wat*s were made in the time of Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan and the road passed through *Ukhdud*, Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan would always recite an *iradah*, a devotional prayer, for him. He would recite a du'a, recite an *iradah*.

So:

> *wa qad ṣāra lahu hādhihi'l-muddah al-madīdah*

And for him this long period has passed. A long period has passed, the questioner says. The questioner adds that he has begun asking many questions and has gone beyond his means, but that his state is such that he is deeply troubled, and so he continues asking. And the one addressed is from the people of favor and bounty, *ahl al-faḍl wa'l-nawāl*, people of generosity and givers of gifts.

### The Answer

*Al-jawāb 'alā dhālika's-su'āl*, the answer to that question.

> *naqūlu bi-'awni'llāhi'l-Kabīri'l-Muta'āl*

We say with the help of Allah, the Great, the Most High.

> *Wa mannat Waliyyi'l-'Aṣri wa'z-Zamān, 'alayhi ṣalawātu'r-Raḥīmi'r-Raḥmān*

And by the grace of the Master of the Era and the Time, upon him be the blessings of the Merciful, the Compassionate.

> *Wa tā'īdi Mawlāyī al-'Alam*

And by the aid of my Master, the distinguished one, Mawlana Sayyidna Hibatullah.

> *Wa sirruhu'l-bāhir bi-nūri'n-naẓm, anna ziyārat al-qubūr sirrun mastūr, wa huwa'l-muttaṣil bi-dhālika'l-ḥaddi 'ala'l-ghurūr*

And his manifest secret with the light of the order, that the visitation of graves is a concealed secret, and it is connected to that rank over deception.

The concealed secret of the visitation of graves is that the one who visits is receiving emanations, *fuḍūl*, from the rank of the one whose grave is being visited, a high rank indeed.

> *Li-yashfa'a lahu 'inda jāmi'i'ṣ-ṣuwar*

So that he may intercede for him at the gathering of the forms.

The one visited intercedes for the visitor in those gatherings of *ṣuwar* where he is present. The higher the rank, the greater their spiritual powers. In life, the *hudūd* of the Dawah were angels *bi'l-quwwah*, in potential, but after death they become angels *bi'l-fi'l*, in actuality. It has also been stated elsewhere that when a visitor visits, the one visited senses the visit. And as has been said here, the luminous form, *ṣūrat nayyirah*, of the one being visited is always turned towards its *fuḍalā'*, its spiritual particle. Just as a person, wherever he may be sitting, keeps his thoughts on his home and the things within it, the mind remaining with what is most precious even at a distance, so too the one who has passed away keeps his luminous form oriented towards what is most precious to him: his *fuḍalā'*, which is advancing. Such a one desires that this *fuḍalā'* should progress as swiftly as possible and ascend into the *aflāk*. Therefore, through the luminous form, which resides within the *hiyākil*, the spiritual structures, or accompanies the *hudūd 'āliyah*, the higher ranks, on its way toward the *Bāb al-Abwāb*, the Gate of Gates, an *ishrāq*, an illumination, is continuously emanating. When a believer enters the circle of that *ishrāq*, he too receives benefit, and the one visited intercedes for him.

As the Master of the Universe stated: when children visit the graves of their parents, the parents sense their arrival and are as joyful as they were when their children were small and would come and sit in their laps. That same joy is felt by the mother and father. This is the saying of the Rasul, that parents become extremely happy upon the visitation of their children and feel pleasure. And when the children pray for them, recite Yasin, or perform *naẓr* and *niyāz*, the parents perceive all of this. This connects to the verse in Surah Bani Isra'il:

> *Rabbirḥamhumā kamā rabbayānī ṣaghīrā*

My Lord, have mercy upon them as they brought me up when I was small.

People often mispronounce it as *rabbiyānī*, but the correct form is *rabbayānī*. The sense is: O Allah, have mercy on them just as they showed mercy to me when I was small. This *du'ā* should be recited both during their lifetimes and after their deaths. Mercy is not reserved for after death alone. When parents are still living, children should pray for them with this Quranic supplication: O Allah, have mercy on them as they had mercy on me in my childhood.

If the visitor is of a higher rank, then the matter is reversed. The aid and help flow from the higher visitor to the lower one, just as water always flows from a higher surface to a lower one.

The grave of a great elder, a prophet: for them there are holy traces. Their *fuḍalā'* is no longer there. But there are traces, and a trace would still signify something.

Yes, traces remain. Even if there is no *fuḍalā'*, the *āthār* endure. And it is on account of the *āthār* that the blessings of the grave persist. Just as a bottle of *'iṭr*, perfume, after the perfume has been emptied out, still retains the fragrance, in the same way the analogy is given that even after the *fuḍalā'* has progressed onward, its *āthār* remain at the grave. Indeed, the very site of the grave will become the place of its future *futteh*.

Yes, yes.

### Intercession and the Means

> *Wa li-yashfa'a lahu 'inda majāmi'i'ṣ-ṣuwar, wa li-yakshifa 'anhu mā 'arāhu min funūni'l-maṣā'ib*

So that he may intercede for him at the gathering of the forms, and so that he may remove from him the various types of calamities that have afflicted him.

To that extent the various calamities befalling the visitor are removed by the one visited. This connects to the practice of going to the graves of the *awliyā' Allāh*, the saints of Allah, and saying: you are the means, the *wasīlah*, through which Allah Ta'ala may hear our prayers and help us, for Allah alone is the one who helps, but you have become the intermediary. This *wasīlah* is itself a profound matter, as it appears in the Quran:

> *Wa'btaghū ilayhi'l-wasīlah*

And seek the means of approach to Him.

Generally, the misguided among the common people, *'awām al-nās*, misinterpret this: they claim that it means to reach Allah through the Quran. But this is not its meaning. It means to reach Allah through the means, that is, through the Rasul and the Ahl al-Bayt, or through their followers who are the *du'āt*, the *ḥujjat*, and the *imām*. Through them one reaches Allah. For the *ma'rifat*, the gnosis, of the Worshipped, the *ma'būd*, is the *ma'rifat* of the *hudūd*, the ranks of the Dawah. The *ma'rifat* of the Worshipped, of Allah, is contained within the *ma'rifat* of the *hudūd*.

> *Wa mā 'alima bihi min jamī'i'l-ma'āqib*

And what he knew of all the consequences.

That is, whatever affliction is befalling the visitor from various circumstances.

> *li-kawnihi yushrifu 'alā fuḍalatihi*

Because he is overseeing his *fuḍalā'*.

The one whose grave is being visited has his *ṣūrat sharīf* exerting *ishrāq* upon his *fuḍalā'*.

> *Wa yaḥummu 'alayhā*

And he cares for it. He is deeply solicitous toward his *fuḍalā'*.

> *Wa yudabbiruhā*

And he manages it. He is managing it, directing how to advance the *fuḍalā'* as swiftly as possible.

> *Wa yaḥḍuru'l-qāṣidīna ilayhā*

And the visitors attend to it. The visitors present themselves in relation to this *fuḍalā'*.

These blessings are then obtained by the visitors, because the *ishrāq* of the visited is directed toward his *fuḍalā'*, and within that circle the visitors arrive and benefit.

### The Degrees of Illumination

It is also the case that the lowest *ishrāq* occurring, beyond which higher *ishrāq*s also operate, is the *ishrāq* of a believer's own *ṣūrat sharīf* upon his body. Although his *ṣūrat sharīf* is not yet fully formed, remaining at the stage of *nafs nāmīyah*, the vegetative soul, *nafs ḥissīyah*, the sensory soul, and *nafs nāṭiqah*, the rational soul, nonetheless, because the *nafs nāṭiqah* is a *ṣūrat sharīf* *bi'l-quwwah*, in potential, its *ishrāq* is already operating upon the body. This is the lowest *ishrāq*. Above it come the higher *ishrāq*s of the Imam and others.

The believer has been placed within Surat al-Fatiha. Surat al-Fatiha has seven verses. But its words, *kalimāt*, are twenty-five. And those twenty-five words correspond to the twenty-five prophets mentioned in the glorious Quran. Although there are 124,000 prophets, Allah Ta'ala has mentioned only twenty-five in the Quran, and Surat al-Fatiha likewise contains exactly twenty-five words. These twenty-five words indicate twelve *hudūd* of the *jazā'ir*, the islands, and twelve *hudūd* of the *ḥaḍarāt*, the presences: that is, the *du'āt* of the *jazā'ir*, who are twelve, and the *du'āt* of the *ḥaḍarāt*, who are twelve. And their leader, the head of these twenty-four, is the Imam of the Time, making twenty-five in all.

The twelve *hudūd* of the *jazā'ir*, the *du'āt* of the *jazā'ir*, each carry out their *da'wat* at their own places, in their own languages, individually. The *du'āt* of the *ḥaḍarāt*, however, operate where the manifestation of the Imam of the Time is present, and so their *da'wat* is a collective one, not individual. This is why, in the *tahajjud* prayer, two-*rak'at* sets are offered, six *salām*s in all, because the *du'āt* of the *jazā'ir* are connected in pairs: each *dā'ī* has a companion. But the *du'āt* of the *ḥaḍarāt* carry out the *da'wat* collectively, and their example is reflected in *ṣalāt al-layl*, whose twelve *rak'at*s are prayed consecutively, all at once. And after the *ṣalāt al-layl* there is a *du'ā*.

There is, there is.

Yes, a very long *du'ā*.

Yes, the *du'ā* is very long.

After no other prayer is there such a *du'ā*.

*Ṣalāt al-layl* is the final prayer before *shaf'a*, and it indicates the *du'āt* of the *ḥaḍarāt*. They are of very high rank. For it is from these very *du'āt*, from their *fuḍalā'* and their *riyāḍ*, that the *Bāb al-Salsal* and the *Bāb al-Anbiyā'* are formed. These are people of the highest rank.

### Conscience and Choice

Conscience, *zamīr*. My conscience does not allow it. I feel that my conscience is speaking to me. That is the guidance of the *nāṭiqah*. The *nāṭiqah* always seeks to keep its *nāmīyah* and *ḥissīyah* with it, and strives toward that end. The stronger the *nāṭiqah*, the more it exerts *ishrāq* upon its *nāmīyah*, its *ḥissīyah*, and upon the body, seeking to elevate them. This is why some *hudūd* of the Dawah have been such that not only the *nāmīyah*, *ḥissīyah*, and *nāṭiqah* join together after death to form the *ṣūrat sharīf*, but even their internal organs and lower bodily parts ascend with them, on account of their nobility and the life of piety and purity they lived.

But conscience: everyone has a conscience. Yet everyone has taken an oath. Some are bound by it, some are not. So consciously, a man says to his conscience: "No, I am going to do this wrong thing." He performs the act, whether right or wrong, with full intention. It is not that merely being from the Ahl al-Silsilah guarantees one walks the straight path. Free will, *ikhtiyār*, exists.

Free will exists. And this free will is a very great thing that nature has given to man alone. Angels have not been given free will, for angels cannot sin, as the Quranic verse states:

> *walā ya'ṣūn*

And they do not disobey.

They do not sin. And Jannah and Dozakh, paradise and hell, have been made for humans alone. Among Allah's creations there are many: the moon, the sun, the *aflāk*, the earth, animals, minerals, and countless other things. But Jannah and Dozakh are for humans alone, because man is the only creature who has the choice, *ikhtiyār*, to do good and to do evil. Goodness is clear to him, and evil is clear to him; yet knowing goodness, he may incline toward evil, and knowing evil, he may incline toward goodness. He has complete freedom of choice. And because of this choice, if he does good, he draws near to Jannah, and if he does evil, he draws near to Dozakh. This condition applies to man alone, not to others.

For the one endowed with *nāṭiqah*, with *juzur*.

Yes. Among all of Allah's creation, it is man alone for whom Jannah and Dozakh have been created. And there is a *du'ā* whose author urges that one should ask for Jannah five times daily, and five times seek refuge from Dozakh:

> *Allāhumma innī as'aluka'l-jannah*

O Allah, I ask You for paradise.

> *As'alu'llāha'l-jannah*

I ask Allah for paradise.

> *Wa a'ūdhu bi'llāhi min al-nār*

And I seek refuge in Allah from the Fire, asking Allah for protection from Dozakh. It has been prescribed to do this five times.

A slightly different tone is used for the *du'ā* after the *adhān*. *Rabbanā ātinā* is in its own place. But here the asking is for Allah to grant Jannah, and the tone is different. That *du'ā* is in its own place; it is, in fact, the *du'ā* of Amir al-Mu'minin, what is recited after the *adhān*. Five times: some recite it five times, at different times. And this one, five times together: five times to ask, and five times to seek refuge.

The *du'ā* after the *adhān* is apt because the time between the *adhān* and the *iqāmah* is one of the five times when *du'ā*s are accepted. During that interval, supplications are answered. So the prayer is offered, as Amir al-Mu'minin prescribed:

> *Allāhumma innī as'aluka'l-jannah... wa an tuzawwajanī min al-ḥūri'l-'īn*

O Allah, I ask You for paradise, and that You unite me with the maidens of paradise.

And who are the *ḥūr 'īn*? They are in truth the *hudūd 'āliyah*. The prayer is that unification, union, may be granted with the *hudūd 'āliyah*.

### The Traces That Remain

> *Wa 'an Sayyidna 'Abdullah ibn Thāmir*

And concerning Sayyidna 'Abdullah ibn Thamir, the question that has been asked.

> *Wa annahu lahu hādhihi'l-muddah*

And that for him has been this duration. A period has passed for him.

> *Wa anna qad zāda 'ala'l-alfi fi'l-'iddah*

And that it has exceeded a thousand in count. A period of more than a thousand years has passed.

> *Wa in kāna dhālika wa qad ṣa'ida'l-fuḍalā'*

And if that is so, the *fuḍalā'* has indeed ascended.

His *fuḍalā'* has progressed after a thousand years, all of it, *jamī'uhā*: its flesh, *laḥmuhā*, its bones, *'aẓmuhā*, and its nerves, *'aṣabuhā*, all have progressed.

> *Fa-'āda'l-āthār*

So the traces returned. Those *āthār*, those traces, remain in that grave.

> *Wa't-turbatu'sh-sharīfah allatī'jtajanat bihā*

And the noble earth in which it was enveloped. The noble soil in which their *fuḍalā'* had been mixed remains.

> *Wa ishrāqu'ṣ-ṣuwar fī tilka'l-amākini'ṭ-ṭahārah*

And the illumination of the forms in those places of purity.

The *ishrāq* that is occurring moves toward the *fuḍalā'*, toward its *āthār*, and toward that soil in which the body was hidden. In that soil too, nobility was created.

> *Wa alā qadri'z-zā'ir wa'l-mazūr tuqābiluhu'z-zubadu'ẓ-ẓāhirah*

And according to the rank of the visitor and the visited, the manifest quintessences meet him.

According to the rank of the visitor and the one visited, they are met by the *zubad*.

### The Twenty-Eight Stages

What are the *zubad*? The *fuḍalā'*, after progressing, must travel to Baytullah. The great grave, as distinguished from the small grave, is Baytullah; the small grave is the one in which a person is placed after death. To journey to Baytullah, there are twenty-eight stages, *manāzil*, to cross. From the place where a person has passed away, the *fuḍalā'* moves gradually, coming first to the spot in the masjid where he used to pray. One who prays habitually makes a particular place his own; that place becomes the gathering-point for his *fuḍalā'*. From there it progresses to his *miḥrāb*, the prayer niche, and from the *miḥrāb* it advances to the shrine of Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan.

The progression of the *fuḍalā'* continues in this manner. From Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan's shrine it departs and moves onward through various noble places, *amākin sharīfah*, until it reaches the *mawāqīt*, the appointed stations, the five stations from which pilgrims bind their intention for *umrah*. It is said that *fuḍalāt* and *riyāḍ* gather at those stations. Progressing further, from Anderganj Charkhan it arrives at the precincts of Baytullah, its *fuḍalā'* reaching the various *rukn*s, the corners, and ultimately arriving at the *rukn al-aswad*, the Black Stone corner, of Baytullah. This process is ongoing and unceasing. From there it ascends.

The state of ascension is called *zubad*. While it rises, it remains in the state of *fuḍalā'*. But when the *fuḍalā'* arrives at the pillars of Baytullah, it is called *zakhā'ir*, treasures. And when it ascends to the *aflāk*, the celestial spheres, it is called *zubad*. The *zubad* is a very exalted state of being.

The *zubad sharīfah* thus meet both the visitor and the one whose grave is visited, and they aid them as well.

> *Wa aydan fa-inna kullu qabrin ḍamma hādhihi'l-ḥudūd*

And also, that every grave that contains these *hudūd*. Every grave is connected to these *hudūd mu'aẓẓamah*.

> *Fa-taḥtahu sirrun 'aẓīm min al-khamā'iri'l-kāminah*

For beneath it is a great secret from the latent leavens.

> *Fī tilka'l-biqā'i'l-mukarramah*

In those honored spots, their noble places.

> *Fa-li-dhālika wajabat ziyāratuhā*

Therefore, their visitation became obligatory.

Because of the hidden leavens present there, because the *ṣuwar* are casting their *ishrāq* upon the grave, because things have progressed while *āthār* remain, for all these reasons visitation becomes obligatory. These places are exceedingly pure, and they benefit the believer and the visitor alike.

### Amanullah Saheb

A story of this kind occurred in the time of Wali al-Muqaddas. A man named Amanullah Saheb, who served as a *Mullā Adīb* in the village of Taliwala, contracted cancer in his final days. The cancer was in his jaw, and his condition was grave. He was seen by various doctors, yet the cancer continued to worsen. He was a man of firm faith with deep trust in Huzur. Taliwala lies very close to Khankhah Sharif; it is the nearest village. At Khankhah Sharif there are two of our elders: Sayyidi Yusuf bin Ali Khan and Sayyidi Sharif Muhammad Saheb Sindhi. Amanullah Saheb would go there and sit as if in *i'tikāf*, in seclusion. He would take the soil of that grave and apply it to the afflicted place. This became his daily routine, and he maintained it for months. When he later returned to the doctors, a disease like cancer had vanished, and he was completely well.

This too is a condition that obtains: these masters of the graves, who lived noble lives, were *bi'l-quwwah*, in potentiality, during their lives and have now become *bi'l-fi'l*, in actuality. By virtue of becoming *bi'l-fi'l*, they have the capacity to benefit their followers and those who hold faith in them. In this way, benefit is received.

Amanullah Saheb was the one whose son has recently built the hospital. Yes, the father of Salim, who built the hospital. That was Amanullah Saheb.

### Seeking Help from the People of the Graves

> *Allatī lā ḥādatah*

Which has no limit.

Those noble *ṣuwar* meet both the visitor and the one whose grave is visited. Their *ṣuwar* are ever oriented toward their *fuḍalā'*, and because of this the *ishrāq* continues uninterrupted, the *ishrāq* of the *ṣūrah* toward its *fuḍalā'*. Therefore, the person who comes to visit, dwelling within that circle, also receives benefit.

As has been mentioned, the soil of Imam Husayn's grave is regarded as *khāk-e-shifā'*, soil of healing. Indeed. And there is also a saying of the Master of the Universe:

> *wa idhā ḍāqati'ṣ-ṣudūr fa'sta'īnū bi-ahli'l-qubūr*

And when the chests become constricted, then seek help from the people of the graves.

When the chests become constricted, when no way out can be found, when the distress is such that a person cannot see how to remove his trouble, then help is to be sought from the people of the graves. It was not said: seek help from Allah directly. Allah is the one who ultimately helps. But these are the people of Allah, and because they are the people of Allah, Allah helps through them, and through them help is received.

As it is in the Quran, in the verses revealed concerning Sayyidna Isa ('ع):

> *man anṣārī ilā'llāh*

Who are my helpers for Allah?

> *Qāla'l-ḥawāriyyūna naḥnu anṣāru'llāh*

The disciples said, "We are the helpers of Allah."

For the sake of Allah, who will be my helper? The disciples answered that they were the helpers of Allah. Does Allah need help? This is a Quranic verse.
