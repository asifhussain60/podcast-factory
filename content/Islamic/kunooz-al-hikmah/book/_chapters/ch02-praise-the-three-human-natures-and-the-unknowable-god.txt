# Praise, the Three Human Natures, and the Unknowable God

*Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim. Al-hamdu lillahi rabbil-'alamin. Wal-'aqibatu lil-muttaqin. Was-salatu was-salamu 'ala Sayyidina Muhammadin wa alihi ajma'in.*

> In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. All praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds. And the best outcome is for the righteous. And blessings and peace be upon our master Muhammad and all his family.

The book opens with praise:

*Al-hamdu lillahil-'azim jalla nasutun khas min riyadh ahlil-ikhtisas.*

> All praise is for Allah, the Great, who manifested a special *nasut* from the gardens of the people of specialty.

All praise and laudation are for Allah, that Exalted Being who created the special *nasut* from the *riyadh*, the gardens, of the people of specialty, the *ahl al-ikhtisas*. The varieties of *nasut* are several. There is *nasut adna*, the lowest *nasut*; *nasut awsat*, the middle *nasut*; and *nasut khas*, the special *nasut*. The *nasut tabi'i*, or natural *nasut*, which is the lowest, is also called *nasut tanasuli*, the procreative *nasut*. After it comes the *nasut dhilli*, the shadow *nasut*. And after that comes the *nasut khas*. The *ahl al-ikhtisas* are the *hudud al-da'wah*, the ranks of the *da'wah*, in the *jaza'ir*, the regions, together with the *mu'minin*, the believers, and the *hadarat*, the dignitaries, among them. The *nasut khas* is fashioned from them.

*Wa ja'ala al-nasut al-dani min riyadh al-madunun fil-maqam.*

> And He made the lower *nasut* from the gardens of those who are lesser in rank.

The *nasut dani*, the lower *nasut*, is made from the *riyadh* of those who are more distant, lesser in rank. This refers to the *nasut dhilli* or the *nasut tabi'i*.

*Ahmaduhu hamda man abda al-ma'na al-zahir.*

> I praise Him with the praise of one who has manifested the apparent meaning.

The praise offered is the worship of one whose meaning is luminous.

*Wa 'arafa haqqa al-ism al-zahir.*

> And he knew the reality of the apparent name.

He knew the reality of this apparent name. That is, the author has grasped both the outward, *zahiri*, reality of the name and its inner, *batini*, meaning.

*Wa ashhadu an la ilaha illa alladhi tasawa fil-'ajzi 'an idraki huwiyyatihi al-muta'aliyah awwalu mawjudin wa akhiruhu min bariyyatihi.*

> And I bear witness that there is no god but He, in the inability to comprehend whose exalted essence the first of existing things and the last of His creation are equal.

The witness borne is that there is no deity worthy of worship but He, and that the first existent thing and the last existent thing in His creation are equal in their inability to comprehend His exalted essence. The first existent thing is the First Intellect, *'aql-e-awwal*. And the last existent thing, from among His creation, is the believer, the believer who has entered the *da'irah-e-iman*, the circle of faith. Both of them are equal in their inability to have gnosis of God. The First Intellect has not attained greater gnosis of God, and this last existent thing has not attained lesser gnosis of the First Intellect. Rather, such gnosis is simply not possible for either.

The word "thing," *shay'*, carries weight here. A "thing" is that which possesses real existence, as in *inna Allaha 'ala kulli shay'in qadir*, Indeed, Allah has power over all things. Here "thing" refers to the believer, whose value and existence are real. *La shay'*, "no-thing," by contrast, is that which is a mirage: it appears to be water, but on reaching it, it is not there. It has no real value. This usage appears in three places in the Holy Quran: *inna Allaha 'ala kulli shay'in qadir*, Allah has power over all things, meaning especially over every believer; *inna Allaha bikulli shay'in 'alim*, Indeed, Allah is knowledgeable of all things; and *inna Allaha bikulli shay'in muhit*, Indeed, Allah encompasses all things. These three expressions refer, in their inner meaning, to the believer, and ultimately to the Imam of the time.

This state of equality in inability, shared by the First Intellect and the last existent thing, calls to mind what is said about Sayyidna Ali ibn Sulayman. When believers would come to meet him, he would rise to receive them. Similar conduct has been observed among the *du'at*, who would remain standing while exchanging the handshake of *juma'*, Friday, and on other occasions. When asked why he would not be seated, he replied: "I remain standing because I stand to shake a believer's hand, for he is my companion in the *Ahl al-Sharif*." In the *tawzi'*, the distribution, of the *Ahl al-Sharif*, the believer takes his share just as the Imam takes his share. In the *Ahl al-Sharif*, the believer is his companion, and so he honors him by rising.

The same principle stands behind what Sayyidna Hussain ibn Isma'il, may his holy secret be sanctified, wrote about Sayyidna Abdullah ibn Muhammad and Sayyidna Ahmad al-Jamari. He used the expression *afdal al-mawjudat*, the most excellent of existing things, in reference to Sayyidna Abdullah ibn Muhammad. People present began to wonder at the *Da'i*'s words. But *afdal al-mawjudat* here means he who is present in the *da'wah*, that is, the believers, and among the believers, he is the most excellent. That was the point being made.
