# Beneath the Ka'bah: Lowlands, Waters, and the Well of the Jinn

The fourth question runs: *al-su'al al-rabi' 'an al-bata'ih allati tahta al-Ka'bah, kam hiya* ("The fourth question, concerning the lowlands that are beneath the Ka'bah: how many are they?"). It asks after the *bata'ih* (lowlands) that lie beneath the Ka'bah, and how many they number. It continues: *wa min ayna abwabuhum* ("And from where are their gates?"), which are their doors; *wa ma tasir wa yasiru ilayha* ("And what travels and goes towards it?"), what things go up to them; *wa ma yadharu minha* ("And what appears from it?"), what things manifest from these *bata'ih*; and *wa Bir Dhat al-'Alam alladhi qala al-shakhs al-'azim* ("And the Well of Dhat al-'Alam about which the great person spoke").

Bir Dhat al-'Alam figures in the Battle of Bani Mustaliq. When the Prophet, salawatullahi 'alayhi, was returning from that expedition with his army, they came to a place whose people said: "We have a well in which there are shayatin who trouble us greatly, and all the people here are in distress." The Prophet commanded his army: "Who among you will go and fight them?" Two young men stood forward. He said: "I give the guarantee of jannah to them," meaning that whoever fights these devils is guaranteed paradise. The two young men fought and were martyred. Then the Prophet said to Ali (ع): "Qum ya Ali" ("Stand, O Ali"), that he should stand and fight them. Ali (ع) thereupon fought them at Bir Dhat al-'Alam, and when he emerged, blood was flowing from his sword. Rasulullah asked: "O Ali, how did you fight them?" He replied: "I fought them with my sword, and I fought them by reciting and blowing the verses of the Quran upon them." The verses of the Quran were so potent that he vanquished them through recitation and breath. He mentioned also a third method, saying: "This is how I fought."

Bir Dhat al-'Alam is thus the name of a well at which Amir al-Mu'minin fought. In one book a question was put to a dai: were all the jinn whom Amir al-Mu'minin fought at Bir Dhat al-'Alam destroyed? The dai answered: "No, they remain, but the coming Qa'im will bring them to an end." The matter resembles that of *'adhab* (punishment) and *thawab* (reward), the punishing of the enemies of the din, which the A'immah do not finally determine. They leave it to the Qa'im, who is the Hashir and Nashir (Gatherer and Resurrector). The decision of whom to send to jannah and whom to send to the fire rests in part with Amir al-Mu'minin, who is Qasim al-Nar wa'l-Jannah (Distributor of the Fire and the Garden), and ultimately with the Qa'im, who will pass the final judgment. He will cast the *azdad* (opposites) into Sakhr, and whatever decision he makes, he will make.

From our *'ulum* (knowledge) it can be gauged that when the Qa'im makes his *zuhur* (appearance), at the occasion of Hajj, he will say to the Mu'minin: "On the yawm al-nahr, the day of 'Id, you kill the azdad." The Mu'minin will kill them, and this will continue for three days. After one or two days such a stench will spread through the valley of Mina that the Mu'minin will find it difficult to breathe. They will plead to the Qa'im: "We cannot endure this state, and yet we must remain in the valley of Mina until the thirteenth." The Imam will then command the winds, and winds will blow; the *manafidh* (outlets) of Sakhr, of which there are four, will draw those things away toward Sakhr. Sayyidna Ali Sahab has listed the names of those four *manafidh* in one of his books.

Asked where this well lies, he said: "I have not been able to determine where it is."

Among the miracles of Amir al-Mu'minin from the perspective of the Qa'im-e-Juzwi (partial Qa'im), Bir Dhat al-'Alam is likely counted as well.

The question continues: *Inni dhahabtu ila tahti mizab al-Ka'bah fi qissat qital al-jinn* ("I went under the water-spout of the Ka'bah in the story of the battle with the jinn"). Amir al-Mu'minin said that he went under the *mizab* (water-spout) of the Ka'bah. Then *wa'l-mawaqif al-arba'* ("and the four stations"): the *mawaqif* are four. Then *wa min ayna manafidh al-bata'ih* ("And from where are the outlets of the lowlands?"), from where are the *manafidh* of the *bata'ih*, the windows. And finally *wa mannu bi'l-jawab fa-antum ahl al-sawab* ("So grant the favor of an answer, for you are the people of correctness"): give the answer and do us the favor, for you are the people of correctness.

The reply opens: *al-jawab 'ala dhalika al-su'al, naqulu bi-'awn Allah al-Kabir al-Muta'al* ("The answer to that question: we say with the help of Allah, the Great, the Most High"). The answer to the question is offered with the help of Allah, the Great, the Most High, and with the grace of the Wali al-'Asr wa'l-Zaman, 'alayhi salawat al-Rahim al-Rahman. And *wa ta'yid Mawlai al-A'lam wa sirrihi al-ba' wa'l-mu'azzam* ("And with the support of my Master, the Most Learned, and his brilliant and magnificent secret"), that is, with the support of Sayyidna Hibatullah and his magnificent and luminous secret.

*anna al-bata'ih allati hawla al-qalb al-gharr hiya arba'* ("that the lowlands which are around the brilliant heart are four"). The *bata'ih* surrounding the luminous *qalb* (heart) are four, *kama sabaqa min al-qawl* ("as has been mentioned in the previous statement"), *li-man haqqaqa al-nazar* ("for one who has verified the observation"). Then: *wa qawluka innaha tahta al-bayt al-munir fa-laysa dhalika kadhalik bal hawlahu* ("And your statement that it is beneath the luminous House: it is not so; rather it is around it"). The saying that they lie beneath the Bayt al-Munir (Luminous House) is not correct; rather they lie around it, on its peripheries, *'ala amrin jalilin khatir* ("upon a great and momentous affair"). The *bata'ih* are on its peripheries, not beneath it.

*wa amma qawluka min ayna abwabuhum wa majariha* ("As for your statement, 'From where are their gates and their channels?'"): which are its doors, and from where are its channels for flowing. *wa amma ma yas'adu minha wa yasiru ilayha* ("As for what ascends from it and what goes towards it"): things ascend and descend towards it. *fa-'alam anna tahta al-qalb ma'un latif* ("So know that beneath the heart is a subtle water"): beneath the *qalb* there is a subtle water. *wa fi wasatihi* ("and in its center"): in its center too there is subtle water. *wa minhu yanba'u ila tahti tilka al-bata'ih* ("And from it gushes forth to beneath those lowlands"): from those waters things ascend towards the *bata'ih*, *bi-majari* ("through channels"), channels that open, *wa manafidh* ("and outlets"), windows also being there, *'ala amrin sharif* ("upon a noble affair").

*wa hawla hadhihi al-bata'ih ahwiyah* ("And around these lowlands are atmospheres"): around these four *bata'ih* there are atmospheres, *al-sharifah al-shaffafah al-munirah* ("the noble, transparent, luminous ones"), transparent and luminous. *wa kadhalika hawla hadhihi al-bata'ih ma'un latif* ("And likewise, around these lowlands is a subtle water"): around the *bata'ih* there is also a very subtle water. *wa la yazal yas'adu minha bukhar sharif* ("And a noble vapor does not cease to ascend from it"): noble vapors continuously ascend from these *bata'ih*. *wa huwa al-mizaj* ("And it is the temperament"): this is also called the *mizaj* (temperament), *alladhi dhakarahu Sayyidna Hamid al-Din, alladhi takawwanat minhu nufus al-nuqa wa'l-a'immah al-mayamin* ("which Sayyidna Hamid al-Din mentioned, from which were formed the souls of the pure ones and the blessed Imams"). This *mizaj*, rising from the subtle water and becoming vapor, was mentioned by Sayyidna Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani: from these vapors the *nufus* (souls) of the *nuqa* (pure ones) and the A'immah al-Mayamin are formed.

*wa amma qawluka Bir Dhat al-'Alam* ("As for your statement, 'the Well of Dhat al-'Alam'"), that is, the question concerning Bir Dhat al-'Alam, *wa qatluhu Amir al-Mu'minin al-mu'azzam al-jinn* ("and the killing of the jinn by the magnificent Commander of the Faithful"), *alladhina zaharu lamma da'ahum sallallahu 'alayhi fa-wallaw wa ankaru* ("who appeared when he, (ع), called them, but they turned away and denied"). When Amir al-Mu'minin extended *da'wat* (invitation) to them, they denied it and turned their backs. *fa-dhalika huwa al-qawl al-wadih al-sarih* ("So that is the clear, explicit statement"): the matter is true, clear, and explicit, that Amir al-Mu'minin gave *da'wat* to these jinn and they denied it. In Surah al-Jinn it is stated that the jinn accepted faith; those were other jinn, whose states are described in that Surah.

*wa'l-hadith al-marwi al-sahih* ("And the authentic, transmitted hadith"). *wa qatala minhum man zahara fi suwar al-haywaniyyah* ("And he killed from among them those who appeared in animal forms"): those who appeared in animal forms, that is, in corporeal forms, he killed. *wa akhmada man tajarrada minhum fi surat al-zulmaniyyah* ("And he extinguished those among them who were divested in the form of darkness"): those who were in the *zulmani* (dark) form rather than the *haywani* (animal) form, being *mujarrad* (abstract) among them, he destroyed and rendered motionless, *bi'l-hujaj al-wadihah* ("with the clear proofs"), *wa'l-barahin al-laihah* ("and the evident demonstrations"), for against those dark forms too the *hujaj* (proofs) were established. *wa aslama minhum kathir* ("And many among them submitted"): many among them accepted faith and became Muslim. *wa la yunabbi'uka mithlu khabir* ("And none can inform you like one who is well-acquainted"): no one else conveys these matters as they truly are.

*wa amma al-mawaqit fa-fiha asrar batinah wa zawahir kaminah* ("As for the miqats, in them are hidden secrets and latent manifestations"). The *miqat*, the appointed place for entering ihram, holds hidden secrets and latent manifestations.

The miqats are five. The most excellent is Dhu al-Hulayfah, which is also called Bi'r Ali and Masjid al-Shajarah. It is the highest miqat, and the Hajj that the Prophet ('ع) performed, the single Hajj of the tenth year of Hijri, was commenced from this miqat. Great events are connected with it. The Prophet had taken Sayyidah 'A'ishah with him, and she had her period at that time. She lamented greatly, and the Prophet said: "When Allah has made you weak and has made you deficient, then you must bear it," admonishing her firmly. He then moved forward, and many incidents occurred with his companions at the miqat.

All our *ri'yat* and *fudulat* pass through the miqat. It is said that if a person does not enter ihram at the miqat and proceeds beyond it, he must turn back, return to the miqat, and make the *'aqd-e-niyyat* (firm intention) there. Such is the importance of the miqat: neither Hajj nor 'Umrah can take place without it, for the miqat is the *bab* (gate) to both.

The sixth miqat is a temporary one, Tan'im, which is in fact Masjid Ali. It has become very grand now; when we first went, it was a very small masjid. It is temporary in the sense that if a person arrives on the eighth of Dhu al-Hijjah with no time to reach the distant miqats such as al-Sa'il, he may make his *niyyat* (intention) at Tan'im and join the pilgrimage. And if he has still less time, he should proceed directly to 'Arafat, for standing at 'Arafat is what completes the Hajj. If a person does not reach 'Arafat, his Hajj is incomplete and has not been performed.

'Arafat is attributed to the Prophet ('ع). Three stations are visited: Mina, the main station, which belongs to Amir al-Mu'minin; 'Arafat, which belongs to Mawlana Rasulullah; and Muzdalifah, which belongs to Sayyidah Bibi Fatimah al-Zahra.

*Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim. Allahumma ashraf al-fatiha hadiyyatan wa wasilatan saniyyatan zakiyyatan mardiyyatan ila arwah al-khamsat al-ashbah al-athar wa ila ruh Sayyidina Ali bin Hibatullah qaddas Allahu ruhahu wa anwara darihahu wa da'afa 'alayna min barakatihi wa lazzatihi wa ta'yidatihi wa taqabbal minna ya Allah al-ifadah wa'l-istifadah bi-ahsani ma yakun bi-haqqi Sayyidina Muhammadin wa alihi ajma'in wa sallim. Al-Fatihah.*
