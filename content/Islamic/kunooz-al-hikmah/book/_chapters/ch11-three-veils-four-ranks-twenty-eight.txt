# Three Veils, Four Ranks, Twenty-Eight

*Nasut* and *lahut* stand in relation to the negation and the affirmation within the word of the *shahadah*. Just as negation and affirmation belong to Allah Ta'ala, the possessor of might and power: `li 'izzat wal-jabarut` (for the Almighty and the All-Powerful).

`Wa lil-imam 'alayhi al-salam thalathat hujub` (And for the Imam, (ع), there are three veils). The Imam (ع) has three veils. Fundamentally there are three, though different veils are also made use of. `Mathalu thalathat jawahir al-nizam` (The example of the three jewels of the order). Just as in the word of the *shahadah* there are three jewels, *alif*, *lam*, *ha*, so likewise the Imam has three veils. These point toward the three *nasut*: *nasut-e-tabi'i* (the natural human form), *nasut-e-zilli* (the shadowy human form), and *nasut-e-khas* (the special human form).

`Wa kullu imam min mawlana husayn jama'a rutub al-arba'ah` (And every Imam from our master Husayn has combined the four ranks). Every Imam from Imam Husayn onward has combined the four ranks.

As for what happened with Imam Hasan: Imam Hasan had two ranks, and Imam Husayn had two ranks. In Imam Hasan's time, the two higher ranks, the ranks of Imamate and succession, were with Imam Hasan, and therefore he was the Imam. Whoever holds the higher ranks is the Imam. The two lower ranks were with Imam Husayn, by virtue of which he stood under the authority of Imam Hasan. This is what occurred in the past as well. In the progeny of Sayyidna Ibrahim, whoever held the two higher ranks was the Imam of the time. Qaydar was there, Mawlana Isma'il was there, and the other Imams of those ages, and all of them held the two higher ranks. The prophets of their time held the two lower ranks and were entrusted with the mission. From the progeny of Sayyidna Ibrahim, from the progeny of Mawlana Isma'il, it was the same: Sayyidna Isma'il held the two higher ranks, while the prophet of that time, Is'haq, held the two lower ranks. That pattern continued through history.

But after the passing of Imam Hasan, when the four ranks were combined with Imam Husayn, those four ranks were not divided again. They remained with all subsequent Imams. Yet it is also a fact that the first Imam with whom the four ranks were fully combined was Mawlana Ali Zayn al-'Abidin, because even with Imam Husayn the two lower ranks had dominated over the higher ones, and he too was subject to that dominance. Once the four ranks were combined with Mawlana Ali Zayn al-'Abidin, his station became elevated above all previous Imams. That is why he is called the *Adam-e-Aal-e-Aba* (the Adam of the Prophet's family). Just as Adam was the first of his kind, so Mawlana Ali Zayn al-'Abidin became the Adam of the Ahl al-Bayt (the People of the House).

It was asked whether the Messenger also had the four ranks, and whether they were given as a trust to Mawlana Ali. The Messenger also had the four ranks. With Sayyidna Rasulullah, what happened was this: the two higher ranks were also gathered with him, but they were held as a trust (*amanat*). After Mawlana Abd al-Muttalib, the four ranks that had been with him were such that he gave the two higher ranks to Mawlana Abu Talib, while Sayyidna Abdullah had already passed away. A grandfather may bequeath to a grandson, so those two remaining ranks were kept as a trust with Sayyidna Abu Talib, on behalf of Sayyidna Rasulullah. Then, when the time came for the appointment of Sayyidna Ali as Imam, Sayyidna Abu Talib gave all four ranks to Sayyidna Rasulullah. The two ranks of mission and prophethood had been coming down from Sayyidna Abd al-Muttalib and were rightfully for Sayyidna Rasul. But the two ranks of Imamate were given to Sayyidna Rasulullah as a trust, that these are for Ali. For Ali. And so:

> `man kuntu mawlahu fa hadha 'aliyyun mawlahu`

Of whomsoever I am the master, Ali is his master. Of whomsoever he was the master, meaning of these ranks, Ali is his master. That is the meaning.

`Wa mathalu arba'at rutub... al-muqatta'at al-arba'ah fil-kalimat al-shahadah: La, ilaha, illa, Allah` (And the example of the four ranks is the four sections in the word of the *shahadah*: La, ilaha, illa, Allah).

`Wa istirahat al-maqam fis-sabi' min al-ayimmah` (And the resting of the station is in the seventh of the Imams). The seventh Imam is called the station of rest. With him the accomplishments of the six preceding Imams are gathered, and so it is a station of rest, a restful station where there is nothing further to add. The seventh becomes the *jami'* (the gatherer). `Min al-ayimmah al-qa'im... li man sabaqahu min al-ruh` (From the Imams, the *qa'im* (one who rises)… for those who preceded him of the spirit). For the past Imams he becomes a station of the spirit. `Mathalu sab'at fusul fil-shahadah` (The example of the seven sections in the *shahadah*). There are seven sections in the word of the *shahadah*: when it is written in the Arabic manner, there are seven places where the pen lifts from the surface of the paper. Those seven liftings of the pen are an indication of the station of the seventh, the *sabi'*, who is the *jami'*, and his rank is very high.

`Wal-imam majma' suwar ithna 'ashar jazirah bil-akhir` (And the Imam is the confluence of the forms of the twelve islands in the end). `Mathalu ithna 'ashar harfan fil-shahadah al-kabirah` (The example of the twelve letters in the great *shahadah*). The Imam is the confluence: the forms of the twelve islands, the twelve *du'at* (summoners), converge with the Imam at the end, just as there are twelve letters in the word of the great *shahadah*.

`Wa jamee' dhalika... thamaniah wa 'ishruna... suratan haddan... nizam al-'alam al-'ala sarmada` (And the total of all of that is twenty-eight forms as a limit… the order of the higher world, eternally). Added together, two and three and four and seven and twelve become twenty-eight, which is the *nizam al-'alam al-'ala* (the order of the higher world), eternal. That is a discussion unto itself.

A question was raised concerning the *falak-e-muheet*, which ascends without coming to the earth: whether it goes and meets the *barzakh-e-'ashir*. The answer was that this has not been mentioned, or has not been read. It was then observed that there is a verse of the Quran:

> `yanfudhuna la tanfudhuna illa bi sultan`

They pass through; they cannot pass through except with an authority.

So whatever is ascending waits in the *barzakh-e-'ashir* until the authority comes. It is deduced from that verse that the same applies to the *falak-e-'ashir*. It is not stated explicitly in the text, but the deduction leads there.
