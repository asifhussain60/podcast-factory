# The Author and His Only Book

*A'udhu billahi minash shaytanir rajim.* (I seek refuge in Allah from the accursed Satan.) *Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim.* (In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.) *Wa bihi nasta'in, sallallahu 'ala rasulihi Sayyidina Muhammadin wa 'ala aali Sayyidina Muhammadin wa barik wa sallim.* (And in Him we seek help. May God's blessings, peace, and benedictions be upon His messenger, our master Muhammad, and upon the family of our master Muhammad.)

The name of the book is *Kunooz al-Hikmah al-madhkurah li-arbab al-'ismah*, The Stored Treasures of Wisdom for the Masters of Infallibility. These are the treasures of wisdom that have been preserved for the *arbab al-'ismah*, the masters of infallibility. The question is who the *arbab al-'ismah* are. They are those who possess *'ismah*, infallibility. In one sense the phrase points to the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) and their progeny, yet that is not the meaning intended here. The Ahl al-Bayt and the *A'immah Tahirin*, the Pure Imams, are the masters of *kulli 'ismah*, total infallibility. It is impossible for them to err, and they are free of all sin. The *du'at* (عليهم السلام), by contrast, are the masters of *juz'i 'ismah*, partial infallibility. This distinction was clarified in a *risalah*, a treatise, by Sayyidna Ali ibn Hussain al-Jamari, who explained that the *A'immah Tahirin* are the masters of *kulli 'ismah*, while the *du'at* (عليهم السلام), and especially the *qa'imi du'at*, the standing *du'at*, are the masters of *juz'i 'ismah*. It is for these *arbab al-'ismah*, the masters of *juz'i 'ismah*, who are the *du'at*, that these matters have been stored away, so that they may draw new understanding out of them.

The book was written by Sayyidna Ali ibn Hibatullah, known as al-Shamsi, and he holds a great station. Sayyidna Hibatullah Sahib, that is, Sayyidna Hibatullah ibn Ibrahim, had several children, and he performed *nass*, designation, upon his eldest son. That eldest child was Sayyidna Ali ibn 'Ibad, and his lineage runs Sayyidna Ali ibn Hibatullah ibn Ibrahim ibn Muhammad, the *shajarah*, the family tree, continuing without interruption all the way back to Adam (ع). Sayyidna Ali ibn 'Ibad, however, passed away during his father's lifetime, and so he became a *ghayr-munfarid Da'i*, a non-independent *Da'i*. Sayyidna Hibatullah Sahib then performed *nass* upon another son, who became eighth in the order of succession. When Sayyidna Ali ibn 'Ibad passed away, he was eighth in that order. Sayyidna Ali ibn 'Ibad had himself performed *nass* upon yet another son, Sayyidna Isma'il, who was tenth in the order of succession. It is said that Sayyidna Isma'il ibn Hibatullah possessed the most excellent outward form, *surat*, in the community, *jama'at*, of the *wulat* (عليهم السلام). Another child of Sayyidna Hibatullah Sahib was Sayyidna Hasan ibn 'Ibad, who likewise became a *Da'i* of great station. And yet another was Sayyidna Ja'far, who was the seventy-fourth *Da'i*. He did not himself reach the rank of *Da'i* in succession, yet his station is great, and he composed *qasa'id*, odes, among other works. The book *al-Kawakib al-Durriyah*, itself a work of great station, opens with a *qasidah* by Sayyidna Ja'far ibn Hibatullah.

As for Sayyidna Ali ibn Hibatullah, he wrote only one *risalah*, only this single book, and nothing besides. He wrote it during his father's lifetime. His station among the *du'at* is nonetheless exceedingly high.

*Wal-hamdulillahi rabbil-'alamin.* (And all praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds.)
