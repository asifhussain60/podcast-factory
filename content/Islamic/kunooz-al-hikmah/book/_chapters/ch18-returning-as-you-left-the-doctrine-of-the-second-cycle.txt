# Returning as You Left: The Doctrine of the Second Cycle

In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. In Him we seek help, and may Allah's blessings be upon His Messenger, our master Muhammad, and upon the family of Muhammad, and may He grant them blessings and peace.

The sixth question was put in these words:

"I reflected within my soul that I fell short of drinking from the spring's source, that is, from the presence of the Da'i. I was not in the presence of the Da'i, and I did not acquire knowledge directly from him. I know that this was a sin and a failing on my part. And so I ask: will I return, in the fudla al-thaniyah, that is, after my passing, when my raj'at (return) comes, in the second karrah (cycle), in the same state as those who are present today? Will I be as I am now? Be gracious, then, and inspire me with that which will remove this uncertainty from me, open up this understanding, and make it clear. Lift from me every concealed veil.

I hasten to seek forgiveness and to repent for my sins. Be gracious unto me with guidance and with knowledge. Cause me to be allotted a Hajj, an opportunity to perform a Hajj of one of the Hudud (ranks of the Da'wah), so that my sins may be forgiven. Or let me perform some action that will release me from the hudud in which I am bound. Or let me intend to go to the Baytullah (House of God) and reside therein for years, in which I may suffice and abide by the command. And we seek forgiveness from Allah, for I ask about a matter of which I have no knowledge. Unless You forgive me and have mercy upon me, I shall be among the ignorant. I seek refuge in Your pleasure from Your wrath, and in Your forgiveness from Your anger."

The answer to that question is given with the help of Allah the Great, the Most High, and with the grace of the Wali al-'Asr wa al-Zaman (Guardian of the Age and Time), may the blessings of the Merciful, the Compassionate, be upon him, and through the support of our master, the most knowledgeable, the most luminous, who is most radiant, and whose secret flows to the subtleties of the forms.

Regarding the question about the fudla al-thaniyah, whether one will return just as one is today, as one was in the past cycles: the answer is yes. Just as a man is in this karrah at the time of his death, so shall he be born in the future. He will return just as it was in the beginning. If he was a Bab, that is, Bab al-Abwab, in the previous karrah, then he will be born as a Bab in this karrah as well, for the people of the milla. If he was a Hujjah, then he will be at the rank of Hujjah here too. If he was a Da'i, then he will be born as a Da'i. He allots the Hujjahs and he allots the guided ones, and this matter clarifies the principle. If he was a Ma'dhun, then he will come as a Ma'dhun, though progressing in rank after being born at that station is a separate matter, for it is the work of Qudrat (Divine Power) to place one at the rank of Ma'dhuniyyat. If he was a Mukasir, then in this karrah too he will be a Mukasir, upon this foundation. And if he passed away as a Mu'min, then it is incumbent upon Qudrat that in the second karrah, when he is born, he will be brought to the level of a Mu'min.

Those whose raj'at occurs quickly die in childhood, and that is a different situation. It has been said that such souls did not get the opportunity to take the 'ahd-e sharif (sacred covenant), or their lifespan did not allow it. So when they come in future karrahs, they will be born in the state of iman.

This rank is not transgressed. One does not move from one rank to another, born at one rank and then arriving at a different rank. It does not happen that way. There will be neither increase nor decrease in this pattern. Upon this very design, the vehicle will keep moving. And he will be born in the locality or town from which he originated, whichever city, whichever land it was where he was born and grew up. According to this course, according to the word of Allah the Exalted, one's birth in this karrah will be in that same place. As Allah Ta'ala says:

"From it We created you, and into it We will return you, and from it We will bring you forth another time."

When the deceased is lowered into the grave and soil is placed upon him, this verse of the Holy Qur'an is recited. "From it We created you": We created you from this very soil. "And into it We will return you": you return into this soil, into this grave. "And from it We will bring you forth another time": We will bring you out from this very place.

Once, someone asked Amir al-Mu'minin (ع): "O Mawla, in the state of prayer, the two prostrations, the two sajdahs, that we perform: what is the reason for them?" He replied: "Have you read this verse of the Holy Qur'an?" And he repeated it: "From it We created you, and into it We will return you, and from it We will bring you forth another time." He explained that when Allah Ta'ala said, "From it We created you," meaning, "I created you from this soil," that is an indication toward the first prostration. And "Into it We will return you," meaning, "I will merge you back into this soil," points to the second prostration.

A man will be born in the era of whichever Imam al-Zaman's time it was in the previous karrah, and in the time of the Da'i of that era, the Da'i in whose time he was born. They will not respond except to the one who called them, and they will not have iman and will not respond except through the very one who had called them in the past. They will not obey anyone except the one who had guided them.

There followed an exchange upon this point.

It was asked: as for increase or decrease, the same Imam al-Zaman and the same Da'i in whose time one is born, the nashist (era, sitting) that is to come will not align precisely with the ages, will it?

The answer given: the point is that the Imam has progressed in rank, but his niyabat (representation) remains established. Whoever becomes his khalifah (successor) and his na'ib (deputy) is the Imam. That is the meaning.

It was said: that part is understood. But the era and the nashist that will come, according to age, that nashist will not align exactly. The era and the age differ for each individual.

The reply: some died at a young age.

It was said: young age, old age, for example the Imam's age, the Da'i's age, the Mu'min's age, or the age of the other Hudud-e Kiram, the nashist spoken of as sitting according to time will not align precisely with the period. For instance, if the raj'at happened after a thousand years, the Imam at that time would be different, and the answer is that it is through his niyabat.

The reply: yes, in his niyabat. It is possible that, since it has not been clarified further, after a thousand years, with whichever Imam one appears, that one would be the khalifah of that Imam, or it lies within the khilafat established among the Imams.

It was said: a statement came earlier that the thousand-year period of returning is a general principle, and that as the ranks increase, the period of their raj'at increases, one coming in a thousand years, or two thousand, or five thousand.

The reply: no, that is fixed, for the Mu'min as well as for the non-Mu'min. That thousand-year period is fixed: after a thousand years, one must come again a second time. That is established. There is no change in it.

It was asked whether the Ahl al-Nass are separate, the du'at, whose raj'at is delayed.

The reply: no, for if the raj'at is delayed, then what of the Mu'minin? The Mu'minin will be born in the era of the Imam or Da'i with whom they had their 'ahd (covenant) or wilayat (allegiance), in that very era, with whoever they had iman in the previous era, upon his hand.

It was asked: for the du'at, whose ranks increase very quickly and whose time is short, is their raj'at also after a thousand years?

The reply: yes, for the du'at, though their khalasi (liberation) happens very quickly. As has been said, the Imam (ع) will not remain more than three days, his raj'at being within three days and no longer. Similarly, the du'at remain for thirty years, their period in the grave being stated as thirty years, and after that they return. For the Mu'minin it is three hundred years. This is the situation. But that concerns their progress. As for the raj'at itself, not all things of the du'at progress completely; some things are left behind, and then Qudrat brings them again for purification. It is the same for the Mu'min.

So too with poverty and honor: in poverty or in a state of honor, or in disgrace, being lowly rather than exalted. With extreme intelligence and cleverness one lives life, and one becomes eloquent and articulate, or conversely one's ability to speak and one's eloquence is diminished, one's intellect is light and weak, and the sharpness of the intellect is absent. All of this occurs according to the measure of what was present in him in the fudla al-ula (first form). His actions cast their reflection here as well, and what his hands wrought and sent forth. According to the measure of the righteous deeds he has performed in obedience to the Hudud, his reward is from the One Worshipped, from Allah Ta'ala. And according to the measure of the good deeds he has done with his Mu'min brother, meeting him with cheerfulness and welcome, with extreme humility, teaching him whatever he possesses, guiding him, and striving to elevate him to the highest of ranks.

And according to the standing and the advice offered with sincere intentions, and the manner in which this person stands, and the life he leads with sincere intentions. And according to the service rendered in the Da'wahs, that is, in the Da'wat-e Hadiyah. And according to the measure of what a man does for the people of the ranks, the people of high ranks who are worthy, the Hudud of the Da'wah, with whom he does some good deeds, according to the time and the moment, and the pressing need, and as the situations arise. And likewise the land-tax and the ransoms: in this way he gives his fiqaqat (ransoms), through which salvation comes. Through these fiqaqat his salvation happens, and success, and arrival at the permanent abode of felicities. He is successful, and he reaches the abode of permanent felicities, the destination.

The fiqaq is also of great importance, because what happens through fiqaq is that the defects and impurities within our nafs namiyah (vegetative soul), nafs hissiyah (sensory soul), and nafs natiqah (rational soul), the flaws within them, are removed. Just as with zakat our wealth is purified and our souls are also purified, so with fiqaq too these souls of ours, namiyah, hissiyah, and natiqah, are purified.

It was asked whether the ahl-o-'ayal (family) also stay together, whether their raj'at occurs along with everyone's raj'at, the family and the children coming together.

The reply: that is not mentioned here. But the matter will be the same. The same ones who had come with the Imam or Da'i will also come. The word itself has not been used, yet for one who is a mu'min and is upon the haqq (truth), and for all those who are upon the haqq, the matters that happen with the people of haqq are to be understood in this way: his track becomes completely different. Then the question arises of how his accounts will be settled, when he will come, and when he will come again.

For this, what has been said is this. There is, for example, a person who is blind, who is a kafir (non-believer). Now, a mu'min brother sees that a kafir is crossing the road. Considering it a work of neki (good deed), he helps him across the road. He does not ask him, "Brother, what is your name? What is your religion?" Rather, he performed a good deed from a purely human perspective. In this way, various works keep happening in our world, and such nekiyan (good deeds) keep accumulating. The question then is how his full hisab kitab (reckoning) will be settled for such good deeds, since the reward, the jaza, of the good work that people did is to be given to the mu'min. It is said that for those who are ghair-e-iman (without faith), in the second realm, in the other chamber, their good deeds are dealt with in this manner. Sometimes it also happens that they had performed good deeds for the mu'min. So from the good deeds of such ghair-e-momineen, water is made, and after being made water, it is brought into his use, into the use of wudu (ablution) and other purposes, and in this way the accounts between them are settled. This too has been said. So the matters that happen at the hands of the Imams, and how many such matters happen, the accounts are fully settled.

But that is the positive side: he was helped. There are also negative ones. Qisas (retribution) also happens. Of this Allah Ta'ala says:

"And for you in legal retribution there is life, O you of understanding."

That is, for you, in qisas is your life, O people of understanding. It is a great verse of the Holy Qur'an. The point is that qisas is a very large circle, and our life is running on a very small track, a small line. The circle of qisas is vast.

Imam Ali Zayn al-Abidin is famous for this, that he said, "I am afraid of Allah that I may not get caught in small sins." And he gave this example: "I used to watch my mother light a fire using small pieces of wood, from which the fire would ignite. In that same way, I am afraid that I might be caught by small, small sins." Although the Imam is ma'sum (infallible) and has no mistakes or sins, this then becomes a seerah, an exemplary way of life, and thus the seerah of the Imams is formed.
