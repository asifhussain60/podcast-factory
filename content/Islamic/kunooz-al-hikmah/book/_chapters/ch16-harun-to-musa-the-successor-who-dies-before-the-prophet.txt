# Harun to Musa: The Successor Who Dies Before the Prophet

The walayah of a person is accepted in the end.

A questioner has raised a matter concerning the saying of Mawlana Zayd, upon him be the blessings of Allah, in the supplication for Wednesday. The supplication reads: *wa bayanihi fi al-manzilah* (and his statement in the station), and speaks of those who were second, *wa irsalihi wa muqasimuhu fi kulli sha'n* (and his sending and his divider in every matter). The questioner asks what this means, since the phrase stands in a supplication and its explanation is sought.

The phrase continues: *wa hayat al-manqul fi hayatihi* (and the life of the one who is transferred in his lifetime), that is, the passing of one who passes during the lifetime of another. *Ma'nahu Harun* (its meaning is Harun). *Wa kana nuqlatuhu Harun qabla Musa* (and his transfer was Harun before Musa). The meaning of "the one who is transferred" is Harun, for Harun passed away before Musa. And the Prophet (ص) said:

> *'Aliyyun minni bi-manzilati Haruna min Musa*

Ali holds toward him the same station that Harun held toward Musa. *Fa-la shakka anna nur al-'azim 'Aliyyun intaqala fi hayati nabiyyihi al-karim* (so there is no doubt that the great light, Ali, passed in the life of his noble Prophet). It has therefore become clear, beyond doubt, that the great light of Ali passed away during the life of the Prophet.

Sayyidna Ismail ibn Hibatullah has also noted that the awsiya' (legatees) pass during the lives of their anbiya' (prophets). The pattern holds for the a'immah as well: the passing occurs within the life of the imam or prophet whom they serve. So the passing of Ali (ع) too occurred in the life of the Prophet. Outwardly it did not happen in this way, but in reality it did.

The questioner continues: *bi-tafaddul wa-tatawwul* (with favor and bounty), asking for favor and kindness, *fa-qad akthartu* (for I have asked much). He has begun to ask many questions. *Wa ana as'aluka* (and I ask you), with the plea that the answer not be given in a single sentence, for otherwise doubt will remain upon him. *Wa maqal al-summa'* (and a deafening statement): a word that comes as a mere concise phrase will not satisfy him. *Wa man yafta'ir sa'il* (and who is it that asks but a beggar): the one who is a beggar is the one who asks. *Wa man sa'ala dhalla* (and he who asks is humbled): the one who asks is humbled. *Wa anta al-mukhtar fi al-i'ta'i wa al-man'a* (and you have the choice in giving and withholding): the choice to grant or to refuse belongs to the one asked, who may answer the question or not answer it. *Wa bayyin li* (and clarify for me): explain the supplication of Mawlana Zayd, the aforementioned supplication whose praise has been mentioned, confirmed by his actions, pertaining to the fourth circle of power. It has been written that aid was given in his actions within that fourth power. What, then, are these circles? *Hal hiya min al-'uqul* (are they from the intellects?), and to what does this point? *Wa qawl al-natiq li-Kalilah, istaqbili* (and the saying of the Natiq to Kalilah: "Come forward"). The saying of the *Natiq*, that is, of Rasulullah, to Kalilah, meaning to the jackal: "Come to me." *Al-'aql al-khamis, sahib al-nur al-a'zam al-anwar, mufid al-khayrat wa mu'ti al-fada'il* (the fifth intellect, the owner of the greatest and most luminous light, the bestower of good things and the giver of virtues), the one who benefits with charities and gives graces and great boons. *Ila an qala... zaharat al-qudar al-Fatimiyyah* (until he said... the Fatimi powers appeared). *Wa minhu ashraqat badayi' al-suwar al-musawwarah* (and from him the splendors of the pictured forms shone forth), its new matters. *Wa qad kharajtu fi su'ali min ma'na fi ma'na* (and I have digressed in my question from one meaning to another). *Awjaba al-hal... kasrat al-su'al* (the situation necessitated... the abundance of questioning): his state is such that he asks more and more. *Wa antum min ahl al-ijzal* (and you are among the people of generosity), among the people of great generosity. The writer is himself very accomplished.

*Al-jawab 'ala dhalika al-su'al* (the answer to that question). Sayyidna Ali ibn Hibah says: the question that has been asked, here is the answer to it. *Wa naqulu bi-'awnillahi al-kabir al-muta'al* (and we say with the help of Allah, the Great, the Exalted), *wa minnati wali al-'asr wa al-zaman* (and with the grace of the Master of the Age and Time), *'alayhi salawat al-rahim al-rahman* (upon him be the blessings of the Merciful, the Compassionate), *wa ta'yid mawlaya al-'alam al-a'lam al-anwar al-sirr al-bahir* (and the support of my master, the most learned scholar, the most luminous, the manifest secret). With the support of Mawlana Hibatullah, most luminous, whose secret is a luminous secret, this answer is given. *Al-muwasil li-suwar* (the one who connects to the forms): such a secret as is obtained by the forms. *'An qawli Mawlana Zayd... salamu 'alayhi, al-malik al-'ashir* (regarding the saying of Mawlana Zayd, (ع), the tenth king). What is meant by *al-malik al-'ashir* is the Sayyid Da'i. *Fa-dhalika huwa al-sirr al-khafi* (for that is the hidden secret): the matter pertains to a great and hidden secret, *alladhi la yustar bil-aqlam* (which is not written with pens), not committed to writing. That is the nature of the question. *Wa la yulqa illa min lisanin ila udhun* (and it is not imparted except from a tongue to an ear): it is transmitted only from tongue to ear and is not set down in writing, *mimman 'ala lahum al-maqam* (from those whose station is high).

*Wa dhalika annahu ilahun al-mufaddal al-awwal al-sabiqah fi al-adwar* (and that is because he is the favored god, the preceding first one in the cycles). He is the one most favored, the first, the preceding, who has appeared across the cycles of old. As the Prophet said, *ana ibn al-karrat* (I am the son of the recurring appearances): he kept appearing through the recurring appearances, and Ali was always with him, appearing alongside him. *Istakhlafat al-mim wa muwazin laha fi khafi al-asrar* (the *mim* became the successor, and it is its equivalent in the hidden secrets). The *mim* assumed his successorship *fi khafi asrarihi* (in his hidden secrets). *Wa kadhalika al-'ayn idh ma istakhlafat al-qubbat al-Muhammadiyyah* (and similarly the *'ayn*, when it became the successor of the Muhammadan dome). In the same way the *'ayn*, the *'azimah*, became the successor of the Muhammadan dome. *Wa raqa ila ufuq al-'ashir* (and he ascended to the tenth horizon): having assumed the successorship, he ascended to the horizon of the tenth. *Al-munfarid bil-ahadiyyah* (the one unique in his uniqueness): he became unique in his oneness, and there is none like him.

A clarification arises here: did the *'ayn* take the successorship of the *mim*? Yes, the *'ayn* took the successorship of the *mim*. The word there does not refer to the physical entity. Sometimes it refers to the *hijab*, and sometimes when the *muhtajib* is spoken of, the *hijab* is meant. The *mim* took the successorship from the *'ayn*.

For the *muhtajib* of Ali, whose name is *'ayn*, is superior (*afdal*) to the *muhtajib* of Muhammad the Arab (ص). Yet among the veils (*hijabat*), the veil (*hijab*) of the Prophet is superior to the veil of Ali.

Mawlana says regarding the three generations (*qurun al-thalathah*) that exist that they are the Messenger of God and Mawlana Ali. He says that Ali (ع) gathered the a'immah, from Hunayd to Hakim, and from Hasan to the Qa'im, gathering them all. This was a rare thing: before him, no Imam had gathered another Imam who would come after him. But this is the station of Ali (ع), that he is the first and the last (*al-awwal wa al-akhir*). "I am the first, and I am the last." He gathered the first ones and also gathered those who would come after. The three envoys (*thalathah sufarah*) he did not gather in this same manner. The Prophet gathered Ali (ع), and Ali (ع) too was gathered; it is a matter between them. As for the successorship of the *'ayn-i 'azimah*, the first to assume it was Mawlana 'Abd al-Muttalib, after him Abu Talib (ع), and with Abu Talib was 'Abdullah (ع), *'indallah* (with God). After that, the third successorship was taken by Ali (ع), or by the Messenger; the Messenger took it. In this way these successorships continue, like a conjunction (*qiran*). 'Abdullah is said to be *'indallah*: 'Abdullah's station is with Allah, meaning with Abu Talib.

*Wa ihtajab bi-hijabi mim al-Maryamah* (and he was veiled by the veil of the *mim* of Mary). He adopted absence (*ghaybah*) through the veil of *mim-i Maryamah*. That is, the apparent passing of the Messenger was not truly the passing of the Messenger; rather it was the passing of Ali's veil that occurred. In the remaining life that followed, the veil of Ali (ع) was at work. After the apparent passing of the Messenger, the one who appeared as Prophet lived for a further thirty years, and there was a gap of thirty years between the Prophet (ص) and Ali (ع). Those thirty years during which he remained alive belonged to the veil of the Prophet and not to the veil of Ali. This is a rare matter.

*Wa thabata al-mim bi-hijab al-'ayn* (and the *mim* was established by the veil of the *'ayn*). The *mim* remained established through the veil of the *'ayn*. This event occurred on the day of Ghadir.

Is there proof for this in the noble Quran? Yes. The noble Quran states that the wife of the Messenger of God cannot remarry; no one else may marry her, for she is *Umm al-Mu'minin* (Mother of the Believers). Yet the Prophet (ص) had given Ali (ع) the choice that after his passing he might divorce 'A'ishah if the situation were as it is. Can one say this is possible? The intellect cannot grasp such things, not while he is working as the soul of the Messenger (*nafs-i Rasul*). For this reason, in the *Mubahalah* as well:

> *Ta'alaw abna'ana wa abna'akum wa nisa'ana wa nisa'akum wa anfusana wa anfusakum*

Come, let us call our sons and your sons, our women and your women, and ourselves and yourselves. For "our sons and your sons" the Messenger brought Hasan and Husayn. For "our women and your women" there were several wives, yet he brought not one of them; instead he brought Fatimah al-Zahra (ع) on his own accord. And for "ourselves and yourselves," meaning "bring our own people," *anfus* signifying one's own self, he brought Ali (ع). This made it clear that he was the soul of the Messenger.

*Talbisan 'ala qasir al-suwar* (as a deception upon those of limited forms). This deception was created for those who were weak-minded, those of limited forms. *Wa-ghusnan 'ala fi midajihi qudur* (and a branch upon... in his disposition... powers). Those who had powerful dispositions did not understand what had occurred. These states could be understood only by the highest-ranking believers.

*Wa kama azhara Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq al-ghaybah bi-hijab waladihi Ismail* (and just as Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq manifested the absence through the veil of his son Ismail). This is a well-known matter. Ismail (ع) passed away, but in reality it was Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq who passed away through his son Ismail. And in the gathering in which Ismail (ع) had passed away and his body lay there, Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) said, "Who will become my ransom (*fidyah*)?" All the *hudud* of the *da'wah* who were present, very high-ranking *hudud*, came forward saying, "O Mawla, we will, we will." But Furat ibn Ahmad, a person of the *hudud* of the *da'wah*, sat with his head bowed in humility, thinking: what is my station and greatness, that the Imam, addressing the gathering in which I am also present, should ask who will become his veil (*hijab*)? This humility was not hidden from the Imam. So he said, *Qum ya Furat* (Arise, O Furat). And at that moment he recited the verse of Surah al-Muddaththir:

> *Ya ayyuha al-muddaththir, qum fa-andhir. Wa rabbaka fa-kabbir. Wa thiyabaka fa-tahhir*

O you who are wrapped in garments, arise and warn. And glorify your Lord. And purify your garments.

As soon as this verse was recited, people began greeting him: "Ya Imam al-Zaman," O Imam, upon you be peace. When the Imam recited a verse upon a *hudud* of the *da'wah*, that person's external garb was transformed. What state, then, must have been created when Jibril (ع), on behalf of Allah, came and recited this very verse upon the Messenger. *Kulla yawmin huwa fi sha'n* (every day He is in a new matter). Every day there is a new station. And the master also says in one place that he never spent a single day in which there was no increase in his knowledge: the knowledge he had yesterday was increased today. This was the state of Rasulullah, a state of great magnificence.

Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq said to Furat, at the end: "I am you, and you are me." Something similar occurred in another Imam's time. A Sindhi man had come for the *ziyarah* of the Imam and was sitting in his presence. The Imam asked the gathering who would become his ransom, and everyone presented themselves and stood. But the Sindhi sat quietly. The Imam addressed him: "Why did you not stand?" And then he said, *Anta ana wa ana anta* (you are me and I am you). At that moment the Sindhi brother became a veil.

In Sindh too, if the historical record is examined, many scholars have passed and remarkable things occurred. In the time of Sayyidna Da'ud ibn Ajabshah, a person came to Ahmedabad who was an Agha Khani in religion and a horse merchant by trade, a very wealthy man whose name was Lad bin Dhannu. The word *Dhannu* is commonly written in that region and means rich (*ghani*), *dhan-wala*, one with wealth. Lad bin Dhannu came to Ahmedabad in connection with his horse trade. He was, however, a man of knowledge. Sayyidna Da'ud ibn Ajabshah met him and was greatly impressed by his character and his learning. When the time for prayer came, Sayyidna Da'ud said, "You may pray your prayer separately, and we will pray ours separately." But Lad bin Dhannu said, "No, Mawlana, I will pray with you, in your congregation." He prayed in that congregation, and afterward he joined the Ahl al-Sharif. Not only did he join, but as he deepened his study he accomplished such a remarkable feat that it is said of him that he became the *da'i* for the Jazira of Sindh. And it is also said of him that he was the جامع, the compiler and gatherer, of a great assembly of poets. These too are wondrous and remarkable states that have come to pass.
