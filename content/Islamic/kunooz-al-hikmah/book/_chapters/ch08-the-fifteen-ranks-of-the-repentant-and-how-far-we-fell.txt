# The Fifteen Ranks of the Repentant and How Far We Fell

Whether Ubayy ibn Ka'b and all those who stood as *hijabs* had any awareness that they were *hijabs* in that form is a question worth raising, and the answer is that they did, absolutely. Without *ma'rifah* (gnosis) such a thing cannot be. *Ma'rifah* is an essential matter. They knew, and they are people of great station. Were they to manifest, they themselves would manifest as prophets and messengers. Such is the height of their rank. Yet no incident shows the Prophet teaching them in any outward manner. It was given from heart to heart.

Across the whole thirteen years of life in Mecca, the dominance of the disbelievers and the polytheists was overwhelming, and across the ten years in Medina most of the time was spent in battles. Still the ethical teachings were given. In Medina, after the prayers, the Prophet, (ع), would sit after the Fajr prayer and ask, "Did any of you see a dream at night?" Someone would answer, "Yes, I saw a dream." And he would say that he was a better interpreter than Yusuf, (ع), that he was an interpreter. Then he would give a lesson. In that gathering the hypocrites also sat, and Muslims, and great personalities such as Salman al-Farsi. Whatever the Prophet said, every person gained knowledge from it according to his own level. The Prophet would say a thing, and its subtleties and deeper realities were received by whomever they were meant for. This was the beauty of the Prophet's discourse: whoever sought, received.

Concerning this same Sayyidna Ubayy ibn Ka'b there are narrated incidents. Once Rasulullah asked him, "O Ubayy, do you know which is the best verse of the Quran? You are the finest reciter (*qari*). You recite very well. You are a great *qari* and you recite the Quran abundantly. So which is the most excellent verse?" He said, "Allah and His Messenger know best." The Prophet said, "It is Ayat al-Kursi." That is what Rasulullah said at that time.

On another occasion Rasulullah was present while Ubayy ibn Ka'b was praying. Rasulullah was likely sitting in the mihrab while Ubayy was engaged in a supererogatory prayer. He called out, "Ya Ubayy!" Janab-i Ubayy heard the voice but continued his prayer. A second and a third time the call came. Ubayy then quickly completed his salutations and came. The Prophet said, "Ya Ubayy, I called you, and you did not come." He submitted, "Ya Rasulullah, I was praying." The Prophet replied, "Who taught you this prayer? Is prayer greater, or my command and call? Is my calling not greater?" For this reason a verse came down in the Quran: that if Allah and His Messenger call, the answer is to be given to the Messenger. The verse is most remarkable, for it does not say that the answer is to be given to Allah and His Messenger, but rather that Allah and the Messenger call, and the Messenger is to be answered.

The treatise continues: *wa ta'azzaz al-maqam al-sani bi-ghayrihi fi lubnani wa-tashahad fi alf surah li-al-anam*, that is, "And the second station was magnified by other than it in its very essence, and he was witnessed in a thousand forms by mankind." This Imam is such that he can manifest in a thousand forms. *wa fi dhalika wujuh wa asbab wa li-hi al-hujjat al-ujab*, "And in that are aspects and reasons, and for him is the wondrous proof." For his manifestation there are various aspects and reasons, and his affairs are strange and wondrous.

*wa amma qawluka kayfa kana qabla wujud al-samawat wa al-ardina*, "As for your saying: how was it before the existence of the heavens and the earths." The question was what existed before the heavens and the earth. *kanat min al-dhawat al-nadimah*, "it was from the penitent essences." They are the *dhawat al-nadimah* (penitent essences), and from these this came into existence. *allati usila minha ma hasula wa tarattaba khalq al-aflak allatihiya aqsam al-nadimin*, "from which was derived what was obtained, and the creation of the celestial spheres was arranged, which are the categories of the penitent ones." The categories of the *nadimin* were divided into fifteen types, and from the first type the celestial spheres (*aflak*) were made, and from these the heavens and the earth were formed.

*wa amma qawluka min ayyi fudulatin kana wujud al-awwal al-jussa' fi safil al-ard*, "As for your saying: from what remnants was the existence of the first particle in the lower part of the earth." The question was from which remnants the existence of the *jussa' ibda'iyyah* in the earth came about, given that the earth is wide and vast. *fa-ta'lam hadaka Allah li al-sawab wa bawwa'aka fi jami' al-asbab*, "So know, may Allah guide you to what is right and settle you in all the means," that is, may Allah guide toward correctness and establish the station in all the *asbab* (means), meaning the *hudud* (ranks). *innama kana wujud al-fudulat al-sharifah illa ba'd dhuhur al-jussa' al-ibda'iyyah*, "The existence of the noble remnants was only after the appearance of the originated particle." The *fudulat sharifah* (noble remnants) came into existence only after the appearance of the *jussa' ibda'iyyah*. *illa ba'd dhuhur al-jussa' al-ibda'iyyah al-dhalifah*, "only after the appearance of the weak originated particle." *wa innama yuqal fudlah illa al-baqiyyah min al-jism ba'da sudur al-ruh wa al-nafs*, "And it is only called a remnant, that which remains of the body after the emanation of the spirit and the soul." So *fudlah* (remnant) is what remains of the body after the departure of the *ruh* (spirit) and the *nafs* (soul). *bi al-wadih min al-khitab*, "with the clarity of the discourse."

*wa amma wujud al-awwal al-jussa' al-ibda'iyyah allati khalafat al-nafs al-kulliyyah*, "As for the existence of the first originated particle which succeeded the Universal Soul." The *jussa' ibda'iyyah* is that which has taken the vicegerency of the *Nafs al-Kulliyyah*. The *Nafs al-Kulliyyah* means the *'Ashir* (Tenth Intellect). The *jussa' ibda'iyyah* takes a partial vicegerency of the *'Ashir*, while the full vicegerency of the *kulli* belongs to the *akhir mawr*, the last person of the *mawr*. *fa-kana min asfa aqsam al-nadimin*, "So it was from the purest of the categories of the penitent ones." Among the *nadimin* (penitent ones), it is from their purest categories that the existence of the *jussa' ibda'iyyah* comes about. *allati kana tartibuha 'ala khamsata 'ashara darajatan fi al-ma'alim*, "whose arrangement was upon fifteen degrees in the realms." These *nadimin* have fifteen categories. *wa asfa man habata wa ashrafa man hafaw wa ta'bata*, "And the purest of those who descended and the noblest of those who stumbled and were trampled." Those who fell are the most pure among them, and those who slipped and were trampled are the most noble.

The *nadimin* too had made a mistake. The *'Ashir* first gave the command: I have acknowledged my mistake, so acknowledge yours and repent. All refused at first, saying *la nasma'u wa la nuti'*, "we do not hear and we do not obey." Then a second command came to guide them once more. He guided them and said that if they would not obey, he would remove them from that realm. At that point the *musirrin* (the insistent ones) remained upon their stubbornness, and so he removed them. The *mutahayyirin* (the bewildered ones), those in a state of confusion about what they should do, were removed as well. And while falling, the *nadimin* (penitent ones) repented; they repented even as they fell. Yet even within that falling, fifteen categories were formed. We believers are in the fifteenth category. Of the fifteenth category it is also said that we are in the tenth division, each category having ten divisions. So the believers of the *sitr* are in the tenth division, the last of the last. From this alone the measure of our station may be estimated.

As for the reason for the believers' falling, and how far a fall may descend: in the prayer recited on the third night the *darkat* (levels of hell) are mentioned and refuge is sought from them, up to *atiran*, in *du'a yazul qadr*, and *damad*, the garment of *damad*, and there it ends, the garment of *damad* being the last, the very last. Within it are the seven *darkat*, and the last of the *darkat*, from which lava comes out.

Those who come later and enter are not within this category. Here guidance is received from the Imam or from his *hudud*, and so the lower forms ascend. The lower forms, the forms of the disbelievers and the polytheists, ascend and move forward. So it is with the *'uyafa'*, as Sayyidna Muhammad bin Tahir has mentioned in *Anwar al-Latifah*: the *'uyafa'* are noble souls who love the Ahl al-Bayt, whether they be non-Muslims, or Muslims who love the Ahl al-Bayt and abide by the shari'ah and are mindful of their halal and haram. They love the Ahl al-Bayt and are thus *'uyafa'*, and they gradually ascend and enter the fold of Iman. Whether one is a disbeliever or a non-Muslim, upon coming and accepting Islam such a person will not be among those fifteen categories.

A question arises about Janab Salman al-Farsi, who was first of another religion, whether he was not therefore of that category. His reality is different: he was already of the *hudud*. Outwardly he was of another religion and then came here, and so a history, a biography (*sirah*), is formed; but before that, he already was what he was.

The disbelievers do not ascend until they accept Islam. They will not enter directly, because *shirkun zulmun 'azim*, "*shirk* is a great wrong." Allah Ta'ala forgives all sins except *shirk*; He does not forgive *shirk*. This is in the Quran as well. Yet the Quran also says *Innallaha yaghfiru al-dhunuba jami'an*, "Indeed, Allah forgives all sins." Allah Ta'ala forgives all sins, but He does not forgive *shirk*, because *shirk* is a great wrong. Allah knows when they will turn back.

For this very reason it is said in Surah Yasin: *dhalika taqdir al-'aziz al-'alim*, "That is the determination of the Exalted in Might, the Knowing." By *al-'aziz al-'alim* is meant the *'Ashir* (Tenth Intellect), because the *'Ashir* knows how many *mawrs* (cycles) it will take for this troop to be purified, and in every *mawr* how many *dawrs* (periods) there will be, and in every *dawr* how many Imams will come. He holds the entire map within his awareness. His station is great and vast. And he was our companion; at one time, he was our companion. From what an exalted station we have fallen may be estimated from this: the *'Ashir* was a soul, an intellect, among us. Yet by not obeying, by reason of the *wahm al-musawat* (illusion of equality), we brought this upon ourselves.

*Wahm al-musawat* is a great affliction, for its first principle is this: no person can occupy the same station as another. Two people cannot sit at one station. Even where there are two Imams, as with Hasan and Husayn, *Imama haqqin qama aw qa'ada*, "Two true Imams, whether they stand or sit," both are Imams, but their ranks are different. Similarly, where there are two *da'is*, as with Sayyidna Gulam Husayn Sahib and Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad, for at one time there were two *da'is*, or even two *ma'dhuns*, their ranks will differ. In one rank no two persons can sit; every person's rank is distinct. This reality was not accepted. The one who was the third intellect and who later became the *'Ashir* did not accept it, and so the *wahm al-musawat* took hold, the notion that he and the *Mumba'ith al-Awwal* (First Emanation) were of the same rank. Whereas the gaze of the *Mumba'ith al-Awwal* was upon the *surah 'ilmiyyah* (intellectual form) of the First Intellect, and his gaze was upon the *surah 'amaliyyah* (practical form) of the First Intellect. From this a difference was created: *nazar-i ashraf* (the nobler gaze) and *nazar-i adwan* (the lower gaze).

O Allah, send blessings and peace upon the Fatiha to the soul of Sayyidna 'Ali ibn Hibatullah, may his soul be sanctified and his grave be illuminated, and may its blessings and support be multiplied upon us. Accept from us, O Allah, the benefit and the benefiting, for the sake of Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon them. Al-Fatiha. Bismillah.
