# The Prophet, His Brother, and the Veiled Successors

*Wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan 'abduhu wa rasuluh.*

And I bear witness that Muhammad is His slave and His messenger.

The witness continues: *al-murtaqi min nasut al-khas ila jawar dhi al-'izzah wal-jabarut*, the one who ascended from the special *nasut* to the proximity of the Possessor of Might and Power. In the *Mi'raj*, the Ascension, the Prophet traveled through the special *nasut*, which was fashioned from the *riyadh* of the *ahl al-ikhtisas*, the people of specialty: the *hudud* of the *da'wah*, the believers, the *hadarat* and the *hudud* of the regions. From those *riyadh* the *nasut khas* was made, and through it the Prophet ascended and was elevated to the proximity of the Possessor of Might and Power. *Jabarut* derives from *jabr* and denotes the all-powerful, the compellingly mighty.

*Sallallahu 'alayhi wa 'ala akhihi*, may God's blessings be upon him and upon his brother, *al-mustakhlaf 'ala al-ummah al-Muhammadiyyah talbisan li-sur al-radiyyah*, the appointed successor over the Muhammadan community, clothing himself in lowly forms. He is the one who was appointed successor over the Muhammadan community. Before the Prophet's passing, at Ghadir Khumm, he declared:

> *Man kuntu mawlahu fa-hadha 'Aliyyun mawlahu.*

Of whomsoever I am master, this Ali is his master.

From that moment, the Prophet lived the remainder of his life in the *hijab*, the veil, of Ali, and Ali lived within the *hijab* of the Prophet. This is what the text alludes to. It is said that Sayyidna Isma'il ibn Ibrahim has stated, though without full elaboration, that the *awsiya*, the successors, pass away before the *natiq*, the speaker-prophet.

The remaining life of Mawla Ali was, in essence, the life of the Prophet. He was within the Prophet's *hijab*. As declared:

> *Lahmi lahmuka wa dami damuka.*

My flesh is your flesh and my blood is your blood.

And this proceeds from *ruh wahidah*, a single soul. When the Prophet's body was present, Ali was within his *hijab*; and when the realities were made manifest, Ali was the one in whom they appeared.

In the Battle of Jamal, Mawla Ali said to Lady A'isha words to the effect of divorce. The Prophet had granted Ali the right to do so after his passing: "I give Ali the right that after my passing, he may divorce you." The proof of Ali's status as the Prophet's very self comes from the Holy Quran:

> *Ta'alaw abna'ana wa abna'akum wa nisa'ana wa nisa'akum wa anfusana wa anfusakum.*

Come, let us call our sons and your sons, our women and your women, our selves and your selves.

When "our sons" was called, Hasan and Hussain were brought. And when *anfusana*, "our selves," was called, Ali was brought. He thus became the *nafs*, the self, of the Prophet.

*Al-muhtajib bihi al-'ashir bil-amr al-nahir*, the Tenth who is veiled by him in the manifest affair.

He was veiled, *al-'ashir*, the Tenth. The one who occupied the rank of *al-'ashir* became veiled, and that rank passed onward. The first *al-'ashir* did not remain; he changed into another rank. The one who became *al-'ashir* is the one who, in the subsequent series, within its sphere, occupied that station, the lofty intellect within its sphere. And from the *sahib al-qawr*, the master of the cycle, the *juz'-e-idafiyyah*, the relative part, of that cycle is what is intended here. It has been consistently noted that *al-'ashir* in this passage does not refer to that *al-'ashir* in the absolute sense, but rather to the *juz'-e-idafiyyah* of this *qawr*, the *Da'i* who is the relative portion of this cycle, not the *sahib al-qawr* himself, but the *juz'-e-idafiyyah*, and he became veiled by the Prophet.

*Wa 'ala fatin mudawij al-a'immah*, and upon the youth who is the spouse of the Imams.

The *fatin*, the youth, is Lady Fatimah, who became the spouse of the Imams. She was the wife of Ali, and from Ali she became the mother and, in the deeper sense, the companion of the Imams. *Bi-sirrin daqqa ma'nahu*, with a secret whose meaning is subtle, a secret whose meaning is most profound. As the Prophet declared in the presence of the Commander of the Faithful, *khalifatu abiha*, she is her father's successor, and *hujjatu ba'liha*, she is the *hujjah* of her husband. And the most excellent *hujjah*, *afdal al-hujjah*, is she herself. It is for this reason that Shahr Rajab al-Murajjab, which is attributed to Ali (ع), has its first night as a night of great station, and that first night alludes to Lady Fatimah, who is the *hujjat al-uzma*, the greatest *hujjah*.

The *du'at* of that era did indeed possess knowledge of these matters. As for the caliphs, some things were known to them. Abu Bakr and Umar had some knowledge of these realities, having lived in proximity to the time of *'Abd al-Muttalib*, but their knowledge was not complete. There is a well-known incident in which Umar said to Hasan or Hussain, in substance, that the honor upon your head was not placed there by me, but by you and your father. This is also preserved in broader tradition.

On another occasion, Imam Hasan or Hussain seated himself upon the *minbar*, the pulpit, and declared: "This *minbar* belongs to my father." And Umar himself testified: "Yes, this *minbar* belongs to your father," and then took his seat below.

So the successor who was appointed for the community of the Prophet was Lady Fatimah al-Zahra. And because of this, her passing occurred within six months. And when she would arrive, the Prophet would stand up. *Wa ajlasaha fi maqamiha*, and he would seat her in his place, or *fi maqamihi*, in his place. He would seat her in his own place. This was a great thing, an indication that the one who will sit in his place is Lady Fatimah al-Zahra.

*Wa ghaba 'an hadha al-sirr illa ahluhu wa ahwah*, and this secret was hidden from all but its people and its folk. Those who were unaware of this secret, of its hidden matters, were unaware of it, except for those who were its people.

*Wa 'ala al-Hasan al-mustawda' sirr al-imamah*, and upon al-Hasan, the trustee of the secret of the Imamate. Hasan is such that he is the trustee of the secret of the Imamate.

*Wal-qa'im min al-nasut ila al-lahut ka-arbab al-za'amah*, and the one who rises from *nasut* to *lahut* like the masters of leadership. He is the one who rises from *nasut* to *lahut*, just as the *arbab al-za'amah*, the masters of leadership, the noble ones, rise likewise. Even while designated as the trustee, *mustawda'*, he possesses both a *nasut* and a *lahut*. That is the meaning.

All the *du'at* are connected to Hasan, and the *A'immah* are connected to Sayyidna Hussain. But all the *du'at* are connected to Imam Hasan. In the Holy Quran, Surah al-Nas alludes to Sayyidna Hasan:

> *Qul a'udhu birabbin-nas.*

Say, "I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind."

The word *nas* here refers to those who are *manusina bid-da'wah*, intimate with the *da'wah*, who have affection for it. The Lord of mankind is Sayyidna Hasan. And *rabb al-falaq*, the Lord of the dawn:

> *Qul a'udhu birabbil-falaq.*

Say, "I seek refuge in the Lord of the dawn."

The word *falaq* means to split, and from that splitting, the Imamate began.

*Wa 'ala al-Hussain al-muzhar al-ghaybah*, and upon al-Hussain, the one who made manifest the occultation, *ghaybah*. He made his own self absent. *Bil-qatli lada ahl al-raybah*, through death at the hands of the people of doubt. Those who harbored doubt watched as his murder took place. An Imam cannot be killed, for he is infallible. Yet he is the one who made death manifest; he manifested his own death.
