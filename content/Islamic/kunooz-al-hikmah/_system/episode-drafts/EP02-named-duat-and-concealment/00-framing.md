# Named Duat And Concealment

## Opening directive
Open warm: welcome the listener to *The Stored Treasures of Wisdom*, the front matter's second half. Preview one sentence: the author binds whoever holds the book to a covenant of reading and teaches that the great summoners' confessions of sin are laments of intended good they were prevented from doing. Land the spine verbatim (R-RECURRING-THESIS, placement 1): "The discipline of reading is the doctrine carried in another register." Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. No "today we'll discuss," no faux-profundity opener.

## Name discipline
- The Prophet, first mention: "the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family." Thereafter: "the Prophet."
- The Father of Imams, every time. NEVER pair that leadership-title with his personal name; the pairing is forbidden.
- Stable labels: "the gatherer of Yemen's secrets"; "the seventh of the protected cycle"; "the holding Mim"; "springs-on-the-tongue"; "the emigrant to Najran"; "the Dai of the time"; "the questioner."
- Book titles in English (*The Stored Treasures*); never Arabic.

## Three-part focus
Beat 1, The praise as a course in the office. Walk the named summoners in order: the gatherer of Yemen's secrets; the seventh of the protected cycle; the holding Mim whose non-independent station kept the line; the springs-on-the-tongue who brought what no thought had framed; the emigrant who carried the call to Najran. Let the niche-prayers and the barefoot walk speak through the bodies that enacted them.

Beat 2, The invocation and the Pharaoh of the age. The author prays for the Dai of the time and for the protected enclosure against the Pharaoh every era brings forth; a calm era only means the Pharaoh has not yet found his opening. The doublet praise reaches into the cluster in the chapter on the pilgrimage, verses 58-65, where fifteen Beautiful Names gather. Land the spine (R-RECURRING-THESIS, placement 2): "The discipline of reading is the doctrine carried in another register."

Beat 3, The covenant and the lament of unfulfilled good. State the covenant: do not open the book except by permission of one who can sift, see, and holds the authority to bring forward and hold back; whoever breaks it is absolved from the Exalted Worshipped and from all the ranks. The great summoners' "sins" are the books they meant to write, the helps they meant to extend, the thoughts they could not set down. Read the chapter on the conquest, verses 1 to 2, in the inner register as forgiveness extended on behalf of the Prophet's deputies. Place the spine verbatim a third time (R-RECURRING-THESIS, placement 3) before the close.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) leads; Host B (female, seeker) challenges three times and concedes once.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — calling unfulfilled good 'sin' sanctifies high office past honest accounting"; "That sounds like wordplay — absolving a casual reader from all the ranks feels disproportionate." At least two genuine challenges; never chorus.

## Tone constraints
Analogy 1: A protected enclosure whose wall the believer's prayer helps hold against the Pharaoh of the age.
Analogy 2: A treasure stored against careless use, opened only by the guardian's permission.
Analogy 3: A spring on a tongue from which unframed doctrine gushes forward.
No further analogies; chapter's source images may pass through.

## Landing
Close on a reflective question grounded in the covenant: what good has the listener intended this year and not enacted, and what would it cost this week to begin the smallest part of it under the discipline of permission rather than alone? Leave it open. Surah names in English; never Arabic.
