# Two Foundational Questions

## Opening directive
Open warmly: "We're with *The Stored Wisdom* — its second chapter, where a student puts two questions to the Dai. One meets a wall. The other unfolds into the book's central map." Land the spine: **The first question meets a wall, the second a map — and the map is the Imam's dissolved heart feeding the believer at every ablution.** (Verbatim 1 of 3.) Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation.

## Name discipline
Stable English label per figure; honorific at first mention only. Rotation: the fourth Imam / the Adam of the family / he.
- The Commander of the Faithful — "peace be upon him."
- The Prophet — "peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family."
- The Father of Imams — never paired with his personal name; that title-and-name pairing is the doctrinally forbidden phrase, never spoken.
- The fourth Imam — the Adam of the Prophet's family.
- The Tenth Intellect — once the third; fell through the illusion of equality.
- The Originator — for the Mubdi.
- The Prophet's three veils — washing-veil, burial-veil, visitation-veil.
- Khadijah / Mary; Avicenna; Husayn, Hasan, Ishmael, Isaac, Joseph, Abraham — English names. Never speak Arabic book titles.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- riyat: ri-yat
- nasut: nasut
- lahut: lahut
- falak al-mustaqim: the encompassing sphere
- shahadah: the declaration of faith
- sahri: the pre-dawn meal
- iftar: the breaking of the fast
- barzakh: the threshold

## Three-part focus
Six beats, each landing once.
Beat 1 — Crisis: the boldest question — what was before the world? — is forbidden territory; voice the weight first.
Beat 2 — Failed framing A: the student's tripartite veil-scheme from the prayer-bead recitation is wrong; the Dai corrects it plainly.
Beat 3 — Failed framing B: the World of Origination is not a container holding Intellects; no "there" beyond, no "before."
Beat 4 — Pivot: the World of Origination IS the Intellects; the special veil is built in the Sacred House; the natural veil is born of mother and father; the lowest is where death falls. Spine VERBATIM: **The first question meets a wall, the second a map — and the map is the Imam's dissolved heart feeding the believer at every ablution.** (Verbatim 2 of 3.)
Beat 5 — Correction: Karbala touched only the lowest veil; the illusion of equality is the deepest curse — no two sit at the same rank.
Beat 6 — Stakes: the encompassing sphere alone ascends from its own place; the Imam dissolves in the House and becomes substance for the ranks; the believer's *riyat* arrive at ablution, *sahri* (last moment), *iftar* (hurried). Tell the Avicenna story whole. Close on 2+3+4+7+12 = 28 — the eternal order of the higher world.

## Host dynamic
Host A (male, scholar) carries the exposition; Host B (female, curious questioner) challenges at least three times, conceding once.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — if even the First Intellects cannot peer behind themselves, why is the refusal a doctrine?"; "That sounds like wordplay — calling the lowest veil 'the one death falls upon' is a rename of the body."

## Tone constraints
Analogy 1: the wall and the map.
Analogy 2: the architecture of regret — cosmos as a building put up around a fall.
Analogy 3: dew on uncooked fruits — purified *riyat* descending, the child nourished from that intake.
Do not invent new analogies.

## Landing
End on a reflective question grounded in this chapter: ablution, *sahri*, *iftar* — not pious habits but intake stations where the purified *riyat* reach the believer at his ordinary basin and table. Ask which station the listener has treated as ceremony rather than nourishment, and what would change this week if he treated the next one as the actual intake. Spine VERBATIM before the turn: **The first question meets a wall, the second a map — and the map is the Imam's dissolved heart feeding the believer at every ablution.** (Verbatim 3 of 3.) Leave the question open.
