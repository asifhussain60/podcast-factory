# Kunooz al-Hikmah al-madhkurah li-arbab al-ismah

## The Stored Treasures of Wisdom for the Masters of Infallibility

*An enhanced reading edition drawn from the lectures of the teacher, faithful to every teaching of the original. Arabic terms are rendered in plain transliteration; Quranic verses and Arabic supplications are preserved verbatim.*

---

## Author's Note: Title, Authorship, and Inheritance

The book before us bears a deliberate and revealing title: *Kunooz al-Hikmah al-madhkurah li-arbab al-ismah* -- The Stored Treasures of Wisdom for the Masters of Infallibility. Each phrase carries weight. *Kunooz* are treasures that have been laid up, stored, guarded against loss. *al-Hikmah* is the wisdom of the realities, the inner knowing by which the universe is read. And *arbab al-ismah*, the masters of infallibility, are not, in this title, the persons we usually first think of when we hear the phrase.

In our tradition the *Aimmah Tahirin*, the Pure Imams, the Ahl al-Bayt, are the masters of *kulli ismah* -- total infallibility. For them sin is impossible; error is impossible; their preservation is complete. But there is also a second order, the *duat*, the summoners who carry the call after the Imams have entered concealment. For these, especially the *qaimi duat* who stand for the era of the Qaim, there is *juzi ismah*, partial infallibility. They are preserved enough to bear the trust; they are not, like the Imams, beyond every possibility of slip. The distinction is not a casual one. Sayyidna Ali bin Hussain al-Jamari wrote an entire treatise to fix it precisely: the Aimmah are masters of total infallibility, and the duat, particularly the standing duat, are masters of partial infallibility. It is to *these* second masters, the *arbab al-juzi ismah*, that the treasures of this book are addressed. The treasures are stored *for them* -- so that they may draw out new things from them, so that what was once handed down may continue to bear fruit in their hands.

The author who stored these treasures is Sayyidna Ali bin Hibatullah, known as al-Shamsi -- the eldest son of Sayyidna Hibatullah bin Ibrahim. His station among the duat was a high one, though the manner of his life shaped that station in a particular way. Hibatullah Sahib had several children, and the eldest, Ali bin Ibad, was the one upon whom he performed the *nass*, the formal designation. But Sayyidna Ali bin Hibatullah passed away during his father's own lifetime, and thus, in the language of our tradition, he became a *ghayr-munfarid Dai* -- a non-independent Dai, one whose station was confirmed though he did not live to take up the office in its full external manifestation. His brother Sayyidna Ismail bin Hibatullah came forward in his place, the tenth in the order of succession, and of all the children of Sayyidna Hibatullah it is said that Sayyidna Ismail had the most excellent form (*surat*) in the community of the *wulat*. Another brother, Sayyidna Hasan bin Ibad, became a Dai of great station after him. And a fourth brother, Sayyidna Jafar bin Hibatullah, did not himself become Dai, but his rank was nonetheless lofty: he wrote *qasidahs* (odes) of profound depth, and the opening *qasidah* of the great book *al-Kawakib al-Durriyah* is from his pen. Sayyidna Jafar, while not the author of a treatise on the realities, ranked among the most cultivated of his line.

Sayyidna Ali bin Hibatullah himself wrote only one book in all his life, and he wrote it during his father's lifetime, in obedience to a request that came to him through his father's command. He never wrote a second. That single book is the one we are reading now -- the treatise that has come into our hands across the centuries, naked of the author's other works because the author left no others.

There is a small mark of style that is worth noting from the very first leaf. Above the *Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim* on the original page is written the *Ism al-Azam*, the Greatest Name. A treatise by Sayyidna Abd al-Ali bin Hasan al-Wajih considered, at length, whether the *nur* (light) of the *Ism al-Azam* is greater than the *nur* of the *Bismillah*. After protracted argument he established that the light of the *Ism al-Azam* is the more excellent. For this reason we write the *Ism al-Azam* above the *Bismillah* whenever the occasion calls for the full inscription. The sign itself is rendered in *khatt al-Teseri*, the Teseri script: the first dot above is a *khatam*, a seal-ring, alluding to the *Ahl al-Sharif*, the noble family. The *madd*, the elongation mark above, points to the *muawanah*, the assistance received from the *Hudud Aliyah*, the higher ranks of the hierarchy. The Commander of the Faithful himself composed verses (*ashar*) about the *Ism al-Azam*; one does not pass over such a sign without acknowledging what it carries.

## The Opening Praise: Three Levels of Human Form

The book opens with a praise that is also a doctrine. *Al-hamdu lillahil-azim jalla nasutun khas min riyadh ahlil-ikhtisas* -- All praise is for Allah, the Great, who manifested a special human form (*nasut khas*) from the gardens of the people of specialty. Already, in the first line, two technical terms appear that organize much of what follows: *nasut* and *riyadh*.

There are three types of *nasut*, three forms of the human vessel. The lowest is *nasut adna*; the middle, *nasut awsat*; and the highest, *nasut khas* -- the special. By another naming, the lowest is also called *nasut tabii* (natural) or *nasut tanasuli* (procreative), the form that is born of loins and wombs in the ordinary way. After this is *nasut dhilli*, the shadow form. And after this, the *nasut khas*, the special form, which is of an order altogether apart.

The *nasut khas* is made from the *riyadh*, the gardens of the *ahl al-ikhtisas*, the people of specialty. The *riyadh* are the spiritual gardens of particular souls: the *hudud al-dawah*, the ranks of the summoning hierarchy in the regions; the *muminin*, the believers; and the *hadarat*, the dignitaries gathered in those regions. From the spiritual essences of these is the *nasut khas* composed. The *nasut dani*, the lower form, is by contrast made from the *riyadh* of those who are at a further distance, lesser in station -- the wider community of souls.

This is the substance behind a quiet astonishment that recurs throughout the book: when the Prophet ascended in the Miraj, through which *nasut* did he ascend? Through the *nasut khas* -- formed from the *riyadh* of the people of specialty. That is, the very souls of the believers and ranks of the regions, in their inner essences, were the substance from which the Prophet's ascending form was woven. The Prophet did not ascend in any other body. He ascended in this one; and we, in our small ways, are gardens that contribute to such a form.

This is also why, throughout the praise, two parallel hierarchies are honored: the Imams, in whom the *nasut khas* is fully realized, and the duat, who serve as the bearers of the work in the era of the Imam's concealment.

## The First Existent and the Last Existent: An Equality in Bewilderment

The author next bears witness in the language of the *shahadah* and unveils a teaching that, by his own statement, had never before been said by any Dai. *Wa ashhadu an la ilaha illa alladhi tasawa fil-ajzi an idraki huwiyyatihi al-mutaaliyah awwalu mawjudin wa akhiruhu min bariyyatihi* -- And I bear witness that there is no god but He, in the inability to comprehend whose exalted essence the first of existing things and the last of His creation are equal.

Here are two ends of a vast hierarchy. The *awwal mawjud*, the first existent thing in the order of creation, is the *aql-e-awwal*, the First Intellect. The *akhir mawjud*, the last existent thing, is the believer -- the soul that has just newly entered the *dairah-e-iman*, the circle of faith. These are the highest and the lowest poles of the order of creation, separated by what one would suppose is an unbridgeable distance of station.

The claim made here is breathtaking: in the matter of *marifat*, the gnosis of the Exalted Essence, these two are *equal*. The First Intellect, despite its lofty station, has not advanced in the gnosis of God beyond the point where this last believer stands; the believer, low in the order of being, has not fallen behind by any measurable distance. Both are equal in the inability. The First Intellect is perplexed before the Originator in the same way that the newest believer is perplexed. The gnosis of the Mubdi al-Taala -- the Originator -- is simply impossible to either. It is not that one has a smaller portion than the other; it is that the very thing called *gnosis of His essence* lies altogether outside the reach of any creature, however high it may stand in the chain of being.

Here the word *shay*, "thing," in the Qur'an must also be noted. *Inna Allaha ala kulli shayin qadir* -- Indeed Allah has power over every thing. *Inna Allaha bikulli shayin alim* -- Allah knows every thing. *Inna Allaha bikulli shayin muhit* -- Allah encompasses every thing. In all three places the word *shay* does not mean every random object in the world. *Shay* means that which is real, that whose value endures. *La shay*, by contrast, is the mirage -- what looks like water in the distance but when you reach it there is no water there. The mirage has no real value. So *shay* points to the believer, who has value; and at the highest reach, *shay* points to the Imam of the time, in whom all value is gathered. When the Quran says Allah has power over every *shay*, knows every *shay*, encompasses every *shay*, it is the believer above all who is being kept in view.

Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman lived this teaching outwardly. When believers came to meet him he would stand up to receive them. When asked why a Dai should rise for a believer who had come to greet him, he answered: because in the *tawzi*, the apportioning of the *Ahl al-Sharif*, the believer is my companion. Just as an Imam takes his share in the noble family, so the believer takes his share. In the matter of belonging to the *Ahl al-Sharif*, the believer is my fellow. And so out of honor, I stand. Likewise Sayyidna Hussain bin Ismail wrote of Sayyidna Abdullah ibn Muhammad and of Sayyidna Ahmad al-Jamari that one of them was *afdal al-mawjudat* -- the most excellent of existing things. People who heard the phrase puzzled at it. *Afdal al-mawjudat* meant: most excellent among the believers present in the dawah. The believer, again, is not a peripheral figure in this universe. The believer is *afdal al-mawjudat*.

## The Veiling of Ali by the Prophet, and of the Prophet by Ali

From the opening praises the book turns to the line of the Imams and to the secret of their veiling.

After the declaration of Ghadir Khumm -- *man kuntu mawlahu fa-hadha Aliyyun mawlahu*, "Of whomsoever I am master, this Ali is his master" -- the Prophet lived his remaining months in the *hijab* of Ali, and Ali, for the rest of his own life, lived in the *hijab* of the Prophet. The two did not simply exist as separate persons; they had become veil and veiled, each interchangeable with the other in the inner reality. The Prophet himself had declared *lahmi lahmuka wa dami damuka* -- my flesh is your flesh, my blood is your blood -- and the two were of one *ruh wahidah*, a single soul. Sayyidna Ismail bin Ibrahim, in one of his writings, drew out the structural consequence of this: the *awsiya*, the successors, pass away before the *natiq*, the speaker-prophet, in the inner reality. What this means is that during the Prophet's remaining time after Ghadir Khumm, it was Ali who, in the inner ordering, had already given his form to be the veil of the Prophet; the Prophet, while his body still walked the earth, was already living in the inner *hijab* of Ali. When his body departed, what departed was a veil; the inner Ali was already in continuity.

The proof of how deeply this *anfusana wa anfusakum*, "our selves and your selves," ran is in the famous matter of Aisha. The Prophet had said in the presence of the Commander of the Faithful that he gave Ali the right that, after his own passing, Ali could divorce Aisha. The companions found this hard. How could this be? But the proof is from the Quran itself. In the verse of *mubahalah* the Prophet was commanded *taalaw abnaana wa abnaakum wa nisaana wa nisaakum wa anfusana wa anfusakum* -- come, let us call our sons and your sons, our women and your women, our selves and your selves. For "our sons" he brought Hasan and Husayn. For "our selves" he brought Ali. So Ali became the *nafs*, the self, of the Prophet -- and what the Prophet may decide, the *nafs* of the Prophet may decide. The divorce-authority over Aisha is a direct corollary.

In the same vein the praise speaks of *al-ashir*, the Tenth, who is veiled by the Prophet. This refers in the broadest sense to the Tenth Intellect; but more precisely, as Abdullah Sahib used to insist, *al-ashir* here does not point to that Tenth alone. It points to the *juz-e-idafiyyah*, the relative part, of the *sahib al-qawr* -- the master of the cycle -- in this cycle of ours. The Tenth has many "relative parts" running through history; what is veiled by the Prophet here is the particular *juz-e-idafiyyah* of *this* cycle's *sahib al-qawr*, the Dai who carries the line of summons through this *qawr*.

## Fatimah al-Zahra: The Successor of the Father

The praise next turns to Fatimah al-Zahra, and what it says of her is among the most quietly extraordinary teachings of the entire book.

The Prophet, before his passing, said of her: *khalifatu abiha*, the successor of her father, and *hujjatu baliha*, the *hujjah* of her husband. And in the inner reckoning, *afdal al-hujjah* -- the most excellent *hujjah* of all -- is Fatimah al-Zahra herself. She is the *hujjat al-uzma*, the greatest *hujjah*. Because of this, Shahr Rajab al-Murajjab, attributed to Ali, has its first night of supreme station as well: the first night of Rajab alludes to Lady Fatimah, the greatest *hujjah*.

The consequence for our doctrine is decisive. The successor whom the Prophet appointed over the Muhammadan community at the inner level was Fatimah al-Zahra. For this reason your passing came so quickly: within six months of your father, you departed. And while the Prophet was alive, whenever you arrived in his presence, he would stand up and seat you in his own place. *Wa ajlasaha fi maqamihi* -- and he seated her in his place. This was no mere gesture of paternal love. It was a continual public sign of who would sit, in the inner reality, in the Prophet's seat after him.

This is also why, in this cycle, those who came after Hussain -- the Imams from his progeny -- are called *Aimmah Fatimiyyin*. Sayyidna Hibatullah, in *Kanz al-Ulum*, has written that the station of Fatimah al-Zahra is such that even the Pure Imams could not attain its gnosis; the Imams who follow her in the line are bewildered before her station, and for this reason they bear her name. The *qudar al-Fatimiyyah*, the Fatimi powers, are the Imams from Husayn's progeny -- and the title rests on the awe in which they stand before her. Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad al-Fad has set this out clearly in his *Kanz al-Ulum*, and Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Husayn al-Hamidi wrote a parallel *Kanz al-Walad* for his son Sayyidna Hatim that takes the same vein further.

The hostility of the early caliphs was not pure ignorance of all this. Some of the matters of the realities were known to those near the family. Abu Bakr and Umar had been around long enough -- from the time of Abd al-Muttalib -- to have caught hints of some of these things. There are even traditions that point to it. Umar once, when a young Hasan or Husayn climbed onto the *minbar* and said, "This *minbar* belongs to my father," himself answered, "Yes, this *minbar* belongs to your father," and stepped down. He was acknowledging publicly that the *minbar*, in its inner significance, was not his. And another well-known saying among the common people goes that Umar admitted of one of these grandsons: "These hairs were not grown by you" -- meaning, the hairs on your head were not grown through any work of mine, but through yours and your father's. The rank was known; that was the bitterness of the rejection.

## Hasan, Hussain, and the Two Branches of the Inheritance

After Fatimah the praise turns to *al-Hasan al-mustawda sirr al-imamah*, Hasan, the trustee of the secret of the Imamate. Hasan is the *mustawda*, the entrusted one who carries the secret of the Imamate forward in a particular manner. Even while bearing the trusteeship he has, like any Imam, his *nasut* and his *lahut*; he rises *min al-nasut ila al-lahut*, from the human form to the divine, as the *arbab al-zaamah*, the masters of leadership, do.

From him a particular line proceeds: all the *duat* are connected to Imam Hasan. From Hussain, by contrast, the line of the Aimmah descends; the *Aimmah* are connected to Sayyidna Husayn. The doctrine, then, is a forked inheritance: one branch runs through Hasan to the duat who carry the summons through the eras of concealment; the other branch runs through Hussain to the Aimmah themselves. To recognize Hasan is to recognize the source of every dai.

The Qur'an itself points to these two branches at the end of its recitation. *Qul audhu birabbinnas* -- Say, I take refuge with the Lord of mankind. The *nas* here are those who are *manusina bid-dawah*, those intimate with the summoning, who have affection for it. Their Lord -- the *Rabb al-Nas* -- is Sayyidna Hasan. *Qul audhu birabbil-falaq* -- Say, I take refuge with the Lord of the dawn, the Lord of the splitting. *Falaq* is to split. From there, from the splitting, the line of Imamate proceeds. The two final Surahs, *al-Nas* and *al-Falaq*, encode the two branches of the inheritance.

Hussain himself, as the praise notes, *al-muzhar al-ghaybah bil-qatli lada ahl al-raybah* -- the one who made manifest the occultation through death at the hands of the people of doubt. The killing at Karbala was the manifestation of an occultation; what the people of doubt saw killed was a veil. The real self was not the thing that was struck. The deeper doctrine of the Imam's veils will return again later in the book. But here at the start, the theme is set: Hussain's body was killed, his real self was protected, and the occultation of the inner Imam was, by that very killing, made publicly visible to those who could read what was happening.

## The Imams as Assemblages of Lights, and the Hidden Imam

The praise next embraces *al-aimmati min dhurriyyatihi majami al-anwar* -- the Imams from his progeny, the assemblages of lights. The distinction between *asrar* (secrets) and *anwar* (lights) is important. *Asrar* are intentions, *niyyat*; *anwar* are the luminous forms, *suwarat*. Sayyidna Sulayman is, in this book, the *jami al-asrar*, assembler of the secrets, of the duat of Yemen who came after Sayyidna Dhuayb bin Musa. He gathered their secrets; he assembled their *suwarat* in himself as one assembles lights. The Imams from Husayn's progeny are, in the same fashion, *majami al-anwar* -- assemblages of luminous forms.

The praise then reaches the Imam of the present time, the hidden one: *Imam al-zaman al-mutawari bil-hijabi al-mustatir bil-sirr an jami al-baraa* -- the Imam of the Time, who is concealed by the veil, hidden by the secret from all of creation. Whatever has appeared of him outwardly is the veil; the original self is hidden. And all the Imams who will come, even unto the Day of Resurrection, are veiled by Amir al-Muminin: they are *his* veils, and he is their veiled essence. *La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah al-Aliyy al-Azim* -- "There is no power and no strength except with Allah, the High, the Great." The *Aliyy al-Azim* here is the *ayn al-aimmah*, the essence of the Imams. All the Aimmah are *hijabs* of the *Aliyy al-Azim*. Every prayer-formula we say that names *al-Aliyy al-Azim* is, in its inner orientation, an acknowledgment of this essence veiled by every Imam.

The praise also brings in *abwabihim*, the gates, whose lights shine forth in the forms, and their *hujjahs*, and the *duat* of this auspicious *dawr* that will last fifty thousand years. The number is striking. The *dawr al-satr*, the era of concealment, ended with Sayyidna Sulayman Sahib -- seven eras, all completed. *Numerically*, by the reckoning, seven cycles of concealment are finished. This present *dawr* of ours, our particular *qaimi* era, is included within the great *dawr* of the Qaim: fifty thousand years of what you count. When the Qaim comes, fifty thousand years will begin from after him. So when the praise says "the duat of this auspicious *dawr* that will last for fifty thousand years," it is gathering our own duat into the company of those who carry the Qaim's era.

## The Specific Duat Named in the Praise

The praise then sets each title against the particular Dai it commemorates -- and reading them in sequence is a quiet course in the history of the line.

*Sulayman bin Hasan al-mujtama ladayhi asraru duat al-Yaman* -- with him are gathered the secrets of the duat of Yemen. The secrets, the *asrar*, of all those duat of Yemen are gathered in his possession. What a station is this!

*Jafar al-sabi fis-sirr al-mamun* -- Jafar the seventh in the protected secret. Sayyidna Jafar, who is the 28th Dai in the overall sequence, is the seventh by another reckoning -- the seventh in the manifest, protected secret. It was said earlier that the 28th in any sequence is a station of rest, the gatherer of those before; that point will return.

*Mimun min lala* -- a Mim from. The Mim here is Muhammad bin Fuad, the Dai who came in between and was non-independent, but whose station was so necessary that without him the entire history would have shifted. *Al-qaim bi-fard al-jihad fi malain* -- the one who established the duty of *jihad* in the midst of the assembly, who established jihad among the people. What a tremendous jihad you carried! Had Sayyidna Muhammad bin Fuad's position been broken in the way that earlier positions had broken in the days of Abd al-Muttalib, the history of the dawah would have changed. But this Mim held, and the line continued.

*Al-ayn al-mutafajjir ala lisanihi yanabi al-asrar* -- and Ayn, upon whose tongue gushed forth the springs of secrets. *Ayn* here is Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman, on whose tongue the springs of the secrets of the doctrines became manifest. The fountains of the various secrets of the *haqaiq*, the realities, gushed forth from his tongue. Many sublime matters of doctrine that he set out in his works had not been said before. *Shual al-Anwar*, his first book, he himself declared to be a book of *al-haqaiq wad-daqaiq* -- of realities and subtleties: *hadha al-kitab fil haqaiq wad-daqaiq*. From there, the books of the subtleties began. By contrast, Sayyidna Jafar (despite the loftiness of his own station) did not write a single book on the *haqaiq*; all his books are on the *hujjat*, the rational proof, or on *tawil*, interpretation.

*Al-ati bi-ma la yakhtur alal afkar* -- bringing forth what had never occurred to the thoughts. The books such as *Anwar al-Shashaniyyah*, and others that came from him, contain matters of doctrine that no thought had previously framed.

The praise continues with the *sarib*, the drinking companion: *Sayyidna Ibrahim, the nourishment of souls with the secrets of the holy malakut*. And then *Muhammad al-muhajir min baldatihi*, his Muhammad, the emigrant from his town -- Muhammad bin Ismail, the first Dai who emigrated from his town toward Najran. Until him, the dawah had been confined to Yemen -- to Sanaa, to Haraz, to Saafan. He was the first Dai to leave Yemen entirely and to settle in Najran. He was also the first and only Dai given permission to lead prayers in *ghayr jazirah*, outside the Arabian peninsula: he led prayers in Habasha (Abyssinia). For the loftiness of your station, this dispensation was granted. He passed away in Najran, and from his time the entire dawah began slowly to move toward Najran. Sayyidna Hasan Shihab and Sayyidna Ali bin Ibad still remained, for a time, in the older centers. But after them, the move was complete.

Sayyidna Muhammad bin Ismail's emigration was a journey of such hardship that it took a year. He passed through cities of Syria along the way; he stayed in Abuna. Abuna is a mountainous area, not a port -- around two hundred kilometers from Najran. He built a mosque there that still stands today; Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan later enlarged it, and Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail expanded it further, building rooms for those sitting in *itikaf*. It is a magnificent mosque. Beside it lies the grave of Sayyidna Rashid bin Isa -- the Dai who, after taking up the office, stood in his prayer-niche for six years to make up the prayers that, in the early days of the dawah, he had missed. Such was the discipline of the office. Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail later assigned a young man named Ahmad to make up four years of Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan's missed prayers, because in the last days of Husayn bin Hasan the Dai had become so transparent to the next world that he had ceased to keep his own outward count. And when Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan passed away, Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail walked from his own house barefoot to the body, a long distance -- as far, as the teacher used to say, as from this house to Grand Road station.

So the praise closes: *Sallam alayhim ajmain abad al-abadin* -- peace be upon them all, forever and ever. With these names and these lives the book is anchored. What follows is the body of the work, opened by a long invocation and a frank confession by the author of his own smallness before what he has been asked to do.

## The Author's Invocation and the Discipline of Concealment

The body of the book opens with an invocation that is at once a prayer for the Dai of the time and a prayer for the questioner who has set off the writing. *Allahumma wahfaz dhata al-dai al-jamia li-asrarihim al-mujtamaa ladayhi anwaruhum* -- O Allah, protect the being of the Dai, who is the collector of their secrets and with whom their lights are gathered. The phrasing looks forward in time. In the Dai's life, the *asrar* are the *riyah* (the spiritual currents and intentions) of the *hudud* of the dawah and of the believers; the *anwar* are their *suwarat*, the luminous forms. Neither the *riyah* nor the *suwarat* are yet "gathered" with him during his lifetime in their full sense, because the believer's form has not yet been finished, and the *riyah* begin to emerge most fully after burial -- for three days, in the manner that will be set out in detail later. So this prayer is a prayer for the *future* gathering. May the Dai's being be protected, so that when all these gather to him after the deaths of those he served, they may find their resting place.

*Wahmi hawzatahu* -- and protect his *hawzah*. A *hawzah* is a piece of ground around which a wall has been drawn, an enclosed sphere. In our context the *hawzah* is the very center of the dawah. *Min fawaran firawni al-zaman* -- from the aggression of the Pharaoh of the age. Here is a doctrine to remember: *li-kulli dain firawn* -- for every Dai there is a Pharaoh. Every era of summoning brings its own opposition. If you do not see the Pharaoh of an age, this does not mean there is no Pharaoh; it may mean that the Pharaoh is only waiting for opportunity. In the time of Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad it was once asked: there does not seem to be a Pharaoh actively opposing this dawi. It was answered: even the Dai of the other branch in that quiet era was a kind of Pharaoh in the passive sense -- one who, given the opening, would have made a claim, as Dawud ibn Qutb did in another setting. The principle holds in every cycle. Outwardly the Pharaoh may be loud or muted; the role exists.

*Wa ballighhu jamia mamoolihi ya Rahim ya Rahman* -- and convey to him all his aspirations, O Rahim, O Rahman. The doublet *Ya Rahim Ya Rahman* is a tasbih, the tasbih of the Pir. In the Quran *al-Rahman* always precedes *al-Rahim*, and in any pairing of divine names *al-Rahim* never comes first -- *Rauf al-Rahim*, *Aziz al-Rahim*, never the reverse. But in books and supplications the order may be inverted for the sake of *qafiya-paymai*, the demands of rhyme. Here, because the preceding phrase ended *firawn al-zaman*, the rhyming requirement supplies *ya Rahim ya Rahman*. The discipline of the verse-pattern overrides the usual order. (*Rahman* is also unique in another respect: it has been used by itself, where no other divine name appears alone in the Quran. *Qulidullaha awidur-Rahman* -- "Call upon Allah or call upon al-Rahman." Even though every other divine name appears only in pairs -- *al-Sami al-Alim*, *al-Ghafur al-Rahim* -- al-Rahman stands by itself.)

Surah al-Hajj, in this connection, holds a particular treasure. Verses 58 to 65 of that Surah are blessed beyond measure: eight verses called *Mafatih al-Jinan*, the Keys of the Gardens. As the heavens are eight, these eight verses are their keys; reciting them is of high merit. And within this short span, in a single gathered sentence, fifteen of the *Asma al-Husna* -- the Beautiful Names -- have been brought together: fourteen explicit names distributed across the verses, with *Ya Dhal-Jalali wal-Ikram* taken as one to make the fifteenth -- the two words counted as a single name. *Al-Rahman* also appears at the end of this same cluster, which is itself a rarity, since al-Rahman is the one divine name in the Quran that can stand alone where every other name appears only in pairs. The density of divine names in this short span is unmatched elsewhere in the Quran.

The invocation continues: *wa anir masabih dawatihi* -- illuminate the lamps of his dawah, meaning the *hudud al-dawah*; *wa kaththir adada shiatihi* -- and increase the number of his followers; *wa asri ilayna mawaddahu* -- and cause his substance (*mawad*) and confirmations (*tayidat*) to flow toward us; *wa allimna bi-lataifi barakatihi* -- and teach us with the subtle blessings; *warzuqna tahannunahu wa shafaqatahu wa afwahu wa rahmatahu* -- and grant us his kindness, compassion, forgiveness, and mercy. And then a second tasbih: *Ya Aliyyu Ya Azim, Ya Aliyyu Ya Azim* -- the tasbih recited on Wednesdays.

After this comes the announcement of the occasion. *Wa badu, fa-inna-ha waradat masail aliyatun ila al-hadrat al-samiyah min bad al-awlad* -- after this, lofty questions came to the venerable presence from some of the "children." *Hadrat al-samiyah*, the elevated presence, is the Dai -- here Sayyidna Hibatullah al-Muayyad, one of whose titles is *Alamuddin*, the banner of religion. So the command of the *Alami* -- of the Dai -- flowed to his other servant, Ali, that he should answer the questions and put the answers into writing. Sayyidna Ali, the author, says plainly that he wished to excuse himself, citing the deficiency of his own knowledge and the distance of his nature and understanding, and citing also the obstacles of the distant lands from which the questions came. But he hastened to obey and accepted the command. *Wa tadharratu ila suratihi al-nuraniyah wa awwaltu ila mawaddihi al-qudsaniyah* -- I supplicated through the Dai's luminous form (*surah nuraniyah*) and I relied upon his holy substance (*mawad qudsaniyah*). This is a striking detail. The Dai's *surah*, his form proper, is only fully made after his passing; in life he is *natiqah*, *hissiyah*, and *namiyah* -- speaking, sentient, and growing. But for our author the Dai's personality is so magnificent that even now, while the Dai still walks the earth, the author must imagine the very *natiqah* itself as the blessed *surah* -- the *surah* that will be the Dai's form on the Day of Resurrection.

*Wa wassamtu hadhihi al-risalah bi-kunuz al-hikmah* -- and I named this treatise *Kunuz al-Hikmah*. The treasures, as we have seen, are stored for the *arbab al-juzi ismah*, the duat, so that they may draw forth substance from them. The author then writes that the very first matter of the book -- that the First Intellect and the last existent thing are equal in the bewilderment before the Originator -- had not been said by any Dai before.

He closes the invocation with two passages of great practical importance. The first concerns the question of attribution. *Fa-ma kana fiha min sawab fa-mansubun ilayya ila man ajra lisani* -- whatever in this book is correct is attributed to the one who caused my tongue to flow. *Sawab* here, the author pauses to explain, is from *sad-waw-alif-ba*; it means "correctness," not *thawab* (heavenly reward). People in Hyderabad, when they accepted an invitation to a gathering, would write *sawab* -- meaning "correct, I will come" -- and many took it to mean "reward," confusing the two. *Jazakallah bil-khair was-sawab* is sometimes written; here too *sawab* means "rightness." So the author says: whatever is correct comes back to the Dai who caused my tongue to flow. *Wa ma kana fiha min khata wa billahi al-iyadh fa-mansubun ilayya, idh ana alaqtu al-jani* -- and whatever in it is in error, may I take refuge in Allah, is from me, for I am the sinner. A small note in passing: the word *jani* in Arabic means "sinner," and so the affectionate Persian-Urdu form of address *jaan* -- "my life" -- should not be lightly used. To call one's beloved or child *jani*, as is common, is in fact to call them "my sinner"; the word *jaan* should also be avoided.

The second passage concerns the discipline of who may read the book. *Wa ana akhidhu hadha al-ahd al-muakkad wa mithaquhu al-mughalladh al-mushaddad ala man waqaa hadha fi yadayhi an la yaqraa fihi wa la yataammala maanihi illa bi-fushin min huwa al-naqid wal-basir man lahu al-taqdim wal-takhir* -- I take a confirmed covenant and a strong, severe pact upon whoever this book falls into his hands: that he should not read in it or ponder its meanings except by the permission of one who is *naqid* and *basir*, one who has the authority of *taqdim* and *takhir*. *Naqid* is one who can sift out the good from the bad, who can criticize and judge what is right and what is wrong. *Basir* is the one with insight. The one who has *taqdim* and *takhir* is the one with the authority to bring forward or hold back among the ranks of the hierarchy -- the Dai, in short. Without his permission, the book is not to be opened.

*Wa illa, fa-huwa barium min al-Aliyy al-mabud wa min kaffat al-mahdudin wal-hudud* -- otherwise, he is absolved from al-Aliyy al-Mabud, the Exalted Worshipped, and from all the *mahdudin* and *hudud*. *Al-Aliyy* is one of the names of Allah. The verse means: Allah will know how to deal with him; and all the hudud are absolved of any duty toward him.

Then comes the questioner's own opening: *Wa hadha qawl al-sail hujjatuhu awradtuhu bi-fassih ruyatihi* -- this is the statement of the questioner, his argument, presented in his own clarity. The questioner prays, *hafidhallahu al-maqam al-azim wal-alam al-rabbani al-alim Alamuddin Hibatullah bin Ibrahim al-Muqrami, hafidhahullahu wa dafaa anhu al-aswa* -- may Allah protect the great station and the lordly knowing banner, Alamuddin Hibatullah bin Ibrahim al-Muqrami; may Allah protect him and ward off evils from him.

Here a question arises: *aswa*, "evils" -- does the Dai have evils that need to be warded off? Sayyidna Sijilmasi has said of himself, *khatayai idhama intalat al-jibali kal-rimali* -- "my sins, when they rise like mountains, like sands." So great? We ourselves, even when we look at our larger sins, do not count them as mountains. So what does Sijilmasi mean? The Dai's "sins" -- the *aswa* -- are in fact not transgressions of any ordinary sort. They are the good deeds he had intended to do but was prevented from doing by some obstacle in the world: a book he had meant to write but did not get to write, a noble thought he had meant to put down but did not put down, a help he had meant to extend but could not extend. These intended-but-undone goods, *they* are the "sins" the Dai laments. He counts them mountains because the intention was huge, and the action prevented. This is also the line on which to read the Quran's own surprising language. Surah al-Fath opens, *Inna fatahna laka fathan mubina, li-yaghfira laka Allahu ma taqaddama min dhanbika wa ma taakhkhara* -- "Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory, so that Allah may forgive you your former and latter sins." What were the Prophet's sins, former and latter? The Prophet's own being is pure (*bari*). The sins here are not in his proper person; they are in his *hujab*, his deputies, who rise from the *hudud al-dawah* and only gradually acquire the substance of *ismah*. They make missteps in the early period, and the verse forgives him by anticipation for their missteps. One of the missteps recorded is that a *hujab* of the Prophet was inclined toward Abu Bakr, and wanted that a portion of Ali's *wilayah* be given to him so that the system of the dawah might continue under his protection. Allah's displeasure with this is precisely what the verse *Wa in ashrakta layahbatanna amaluka* -- "if you associate, your works will surely be nullified" -- addresses. This is not an address to the Prophet about ordinary polytheism. It is an address to that *hujab* about an inclination to dilute Ali's *wilayah*: if you include anyone, even an iota, in the dawah of Ali, all your work is wiped. So what looks like a verse of warning to the Prophet is, in the inner reading, a verse of preservation -- a forgiveness for the prior and later inclinations of the *hujab*, lest the dawah itself be put at risk.

The questioner closes his preface with humility. He says that he is not asking out of pride in eloquence or sharpness of mind -- *la yujidu lahu mana*; he is asking because he cannot drink directly from the head-spring (*ras al-main*), meaning from the Dai's own presence, and so he requests answers in writing. He invokes the right of every great *wasiyy* and Imam, by every circle that has revolved, by every noble point that has come into being, *an la tahjibuni* -- that the Dai may not turn him aside on account of his master's weakness; *fa-mathaluka man yasturu ala misli* -- for your example is one who covers the faults of someone like me. *Wa idha tafaddalta an la tukhayyibni mimma ammaltu* -- and when you bestow your grace, may you not disappoint me in what I have hoped for. So the introduction closes, and the questions begin.

## The First Question: What was Before the World of Origination?

The first question is bold. The questioner says that he has searched and pondered, in the books of the *awliya Allah* and from his teachers, and has not found anything that points him to *what was before the existence of the alam al-ibda*, the World of Origination, and *what was the state of affairs* there. He has heard, from the books, that the Imam's *hijab al-khas* is from the *riyadh al-hadrah*, the gardens of the Presence -- and he wishes to know if this is right. He also asks how the existence of *the heavens and the earth* came to be, and from what remnants the first *jussa* (particle / corporeal foundation) in the lower part of the earth came into being.

Before we read the Dai's answer, a clarifying note is in order. Al-Walid Abdullah Sahib used always to say: *do not rely on the question; rely on what the Dai answers in response*. The questioner may state his concept clumsily or even wrongly. We do not learn doctrine from his concept; we learn it from the correction the Dai gives.

What concept has the questioner brought? He has placed in the prayer-bead recitation (*tasbih*) a tripartite scheme of the Imam's veils: when one says *Allahu Akbar*, that points to the *hijab al-adna*, which is also called *hijab al-tabii* (natural veil) or *hijab al-tanasuli* (procreative veil); the second portion, *Subhanallah*, points to the *hijab al-khas*, the special veil; the third portion to the *dhat-i Imam*, the very essence of the Imam. He has assumed that the *hijab al-khas* is made from the *riyadh* of the *hadarat*, and the *hijab al-tabii* from the *riyadh* of the *hujaj al-jazair*, the Proofs of the Islands; and he has assumed that the *hijab al-tabii* is what marries some of the women of the outward veil, leading to ordinary birth.

The Dai answers in three parts.

**On what was before the alam al-ibda**: *anna wujud alam al-ibda la min shayin wa la fi shayin wa la yajibu al-bahth an kayfiyyatihi* -- the existence of the World of Origination is not from a thing, and not in a thing, and inquiring about its modality is not permitted. The *Mubdi*, the Originator, brought the World of Origination into being not from any prior material and not within any prior space. Our ordinary picture -- a world inside of which intellects were placed -- is mistaken. *Alam al-ibda hum al-uqul* -- the World of Origination *is* the Intellects themselves. Mawlana Iddan Sahib used always to teach this. There is no world surrounding the Intellects separately. The Intellects, in their luminous being, are themselves what is called *alam al-ibda*.

The strict prohibition follows: *wa la sual idh laysa lil-aql fihi majal* -- one may not even ask, for the intellect has no scope in it. To speculate on the *kayfiyyah*, the modality of the *sudur*, the emanation of the *ibda*, is *dalal* (misguidance) and *ishrak* (associating partners) and *tahawwutun fi bahr al-halak* -- a plunging into the sea of destruction. We seek refuge in Allah from that. The originated Intellects themselves are at a loss to know how they came to be; they cannot peer back behind their own origin. How then could the lower forms below them, our forms, pretend to know it? *Awham* (estimations) and *damair* (inner thoughts) cannot encompass it. The only thing the gnostic needs to know is *suduruhu an al-huwiyyah al-mutaaliyah la min shayin* -- that the emanation of the World of Origination is from the Exalted Identity, not from anything. To go further is forbidden.

**On the Imam's veils and the *nasut khas***: the questioner's location of the *hijab al-tabii* in the *hujaj al-jazair* is, the Dai says plainly, an error -- *minka ghalat* -- and *man itaqada dhalika fa-qad taradda wa saqat* -- whoever believes that is rejected and has fallen. To find the truth of the *nasut khas* and the *nasut tabii al-dani*, look back to the opening preface: *jalla nasutun khas min riyadh ahlil-ikhtisas* and *wa jaala al-nasut al-dani min riyadh al-madunun fil-maqam* -- "He manifested the special nasut from the gardens of the ahl al-ikhtisas, and made the lower nasut from the gardens of those of lesser rank." The principle behind both is that *fi kulli nasutin riyadatu jami al-sairin ila al-lahut min alin wa danin* -- in every *nasut*, the *riyadat* of all those who are traveling to the *lahut* are gathered, high and low. Sayyidna Sarimuddin, Sayyidna Ibrahim ibn Muhammad ibn Fahd, has set this out in his lofty books, and other duat in our line have pointed to it in the luminous collections: when the *suwarat* and *riyadat* ascend, *the riyadat ascend within the nasut and the suwarat ascend within the lahut*, and they ascend in *parallel*. Whichever place the *riyadat* go in the *nasut*, in that very place the *suwarat* go in the *lahut*. They do not split between unrelated organs of the Imam's being.

The *hijab al-khas*, the special veil, is then defined precisely: *al-mubtana fi al-bayt al-haram* -- it is built in the Sacred House. *Huwa alladhi la taazibuhu qaraib al-aslab wal-arham wa la tahwih afniyah wa la dur wa la yaliju fi izam al-qubur* -- the special veil is not confined by the proximities of loins and wombs, nor contained by any courtyard or any house, nor does it enter into the bones of any grave. It is not born of a mother and father; its construction takes place in the Sacred House of God. It is not so contained because it is so high. By contrast, the *tabii* veil is born of a mother and father in the ordinary way.

And so the great principle that recurs again and again in this book is set down here at the start: *wal-ladhi yaqau alayhi al-mawt wal-qatl wal-fawt huwa adna min al-hujub* -- the one upon whom death, killing, and passing away occur is the lowest of the veils. *Li-yaqdiya ma alayhi wa yajib* -- so that he may discharge what is owed and obligatory. The killing that befell Imam Husayn at Karbala did not touch his real self; it touched his lowest veil. The teacher acknowledges: we do not know, from the texts, exactly which of his veils came forward at that moment to be killed -- the matter is not specified, and Hadrat Gyani Sahib *muqaddas* used to inquire whether anyone had read it anywhere; no one had. But for Rasulullah, *salawatullah alayh*, the structure of the veils at his passing is on record. The veil that received the *ghusl* was *Abdullah ibn Masud*. The veil that was buried was *Abdullah ibn Rawahah*. The veil present for *ziyarat* is *Ubayy ibn Kab*. And these *are* their own *hijabs*. Ubayy ibn Kab's station is itself enormous -- Rasulullah himself called him *ummi al-ruhaniyyah*, "my spiritual mother." Ubayy was the first teacher who taught the Prophet, before the *bathah*, the laws and principles of Adam *alayhis salam*. Five teachers in succession taught the Prophet in those years; each of them, once their teaching was complete, became *mamnun* -- under obligation -- to the Prophet, because the very content they had taught had been received from him in another order of being. The final pre-*bathah* teacher was Janab-i Khadijah. She became *mamnun* to the Prophet, and she in turn became the teacher of Janab-i Ali. For this reason it is said: *Ibn Maryam*, son of Mary, is Janab-i Ali; Maryam is Janab-i Khadijah. And for this reason Maryam herself was exempted from the five prayers -- her station as teacher of Ali made the obligation work in a different mode.

These *hijabs* themselves, of course, had to know who they were. Without *marifah*, *gnosis*, the work of being a *hijab* cannot be carried; "they knew, they were people of such station that if they were to manifest, they would themselves manifest as prophets and messengers." The transmission from the Prophet to them was heart to heart -- you will not find scenes in any of the books where the Prophet sits and teaches them their lessons. In the thirteen years of Mecca the opposition was too great; in the ten years of Medina, the wars consumed the time. But the lessons came across. There is the famous incident with Ubayy ibn Kab himself: once, when the Prophet was sitting in the *mihrab* and Ubayy was performing a supererogatory prayer, the Prophet called out, "Ya Ubayy!" Ubayy continued his prayer, and only after the second or third call cut the prayer short to come. The Prophet said: "I called you, and you did not come." Ubayy said: "Ya Rasulullah, I was praying." The Prophet said: "Who taught you this prayer?" -- making the point that the call of the Messenger is greater than the prayer itself, since the prayer is from him. From this incident the verse came: that if Allah and His Messenger call you, you should answer the Messenger. The phrasing -- *answer the Messenger* rather than the doubled *answer Allah and His Messenger* -- is itself the proof of the matter.

The Prophet was also asked once by Ubayy which is the most excellent verse of the Quran -- Ubayy being a great *qari*, a great reciter. Ubayy answered, "Allah and His Messenger know best." The Prophet said: "It is Ayat al-Kursi."

**On what was before the heavens and the earth, and the first jussa**: the Dai answers that what was there was *al-dhawat al-nadimah*, the penitent essences -- *nadin dhawat*. From them what was derived was derived, and from them was arranged the creation of the *aflak*, the celestial spheres, which are the categories of the *nadimin*, the penitent ones. There were fifteen categories of these *nadimin*. From the first category, the *aflak* were arranged. From this came the heavens and the earth.

The story of the *nadimin* -- the penitents -- is itself the story of how we, the believers, came to be where we are. The *Ashir* (the Tenth Intellect) commanded the souls in the order above: *I have made a mistake; you should also accept your mistake and repent*. The first time, they all refused: *la nasmau wa la nuti* -- we do not hear and we do not obey. A second command went out, urging them once more to repent. Some of them -- the *musirrin*, the insistent -- held to their refusal. Others, *mutahayyirin*, in confusion, did not know what to do. The Ashir removed both groups from the upper world. As they fell, the *nadimin* repented. They were all falling together; but among them, fifteen categories were formed. We believers are in the *fifteenth* category. Of that fifteenth, we are in the *tenth* division. Each category has ten divisions. The believers of the *sitr*, the era of concealment, occupy the very last of the last. From this one may gauge the descent: from such a height we have fallen.

And so a difficult and central question arises here for the believer: what is the reason for our falling? In what does our falling end, how low does it go? In one of our supplications, the daily prayer recited on the third (in *dua yazul qadr*), we name the *darkat* (the lowest levels of hell) we seek refuge from -- *atiran*, all the way to the garment of *damar*, the last garment, the last seven *darkat*, from which the lava itself bursts. That is the floor.

It is in connection with this Ashir-knowledge that the Quranic reading *dhalika taqdir al-aziz al-alim* -- "that is the determination of the Mighty, the Knowing" (Surah Yasin) -- is taken. By *al-aziz al-alim* in that verse is meant the *Ashir*, the Tenth Intellect: it is the Ashir who knows how many *mawrs* (cycles) each troop of the fallen requires for its purification, and within each *mawr* how many *dawrs* (periods), and within each *dawr* how many Imams will come. The whole map is in his mind. This is why his station, even after his own descent, is great and vast -- and the bitterness of our condition is that this Ashir, the one who now holds the map of every troop's purification, was once our companion at the higher station from which we fell.

The *nadimin* in their original error did not stop at one step. The *Ashir*, who later became the Tenth, was originally the *third* Intellect. He fell because of *wahm al-musawat*, the illusion of equality. He thought he stood at the same rank as the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*, the First Emanation. But the gaze of the *Mumbaith al-Awwal* was upon the *surah ilmiyyah*, the intellectual form, of the First Intellect; the gaze of the Ashir was upon the *surah amaliyyah*, the practical form. A difference in *nazar* (gaze) -- *nazar-i ashraf*, the nobler gaze, and *nazar-i adwan*, the lower gaze -- separates them. This he did not see. The deep principle here is one to keep: *no two persons can sit at the same rank*. Not even two Imams; not even two duat. *Imama haqqin qama aw qaada* -- "two true Imams, whether they stand or sit." Hasan and Husayn were both Imams, but their ranks differed. Sayyidna Ghulam Husayn Sahab and Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad were both Dai at one time, but their ranks differed. Two *madhuns* together -- their ranks will differ. To imagine equality where in truth there is differentiation, that is *wahm al-musawat*, and that is the deepest curse.

This is also why, when later in the believer's path he sees someone advancing very rapidly, the explanation is often that *they had been established at that rank in a previous karrah*, and now their progress only takes them from that point further. We see people leap ahead in our community, and at first it does not match our reckoning. But the elder used to say: *we do not give fursat on the basis of merit alone; sometimes we give it so that the believer may complete his hasanat* -- because the believer was already established at that rank in the previous *karrah*, and it is our duty to bring him back to it.

The disbeliever is in a different case. *Shirkun zulmun azim* -- *shirk* is a great wrong, and Allah forgives all sins but does not forgive *shirk* until the *shirk* itself is given up. Until they accept Islam, the disbelievers do not ascend through the categories at all. The *uyafa* are a peculiar case treated by Sayyidna Muhammad bin Tahir in *Anwar al-Latifah*: they are noble people who love the Ahl al-Bayt and who abide by the *shariah* and are mindful of *halal* and *haram*, whether they are non-Muslims or Muslims who have not yet entered our circle. They gradually ascend into *iman*. Salman al-Farsi was already from the *hudud* in his deeper reality, even when his outer life passed through other religions; his case is a sealed one and does not fit the general rule.

The *jussa ibdaiyyah*, the first originated particle, takes the vicegerency of the *Ashir* in a *partial* capacity. The vicegerency of the *kulli*, the universal, belongs to the *akhir mawr*, the last person of the cycle.

The *fudulat sharifah*, the noble remnants, do not exist *before* the *jussa ibdaiyyah*. The remnant exists only *after*. *Wa innama yuqal fudlah illa al-baqiyyah min al-jism bada sudur al-ruh wal-nafs* -- a remnant is properly that which remains of a body after the spirit and the soul have emanated from it. Before the *jussa*, there is no body and no remnant.

The questioner closes the first question with thanks, and the prayer of the gathering is recited.

## The Second Question: Falak al-Mustaqim and the Movements of the Believer's Substance

The second question, brought from Imam Ahmad bin Yusuf, concerns *falak al-mustaqim* -- the straight sphere, also called *falak al-muhit*, the encompassing sphere -- the highest sphere, which is *ghayr-mari*, invisible. After this sphere the *alam al-ruhani*, the spiritual world, is to begin. The questioner asks: is the light of the sun from the great light of *falak al-mustaqim*? And what becomes of *falak al-mustaqim* at the end of the *kawr*, when the *rubaataat* (the bonds that hold the celestial order in place) terminate? Where does it go? Where is its ascent? And what do the Imams attain from it?

The principle that governs every other ascending entity is that it must descend to the earth and then ascend. *Falak al-mustaqim* is unique in not following this principle. *Yasadu min mawdiihi* -- it ascends from its own place. From the Dai we receive: yes, your concept is correct. At the end-of-*kawr*, the *falak al-mustaqim* ascends straight from where it is. It enters into the tenth *arsh* that is then prepared, and it becomes an intellect *qaiman bil-fil* -- existing in actuality. Until then it had been *qaiman bil-quwwah*, existing in potentiality: from its potential station it had been giving help to others, particularly to the light of the sun, which was connected to it; from there the *aimmah* also received their helps. But in the new dispensation it stands as Universal Intellect, in actuality, in its own right.

A note made by the audience captures the underlying principle from the Quran: *yanfudhuna la tanfudhuna illa bi-sultan* -- "they pass through; you do not pass through except with an authority." Whatever ascends waits at the *barzakh-e-ashir* until the authority comes. Though the text does not say so explicitly, the same waiting applies to *falak al-mustaqim*: it waits, and then ascends.

The other part of the questioner's intuition is also confirmed: the light of the sun draws from the great light of *falak al-mustaqim*. But there is a further refinement. The light of the sun also draws *lights from the heart*. By "heart" here is meant the Baytullah that stands opposite the *taamur*, the heavenly counterpart -- the *qalb haqiqi*, the true heart, which lies beneath the outer Baytullah. The pronoun in the text is feminine -- *yuwasiluha* -- referring back to the lights of the sun. The point is that the *fi jami al-masalik*, "in all paths," at every moment, the sun's light receives a stream of lights from this hidden heart.

Now the answer moves to the believer's case and to the deeper doctrine of the Imam's soul -- which becomes the great teaching of the book.

When the Imam is to be transferred to the *dar al-naim al-muqim*, the Eternal Abode, *tahallala fil-qalb* -- he dissolves in the heart, that is, in the Baytullah. His ascent takes place from there.

When a believer dies, the same Baytullah is the conduit, though the order of events differs. His *surah* (luminous form) goes to the *hudud-e uliyyah*, the higher ranks; his *riyat* go into the *aflak*, the spheres (the sun, Jupiter, and so on). His *fuzala*, his excellences, ascend; while ascending they are called *zuubat*, and then become *zakhair*, treasures. Their ascent passes through twenty-eight stations. They pause at the *mihrab*, the prayer-niche, where in life the believer used to stand to pray -- ascending to that point of his prayer in the mosque. From the *mihrab* they pass to the center of his *jazirah* -- which, for us of these regions, is the tomb of Sayyidna Sulayman. From there the ascent continues all the way to the Baytullah, where the four pillars (the *asatir*) stand. The *asatir*, the pillars, are the *atqiyah*, the pious ones, in physical form. The believer's *fuzala* travel up to the *rukn-e-aswad*, the Black Stone corner, and from there their ascent into the upper world is completed.

Now the deeper teaching. When the Imam is about to pass, he dissolves in the *qalb*, the Baytullah, and *yasaru mawaddan wa qiwaman li-quloob al-ulama min al-hudood* -- he becomes substance (*mawad*) and strength for the hearts of the learned ones from the *hudud*. The strength of speech, the strength of benefiting others, the right decisions, the safety from sin -- all that the *hudud* receive after the Imam's passing is this *mawad* from the dissolved heart of the Imam. This is the meaning of the verse of Yusuf: *lawla an raa burhana rabbihi*, "had he not seen the proof of his Lord." The *burhan* of the Lord that protected Yusuf from Zuleikha is precisely this *mawad* received from the Imam.

When the *hudud* themselves come to pass, *ittasala dhalika al-maddah bil-qalb al-safi min al-akdar yarjiu ila aslihi* -- that substance reconnects with the pure heart, free of impurities, and returns to its origin. The *mawad* given them in life was a trust for their service; at their passing, the trust is returned. They are masters of those states, but only temporarily; the substance does not stay with them but flows back to the *qalb*.

And from the very same *qalb*, the next Imam's soul is formed. *Yakunu min mauneen nafsu imam al-sharqi wal-gharbi ma madda lahu min al-qalb wa min al-mai alladhi hawlahu wa shayin min al-bataih allati talyihi wa mimma zukhira lahu mimma inhallah min awwal al-fitrah*. The soul of the Imam of the East and the West is supplied from what comes to him out of the heart, from the water around it, from a portion of the marshes adjacent, and from what was stored for him out of what dissolved at the beginning of creation. The mention is concentrated: a stream from the heart, a stream from the water around it, a stream from the bataih (marshes), and a stream from what has been stored from the first interval -- the era of creation onwards. Sayyidna Hibatullah bin Sayyidna Ibrahim has set all this out in his treatise *Saqilat al-Quloob min al-Fikr* -- also called *al-Munawwirah lil-Basair* and *al-Musfiyah lil-Suwar* -- in the answer to its twelfth question, with the clearest of explanations and the most evident of proofs.

The Dai now sets out a *second mode* of explaining the same: *fi muqabalat al-shahadah*, in comparison to the *shahadah*. In the Imam are *nasut* and *lahut* -- the two poles of the Imam parallel the negation and affirmation of the *shahadah*. The *nasut* is the collection of *riyat*. When a believer dies, the *riyat* that emerge *immediately*, before burial, are the higher *riyat*; from these are formed the *nasut-e-zilli*, the shadow-forms, of the *nawasi*, the successors. The *riyat* that emerge in the three days *after* burial -- and only for three days -- form the *nasut-e-tabii*, the natural form. After three days, what remain are vapors only.

Here a practical guidance follows. A relative of the deceased should give charity, conduct *fatiha-khwani*, *sadaqallah*, *beeswa khwani*, hire someone to read Quran, give meals, within those three days. The *riyat* are giving notice to the family in a subtle sense: whatever you intend to do for me, do in these three days; after that, I am gone for a thousand years. Whatever you do later will be for the noble form (*surah*) and for the *fuzala*, not for the *riyat*. The Prophet's guidance to bury the dead quickly is wise on its own; but the three-day window for the family's offerings is a window not to be wasted.

The purified *riyat* that are ascending do not stop in the sky. They have a long process. They go first to the moon, then to Jupiter, then back to the sun, then back again to Jupiter; their main station is the moon. The bright shining on the thirteenth, fourteenth, and fifteenth nights is said to be from the great congregation of the *riyat* that gathers there. Then, like drops of dew, the purified *riyat* descend on uncooked fruits -- apples and things like them, things that are not cooked. The Imam and his wife eat these, and from this nourishment the *Farzand-e-Imam*, the child of the Imam, is conceived and is born. And when the child is born, his nourishment is also *riyat* -- though he appears outwardly to drink milk and later to eat ordinary food, in inner reality he is being nourished on *riyat*. Imam Jafar al-Sadiq alluded to this to his *bab al-abwab*, Mufaddal bin Umar. When Mufaddal came to him and the Imam was eating, the Imam said, "Join me." Mufaddal said, "Mawlana, I have just eaten." The Imam said: *what you ate, we do not eat. And what we eat, you have not eaten.* He meant: your food is external food; ours is *riyat*. Join me in *riyat*.

The believer too receives *riyat*. He receives them in three places. First, during *wudu*: when he says *Bismillah*, when he takes a breath, when water touches his body, when water enters the pores -- these moments transmit *fuzala* and *riyat* to him from the purified *riyat* gathered in the moon. This is why *isbagh al-wudu*, performing *wudu* in completeness, is of high merit. The atonements for major sins are several, and *isbagh al-wudu fil-burudat* or *al-sabarat* is among them. *Burudat* does not only mean external cold; it includes the coldness of disposition, the laziness that makes the limbs unwilling. *Isbagh* in *that* condition is what is meant. The believer is also instructed, after the *wudu*, to shake the water off his hands -- because from each drop shaken, an angel is created who prays for the believer's forgiveness. Reciting *salawat* and then Surah al-Qadr (*Inna anzalna*) after *wudu* makes the prayer to follow be one that is accepted.

The second place is *sahri*, the pre-dawn meal. The third is *iftar*, the breaking of the fast. The *riyat* received at these are of a high order; this is why *sahri* should be taken at the very last moment (not at two in the night), and *iftar* should be hurried (not delayed for prayers or other affairs). Hurry to receive what is being given.

The *riyat* the believer receives are not his own; he has not yet passed away. They are the purified *riyat* of the believers gathered in the moon, and from there they reach him.

It was at this point in the lesson that the well-known story of Avicenna and his student arose. The student used always to say to Avicenna -- known here as Sayyidna Ali ibn Sina, a great *dai* and *hujjat* -- that "if you were to claim prophethood, no one would deny you." Avicenna would smile and not answer. Once they were traveling in Iran during snowfall, and at the time of *maghrib* they came upon a man in the distance who was breaking the snow with his hands to gather water for his *wudu*. Avicenna turned to the student: "You always ask me why I do not claim prophethood. Here is the answer. Three hundred years have passed since the Messenger of Allah, and in his *shariah*, in his sacred self, what effect is in this man, who without caring for his own life is breaking snow to perform *wudu*?" And the night before, in a traveler's inn, Avicenna had been thirsty and had asked his student to bring him water from the pot in the veranda, and the student had said, "Mawlana, you can drink in the morning; it is cold now." So Avicenna closed: "My state is such that I could not get my own dear student to bring me water; and three hundred years after the Messenger this man breaks snow to perform *wudu*. That is the answer to your question."

The Dai now gives the comparison with the *shahadah* more strictly. *Nasut* and *lahut* are negation and affirmation. The *li-l-izzat wal-jabarut*, "to the Possessor of Might and Power," is what the *shahadah* affirms. (*Jabarut* means the powerful one, from the root *jabr*.) The Imam has three veils as fundamental veils -- though other veils take work as needed -- and these three veils are the example of the three jewels of the *shahadah* (alif, lam, ha): the *nasut-e-tabii*, *nasut-e-zilli*, and *nasut-e-khas*.

Then comes a teaching that organizes our understanding of the Imams: *wa kullu imam min mawlana Husayn jama rutub al-arbaah* -- every Imam from our master Husayn has combined the four ranks. With Hasan, there were two ranks (of Imamate and succession), and with Husayn the other two. In Hasan's time the higher two were with Hasan, and he was thus the Imam; Husayn had the two lower, and he was thus under Hasan. The same pattern had run in the past: in the progeny of Janab Ibrahim, Janab Ismail had the two higher ranks and Is'haq had the two lower, and so Ismail was the Imam of his time and Is'haq the prophet.

After Husayn the four ranks came together permanently with the Imams. But strictly speaking, *the first Imam in whom the four ranks were combined in the full sense* was Ali Zayn al-Abidin -- with Husayn the two lower ranks had still dominated over the higher, and you, even being Imam, were dominated. So Ali Zayn al-Abidin is called *Adam-e-Aal-e-Aba* -- the Adam of the family of the Prophet. Just as Adam was the first man, Ali Zayn al-Abidin is the first man of the family of the Prophet in the fully combined sense.

The Prophet, of course, had all four ranks himself. With him, however, the two higher were preserved as *amanat* (trust). After Mawlana Abd al-Muttalib, the four ranks with him were divided: he gave the two higher to Abu Talib and the two lower (since Abdullah had passed) were preserved for Rasulullah as a grandson's inheritance. Abu Talib, in turn, gave all four to Rasulullah; the two higher he gave were a trust for Ali. *Man kuntu mawlahu fa hadha aliyyun mawlahu* -- "Of whomsoever I am master, Ali is his master" -- means: the ranks Allah made me master of, Ali is now master. The transmission was a fulfillment of the trust.

The four ranks parallel the four sections of the *shahadah* -- *la, ilaha, illa, Allah*. *Wa istirahat al-maqam fis-sabi min al-aimmah* -- the seventh of the Imams is the station of rest. In him, the matters of the previous six are gathered, and so the seventh has nothing more to do. He is *jami*, the gatherer. Six Imams' work comes to rest in him. The parallel in the *shahadah* is the seven *fusul*, the seven places where the writing pen lifts off the paper as one writes the *shahadah* in the Arabic style. At those seven places the pen lifts -- and so the seventh Imam is the rest of the writing.

And finally, the Imam is *majma suwar ithna ashar jazirah bil-akhir* -- the gatherer of the forms of the twelve islands in the end. Twelve *jazair*, twelve duat, twelve noble *qalban* -- *hearts*, every *jazirah* itself being a *qalb*. The parallel is the *twelve letters* of the great *shahadah* (*la ilaha illa Allah*, counted as twelve letters). And when we sum: two (Hasan and Husayn) + three (the three veils) + four (the four ranks) + seven (the seven Imams) + twelve (the twelve islands) = twenty-eight. *Nizam al-alam al-ala sarmada* -- this is the eternal order of the higher world. The number 28 returns at every level of our doctrine.

A side-question is raised at this point about whether *falak al-muhit*, which alone ascends without descending to earth, goes through the *barzakh-e-ashir* before its ascent. The text does not say so explicitly, but the verse *yanfudhuna la tanfudhuna illa bi-sultan* deduces it; it must wait, like all ascending things, for the authority of the *ashir* before passing.

## Beneath the Bayt: The True Heart, and the Sakhrah of the Abrar

The third major teaching turns to a structural fact about the *Bayt al-Haram* itself.

*Al-qalb al-haqiqi al-mutalalil bi-nur huwa tahta hadha al-bayt al-mamur* -- the true heart, glittering with light, is beneath this Bayt al-Mamur. *Alladhi hadha lahu kal-hijab wa huwa hakhadha kal-muhtajib* -- this outer Bayt is its veil, and the inner heart is the veiled one. *Fi jami al-asbab mithlu al-nafi wal-ithbat* -- in all the means, just as in the *shahadah* there is negation and affirmation, here too there is negation and affirmation: the outer Baytullah which we circumambulate is one, and beneath it, opposite it in the *taamur*, lies the *qalb haqiqi*. The *kalimat al-shahadah* -- *La ilaha illa Allah* -- whose speaker, said sincerely, enters Paradise, is paired structurally with this Bayt: the outer Bayt and the inner heart are the two poles. The teacher recalls praying once in the basement of the Holy Mosque -- not in any basement of the Kaaba itself, but on a level beneath the Bayt, beneath its surface. As we may pray above the Bayt and it is valid, so we may pray below it and it is valid. Whoever says the *kalimah* with sincerity enters the Paradise *u'iddat li-lladhina ahsanu al-husna wa ziyadah* -- prepared for those who do the good deed and add.

What is *al-husna*, the good deed? Amir al-Muminin tells us: *al-hasanatu hubbuna wal-sayyiatu bughduna* -- the good deed is our love, the evil deed is the hatred of us. Of the Ahl al-Bayt. So those who in this *karrah* took the *ahd al-sharif*, the noble covenant, and increased in their faith, will be raised in the next *karrah* to a rank higher than the one they were at when they died. *Li-lladhina ahsanu al-husna wa ziyadah*. And whatever rank a believer dies in, it is incumbent upon the power of the Creator to bring him back to that rank in some manner in the next *karrah*. We see in our experience that some come and pass through the ranks quickly, leaping ahead -- the explanation is, again, that they were established at that rank before. The journey now is from that place onwards.

Within the *qalb* there are *thalathat tajwifat*, three cavities -- the example of the three jewels of the *shahadah*. The same triad recurs.

Now beneath the basement of the Baytullah lies the *sakhrah*, the rock, separate again. In the *sakhrah* are ten *abrar*, the righteous ones. The last among them is Nimrud. Once it was asked of Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail who the last of the ten *abrar* is, and the audience replied "Yazid" -- but the answer is no, Yazid is not even among the ten. Nimrud is the last. Why? Because he claimed *ana Rabbukum al-Ala* -- "I am your Lord, the Most High" -- the worst claim of all, the claim to be God. (The other names of the ten the teacher could not recall, but he affirmed that Nimrud is the last because of this claim.)

The twelve *jazair* themselves are twelve noble *qalban*, twelve hearts -- the example of the twelve letters of the great *shahadah*. Each *jazirah* is itself a *qalb*. *Min qawlihi al-jamil, jami dhalika thamaniah wa ishruna addan* -- the total of these is twenty-eight, the same eternal number, *li-l-diyanah wal-huda* -- for the religion and the guidance. So preserve this guarded secret.

Sayyidna Hibatullah bin Ibrahim, in the dispute with Dawud bin Fi, wrote a treatise in which he listed the *hudud* by rank. Dawud bin Fi had claimed to be of higher knowledge and rank than the Dai -- a claim he later repented of, dying as a believer, his repentance accepted at Najran though his rank kept at the level of a believer. In the rank-list Sayyidna Hibatullah Sahab placed Sayyidna Mulla Karamullah Sahab -- the *mansub* (high officer) of Hind and Sindh -- at the *twenty-eighth* position. Even being the *mansub* of two islands, twenty-seven were above him. At the *eighth* position was Sayyidna Ismail bin Hibatullah Sahab. At the *first* position was Sayyidna Ali bin Hibatullah -- the author of our book -- the one on whom the *nass sharif* had been performed. But he passed in his father's lifetime. So Sayyidna Sahab, skipping over seven, performed *nass sharif* on Sayyidna Ismail at the eighth. Such states are the truth of the dawah, and they do not always correspond to outer expectation.

The same arrangement showed itself in the time of Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan, *qaddasa Allahu ruhahu*. Once on a Friday Sayyidna Ali bin Husayn al-Mukarrami, the *sahib al-waqt*, could not come to the *majlis*. He sent his Abyssinian servant Rayhan to say, "Husayn bin Hasan will lead the prayer." There were fifty men in the gathering named Husayn bin Hasan. The people sent back to ask which Husayn bin Hasan. Sayyidna Sahab from his place said in a loud voice the lineage to ten generations -- *Husayn bin Hasan bin Abdullah bin Ahmad al-Mukarrami...* And Sayyidna Husayn bin Hasan, who was at the eighth or tenth seat from the front, walked up. Five *manasib* outranked him publicly -- the *mansub* of Yemen, of Najran himself, the father of Sayyidna Muhammad bin Jamali and Sayyidna Ahmad bin Jamali -- all of them present -- but the Dai had named Husayn bin Hasan to lead. Later, when Sayyidna Ali bin Husayn left Najran and took Sayyidna Abdullah bin Hamid with him, announcing publicly that *Husayn bin Hasan will look after the affairs here*, the listeners said this is *nass sharif*: the very fact that Abdullah bin Hamid was being taken away meant he would not be the next Dai. The principle is settled: the Dai of the three *jazair* cannot leave Yemen, the *qalb*, without leaving a *naib al-maqam*, a deputy, there.

## The Shahadah in Five Tones, and the 29 as the Breaker of Sequence

The teacher now turns to a beautiful piece of Quranic accounting that pulls together the structural number 28.

The *kalimat al-shahadah* has come in five tones across the Quran: *La ilaha illa Allah*, *La ilaha illa Hu*, *La ilaha illa Ana*, *La ilaha illa alladhi*, and *La ilaha illa Anta*. Of these, *La ilaha illa Anta* is rare -- it has come only once, in the supplication of Yunus *alayhi salam*. The rest have been repeated.

*Muhammadun Rasulullah* itself has come only once in the Quran, and that one occurrence is in Surah al-Fath, in the twenty-ninth verse. The Quranic *Muhammadun Rasulullah* opens *muhammadun rasulullah wal-ladhina maahu...* And from this the Prophet's place in the Imam-line counting from Janab Ibrahim is settled: *he is the twenty-ninth*. Janab Ali is the twenty-eighth. The 28th, in any of our sequences, is a station of great elevation -- the *jami*, the gatherer of those before. But the 29th is the breaker: he disrupts the running sequence, *entering into* the line and reordering it.

Janab Rasulullah entered into the Imam-line in just this way. After Janab Abu Talib (the Imam in his time), in the regular sequence Janab Ali should have been the Imam. But the Prophet *entered* the line as the 29th, with all four ranks combined, and Ali himself became his follower for a time. Among the *duat*, the same pattern plays again: Sayyidna Jafar is the twenty-eighth, and Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj is the twenty-ninth. Muhammad bin Faraj was originally a *dai ghayr munfarid*, non-independent, but he was established in the rank of *munfaridin*. The reason was that Sayyidna Jafar passed away during his time -- as principle would have it, the *non*-independent passes before the independent. But Sayyidna Jafar's state was so elevated that he was established in the rank of *hujjah*. A *hujjah* equals thirty *duat*. After Sayyidna Jafar, all *duat* who came were established in the rank of *hujjah*. When Sayyidna Jafar was lifted to *hujjah*, the rank of the *dai al-balagh* of the three *jazair* fell vacant -- and the *nass sharif* was performed on Muhammad bin Faraj to fill it. So *he* is the 29th in our line.

This is why the old *sahifah* -- those of Sayyidna Ghulam Sahab, of Sayyidna Farhat Ali Sahab, of Sayyidna Ziyayi Sahab Muqaddas (*Zul Maani*) -- did not have Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj in the sequence. When the new *sahifah* was being made, Muhammad bin Faraj was omitted again in keeping with the old. But Sayyidna Ali bin Husayn (then Dai), when the manuscript came into his hands for three days before printing, sent word: include him. Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj is in our line of duat, as the 29th, the breaker who entered the sequence.

The *muqattaat* of the Quran connect to this 29 by a separate route. The Quran's letters classify in several ways. *Dotted and undotted* is one classification. *Nurani* (luminous) and *zulmani* (tenebrous) is another. Across the 28 alphabet letters of the Quran the further sevens-by-sevens are: *nurani khafifah* (light luminous), *nurani thaqilah* (heavy luminous), *zulmani khafifah* (light tenebrous), *zulmani thaqilah* (heavy tenebrous). Throughout the Quran *nurani* and *zulmani* letters mingle. Even Surah al-Fatihah has one *zulmani* letter. But in one place, no *zulmani* letter has appeared at all: in the *muqattaat*, the disjoined letters at the openings of the surahs. Not one *zulmani khafifah*, not one *zulmani thaqilah*. The *muqattaat* are entirely *nurani*. Their station is therefore high indeed. For this reason the duat, in their amulets (*tawizat*) and in some supplications, have used the *muqattaat* as *wasilah*. The daily brief supplications of Imam Jafar al-Sadiq include, as the second supplication, *Kaf-Ha-Ya-Ayn-Sad* used as *wasilah*. The supplication begins *Allahumma kaffayta...* and the rest, drawing on the five letters.

The total *muqattaat* are 29. Of these, some are repeated (such as *Alif-Lam-Mim*) and some come only once (such as *Nun* and *Ya-Sin*). When the non-repeated ones are arranged, a single sentence emerges. The sentence is: *Siratu Aliyyin haqq numsikuhu* -- "the path of Ali is truth; we hold to it." No other sentence can be formed from the same letters. In the public translations of the Quran the *muqattaat* are passed over as "Allah knows best." For those outside the dawah, the meaning could not reach them: they did not stay attached to the Ahl al-Bayt. Even the Shia, who love the Ahl al-Bayt with full sincerity, do not have the knowledge that we have -- knowledge of *lahut*, *naut*, *nasut*, *nafs-i imam*. With Imam Jafar al-Sadiq the two states already showed: an outward Shia community, and an inner Shia who through the Imam's silence were being moved forward step by step. The teaching was not lost on those for whom it was meant.

## Lecture Six: The Rukn al-Asad, the Karrat of the Prophet and Ali, and the Question of Aristotle

The third question of the original treatise concerns the *Rukn al-Asad* -- the Most Fortunate Corner of the Bayt -- and the cycles. Umm al-Nada had asked Imam Zayn al-Abidin how much time had passed on the world and how much remained. The Imam said: I will tell you how much has passed but not how much remains; you do not have the standing (*istihqaq*) for that. He told her: *one mawr has passed*, and we are sitting in this *kawr* of the second *mawr*. The accounting of the *mawr* and *kawr* will be set out later in the book.

Sayyidna al-Qadi Sarim al-Din, *qaddasa Allahu nafsahu* -- that is, Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad Fad, the grandfather -- has said *anna al-rukn min fudulat Amir al-Muminin al-qadimah fi al-akwar al-ula*: the Corner is from the ancient remnants of Amir al-Muminin in the first cycles. *Rukn al-Asad* -- *as-ad*, the most fortunate -- is the corner of the Bayt in which the *Hajar al-Aswad* is set. It is formed from the *fudulat* of Amir al-Muminin gathered across the first cycles. Safi al-Din Ahmad bin Ismail set this out in two leaves in his own hand. In each of those cycles the *raja* (return) of Amir al-Muminin continued: he would be born, would pass, would return; and through the repetition his *fudulat* accumulated and formed the Rukn al-Asad.

The questioner asks: in what *kawr* did his affair manifest? How was its appearance? Does the term *Subhaniyyah* apply to him -- can we say *Subhan Allah* with reference to him in any sense? And how much of the world has passed?

A clarifying objection arose in the discussion. *Fudulat* of Amir al-Muminin -- but Amir al-Muminin and the Aimmah, in the purity of their *din*, do not even *have* the kind of *fudulat* that we have. Their excretions become *riyat* and depart at once; what is held in the grave is for three days only, then is flowing. The teacher answered: yes, exactly. *Fudulat* here does not mean the bodily remnant. *Fudulat* here means *the riyat that have progressed* -- the *purified* riyat of Amir al-Muminin that have made progress, that have come to the Baytullah and gathered. In an earlier lesson the same word was used in another precise sense: the riyat that are stored in the Baytullah are divided into five parts; one-fifth goes to the *hudud al-dawah*, helping them make correct decisions and preserving them from sin and elevating their *taqwa*. *That one-fifth* is also called *fudulat* in this technical sense, and returns to the Baytullah at the *hudud*'s passing. Here, for the Rukn al-Asad, *fudulat* is the *progressed riyat* of Amir al-Muminin, gathered at the Baytullah.

But then -- a questioner pursues -- the *Rukn al-Asad* is a material stone. How can *riyat* form a stone? The teacher answered: the *Rukn al-Asad* is not strictly speaking material. The blessing within the stone is what *Rukn al-Asad* points to; the inner reality is not the chemistry. The same with our holy stones -- *aqiq* (carnelian), *yaqut* (ruby) -- which are formed from the *fudulat* of the *Duat Kiram* in this same elevated sense.

The conception of the Imam's *nasut khas* takes place in the Bayt al-Haram, not in any womb. When a man in *tawaf* once recited the verse *wajalna lil-muttaqin imama* ("And make us imams of the pious"), the Imam present sharply rebuked him: this is not a verse to recite during the *tawaf* itself. The structure of the conception of the *nasut khas* in the Bayt is too closely tied to the verse for it to be recited there carelessly.

The Prophet himself declared *ana ibn al-karrat wa mumtahin al-awqat* -- I am the son of the recurring cycles and the one tried by the times. He had passed through *karrat*. And Allah tested the Prophet seven times before *risalat* and seven after; in the same fashion Ali was tested fourteen times. The shape of the trial recurs. Sayyidna Yusuf bin Sulayman was tested for forty days by Sayyidna Muhammad Husayn: each morning he was sent away, and only after this stretch of trial was he received. So the principle of the test runs at every level of the line.

Mawlana al-Sadiq -- Imam Jafar al-Sadiq -- said: *we were in the past cycles in our persons (ashkhas) and in our representatives (ayan)*. Some of those representatives, while different in outward dispensation, were ours. The teaching is open-ended: many figures in the past, whose outer biographies seem to come from outside our line, may in the inner reckoning have been from us. *Aristotle* (Aba Muhammad Aristatalis) -- Imam Jafar al-Sadiq said *was from us, Ahl al-Bayt*. So too was Salman al-Farsi. Salman is a case apart, the *only* person in the *Dawr al-Sitr* who became *Bab al-Abwab* for both the Nabi *and* the Wasi. His outward biography passed through other religions, but his inner reality was already with us.

The angels glorified Ali. *Sabbahatuhu al-malaikah al-qudsaniyyah* -- the holy angels yearned to see him; and Allah created Ali's likeness in the heavens, which the angels glorify night and day. The Quranic *fi al-samai ilahun wa fi al-ardi ilah* -- "in the heavens is a God and on the earth is a God" -- refers to Ali's likeness in heaven and Ali's presence on earth.

## Lecture Seven: Harun, the Mim, the Ayn, and the Confidants of Isa

The pairing of Harun and Musa is the key. Harun (peace be upon him) died in Musa's own lifetime. The Prophet said *anta minni bi-manzilat Harun min Musa* -- "you are to me as Harun was to Musa." Harun is the meaning of one *transferred in his own lifetime*. The Prophet's saying to Ali, then, contains within it the doctrine that the *awsiya* pass in the inner reality before the *natiq*. The *nur al-azim* of Ali passed in the Prophet's lifetime, while Ali's outward person continued. *Awsiya pass before their anbiya in inner reality* -- this is the structural meaning of the Harun-Musa parallel.

After this, the Mim took the successorship of the Ayn. Ali is veiled by the Mim of Maryamah; the Mim is established by the veil of the Ayn at Ghadir. The *muhtajib* of Ali -- the one veiled by him, whose own name is Ayn -- is *afdal*, more excellent, than the *muhtajib* of the Prophet. But the *hijab* of the Prophet (Ali himself) is superior to the *hijab* of Ali (the Ayn). Each direction has its own ordering.

There are three *sufara* of the Rasul, three envoy-pairs, in a *qiran* (conjunction) sequence: Abd al-Muttalib, Abu Talib (with Abdullah), and Ali (with the Rasul). And Ali is *al-awwal wal-akhir* -- the first and the last. He has gathered the past Imams from Hunayd to Hakim, and he has gathered those who would come from Hasan up to the Qaim. His gathering reaches in both directions.

Imam Jafar al-Sadiq, who in our line is the *natiq* of his own quiet revolution, manifested his *ghaybah* through his son Ismail's death. Then he said to Furat bin Ahmad: *anta ana wa ana anta* -- you are I, and I am you. When the Imam recited Surah al-Muddaththir on Furat, Furat's external garb itself changed, and the people who came in greeted him as *Imam al-Zaman*. The Imam continues through such transfers, though the outer eye does not see the transfer.

In the line of duat, in the time of Sayyidna Dawud bin Ajabshah, Lad bin Dhannu of Ahmedabad -- an Agha Khani horse-merchant -- embraced the Dawudi rite and became the dai of the Jazira of Sindh. Such transfers from outside our community to its center recur across the eras.

The seven great intellects each give material support to a paralleling *natiq* in the rank order. The third intellect supports Adam; the fourth supports Nuh; and so on, each intellect feeding its natiq. The *fifth intellect*, in this scheme, is al-Husayn ibn Ali -- the fifth from the Prophet in the *Panjatan-i Pak*. The Aimmah from Husayn's progeny are the *qudar al-Fatimiyyah*, the Fatimi powers, called Fatimi because of awe before Fatimah al-Zahra's station. Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad al-Fad in *Kanz al-Ulum* clarifies this. Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Husayn al-Hamidi wrote *Kanz al-Walad* for his son Sayyidna Hatim in the same line.

The opening word of Surah al-Fatihah, *al-hamd*, has the numerical value 52 (*ha* 8 + *mim* 40 + *dal* 4). This 52 refers to three (Muhammad, Ali, and Fatimah) plus forty-nine Imams up to Abu al-Aimmah. The very *al-hamd* of the daily prayer encodes the line.

*Wabud rabbaka hatta yatiyaka al-yaqin* -- "and worship your Lord until certainty (*yaqin*) comes to you." *Yaqin* has two meanings: it is death, and it is Amir al-Muminin, who will question you after death. Worship to be lifelong; certainty is in the meeting.

The Prophet said: the essence of *iman* is *walayah* and love for Ali. It is not prayer alone, not fasting alone, not Hajj alone, not jihad alone. *Walayah* is the essence; the rest are the limbs.

There are matters about the early caliphs that the realities make plain. *Risalah* by Nasir al-Din al-Tusi excoriates Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Muawiyah, and Yazid with the severest titles in a treatise of fifteen to twenty pages. And the Quran itself documents the line: in the cave during the *hijrah* (Surah al-Tawbah), tranquility (*sakinah*) descended on the Prophet alone, not on Abu Bakr; Shahriyar the dai proved from this verse that Abu Bakr was outside the circle of faith. After *Saqifah Bani Saidah*, the body of the Prophet was left unburied while Abu Bakr's allegiance was extracted at swordpoint by Umar. Hudhayfah al-Yamani, who had been with the Prophet on the night of the cave conspiracy when munafiqin planned the killing in a dark valley before Ghadir, later confirmed to Umar by oath that he was among the conspirators. The Prophet during his final illness repeatedly asked whether Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman had joined Usamah's expedition; they had refused. The line of refusal is a continuous one.

The confidants of the era of Isa -- six individuals -- gathered the precious treasures of Isa's teachings and joined the Prophet's circle. The connection across the bathah from the previous natiq is preserved in such transfers.

## Lecture Eight: Raja, and Return at the Same Rank

The doctrine of *raja*, return, is set out cleanly. In raja, *each returns in the same rank as he died*: Bab as Bab, Hujjah as Hujjah, Dai as Dai, Madhun as Madhun, Mukasir as Mukasir, mumin as mumin. The structure of the dawah is repeated in raja with each person taking their old place.

Those who die in childhood without having taken the *ahd al-sharif* will be born in the state of *iman* in future *karrahs*. The Quranic verse *minha khalaqnakum wa fiha nuiidukum wa minha nukhrijukum* -- "from it We created you, and into it We return you, and from it We will bring you forth" -- means in raja that one is born in the same *place*. The two prostrations in prayer correspond to *khalaqnakum* (creation) and *nuiidukum* (return). The 1000-year period of return is fixed for both mumin and non-mumin alike. So the time-cycle of raja is not negotiable.

## Lecture Nine: The Twenty-First, the Sirat of Ali, and the Discipline of Slaughter

The 21st of any month gathers inauspiciousness (*nahusat*) to itself. For this date Imam Jafar al-Sadiq composed a rare insistent supplication, an exceptional one. The 21st is also the day on which the *awsiya* of the line pass. The pattern of the calendar is no accident.

A common mistake in the recitation of *Ayat al-Kursi* should be corrected: the final phrase should be *al-Aliyya al-Azim* (with *zabar*), not *al-Aliyyu al-Azim*. The grammar matters. After the verse the seven *istighfars* are recited; the seven correspond to the seven *dawr al-sitr* cycles, ours being the seventh manifestation of the same pattern.

*Inna hadha siratu Aliyyin mustaqim* -- "Indeed, this is the straight path of Ali." Ali is the *sirat al-mustaqim* in the inner reading. The opponents of the Aimmah and their followers are gathered, in the cosmic register, in *dhanab* -- the Tail, Ketu; the opponents of the Anbiya and the Duat in *ras* -- the Head, Rahu. The souls of opponents of the Aimmah and Anbiya, when they pass, are thrown by divine providence to inauspicious peripheral places, made into animal forms or mountain-forms, with their *senses kept intact* so that they may feel the torment. They are bound but they sense.

The discipline of slaughter follows from the doctrine of the senses. In *dhabihah*, saying *Allahu Akbar* separates the *nafs al-hissiyyah*, the sensitive soul, from the animal, making its meat halal. After cooking, allow the steam to escape; the remaining *nafs al-hissiyyah* departs through the steam.

In states of punishment they neither live nor die -- *la yahya wa la yamut* (Surah al-Ala). They are killed and revived a thousand times daily; their senses are not blunted.

The form of every believer gathers at the *Bab al-Abwab*, called *bahr al-zakhar* and *fasl al-khitab*; the Imam grants this gathering to his son, and through the gathering the son becomes Imam *bil-fil*, in actuality. The Quranic warrant is the verse of Surah Yasin -- *wa kullu shayin ahsaynahu fi imamim mubeen*, "and We have enumerated everything in a clear Imam." Two precise details govern the reading. First, the word *shay* ("thing") here -- as in *inna Allaha ala kulli shayin qadir*, *inna Allaha bi-kulli shayin alim*, and *inna Allaha bi-kulli shayin muhit* -- does not mean the carpets and lamps of the room; it means the *mumin*, the form of the noble believer. Second, the verb is *ahsayna* ("We have enumerated"), not *jamana* ("We have gathered"). The choice is deliberate: in *jam* the gathered things are jumbled together and their individuality is lost; in *ihsa* each form is counted in such a way that its separate nature is preserved even within the gathering. The believers' forms at the *Bab al-Abwab* are therefore *enumerated*, not *jumbled*: their individuality is retained even as they are gathered, and so they remain separable -- as the Imams have demonstrated, manifesting in distinct forms out of what is gathered. Had the verb been *jamana*, that separation would have been impossible.

*La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah al-Aliyy al-Azim* -- the formula's *Aliyy al-Azim* is the *ayn al-aimmah*, the essence of the Imams; all the Aimmah are *hijabs* of *Aliyy al-Azim*.

And a stern principle for theology and conduct: Allah's favor was to *form the soul from the body*, the higher from the lower in its ascent. To attempt to reverse this -- to insert the soul back into the body, to claim such a return -- is *shirk zulmun azim*, an associative wrong of the gravest order. Reversal is not allowed in either doctrine or in any claim of practice.

## Lecture Ten: The Ghayah of Beings, the Believer at the Summit, and the Doctrine of Visitation

The *ghayah*, the ultimate point of perfection, differs for different orders of being. The ghayah of stones and minerals is the *yaqut*, the ruby. The ghayah of animals is the elephant -- for its intelligence above other animals. The ghayah of man is the *mumin*, the believer. The believer is the term of the human ascent.

*Awwalu man amana* -- "the first who believed" -- has the numerical value of *Ali ibn Abi Talib*. The structural pointer is again Ali.

On the night of Miraj two unique gifts were bestowed on the Prophet that he had not had before: *Surat al-Fatiha*, and the last two verses of *Surah al-Baqarah*. These were given to him alone.

The *Falak al-Muhit*, alone of all entities, ascends *directly* without touching the earth, becoming the Universal Intellect. At the end of the *kawr*, the *aflak* and *kawakib* (planets) descend; the jewels and rubies of the earth then take their *khilafat* in service for one *kawr* of Adam, performing in the higher orders what those higher orders had earlier performed.

Sayyidna Abdullah ibn Thamir was a *mubashshir al-nabi*, a giver of glad tidings of the Prophet's coming. He died at Ukhdud, Najran -- the place mentioned in Surah Buruj as the site of the trench in which the people of faith were burned. His name belongs to the pre-prophetic line of those who carried the news ahead.

The doctrine of visitation -- *ziyarat* -- is doctrinally rich. Visitation of the graves is a connection to the rank above the visited, for intercession at the *majami al-suwar*, the gathering of forms. The *hudud al-dawah* were angels *bil-quwwah* in life and become angels *bil-fil* after death. The Prophet said that parents feel joy when children visit their graves, just as they did when those children sat in their laps in life. Visitation is not symbolic; it is a passage of substance.

The Quranic *wabtaghu ilayhi al-wasilah* -- "and seek the means to Him" -- specifies the means. It means reaching Allah through the Rasul, through the Ahl al-Bayt, through the duat, *hujjat*, or Imam. It does not mean through the Quran. The Quran is the source of guidance; the *wasilah* is the living chain through which the guidance is held.

*Marifat* of the Worshipped is *marifat* of the *hudud*. The gnosis of Allah is *inside* the gnosis of the *hudud*. There is no shortcut around the ranks of the dawah.

Surah al-Fatihah has seven verses and twenty-five words. The twenty-five words equal the twenty-five Quranic prophets and correspond to the twelve duat of the *jazair* + the twelve duat of the *hadarat* + the Imam (twelve plus twelve plus one). The duat of the *jazair* pair off; for this reason the *tahajjud* prayer's *rakat* are paired. The duat of the *hadarat* are collective; for this reason *salat al-layl* has twelve consecutive *rakat*. The structures of our prayers encode the structures of our hierarchy.

Holy graves remain blessed by their *athar* (trace) even after the *fuzala* of the buried have progressed. The analogy is the empty perfume bottle in which the fragrance lingers after the contents have gone. The body's place is fragrant with the trace.

If the visitor at a grave is of higher rank than the buried, the flow of aids reverses: it descends from the visitor to the buried. The ordinary direction of intercession is from buried to visitor; in the reversed case, the visitor's higher rank causes the descent to go downward.

## Lecture Eleven: The Mina Sacrifice, the Litany of the Four, and the Doctrine of the Souls

The *dhibh al-hadi* at Mina elevates the meat to the upright lineage; the *hissiyya* of the animal is expelled by the uttering of *Bismillah*. The discipline of slaughter is part of an inner pedagogy of the soul. Slaughter by Jews and Christians using their own language is permitted (*jaiz*); their slaughter is halal for us.

*As-salatu qurbanu kulli taqiyyin* -- prayer is the sacrifice of every pious person. When the believer says *Allahu Akbar*, he says it over himself: he is the offering.

The Prophet's recommended four-phrase litany is: *Subhanallah, walhamdulillah, wa la ilaha illallah, wallahu akbar* -- with *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah* added as the fifth. Each of the four operates on a different soul-layer. *Allahu Akbar* acts on the *namiyah*, the growing soul. *Alhamdulillah* acts on the *hissiyah*, the sensitive soul. *Subhanallah* acts on the *natiqah*, the rational soul. *La ilaha illallah* is the *form* upon the *nayyirah*, the luminous soul. Each phrase is calibrated to the level at which it works.

The *natiqa* absorbs the *hissiya* throughout life, and especially at death. If the *hissiya* remains in the body at death, that is a *bad* death; if it has been absorbed into the *natiqa*, that is the noble death. The discipline of a lifetime is meant to make this absorption complete.

Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman said: *if pre-eternity had not decided that I would precede Ibrahim, I never could have preceded him*. The reference is to Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad bin Fahd. Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman performed eleven *nusus* on Ibrahim bin Muhammad bin Fahd during the latter's fourteen-year imprisonment by the Zaidi Imam. Eleven designations in succession, across the years of detention. The line was held through every condition.

The *azdad*, the enemies of the *awliya Allah*, are given forty days of respite after death to roam between heavens and earth, harming believers in subtle ways; then they are pulled into *Ras al-Zanab*, the tail node. Forty days is the window of their final mischief.

From the *fuzala* of the *hudud al-dawah* three things are formed in the world: (1) the jewels of royal crowns; (2) the pigeons of the Baytullah and of Madinah, whose own natural death is halal -- exceptional among wild birds, whose ordinary death without slaughter is haram; and (3) the salt used at meals. The last is not a flavoring only. The salt at the meal is the same trace as the pigeons of the Bayt.

Umar bin Abd al-Aziz returned *Bagh-e-Fadak* to the Ahl al-Bayt and stopped the cursing of Ali from seventy thousand pulpits. For these two acts his punishment in the next realm was shortened. The mercy follows the action.

At the *Qiyamah* of the Qayam, the opposers -- Yazid, Muawiyah, Marwan, Nimrud, Firaun -- will be dragged in human form to Mina, killed there by believers; the stench of their bodies will be carried by the wind through the four doors of *safrah* (one of which lies in Hadramaut). The justice of that day is not bloodless.

The dawah is *presented* to non-believers at the time of every death and at every change of body (*qalib*), until they reach the *garment of tar (damar)*, where the dawah is no longer offered. There is a final door, and beyond it the offer is no longer made.

A protective dua of high power: *Bismillahilladhi la yadurru maa ismihi shayun fil-ardi wa la fis-samai* recited three times in the morning and three in the evening protects the reciter from all harm. The Bismillah here is a fortification.

The meat of *Eid al-Adha* and of *aqiqah* must not be given to non-Muslims. A nursing mother in particular must not eat or drink from her child's *aqiqah*, lest the child's milk become unlawful for him. The rule guards the inner channel of nourishment.

Wild animals dying their own death remain in a fallen state until *raja*. The pigeons of Baytullah and Madinah are excepted: their natural death is halal, in the way already explained.

At the end of one *kawr*, the lava-released souls who had done some good deeds emerge before the completion of *qawr-e-azam* through volcanoes, becoming soil, vegetation, and food for further refinement. The earth is, at its eruptions, releasing such souls into the next round of their refinement.

## Lecture Twelve: Arafat, the Plot Before Ghadir, and the Two Stations of the Dai

Mawla Bibi Fatimah corresponds to Muzdalifah; Mawla Ali to Mina; Rasulullah to Jabal Arafat. These three are the *lubab*, the inner core, of the *Dawr al-Satr*. The geography of the Hajj is the geography of the line itself.

On *Yawm Arafah*, the camel of the Prophet would sit down under the weight of descending revelation. On Jabal Arafat, luminous *suwarat* manifested that had never occurred to a human heart; the intensity was the more profound because of what was unfolding regarding Ghadir Khumm.

Aisha had told her father Abu Bakr the Prophet's secret about Ali's succession. The munafiqin planned to kill the Prophet in a dark valley before Ghadir. At the valley before Ghadir, lightning flashed, prolonged, so that the Prophet and Hudhayfah al-Yamani saw the faces of the would-be killers. The eyewitness was preserved; the killing did not succeed.

Cutting trees in Arafat is forbidden because they neighbor the hidden *khamair*, the treasures of the inner reality. Whoever broadcasts the secrets of the dawah has prepared his seat in fire and is disavowed by *al-Aziz al-Ghaffar*. The discipline of concealment is enforced by the gravest threat.

Dawud bin Ajabshah's first *nass* on Sulayman bin Hasan was long, written by Dawud bin Qutub, using *twenty-eight titles* corresponding to the twenty-eight letters of the *kalimat al-shahadah*. The structure is exact. Sayyidna Muhammad bin Fad al-Alawi then established that after this *nass*, anyone reciting the *kalimah* must also bear *shahadah* for Sulayman bin Hasan. The structure of belief was widened by the structure of the *nass*.

Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan was at *maqam-e-nasut* for his Imam and at *maqam-e-lahut* for his fellow believers. Two stations in two directions, simultaneously. He was given the title *Amin al-Mutaman* by Sayyidna Dawud bin Ajabshah.

The Dai of Yemen is superior to all duat of other islands and does not lead prayer outside the three *jazair* (Yemen, Hind, Sindh). The *mansub* of Yemen (Najran) is superior to all *manasib* because he is the Dai's successor *bil-quwwah*. The hierarchy is geographic as well as functional.

When *ifadah* (giving) and *istifadah* (receiving) occur in a *majlis*, the souls of the deceased believers attend. The questioner asks through the tongue of a *hadd-i ali*. Sayyidi Fardali Sahib Muqaddas once sent a possessed Bohra woman to Ziyali Sahib Muqaddas to instruct him to conduct *asbaq* in the mosque, so that the departed *suwarat* could hear. The teaching is not only for the living in the room.

At Ali bin Sulayman's *majlis* Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan would visit invisibly; Ali would stand and greet him as one greets a present visitor. The visit was not metaphor.

Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail, while at *tawaf*, saw the souls of Fardali, Ziyali, and Ghulam Husayn doing *tawaf* at very high speed; the sight prompted him to cry out *Wa shauqah*, "Oh, the longing." And Husayn bin Ismail in Najran kissed the hand of a sudden visitor who vanished -- believed to have been Imam Ali himself, manifesting briefly. Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan also had, beyond his island form, a form in Hadramawt named *Sulayman-i Humam*, an unprecedented arrangement among duat. Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad was under house arrest in Taif and would come (in the inner ascent) to Baytullah, performing seven *tawaf* daily.

Sayyidi Raj wrote down the second *nass* of Dawud bin Ajabshah on Sulayman bin Hasan three days before his passing in 970 Hijri. Today's Imam (the 35th) is the seventh of his *haftah*, and combines two *haftahs*, possessing fourteen Imams in himself; his Dai's station rises with him.

## Lecture Fourteen: The Two Mehdis, the Believer's Sirat, and Spiritual Progress

There are two Mehdis. The first is the eleventh Imam Mehdi. The second is the fiftieth Imam Mehdi, the *Mehdi Akhir al-Zaman*, who is outside the circle of Imamate, the *Mim al-Akhirah*. They are two different personalities. The Qaim of *dawr al-sitr* was, properly speaking, called Mehdi -- and today's Imam Abul Qaim's Mehdi (the fiftieth) and the Qaim are two distinct personalities, though the Qaim of *dawr al-sitr* was called Mehdi.

Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail taught: *al-Mehdi huwal Qaim wal Qaim huwal Mehdi* -- the Mehdi is the Qaim and the Qaim is the Mehdi. The teaching holds at the level at which the two functions overlap.

The Mehdi is the Qaim of *dawr al-sitr*; he is the confluence of eight surahs corresponding to the eight angels carrying Allah's throne. The structural number is eight.

The Qaim takes the *khilafah* of the Ashir upon his departure from this abode and remains *qaim* for only three days. The duration of the office in this dispensation is short.

*Sirat mustaqim*, the straight path, refers to the line of *juz-i duat* in which one stands. The believer's *sirat mustaqim* is the chain that runs from the believer through the lower and higher *hudud* to the *Bab al-Abwab*. The path is not abstract; it is *one's own chain*.

Spiritual progress is of two types: one is striven for, and the other is obtained by sitting in someone's lap. The teaching child receives the second by transfer; the seeker earns the first by labor. Both kinds of progress are real, and a single life can carry both.

In a *kawr* there are 360 manifestations of the *walad*'s integration. The human body contains 360 *nufus*; of these, one part progresses per *kawr*. The body's structure encodes the rate.

In Surah Yasin, the Quranic *ana ala siratin mustaqim* -- "I am on a straight path" -- refers, in the inner reading, to Abu Talib (my Lord) being on the straight path of the *juzs*. The verse, on its face about the speaker, is about the chain.

## Lecture Fifteen: The Roll of the Heaven, the Cycles, and the End of the Mawr

The Quranic *yawma natwi al-samaa* -- "the day We will roll up the heaven" -- describes the end of the *kawr*, when the *ribatat* (the bonds that hold the order in place) terminate.

After each end-of-*kawr*, the earth is changed: length becomes width, north becomes south. *Kama badana awwala khalqin nuiduhu* -- "as We began the first creation, We will repeat it" -- applies, with literal reorganization.

*Dawr al-sitr* and *dawr al-kashf* succeed each other like night and day until the *Mawr al-Azam*, the Great Cycle, is completed.

One *mawr* contains 400,000 *kawr* multiplied by 400,000 *kawr*. One *mawr* has passed, and *nine* remain. The accounting Umm al-Nada was told -- one *mawr* gone -- can now be located in this larger frame.

At each end-of-*mawr*, the Ashir's place is taken by the ninth *aql*; all ascending forms gather in the domain of the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*. In the end, all are absorbed into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*; only the domain of the *Aql al-Awwal* and the *Mumbaith al-Awwal* remain. The horizon is settled; the great cycle has its term.

## The Discipline of the Adhan, and Other Practical Rulings

Within the body of the book, several rulings on practice arise from the discussion and are placed here as a single short section for clarity, since they are practical and recurrent.

The Quranic command *wa idha qarata al-Quran fastaidh billahi min al-shaytan al-rajim* -- "when you recite the Quran, seek refuge in Allah from Satan, the accursed" -- is acted on as the *taawwudh*. Why is the Book of Allah, the source of guidance, surrounded by a refuge? Because it is precisely through misinterpretation of the Quran that communities have been led astray. One dai held up this *Kitabullah* and said *this is the book of Allah by which communities have been misguided*. The refuge is therefore against the misappropriations that begin with taking up the text.

*Taawwudh* should not be done during the *adhan*; only *Bismillah* is said. And in the *adhan*, the last *La ilaha illallah* is recited softly, in a different tone from the first. The reason is the *ihtizaz* of a particular being whose departure from his whole circle will happen in the blink of an eye -- the last *mushir*, whose passing the soft tone alludes to. The tone must therefore not be elongated.

A subtlety about the closing of the *adhan*: the verse *wa qulil hamdu lillah ... wa kabbirhu takbira* (from the last verse of Surah Bani Israil, called *Ayat al-Izz*, the Verse of Honor) is what the practice of the *takbira* refers to. After this verse, *salawat* may be recited; some hold that *Bismillah* should also be said before the *takbira*. Imam-ul-Mamool, when asked about the order, said that *hamd* is the higher matter and should come first; *salawat* comes after; this is the *tartib* he gave.

The Quranic *and do not make your hand tied to your neck, nor open it completely* (Surah Bani Israil) and the parallel verse in Surah al-Furqan (*walladhina idha anfaqu lam yusrifu wa lam yaqturu wa kana bayna dhalika qawama*) give us the middle path of generosity for the ordinary believer. The Prophet's own state of giving everything in charity is not contradicted: that state belongs to the *Awliya Allah* and the *masumin*. Amir al-Muminin, when a wealthy man's two sons came -- the elder living a prosperous life, the younger imitating Amir al-Muminin's harsh asceticism -- said to the father that the younger should not take Amir al-Muminin's life as his example, that he should benefit from the blessings Allah had given. The middle path is for us.

The supplication of the Prophet -- *kullun baqiya illa hadha* -- when Aishah said *ma baqiya illa hadha* over the single leg of a goat that remained after his charity, is the famous riposte: "everything *remains* except this." The given is preserved; only what is held back is lost.

## Closing of the Book

The book closes as it began, with prayers for the duat across the ages and for the present Dai of the line. The *Fatiha* is recited; the names of the duat from the line are invoked; the Imam of the time is named with all his ranks and the prolongation of his life is asked. The author prays that whoever reads these treasures may not read them save by the permission of one who has authority of *taqdim* and *takhir*, and that the discipline of concealment may be guarded; and that whoever reads with permission may carry away what is correct, attributing the correctness to the Dai who caused the tongue to flow, and whatever error is in the book is attributed to the author himself, *idh ana alaqtu al-jani* -- for he is the sinner.

These are the *Kunooz al-Hikmah*, the stored treasures of wisdom, gathered for the masters of partial infallibility, that from them the line of summons may continue to draw forth substance and light, until the Day of Resurrection, *salam alayhim ajmain abad al-abadin*.

## Appendix to the Reading: Further Expositions Drawn from the Lectures

What follows is the same teaching set out at greater length, with the original conversational texture of the lectures preserved where it is essential to the doctrine. The aim is not to add anything that is not in the source, but to give the teachings the space they need in order to be received as the teacher unfolded them.

### On the Author's Excuse Before the Command

When Sayyidna Ali al-Shamsi accepted the command of his father to answer the questions that had arrived, he records in the introduction that his first impulse was to excuse himself. *Wa kuntu aradtu an atadhira ila mawlayya li-qusuri ilmi wa budi fitrati wa fahmi* -- "I had wanted to make an excuse to my master because of the deficiency of my knowledge and the distance of my nature and understanding." And then he adds that he intended to plead the obstacles of distant lands: the questions had come from many places, and how could he answer them all in writing? *Fa-aradtu mawania bad al-bilad* -- "I presented the obstacles of distant lands."

The teacher dwells on this excuse because it is itself a teaching about the discipline of the *dai* and the discipline of the seeker. The author -- himself in the line of *nass*, himself eventually to be designated -- does not approach this work casually. He says first that he is deficient. He says that his understanding is far from the matter. He says that he has not the means to reach all the places from which the questions have come. But he then says: *fa-asratu al-taata wa asdaqtu al-murad* -- "I hastened to obedience and I attained the goal." Once the command is given, the excuse is over; the obedience must be immediate. And the goal -- *asdaqtu al-murad*, "I attained the desired object" -- is the very fact of the obedience itself. The murad of any task given by the Dai is, in part, the obedience of the one to whom it is given. Once obedience is offered, the goal is half-reached before any word is written.

The teacher also notes a quiet, beautiful detail. The author writes that *tadharratu ila suratihi al-nuraniyah* -- "I supplicated through his luminous form." But the Dai is still living. The Dai's *surah*, his luminous form proper, is not yet made; in life he is *natiqah*, *hissiyah*, *namiyah*. So how can the author supplicate through it? The teacher answers: because the Dai's personality is so magnificent that even his *natiqah* must be treated, even now, as if it were the *surah* that will be his form on the Day of Resurrection. The Dai's living word is the *surah* before its time. This is why our reverence for the *natiqah* of the Dai is not a reverence for an ordinary spoken word; it is a reverence for the *surah* that is already speaking through it.

### On the Nature of "Sin" in the Higher Ranks

The matter of *aswa* in the prayer for the Dai opened, in the original lecture, into a deeper meditation on what "sin" means at all in our doctrine.

The teacher reminded the gathering of Sayyidna Sijilmasi's lament. Sijilmasi, whose station is high in our line, says of himself, *khatayai idhama intalat al-jibali* -- "my sins, when they rise like mountains." We hear this and pause: our sins, even when we look at them honestly, do not feel like mountains to us. We may know them to be heavy in the inner reckoning. But "mountains" -- and even "like sands" -- this is the language of one who counts his sins by a different measure altogether. So what measure is he using?

The teacher pointed to the principle: *for the Dai, the "sin" is not the transgression we know. It is the good deed that was intended but not done.* The Dai means to write a book, and the time does not come; that, for him, is a sin. The Dai means to set out a noble concept, and the occasion does not arise; that, for him, is a sin. The Dai means to help a believer in a particular way, and another thought enters his attention, the believer departs, and the help is not given; that, for him, is a sin. The Dai's intention is so great that the volume of his intended-but-undone goods accumulates, in his own honest reckoning, into mountains and sands.

And this, the teacher observed, is also the measure that ordinary believers should learn to apply to themselves. We do not lack for "sins" in the ordinary sense; we have them. But we also have intentions that we let slip away. We mean to call a relative who is unwell. We mean to give a charity that has stayed in our mind. We mean to teach our children a particular *adab*, and we do not get to it. These too are matters of the *aswa* in the deeper sense. They are not, in our case, mountains; but they are real, and the practice of acknowledging them shapes us. We learn, slowly, to count what we did not do as part of what we are responsible for.

The teacher then connected this to the verse of Surah al-Fath about the Prophet's "sins." The Prophet's own being is *bari*, pure. He has no transgressions to forgive. But the verse names *ma taqaddama min dhanbika wa ma taakhkhara* -- "what has gone before of your sins and what has come after." The forgiveness offered, in our doctrine, is to the *hujab* of the Prophet, not to the Prophet's own person. The *hujab* rise from the *hudud al-dawah*; in the early period their substance of *ismah* has not yet become complete. They are still acquiring it. They make missteps. One of those missteps the teacher noted is the inclination of the *hujab* of the Prophet toward Abu Bakr: a wish, never spoken aloud as such, that a portion of Ali's *wilayah* be given to Abu Bakr so that the system of the dawah might continue under his cover. The wish is not realized; it is only an inclination. But even at the level of inclination, the verse *Wa in ashrakta layahbatanna amaluka* -- "if you associate, your works will be nullified" -- comes down. The address of the verse is not to the Prophet's pure person; it is to that *hujab*'s inclination. The dilution of Ali's wilayah by even a portion is, in the doctrine, *shirk*. And this is forgiven in the Surah al-Fath verse by anticipation: *the prior and the latter sins are forgiven*, the *hujab*'s prior and latter inclinations.

This reading rescues the verse from a great difficulty. If the Prophet were said to have sins of his own, the doctrine of *ismah* would collapse. The verse must be read against the doctrine of the *hujab*, and when so read, it stands as a verse of preservation: a forgiveness, in advance, of the inclinations of those who rise into the position of the Prophet's deputies. The dawah continues under the cover of that forgiveness, and the *hujab*, by gradually acquiring *ismah*, become the deputies they were meant to be.

### On the Discipline of Concealment

The teacher returned, more than once, to the discipline of concealment, because it is the discipline most easily broken and most heavily punished.

The author's covenant is severe. *Wa ana akhidhu hadha al-ahd al-muakkad wa mithaquhu al-mughalladh al-mushaddad ala man waqaa hadha fi yadayhi* -- "I take this confirmed covenant and a severe, strengthened pact upon whoever this falls into his hands." The covenant is taken now, in writing, against any future reader who may come into possession of the book. The reader is bound, before he opens, not to read.

Why? Because the book is full of *haqaiq*, realities -- and the realities are not meant for every reader. The author goes through, painstakingly, the qualifications of the one whose permission alone makes reading lawful: *bi-fushin min huwa al-naqid wal-basir man lahu al-taqdim wal-takhir* -- "by the permission of one who is *naqid* and *basir*, one who has the authority of *taqdim* and *takhir*." *Naqid* sifts the good from the bad. *Basir* sees. The one who has *taqdim* and *takhir* may move the *hudud* forward and backward in the ranking. This is, plainly, the Dai. The book may be opened only by his permission.

And the punishment for unauthorized reading is severe. *Fa-huwa barium min al-Aliyy al-mabud wa min kaffat al-mahdudin wal-hudud* -- "he is absolved from al-Aliyy al-Mabud, and from all the *mahdudin* and *hudud*." Allah will know how to deal with such a one; and all the *hudud* are released from any duty toward him. This is not a small consequence. To be released from the duty of the *hudud* is to be cut off from the chain of intercession, the chain of substance, the chain of guidance. To have read without permission is to have, in effect, severed oneself from the line.

The same discipline runs through later teachings of the book. *Whoever broadcasts the secrets of the dawah has prepared his seat in fire, and is disavowed by al-Aziz al-Ghaffar.* The discipline is enforced at every level. The trees in Arafat may not be cut, because they neighbor hidden *khamair*, the treasures of the inner reality. The geography itself is laden with what cannot be openly disturbed. And the silence of Imam Jafar al-Sadiq, by which the *batini* Shia were gradually moved forward without the *zahiri* Shia knowing what was being transmitted, is itself the model of the discipline. The Imam speaks; the silence is also speech; and what is given is given only to those who are prepared to receive it.

The teacher, drawing this together, reminded the gathering that this discipline is not a defect of the doctrine. It is part of the doctrine. The *haqaiq* are not for everyone, not because the doctrine wishes to be elitist but because the *haqaiq* require a substance of preparation in the receiver. Without that substance, the *haqaiq* themselves become a destruction. The very same words that bring one to *iman* through a prepared substance can dislodge another whose substance is not prepared. The book is laid up as a treasure; the treasure has its key; the key is the permission of the Dai; without the key the treasure can also be a snare.

### On the World of Origination: The Refusal to Speculate

The teacher dwelt at length on the refusal of speculation about the *alam al-ibda*. *La min shayin wa la fi shayin* -- not from a thing, not in a thing. *La yajibu al-bahth an kayfiyyatihi* -- inquiring about its modality is not permitted. *Wa la sual idh laysa lil-aql fihi majal* -- and no question, since the intellect has no scope in it.

This is a hard saying. The intellect by its nature reaches for explanations. The intellect by its nature wants to know how. The intellect by its nature is uncomfortable with closed doors. The doctrine says: this is the one door that must remain closed. Speculation about how the Originator brought the World of Origination into being is *dalal*, misguidance; *ishrak*, the joining of partners; and *tahawwutun fi bahr al-halak*, a plunging into the sea of destruction. We do not seek to know it. We seek refuge from seeking to know it. *Naudhu billahi min dhalika*.

Why so severe? Because to imagine a *kayfiyyah* of the *ibda* is to imagine that the Originator's act fits within some prior structure that can be described. The act is precisely what does not fit. To describe it would be to attach the Originator to a frame. And to attach the Originator to a frame is, exactly, *shirk* -- the joining of something to the divine that is not the divine.

The originated Intellects themselves cannot peer back behind their own origin. They do not know how they came to be. They emerged; they have being; and behind that emergence is the silence which is also the divine *kayfiyyah*. They themselves do not look back into that silence. If the originated Intellects do not look back, how could we, the lowly forms below them, presume to look back?

The teacher closed this matter with the principle that defines the gnostic. *Yahtaj al-arif li-dhalika ala an yakuna maahu surah aw aql aw wahm qabla huduth al-ibda* -- the gnostic only needs to know, with him, that there was *no surah, no aql, no wahm before the huduth al-ibda*. That is what the gnostic must hold. The emanation is *an al-huwiyyah al-mutaaliyah la min shayin* -- from the Exalted Identity, not from anything. The gnostic does not ask "how"; he asserts "not from anything," and he stops.

This refusal is itself a positive doctrine. We are not nihilists about the *alam al-ibda*; we are reverent. We hold what we can hold and we do not push further. The discipline is what makes us gnostics rather than speculators.

### On the Imam's Soul and the Continuity of the Line

The doctrine of the Imam's soul -- formed from the *mawad* dissolved in the *qalb* of the Bayt, from the water around it, from the marshes adjacent, and from what has been stored from the first interval -- requires that we understand how the line of the Imamate persists across time.

The Imam at his passing dissolves in the *qalb*. This *qalb* is the Baytullah at the spiritual level, the *qalb haqiqi* beneath the outer Bayt. His being becomes substance for the *hudud* of the *dawah*. The *hudud* are given, during their lives, the strength of their service: strength of speech, strength of benefiting others, accuracy of decision, preservation from sin. All of this is *mawad* drawn from the dissolved Imam.

At the *hudud*'s own passing, this trust is returned. *Yarjiu ila aslihi* -- it returns to its origin. The *mawad* does not stay with them. They were stewards. The substance flows back to the *qalb*.

And the next Imam's soul is formed from this returning substance, joined to the water around the *qalb*, the marshes, and what has been stored from the beginning. This is the principle that makes the line continuous. Each Imam dissolves into the heart; each Imam's substance becomes the strength of the *hudud*; the *hudud* return what was given them at their own passing; and the new Imam's soul is shaped from what has thereby gathered. The Imamate is not interrupted by the Imam's passing. It is *carried* by the passing.

The teacher then read the verse of Yusuf in this light. *Lawla an raa burhana rabbihi* -- "had he not seen the proof of his Lord." Yusuf, in the moment of trial with Zuleikha, was protected by a *burhan*. What was that *burhan*? The teacher said: it was the *mawad* received from the Imam. Yusuf was, in the inner reality, of the *hudud*; the *mawad* of the Imam protected him at the moment when his own faculties might have failed. The *burhan* is not an abstract argument that flashed in his mind. It is the substance of the Imam, indwelling, that held him fast.

This is also why Yusuf, after the moment, refuses to credit himself with the strength. The strength was not his own. It was a strength on loan, in the technical sense. And so the verse describes him as preserved by what was lent to him, not by what was his.

### On the Three Days of the Riyat

The teacher returned, in several lectures, to the three days of the *riyat* after burial, because the practical consequences are large.

The body is buried. For three days, *riyat* emerge -- the lower *riyat*, distinct from the higher *riyat* that had emerged before burial. The higher *riyat* form the *nasut-e-zilli* of the successors. The lower *riyat*, those of the three days, form the *nasut-e-tabii*. After three days, what remain are vapors only. The grave's high traffic has ended.

This three-day window is, for the family of the deceased, a window of opportunity that the family is not always aware of. The *riyat* are giving notice: whatever you intend to do for me, do now. Whatever charity you would give, give now. Whatever readings of *Quran* you would commission, commission now. Whatever *fatiha-khwani* you would conduct, conduct now. Whatever *beeswa khwani* you would arrange, arrange now. After three days, the *riyat* are gone; whatever you do then will go to the *surah* and to the *fuzala*, not to the *riyat*. The *surah* and *fuzala* benefit also; but the *riyat* benefit is concentrated in these three days.

The Prophet's guidance to bury the dead quickly is, of course, sound on its own. But this three-day discipline is independent: it begins from burial and runs three days. The family that knows this is a family that does not waste the window.

It is also true that the higher *riyat*, emerging before burial, are *more excellent*. This is why the higher *riyat* form the more excellent veils of the successors. But this does not contradict the Prophet's guidance to bury quickly. The discipline of quick burial is for the dignity of the body and for the spiritual order; the discipline of the three-day window is for the offerings of the family.

The teacher noted that the believer himself, during his life, receives *riyat* at three principal moments. At *wudu*: when *Bismillah* is said, when breath is drawn, when water touches the skin, when water enters the pores. At *sahri*: in the food eaten at the very last moment of the night, before *fajr*. At *iftar*: in the food eaten immediately at sunset, without delay. These are the moments when the believer's substance is being added to. The advice to be punctilious about *wudu*, to delay *sahri* to its last possible moment, and to hurry *iftar*, are not arbitrary disciplines. They are precise instructions about how to receive the most of what is being given.

The angels created from the shaken drops of *wudu* are a further mark of the moment's density. Each drop is a soul who prays for the believer's forgiveness. Imagine the multitudes that gather at a careful *wudu*. The believer who shakes his hands carefully has created, by the act, an entire choir of intercessors.

The reciting of *salawat* and *Surah al-Qadr* after the *wudu* is a further tightening of the moment. The salawat anchors the consciousness in the line; the Surah of Power -- *Inna anzalna* -- carries the *Laylat al-Qadr* into the *wudu* and into the prayer that follows. The prayer offered with this preparation has been made acceptable in advance.

### On the Sin That Is Not Forgiven

The teacher returned to the matter of *shirk*, the sin that Allah does not forgive.

*Innallaha yaghfiru al-dhunuba jamian* -- "Indeed, Allah forgives all sins." This verse is famous; many believers carry it. But it is paired in the doctrine with the strict exception: *shirk* is not forgiven until it is given up.

The teacher noted that the doctrine of the *uyafa* is the doctrine of those who are slowly moving toward *iman* without yet being inside the circle. They love the Ahl al-Bayt. They follow the *shariah*. They observe *halal* and *haram*. They are *noble* people, the teacher said, whether their outward religion is Islam or something else. They ascend, gradually. Sayyidna Muhammad bin Tahir set this out in *Anwar al-Latifah*.

But the disbeliever who insists on *shirk* does not ascend. He waits. Allah's clemency for his case is not engaged until he gives up the *shirk*. Why? Because *shirk* is itself an active opposition to the order of being. It is not the absence of *iman*; it is the joining of something else to the divine, which is structurally incompatible with the divine. Until the *shirk* is dropped, no entry is possible. *Shirkun zulmun azim* -- *shirk* is a great wrong, in the literal sense of placing-out-of-place; placing what is not the divine in the position of the divine.

The teacher noted that the believer must be very careful about even subtle forms of *shirk*. The *shirk* that the verse *Wa in ashrakta layahbatanna amaluka* addresses is not the *shirk* of the idol-worshipper. It is the *shirk* of including anyone in the wilayah of Ali by even an iota. So *shirk*, in our register, can be very subtle -- it can be an inclination, an inner deflection, a granting of a small portion of devotion to one whose due that portion is not. The believer's daily prayer is, in part, a daily clearing of even these small deflections.

This is the connection between the doctrine of *shirk* and the discipline of conduct toward the *hudud*. To honor the *hudud* is to be in their chain. To divide one's honor among the *hudud* and others is, in the inner reckoning, a small *shirk*. The discipline of the line is also a discipline against the diluted attention that would otherwise creep in.

### On the Honor of the Believer

The teacher returned more than once to the doctrine that the *mumin* is the *ghayah* of man.

For other orders of being, the *ghayah* -- the term of perfection -- can be named. The stones have their *ghayah*: the *yaqut*, the ruby, with its compressed clarity. The animals have their *ghayah*: the elephant, with its intelligence that approaches the human. The earth's growths have their *ghayah*: the various fruits and grains in their full bloom. But for man, the *ghayah* is not a physical perfection. It is a state of being: the *mumin*.

The believer is the term of human ascent. The believer is what the human form is for. To be born human and not become a believer is to fail of the *ghayah*. To be born human and become a believer is to attain it. This doctrine elevates the believer enormously. The believer is not a small person in the world. The believer is the term of the world, in the human sphere.

This is also why the verse *innallaha ala kulli shayin qadir* -- "Allah is powerful over every thing" -- has, in the inner reading, the believer as its *shay*. *Allah is powerful over every believer.* This is no rhetorical flourish. It is a statement of how the believer is positioned in the divine attention. The believer is the *thing* Allah's power especially attends to. The believer is the *thing* Allah's knowledge especially encompasses. The believer is the *thing* Allah's reach especially holds.

When Sayyidna Hussain bin Ismail wrote of Sayyidna Abdullah ibn Muhammad as *afdal al-mawjudat*, "the most excellent of existing things," the puzzlement of the readers was the puzzlement of those who had not yet absorbed this doctrine. *Most excellent of existing things* -- surely this is the Imam? Surely this is the Prophet? The teacher answered: in this particular phrasing, *afdal al-mawjudat* means *afdal among the believers present in the dawah*. The believer is again at the summit of the meaning of *mawjud*. And of the believers, the one who occupies a particular elevated station is *afdal* in this sense.

Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman lived this doctrine. He stood up to receive the believer. The believer, after all, is his companion in the *Ahl al-Sharif*. The believer takes his portion; Sayyidna Ali, the *dai*, takes his portion; in the *tawzi* of the *Ahl al-Sharif*, they are fellow-portioners. Honor goes to the fellow. And so the *dai* stands.

### On the 28 as Eternal Order

The number 28 recurs at every level of the teaching, and the teacher repeatedly drew it out.

We are told: two (Hasan and Husayn) + three (the three veils, *tabii*, *zilli*, *khas*) + four (the four ranks, *nubuwwah*, *risalah*, *imamah*, *wasayat*) + seven (the seven sections of the *shahadah*) + twelve (the twelve islands, the twelve duat, the twelve hearts, paralleling the twelve letters of the great *shahadah*). The total is 28. *Hadha al-nizam al-alam al-ala sarmada* -- "this is the order of the higher world, eternally."

The first *nass* of Dawud bin Ajabshah on Sulayman bin Hasan used 28 titles, in honor of the 28 letters of the *shahadah*.

The 28 alphabet letters of the Quran classify into the seven-by-seven categories of *nurani khafifah*, *nurani thaqilah*, *zulmani khafifah*, and *zulmani thaqilah*. The *muqattaat* are entirely *nurani*; in those disjoined letters, no *zulmani* letter has appeared at all.

The 28th in any sequence is the *jami*, the gatherer of those who came before -- the station of rest. Sayyidna Jafar is the 28th of the duat and is the *jami* of his line. The 29th in any sequence is the breaker, the one who enters in and reorders. Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj is the 29th, the breaker who enters the line. The Prophet is the 29th in his Imam-line, the breaker. *Muhammadun Rasulullah* appears only once in the Quran, in verse 29 of Surah al-Fath; the number is no coincidence.

This patterning is not merely decorative. The teacher would say: when you see the number, look for the structure. When you see the structure, look for the doctrine. The doctrine is not in the number; the doctrine is in what the number points to. But the number is a reliable pointer. The pen lifts seven times in writing the *shahadah*; the seven lifts of the pen are the seven Imams' stations of rest; the seven stations of rest are the seven Imams who gather and complete their lines.

The teacher closes this with the soft reminder: do not be a hunter of numbers. Be a hunter of doctrine. The numbers will come to you as you find the doctrine. If you start with the number and chase the doctrine, you will likely end with neither. If you start with the doctrine, the numbers will arrange themselves around it; the patterning is part of the doctrine, not a separate game.

### On the Tasbih of the Believer

The four-phrase litany commended by the Prophet -- *Subhanallah, walhamdulillah, wa la ilaha illallah, wallahu akbar*, with *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah* added -- is, the teacher unfolded, the most precise of teachings about the soul's structure.

The phrases are not interchangeable. Each operates on a particular level.

*Allahu Akbar* acts on the *namiyah*, the growing soul. The growing soul is what gives us our increase in matter and form. It is what makes the child grow into the adult. It is what makes the body persist in its development. *Allahu Akbar* -- "God is greater" -- humbles this growth before the greatness that exceeds it. The growing soul is reminded, by the phrase, that whatever it grows into is small before the divine.

*Alhamdulillah* acts on the *hissiyah*, the sensitive soul. The sensitive soul is what feels, what perceives, what reacts to pleasure and pain. *Alhamdulillah* -- "all praise is for Allah" -- orients the sensations themselves toward praise. Every sensation, in the moment of its arising, can be received as a sensation for which Allah is praised. The sensitive soul that has learned this orientation does not get caught in the sensation. It opens upward.

*Subhanallah* acts on the *natiqah*, the rational soul. The rational soul is what thinks, what argues, what understands, what speaks. *Subhanallah* -- "Glory to Allah" -- transcends the rational. The rational soul learns to praise what is beyond its own reach. *Subhan* is the praise that recognizes incomparability. The rational soul that says *Subhanallah* is the rational soul that has acknowledged its own boundary.

*La ilaha illallah* is the *form* upon the *nayyirah*, the luminous soul. The luminous soul is the *form* on which *la ilaha illallah* is inscribed. This is the highest soul in the structure. The phrase is not an act of one of the three lower souls; it is the *form* of the highest soul. To say it is to recognize what the luminous soul itself is constituted by.

*La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah* is the fifth phrase, and it has its own work. It denies our own power and strength. It returns all power and strength to Allah. It is the formula of complete dependence. And we have seen that the *Aliyy al-Azim* in this formula is the *ayn al-aimmah*, the essence of the Imams. To say *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah al-Aliyy al-Azim* is to deny our own power and to attach the affirmed power to the essence of the Imams.

The discipline of saying these phrases is a discipline of working through the soul-layers in their proper order. *Allahu Akbar* humbles the growth; *Alhamdulillah* orients the sensation; *Subhanallah* transcends the reasoning; *La ilaha illallah* inscribes the form; *La hawla wa la quwwata* returns the power. A believer who learns to say these in their proper order is a believer who is being remade by the very phrases.

The teacher would also note that the *natiqa* absorbs the *hissiya* through life and especially at death. A believer who has lived in praise has been gathering the sensitive into the rational throughout his life. At death, the rational soul takes the gathered sensitive with it. The *hissiya* does not remain in the body. The death is noble. The believer who has not done this gathering, by contrast, leaves the *hissiya* in the body at death; the body retains the sensation; the death is not noble. This is why the discipline of praising is itself the discipline of dying well.

### On the Two Stations of the Dai

Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan was, the teacher said, at *maqam-e-nasut* for his Imam and at *maqam-e-lahut* for his fellow believers. Two simultaneous stations. This is not a contradiction. It is a structure.

The Dai serves the Imam. In the relation to the Imam, the Dai is *nasut* -- human, embodied, a vessel. The Imam is *lahut*; the Dai is *nasut*. The Dai is gathered into the Imam's purpose as a human form is gathered into the divine purpose.

But the Dai also serves the believers. In the relation to the believers, the Dai is *lahut*. The believers are *nasut* in relation to him. He is, for them, the divine principle of order. They look toward him as believers look toward what is higher than themselves; he gives them what they require, as the Imam gives him what he requires.

The two stations are not at war. They are simply two directions of the same Dai. To his Imam, he is the *vessel*; to his believers, he is the *principle*. To his Imam, he is the *receiver*; to his believers, he is the *giver*. The Dai's life is constituted by these two directions running through him at every moment.

This is also why the Dai must never confuse them. The Dai who takes himself to be *lahut* in relation to the Imam has fallen out of his station. The Dai who takes himself to be *nasut* in relation to the believers has also fallen out of his station. The discipline of the office is to hold both with precision: *nasut* upward, *lahut* downward.

The teacher noted that the title *Amin al-Mutaman* -- given to Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan by Sayyidna Dawud bin Ajabshah -- is one of the high titles of trust. *Amin al-Mutaman* is the trusted of the trusted. The title acknowledges that the Dai bears two trusts: the trust given to him by the Imam, and the trust given to him by the believers. He is *amin* of both; and the believers' trust in him is itself part of his being *amin* before the Imam.

### On the Visitation of Graves

The teacher returned to the doctrine of visitation in detail, because it is a practical doctrine that organizes much of the daily life of the believer.

Visitation is a connection. The visitor at the grave is not merely paying respect; he is opening a channel. The channel runs upward, from the visited to the rank above the visited, to make intercession at the *majami al-suwar*, the gathering of forms, on behalf of the visitor. The doctrine is concrete. The visitor benefits, by the visitation, through the chain that the visited can mobilize on his behalf.

The *hudud al-dawah* are *angels bil-quwwah* in life and become *angels bil-fil* after death. In life, they were angels in potentiality; their daily service was the work of angels in the inner reality. In death, the potentiality becomes actuality. They are now angels at the *majami al-suwar*. The visitor who visits them is opening a channel to those angels-in-actuality. Intercession is no metaphor; it is the work of the visited angel.

The Prophet's saying that parents feel joy when children visit their graves is, in the inner reading, the literal sense. The parents do feel the visit. The joy is not a poetic phrasing of the visit's value to the children. The joy is the parents' own response, in their angelic state, to the children's presence at the grave. It is the same joy as when the children sat in their laps in life.

Holy graves -- the graves of the *hudud*, of the *atqiyah*, of the *awliya* -- retain their blessing by their *athar*, their trace. Even after the *fuzala* of the buried have progressed beyond, the *athar* lingers. The teacher used the comparison of the empty perfume bottle: the fragrance lingers in the bottle long after the perfume is gone. The grave is the bottle of the *fuzala*; the *athar* of the *fuzala* persists. The visitor at such a grave is breathing in the *athar*; the *athar* is itself a vehicle of blessing.

There is a precise rule for the reverse case. If the visitor at a grave is of *higher* rank than the buried, the flow of aids reverses: it descends from the visitor to the buried. The ordinary direction is the buried interceding for the visitor; in the reversed case, the visitor's higher rank causes the descent to go the other way. This is why the great Dais, when they visited the graves of the lesser, brought benefit to those buried. The visit was a gift descending. And this is why visitations by figures of high station to the graves of those of lower station are themselves a kindness; the buried receive what they could not have received otherwise.

The verse *wabtaghu ilayhi al-wasilah* is the structural doctrine. *Seek the means to Him.* The means is the *wasilah*. The *wasilah* is the Rasul, the Ahl al-Bayt, the duat, the *hujjat*, and the Imam. The Quran is the source of guidance, but the *wasilah* is the chain through which the guidance is held. To approach Allah through the chain is the way; to bypass the chain is not the way. *Marifat* of the Worshipped is *marifat* of the *hudud*. The gnosis of Allah is *inside* the gnosis of the *hudud*. The believer who learns to know the *hudud* is the believer who is learning, by that very act, to know the Worshipped.

### On the Two Mehdis

The teacher set out, in lecture 14, the doctrine of the two Mehdis with care, because the matter is easily confused.

There are two Mehdis. The first is the eleventh Imam, Imam Mehdi. He is in the line of the Imams. He is the *Imam Mehdi* of the *Imams*. His office is within the circle of the Imamate.

The second is the fiftieth Imam, *Mehdi Akhir al-Zaman* -- the Mehdi of the End of Time. He is *outside* the circle of the Imamate. He is the *Mim al-Akhirah*, the last Mim. He is a different personality.

This must be held distinctly. To merge the two is to confuse the doctrine. They are two different Mehdis with two different offices.

The teacher then noted Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail's teaching: *al-Mehdi huwal Qaim wal Qaim huwal Mehdi* -- "the Mehdi is the Qaim and the Qaim is the Mehdi." This applies at the level where the two functions overlap. The Qaim of *dawr al-sitr* was, in his time, called Mehdi -- and indeed the doctrine that the Qaim of *dawr al-sitr* was called Mehdi must be understood within the doctrine of the two Mehdis. The Qaim of *dawr al-sitr* and today's Imam Abul Qaim's Mehdi (the fiftieth) are two different personalities, though the first was called Mehdi.

The Mehdi is the confluence of eight *surahs* corresponding to the eight angels who carry Allah's throne. The structural number for the Mehdi is eight. He gathers the eight as the throne is carried by the eight.

The Qaim, when he comes, takes the *khilafah* of the Ashir upon his departure from this abode. And he remains *qaim* for only three days. The duration is short in this dispensation. The work of the Qaim, on his manifestation, is concentrated into those three days.

Today's Imam, the 35th, combines two *haftahs* and possesses fourteen Imams within himself. The seventh of his *haftah*, he is also the gatherer of the prior six and of the prior six of the prior *haftah* -- fourteen in all. His Dai's station rises with him; the proximity to the Imam who carries fourteen elevates the Dai's office in turn.

### On the Great Cycle and the End

The teacher closed the cosmology with the doctrine of the *Mawr al-Azam*.

A *mawr* contains 400,000 *kawr* multiplied by 400,000 *kawr*. The number is enormous. The total time is past human comprehension. One *mawr* has passed; nine remain.

Within each *mawr*, the *dawr al-sitr* and the *dawr al-kashf* succeed each other like night and day. Eras of concealment are followed by eras of openness; eras of openness are followed by eras of concealment. The pattern of night and day is the pattern of the eras.

At each end-of-*mawr*, the place of the Ashir is taken by the ninth *aql*. The ascending forms gather in the domain of the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*. And in the end, all are absorbed into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*. Only the domain of the *Aql al-Awwal* and the *Mumbaith al-Awwal* remain.

This is the horizon. The horizon is settled. The great cycle has its term. The pattern of nights and days runs its course. At the end, the great absorption brings all that has ascended into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*; the domains of the highest emanations remain; what was below is gathered into what was above.

This is no cause for fear. It is a cause for orientation. The believer who knows this horizon is the believer whose life is rightly oriented. He does not pour his life into what will be gathered up. He pours his life into the chain through which he himself, in his small way, ascends. The chain is what carries him forward through the eras. The chain is what carries him to the *Aql al-Awwal* and the *Mumbaith al-Awwal* in the end.

### On the Practical Frame: Charity, Salt, and the Aqiqah

The teacher pointed out that the practical rulings on charity, on the meal, and on the *aqiqah* are themselves teachings.

The middle path of generosity -- not tying the hand to the neck, not opening it completely -- is the path of the ordinary believer. The Prophet's state of giving everything, to the point that a single goat-leg came home, is not the state for us. That state belongs to the *masumin*. We are commanded a *qawama*, a middle way. We give without being miserly; we hold without being extravagant. The *Ibad al-Rahman* are praised for precisely this balance.

Amir al-Muminin's instruction to the wealthy man's son is the model. The son was imitating Amir al-Muminin's asceticism; Amir al-Muminin himself sent him back to enjoy what Allah had given him. *Do not take my example as yours.* The example of the *masum* is not the example of the ordinary believer. The ordinary believer is to use what he has been given.

The salt at the meal is the *fuzala* of the *hudud al-dawah*. The pigeon of the Baytullah and Madinah, whose natural death is halal, is from the same *fuzala*. The salt and the pigeon are not mere items in the world. They are bearers of the trace. The believer who salts his food is in contact with the *hudud*; the pilgrim who sees the pigeons of the Bayt is seeing the *hudud* in their feathered form.

The *aqiqah* meat and the Eid al-Adha meat must not be given to non-Muslims. A nursing mother must not eat from her child's *aqiqah*. The disciplines are precise. The reasoning is precise: the *aqiqah* is a foundational gift for the child, and what passes through the mother becomes the milk that returns to the child. To eat the *aqiqah* is to send the child his own *aqiqah* back as milk -- and that, the doctrine says, makes the milk haram for him. The child cannot lawfully drink what is, in the inner reckoning, his own gift returning. The discipline guards the channel.

The dua *Bismillahilladhi la yadurru maa ismihi shayun fil-ardi wa la fis-samai*, recited three times in the morning and three in the evening, protects from all harm. The *Bismillah* here is the formula of protection in the most concentrated form. The believer who keeps this dua is a believer whose mornings and evenings are armored.

Wild animals dying their own death remain in the fallen state until *raja*. The slaughter discipline applies because the animal's *nafs al-hissiyyah* is to be separated from it; the natural death does not do this separation. The pigeons are excepted because they are *fuzala* of the *hudud*; their station is already different. The general rule and the exception both have their reasons in the inner doctrine.

### On Volcanoes and Souls

The teacher noted, almost as an aside but with full doctrine behind it, that at the end of one *kawr* the lava-released souls who had done some good deeds emerge before the completion of *qawr-e-azam* through volcanoes. They become soil, vegetation, and food for further refinement.

This is a strange and rich teaching. The earth's eruptions are not merely geological; they are spiritual. Souls held in the lower levels for their offenses, but who had done some good deeds, are released through volcanoes. Through the eruption they come into the world again. They become soil; they nourish vegetation; they enter food. Each cycle of soil, vegetation, food becomes a cycle of further refinement. What was held below is being moved upward, slowly, through the food chain itself.

This means that even the most ordinary act of eating is, in the inner reading, an act of participating in the refinement of souls. The believer who eats with awareness is helping souls move through their refinement. The believer who eats with carelessness is squandering the work that the volcanoes have begun.

The earth, then, is not inert. The earth is processing souls. The volcano is a release valve. The soil is a refinement medium. The vegetation is a vehicle of upward movement. The food is the point of contact between the released souls and the human bodies that will help them ascend further. This is a teaching about why the world is structured as it is. The world is not a stage for our convenience; it is a machinery of return and refinement, and we are participating in the machinery whether we recognize it or not.

### On the Visit of the Souls in the Majlis

The teacher noted that when *ifadah* and *istifadah* occur in a *majlis*, the souls of the deceased believers attend. The questioner asks through the tongue of a *hadd-i ali*. The teaching of the *majlis* is not only for the living in the room.

This is why Sayyidi Fardali Sahib Muqaddas once sent a possessed Bohra woman to Ziyali Sahib Muqaddas with an instruction: *conduct asbaq in the mosque, so that the departed suwarat may hear*. The teaching in the mosque was not for the audience of the mosque alone. The departed *suwarat* were listening too. They were as much part of the gathering as those whose bodies sat on the carpets.

This doctrine reframes the meaning of teaching. The teacher who teaches in a *majlis* is teaching to a far larger gathering than he sees. The seekers in the room are joined by the *suwarat* who have come for the same teaching. The teacher's words go to both. The teacher's discipline is therefore the discipline of one who knows his audience is invisible as well as visible.

Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail at *tawaf*, seeing the souls of Fardali, Ziyali, and Ghulam Husayn doing *tawaf* at very high speed, cried out *Wa shauqah* -- "oh, the longing." The speed of their *tawaf* was the speed of the souls who have been released from the body's drag. The *tawaf* in their state is something like flight. The longing of Husayn bin Ismail was for that lightness, for that speed, for the state where the *tawaf* is no longer slowed by the limbs.

And Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail, in Najran, kissed the hand of a sudden visitor who vanished. The visitor was believed to have been Imam Ali himself, manifesting briefly. The believer who knows this doctrine knows that such manifestations are not impossible. The Imam, in his concealment, can manifest in any place to any person whom he chooses. The kiss of the hand is the kiss of one who recognized the visitor in the moment.

Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan, with his island form, also had a form in Hadramawt named *Sulayman-i Humam* -- the unprecedented arrangement among duat. To be present in two places, with two forms, simultaneously, is not the ordinary state of a *dai*. Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan was, in this respect, an exception, and the exception itself is part of the doctrine. The line is not a uniform line; it admits of such exceptions when the office requires them.

Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad, under house arrest in Taif, would come to Baytullah in the inner ascent and perform seven *tawaf* daily. The body in Taif and the *tawaf* at the Bayt are not contradictions; the inner ascent is real, and the doctrine holds them both.

### On Today's Imam and the Combination of Haftahs

Today's Imam is the 35th. He is the seventh of his *haftah* (week of seven Imams), and he combines two *haftahs*. He possesses fourteen Imams within himself.

The doctrine of the *haftah* is the doctrine of how the Imams are grouped in their structure. Seven Imams form a *haftah*. The seventh of each *haftah* is the *jami* of his seven, as we have seen with the seven sections of the *shahadah*. The combining of two *haftahs* in today's Imam means that the 35th gathers within himself not seven but fourteen.

This raises the station of today's Imam, and with it the station of his Dai. The Dai who serves a 35th Imam carrying fourteen is serving an office that has, in his time, an unusual gathering. The Dai's own station rises with the gathering. The believers of today, then, are believers in an extraordinary moment of the line. The accumulation in the 35th is itself a gift of the era.

### On the Discipline of the Adhan and the Tone of the Last Kalimah

The teacher took some care with the adhan, because the small disciplines of the call to prayer carry weight.

*Taawwudh* is not done in the adhan. *Bismillah* is said; *Taawwudh* is not. Why? Because the adhan is itself an opening of the call; the *taawwudh* is for when the recitation of the Quran begins. The two have different functions, and they should not be mixed.

The last *La ilaha illallah* in the adhan should be said softly. The first *La ilaha illallah* is recited with emphasis; the last, with a soft tone. The reason is the *ihtizaz* of a particular being whose departure from his entire circle will happen in the blink of an eye -- the last *mushir*, the last person to depart from the circle. His passing will be instantaneous; the tone of the closing *La ilaha illallah* alludes to this instantaneity. To elongate it would be to misrepresent the *ihtizaz*. The soft tone is the right tone.

The closing verse of Surah Bani Israil -- *wa qulil hamdu lillah ... wa kabbirhu takbira* -- is called *Ayat al-Izz*, the Verse of Honor. It is the verse that anchors the *takbira* of the *adhan*. The order of *hamd*, *salawat*, and *takbira* has been clarified by Imam-ul-Mamool: *hamd* is the higher matter and should come first; *salawat* comes after; this is the *tartib*. So the discipline runs: *hamd* first, then *salawat*, then *takbira*.

Some hold that *Bismillah* should also be said before the *takbira*. The teacher noted that this is permissible; it does not have to be done, but there is no harm in doing it. The discipline is not rigid; the principle of *hamd* first is what must be preserved.

### On the Believer's Own Sirat al-Mustaqim

The teacher returned, in lecture 14, to the doctrine of *sirat al-mustaqim*, the straight path.

The path is not abstract. It is *one's own chain*. The believer's *sirat mustaqim* is the chain that runs from him through the lower *hudud* to the higher *hudud* to the *Bab al-Abwab*. The path is constituted by the line of those who carry the dawah above him.

This means that the believer's path is also his own. No two believers have exactly the same chain. The Madhun who teaches a believer is the immediate *hudud* above him; the Dai is the next level; the *hujjah* above the Dai is the next; and so on, until the chain reaches the *Bab al-Abwab*. Each believer's chain is constituted by the specific persons through whom his dawah-life passes.

To stand in the *sirat mustaqim* is to stand in this chain. To leave the chain is to leave the *sirat*. The verse *Ihdina al-sirat al-mustaqim*, recited in every prayer, is the believer's request that his own chain be preserved -- that the *hudud* above him remain firm, that his connection to them remain intact, that the line through which his dawah-life is carried not be broken.

This is also why the *Quranic ana ala siratin mustaqim* of Surah Yasin -- the verse where the speaker affirms *I am on a straight path* -- refers, in the inner reading, to Abu Talib being on the straight path of the *juzs*. Abu Talib, the *jadd*, the grandfather, of the line through Ali, was on the *sirat* that he himself, in his time, constituted for those below him. The verse is, in the outer reading, the speaker's affirmation; in the inner reading, it is the affirmation of every grandfather of every line, that his own *sirat* of the *juzs* is straight.

### On the 360 of the Kawr

The teacher noted in passing that in a *kawr* there are 360 manifestations of the *walad*'s integration. The human body contains 360 *nufus*; of these, one part progresses per *kawr*.

This is a doctrine about the rate of progress. The body is not a single soul; it is 360 *nufus* gathered. Across a *kawr*, one of these *nufus* makes its progress. The rate is slow; the rate is precise; and the rate is built into the body's very structure.

The believer who knows this is a believer who is patient with himself. He is not surprised that progress is slow; he knows the rate. He is also not despairing; he knows that the rate is real. One part per *kawr* is movement. Across the *kawrs* that lie ahead, the 360 *nufus* will all make their progress in turn. The body is being processed.

This also gives the doctrine of the *karrahs* its place. The 1000-year period of return is fixed for both *mumin* and non-*mumin*. The believer dies in his rank; he returns 1000 years later, in the same place, at the same rank or above, depending on his *ahd* and his *ziyadah*. The 360 *nufus* of the body are working through their progress at the rate of one per *kawr*. The eras are long, but they are not unbounded. At the end of the *Mawr al-Azam*, the absorption into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal* is the term.

### Closing Reflection

The teacher closed many of the lectures with a soft phrase of orientation: *yes, these are great matters; let us preserve them; let us not broadcast them; let us let our hearts be opened to them only as far as the discipline permits*. The *Kunooz al-Hikmah* is a book that opens slowly to its readers. It does not yield itself to one quick reading. Each return brings out a layer that was not visible before.

The 28 returns. The chain returns. The Imam dissolving in the *qalb* returns. The *fudulat* gathering in the Rukn al-Asad returns. The Mehdi and the Qaim, the two Mehdis, the *jami* and the breaker, the seven and the twelve, the four ranks and the three veils, all return -- because the doctrine is one doctrine, and each lecture circles back to the same patterns from a different direction.

The author has written one book in his life. He has stored these treasures for the masters of partial infallibility, for the duat who carry the call in the era of concealment. We, the believers, may also draw substance from this storehouse, with permission, and with the discipline of concealment that the book itself requires. What we draw, may we hold; what we hold, may we transmit, by permission, to those who can hold; and may the chain through which we hold be preserved by the prayers of the Dai of our time and the Imam of our time, *salawat allah alayhim ajmain*.

*Salam alayhim ajmain abad al-abadin.*

## Further Lectures: The Doctrine in Greater Depth

### Lecture 6, Continued: The Cycles, the Karrat, and the Sons of the Cycles

The Prophet's declaration *ana ibn al-karrat wa mumtahin al-awqat* -- "I am the son of the recurring cycles and the one tried by the times" -- opens a doctrine that runs through the entire book and reaches its fullest expression in the cosmology of the *mawr*.

To be *ibn al-karrat* is not a metaphor. The Prophet, in the inner reality, has passed through cycles. *Karrah* after *karrah*, his being has gathered itself. The forty figures who came before the *bathah* in his outer biography -- his ancestors -- are themselves arrangements of *karrat*; his life, when finally manifest in the Hijaz, is the manifestation of what was prepared across cycles. He is the son of those cycles. His being is what they were directed toward.

To be *mumtahin al-awqat* is to be tested by the times. *Imtihan* in the language of our doctrine is not a passing examination; it is a thorough sifting. The times have tested the Prophet. They have stripped him of comfort; they have set him among hostile peoples; they have raised his nearest enemies in his own town; they have surrounded him with those who would betray him at every turn; and through all of this his *ismah* has held. The seven tests before *risalat* and the seven after, the fourteen tests of Ali in parallel, the forty days of Sayyidna Yusuf bin Sulayman -- these are not catalog entries. They are the proper biography of the office. The office of *natiq*, of *wasi*, of *dai* is constituted by being tried.

The teacher would say: a believer who feels his trials must remember that he is being shaped by them in the only way the office below the Prophet can be shaped. The trial is not a side-effect of the office; it is the office itself, in the small share that the believer carries. We are not *masum*, but our small *imtihans* are the discipline of our small share of the office. To resent the trial is to resent the share. To bear the trial is to grow into the share.

The matter of Mawlana al-Sadiq's saying is one that bears repeating. *Imam Jafar al-Sadiq said: we were in the past cycles in our persons and in our representatives.* The two modes of presence are kept distinct. *Ashkhas* is the mode of personal presence: the very person of the Imam, the very person of the Dai, the very person of the *hudud*. *Ayan* is the mode of representational presence: the eyes, the agents, the figures who, in any given era, stand for the line even if they are not the line's central persons.

So Aristotle is from Ahl al-Bayt. The teacher allowed the strangeness of this statement to sit. Aristotle, the Greek philosopher whose works have come down to us through translations, *was from us, Ahl al-Bayt.* Imam Jafar al-Sadiq is the authority. In what sense? Not in the sense of *ashkhas*; not in the sense that Aristotle was, biographically, one of the Imams or duat. But in the sense of *ayan*: Aristotle, in his cycle, was a representational presence of our line, carrying our work into the philosophical tradition of his time. He was *aba Muhammad Aristatalis* -- the father of Muhammad's Aristotle, the Aristotle whose work would, centuries later, be received into our tradition with the recognition that he had been one of our representatives.

Salman al-Farsi is the parallel case. Salman is not, in his biography, an Arab. He is from Persia; he has passed through the religions of his time. But in the inner reality he was already from the *hudud*. Sayyidna Salman is the only person in the *Dawr al-Sitr* who became *Bab al-Abwab* for both the Nabi and the Wasi. His double *Bab al-Abwab* is the mark of his exception. His outward biography is not the measure of his inner reality.

This doctrine of *ashkhas* and *ayan* is liberating, when it is rightly understood. It says: do not be quick to draw the line of the *hudud* tightly around the persons you can name. The line of *hudud* extends, in the cycle behind us, into figures whose names we do not know and whose work we have not catalogued. Their presence among us, as *ayan*, was preparatory. They were doing the work of the line in their own corner.

The angels glorified Ali. *Sabbahatuhu al-malaikah al-qudsaniyyah* -- the holy angels yearned to see him. Allah created his likeness in the heavens, and the angels glorify that likeness night and day. The doctrine is that the angels' object of glorification is Ali. The verse *fi al-samai ilahun wa fi al-ardi ilah* -- "in the heavens is a God and on the earth is a God" -- is read accordingly: Ali's likeness is in the heavens, and Ali's presence is on the earth. The angels do not glorify in the abstract; they glorify what is there. What is there is Ali's likeness.

This too is a teaching about how we should read the verses of the Quran. The verse, on its face, may seem to describe two divinities. But the verse names what is in the heavens and what is on the earth; what is in the heavens is Ali's likeness; what is on the earth is Ali. The two are one; the verse is rescued from any double-divinity by the inner reading.

### Lecture 7, Continued: The Cave, the Sakinah, and the Continuous Refusal

The teacher dwelt on the cave verse of Surah al-Tawbah at length, because the verse, rightly read, is a verse of the line's first separation.

The verse describes the moment in the cave during the *hijrah*. *Sakinah* descends. *Sakinah* is tranquility, the divine calm that settles on the one whom it visits. The verse says *sakinah* descended on the Prophet. It does not say *sakinah* descended on Abu Bakr.

The teacher pointed out that the verse, in the literalism of the Arabic, is precise. The pronoun for *sakinah* falls on the Prophet. Abu Bakr is mentioned as present in the cave; the *sakinah* is mentioned as descending; the two are not joined. The descent is on the Prophet alone. Shahriyar the dai built from this verse a precise argument: *sakinah* is the mark of being inside the circle of faith; *sakinah* did not descend on Abu Bakr; therefore Abu Bakr is outside the circle.

This is not a polemical argument. It is a verse-based argument, drawn from the verse's own pronoun. The verse is its own witness. Whoever reads carefully reads what the verse says: the *sakinah* descended on him, and not on the one with him.

After *Saqifah Bani Saidah*, the verse meets its outer counterpart in history. The body of the Prophet was left unburied while the contest for the caliphate was being settled. Abu Bakr's allegiance was extracted at swordpoint by Umar -- at swordpoint, in the literal sense; the matter is not glossed in the doctrine. The body of the Prophet was the body of the *natiq*; the body of Ali was the body of his *wasi*; and the body of the Prophet was being left unburied while the *wasi*'s allegiance was being demanded.

This is the deepest scandal of the early caliphate. The body of the *natiq* in the open while the *wasi* is being approached for an allegiance he did not give. The verse of the cave and the scene of the *Saqifah* are not separate matters; the *sakinah* that did not descend in the cave is the *sakinah* that did not descend at the *Saqifah*. The continuity is preserved.

Hudhayfah al-Yamani is the witness. Hudhayfah was with the Prophet on the night of the cave conspiracy, when munafiqin had planned the killing in a dark valley before Ghadir. The lightning that flashed prolonged so that the Prophet and Hudhayfah saw the would-be killers' faces was a moment of preservation; the killing did not succeed. Later, when Umar pressed Hudhayfah about the conspirators' identities, Hudhayfah confirmed by oath that he was among them. The oath is the second moment of preservation. Hudhayfah preserved the testimony by the oath.

The Prophet's continuous refusal of Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman in his final illness is the third moment. The Prophet, in his last days, repeatedly asked whether these three had joined Usamah's expedition. The expedition had been ordered; they had refused. The Prophet's repeated asking is the repeated marking of the refusal. They were not where they had been told to be; they were positioned, instead, near the body and near the *Saqifah*. The Prophet's asking is the asking of one who saw the positioning.

These are not pleasant matters. The teacher allowed that they are not. But the doctrine does not gloss them. The verse of Surah al-Fath, the verses of the cave, the verses of the *Saqifah* indirectly -- all of these run in the same line. The line is the line of the refusal of the *wilayah*. Once the *wilayah* of Ali is refused, the rest follows. The doctrine of our line is that this refusal is the line's first separation; the line of the *Aimmah Tahirin* and the *duat* continues from that separation, on the side of those who did not refuse.

The Risalah of Nasir al-Din al-Tusi -- the fifteen-to-twenty-page treatise that excoriates Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Muawiyah, and Yazid with the severest titles -- is in this line. The teacher noted that the treatise's severity is striking even for those who know the doctrine. But the severity is in proportion to the refusal that the treatise records. The titles are not gratuitous; they are the verbal counterpart of the verses of the cave and the *Saqifah*.

The confidants of Isa -- the six individuals who gathered the precious treasures of Isa's teachings and joined the Prophet's circle -- are the bridging figures. They are the figures who carry the previous *natiq*'s treasures forward into the next *natiq*'s circle. Without them, the inheritance of Isa would not have been received. With them, the inheritance was carried across the bathah from Isa to Muhammad. The six are unnamed in the teaching here; the doctrine names them only as the six. Their role is the role of the bridging figures of every era of the *natiq*'s passing.

### Lecture 8, Continued: The Precision of the Raja

The teacher spent some care on the doctrine of *raja*, because the precision of the doctrine is easily missed.

The headline of the doctrine is: in *raja*, each returns in the same rank as he died. *Bab as Bab, Hujjah as Hujjah, Dai as Dai, Madhun as Madhun, Mukasir as Mukasir, mumin as mumin.* The ranks are preserved.

This means that the *raja* is not a great mixing. It is not as if the souls return in a general way and find their new ranks by some second judgment. They return in the ranks they had earned. The line of the dawah, in *raja*, reproduces itself from the inside out. The *Bab* is again *Bab*; the *Hujjah* is again *Hujjah*; the believer is again believer. The structure that obtained at the end of the cycle obtains again at the beginning of the next.

The further teaching is on those who die in childhood without having taken the *ahd al-sharif*. These will be born in the state of *iman* in future *karrahs*. They are not lost. They did not have time to take the *ahd*; they had not yet been formed into the rank that would have been their own. But the *karrah* gives them what time had not given them; they are born, in the next *karrah*, in the state of *iman*. The mercy of the cycle catches up with them.

The verse *minha khalaqnakum wa fiha nuiidukum wa minha nukhrijukum* is the structural verse of the *raja*. *From it we created you, into it we return you, from it we will bring you forth.* In the inner reading, this verse is about the *raja*. The *minha* is the earth, but more precisely the *place* in which the believer was. He was created from there. He will be returned to there. He will be brought forth from there. The *place* is preserved across the *karrah*; the believer is reborn in the same *place*. Continuity is geographic as well as personal.

The two prostrations in prayer correspond to *khalaqnakum* (creation) and *nuiidukum* (return). The first sajdah is the bowing of the creature into his being-created; the second sajdah is the bowing of the creature into his being-returned. Each prayer enacts, on a small scale, the cycle of the *karrah*. The believer who prays with this awareness is participating, in each prayer, in the cycle that his life inhabits.

The 1000-year period of return is fixed for both *mumin* and non-*mumin*. The fixity is structural. The believer who returns in 1000 years is not in a special category; he is in the general category. The non-believer who returns in 1000 years is also in the general category. The cycle of 1000 years is the cycle of the *raja*; both sides of the rank-line are inside the same cycle.

This means that the wait, for the believer, is not endless. The believer who dies today will be back. The wait is 1000 years. In the believer's own consciousness across the *raja*, the wait may or may not be felt; the doctrine does not specify the subjective experience of the interval. But the objective interval is the same for all.

### Lecture 9, Continued: The Twenty-First, the Senses, and the Slaughtered

The 21st of any month gathers inauspiciousness. The teacher noted that Imam Jafar al-Sadiq composed a rare insistent supplication for this date. The supplication is one of the *qaha'iz* of the daily life of the *dawah*; it is recited on the 21st of each month with an intensity that does not come into the other days.

Why the 21st? Because the 21st is also the day on which the *awsiya* of the line pass. The teacher allowed that this is not a coincidence. The *awsiya* are the bearers of the line; their passing concentrates an inauspicious weight that is then carried across the calendar by association. The 21st is therefore the day on which to push back the gathered weight, with a supplication that meets it directly.

The grammatical correction in *Ayat al-Kursi* -- *al-Aliyya al-Azim* with *zabar*, not *al-Aliyyu al-Azim* -- is a small point but a precise one. The grammar of the verse fixes the case. The believer who recites with the wrong case is reciting a recitation that is not, in the literal grammar, the verse. The correction is therefore essential.

The seven *istighfars* after *Ayat al-Kursi* correspond to the seven cycles of *dawr al-sitr*. Our cycle is the seventh. The seven *istighfars* are, in the inner reading, the seven cycles of the line's concealment. The believer who recites them is acknowledging the seven, and is also acknowledging that his own *iman* is in the seventh of the seven. The *istighfar* is not only a personal asking-for-forgiveness; it is a structural recitation of the line's cycles.

The verse *Inna hadha siratu Aliyyin mustaqim* is the verse-form of Ali's being the path. The Quran's outward reading places this verse on the path of the speaker; the inner reading places it on Ali. The *sirat al-mustaqim* is, again, *one's own chain*; and the chain runs through Ali at its origin and at its term.

The cosmology of the *azdad*, the enemies, is precise. The opponents of the *Aimmah* and their followers are gathered in *dhanab* -- the Tail, Ketu. The opponents of the *Anbiya* and the *Duat* are gathered in *ras* -- the Head, Rahu. The two cosmic nodes of *Rahu* and *Ketu*, known to the astrologers, are in our doctrine the gatherings of two categories of enemies. *Rahu*, the Head, gathers the opponents of the *Anbiya* and *Duat*; *Ketu*, the Tail, gathers the opponents of the *Aimmah* and their followers. The astrological inauspiciousness of these nodes is, in the inner reading, the gathered weight of the opposed souls.

The souls of opponents of the *Aimmah* and the *Anbiya*, when they pass, are thrown by divine providence to inauspicious peripheral places. They are made into animal forms, into mountain-forms, with their senses kept intact for torment. This is a teaching about the punishment in the next world. The opponents are not simply removed; they are kept in forms in which the senses register the torment. The mountain that endures the lightning over millennia, the wild animal that runs from predators, the stone that suffers the heat of the sun -- all of these may be forms of opponents whose senses are kept intact.

This connects to the discipline of slaughter. In *dhabihah*, saying *Allahu Akbar* separates the *nafs al-hissiyyah*, the sensitive soul, from the animal. The *Allahu Akbar* is not a ritual flourish; it is the operative phrase by which the separation occurs. Without it, the *nafs al-hissiyyah* remains in the animal; the meat would then carry the sensitive soul into the eater. With it, the *nafs* is detached; the meat is halal.

After cooking, the steam is to be allowed to escape. The remaining *nafs al-hissiyyah* -- what had not been fully detached at slaughter -- departs through the steam. The believer who covers the pot tightly and traps the steam is, in the inner reading, trapping the remaining *nafs* in the food. The discipline of letting the steam go is not culinary; it is spiritual.

The verse *la yahya wa la yamut* -- "they neither live nor die" (Surah al-Ala) -- describes the state of the punished in the afterlife. They are killed and revived a thousand times daily; their senses do not blunt. The cycle of killing-and-reviving is the structure of the punishment. It is severe; the doctrine does not soften it.

The doctrine of the *Bab al-Abwab* is the doctrine of where the believers' forms gather. The *Bab al-Abwab* is also called *bahr al-zakhar*, the swelling sea, and *fasl al-khitab*, the decisive speech. It is the great gathering of forms; the Imam grants this gathering to his son, and the son thereby becomes Imam *bil-fil*, in actuality. The gathering at the *Bab al-Abwab* is the constitution of the Imam's office in the next generation. The *Bab al-Abwab* is, in this sense, the formal mechanism by which the Imamate is transmitted.

The formula *La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah al-Aliyy al-Azim* has, again, the *Aliyy al-Azim* as the *ayn al-aimmah*. To say the formula is to acknowledge that all the Aimmah are *hijabs* of the *Aliyy al-Azim*. The formula is not a generic invocation of divine help; it is the structural acknowledgment of the *ayn* veiled by every Imam.

And the principle on the soul and the body: Allah's favor was to *form the soul from the body* in the ascent. The lower is formed first; the higher comes out of the lower. To insert the soul back into the body -- to claim, in any doctrine of return, that the soul comes back into the same body -- is to reverse the favor. The reversal is *shirk zulmun azim*, an associative wrong of the gravest order. The doctrine of the *raja* does not require this reversal; the believer returns *in a new body*, in the same place, but not in the old corpse. The corpse stays where it is; the believer arises in a new vessel.

### Lecture 10, Continued: The Pigeons, the Salt, and the Believer at the Summit

The teacher returned to the believer's ghayah and dwelt on the structure of the *ghayat*.

The *ghayah* of stones and minerals is the *yaqut*, the ruby. The ruby is the most condensed clarity that stone can become; in its form is the term of the stone's reach. The ghayah of animals is the elephant. The elephant has intelligence above other animals; its compassion, its memory, its self-control are the term of what the animal-soul can reach. The ghayah of man is the *mumin*. The believer is what the human form is for.

The teacher noted that *Awwalu man amana* -- "the first who believed" -- has the numerical value of *Ali ibn Abi Talib*. The numerical encoding is precise: *awwalu man amana* numerically equals *Ali ibn Abi Talib*. The first who believed is Ali, by the numerical reading; and Ali is also the term of the line of believers, the *jami* of those who follow him in *iman*. The first and the term are the same.

On the night of Miraj two unique gifts were given to the Prophet that he had not had before: *Surat al-Fatihah*, and the last two verses of *Surah al-Baqarah*. These were given to him alone; no other prophet received them. *Surat al-Fatihah* is the opening of the Quran; the last two verses of *Surah al-Baqarah* are the closing of the second Surah. The two are structural: an opening, and a closing of the longest Surah. They constitute, between them, a frame.

*Surat al-Fatihah*'s seven verses and twenty-five words encode the twelve duat of the *jazair* + the twelve duat of the *hadarat* + the Imam. The verse-count and the word-count are not arbitrary. The duat of the *jazair* pair, and the *tahajjud* prayer's *rakat* are in pairs to honor the pairing. The duat of the *hadarat* are collective, and *salat al-layl* has twelve consecutive *rakat* to honor the collective.

The teacher noted that this is why our prayers feel structurally heavier than the prayers of some other communities. The structure is encoded; the *rakat* are not arbitrary; the pairs and the twelves are honoring the *hudud*.

The *Falak al-Muhit*, alone of all entities, ascends directly without touching the earth. It becomes the Universal Intellect. Every other ascending entity must touch the earth and rise; *Falak al-Muhit* is the exception. This exceptionality is itself a marker of its station.

At the end of the *kawr*, the *aflak* and *kawakib* descend; the jewels and rubies of the earth then take their *khilafat* in service for one *kawr* of Adam. The hierarchy is inverted for a cycle. What had been low rises to take the place of what had been high. This is itself a teaching about the *karrah*: the *karrahs* are not all alike; in each, the relative positions can shift. The jewels of the earth, which were below the *aflak*, will at the right time take the *khilafat* of those *aflak*. Their *kawr* of service is one *kawr* of Adam.

Sayyidna Abdullah ibn Thamir was a *mubashshir al-nabi*, a giver of glad tidings of the Prophet's coming. He died at Ukhdud, Najran -- the place mentioned in Surah Buruj as the site of the trench in which the people of faith were burned. The *mubashshirun* of the *nabi* are the figures who carry the news of the coming *natiq* in advance. They prepare the ground; they tell the people; they form the receiving substance. Sayyidna Abdullah's death at Ukhdud is the death of a *mubashshir* at the site of the trench-fire; his place in the line is preserved by the witness of his death.

The doctrine of *ziyarat* has, the teacher repeated, two precise mechanisms. The first is the visitor's connection to the rank above the visited, for intercession at the *majami al-suwar*. The second is the reverse case: if the visitor is of higher rank than the buried, the flow descends from visitor to buried. The two cases together exhaust the structural possibilities.

The *hudud al-dawah*'s state as *angels bil-quwwah* in life and *angels bil-fil* after death is the doctrine that the *hudud*'s service is angelic from the start, though potential. The *fil* is what brings the potential into actuality. The believer who visits the grave of a *hadd* is visiting an angel-in-actuality. The visit is the engagement of that angel.

The Prophet's saying about the parents' joy at the visit of their children to their graves is, again, literal. The parents feel the joy; the joy is the same joy as when the children sat in their laps. The teacher allowed that this saying may sound sentimental to ears that have not heard the doctrine. To ears that have heard the doctrine, it is literal: the parents *are* there, in their angelic state; the children *are* there, in their bodies; the visit *is* the family reunion.

The verse *wabtaghu ilayhi al-wasilah* is the structural doctrine of the means. The means are *the Rasul, the Ahl al-Bayt, the duat, the hujjat, and the Imam.* Not the Quran. The Quran is the source of guidance; the *wasilah* is the chain.

The teacher would say: people sometimes think they can approach Allah through the Quran alone. The verse itself rejects this. The verse says: seek the *wasilah*. The *wasilah* is not the Quran. The *wasilah* is the chain of those through whom the guidance is held. The believer who approaches through the chain is the believer who is approaching as the verse commands.

And the closing structural teaching of this lecture: *marifat* of the Worshipped is *marifat* of the *hudud*. The gnosis of Allah is *inside* the gnosis of the *hudud*. To know the *hudud* is to know what they know; to know what they know is to know what the chain holds; to know what the chain holds is to know the Worshipped in the only way the Worshipped can be known by us. There is no other path. The path of the *hudud* is the path of *marifat*.

### Lecture 11, Continued: The Mina Sacrifice and Its Inner Mechanism

The teacher took the matter of the *dhibh al-hadi* at Mina with care, because the doctrine of slaughter is, throughout this book, a doctrine of inner mechanism.

The *hadi* at Mina is the *qurban* of the pilgrim. The pilgrim has come, in his *hajj*, to enact the cycle of approach. The *hadi* is the offering by which the approach is completed. The meat of the *hadi* is elevated to the upright lineage: this is its first inner consequence. The *hissiyya* of the animal is expelled by the uttering of *Bismillah* at the slaughter: this is its second inner consequence. The pilgrim who eats the *hadi* eats meat that has been elevated and from which the *hissiyya* has been expelled. The eating is therefore a participation in the elevation.

The teacher noted that the slaughter at Mina is therefore not only a ritual; it is a doctrine in action. The pilgrim is participating in the doctrine of the slaughter as the doctrine of the slaughter participates in the pilgrim. The two are mutually constituted by the act.

Slaughter by Jews and Christians using their own language is permitted. The doctrine is precise: their slaughter is halal for us, in their own language. The principle is that the Bismillah-equivalent in their tradition can do the same work, because the people of the Book are tied to the original revelation in a way that allows the *hissiyya* to be expelled by their own form of the dedication. The teaching is generous: we may eat their slaughter.

This generosity has its limits. The animals killed in industrial settings without any dedication, the animals killed by hunting without the dedication, the animals killed by accident -- these are not in the same category. The slaughter of the people of the Book in their own language is included; slaughter without any dedication is not.

*As-salatu qurbanu kulli taqiyyin* -- prayer is the sacrifice of every pious person. The believer's *Allahu Akbar* at the beginning of the prayer is, in this reading, the believer's slaughter of himself. He is the offering. The *Allahu Akbar* over the *qurban* and the *Allahu Akbar* over himself are the same phrase performing the same work. The believer's prayer is therefore his daily *hadi*. He is, every day, offering himself.

This is also why the *Allahu Akbar* of the *takbira al-ihram* must be said with precision. It is not a routine opening; it is the slaughter-phrase. The believer who says it without attention is performing the slaughter without attention; the *hissiyya* of his own attention is not properly expelled; the prayer-offering is incomplete.

The four-phrase litany commended by the Prophet -- *Subhanallah, walhamdulillah, wa la ilaha illallah, wallahu akbar* -- and the fifth, *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah*, organize the soul-layers in their proper relations to the divine. *Allahu Akbar* on the *namiyah*. *Alhamdulillah* on the *hissiyah*. *Subhanallah* on the *natiqah*. *La ilaha illallah* is the *form* upon the *nayyirah*. *La hawla wa la quwwata* returns power.

The *natiqa* absorbs the *hissiya* through life and at death. This absorption is gradual. The believer who lives in praise is, slowly, gathering the *hissiya* into the *natiqa*. By the time of death, the gathering is complete or incomplete. If complete, the death is noble: the *hissiya* has been carried into the *natiqa*; the body is left without the *hissiya*; the *natiqa* with its absorbed *hissiya* departs. If incomplete, the death is not noble: the *hissiya* remains in the body, the body retains the sensitive soul, the death is, in the inner reading, only partial.

The teacher noted that this is also why our funeral disciplines pay attention to the body. The body is the locus of what has or has not been absorbed. The treatment of the body honors the absorption that has occurred and supports the absorption that may still be possible. *Ghusl* is not a hygienic discipline; it is a discipline that respects the *natiqa*'s work on the body.

The teacher then turned to the matter of Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman and Sayyidna Ibrahim bin Muhammad bin Fahd.

Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman's saying *if pre-eternity had not decided that I would precede Ibrahim, I never could have preceded him* is one of the most striking statements in the entire book. The Dai of one's own time is saying: *I am Dai only because pre-eternity decided I would be Dai before Ibrahim. Otherwise Ibrahim would have been Dai. Ibrahim's station, in the absolute, is higher than mine. But the pre-eternal decision placed me first.*

This is not modesty. This is doctrine. The Dai's office is not held because the Dai is the best person available; it is held because the Dai is the one whom pre-eternity designated for the office. The relation of office and person is not a relation of merit alone; it is a relation of pre-eternal designation. Ibrahim's higher station, in the absolute, does not contradict Ali bin Sulayman's holding the office; the two are reconciled by the pre-eternal designation.

Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman performed eleven *nusus* on Ibrahim bin Muhammad bin Fahd during the latter's fourteen-year imprisonment by the Zaidi Imam. Eleven designations in succession, across fourteen years. The Zaidi Imam, the political opponent, had imprisoned Ibrahim; the line of designation, the spiritual ordering, was nonetheless held. Eleven times, the *nass* was performed.

The teacher noted that this is the discipline of the line under persecution. The political opponent imprisons the bearer of the office; the spiritual ordering persists across the prison walls. The *nass* is performed by the Dai of the outside on the Dai-designate of the inside; the line is held through the prison; eleven times, the holding is reaffirmed. When at last Ibrahim emerges, or in the inner reality whenever he emerges, the office has been held for him in his absence by the bearer of the previous *nass*.

This is also the model for the smaller persecutions of the line. Wherever the line is constrained by the political moment, the spiritual ordering persists. The believer who is constrained by political circumstance is still inside the line; his *nass*, in his case, is the *nass* of his connection to the chain above him. The Dai's holding of Ibrahim's office across the prison walls is the model for every believer's holding of his connection across whatever walls his life encounters.

The *azdad* are given forty days of respite after death. They roam between the heavens and the earth, harming believers in subtle ways. After forty days, they are pulled into *Ras al-Zanab*. The doctrine is that there is a window after the *azdad*'s death in which their work continues; after the window, they are removed.

This is also a teaching about subtle harm. The believer who finds himself, after the death of an enemy of the line, in a difficult forty-day period may be in the period of that enemy's respite. The period passes; the difficulty eases; the doctrine has been borne out. The believer who knows this is not surprised by the period; he sustains his practice through it; at the end he finds the air clearer.

From the *fuzala* of the *hudud al-dawah*, three things form. The jewels of royal crowns; the pigeons of the Baytullah and Madinah, whose natural death is halal; and the salt used at meals. The list is not random. The crown-jewels are the *hudud*'s *fuzala* in their concentrated, sovereign form. The pigeons of the Bayt are the *hudud*'s *fuzala* in their winged, devotional form. The salt is the *hudud*'s *fuzala* in their everyday, table-bound form. Three forms of the same *fuzala*; three points of contact between the believer's daily life and the *hudud*'s legacy.

This is also why the salt at the meal is honored in our tradition. To start and end the meal with a touch of salt is to acknowledge the *hudud*'s presence at the table. The trace of the *hudud* is being received in the salt; the meal is being framed by their legacy.

Umar bin Abd al-Aziz returned *Bagh-e-Fadak* to the Ahl al-Bayt and stopped the cursing of Ali from seventy thousand pulpits. For these two acts his punishment in the next realm was shortened. The doctrine is precise: mercy follows action. Umar bin Abd al-Aziz was a caliph; his punishment was already heavy because of the office he held; but the two acts of justice -- the return of Fadak and the end of the cursing -- shortened the punishment. The mercy is in proportion to the action.

This is itself a teaching about the believer's life. The believer may find himself in a heavy situation; the question is whether he performs the acts of justice that are open to him. The acts shorten the heaviness. They do not remove the situation, but they reorient its weight. Umar bin Abd al-Aziz's case is the case in extremis; the same principle applies in the more modest cases of the ordinary believer.

At the *Qiyamah* of the Qayam, the opposers will be dragged in human form to Mina and killed by believers. The names are listed: Yazid, Muawiyah, Marwan, Nimrud, Firaun. The list runs from the *Dawr al-Sitr* opponents backward to the early *Dawr al-Kashf* opponents. They are all gathered.

The stench of their bodies will be carried by the wind through four doors of *safrah*. One of the doors is in Hadramaut. The geography is precise: there are four doors, and one is in Hadramaut. The stench is dispersed through the doors; the dispersal is the final purification of the place of the Qayam.

The teacher allowed that the doctrine of the *Qiyamah* of the Qayam is severe. It is severe because the work being done is the final accounting of the line's history of opposition. The opposers are not allowed to slip out unaccounted for. They are gathered, in human form, in the place of the line's central work; they are slain by the believers; their remains are dispersed through the doors of *safrah*. The justice is concrete.

The dawah is presented to non-believers at every death and every change of body, until they reach the garment of *damar* -- the tar-garment -- where the dawah is no longer offered. The doctrine is mercy at the level of structure: the non-believer is given the offer at every transition, again and again, across his cycles. He has many chances. Only at the garment of *damar* does the offering stop. The garment of *damar* is the final layer; below it is the lava. The non-believer who has refused at every transition all the way to the *damar* is one who has, in effect, refused so often that the structure no longer offers.

This is not arbitrary. The structure's offering is conditioned on the structural possibility of receiving. At the *damar*, the structural possibility is closed. The non-believer's choice has closed it; the structure has not closed it without his choice.

The dua *Bismillahilladhi la yadurru maa ismihi shayun fil-ardi wa la fis-samai* is the dua of structural protection. The Bismillah here is the formula of the protected Name. Reciting three times in the morning and three in the evening covers the day and the night; the believer who keeps this dua is a believer whose day and night are framed by the formula. The protection is from harm in the earth and in the heaven. The two domains are covered.

The Eid al-Adha meat and the *aqiqah* meat must not be given to non-Muslims. The reason is the same as elsewhere: the meat carries the inner consequence of the slaughter; the giving to non-Muslims breaks the chain through which the consequence is preserved. The non-Muslim eats the meat without the chain; the meat is wasted; the consequence is dispersed.

The nursing mother must not eat from her child's *aqiqah*. The doctrine is that the *aqiqah* is a foundational gift for the child; the mother who eats it passes it back into the milk; the milk that returns to the child is the child's own *aqiqah* coming back. This is, in the inner doctrine, an interruption of the proper direction of the *aqiqah*. The *aqiqah* should go to others, who pray for the child; the child receives the benefit through the prayers. If the mother eats it, the prayers are short-circuited; the child receives the meat's substance again through the milk, but without the prayers that would otherwise mediate.

The discipline is precise. The mother does not eat. The meat goes to others. The others pray for the child. The child receives through the prayers. The *aqiqah*'s benefit is preserved.

### Lecture 12, Continued: The Geography of the Line and Its Inner Resonances

The teacher's most striking teaching in lecture 12 is the correspondence of the *Dawr al-Satr*'s figures to the geography of the Hajj.

Mawla Bibi Fatimah corresponds to Muzdalifah. Mawla Ali to Mina. Rasulullah to Jabal Arafat. The three sites of the Hajj are the three figures of the *Dawr al-Satr*'s inner core. *Lubab al-Dawr al-Satr* -- the inner core of the Era of Concealment -- is constituted by these three.

What does this correspondence mean? It means that the pilgrim, in his Hajj, is moving through the inner geography of our line. He stands at Arafat: he is standing at Rasulullah. He moves to Muzdalifah: he is moving to Fatimah. He moves to Mina: he is moving to Ali. The motions of the Hajj are the motions of the inner pilgrimage through the line.

This is also why the rituals at each site are weighted with the inner reality. At Arafat, the camel of the Prophet would sit down under the weight of descending revelation. The *wahi* would come heavy on Arafat. The pilgrim who stands at Arafat is standing where the revelation came; he is opening himself to receive what the place still carries.

On Jabal Arafat, luminous *suwarat* manifested that had never occurred to a human heart. The intensity was the more profound because of what was unfolding regarding Ghadir Khumm. The *suwarat* were of an order that the prophets before had not seen. The intensity, the teacher said, was the intensity of the imminent declaration: the Prophet was, on this Arafat, about to issue the declaration that would set the line for centuries. The *suwarat* gathered around the imminent declaration.

The plot against the Prophet's life before Ghadir is part of the same geography. Aisha had told her father Abu Bakr the secret about Ali's succession. The munafiqin -- a group of them -- planned to kill the Prophet in a dark valley before Ghadir. The plot was real; the threat was real.

At the dark valley, lightning flashed, prolonged. The flashes lasted longer than ordinary lightning. The Prophet and Hudhayfah al-Yamani, riding together, saw the faces of the would-be killers in the prolonged light. The seeing was the preservation; the killing did not succeed. The would-be killers withdrew, knowing they had been seen.

This is the moment of providence that the doctrine remembers. Hudhayfah's later oath to Umar -- that he had been among the conspirators -- is the witness to the moment. The conspirators' faces were preserved in Hudhayfah's memory; the oath sealed the memory in history.

The doctrine of the *kham'ir* in Arafat -- the hidden treasures -- explains the prohibition on cutting trees in Arafat. The trees neighbor the treasures. To cut a tree near the treasure is to disturb the treasure's neighborhood. The discipline preserves the *kham'ir* by preserving their neighbors.

The verse *whoever broadcasts the secrets of the dawah has prepared his seat in fire and is disavowed by al-Aziz al-Ghaffar* is the verse of the discipline of concealment. The seat in the fire is the structural consequence of the broadcasting; the disavowal by al-Aziz al-Ghaffar is the second consequence. *Al-Aziz al-Ghaffar* is the divine attribute that combines the Mighty and the Forgiving. To be disavowed by the combined attribute is to be cut off from both the Mighty and the Forgiving. There is no remaining channel.

The doctrine of the first *nass* of Dawud bin Ajabshah on Sulayman bin Hasan -- written by Dawud bin Qutub, using twenty-eight titles corresponding to the twenty-eight letters of the *kalimat al-shahadah* -- is the doctrine of the *nass* as a structural recapitulation. The twenty-eight titles are not decorative; they enact the twenty-eight letters; the *nass* is, in its very form, the *shahadah*-form. Whoever recites the *shahadah* after this *nass*, Sayyidna Muhammad bin Fad al-Alawi established, must also bear *shahadah* for Sulayman bin Hasan. The structural recapitulation has made the *shahadah* widen to include the *nass*-recipient.

This is itself a precise doctrine of how the *nass* operates. The *nass* is not an external designation. It is a structural enactment that, when properly performed, draws the *shahadah* itself into a wider form. The believer's daily *shahadah*, after such a *nass*, includes the *nass*-recipient by structural necessity.

Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan's title *Amin al-Mutaman* is the title of the doubly-entrusted. The trust of the Imam is one; the trust of the believers is another; he is *amin* of both. The believers' trust in him is itself part of his being *amin* before the Imam. The doctrine is recursive: the *amin* is *amin* of both directions simultaneously.

He was at *maqam-e-nasut* for his Imam and at *maqam-e-lahut* for his fellow believers. The two stations are simultaneous and structural. They are not contradictions; they are directions.

The Dai of Yemen is superior to all duat of other islands and does not lead prayer outside the three *jazair* (Yemen, Hind, Sindh). The discipline is geographical: the Dai of Yemen serves these three; his prayer-leadership is confined to these. The exception of Sayyidna Muhammad bin Ismail leading prayer in Habasha is precisely the exception; he was given the dispensation for the loftiness of his station, and the dispensation was not given to others.

The *mansub* of Yemen, of Najran itself, is superior to all *manasib*. The reason is that he is the Dai's successor *bil-quwwah* -- in potentiality. His potential succession to the Dai elevates his actual station among the *manasib*. He is, in the structural ordering, the Dai-in-waiting; the office before its time confers a station.

The phenomenon of the *majlis* at which the souls of the deceased believers attend is, the teacher said, a phenomenon that every Dai's *majlis* exemplifies. The questioner asks through the tongue of a *hadd-i ali*. The asker may be living; the asking is also being voiced by the *hadd* whose tongue gathers the questions of his lower *hudud*. The teaching responds to both: the visible asker and the gathered askings of the chain.

Sayyidi Fardali Sahib Muqaddas's sending of a possessed Bohra woman to Ziyali Sahib Muqaddas, with the instruction to *conduct asbaq in the mosque so that the departed suwarat could hear*, is the witness to the larger gathering. The possessed woman was the vehicle of the message; the message was that the *asbaq* in the mosque must continue, because the departed *suwarat* were present and were continuing to learn.

This teaches us that the *asbaq* is not for the present audience alone. Whoever teaches in the *masjid* of our community is teaching to a gathering that includes the present and the departed. The departed are listening. The teaching is therefore eternal in its audience.

Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan's invisible visits to Sayyidna Ali bin Sulayman's *majlis* are of the same order. The Dai of an earlier era visits the Dai of a later era. The visit is invisible; the receiving Dai stands and greets him as one greets a present visitor. The believers in the room may not see the visitor; the receiving Dai sees, and the standing-up signals to those in the room that the unseen visitor is being received.

Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail's vision at *tawaf* -- of the souls of Fardali, Ziyali, and Ghulam Husayn performing *tawaf* at very high speed -- is the vision of the body-less *tawaf*. The souls have shed the drag of the limbs; their *tawaf* is at the speed of the soul's natural movement. The longing of Husayn bin Ismail, *Wa shauqah*, is the longing for that lightness. The believer who has not yet shed the body will, by the discipline of his life, shed it eventually; the longing in the meantime is a healthy longing.

Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail's kissing of the hand of a sudden visitor who vanished in Najran is the receiving of an Imam-manifestation. The Imam is in concealment; he can nonetheless manifest briefly to those whom he chooses; the manifest visit is kissed, the kiss is received, and the visitor vanishes when the visit is complete. The believer who is so visited keeps the visit close; the visit does not require external attestation.

Sayyidna Sulayman bin Hasan's having a form in Hadramawt named *Sulayman-i Humam*, alongside his island form, is the witness to the unusual office of double-presence. He was present in two places, in two forms, simultaneously. The two were not in conflict; they were complementary. The double-presence is, the teacher said, unprecedented among duat. The exception itself is part of the doctrine: when the office requires it, the exception is made.

Sayyidna Husayn bin Ahmad, under house arrest in Taif, would come to Baytullah in the inner ascent and perform seven *tawaf* daily. The body in Taif and the *tawaf* at the Bayt are not in contradiction. The inner ascent is real. The believer who is constrained outwardly may still travel inwardly. The seven *tawaf* daily, performed across the inner ascent, were the *ibadah* of the Dai despite the house arrest.

Sayyidi Raj's writing down of the second *nass* of Dawud bin Ajabshah on Sulayman bin Hasan, three days before his passing in 970 Hijri, is the witness to the *nass*'s preservation. The *nass* could have been lost in the confusion of the death; the writing preserved it. Sayyidi Raj's hand carried the *nass* forward in writing; the *nass*-recipient was confirmed by the writing.

Today's Imam, the 35th, is the seventh of his *haftah* and combines two *haftahs*. He possesses fourteen Imams within himself. The seventh of any *haftah* is the *jami* of his seven; the seventh of two *haftahs* combined is the *jami* of fourteen. Today's Imam carries this fourteen.

His Dai's station rises with him. The Dai of the 35th Imam serves an office that has, in its time, the gathering of fourteen. The believer of today is therefore a believer in an extraordinarily gathered moment of the line. The accumulation is the gift of the era.

### Lecture 14, Continued: The Three Days of the Qaim and Spiritual Progress

The doctrine of the Qaim's three days is, the teacher said, easily under-stated.

The Qaim takes the *khilafah* of the Ashir upon his departure from this abode. The *khilafah* is the office of vicegerency; the Ashir is the Tenth Intellect, the one whose vicegerency is being transferred. The Qaim, in taking the *khilafah*, is taking the office that the *Ashir* had held in this dispensation.

He remains *qaim* for only three days. The duration is short. Why? Because the *Qiyamah* is concentrated. The work of the *Qiyamah* is the work of the final accounting -- the gathering of the believers, the slaying of the opposers at Mina, the dispersal of the stench through the four doors of *safrah*, the elevation of the gathered. The work is severe; the work is intense; the work does not require extension. Three days suffices.

The believer who imagines the *Qiyamah* as a long event is misunderstanding the doctrine. The *Qiyamah* of the Qayam is three days. The work compressed into those three days is enormous. The Qaim, in those days, performs the work that the cycle has prepared for him.

After the three days, the new dispensation begins. The fifty thousand years that follow are the fifty thousand years of the new era; the Qaim's role in them is, in the standing doctrine, in another mode. The three days are the *Qiyamah* proper; what follows is the new cycle.

*Sirat mustaqim* refers to the line of *juz-i duat* in which one stands. The believer's *sirat mustaqim* is the chain from the believer to the Imam through the lower and higher *hudud* to the *Bab al-Abwab*.

The teacher elaborated. The believer has a Madhun who is immediately above him -- the person who has taken his *ahd*, who teaches him, who corrects him. Above the Madhun is the Mukasir; above the Mukasir is the Dai; and so on up through the ranks. Each believer's chain is constituted by the specific persons at each level.

The believer who stands at the bottom of his chain is also standing on the *sirat*. The *sirat* is *the chain*. The believer who walks the *sirat* is the believer who walks the chain; he obeys his Madhun, he honors the rank above, he holds the line. The verse *Ihdina al-sirat al-mustaqim* is his daily prayer that this chain be preserved.

Spiritual progress is of two types: one is striven for, the other is obtained by sitting in someone's lap.

The first type is the type of effort. The believer reads, prays, fasts, gives charity, attends *majlis*, takes the *ahd*, renews the *ahd*. By these efforts, his progress is earned. The progress is his to celebrate; the progress is also his to be humble about, because the effort is also a gift.

The second type is the type of transfer. The believer who sits in the lap of a teacher receives what the teacher transmits; the teacher's substance becomes the believer's substance; the progress is by transfer, not by effort. This is the progress of the child who learns by being in the presence of the elder. The child's heart is moved by the elder's example; the child's understanding is shaped by the elder's words; the child becomes what the elder makes.

The two types are not mutually exclusive. The same believer can have both. The same lifetime can carry both. The believer who has the wisdom to seek both is the believer who is making the fullest use of the means of progress.

In a *kawr* there are 360 manifestations of the *walad*'s integration. The human body contains 360 *nufus*; one part progresses per *kawr*. The doctrine is precise: the body is 360 *nufus*; the rate is one per *kawr*. The believer's progress across his many *karrahs* is the slow integration of these 360.

This is also why the believer's progress can feel small in one *karrah*. He has worked hard; he sees small movement. But the doctrine says: one part per *kawr*. The small movement is the proper rate. The believer who knows this is not discouraged by the rate; he knows the rate; the rate will compound across the *karrahs* into the full integration of the 360.

The verse *ana ala siratin mustaqim* in Surah Yasin refers, in the inner reading, to Abu Talib. *I am on a straight path* -- the speaker is, on the face of the verse, the *natiq* of the surah; in the inner reading, the *Rab* whom the speaker addresses is also being identified. *Rabbi* -- "my Lord" -- is Abu Talib, the *jadd* of the line through Ali. Abu Talib is *Rabbi*; Abu Talib is on the *sirat al-mustaqim* of the *juzs*; the verse, on the inside, says: my Lord Abu Talib is on the straight path of the chain.

The teacher noted that this reading does not contradict the outer reading; it deepens it. On the outside, the verse is the speaker's affirmation. On the inside, the verse names the line that the speaker's affirmation honors. Both are true; the inside is the deeper layer of the outside.

### Lecture 15, Continued: The End of the Cycles

The teacher closed the cosmological section of the book with the doctrine of the end of the cycles.

The Quranic *yawma natwi al-samaa* -- "the day We will roll up the heaven" -- is the verse-form of the end of the *kawr*. The *ribatat*, the bonds that hold the celestial order in place, terminate at this point. The heaven is rolled up like a scroll. The ordering of the *aflak* dissolves.

After each end-of-*kawr*, the earth is changed. Length becomes width; north becomes south. *Kama badana awwala khalqin nuiduhu* -- "as We began the first creation, We will repeat it" -- applies in a literal sense. The cardinal directions reorganize. What was the length becomes the width; what was the width becomes the length. The reorganization is total.

This is itself a teaching about the impermanence of the orientations we take for granted. The directions of our compasses are not eternal. The *kawr*'s end will redo them. The believer who has invested too heavily in the directions of his moment will find his investment recast at the end-of-*kawr*. The believer who has invested in the chain through which he is connected upward will find his investment preserved.

*Dawr al-sitr* and *dawr al-kashf* succeed each other like night and day until the *Mawr al-Azam* is completed. The pattern is the pattern of night and day; concealment is followed by openness; openness is followed by concealment. The pattern runs across the *kawrs* until the *Mawr*, the great cycle, is at its end.

One *mawr* contains 400,000 *kawr* multiplied by 400,000 *kawr*. The product is enormous; the number is past the human imagination. One *mawr* has passed; nine remain.

This number is the answer that Imam Zayn al-Abidin would not give to Umm al-Nada. Umm al-Nada had asked: how much has passed, how much remains? The Imam gave her the first answer: one *mawr* has passed. The second answer he withheld: she did not have the standing for it. Now, in this book, both are given. One has passed; nine remain. The nine are the future. The future is structurally bounded.

The believer who is given the number is, the teacher said, given a gift. The gift is the comprehension of where in the cosmos he stands. He stands in the second *mawr*. Eight more *mawrs* will follow this one. At the end of the ninth that remains, the *Mawr al-Azam* will be at its end; the absorption into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal* will be complete.

At each end-of-*mawr*, the Ashir's place is taken by the ninth *aql*. The Ashir, who has stood as Tenth in each *mawr*, is succeeded by the ninth at the end-of-*mawr*. All ascending forms gather in the domain of the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*. The gathering is the consolidation; the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*'s domain is the receiving place.

In the end, all are absorbed into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*. Only the domain of the *Aql al-Awwal* and the *Mumbaith al-Awwal* remain. The horizon is at this absorption.

The believer who is given this horizon is the believer whose life can be rightly oriented. He is not investing in the local, in the temporary, in the surface; he is investing in the chain that runs through the *hudud* to the *Aql al-Awwal* and the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*. The chain is what carries him forward. The chain is what survives the absorptions.

The teacher closed: this is the *Kunooz al-Hikmah*. The treasures are stored for the *arbab al-juzi ismah*. The duat are to draw from the treasures across the eras. The believers are to receive what the duat distribute. The chain is to be preserved across the *karrahs* and across the *kawrs* and across the *mawrs*. The end is the absorption into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*. Until then, the work is the work of the line.

*Sallallahu ala Sayyidina Muhammadin wa aali Sayyidina Muhammadin wa barik wa sallim.*

*Salam alayhim ajmain abad al-abadin.*

## Connected Meditations: The Doctrine in Its Living Context

The teacher would often, in the course of the lectures, return to the same themes from different angles, drawing the same teaching out by approaches that the previous treatment had not used. The following meditations gather the longer expositions that arose in this way, faithful to the lecture-flow but set out continuously here for the reader.

### The Riyat and the Believer's Body

It is worth dwelling further on the doctrine of *riyat* and what it teaches about the believer's body.

The *riyat* are the spiritual currents -- the inner essence-streams that the soul carries within itself and that emerge most fully at death. During life, the believer's *riyat* are still gathered in his body. At the moment of death, they begin to emerge. The higher *riyat* emerge first, in the moments immediately after the soul departs, before burial. The lower *riyat* emerge in the three days after burial. After three days, what remain are vapors only.

From the higher *riyat* of the believer, the *nasut-e-zilli* of the successors is formed. The *nasut-e-zilli* is the shadow-form -- the form that the successors of the believer inherit. When the believer's children or grandchildren step into a place of service in the *dawah*, the *nasut-e-zilli* they receive includes a portion shaped from his higher *riyat*. This is the meaning of the inherited disposition in our community: not merely a social inheritance, but a substance-transfer in the inner order.

From the lower *riyat* of the believer -- the *riyat* of the three days after burial -- the *nasut-e-tabii* is formed. The *nasut-e-tabii* is the natural form of subsequent generations. These *riyat* shape the basic embodiment of those who will come after.

This is also why the family's behavior in those three days matters spiritually beyond what we might suppose. The *fatiha-khwani*, the *sadaqallah*, the *beeswa khwani*, the meals for the poor, the commissioning of Quran readings, the giving of charity -- all of these in the three days reach the *riyat* directly. After three days, they reach the *fuzala* and the *surah*. Both are real; both have value; but the three-day window is the time when the *riyat* themselves are still ascending and can be addressed.

The Prophet's instruction to bury the dead quickly is not in tension with this. The instruction is for the dignity of the body and for the order of the soul's release. The three-day window begins from burial; whether burial is on the day of death or several days after, the window runs three days from the burial. But the Prophet's preference is for the body not to be held; the believer's family does best to bury promptly and then to commit themselves to the three days of offerings.

The believer in his own life receives *riyat* in three principal moments. The teaching has been set out in the second question; let us restate it with what it implies about daily practice.

At *wudu*, the believer says *Bismillah*. The first breath after *Bismillah* is itself a vehicle of *riyat*. The water that touches his skin opens channels. The water that enters his pores is the carrier. The angels created from the shaken drops are at the open channels, praying for him. The believer who performs *wudu* hurriedly, without the *Bismillah*, without attention to the breath, without the shaking of the hands, is performing a *wudu* that the doctrine acknowledges as valid for the prayer but that does not bring the full benefit. *Isbagh al-wudu* -- the completeness of *wudu* -- is the discipline of attention through all of these moments.

The two *atonements* for major sins through *wudu* are precisely about this completeness. The *isbagh al-wudu fil-burudat* -- in the cold -- is the completeness when external cold makes the limbs reluctant. The *isbagh al-wudu fil-sabarat* -- in the *sabarat* -- is, in one reading, the same external cold; in another, the *coldness of the disposition*, the laziness that internal disinclination would lead to incompletion. To perform *wudu* completely in those moments of internal coldness is itself the atonement.

At *sahri*, the believer eats at the last moment of the night. The *riyat* that descend on uncooked fruits during the night -- the dew-like descent of purified *riyat* that we have heard described -- are present in greater concentration at the very last moment, just before dawn. The believer who delays *sahri* to the last moment is therefore catching the highest concentration of the descending *riyat*. The believer who eats early loses the high-concentration window.

At *iftar*, the believer breaks his fast immediately at sunset. The *riyat* at *iftar* are not in the same descending pattern as at *sahri*; they are in a returning pattern. The body, having been emptied by the fast, opens to receive. The first morsels after the fast are the most receptive moments. The believer who delays the breaking, even for the sake of further prayer, misses the most receptive moment. The Prophet's instruction to hurry the *iftar* is precisely about this.

The believer's body is, in this teaching, a body in active exchange with the spiritual currents around it at every breath. The body is not a closed vessel; it is a porous vessel through which the *riyat* are received and through which, at death, the *riyat* will emerge. The discipline of life is to recognize the body as porous and to attend to the moments when the porousness is most active.

### The Lecture-Gathering and Its Hidden Audience

The doctrine that the souls of deceased believers attend the *majlis* when *ifadah* and *istifadah* occur reframes the lecture itself.

The teacher in our community is not addressing only those whose bodies sit before him. He is addressing the gathered chain. The departed *suwarat* of believers come to the *majlis* to listen. The questioner who asks a question is asking on behalf of more than himself; his question is voiced through the tongue of a *hadd-i ali* who carries the askings of those who cannot ask in their own bodies.

This is why Sayyidi Fardali Sahib Muqaddas's instruction to Ziyali Sahib Muqaddas to conduct *asbaq* in the mosque was structured: the *asbaq* in the mosque allowed the departed *suwarat* to hear. The instruction was for the benefit of the audience that was invisible. If the *asbaq* had been held only in a private setting, the departed *suwarat* might not have had access. The mosque, the public site of teaching, is open to them.

The believer who attends the *majlis* is therefore in the company of more believers than he can see. The verses recited, the doctrines unfolded, the questions asked and answered, all reach the wider audience. The believer who attends with attention is also a representative of his own deceased relatives; through his attention, they too may be brought into the teaching.

The discipline of the *majlis* is the discipline of public teaching with this awareness. The teacher prepares as for a larger audience than the visible one. The believer attends as a vehicle of a larger gathering than himself.

Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail's witness of the souls of Fardali, Ziyali, and Ghulam Husayn at *tawaf*, performing the *tawaf* at very high speed, is a witness to what the body of the believer carries underneath the *tawaf*. The body is, in our state, slowing what the soul could do at full speed. The *tawaf* of the body is a small portion of the *tawaf* the soul could perform. To know this is also to be patient with the body's pace.

The *Wa shauqah* that Husayn bin Ismail cried out is the cry of the believer who sees what is possible. The believer who has not yet seen still walks the *tawaf* at the body's pace; the discipline is to walk it with sincerity, knowing that the released soul will perform a faster *tawaf* in due time. The longing is not despair; it is anticipation.

### The Imam in His Concealment

The Imam of the present time is concealed. *Imam al-zaman al-mutawari bil-hijabi al-mustatir bil-sirr an jami al-baraa* -- "the Imam of the Time, who is concealed by the veil, hidden by the secret from all of creation." His original self is hidden; what has appeared is a veil; the structure of his presence in this era is the structure of veiled presence.

The teacher took some care to distinguish concealment from absence. The Imam is not absent. The Imam is present. The presence is veiled. The veil is not the body of the Imam; the veil is the structural condition in which the Imam manifests in this era. We cannot ordinarily see the Imam. We can, in the inner reality, be addressed by him. We can, by his choice, be visited by him. Sayyidna Husayn bin Ismail's kiss of the visitor's hand in Najran is the witness to this kind of visit.

The presence of the Imam is also mediated through the Dai. The Dai of the line stands between the believer and the Imam, carrying the substance of the Imam to the believer and carrying the believer's connection back to the Imam. The Dai is the visible representative of the invisible office. To honor the Dai is to honor the Imam through him; to disrespect the Dai is to disrespect the Imam through him.

The fifty thousand years of this *dawr* are the structural duration of this era of veiled presence. The number is not a metaphor; it is the era's bounded extent. When the Qaim manifests, the bounded extent will close and the new era will begin.

The believer in our era is therefore in a long era of veiled presence. The discipline of the era is the discipline of receiving the veiled. The Dai's lessons are the veiled Imam's lessons; the *majlis* is the veiled Imam's *majlis*; the *masjid* is the veiled Imam's *masjid*. The believer who recognizes this structure receives the era as it is given.

The fifty thousand years connect, also, to the larger cosmology of the *Mawr al-Azam*. One *mawr* is 400,000 *kawr* multiplied by 400,000 *kawr*. The current era's fifty thousand years are within this larger calculation. The believer who is given the number is given a sense of where he stands.

### Today's Dai and Today's Imam

The teacher would, at the end of many of the lectures, recite the prayer for today's Dai and today's Imam.

Today's Imam, the 35th, is the seventh of his *haftah* and combines two *haftahs*. He possesses fourteen Imams within himself. The seventh of any *haftah* is the *jami*, the gatherer of his seven; the seventh of two *haftahs* combined is the *jami* of fourteen. This is an extraordinary moment in the line.

The Dai of today's Imam serves an office whose station rises with the Imam's gathering. The believer of today therefore is a believer in an extraordinarily gathered moment of the line. The accumulation in the 35th is the gift of the era.

The prayer for the Dai's prolonged life, for the Imam's prolonged life, for the protection of both, for the elevation of both, is the structural prayer of the believer of today. To pray it is to acknowledge the gathered moment and to ask for the moment's continuation.

The Fatiha is recited as a gift -- a *hadiyyah*, a means of connection, lofty and pleasing -- to the soul of the author Sayyidna Ali bin Hibatullah, and to the souls of the duat of the line, and to the soul of the Imam of the time. The Fatiha is the prayer that anchors all these recitations; reciting the Fatiha at the close of the *majlis* is the way the believer closes the circle that the *majlis* has opened.

### The Sirat and the Believer's Own Chain

The teacher returned to the doctrine of *sirat al-mustaqim* with a precision that bears restatement.

The *sirat al-mustaqim* is not an external map. It is *the chain of the believer's connection upward*. The Madhun above the believer, the Mukasir above the Madhun, the Dai above the Mukasir, the *hujjah* above the Dai, the *Bab al-Abwab*, the Imam -- this sequence is the believer's *sirat*. The believer walks this chain in the only way he can walk it: by being faithful to each rank in turn.

The believer's *sirat* is therefore *unique to him*. No two believers have exactly the same *sirat*. Each believer's chain is constituted by the specific persons through whom his connection to the line passes. To walk the *sirat* is to honor those specific persons.

The verse *Ihdina al-sirat al-mustaqim* is the daily prayer of the believer to keep his chain firm. *Ihdina* -- "guide us." The guidance asked for is the guidance to remain in the chain. *Al-sirat al-mustaqim* -- "the straight path." The straight path is the believer's chain. The believer asks Allah to keep him in his chain.

This is also why the believer's most important worldly responsibility is to honor his Madhun. The Madhun is the rung immediately above him; without the Madhun, the chain is broken at its closest point. The believer who disrespects his Madhun has broken the chain at the rung where the chain most matters to him. To honor the Madhun is to keep the chain alive.

The doctrine of the two types of progress -- one striven for, the other obtained by sitting in someone's lap -- is connected to this. The believer who sits in the Madhun's lap (in the figurative sense) receives the second type of progress; the believer who strives, by reading and practicing, receives the first. Both are honored. The believer who has both is fortunate.

The doctrine of the 360 *nufus*, one part progressing per *kawr*, is the rate. The believer should not be surprised at the slow pace. The pace is structural. The *karrahs* will compound it across the cycles.

### The End of the Book: The Author's Final Prayer

The author closes the book with a prayer that we should not pass over.

He prays for the protection of the Dai of his time, Sayyidna Hibatullah al-Muayyad. He prays that the Dai's *hawzah* be protected from the Pharaoh of the age. He prays that the Dai's aspirations be conveyed. He prays that the lamps of the *dawah* be illuminated. He prays that the followers of the Dai be increased. He prays that the substance of the Dai flow to the questioner. He prays that the subtle blessings reach. He prays for kindness, compassion, forgiveness, and mercy.

These prayers are not formulaic. They are the structural prayers of the believer for the Dai. Every prayer the author makes for his Dai is a prayer that we, in our era, should be making for our Dai.

The author closes by attributing the correctness of the book to the Dai who caused his tongue to flow, and the errors to himself. This attribution is also structural. The correct doctrine is from the Dai through the author's tongue; the errors are from the author's own deficiency. The reader, in receiving the doctrine, attributes the correctness back to its source.

And the author closes with the covenant: that whoever reads this book without the permission of one who has the authority of *taqdim* and *takhir* is absolved of all duty toward al-Aliyy al-Mabud and toward all the *mahdudin* and *hudud*. The covenant is severe. The discipline of concealment runs to the end.

The book is then sealed by the prayer of the gathering: *Salam alayhim ajmain abad al-abadin*. Peace be upon them all, forever and ever. With this prayer the *Kunooz al-Hikmah* is closed. The treasures have been displayed; the conditions of their reception have been set; the readers who come to them by permission may carry away what they can hold.

## Concluding Reflection on the Structure of the Whole

When we step back from the particular teachings and look at the *Kunooz al-Hikmah* as a whole, what do we see?

We see, first, a book that begins with the praise of the divine through the *nasut khas* and the *riyadh* of the *ahl al-ikhtisas*. The opening is already a doctrine: the divine has manifested a special human form from the gardens of the specially placed; the divine's manifestation is mediated by the believers and the *hudud*. This doctrine sets the tone for what follows. The divine is not approached through abstraction; the divine is approached through the chain.

We see, second, the witness to the line: Hasan, Hussain, the Aimmah Fatimiyyin, the duat of Yemen, the Mim of Muhammad bin Fuad, the Ayn of Ali bin Sulayman, the emigrant Muhammad bin Ismail. The line is named with care; each name carries its particular station; the praise honors what each carried.

We see, third, the discipline of the book: the covenant of concealment, the attribution of correctness to the Dai, the prayer for the questioner. The book is structured as a transaction: a question from below, an answer from above, a discipline for who may handle the answer.

We see, fourth, the first question and its three-part answer: what was before the *alam al-ibda* (the refusal of speculation), how the *nasut khas* is built (in the Bayt al-Haram, not in any womb), and what the heavens and the earth came from (the *dhawat al-nadimah*). These three subjects organize much of the cosmology that follows.

We see, fifth, the doctrine of the *falak al-mustaqim* and the believer's substance: where the believer's *surah* goes, where his *riyat* go, where his *fuzala* go, how the Imam dissolves in the *qalb* and becomes substance for the *hudud*, how the next Imam is formed.

We see, sixth, the doctrine of the Bayt, the *qalb haqiqi*, the sakhrah and the abrar, the twelve *jazair* as twelve hearts, the twenty-eight as the eternal order. This is the structural doctrine of the cosmos.

We see, seventh, the doctrine of the 28 and the 29: the *jami* and the breaker, Sayyidna Jafar as 28th, Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj as 29th, the muqattaat as 29, *Muhammadun Rasulullah* in verse 29 of Surah al-Fath. The structural numbers organize the line.

We see, eighth, the cycles: the Rukn al-Asad from the *fudulat* of Amir al-Muminin in the early *akwar*, the *karrat* of the Prophet and Ali, the Aristotle and Salman of the *ayan*. The doctrine of the *karrat* opens the doctrine of *raja*.

We see, ninth, the doctrine of Harun, the Mim, the Ayn, and the confidants of Isa. The line is traced backward and forward; Ali is *al-awwal wal-akhir*.

We see, tenth, the doctrine of *raja*: same rank, 1000-year cycle, two prostrations as creation and return.

We see, eleventh, the doctrine of the 21st, the senses, the *sirat* of Ali, and the discipline of slaughter.

We see, twelfth, the doctrine of the *ghayah*, the believer at the summit, and visitation.

We see, thirteenth, the doctrine of the Mina sacrifice, the litany of the four, the souls and their absorption.

We see, fourteenth, the doctrine of Arafat, the plot before Ghadir, and the two stations of the Dai.

We see, fifteenth, the doctrine of the two Mehdis, the Qaim's three days, and the rate of spiritual progress.

We see, sixteenth, the doctrine of the end of the cycles: the rolling-up of the heaven, the *Mawr al-Azam*, the absorption into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*.

Across all these, certain principles recur:

*The chain is everything.* The believer's *sirat* is his chain. The gnosis of Allah is inside the gnosis of the *hudud*. The *wasilah* is the chain.

*The number 28 is the eternal order.* It returns at every level: in the *shahadah*'s letters, in the Bayt's structure, in the duat's sequences, in the Imams' lineage. To see 28 is to see the structural ordering.

*Death does not interrupt the line.* The Imam dissolves in the *qalb* and becomes substance for the *hudud*. The *hudud* return the substance at their passing. The next Imam is formed from the gathered substance. The line is carried by the passings, not despite them.

*Concealment is part of the doctrine, not a defect.* The Imam is concealed by structural necessity. The book is sealed by the discipline of concealment. Whoever broadcasts the secrets has prepared his seat in fire. The discipline is severe because the realities are dangerous to the unprepared.

*The believer is the term of the human ascent.* The *ghayah* of man is the *mumin*. The Quran's *shay* is the believer. The first who believed -- Ali -- and the term of those who follow are the same. The believer's small life is enormous in its meaning.

*The cycle is bounded.* The *Mawr al-Azam* has its term. The absorption into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal* is the horizon. The believer is not in an endless drift; he is moving toward a determined end through determined cycles.

These principles together constitute the doctrine of the *Kunooz al-Hikmah*. The treasures stored for the masters of partial infallibility are these principles in their full expression. The duat who draw from them draw the principles in new applications for each era. The believers who receive what the duat distribute receive the principles in the forms appropriate to their stations.

May the chain be preserved. May the Dai of our time live long, with prolonged life, in health and in strength. May the Imam of our time be protected. May our connection to the chain be guarded. May we receive what we are given. May we transmit what we have received, only to those who can hold it, by permission, with discipline.

*Sallallahu ala Sayyidina Muhammadin wa aali Sayyidina Muhammadin wa barik wa sallim. Wa alayhim al-salam ajmain abad al-abadin.*

The treasures are stored. The book closes. The reader who has been given permission to read it closes the book also, with the same prayers, with the same orientation toward the line, with the same discipline of what may and what may not be passed on.

*Al-Fatihah.*

## Particular Doctrines Drawn Out at Greater Length

The teaching as it was unfolded across the lectures often opened a doctrine, set it down, and then returned to it later in the cycle from another direction. The following sections take some of those doctrines and unfold them at the length their importance deserves.

### The Mubahalah and the Self of the Prophet

The verse of *Mubahalah* -- *taalaw abnaana wa abnaakum wa nisaana wa nisaakum wa anfusana wa anfusakum* -- has been touched upon already in the matter of the divorce-authority over Aisha. But the verse itself is so foundational that it bears a fuller treatment.

The verse occurs in the famous incident of the Christian delegation from Najran. The Prophet, having explained the doctrine of Isa to them and finding them unwilling to receive it, was commanded by Allah to issue the challenge: come, let us call our sons and your sons, our women and your women, our selves and your selves; then let us invoke the curse of Allah upon the liars. This is *mubahalah* -- mutual invocation of the curse.

The Prophet, complying with the command, brought his sons: Hasan and Husayn. He brought his women: Fatimah. He brought his selves: Ali. Now, "selves" -- *anfusana* -- is the key.

For "our sons," the Prophet brought his actual sons, the children of Fatimah and Ali. The doctrine is therefore that Hasan and Husayn are the Prophet's sons; the verse confirms it. They are not his grandsons in the way the outer reckoning would have it; they are his sons in the inner reading of the verse.

For "our women," the Prophet brought Fatimah. The doctrine is therefore that Fatimah is the Prophet's woman in the structural sense of the verse; she stands in the position of the women of the Prophet's household.

For "our selves," the Prophet brought Ali. The doctrine here is the deepest. Ali is the Prophet's *nafs*, his self. Not in some metaphorical sense but in the structural sense that the verse establishes. What the Prophet's self could do, Ali could do. What was the Prophet's authority, was Ali's authority. The Mubahalah verse is therefore the Quranic establishment of Ali's identity with the Prophet at the level of the self.

The divorce-authority over Aisha follows from this. If Ali is the Prophet's self, then what the Prophet can do, Ali can do; and after the Prophet's passing, what the Prophet would have done, Ali can do in his stead. The Prophet's giving Ali the right to divorce Aisha after his passing is the explicit drawing-out of the structural consequence of the Mubahalah verse.

The teacher noted that this is also why our reverence for Ali is at the level it is. Ali is not, in our doctrine, merely the Prophet's cousin or son-in-law or close companion. He is the *nafs* of the Prophet by the Quranic verse. Our reverence for Ali is the reverence appropriate to that station; anything less is a structural underestimation.

The teacher would also draw out the corollary: those who undermined Ali's wilayah after the Prophet's passing were undermining the Prophet's own *nafs*. The Saqifah was the undermining of the Prophet's *nafs*. The cursing from the seventy thousand pulpits later was the undermining of the Prophet's *nafs*. Umar bin Abd al-Aziz's stopping of the cursing was therefore the restoration of the proper relation; that is why his punishment in the next realm was shortened. The mercy follows the action that restores the structural relation.

### The Heart as the Center of the Bayt

The doctrine of the *qalb haqiqi* beneath the outer Bayt is one of the structural doctrines of the book that bears further attention.

The Baytullah we circumambulate is the outer Bayt. The *taamur*, the heavenly counterpart, is opposite. Between them, beneath the outer Bayt and opposite the *taamur*, is the *qalb haqiqi*, the true heart. The outer Bayt is the veil; the inner heart is the veiled.

This is the structural meaning of *La ilaha illa Allah*. The negation -- *La ilaha* -- is the veil; the affirmation -- *illa Allah* -- is the veiled. The two together constitute the *shahadah*. So the *kalimat al-shahadah*, the outer Bayt, the *taamur* with its inner heart, are all enacting the same negation-affirmation structure.

The teacher noted that within the *qalb* are three cavities -- *thalathat tajwifat* -- like the three jewels of the *shahadah*. The same triad recurs at this level. The three jewels of the *shahadah* are *alif*, *lam*, *ha*; the three cavities of the heart are their structural parallel; the three veils of the Imam (*tabii*, *zilli*, *khas*) are their further structural parallel.

The doctrine of *al-husna* -- the good deed -- is set within this structure. *Al-hasanatu hubbuna wal-sayyiatu bughduna* -- "the good deed is our love, the evil deed is the hatred of us." The good deed is the love of the Ahl al-Bayt. The evil deed is the hatred of them. This is doctrine, not metaphor. Every action that increases the love is a good deed in the deepest sense; every action that decreases the love or substitutes hatred is an evil deed in the deepest sense. The believer who lives by this understanding orients his life by what is happening, in his own heart, with regard to the Ahl al-Bayt.

The verse *u'iddat li-lladhina ahsanu al-husna wa ziyadah* -- "prepared for those who do the good deed and add" -- is therefore the verse of the Paradise of those who increase in love. The Paradise is for those who have taken the *ahd al-sharif* and have not stopped at the taking but have *added* to it. The taking is the entry; the adding is the deepening. Whoever enters the *karrah* having added receives, in the next *karrah*, a rank above the rank he entered.

This is the structural basis of progress through the *karrahs*. The taking opens the chain; the adding deepens the connection; the deepening compounds across the *karrahs*. The believer who knows this structure is the believer who does not merely take the *ahd* and rest; he renews it; he adds; he deepens. Each renewal is a structural deepening of the chain.

The teacher would also note that the believer who has not added enough in one *karrah* is not punished for the lack; he is simply brought back, in the next *karrah*, to the rank he had earned. The structure is fair: you are at the rank you earned. To rise above it requires the adding. To remain at it requires nothing; the structure preserves it. The choice is the believer's.

### The Ten Abrar and the Bottom Layer

The *sakhrah* beneath the Baytullah holds ten *abrar*, the righteous. Nimrud is the last. The names of the other nine are not recovered in the lecture, but Nimrud's position as the last is doctrine.

Why is Nimrud the last? Because he made the worst claim: *ana Rabbukum al-Ala*, "I am your Lord, the Most High." The claim to be God is the worst of claims. It is worse than Yazid's actions, worse than Muawiyah's actions; Yazid and Muawiyah are not among the ten *abrar* at all. Why? Because the ten *abrar* are at a particular structural layer, and Yazid and Muawiyah are at other layers. Nimrud is the last of the ten not because he is the worst overall but because, within the structural category of the ten *abrar* in the *sakhrah*, his place as last is fixed by his claim.

The teacher allowed that the doctrine of the structural layers of the punished is complex, and we do not have the full mapping in our texts. We have certain names placed in certain positions: Nimrud at the bottom of the ten in the *sakhrah*; the opposers (Yazid, Muawiyah, Marwan, Nimrud, Firaun) dragged to Mina at the *Qiyamah* of the Qayam; the *azdad* given forty days of respite after death; the souls thrown to inauspicious peripheral places with senses intact. Each placement has its own meaning. The complete mapping is not in our hands; what we have is enough for the believer to know that the structure of the punishment is precise and that the choices of life have precise structural consequences in the afterlife.

The believer's discipline is therefore not abstract. The disciplines of *iman*, *walayah*, *tawalla*, *tabarra* (loving the friends and dissociating from the enemies) have precise structural consequences. *Tabarra* -- the discipline of disavowal from the enemies of the Ahl al-Bayt -- is not gratuitous; it is the structural safeguard of one's own placement after death. To remain in *tawalla* without *tabarra* is to leave the structure half-set; the full structure requires both.

### The Numerical Values in the Doctrine

The book uses numerical values (the *abjad* values of letters) in several places. *Al-hamd* equals 52 (ha 8 + mim 40 + dal 4). The 52 refers to three (Muhammad, Ali, Fatimah) plus forty-nine Imams up to Abu al-Aimmah. *Awwalu man amana* equals *Ali ibn Abi Talib*.

The doctrine here is that the numerical values of certain Quranic phrases encode the structures of the line. The encoding is not arbitrary; it is part of the design of the revelation. *Al-hamd*, opening every prayer, opens it with the structural count of those who are praised through the prayer. The believer who recites *al-hamd* is invoking, by the very numerical value of the word, the three and the forty-nine.

This is also why our community pays attention to numerical readings of the Quran. The readings are not games; they are part of the verse's structural integration into the doctrine of the line. The reader who has the inner reading is the reader who sees the numerical structure as well as the surface meaning.

The teacher would also note: there are many such readings, and not all are equally weighted. The *abjad* reading of *al-hamd* as 52 is structural; the reading of *Awwalu man amana* as Ali is structural; these are doctrine. Other numerical readings may be more speculative; the believer should be careful not to import speculation as doctrine. The structural readings are those that the *hudud* have preserved.

### The Discipline of the Believer's Mouth

The teacher made, in passing, the observation about the word *jaan* in the Urdu-Persian register. *Jani* in Arabic means "sinner." The affectionate address *jaan* -- "my life" -- can also be heard, in the Arabic root, as "my sinner." The believer should be careful not to address his beloved or his child with *jaan* in a way that, in the Arabic, could be heard as the wrong word.

This is a small point but it stands as a model of a larger discipline: the discipline of the believer's mouth. The believer should be careful of his speech. The words he uses carry, often, structures from beyond his immediate intention. To call his child *jaan* is, in his intention, to call the child his "life." But the Arabic root permits another reading. The believer who is aware of the root may prefer another endearment that does not permit the misreading.

The same discipline applies to the names of Allah, the names of the Imams, the formulas of prayer. The mouth that speaks them should speak them with care. The teacher would say: the believer's mouth is also part of his connection to the chain. The mouth that speaks rightly is the mouth that maintains the chain; the mouth that speaks carelessly is the mouth that weakens the chain.

The believer who maintains the small disciplines of the mouth -- the proper recitation of *Ayat al-Kursi* (with the *zabar* on *al-Aliyya al-Azim*), the soft tone on the last *La ilaha illallah* of the *adhan*, the avoidance of *jaan* -- is the believer who is being shaped by the small disciplines into a more durable connection.

### The Adhan in Its Inner Reading

Let us return to the adhan in its inner reading, because the small disciplines around it carry meanings we have only touched on.

The adhan is the call to prayer. It calls the believers to gather; it also, in the inner reading, calls the souls of those who hear it on other layers. The angelic hearers also gather; the *suwarat* of the departed also hear.

The first *Allahu Akbar* of the adhan is said with emphasis. The emphasis is structural; it announces the beginning. The believer who hears the emphasis is alerted: the call has begun.

The first *La ilaha illallah* is said with emphasis, again. The first declaration is the structural declaration: there is no god but Allah. The emphasis says: this is what we are gathering for.

The last *La ilaha illallah* is said softly. The softness is structural. It alludes to the *ihtizaz* of the last *mushir*, the last person who will depart from his entire circle in the blink of an eye. The softness honors that imminence. The believer who knows the doctrine hears, in the soft last *La ilaha illallah*, the allusion to the final departure.

The *taawwudh* is not said in the adhan because the adhan is not a Quran recitation in the technical sense; it is a call. The *taawwudh* belongs at the opening of Quran recitation; in the adhan, it has no place.

Some may say *Bismillah* before the adhan begins. There is no harm in this; it is not required. The *Bismillah* is an opening that is generally permissible. But the structure of the adhan does not require it.

The verse *wa qulil hamdu lillah... wa kabbirhu takbira* from Surah Bani Israil is the structural anchor of the *takbira*. The verse commands the *takbira*; the adhan enacts the *takbira* in its opening. After the verse, the *salawat* may be recited; this is permitted but not strictly required. Imam-ul-Mamool's *tartib* -- *hamd* first, then *salawat*, then *takbira* -- preserves the proper structural order.

The believer who hears the adhan with this awareness is the believer for whom the adhan is not a routine sound but a structural call. He pauses; he listens; he allows the call to reach him. The call carries the structural declarations; he receives them at every layer of his soul.

### The Doctrine of the Imam's Soul in Final Summary

The doctrine of the Imam's soul, set out in the second question of the book, is so central that it bears a final summary.

*When the Imam passes, he dissolves in the qalb of the Baytullah.* His being becomes substance, *mawad*, in the heart of the Bayt.

*The mawad becomes strength for the hearts of the learned ones from the hudud.* The *hudud* in their lives receive the *mawad* as a trust. The strength of speech, the safety from sin, the accuracy of decision -- these are the *mawad* in action.

*This is the meaning of the burhan of Yusuf.* When the verse says *lawla an raa burhana rabbihi*, the *burhan* is the *mawad* of the Imam that protected Yusuf at the moment of trial.

*At the hudud's passing, the mawad returns to its origin.* The trust is not theirs to keep. They are stewards. At their passing, the *mawad* flows back to the *qalb*.

*The next Imam's soul is formed from what is supplied from the qalb.* The supply includes the *mawad* returning, the water around the *qalb*, the *bataih* adjacent, and what was stored from the first interval.

*The line is therefore continuous.* The passing of the Imam is not the breaking of the line; it is the *constitution* of the line at the next generation. The dissolution in the *qalb*, the strengthening of the *hudud*, the return of the *mawad*, the formation of the next Imam's soul -- all are part of a single continuous mechanism that the *qalb* of the Bayt holds.

*The believer at his own passing follows a parallel pattern.* His *surah* goes to the *hudud-e uliyyah*; his *riyat* go into the *aflak*; his *fuzala* ascend through the 28 stations, the *mihrab*, the center of his *jazirah*, the four pillars, the *rukn-e-aswad*. The believer's mechanism is parallel in structure, scaled to his station.

*The Bayt is therefore the central organ of the cosmos.* All the mechanisms of dissolution, of strengthening, of return, of formation, of ascent run through it. The pilgrim who performs *tawaf* around the Bayt is, in the inner reading, circling the central organ.

This central position of the Bayt connects to many other teachings in the book. The conception of the *nasut khas* of the Imam is in the Bayt al-Haram. The *Rukn al-Asad* is formed from the *fudulat* of Amir al-Muminin gathered there. The *qalb haqiqi* is beneath the Bayt. The *sakhrah* with the ten *abrar* is at the deepest. The verse *wajalna lil-muttaqin imama* -- "and make us imams of the pious" -- is not to be recited in *tawaf* because the structure is too close to the conception there. All of these point to the Bayt as the organ where the deepest mechanisms run.

### The Final Word on Concealment

The book closes with the discipline of concealment as it began. The author's covenant is severe; the reader is bound; the punishment for unauthorized reading is the absolution of *al-Aliyy al-Mabud* and the *mahdudin* and the *hudud* from all duty toward him.

The teacher would say: the discipline is not for the protection of an in-group's pride. The discipline is for the protection of those who are not yet ready. The *haqaiq* are dangerous to the unprepared. The verse-readings, the cosmological mappings, the structural numbers, the doctrines of the soul -- these can dislodge an unprepared soul as easily as they can settle a prepared one. The discipline preserves the readers who have not yet built the substance of preparation.

The believer who has been given the doctrine should therefore be cautious in passing it on. The Madhun who has taken his *ahd* is the layer through which the doctrine descends; below the Madhun, the descent is regulated by the Madhun's judgment. The believer does not pass the doctrine to anyone who asks; he passes it through the layers that the *hudud* preserve.

This is also why we do not write of the doctrine in public ways without care. The book in our hands has been preserved across the centuries by the discipline of who may handle it. The believer who passes it on too widely undoes the preservation. The believer who guards it with the discipline preserves it for those who, after him, will need it.

The treasures are stored. They are stored for the masters of partial infallibility, for the duat. The believers participate in the treasures through the chain. The discipline is part of the participation.

*Salam alayhim ajmain abad al-abadin.*

## Doctrinal Synthesis: How the Pieces Hold Together

We close with a synthesis. The book is a single work, though it has come to us through fifteen lecture sessions. The synthesis is the seeing-together of what the lectures together establish.

### The Two Hierarchies

There are, throughout the book, two parallel hierarchies that the doctrine continually keeps in view. The first hierarchy is the hierarchy of the *Aimmah*: the Pure Imams, in whom *kulli ismah* is realized, descending from Hussain through the line of the Aimmah Fatimiyyin, culminating in the present Imam and, in the future, in the Qaim. The second hierarchy is the hierarchy of the *duat*: the summoners, in whom *juzi ismah* is realized, connected through Hasan and carrying the call across the eras of concealment, culminating in the Dai of the present time.

The two hierarchies are not in competition. They are complementary. The Aimmah carry the Imamate; the duat carry the summoning. The Aimmah are veiled in the era of concealment; the duat are visible, serving the veiled Aimmah and the believers in their connection to the Aimmah. The believer's chain of *sirat al-mustaqim* runs through the duat to the Aimmah. The duat are the visible portion of the chain; the Aimmah are the veiled portion.

This structure is what makes the doctrine of *juzi ismah* meaningful. The duat are not the same as the Aimmah; they are preserved at a different level. *Kulli ismah* is for the Aimmah; *juzi ismah* is for the duat. The treasures of the book are stored *for the duat* -- because the duat, in their *juzi ismah*, need treasures from which to draw. The Aimmah do not need treasures stored in books; their substance is direct. The duat need the books because the books are the structured deposit from which the duat draw the substance to carry the era's call.

This is also why the book is by Sayyidna Ali bin Hibatullah, son of a Dai, in the line of duat. The author writes from inside the office of *juzi ismah*; he writes for his fellows in that office; the book is the dais' book for the duat.

### The Discipline of Boundaries

A second principle that holds the book together is the discipline of boundaries.

The boundary of speculation: we do not speculate about *kayfiyyat* of the *ibda*. *La min shayin wa la fi shayin*; the intellect has no scope there. Speculation is *shirk* and *halak*.

The boundary of broadcasting: we do not broadcast the secrets. *Whoever broadcasts has prepared his seat in fire.* The doctrine is preserved by being kept where it can be received properly.

The boundary of giving: we do not give the *aqiqah* meat or Eid al-Adha meat to non-Muslims. The discipline preserves the structural channel of the meat's consequence.

The boundary of slaughter: we do not eat the natural death of wild animals (except the pigeons of Baytullah and Madinah). The discipline preserves the separation of the *nafs al-hissiyyah* from the body.

The boundary of reading: we do not open this book without the permission of one who has authority of *taqdim* and *takhir*. The discipline preserves the doctrine from those who are not yet ready.

The boundary of imitation: we do not take the Prophet's pattern of total charity as our personal model. We follow the middle path. The discipline preserves us from the extremes that belong to the *masum*.

These boundaries are not arbitrary restrictions. They are the structural conditions under which the doctrine can be carried forward in the eras of the *karrahs*. Without them, the doctrine would be diluted in the broadcasting, the eating, the imitating, the reading. With them, the doctrine is preserved at the depth where it can continue to feed the line.

### The Pattern of the Numbers

A third principle is the pattern of the structural numbers. We have traced 28 and 29; we should add a note on 3, 7, 12, and 4 as well, because these numbers also run through the book.

*Three*: three veils of the Imam (*tabii*, *zilli*, *khas*), three jewels of the *shahadah* (alif, lam, ha), three cavities of the heart, three days of the *riyat* after burial, three days of the Qaim, three *sufara* of the Rasul (Abd al-Muttalib, Abu Talib with Abdullah, Ali with the Rasul). Three is the structural triad.

*Four*: four ranks (*nubuwwah*, *risalah*, *imamah*, *wasayat*), four sections of the *shahadah* (la, ilaha, illa, Allah), four pillars of the Bayt, four doors of *safrah* (one in Hadramaut). Four is the structural square.

*Seven*: seven Imams' station of rest, seven *fusul* of the *shahadah*'s writing, seven *istighfars* after *Ayat al-Kursi*, seven *dawr al-sitr* cycles, seven verses of *Surat al-Fatihah*, seven great intellects, seven tests of the Prophet before *risalat*, seven after. Seven is the structural completion.

*Twelve*: twelve *jazair*, twelve duat, twelve hearts, twelve letters of the *kalimat al-shahadah*, twelve duat of the *hadarat*, twelve duat of the *jazair*, twelve consecutive *rakat* of *salat al-layl*. Twelve is the structural span.

*Twenty-five*: twenty-five words of *Surat al-Fatihah*, twenty-five prophets of the Quran.

*Twenty-eight*: 2 + 3 + 4 + 7 + 12 = 28, the eternal order; 28 letters of the *kalimat al-shahadah*; 28 stations of the believer's *fuzala* ascent; 28 letters of the Quran alphabet; 28th in any sequence as *jami*; 28 titles of the first *nass* of Dawud bin Ajabshah.

*Twenty-nine*: 29 *muqattaat*; 29th in any sequence as the breaker; Muhammadun Rasulullah in verse 29 of Surah al-Fath.

*Three hundred sixty*: 360 manifestations of the *walad*'s integration per *kawr*; 360 *nufus* in the human body.

*Forty thousand and four hundred thousand*: 400,000 *kawr* x 400,000 *kawr* = one *mawr*.

*Fifty thousand*: 50,000 years of this *dawr* of the Qaim.

These numbers are the structural lattice. They are not numerology in the loose sense; they are the structural countings of the elements of the doctrine. When the believer sees one of these numbers, he can usually find the structural element it corresponds to. The believer who has internalized the lattice has internalized the doctrine at the structural level.

### The Triad of Disciplines

A fourth principle that organizes the believer's life is the triad of disciplines: *iman* (faith), *amal* (action), *adab* (manners).

*Iman* is the inner orientation toward the chain. The believer's heart is set on the Imam, the Dai, the *hudud*. *Iman* is not abstract belief; it is the structural orientation.

*Amal* is the action that follows from *iman*. The prayer, the fast, the *zakat*, the Hajj, the giving of charity, the attending of *majlis*, the taking of *ahd*. *Amal* is the embodied expression of *iman*.

*Adab* is the manners of the chain. How one stands in the presence of the Dai. How one greets a fellow believer. How one handles the *aqiqah* meat. How one performs *wudu*. How one closes the *Bismillah*. *Adab* is the small disciplines that maintain the connection.

The three together constitute the believer's life. *Iman* without *amal* is empty; *amal* without *adab* is graceless; *adab* without *iman* is theatrical. The triad must hold.

This is also why the book has so many practical rulings alongside its cosmological doctrines. The doctrines are not for cognition alone; they are for the *amal* and the *adab*. The believer who reads the doctrine of the three veils of the Imam is also reading the doctrine of how to perform his *tasbih* with attention to *Allahu Akbar*, *Subhanallah*, and the *dhat-i Imam*. The cognition and the practice run together.

### The Continuity of the Line

A fifth principle is the continuity of the line.

The Imam dissolves; the *mawad* enters the *hudud*; the *hudud* return the *mawad*; the next Imam is formed. The mechanism runs across generations. The line is, structurally, never broken.

The duat are named in the praise: Sulayman, Jafar the seventh, Muhammad bin Fuad the Mim, Ali bin Sulayman the Ayn, Ibrahim the *sarib*, Muhammad bin Ismail the emigrant. Each name carries its station; the names together carry the line.

The believer connects to the line through his Madhun, his Mukasir, his Dai. The connection is the *sirat al-mustaqim*. The connection is preserved across his life and, through the *karrah*, into his next life.

The continuity is therefore at every level: in the formation of the Imam's soul, in the naming of the duat, in the believer's chain, in the *karrah*. The cycle and the line work together.

This is the structural answer to the question of whether the line can be lost. It cannot. The mechanism of continuity is too deeply embedded. The duat may face the Pharaoh of the age; the believers may face persecution; the discipline of concealment may be severe; but the line itself, by structural necessity, continues.

The believer's role is to participate in the continuity, not to worry about it. The continuity does not depend on his worry; it depends on his participation. His daily prayer, his *adab*, his *amal*, his *iman* -- these are his participation. The line takes care of itself through the participation of its members.

### The End and the Return

A sixth and final principle is the doctrine of the end and the return.

The end is the *Mawr al-Azam*, the great cycle. Nine *mawrs* remain. At the end, all are absorbed into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*; only the domain of the *Aql al-Awwal* and the *Mumbaith al-Awwal* remain.

The return -- *raja* -- is the cycle of the 1000 years. Each believer returns in the same rank, in the same place. The cycle is structurally fair.

The two together orient the believer's life. He is not in an endless drift; the *Mawr al-Azam* has its end. He is not in a single life; the *raja* gives him return. His effort in this *karrah* shapes his rank in the next; his ranks across the *karrahs* compound; the compound brings him, over the *mawrs*, toward the absorption.

The believer who has internalized this orientation is the believer whose decisions are made on the right scale. He is not deciding for this lifetime alone; he is deciding for the *karrahs* and the *mawrs*. The disciplines that feel difficult in the lifetime are easy at the scale of the *mawr*. The luxuries that feel necessary in the lifetime are nothing at the scale of the *mawr*. The orientation transforms the felt weights.

This is also why the line's wisdom is gathered for the duat in the *Kunooz al-Hikmah*. The treasures are scaled to the *mawr*. The duat draw from them at the scale at which they must serve. The believers receive what the duat distribute at the scale appropriate to their position. The whole is scaled to the cycle that contains it.

## Supplementary Expositions: Doctrines That Bear Further Setting Out

### The Five Tones of the Shahadah and the Place of Yunus

We noted earlier that the *kalimat al-shahadah* has appeared in five tones across the Quran: *La ilaha illa Allah*, *La ilaha illa Hu*, *La ilaha illa Ana*, *La ilaha illa alladhi*, and *La ilaha illa Anta*. Of these, *La ilaha illa Anta* has come only once, in the supplication of Yunus *alayhi salam*. The rest have been repeated.

This singular occurrence of *La ilaha illa Anta* in Yunus's supplication is itself a doctrine worth unfolding. Yunus was the prophet who was swallowed by the great fish; he was in the depth of the sea, in the belly of the creature, in a darkness within darkness within darkness. In that state he called upon Allah with the unique formula. *La ilaha illa Anta, subhanaka, inni kuntu min al-zalimin* -- "There is no god but You, glory be to You, indeed I was among the wrongdoers."

The teacher would say: this is the formula of the soul in the deepest enclosure. *Illa Anta* -- "but You" -- addresses the divine directly, in the second person, in a way that the other tones do not. *Illa Hu* keeps the divine in the third person; *Illa Ana* speaks as the divine in the first; *Illa Anta* speaks *to* the divine in the second. The vocative directness is the mark of the depth of Yunus's enclosure.

The formula has come once because the situation calling it forth is rare. The believer in the depths -- in the darknesses within darknesses -- may also reach for this formula. It is not for the ordinary moment; it is for the moment in the belly of the fish.

The believer who has, in his life, been in such a moment, knows what the formula carries. It carries the recognition that no door is open except the door of the divine address. It carries the bowed *subhanaka*, the glory ascribed even in the depth. It carries the *inni kuntu min al-zalimin*, the acknowledgment that the depth was earned by the speaker's own wrongs. The formula does not bargain; it confesses.

The deliverance of Yunus from the fish is the consequence of the formula. The depth was opened by the wrongs; the depth was closed by the formula; the deliverance was given because the formula was rightly spoken. This is itself a teaching about the structure of deliverance: the depth is not opened by mere endurance; it is opened by the formula spoken from the depth.

### The Verse of the Dark Valley and the Lightning

The plot against the Prophet before Ghadir bears further setting out, because it is the moment when the line was preserved at the cost of revelation.

Aisha told her father Abu Bakr the Prophet's secret about Ali's succession. Aisha had been told something in confidence; she did not keep it. The breaking of the confidence set the chain of events in motion. The munafiqin, on learning the secret, knew that the declaration was coming at Ghadir; they decided to prevent it by killing the Prophet before he could make it.

The dark valley before Ghadir was the chosen place. The Prophet would have to pass through it on the journey. The munafiqin gathered there, hidden, waiting.

Allah intervened directly. The lightning that flashed was not ordinary lightning. It was lightning that lasted longer than the natural lightning lasts; it was lightning that lit the dark valley as if it were noon; it was lightning that the Prophet and Hudhayfah al-Yamani saw together, and in the seeing they recognized the faces of those who had gathered.

The recognition was the preservation. The munafiqin, once their faces were seen, withdrew. The killing did not proceed. The Prophet passed through the valley and arrived at Ghadir. The declaration was made.

Hudhayfah's later oath to Umar -- that he had been among the conspirators -- is the witness. Umar pressed Hudhayfah to name them; Hudhayfah, under the oath, confirmed his presence in the group. The doctrine preserves the name of the witness; the doctrine preserves the fact of the oath.

The structural meaning is precise. The line was preserved by direct divine intervention at the moment of greatest threat. The intervention took the form of prolonged lightning. The recognition of the conspirators was the means of their retreat.

This is also a teaching about the moment of crisis. At the moment when the line is most threatened, the line is most directly preserved. The believer who finds himself, in his own life, at a moment of crisis around the chain may pray for a similar intervention. It may not take the form of lightning; it may take some other form. But the structural principle is preserved: at the moment of greatest threat, the line is most directly preserved by the divine attention.

### The Two Brothers of Imam Husayn

The doctrine that with Imam Husayn the two lower ranks dominated over the two higher ranks bears a fuller explanation.

In every Imam, all four ranks are present. The four ranks are *nubuwwah* (prophethood), *risalah* (mission), *imamah* (Imamate), and *wasayat* (succession). The two higher ranks are *imamah* and *wasayat*; the two lower are *nubuwwah* and *risalah*. The classification places the higher above the lower because the higher pertain to the inner reality of the Imamate, the bearing of the *batini* doctrine, and the lower pertain to the outer reality, the public office, the carrying of the *zahiri* responsibilities.

In Hasan's time, the two higher ranks were *manifest* with Hasan; he was the Imam. The two lower were manifest with Husayn; he was under his brother's authority. In Husayn's time, after Hasan's passing, the four came together with Husayn; but, the teacher noted, the two lower dominated over the two higher in his own being. Husayn was the Imam, but his outward office -- the bearing of the *zahiri* responsibility, the standing against Yazid, the procession to Karbala, the martyrdom -- was a manifestation of the two lower ranks. The two higher were present but veiled by the two lower.

The first Imam in whom the four ranks were combined with the two higher *dominating* was Ali Zayn al-Abidin. He is therefore called *Adam-e-Aal-e-Aba*, the Adam of the family of the Prophet. The Adam-status marks the structural priority: just as Adam was the first man, Ali Zayn al-Abidin is the first man of the family of the Prophet in the fully-combined-with-higher-dominating sense.

This is a precise doctrine that resolves what might otherwise be a tension. Husayn is, doctrinally, the Imam; we revere him as such; the line of the Aimmah passes through him. But the *internal* structure of his Imamate had the two lower ranks dominating; this is part of the meaning of his being the manifest occulter -- *al-muzhar al-ghaybah bil-qatli lada ahl al-raybah*. He manifested the occultation through the death; the death was the work of the two lower ranks; the two higher remained protected within the two lower.

Ali Zayn al-Abidin then began the proper combined manifestation. From him forward, the structure is the dominance of the higher over the lower. The pattern thereafter is settled.

### The Five Teachers of the Prophet Before Bathah

The teacher noted, in connection with Ubayy ibn Kab and Khadijah, that the Prophet had five teachers in succession before the *bathah*. Ubayy was the first; Khadijah was the last; the three between them are not named in the lecture but are implicit in the structure.

The five teachers each taught the Prophet a portion of the laws and principles of the past *nutuqa*. Ubayy taught Adam's laws; the structure requires that the other teachers taught the laws of Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, and Isa in turn -- the five natiqs of the previous era. The Prophet's full inheritance of the line is therefore constituted by these five.

Each teacher, after teaching, became *mamnun* to the Prophet. This is the doctrine: the teacher of the *natiq* receives the *natiq*'s authority back into himself, because what he taught had been received from the *natiq* in another order of being. The *mamnun* is not a debtor but an inheritor of the connection.

Khadijah, the last teacher before the *bathah*, became *mamnun* to the Prophet and then became the teacher of Ali. This double-relation is what makes Khadijah's place so unusual. She is teacher and *mamnun* simultaneously; she carries Ali's instruction; she is therefore the *Maryam* of the inner reading, with Ali as *Ibn Maryam* in our doctrine. Her exemption from the five prayers follows from this teacher-relation: a teacher of the *natiq* and of the *wasi* operates in a different mode from the ordinary ritual obligation.

The doctrine of the five teachers also explains why the *bathah* was not an interruption from outside the Prophet's preparation but the manifestation of what had been gradually built. The Prophet had been receiving the laws and principles of the *nutuqa* from the five teachers; the *bathah* announced what he already held. The *nubuwwah* did not descend on an unprepared vessel; it descended on a vessel that had been prepared by the structural transmission of the inheritance of the line.

### The Salman al-Farsi Exception

We have noted that Salman al-Farsi is exceptional: the only person in the *Dawr al-Sitr* who became *Bab al-Abwab* for both the Nabi and the Wasi. Let us draw out what this exception means.

The *Bab al-Abwab* is the structural office of the gate. The Imam's son becomes Imam *bil-fil* through the *Bab al-Abwab*; the office gathers the believers' forms and transmits the gathering. There is normally one *Bab al-Abwab* per office. Salman is the exception: he served as *Bab al-Abwab* for the Nabi (the Prophet) and for the Wasi (Ali).

The doctrine of the exception is that Salman's inner reality was already constituted in a way that allowed the double office. His outward biography passed through other religions, but his inner reality was already from the *hudud* of the line. By the time he came to the Prophet, he was prepared to serve at the office that ordinarily would have required a different individual.

This is also why, in our community, Salman is honored as he is. *Salman minna Ahl al-Bayt* -- "Salman is from us, the People of the House." The Prophet's statement is not a courtesy; it is a doctrinal recognition. Salman was already from the Ahl al-Bayt in the inner reading, and the Prophet's statement makes the inner reading visible.

The structural lesson is that the line of the *hudud* extends, in its inner reality, into figures whose outer biographies pass through other communities. The believer should be cautious about drawing the line of the *hudud* tightly around the persons of one's own immediate community. Salman, Aristotle, the confidants of Isa -- these are reminders that the *ayan* of the line are wider than the *ashkhas* we can see in the present.

### The Discipline of the Body in Death

The teacher noted, in connection with the *natiqa* and the *hissiya*, the doctrine of the noble death.

The *natiqa* absorbs the *hissiya* through life. The absorption is gradual. By the time of death, the absorption is more or less complete depending on the discipline of the life.

If the absorption is complete: the *natiqa* departs with the *hissiya* gathered within it. The body is left without the *hissiya*. The body is, then, properly dead -- without the sensitive soul, the body cannot retain its sensations; the corpse does not linger as a sentient thing. This is the noble death.

If the absorption is incomplete: the *hissiya* remains in the body at death. The body retains the *hissiya*; the corpse has some residue of the sensitive soul; the death is not the clean separation it should have been. This is what the doctrine calls the bad death.

The remedy across the lifetime is the discipline of praising -- *Allahu Akbar, Alhamdulillah, Subhanallah, La ilaha illallah*. These phrases, especially *Alhamdulillah*, work on the *hissiya*; they gradually orient the sensations toward praise; they gradually carry the *hissiya* into the *natiqa*'s frame. By the end of life, the believer who has practiced the praising has gathered the *hissiya* into the *natiqa* substantially.

This is also why our funeral disciplines pay attention to what is done in the three days. The *fatiha-khwani* and the other offerings work on what remains. If the absorption has been incomplete, the offerings can still help; the family's prayer and the meals for the poor can still affect the gathering after death. The discipline of the three days is therefore a continuation of the lifetime's discipline; the family takes up what the deceased could not complete.

This doctrine also organizes how we think about the death of a believer. The believer who dies after a life of praising has likely died the noble death; we honor him as such. The believer who dies after a life of carelessness may have died the bad death; we pray for him intensively, and the three days are used as fully as the family can use them. The doctrine does not condemn; it organizes our response.

### The Steam from the Pot

The small rule about letting the steam escape from cooked meat carries further implication.

The slaughter has separated the *nafs al-hissiyyah* from the animal by the *Allahu Akbar*. But the separation is not always complete at the moment of slaughter. Some of the *nafs* may remain in the body. The cooking opens the body further; the heat releases the remaining *nafs*; the *nafs* departs through the steam.

If the pot is sealed tightly and the steam does not escape, the *nafs* may be trapped in the food. The food, when eaten, would then carry the *nafs* into the eater. The eater would receive the *nafs al-hissiyyah* of the slaughtered animal as part of his own body.

The doctrine therefore requires that the steam be allowed to escape. The pot is not sealed; some opening is left; the steam departs; the *nafs* departs with it. The food, when eaten, is free of the *nafs*.

This is a small discipline of the kitchen but it bears the same structural weight as the slaughter discipline. The slaughter and the steam together complete the separation of the *nafs* from the meat. Either one alone is insufficient.

The believer who is mindful of the steam in cooking is being mindful at the level of detail the doctrine intends. The discipline is not in the slaughter alone; it is in every step from the animal to the plate.

### The Mansub of Yemen

We have noted that the *mansub* of Yemen, of Najran, is superior to all *manasib*. Let us draw out the structural reason.

The *mansub* is an officer of the dawah, ranking below the Dai but above the Mukasir and the Madhun. There are *manasib* across the *jazair*; each *jazirah* has its own structure of *manasib*. But the *mansub* of Yemen, of Najran, is the *Dai's successor bil-quwwah* -- the Dai's successor in potentiality.

When the Dai passes, the *nass sharif* may be performed on the *mansub* of Yemen, elevating him to the Dai. The structural potentiality of his being the successor confers a present elevation. His station today is above the station of the other *manasib* because his future, if the *nass* falls on him, is to be Dai.

This is itself a doctrine about the relation of present and future in the ranks. The future office, in our doctrine, casts a present shadow. The one who may, by *nass*, ascend to a higher office holds a higher station now because of the potentiality. The structure honors the future as part of the present.

The *mansub* of Yemen, of Najran, therefore holds a uniquely elevated station. He is the Dai's right hand in his own time; he is the Dai's potential successor for the future. The believers of Najran, in particular, honor him with the depth this dual position requires.

### The Three Jazair

The three *jazair* are Yemen, Hind, and Sindh. The Dai of Yemen leads prayer in these three only; he does not lead prayer in the other islands.

The structural meaning is that the three are connected in a special way through the Dai of Yemen. The Dai's office spans the three; his prayer-leadership in the three is the marker of the span. The other islands are not connected through his prayer-leadership; they have their own structures.

This is also why the *mansub* of Yemen, the Dai's potential successor, is structured to cover the three. The Dai's office and the *mansub* of Yemen together hold the three together.

For the believer in Hind or Sindh, the connection to Yemen is therefore not merely sentimental. It is structural. The Dai of Yemen is the Dai of his three *jazair*; the believer in Hind or Sindh is in the Dai's prayer-circle in a way that the believer in another *jazirah* is not. The reverence and the connection are at this level.

The Dai's leading prayer outside the three is an exception that, as we have noted, was given only to Sayyidna Muhammad bin Ismail for Habasha, for the loftiness of his station. The exception is therefore very rare; the rule is precise.

### Final Note on the Author and His Single Book

We close with a return to the author.

Sayyidna Ali bin Hibatullah wrote one book. He wrote it during his father's lifetime, in obedience to the command. He passed away during his father's lifetime, becoming a *ghayr-munfarid Dai*. He did not have the time, or the office, to write more.

The single book is *Kunooz al-Hikmah*. The treasures stored for the masters of partial infallibility are in this one book. The duat after him have drawn from these treasures; the duat of our time still draw.

The author's humility in the introduction is precise. He pleads deficiency of knowledge, distance of nature, weakness of understanding. He pleads the obstacles of distant lands. He attributes all correctness to the Dai who caused his tongue to flow; he attributes all error to himself.

This humility is not theatrical. It is structural. The author is at the office of *juzi ismah*; he writes from inside that office; the office requires the humility because the office is not *kulli ismah*. The humility is the correct epistemic disposition of one who carries *juzi ismah*.

The reader who comes to the book with this awareness comes appropriately. He is reading a book by a master of partial infallibility, written for the masters of partial infallibility, about the treasures stored for them. He is, in the reading, participating in the office's work.

The treasures will yield what they will yield. The discipline of concealment will hold what it must hold. The line will continue across the *karrahs* and the *kawrs* and the *mawrs*. The Mehdi will come at his time; the Qaim will manifest in the three days of his office; the believers will perform the *raja* in the 1000-year cycle; at the end of the ninth *mawr* remaining, all will be absorbed into the *Mumbaith al-Awwal*.

Until then, the believers participate in the line. The duat draw from the treasures. The author's book continues, century after century, to feed the office for which it was written.

## A Last Word on Reading This Book

The reader who has come this far will recognize that the book repays slow reading. The doctrines do not yield to a single pass. The structural numbers come back at different levels. The same teaching is opened from one direction in an early section and from another direction in a later one. The Imam's three veils in the second question return as the three jewels of the *shahadah*, return again as the three cavities of the heart, return again as the three layers of the *nasut*. The believer who has been through the book once will, on the second reading, see what he could not have seen the first time; on the third, he will see what he could not have seen on the second.

The teacher would say: this is the nature of the *haqaiq*. They are not propositions to be memorized; they are structures to be inhabited. The believer who has inhabited the structure for a year reads them differently from the believer who is encountering them for the first time. The doctrine is not in the words; it is in what the words point to. The pointing requires the believer to follow with attention.

The book is also one that, the discipline of concealment requires, is not for every reader. The author has bound the reader by covenant. Whoever has come this far without proper permission is, in the strict reading, in violation. Whoever has come this far with the permission of one who has authority of *taqdim* and *takhir* is in the proper relation. The covenant should not be set aside.

For the reader in the proper relation, the book is a treasure to live with. The doctrines settle into the believer over years. The disciplines of *wudu* and *adhan* and *sahri* and *iftar* and slaughter and *aqiqah* and visitation, taken from the book, shape the believer's days. The cosmology of the *karrah* and the *mawr* shapes the believer's sense of time. The doctrine of the *sirat al-mustaqim* as one's own chain shapes the believer's relation to those above him in the hierarchy.

The book is, in this sense, a manual for the office of *juzi ismah*. The duat who hold the office use the book as their structured deposit. The believers receive what the duat distribute. The chain is preserved across the eras.

We close with a small return to the very opening of the book. *Al-hamdu lillahil-azim jalla nasutun khas min riyadh ahlil-ikhtisas.* All praise is for Allah, the Great, who manifested a special human form from the gardens of the people of specialty. The phrase is opening; it is also a doctrine. We are the gardens. The Prophet ascended through what we, in our small way, contributed to. The line that runs from the Prophet down through the Aimmah and the duat passes also through us. We are not bystanders to the line; we are constituents of its substance.

To know this is to receive the book at the right level. The book is not by an author writing about events outside our reach; it is by one of the line, writing about the inner reality of the line, in which we are participants. The treasures stored for the masters of partial infallibility are stored, ultimately, for those who, through them, will hold us together across the *karrahs* and bring us to the absorption at the end.

May we be kept in our chain. May the chain be preserved. May the chain bring us, in the end, to where the chain itself ends.
