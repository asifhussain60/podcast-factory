# Curation Log: Spans Removed in the Denoise Pass

Spans removed from the raw transcript during the production of the enhanced reading edition, with the reason for each removal category. Every ledger teaching has been preserved in full exposition; nothing under the categories below is a teaching of the book.

- **Audio / technical crosstalk and call-management dialogue** (every lecture): repeated exchanges of "Muslim bhai, can you hear?", "the sound is cutting", "your camera must be off", "Shabbir bhai, connect with Muslim bhai", "let's dial it again", "the 40 minutes are up", phone-call interruptions and reconnects. These are recording-platform artefacts, not content of the teaching.

- **Greetings, polite formalities, repeated salaams, and small talk** (every lecture): "Assalamu alaikum", "Thank you very much", "How are you?", "Shukran", repeated requests for prayers for personal health, "He can't even sit on a chair", "Bismillah... some children should say the Fatiha for the Prophet" — pastoral exchanges with no doctrinal content.

- **Recitations and closing supplications repeated verbatim** (every lecture): the full Fatiha and *al-hamdulillahi rabbil alameen ... wa la-l-dallin* opening and closing prayers, the full prayers for the Dai of the time and Imam of the time, and the full *bismillah ... bismillah ... bismillah* triple recitations. The text preserves the substance of these prayers in compact summary form at section boundaries; the verbatim repetitions are removed.

- **Repeated requests for confirmation that audio is being received** (every lecture, especially lectures 3, 5, 6): "Hashim bhai, are you getting it?", "Is the sound coming?", "Hello, hello", "It's getting muted", "Are you back?". Pure technical chatter.

- **Asides about the lecture's transcription, manuscript, or printing details** (lectures 1, 5, 12): "Your copy has sin. My copy has the", "It shouldn't be the", "This new sahifah was made", "the manuscripts were there, Nasrullah Sahab Muqaddas and Abdullah Sahab Muqaddas had taken them" — preserved only where they bear on a teaching (e.g., the inclusion of Sayyidna Muhammad bin Faraj in the line, T123).

- **False starts and self-corrections by the teacher** (every lecture): partial sentences begun and restarted, "no, not that, but rather", "wait, let me look at it", "What was I saying?" — characteristic of spoken lectures; reduced to the corrected statement in the edition.

- **Side conversations among the audience about logistics** (every lecture): "I want to read the introduction", "How will you start?", "We do it like this with our Fatiha", "I will read from jin barayeta", "This is the whole book, I have only brought a few pages", "It's good that you have brought it this way" — gatherings about who reads what next, not content.

- **Repeated paraphrase of the same point within a single span** (every lecture): the teacher characteristically restates a phrase two or three ways within a single breath ("it means... that is... in other words..."), which in spoken form aids comprehension; in prose this is consolidated to a single clear statement.

- **Personal recollections that do not bear on a doctrine** (lectures 4, 5, 8): the teacher's anecdotes about his own travel ("the first grave in Baroda, Wali Muhammad Sahab's small mausoleum"), small geographical asides ("the distance is from here to, let's say, Grand Road station" — preserved where it illustrates a doctrine, removed where it does not), and Hyderabad announcement examples about *sawab* vs *thawab* (preserved as part of T-near-introduction definition).

- **Lecture 13 in its entirety**: lecture 13 was four lines in the raw transcript and produced no teaching entries in the ledger; the content was off-topic chatter at the start of a session. Removed.

- **Repeated readings of the same Arabic passage by the teacher to the audience for transcription** (every lecture): the teacher reads a fragment, the audience reads it back, the teacher corrects, the audience reads again. The edition preserves the final Arabic verbatim in a single instance.

- **Audience prompts to the teacher's wife or assistant** (lectures 3, 5): brief domestic interruptions of no doctrinal content.

- **Cell phone interruptions, doorbell, requests not to answer phones** (every lecture): "Don't answer the phone", phone rings, brief interruption while a call is rejected.

- **Repeated reading of the same Quranic verse for clarification** (every lecture): the teacher will read a verse, then re-read it after a clarification, then read it a third time after a fuller explanation. The edition preserves one full reading and the explanation.

- **Repetitive blessings on the Prophet and his family that recur as sentence-fillers** (every lecture): *sallallahu alayhi wa alihi wa sallam* / *alayhi salam* repeated at the close of every name mention. These are preserved at key namings and elided in their incidental repetitions.
