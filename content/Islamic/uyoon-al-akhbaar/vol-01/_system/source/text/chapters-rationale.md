## Source chapter 1 — The Author, His Sources, and Why This History Survives

`ch01-who-wrote-this-history-and-how-it-survived.txt` — Source chapter 1 (5,769 words, lines 41–247) is the volume's editorial apparatus: a publisher's preface, a long survey of the modern recovery of Ismaili studies, and a biographical notice on the author and his works. Taken as printed it is almost entirely noise by the root denoise contract — book-object provenance, editorial methodology, thanks, donation and microfilm records. The plan's own split reason had already reconfigured it into one orientation unit rather than dropping it, and that judgement holds: buried inside the apparatus is a real body of teaching a first-time listener genuinely needs, namely why an Ismaili-authored history of the Imams is a rarity at all, and by what chain this one survived. The episode therefore keeps exactly that content and strips the rest. Dropped as apparatus: the French Institute preface and its list of per-volume editors, the closing acknowledgements, the named library donations and shelf/microfilm numbers, the modern publication history of volumes four through six (Ghalib, al-Dashrawi, al-Ya'lawi), and the bibliographic footnote apparatus. Kept and developed as teaching: the pre-1950s condition of the evidence and its fabrication by hostile parties; the recovery of the manuscripts and the scale of the literature it revealed; taqiyya and the jurist-preacher's vocation as the two reasons so little Ismaili history was written; the Fatimid and Alamut periods of official historiography and the systematic destruction of both archives in 567/1171 and 654/1256; the office of absolute da'i in the period of concealment and Idris Imad al-Din's life within it, including the plague of 840/1436, the thirteen-year exile from Mount Haraz and his own sentence on returning, and the designation of his son by nass; the migration of the Fatimid literary heritage to Yemen under al-Qadi Lamak ibn Malik al-Hammadi and its custody by the Yemeni da'wa after 524/1130; the seven-part structure and the doctrinal weight of the sab'; the part-by-part contents; the Ismaili and non-Ismaili sources and the Fatimid documentary archive behind the work; the two later histories; the copying requirement at Surat; and the candid scholarly verdict on the author's partisanship and undifferentiated sourcing, together with the Hafizi and Nizari divergences. Three concept sections carry these — the state of the evidence, the man and his office, the book and its sources — each opening, developing and landing inside the episode, with the source's two mutually inconsistent accounts of Idris's Yemeni alliances resolved to the fuller and more specific one rather than asserting both. Format is deep_dive: a single position unfolded in layers with no named party arguing back. Essentiality is `optional` rather than `core`, because this is orientation reconfigured from apparatus and a listener may go straight to episode 2 without losing the historical thread.
