# Qusayy Takes Mecca

From Naṣr the line runs down to Qusayy ibn Kilab, and with him the story settles in Mecca.

His father, Kilab ibn Murrah, died while Qusayy was still a small child. His brother Zuhrah was older than he, and their mother was Fatimah bint Saʿd ibn Sayl. After the death of Kilab ibn Murrah, Rabiʿah ibn Ḥaram ibn ʿUdhrah ibn Saʿd ibn Zayd ibn Manat came from Quḍaʿah to Mecca and married Fatimah, the mother of Zuhrah and Qusayy, the two sons of Kilab. He took her away with him to his people, the Banu ʿUdhrah, and she carried Qusayy with her, for he was newly weaned. Zuhrah remained behind with his own people, since he was by then a grown man.

The name of Qusayy was Zayd. His mother called him Qusayy because he had been carried far from his home. He grew up in the household of Rabiʿah, and he came to dislike living anywhere but Mecca and longed to join himself to it. He went out with the pilgrims of Quḍaʿah, and when he reached Mecca he settled there and grew up in nobility, mastery, and generous character.

He was a handsome man, hardy, full of vigour, intelligent, dignified, chaste, and generous, for God had favoured him with the fatherhood of His Messenger and had placed the line of prophethood in his offspring, and so completed in him the traits of goodness and honour.

Concerning the Banu Qusayy, al-Ḥarith ibn Ẓalim said:

> وَإِخْوَتَهُمْ نُسِبْتُ إِلَى لُؤَيِّ
> إِذَا فَارَقْتُ ثَعْلَبَةَ بْنَ سَعْدِ
> وَحَيِّ هُمْ أَكَارِمُ كُلِّ حَيِّ
> إِلَى نَسَبٍ كَرِيْمٍ غَيْرٍ وَغْلٍ
> قَرَابِيْنُ الإِلَهِ بَنُو قُصَيِّ
> فَإِنْ يُعْصَبْ بِهِمْ نَسَبِي فَمِنْهُمْ

"And to their brothers I traced myself, to Luʾayy, when I parted from Thaʿlabah ibn Saʿd; a clan who are the noblest of every clan, of a lineage generous and free of baseness. The Banu Qusayy are the offerings of God; and if my lineage be bound to them, then I am of them."

It was said that he called them offerings, meaning that through them nearness to God is sought, because they are the dwellers of the House, the people of السِّقَايَة, the الرِّفَادَة, the السِّيَادَة, the الرِّياسَة, and the اللِّوَاء.

They held to an inheritance from the religion of their fathers Ibrahim and Ismaʿil (عليهم السلام): the hosting of the guest, the provisioning of the pilgrim and the one performing ʿumrah, the honouring of the Ḥaram and the guarding of it from wrongdoing and profanation within it, the suppression of the oppressor, and the protection of the wronged.

The affair of Qusayy grew great in Mecca. He became master of those in it, and the city surrendered its keys to him. He made war on Khuzaʿah and Banu Bakr, and Quḍaʿah stood firm with him, and his brother on his mother's side, Rizah ibn Rabiʿah, came to his aid with his people from Quḍaʿah. Qusayy had called upon Quraysh to fight Khuzaʿah, but they feared them and were slow to answer him, so he sought help from his brother Rizah, who came to him with his own people and with those of Quḍaʿah who obeyed him. Concerning that, Rizah said:

> وَلَمَّا أَتَى مِنْ قُصَيِّ رَسُولٌ
> فَقَالَ الرَّسُولُ أَجِبُوا الْخَلِيْلا
> نَهَضْنَا إِلَيْهِ نَقُودُ الْجِيَادَ
> وَتَطْرَحُ عَنَّا الْعَبَاءَ الثَّقِيْلا
> نَسِيرُ بِها الليلَ حتى الصَّباحِ
> وَتَكْمِيِ النَّهَارَ لَئِلاً نَزُولا
> فَهُنَّ سِرَاعٌ كَوِرْدِ الْقَطَا
> يُحِبْنَ بِنَا مِنْ قُصَيِّ رَسُولا
> فَلَمَّا مَرَرْنَ عَلَى عَسْجَدِ
> وَأَسْهَلْنَ مِنْ مُسْتَنَاخٍ سَبِيْلا
> جَمَعْنَا مِنَ السِّرِّ مِنْ أَشْمَذَيْنِ
> وَمِنْ كُلِّ حَيِّ جَمَعْنَا قَبِيْلا
> كَرِنْدُ عَلَى الْأَلْفِ سَيْبً رَسِيْلا
> فَيَالَكِ حَلْبَةَ مَا لَيْلَةِ
> وَجَاوَزْنَ بِالعَرْجِ حَيَاً حُلُوْلا
> وَجَاوَزْنَ بِالرُّكْنِ مِنْ وَرِقَانٍّ
> وَعَالَجْنَ مَنْ مَرَّ لَيْلاً طَوِيْلا
> مَرَرْنَ عَلَى الْحِلِّ ما ذُقْنَهُ
> إِرَادَةَ أَنْ يَسْتَرِقْنَ الصَّهِيْلا
> تُدَّنِّي مِنَ الْعُوْذِ أَفْلاَءَهَا
> فَلَمَّا انْتَهَيْنَا إِلَى مَكّةِ
> أَبَحْنا الرِّجَالَ قَبِيْلاً قَبِيْلا
> وَفِي كُلِّ أَوْبِ خَلَسْنَا الْعُقُولا
> تُعَاوِرُهُمْ ثَمَّ حَدَّ السُّيُوفِ
> تُخَبِّزُهُمْ بِصلابِ النُّسُورِ
> خَبْرَ القَوِيِّ الْعَزِيْرِ الذَِّيْلا
> قَتَلْنَا خُرَاعَةَ فِي دَارِهَا
> وَبَكْراً فَتَلْنَا وَجِيْلاً فَجِيْلا
> نَفَيْنَاهُمُ مِنْ بِلادِ الْمَلِيْكِ
> كَمَا لا يَحُلُّونَ أَرْضاً سُهُولا
> فَأَصْبَحَ سَبِيُّهُمُ فِي الْحَدِيْدِ
> وَمِنْ كُلِّ حَيِّ شَفَيْنَا الْغَلِيْلا

"When a messenger came from Qusayy, the messenger said: answer the friend. We rose to him leading the coursers, casting off from ourselves the heavy cloak. We travelled with them by night until morning, hiding by day so as not to halt. They were swift as the sandgrouse coming to water, bearing us gladly, a summons from Qusayy. When they passed by ʿAsjad and made easy the road from the resting place, we gathered from the secret places of Ashmadhayn, and from every clan we gathered a company: a thousand strong, flowing on in a stream. What a night of hard riding that was! They passed by al-ʿArj a settled clan, and passed by the corner of Wariqan, and laboured through a long night's march. They passed by al-Ḥill and tasted nothing of it, willing to steal away even their own neighing, keeping the young close to the she-camels. And when we came at last to Mecca, we laid the men open, tribe after tribe, and in every quarter we snatched away their wits. The edges of the swords passed over them in turn, and struck them with the hard vultures' beaks, the blow of the strong, the mighty, the trailing in power. We killed Khuzaʿah in their own home, and Bakr we slew, generation upon generation. We drove them from the land of the King, so that they could not settle even an easy plain, and their captives came to be in irons, and from every clan we healed our thirst for vengeance."

Quraysh answered him when Quḍaʿah arrived, and Qusayy prevailed over the affair of Mecca. He expelled Khuzaʿah from the Ḥaram, for they had made light of its sanctity and had committed innovations within it.

So Qusayy took charge of the affair of Mecca and of the pilgrimage. To him belonged the السِّدانَة and the السِّقاية. When the affair of Mecca came into his hands and the pilgrims arrived, he fed them and gave them drink and made ample room for them in lodging and in honour. Concerning that, someone said:

> آبَ الْحَجِيْجُ طَاعِمِيْنَ لَحْمَا
> أَوْسَعَهُمْ رَفْدُ قُصَيِّ شَحْمَا
> وَلَبَناً مَحْضاً وَخُبْزاً هَشْمَا
> يَمْلُ مِنْ ذَاكَ جِفَاناً رُذْمَا

"The pilgrims returned fed with meat; the bounty of Qusayy gave them fat in plenty, and pure milk, and bread broken small, filling with it great bowls brimming over."

He used to feed the pilgrims bread and meat.

The cause of Qusayy's war against Khuzaʿah was that the key of the Kaʿbah was in the hand of Abu Ghabshan al-Khuzaʿi, who had charge of the House. Qusayy met him at al-Ṭaʾif and bought it from him for a skin of wine. He brought it to his people and said: "هذا مِفْتاح أبيكم إِسْماعيل قد رَدَّهُ اللهُ إِليكم من غير ظُلْمٍ ولا غَدْرٍ" ("This is the key of your father Ismaʿil, which God has returned to you without wrongdoing and without treachery"). Then Khuzaʿah came blaming their brother, and he said: "إِنَّمَا رَهَنْتُهُ إِيَّاهُ، وَلَمْ أَبِعْهُ" ("I only pledged it to him; I did not sell it"). And so war ran between them because of that.

From this the proverb is said: "أَحْسَرُ صَفْقَةً مِنْ أَبِي غُبْشَانَ" ("A more regrettable bargain than that of Abu Ghabshan"). Concerning it the poet said:

> وَأَظْلَمُ مِنْ بَنِي فِهْرٍ خُزَاعَهْ
> أَبُو غُبْشَانَ أَظْلَمُ مِنْ قُصَيِّ
> وَلُوْمُوْا شَيْحَكُمْ إِذْ كَانَ بَاعَهْ
> فَلَا تَلْحُوْا قُصَيّاً فِي شِرَاهُ

"Abu Ghabshan was more unjust than Qusayy, and Khuzaʿah more unjust than the Banu Fihr. So do not blame Qusayy for his purchase; blame your own elder, since it was he who sold it."

Qusayy sought in marriage from Ḥulayl ibn Ḥubshiyyah ibn Salul al-Khuzaʿi his daughter, Ḥubba bint Ḥulayl. Ḥulayl had charge of the Kaʿbah and of the affair of Mecca, and when he recognized Qusayy's worth and his lineage, he married her to him. She bore him ʿAbd al-Dar, ʿAbd Manaf, ʿAbd al-ʿUzza, and ʿAbd, the sons of Qusayy.
