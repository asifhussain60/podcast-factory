# The Lion of God and the Liver of Hind

Among the accounts narrated concerning that day, Abu Rafi' reported: On a Sunday, when the battle was at its fiercest and the polytheists were pressing hard against the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, Ali ibn Abi Talib stood beside him. The Messenger of Allah said, "Go, O Ali." Ali replied, "Where shall I go while I am with you?" At that moment Ali spotted a battalion advancing, and the Messenger of Allah said to him, "O Ali, charge that battalion." So he charged it and broke through it, killing Hisham ibn Ummah al-Makhzumi, and drove it off. Then a second battalion came forward, and the Prophet said, "Charge it." So he charged it and killed 'Amr ibn 'Abd Allah al-Jumahi and scattered it. Then a third battalion advanced, and the Prophet said, "Charge it." So he charged it as well.

There was no young man like Ali on the day of Uhud.

He killed Shaybah ibn Malik, the brother of Banu 'Amir ibn Lu'ayy, in that encounter.

When Gabriel, peace be upon him, witnessed what Ali, peace be upon him, was doing in defense of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, he said: "O Muhammad, this is true loyalty." The Prophet said: "He is of me, O Gabriel, and I am of him." Gabriel replied: "And I am of you both, O Muhammad." Thus the angels were called Hashimite.

A voice was then heard calling out from heaven:

> *There is no sword but Dhu al-Fiqar,*
> *and there is no young man but Ali.*

When Ali, peace be upon him, held firm, the Muslims rallied back around the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him. Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas also took a stand beneath him. Malik ibn Sinan, the father of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, came and sucked the blood from the wound of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then swallowed it. The Messenger of Allah said: "Whoever's blood mingles with mine, the Fire shall not touch him."

The two rings of the helmet had driven into the cheek of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and Abu 'Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah drew them out with his teeth; two of his own teeth fell out in doing so.

At that point the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had cast aside his bow until it was no longer of use.

Ubayy ibn Khalaf came forward, calling out: "Where is Muhammad? I shall not survive if he survives." Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, said: "O Messenger of Allah, this is Ubayy ibn Khalaf — shall I go to him?" The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "I will deal with him myself." Those around him tried to hold him back, but he broke free from their hands, snatched a spear from one of them, and went to meet Ubayy. He struck him such a blow that Ubayy nearly fell from his horse, and Ubayy fled.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had previously met him in Makkah, where Ubayy had said: "Muhammad, if you do not finish me off, I will kill you." The Messenger of Allah had replied: "I will kill you, God willing."

When Ubayy rejoined his companions, he was writhing in agony. They said to him: "There is nothing seriously wrong with you — why this state?" He replied: "He told me in Makkah that he would kill me. Had he but spat upon me, it would have killed me." And so the enemy of Allah died from that wound while they were carrying him back toward Makkah.

It is related that on that day the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, prayed the noon prayer with his companions sitting, because of the pain of his injuries and his inability to stand, and he ordered those praying behind him to pray sitting as well.

It is also said that the one who broke the facial bones of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, was 'Utbah ibn Abi Waqqas, who struck him with a stone and hit him. 'Abd Allah ibn Shihab al-Zuhri stabbed him in the forehead, and Ibn Qumi'ah al-Harithi inflicted a wound on his cheek. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, received these blows from the volley the polytheists unleashed against him. The polytheists were then repelled and driven back, but the Muslims had already retreated — it was a day of trial and severe testing.

Hamza, peace be upon him, was killed and then mutilated. Hind tore out his liver, chewed it, and found it would not go down. When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, saw what had been done to him, he was deeply grieved and said: "If Allah grants me victory over them, I will mutilate seventy of them in return."

Allah, glorified and exalted be He, then revealed to him:

> ﴿ وَإِنْ عَاقَبْتُمْ فَعَاقِبُوا بِمِثْلِ مَا عُوقِبْتُمْ بِهِ وَلَئِن صَبَرْتُمْ لَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لِّلصَّابِرِينَ ﴾
> "And if you retaliate, retaliate with the like of that with which you were afflicted; but if you are patient, it is better for those who are patient." (al-Nahl: 126)

And the Muslims were also gladdened by what they had inflicted on the enemy. So Allah revealed:

> ﴿ أَوَلَمَّا أَصَابَتْكُم مُّصِيبَةٌ قَدْ أَصَبْتُم مِّثْلَيْهَا قُلْتُمْ أَنَّىٰ هَٰذَا ۖ قُلْ هُوَ مِنْ عِندِ أَنفُسِكُمْ ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ ﴾
> "When a disaster struck you, though you had struck twice as much, you said: 'How is this?' Say: 'It is from yourselves, for Allah is over all things competent.'" (Al 'Imran: 165)

— meaning: because they had disobeyed the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, when they left Madinah and then abandoned the archers' posts.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then ordered that the martyrs be buried where they had fallen.

Hind al-Khabiyyah said, in verse, regarding the killing of Hamza, peace be upon him, and what she did to him:

> *I satisfied myself on Hamza*
> *when his liver was drawn from his belly.*
> *Then it left me — I could not keep it down —*
> *from the piercing grief that had seized hold of me.*

She also said:

> *We repaid you for the day of Badr,*
> *for war follows war, blow for blow.*
> *I could not bear the loss of my father,*
> *nor my brother, my uncle, and my firstborn.*
> *I have healed myself and fulfilled my vow,*
> *and mended my fierce, unyielding heart.*
> *A wild gratitude remains in my life*
> *until my dearest joins me in the grave.*

Hind bint 'Utbah ibn 'Abbad ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib ibn 'Abd al-Manaf replied to her, saying:

> *I was shamed by Badr and what came before Badr —*
> *O woman, the depth of that disgrace is immense.*
> *May Allah have blessed your morning after that dawn*
> *at the hands of the towering Hashimites in full bloom.*
> *In every quarter a Husam was running them through —*
> *Hamza the lion, and Ali the hawk.*
> *If your Rabb and your father were treacherous,*
> *the outskirts of the sacrifice were shaken by it.*
> *I warn you of the evil of the vow —*
> *you were brutally given to a hardened conscience,*
> *a brutal assault behind the veil of concealment.*
> *The harlots have no harlotry left now.*

It is related that Hind would have mutilated herself as she mutilated Hamza, had he been taken alive. She was among those who had held the tambourines. Hassan ibn Thabit al-Ansari composed verses about her, saying:

> *Hind of the long clitoris —*
> *may Allah curse her and her husband with her.*
> *She brought out a dancer for someone*
> *— your father and your son — on the day of Badr.*
> *The young men of Makkah are not those who cover,*
> *and your uncle is at the level of deterrence.*

It is related that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had never stood in any position that filled him with more grief than when he saw what they had done to Hamza. He said: "I have never stood in a position that has made me angrier than this."

Gabriel, peace be upon him, then descended and said: "O Muhammad, it is written among the people of the seven heavens that Hamza is the lion of Allah and the lion of His Messenger."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then ordered that a cloak be brought, and he prayed over Hamza with seven takbirs. The other martyrs were then brought and laid beside Hamza, may Allah be pleased with him, and the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, prayed over them — each group laid beside Hamza — until he had prayed over him seventy-two times in all.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, was told: "Safiyyah bint 'Abd al-Muttalib has come to look upon her brother Hamza, peace be upon him." He said to al-Zubayr: "Go to her and turn her back — do not let her see what has been done to her brother, for she will be greatly distressed." Al-Zubayr went to her and said: "O my mother, the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, commands you to return." She replied: "Why? I have been told that my brother was mutilated, and that this was in the cause of Allah. I am content with it, and I shall be patient, God willing."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Let her go to him." So she came, looked upon him, prayed over him, sought forgiveness for him, and then returned.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then commanded that Hamza and the rest of the martyrs be buried. He also ordered that the martyrs be buried where they had fallen, each in the spot where he lay. He said: "I bear witness over these people. Every Muslim who is wounded in the path of Allah — his wound shall come on the Day of Resurrection as it was: its color the color of blood, its scent the scent of musk."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then returned to Madinah. As he entered the city and passed by the houses of the Ansar, he could hear their women weeping for their dead. Tears came to his own eyes, and he said: "But there is no one weeping for Hamza." The Ansar heard this and ordered their women to go to the mosque and weep for Hamza. They did so, and the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, came out, weeping for Hamza. Then he had the door of the mosque closed and said to them: "Return home — may Allah have mercy on you — for you have done what was right. But lamentation is forbidden."

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, entered his house, Fatimah, peace be upon her, came out to receive him. He handed her his sword and said: "Wash the blood from this, O my daughter — it has served me faithfully today."

And Ali, peace be upon him, gave her his sword, Dhu al-Fiqar, saying to her:

> *I have not been moved by wealth or any price,*
> *but by obedience to a merciful and devoted servant.*
> *For my life: she declared her love for Ahmad —*
> *may he be rewarded from every rebel and hard of heart.*
> *My sword alone, like a shooting star, sufficed —*
> *I drove on until Allah broke up their ranks.*
> *And even the breath of every clement soul found healing.*
> *Fatimah, this sword bears no reproach.*

On the following day — Sunday, the sixteenth of Shawwal — the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, called upon the people to pursue the enemy. He stipulated that only those who had gone out on the first day should march with him, and none who had stayed behind. He advanced with them — may Allah's blessings and peace be upon him and his family — as far as Hamra' al-Asad, which is eight miles from Madinah. He remained there on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday, then returned to the city.

Along the way he encountered the Banu Ibn Abi — a tribe divided between polytheists and Muslims — who served as advisors to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him. On that day one of them said: "O Muhammad, by Allah, what has befallen your companions has grieved us deeply." They then marched alongside the Messenger of Allah in the wake of the enemy, until word reached Abu Sufyan ibn Harb and those with him. They had been unanimous in turning back against the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and his companions, saying: "We have struck down their companions, their nobles, and their leaders — let us go back and finish them off before they recover." But when Abu Sufyan heard the account of Ma'bad, he asked: "What news do you bring, Ma'bad?" Ma'bad replied: "Muhammad has gone out with his companions to seek you, in a gathering the likes of which I have never seen — they burn with fury against you, and those who had stayed behind have now joined them."

Abu Sufyan asked Ma'bad what lay behind him. Ma'bad replied that the Prophet had marched out with his companions to pursue them, assembling a force the like of which he had never seen — men burning with fury against them, joined now by those who had previously held back. He said: "Woe to you! What are you saying?" Ma'bad answered: "By Allah, I think you should not leave until you see the hooves of the horses." Abu Sufyan said: "By Allah, we had resolved to fall upon them again and finish off the rest of them." Ma'bad said: "I am warning you. By Allah, what I witnessed drove me to compose verses about it."
