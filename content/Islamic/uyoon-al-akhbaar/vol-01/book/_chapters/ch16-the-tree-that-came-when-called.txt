# The Tree That Came When Called

Abu Qays ibn al-Aslat was a relative by marriage to the Quraysh; he had a wife, the daughter of a lion — Ibn Abd al-Ar'i — and he would bring her to Mecca and stay there for a period with the followers of her people. He had close ties with them, and he was a poet. He sent them a poem urging them to cease from war, exhorting them to follow the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, reminding them of what God had done for them and how He had turned away from them the army of the elephant and its cunning stratagem. He said:

> *Luay ibn Ghalib is not unaware of me —*
> *any traveler, whether he offered or reported.*
>
> *Concerning the distant ones — they grieve that we stand*
> *with the article of a man who has taken care of his own among you.*
>
> *And I did not free myself of my needs and views of it,*
> *and I had a groom burdened with worries.*
>
> *She has the finest of endings and the woodcutter —*
> *you silence two on every kiss.*
>
> *And the evil of your deceit and the treading of scorpions —*
> *may God protect you from the evil of your deeds.*
>
> *The pain of the healers has struck it rightly,*
> *and the morals are shown and the soundness of private counsel —*
> *and the replacement of the sacred garment of the antelope.*
>
> *So he reminded them of God at first glance —*
> *war is departing from you in greetings.*
>
> *And tell them, for God rules with His wisdom —*
> *it is the dependence for the two allies or for the kin.*
>
> *Hopes — send them, send them reproach,*
> *and acquit the Sadeef from the hump and the stranger.*
> *It severs a soul and destroys a nation —*
> *Shella, and the echoes of the warrior's garb.*
>
> *Or replace it with warming afterward,*
> *as though the eyes of locusts had purchased it,*
> *with musk and the dust of kagur.*
>
> *In its days, and knowledge is the knowledge of warfare,*
> *and a basin and tents of water — the bitter stripes —*
> *with the outcome of a loyal companion's mother.*
>
> *The people among you are on the right path —*
> *do you consider it, or was there a war of wood?*
> *Did you not know that there is no war*
> *for the one of great provision, a guest who is not turned away?*
>
> *How many a nobleman, Maswad, has been wounded by it,*
> *and this is pure charisma — the cream of the deliberate.*
> *Great are the embers of fire — he is praised,*
> *the vitality of youth broadcast with him,*
>
> *and the water of the rocky stream in the crags, as though —*
> *concerning war, in its advance and its aftermath,*
> *he tells how many people are right about her —*
> *your reckoning, I swear, by the finest of reckoners.*
>
> *So they sold the spears to a warrior and remembered:*
> *you have a guardian other than the Lord of the piercings.*
> *A man confused by a religion — it must not be so —*
> *we have a purpose that the melts may guide.*
>
> *Establish for us a true religion, for you*
> *are the faithful, and dreams are not isolated things.*
> *And I pay tribute to these people — light and infallibility —*
> *bands guided by bands.*
>
> *The seeker sees needs drawing toward your homes*
> *in any case — the finest people of the brow.*
> *The nations have known that your secrets*
> *are what I state truly in the midst of the processions,*
>
> *and I favor it in my view, and above it is a year's standing*
> *in the corners of this House among the trees.*
> *So arise, single out your Lord and be anointed —*
> *the day after Abu Yakhsum, the guide of the battalions.*
>
> *You have a warning from him — believe it —*
> *so do not worry, for war will not ensnare you;*
> *it is adorned for people, and then they see it.*
>
> *Burn — do not grill the weak — and step down.*
> *If you do not know war, then ask,*
> *and I shall be rewarded if people do not grasp the essence.*
>
> *You have the navel of al-Batha', smelling of rabbits,*
> *a polished lineage without flaw —*
> *you preserve the ancient dignities of nobility,*
> *on the slingers at the heads of the virtues —*
> *his battalion is easy to touch and his leg,*
> *Jude al-Malik between Saf and Hasib.*
> *When the victory of the Lord of the Throne came to you, he saw them:*
> *to his family there is no one but Asaib.*
> *Hurry, Harin — and he does not repent.*
> *He lives by it, the saying of a man who is no liar.*
> *If you perish, you will perish, and the seasons will perish.*

When the Quraysh were discouraged from rallying the Arabs to support him, they refrained from speaking directly against the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, resorting instead to harming him with their tongues, even as his uncle Abu Talib stood in his defense. Meanwhile, the Prophet called them to Allah the Almighty, reciting to them the verses of His Book that had been revealed to him. Yet they persisted in their disbelief and denial, stubbornly rejecting the signs that were shown to them.

The following is narrated from the Commander of the Faithful, peace be upon him, in one of his sermons. He said:

"I was with him, may Allah bless him and his family, when the Quraysh came to him. They said: 'O Muhammad, you have made a great claim that neither your fathers nor any of your kinsmen ever made. We will put a matter to you, and if you show it to us and answer it, we will know that you are a prophet and messenger. If you cannot, we will know that you are a lying sorcerer.'

He said to them: 'What do you ask?'

They said: 'Call this tree to come to you, so that it uproots itself and stands before you.'

He said: 'Allah is Able over all things. If Allah does this for you, will you believe and bear witness to the truth?'

They said: Yes.

He said: 'I will show you what you ask, and I know full well that you will not turn to what is good — for among you are those in whose hearts it has been sealed, and those who are partisan.'

Then he said: 'O tree, if you believe in Allah and the Last Day, and you know that I am the Messenger of Allah, then uproot yourself and stand before me.'

By Him who sent him with the truth, it uprooted itself with a great rushing sound, thundering like the beating wings of a bird, until it stood in the hands of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, and flung its upper branch toward him, while one of its branches grazed my forehead — for I was standing at his right hand.

When the people saw this, they said: 'Arrogance and sorcery!'

They asked that half of it return. He commanded it, and half of it came back as though it had never moved. Then it drew closer still until it nearly encircled the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, and they said: 'Command it to return the other half as well.' So he commanded it, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, and it returned.

I said: 'There is no god but Allah. I am the first to believe in you, O Messenger of Allah, and the first to acknowledge that the tree did what it did by the command of Allah, exalted be He, as a testament to your prophethood and in reverence for your word.'

Yet all the people said: 'He is a lying sorcerer — and sorcery is well-suited to his kind. Is there no one who will believe you in your claim except someone like this?' — meaning me.

And I am among those people whom you do not blame regarding Allah, especially the righteous — those whose words are the words of the righteous, who spend their nights in worship and their days in journey, holding fast to the rope of the Quran. They follow the Sunnah of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger; they are neither arrogant nor conceited nor haughty. Their hearts are tender and their bodies are at work!"

This is the saying of the Commander of the Faithful, peace be upon him.

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, showed them mighty signs — among them the splitting of the moon, and other things beyond the power of all humankind — yet they only grew more arrogant, and their denial and disbelief only deepened.

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, reached out to those in the Arab lands — the Jews, the Christians, their scholars, monks, and rabbis — and they came to him to hear his arguments. Some of them recognized in him the evidence of prophethood from what they found in their books: they submitted to him and to what pointed to his nature, and their knowledge of him. Yet when they returned, most of them spoke of him only under the shadow of envy and enmity, which prevented them from acknowledging his prophethood, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family.

Islam spread and news of the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, became known across the horizons, and hearts inclined toward it. Many things happened in this period that would take long to recount. Abu Talib warned the Quraysh sternly against their denial of him and said, in part:

> *Wake up, O Banu Ghalib, and be forewarned —*
> *outstanding men from your own house will face you.*
> *Otherwise, I fear what may come upon you.*
> *By the Lord of the West and the East,*
> *let your forebears be a lesson to you:*
> *Thamud and 'Ad — who remains of them*
> *after they tasted what came before you?*
> *The she-camel of the Throne when she was watered —*
> *the next day they hamstrung her.*
> *From Allah came a blow from the blue,*
> *Hasama from India — the Rotaq.*
> *So wrath descended upon them.*
> *The next day he severed her hock —*
> *wonders in the desert laid bare —*
> *for those who are patient, truthful, and pious.*
> *And I am more awed by that than you,*
> *by the hand of one who has risen through hardship*
> *in spite of this foolish opposition.*
> *So Allah held him in His palm:*
> *He dissolved the seduction and they would not believe it —*
> *I will outfox your obstinate one when he is beguiled.*

It is related by "the Sticky Stone": "When the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, had made his command firm and openly proclaimed the worship of his Lord, he would come to the House and pray there. Abu Jahl ibn Hisham al-Makhzumi came upon him one day while he was praying, stood up, and seized a stone, filling both hands with it. He advanced toward the Messenger, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, as he was prostrating, intending to strike his head — all this after the disbelievers of the Quraysh had incited him against him, and they sat watching to see what he would do. But when he drew close to the Prophet while he was prostrating, he recoiled, his face drained of color, and he cast the stone away from his hand. The people stood up to him and said: 'What is the matter with you, O Abu al-Hakam?'

He said: 'When I drew near him, a great stallion camel loomed before me, opening its mouth — and I fled from it.' They said to one another: 'By Allah, Muhammad has been safeguarded against us, as no man among a people before him was ever safeguarded.'"
