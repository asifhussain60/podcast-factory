# Ransom, Grief, and Triumph in Verse

After the battle, the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, distributed the spoils among his companions and entrusted the captives to a guard. When he reached al-Safra', he went to 'Ubaydah ibn al-Harith ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib ibn Hashim ibn 'Abd Manaf, who had already been struck before this. He was in great pain. He asked about the wound to his leg, and 'Ubaydah said: "O Messenger of Allah, Abu Talib believed," citing his words:

> *And we hand him over until we insist around him, and we marvel at our sons and the lawful.*

"If Abu Talib had seen this, he would have kept it to himself." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, praised him, and 'Ali ibn Abi Talib was with him. 'Ubaydah died — may Allah have mercy on him and be pleased with him — of his wound.

The Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, ordered the execution of al-Nadir ibn al-Harith. While at the valley of the doe, he also ordered the execution of 'Uqbah ibn Abi Mu'ayt. When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, ordered his killing, 'Uqbah said: "Who will look after my children?" The Prophet said, may Allah's prayers be upon him and his family: "The Fire." So 'Ali, peace be upon him, struck his neck. It was also said that the one who struck him was 'Asim ibn Thabit ibn Abi al-Aqlah.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, returned to Madinah with the captives under guard. Some of the captives were ordered to ransom themselves. Among those taken prisoner on the day of Badr were al-'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, the uncle of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and 'Aqil ibn Abi Talib. The Quraysh had compelled them to march out against their will, and the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said to the Muslims: "Whoever among the Banu Hashim you are able to capture, do not kill them, for they were brought out against their will."

So al-'Abbas and 'Aqil ibn Abi Talib were captured. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said to al-'Abbas: "Ransom yourself and your nephew, for he has no money." Al-'Abbas said: "I have nothing." The Messenger of Allah then said to him: "Where is the money you left with Umm al-Fadl on the day you departed from Mecca? You said to her: 'If I am killed, then such-and-such goes to 'Abdullah, and such-and-such to al-Fadl.'" He described to him exactly what he had said to her. Al-'Abbas said: "No one knew of this except Allah, and so I bear witness that there is no god but Allah and that you are the Messenger of Allah." 'Aqil testified to the same, and al-'Abbas ransomed himself and 'Aqil.

Abu Rafi', the freedman of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"I was a slave of al-'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib. Islam had entered our household: al-'Abbas had embraced it, and Umm al-Fadl had become Muslim. Yet al-'Abbas feared his people, was reluctant to oppose them openly, and so concealed his Islam. He had considerable wealth distributed among his people.

Abu Lahab had stayed behind from Badr and sent al-'Asi ibn Hisham ibn al-Mughirah in his place. When news arrived in Mecca of the Quraysh's defeat at Badr, Allah humbled Abu Lahab and shamed him, and we felt a sense of strength and pride within ourselves.

I was carving goblets in the room of Zamzam. Umm al-Fadl, wife of al-'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, was with me, and we were delighted by the news that had reached us. Then Abu Lahab came dragging his feet and sat down on the edge of the room, his back against mine. The people said: 'Here comes Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib.' Abu Lahab called out: 'Come to me — you have news.' He sat down, while the other people stood around him, and Abu Lahab said: 'O nephew, tell me how it went.' He replied: 'By Allah, we had barely met the enemy when they turned on us and did with us as they pleased — they cut us down, they led us captive at will. And yet, I tell you, between heaven and earth we saw white horsemen, and there was nothing we could do against them. Nothing could be done against them, and nothing availed.'"

Abu Rafi' said: "I raised the support-pole of the room with my hand, and then I said: 'Those, by Allah, were angels.'" He then said: "Abu Lahab raised his hand and struck me hard across the face. I fought back against him, but he overpowered me, threw me to the ground, and then knelt on my chest. Umm al-Fadl seized one of the room's pillars and struck him with it, splitting his head open, saying: 'You bully him because his master is away?' He got up and left. He lived only seven more nights before Allah struck him with a pestilence that killed him, and the people were rid of him."

'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, recited on the day of Badr:

> *A noble scourge, with power and grace —*
> *did you not see that Allah has fulfilled His Messenger's promise?*
> *When the disbelievers were brought low in humiliation,*
> *they found awaiting them captivity and slaughter.*
> *The Messenger of Allah was sent with justice —*
> *and the Messenger of Allah was granted his victory.*
> *What signs these are for those who possess understanding.*
> *He brought a house from God,*
> *and people believed in that and were guided.*
> *So they were reunited — praise be to Allah —*
> *their vain one, against the Throne, in ruin.*
> *The nations that deny have broken hearts.*
>
> *By now, the reasons for the matter have become plain.*
> *Did he not reproach what was among the wonders of the ages?*
> *They grew angry and did what they considered best,*
> *holding in their hands what they clung to.*
> *Yet you met it with clarity and resolve.*
> *How many of the young men of Dhi Hama were left behind,*
> *crying out — and among them an old man.*
> *The eyes of mourning women linger over them,*
> *turning with wailing and lamentation.*
> *An old man mourns, and mourns Abu Jahl;*
> *Nawaih mourns the threshold of al-Ghayy and his son.*
> *The obelisk of Hurra, tarnished.*
> *Al-Dahl mourns, and Ibn Haddan among them.*
> *It was in their power on the day of the full moon, before his prophet's time,*
> *a few of them in the midst of the gathering.*
> *People tested in wars and in the fray,*
> *whose mighty bonds of kinship were dissolved.*
> *He called for the dissolution of those who had prayed, and he was answered,*
> *over the people, in the most consequential of affairs.*
> *So they were left isolated at the house of the Humvees.*

Hamza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib said on the day of Badr:

> *They marched against us and we met at a fated hour,*
> *and we would have sought no other outcome.*
> *Now they lie in disobedience and disbelief,*
> *mortgaged to destruction from Badr.*
> *On the eve of their departure they gathered toward Badr,*
> *and we had no recourse but to strike with the dark blade.*
> *When we met, she did not hesitate.*
> *It was only that some men gave them benefit,*
> *and struck with a pulse that concealed the inspiration.*
> *We left the threshold, canceling their pride,*
> *displaying the color of manifest proof.*
> *An old man among the dead, dragged into the pits,*
> *the pockets of the mourners opened for 'Amr.*
> *And 'Amr Thawa is more than their mother-in-law.*
> *Noble men turned aside from Fahr,*
> *and let a commander not perish for the sake of victory —*
> *feel them, for treachery is treacherous.*
> *I swear to you I have no patience today.*

And he said to them, when the matter became clear:

> *I fear Allah's punishment — by Allah, it is a compulsion.*
> *I see what you do not see,*
> *and what the people have not yet heard is news.*
> *So he pressed them until they became embroiled,*
> *three times like a blossoming.*
> *So they became a thousand on the day after the storm, and we gathered —*
> *they in one place, then the matter clarified.*
> *Among us are the soldiers of Allah, when He grants us*
> *a trial in which their wishes are tested.*
> *So Jibril led them beneath our banner,*
> *the women's households of Luayy ibn Ghalib.*
> *Those are the ones who err in their transgression —*
> *a misguided brigade, led by Satan to his own.*

'Ubaydah ibn al-Harith ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib said, when he was carried away from Badr to al-Bifur:

> *The people of Mecca have informed us of a calamity — those who were far from it felt it keenly,*
> *and there was no Bakr in it who was satisfied.*
> *I ask from it life from Allah, close at hand,*
> *with the highest of heaven for those who were noble.*
> *I struck until the lowest were lost,*
> *and sold that life and came to know clarity.*
> *With a garment of Islam that covered all alike,*
> *the next day the capable called out to whoever would preach.*
> *With a threshold when he is gone, and an old man after him.*
> *If my leg is cut off, then I am still a Muslim*
> *among the sincere, like a swaying palm.*
> *The Merciful has honored me with His bounty,*
> *and it was no hardship to fight them.*
> *He did not falter when they asked the Prophet about us —*
> *the three of us — until we came to the one who called.*
> *We found them like lions guarding a thicket,*
> *we fight in the cause of the Merciful against the disobedient.*
> *The three of us, until the fates came to them,*
> *our feet still planted in our place.*

Ka'b ibn Malik, of Banu Salamah, said:

> *I obeyed the command of Allah, and Allah is capable*
> *of whatever He wills — Allah is not powerless.*
> *The day of Badr overtook you as you met a tithe*
> *of corruption, and the ways of corruption among the people are unjust.*
> *From a people whose gathering grew and multiplied,*
> *they assembled and sought out those who came after them.*
> *They marched in harm and soaked themselves in revolt,*
> *and he gathered the sons of al-Nahar beneath his banner —*
> *all of them from 'Aqil and 'Amir.*
> *She marched against us — let no one take the blame but her —*
> *she had a fortress of them, abundant and victorious.*
> *And the Messenger of Allah, with the Aws around him,*
> *and his companions standing firm.*
> *When we met them, all fighters together,*
> *and the Messenger of Allah, with the truth made plain,*
> *we testified that there is no god but Allah —*
> *a testimony that adorns the eyes of those who witness it.*
> *I was exposed to waves of spears as though they were*
> *blossoms, and now one would discover who was true.*
> *A threshold they abandoned while it stood in difficulty,*
> *Abu Jahl shouting in its face —*
> *and none of them but the Lord of the Throne shall judge.*
> *Shayba and Taymi were left in the valley,*
> *and every disbeliever in Hell shall remain.*
> *So they stoked the fuel of the fire in its place —*
> *iron and stone together.*
> *It laughs at them, young and ancient alike,*
> *and with them we began — gathering them — and they scattered.*
> *The Messenger of Allah was called a sorcerer,*
> *and yet he acted not but by the command of Allah's guardian.*
> *Allah willed that they perish through it.*

Hasan ibn Thabit said:

> *You heal what is right with a cold, bright smile,*
> *or the burden of blood upon the mind endures.*
> *Fools do not hasten the appointed portions —*
> *the bag is smoothed out.*
> *If you sit, like marble set*
> *upon cotton, it is as though*
> *it has a soft and graceful form,*
> *she almost stirs too languid to rise from her bed.*
> *And the night distributes my dreams through it,*
> *as for the day, I mention it no more.*
> *Until my bones dissolve in frank declaration,*
> *I swore to forget her and leave off her mention,*
> *and I have defied my own desires for my reproach.*
> *O you who blame me for my foolishness —*
> *I was raised from a spell after the curse,*
> *and a convergence of the incidents of days,*
> *not for being with you in insistence.*
> *I claimed that a man grows weary of his life —*
> *so the fate of al-Harith ibn Hisham came to pass,*
> *and survived with the head of resolve and courage.*
> *Blood passed by a reaper and a stone,*
> *the scattering of neutrality in the desert.*
> *And Thawa loved him as a noble man.*
> *I filled the two hollows with him and cast him there,*
> *the victory of Allah with the people of Islam.*
> *Build a self and a brave man in battle —*
> *your heart stirred in a dream like musk*
> *mixed with the water of clouds.*
> *If you are a liar, what has befallen me —*
> *leaving loved ones to fight without them.*
> *A war whose fury rages without mercy —*
> *I struck them down, and God will carry out His decree.*
> *Were it not for God and her flight, I would have left him*
> *at the Seven Islands and Dasna, mired in turmoil.*
> *He falls silent when he finds tongues running hot*
> *among captives whose bonds are drawn tight,*
> *until the banners vanish from sight*
> *and Majdal feels no shame at the call.*
> *Al-Suf's egg-market, all in a stir —*
> *shame and disgrace laid bare when they see*
> *the lineage of the short-robed: Smeida, Miqdam.*
> *By my hand I will act if he belongs, and he shall not go unpunished,*
> *like lightning beneath the shadow of every cloud —*
> *when it meets iron, it is forged.*

Ka'b ibn Malik mourned 'Ubaydah ibn al-Harith:

> *Weep with your tears truly, and hold nothing back —*
> *O eye of Judy, do not be sparing.*
> *A scene of nobility and generosity,*
> *over the master of this fate now sealed.*
> *A bold commander, steadfast, bearing arms —*
> *generous as tea, a crusher of enemies.*
> *I would not deny our nakedness or our loss:*
> *'Ubaydah, yesterday, and we have not yet rested from grieving him.*
> *He guarded the courtyard with the mulberry grove,*
> *protecting the day after the battle of al-Qatta.*

Hind bint Athathah ibn 'Abbad ibn al-Muttalib lamented 'Ubaydah ibn al-Harith:

> *A true vision that floods the heart and mind —*
> *the yellow has made its pledge, and I will honor it.*
> *A widow brought low, trembling like a fool —*
> *weep, O 'Ubaydah, and cry for those who die in exile.*
> *If the sky's horizons turn red from the blaze,*
> *a shelter for nations through every winter.*
> *And the youth of the pot, as long as it froths and boils,*
> *and weeping for orphans while the wind howls.*
> *He used to feed them with firewood,*
> *but when the one who kindled the fire dies, his throat grows cold.*
> *He wore a rosary of the messengers' trust,*
> *whether by night or calling on the villages.*

Talib ibn Abi Talib praised the Messenger of God, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, and mourned those among the Quraysh who had been wounded:

> *She weeps over a heel; and how quickly one grows rich —*
> *have my eyes not already shed their tears?*
> *And the cat coveted them, and they offered a tail,*
> *yet in wars they were weakened.*
> *Would that I knew — might I yet see closeness to them?*
> *And 'Amir weeps over the blame of tomorrow.*
> *They give promises, and their neighbor shall not be oppressed by force;*
> *they are my brothers, whose tongue shall speak no more.*
> *And the army of Abi Yaksum when they filled the people —*
> *you have no squadron left.*
> *Were it not for God's defense, there would be nothing at all*
> *except that our protector is better than the one who stepped down and faltered.*
> *When we were born into Quraysh, greatness was already there —*
> *he is generous, neither miserly nor mean.*
> *A brother to trust, among the noble deputies, a support —*
> *believing in the sea, not the Zorah or the Serbs.*
> *Persist until you believe, through the striking of Khazraj —*
> *O our brothers of 'Abd Shams and Nawfal,*
> *a ransom for you both — send no war between us;*
> *these are hadiths in which all of you lament the catastrophe.*
> *Do not, after friendship and familiarity,*
> *forget that war was not something noble men desired —*
> *good people guard his door from treachery.*
> *I swear by God I will not keep my soul in sorrow.*
