# Camels for the Newly Reconciled

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, reached al-Ji'ranah, the people of Hawazin came to him and asked him to return their wives and children. They said: "O Messenger of Allah, among the captives are your aunts, your maternal relations, and the women who nursed and raised you. Even if we were like al-Harith ibn Abi Shimr or al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir, and he then came down to us with what he had received, we would beg for his generosity — and you are the best of those who are generous. We have come to you as Muslims."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Which is dearer to you — your wealth, or your women and children?"

They replied: "O Messenger of Allah, you have given us a choice between our wealth and our lineage. Our wives and our children are dearer to us."

Hawazin had been taken as captives — six thousand in all, comprising women, camels, and sheep beyond counting.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "As for what belongs to me and to the family of 'Abd al-Muttalib, it is yours. And when I lead the people in the Dhuhr prayer, stand and say: We are —" The people stood and said what he had commanded them. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whatever belonged to me and to the sons of Abd al-Muttalib is yours." The Muhajiroon and the Ansar said: "Whatever belonged to us is for the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and his family." Al-Aqra' ibn Habis said: "As for me and Banu Tamim, no." Uyaynah ibn Husn said: "As for me and Banu Fazarah, no." Abbas ibn Mardas said: "As for me and Banu Sulaym, no." But the Banu Sulaym said: "Yes — whatever was ours belongs to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and his family." At that, Abbas ibn Mardas said to his own people of Banu Sulaym: "And you are content with this?"

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever holds fast to his share of these captives shall receive six shares from the first spoils I acquire as compensation." The people had no wives or children among the captives except Uyaynah ibn Husn, who had captured an old woman. He said: "I count her of noble lineage in her clan, and perhaps a great ransom will come for her." Abu Surad Zuhayr ibn Surad said to him: "Give her up — by Allah, her breast gives no milk, her belly bears no child, her husband is gone, and her youth is spent." So he returned her in exchange for six shares of compensation, once Zuhayr had said what he said. It is claimed that Uyaynah later regretted it.

Al-Aqra' ibn Habis complained to the Prophet about this and said: "By Allah, you have not given to a man of standing, nor to one of influence."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Tell Malik that if he comes to me as a Muslim, his family and his wealth will be returned to him, and I will give him a hundred camels." Malik did so and came out from Ta'if. He had feared that the people of Ta'if would learn of this and detain him, so he slipped away under cover of night. He then came to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and met him at al-Ji'ranah or Makkah, and there he submitted to Islam. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, restored his family and his wealth to him and gave him a hundred camels.

Malik ibn Awf said, upon becoming Muslim:

> *Who among all people is like Muhammad?*
> *Never have I seen or heard of anyone like him.*
> *When you seek to know what tomorrow will bring,*
> *he delivers and gives to the lowly when imitated.*
> *With the polished spear he strikes every keen blade,*
> *and if scribes were to count their ranks,*
> *in the midst of confusion, he stands watch like a sentinel —*
> *as though he stands guard over his cubs.*

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then appointed Malik over those of his people who had accepted Islam, from the surrounding tribes. These tribes had caused persistent trouble, and he fought them hard, never ceasing to raid them until they were subdued. Abu Muhajin ibn Habib ibn Amr ibn Umayr al-Thaqafi said:

> *Then Banu Salamah brought us comfort —*
> *the enemies press in on every side.*
> *They broke their covenant and violated what was sacred,*
> *yet I have no concern for them.*
> *We were the first to rebuke them,*
> *and still they came against our homes.*

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, finished returning the captives, he rode on. The people called out: "O Messenger of Allah, divide among us the spoils — the camels and the sheep!" They pressed him so urgently that his robe was pulled from him as he was backed against a tree. He said: "Return my robe to me, O people! By Allah, if you had as many camels as the trees of Tihamah, I would divide them all among you, and you would not find me miserly, cowardly, or a liar." He then went to a camel, took a tuft of hair from its hump, held it up between his fingers and said: "O people, by Allah, I have nothing of your spoils — not even this thread — that is not returned to you. So hand over even the needle and thread, for those who steal from the spoils will carry their shame before their families on the Day of Resurrection."

It is related that Aqeel ibn Abi Talib came home on the day of Hunayn to his wife Fatimah bint Shaybah ibn Rabi'ah, his sword still stained with blood. She said: "I know you fought bravely — so what did you bring from the spoils of the polytheists?" He said: "I have nothing but this needle." Then he heard the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, proclaim that whoever had taken anything must return it, and that even a needle and thread must be given back. Aqeel said to his wife: "I think you have lost your needle." He then took it from her and went and threw it back into the spoils.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, gave generously to those whose hearts were to be won over — people who had submitted to Islam out of worldly desire and ambition. By winning them over with gifts, he hoped to draw them closer to Islam. He gave Abu Sufyan ibn Harb one hundred camels, and he gave his son Mu'awiyah one hundred camels as well. Hakim ibn Hizam received one hundred camels, and al-Harith ibn al-Harith ibn Kaldah's son received one hundred camels. A man from Banu Abd al-Dar also received one hundred camels; it was said he was Nusayr ibn al-Harith ibn Kaldah.

Al-Harith ibn Hisham received one hundred camels, Suhayl ibn Amr received one hundred camels, Huwaytib ibn Abd al-Uzza received one hundred camels, and al-Ala ibn Jariyah al-Thaqafi — an ally of Banu Zahra — received one hundred camels. Uyaynah ibn Husn ibn Hudhayfah ibn Badr received one hundred camels, al-Aqra ibn Habis al-Tamimi received one hundred camels, Malik ibn Awf al-Nasri received one hundred camels, and Safwan ibn Umayyah received one hundred camels. Men from the Quraysh below that rank received less than one hundred.

Abbas ibn Mardas received less than a hundred camels and was aggrieved by this. He reproached the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him:

> *Early in the morning at al-Jarra,*
> *she was one who plundered three.*
> *If the people settle down, I shall not settle —*
> *my salvation is that the people sleep.*
> *Between Uyaynah and al-Aqra*
> *I have grown weary while my game was plundered.*
> *I was given nothing, nor was I withheld —*
> *yet I was present in the war that was turned aside,*
> *among its many raids.*
> *Except that she was given —*
> *they have surpassed my sheikh in the assembly,*
> *though he was neither a stronghold nor a prisoner.*
> *Whoever is diminished today will not rise again,*
> *and I was not without my place among them.*

The Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Go to him and silence his tongue with gifts." So they gave him until he was satisfied.

It was said to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him: "Al-Aqra ibn Habis was given one hundred camels, and Uyaynah ibn Husn was given one hundred camels, while Ja'il ibn Suraqah al-Damri was passed over." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "By Him in whose hand is Muhammad's soul, Ja'il ibn Suraqah is better than the whole earth filled with the likes of Uyaynah ibn Husn and al-Aqra ibn Habis. But I gave to those two in order to draw them toward surrender, while I entrusted Ja'il ibn Suraqah to his Islam."

Dhu al-Khuwaysirah — who was from Banu Tamim — came to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, while he was distributing the spoils among the people, and said: "O Muhammad, you have not been just in what you have divided." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, grew angry and said: "Woe to you — if I am not just, then who is?"

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then said: "From his followers will come a group who immerse themselves so deeply in religion that they pass right through it, just as an arrow passes clean through its target — nothing remains on the arrowhead, nothing on the shaft, nothing on the fletching. They will have gone beyond even the blood."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "They are the worst of creation, and the best of creation will be those who kill them." And those are the Khawarij who rose up against the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, and whom he killed at Nahrawan.

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, gave generously to those he gave — the Quraysh and the Arab tribes — while the Ansar received nothing, Hassan ibn Thabit composed verses reproaching this:

> *For the graceful women who neither bowed nor sacrificed,*
> *nor hastened for the sake of Allah and made their confession —*
> *if she is a lesson fashioned from pearls,*
> *full of grace, neither profane nor violated,*
> *and you find a smile within her smile,*
> *a glance and the worst of connections the visitor brings —*
> *leave Shama for you as her affection was*
> *for the faithful, if there are no others,*
> *when the prophet came and said: O finest of the trusted —*
> *they gave up their homes and gave victory.*
> *The banner is called Salim and May is uprooted —*
> *those who bear the guidance through the raging storm of war.*
> *Allah named them Helpers for their aid;*
> *the worries of the eye's corner slope inward —*
> *except for swords and the tips of the lances and a single button.*
> *And the people crowd around you for what is not ours,*
> *and do not let what the wall implies go to waste.*
> *We deliver people and keep no one back,*
> *and when its fire blazes, it is bought dearly —*
> *and do not let the makers of war call us*
> *the hope of the wakeful — among us the peg is driven home.*
> *As we turned back Badr without their entering it,*
> *if they fought their parties with a hero, it is of consequence.*
> *And we were your soldiers on the day of the throttle at Uhud —*
> *we stumbled, and all the people stumbled.*
> *So what did we say, and what did we imagine, and what were we told?*

The Ansar felt it keenly when the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, distributed the spoils among others and left them with nothing, until many of them were saying: "The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, has found his own people."

So Sa'd ibn Ubadah al-Ansari went in to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "O Messenger of Allah, a number of the Ansar have taken it hard that you did not include them in what you divided among your people — the great gifts you bestowed on the tribes of the Arabs — while the Ansar received nothing of it."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said to him: "And where do you stand in all of this, Sa'd?" He said: "O Messenger of Allah, I am only a man of my people." The Prophet said: "Gather your people for me in this enclosure."

When they had gathered, Sa'd came to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "The Ansar have assembled." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, came to them, praised Allah and gave Him His due, then said: "O community of the Ansar, what is this talk that has reached me from you — this grievance you feel toward me? Did I not find you astray and Allah guided you? Were you not poor and Allah enriched you? Were you not enemies to one another and Allah made your hearts one?" They said: "Yes — to Allah and His Messenger belong grace and bounty." Then he said: "Will you not answer me, O community of the Ansar?" They said: "And what shall we say in answer to you, O Messenger of Allah? To Allah and His Messenger belong grace and credit." He said: "By Allah, if you wished, you could say — and you would be telling the truth, and you would be believed — 'You came to us rejected, we believed in you, we supported you, we struggled alongside you, and we brought you joy. You have appointed, O community of the Ansar, a portion of your kinship through which you have bound people to submit to you. I entrust you to your Islam. Are you not content that the people depart with worldly goods while you return with the Messenger of Allah? By Him in whose hand Muhammad's soul rests, had it not been for the Hijrah I would have been a man of the Ansar. If the people took one path and the Ansar took another, I would follow the path of the Ansar. O Allah, have mercy on the Ansar, the sons of the Ansar, and the sons of the sons of the Ansar.'"

The Ansar wept until their beards were wet, and they said: "We are content with the Messenger of Allah as our lot and our fortune." And they parted from him.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, departed from al-Ji'ranah to perform 'Umrah. He ordered the remainder of the fay' to be held at Majannah, in the region of Marr al-Zahran. When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had completed his 'Umrah, he set out for Madinah and appointed 'Attab ibn Usayd as his deputy over Mecca. Mu'adh ibn Jabal was left with him to teach the people the Quran and instruct them in religion. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, provided them with the remainder of the fay'. His 'Umrah was in Dhu al-Qa'dah, and he arrived in Madinah during the nights of Dhu al-Qa'dah. That year he led the people on the pilgrimage — with 'Attab ibn Usayd.
