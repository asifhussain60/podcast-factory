# Ta'if, Addas, and the Tribes

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and his family then went to the Arabs, presenting Islam to them, seeking their support during the pilgrimage seasons and urging people toward their lands.

This is narrated with a chain of transmission from the Commander of the Faithful, Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, who said:

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, was instructed to present himself to the Arab tribes during the pilgrimage seasons, he set out for that purpose and ordered me to accompany him, so I went out with him. Abu Bakr — a man of deep knowledge of lineage — went out with him too. We made our way to a gathering of people, and Abu Bakr stepped forward to address them.

He said: "Who are these people?" They said: "From Rabi'ah." He said: "Are you from her nobles, or from those who were defeated by her?" They said: "From her nobles." He said: "And who among her nobles are you?" They said: "The mightiest of the mighty." He said: "Is your strength 'Awf ibn Muhamalim, who used to say, 'Awf ibn Muhamalim, is there no generosity in Wadi Auf?'" They said: "No." He said: "Are you Bastam ibn Qays, the magnanimous one, the pride of the living?" They said: "No." He said: "Are you Jassas ibn Murrah, the defender of honor and the protector of the neighbor?" They said: "No." He said: "Are you the son of Shreek, the slayer of kings and the one who put them to the test?" They said: "No." He said: "Are you the one who bears the unique crown?" They said: "No." He said: "Are you the uncles of kings from Kindah?" They said: "No." He then said: "Are you the sons-in-law of the kings of the flesh?" They said: "No." He said: "Then you are not the eldest wonder, nor the wonder of the youngest."

At that, a man from Bani Shayban with a lowly bearing rose to face him; he was known as Daghl. He said:

"The burden we neither know nor carry — our questioner must ask elsewhere."

"You there — you have questioned us while we put our questions to you. Do not conceal it from us: who is this man?" He said: "From Quraysh." He said: "A fine people of honor and leadership. And from which branch of Quraysh?" He said: "From Bani Taym ibn Murrah." He said: "I swear by Allah, the Thrower, you come from the very same threshing floor. Do you know that Qusai ibn Kilab ibn Murrah gathered the tribes of Fihr and was called al-Mujammi'?" He said: "No." He said: "Are you Hashim, who crumbled bread for his people and fed the pilgrims?" He said: "No." He said: "Are you Shaybah al-Hamd — 'Abd al-Muttalib — whose face shone like a moon in the deep darkness?" He said: "No." He said: "Who then are you from among the overflowing people?" He said: "No." He said: "Are you from the people of the council?" He said: "No." He then said: "Are you from the people of al-Rifadah?" He said: "No." He said: "Are you from the people of the hijab?" He said: "No." He said: "Are you from the people of the watering?" He said: "No."

At that, Abu Bakr pulled the reins of his camel and turned back toward the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him. The man called after him — meaning something to the effect that the flood had been diverted, pushed away, digested by force, or split — and said: "By Allah, if I had stood my ground I would have told you that you are one of the fools of Quraysh — or what else would you be? A Badghug."

Ali, peace be upon him, said: When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, heard this, he smiled. I said to Abu Bakr: "I have found you to have fallen into a fool among the Arabs." He said: "Yes, O Abu al-Hasan — for every affliction there is a greater affliction, and the affliction of wit is entrusted to logic."

Then we rode on to another gathering with composure and dignity. Abu Bakr went forward to greet the people, and they returned his greeting. He said: "Who are these people?" They said: "From Shayban ibn Rabi'ah." Abu Bakr turned to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "By my father and my mother, there is no greater glory to be found among their people." Among those gathered were Mafruq ibn 'Amr, Hani' ibn Qabisah, al-Muthanna ibn Haritha, and al-Nu'man ibn Shreek. Mafruq ibn 'Amr was the most commanding of them in speech and the most eloquent of tongue; he had two locks of hair that fell onto his chest, and the ears of the people in that gathering were turned toward Abu Bakr.

Abu Bakr said to him: "How many are you?" He said: "We number in the thousands, and a thousand are not overwhelmed by a small force." He said: "And how strong is your defense?" He said: "We spare no effort, and every people has its mettle." He said: "And how do you fare in war against your enemy?" He said: "We are the most fierce in the press of battle. When we are stirred to anger, we drive our horses over our children, preferring the weapon above all else. Victory comes from Allah — sometimes for us and sometimes against us. Yet perhaps you are the brother of Quraysh?" He said: "If the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, has reached you" — and he pointed to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him — "this is he." He said: "We have heard that this is what he says."

He drew near to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "To what do you call, O brother of Quraysh?"

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "I call you to bear witness that there is no god but Allah and that I, Muhammad, am the Messenger of Allah; and to protect me and defend me and support me. The Quraysh have set themselves against the command of Allah, denied His Messenger, and clung to falsehood over truth — except those whom Allah has protected and guided to His religion. Allah is the Protector and the Sufficient."

He said: "And to what else do you call, O brother of Quraysh? All that is in the earth" — meaning: if these words were from their own, we would have recognized them. So he recited to them, may Allah bless him and his family:

> ﴿ تَعَالَوْا أَتْلُ مَا حَرَّمَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَيْكُمْ ﴾
> "Say: Come, I will recite what your Lord has forbidden to you," to the Almighty's words: "This He has enjoined upon you, so that you may use reason." (al-An'am: 151)

He said: "And what else do you call for?"

He recited to them:

> ﴿ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُ بِالْعَدْلِ وَالْإِحْسَانِ وَإِيتَاءِ ذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَيَنْهَىٰ عَنِ الْفَحْشَاءِ وَالْمُنكَرِ وَالْبَغْيِ ﴾
> "Indeed, Allah commands justice and good conduct and giving to relatives, and forbids immorality and bad conduct and oppression — He admonishes you, that perhaps you will take heed." (al-Nahl: 90)

Mafruq ibn 'Amr said: "You call to the noblest of character and the finest of deeds. Yet some people have come out against you and denied you." As though he wished to draw Hani' ibn Qabisah into the conversation, he said: "This is Hani' ibn Qabisah, our elder and the custodian of our religion."

Hani' ibn Qabisah spoke and said: "O brother of Quraysh, we have heard your words, and we find that for us to abandon our religion and embrace yours in a single sitting — a sitting in which you have not fully set out your case — and without seeing what the outcome of what you invite us to will bring, would be a slip of judgment, a hasty conclusion, and a misfortune for those behind us who would hate to have a binding agreement made without their counsel. Let us return and let you return, and we will reflect and consider." As though he wished to draw al-Muthanna ibn Haritha into the conversation, he said: "This is al-Muthanna ibn Haritha, our elder, our chief, and the master of our wars." So al-Muthanna ibn Haritha spoke and said: "O brother of Quraysh, I have heard your words, and the answer — as for leaving our religion and embracing yours — that would be a fine answer. But the answer is that we dwell between two streams: that of Khusraw and that of the Arabs."

The Messenger of Allah said to him: "What are these two streams?" He replied: The waters of the Arabs and the rivers of Khusraw. As for what lies along the waters of the Arabs — the sin of one's companion there is forgiven and his excuse accepted; but as for what lies along the rivers of Khusraw — the fault rests entirely with its owner, who is neither forgiven nor excused. We went down there under a covenant we made with Khusraw: we cause no disturbance and we take no independent action. Yet what you call us to — that which kings despise — if you wish us to shelter you and support you in it, then after the waters of the Arabs, we will give you victory and aid.

The Prophet replied: "You have done no wrong in your answer, since you spoke truthfully. Only one who embraces the religion of Allah in all its dimensions truly fulfills it. But tell me — if Allah grants you their wealth, their homes, and their women, will you praise and glorify Him?" He said: That would be for you, O Messenger of Allah. The Messenger of Allah then said to them: "We have sent you as a witness, a bearer of glad tidings, and a warner, and a caller to Allah by His permission and a shining light." (al-Ahzab)

He then rose and took my hand and said: "O Ali — these are the dreams of the pre-Islamic era in which Allah responds, one bolstering the other. And they will contend against it in this world?!"

Abu Talib died three years before the Hijra. During that time, the Prophet began to hear more harm from his people; he saw them holding back from accepting his message, keeping the Arabs away from it, and repelling them from following him.
