# A Quarrel at the Watering Place

At the water of al-Muraysi', in the wells of Banu al-Mustaliq, the people came down to drink. Among them was a hired hand of 'Umar ibn al-Khattab from Banu Ghifar, called Jahjah ibn Mas'ud, who was leading 'Umar's horse. He jostled at the water with a man of Juhayna who was an ally of Banu 'Awf ibn al-Khazraj, and a quarrel broke out between them. The man of Juhayna cried: "O people of the Ansar!" and Jahjah cried: "O people of the Muhajirun!" From this there followed a heated exchange between 'Umar and 'Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul, together with a company of the hypocrites, and by it they came near to driving a wedge between the Muhajirun and the Ansar.

The Messenger of Allah then set out with the people and did not halt with them that day until the night, so that the march wearied them and the sun distressed them, and only then did he make camp. When they felt the touch of the ground beneath them they fell asleep where they lay. The Messenger of Allah did this for one purpose: to occupy the people and turn them away from the talk that had passed among them.

'Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul had said:

﴿لئن رجعنا إلى الْمَدينة ليخرجن الأعزّ منها الأذلّ، فإنّ هؤلاء قد وردونا في بلادنا﴾

"When we return to Madinah, the mightier of us will surely drive out from it the humbler, for these people have come down upon us in our own land."

When the Messenger of Allah brought the march to its end, Usayd ibn Hudayr met him and greeted him with the greeting of prophethood, then said: "O Messenger of Allah, you have set out at an unaccustomed hour, one you would not normally have chosen for travel." The Messenger of Allah said to him: "Did you not hear what Ibn Ubayy ibn Salul said?" Usayd said: "And what did he say, O Messenger of Allah?" He said: "He claims that when he returns to Madinah, the mightier of them will drive out from it the humbler." Usayd said: "O Messenger of Allah, by Allah, you will drive him out of it if you wish. By Allah, he is the humbler and you are the mightier." Then he said: "O Messenger of Allah, deal gently with him. By Allah, you came to us and his own people were stringing beads for him to crown him their king, and he sees that you have stripped him of a kingdom."

Miqyas ibn Sababah came from Mecca after the killing of his brother, claiming that he had embraced Islam. He said: "O Messenger of Allah, I have come to you seeking the blood money of my brother." So the Messenger of Allah ordered that his brother's blood money be given to him. He stayed with the Messenger of Allah for some days, then fell upon his brother's killer and slew him, and went out to Mecca as an apostate. Miqyas said in that:

جَلِّلْتُهُ ضَرْبَةً بَاءَتْ لَهَا وَشَلٌ
مِنْ نَاقِعِ الْجَوْفِ يَعْلُوهُ وَيَنْصَرِمُ
لاَ تَأْمَنَنَّ بَنِي بَكْرٍ إِذَا ظُلِمُوا
فَقُلْتُ وَالْمَوْتُ تَغْشَاهُ أَسِرَّتُهُ

"I covered him with a blow from which there welled a trickle
of blood steeped in the body's depths, rising and breaking off.
Never trust the sons of Bakr when they have been wronged.
So I said, while death was covering the lines of his face."

In the days that followed, Banu al-Mustaliq accepted Islam, and after their acceptance the Messenger of Allah sent al-Walid ibn 'Uqbah to them. When they heard of his coming they rode out to meet him, rejoicing that the Messenger of Allah had sent him to them. Yet he turned back to the Messenger of Allah and claimed that they had apostatized, that they had intended to kill him, and that they had withheld from him the charity due from them, until the Messenger of Allah was minded to send an expedition against them. While the matter stood thus, their delegation arrived before the Messenger of Allah and said: "O Messenger of Allah, we stand firm upon our Islam. We heard of your messenger, so we went out to meet him and honor him, and to hand over to him the charity that was with us. He turned back and departed, and claimed that we had gone out to kill him. By Allah, we did not go out for that."

Then Allah revealed concerning al-Walid ibn 'Uqbah:

﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِن جَاءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌ بِنَبَإٍ فَتَبَيَّنُوا أَن تُصِيبُوا قَوْمًا بِجَهَالَةٍ فَتُصْبِحُوا عَلَى مَا فَعَلْتُمْ نَادِمِينَ ۞ وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ فِيكُمْ رَسُولَ اللهِ لَوْ يُطِيعُكُمْ فِي كَثِيرٍ مِّنَ الْأَمْرِ لَعَنِتُّمْ﴾

"O you who believe, if a wicked person comes to you with news, verify it, lest you harm a people out of ignorance and then become regretful over what you have done. And know that among you is the Messenger of Allah; were he to obey you in much of the matter, you would surely suffer." [al-Hujurat: 6] and what follows.

'A'isha bint Abi Bakr was with the Messenger of Allah on that journey. She stayed behind, and it was said that she had gone out to relieve herself; the litter was lifted and she was not in it, and what was said concerning her was said. It is reported that a verse was revealed to the Messenger of Allah declaring her innocence in that affair.

It is narrated from Salman al-Farisi (رض) that he said:

When the Messenger of Allah turned back from the raid of Banu al-Mustaliq, he was at the head of the people, and he commanded 'Ali (ع) to be in their rear to guard them. When the Messenger of Allah reached Madinah he came to the door of the mosque and sat waiting for 'Ali (ع); he did not enter his dwelling. He was wiping the sweat from his brow, then he said:

﴿يأتيكم هذه السَّاعة من هذا الشِّعْب رَجُل أشبه النَّاس بالمسيح -عليه السّلام-، وهو أفضل النَّاس بعدي يوم القيامة، وأوَّل مَنْ يدخل الجنَّة﴾

"There will come to you at this hour, from this ravine, a man who is of all people the most like the Messiah (عليهم السلام), and he is the best of people after me on the Day of Resurrection, and the first to enter Paradise." And he gestured with his hand toward one of the ravines.

They kept watching the ravine, and the first to appear from it was 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع). When he reached the Messenger of Allah, he rose toward him (إليه) and embraced him and kissed him between the eyes, and the two of them (عليهم السلام) went in together. A group of the hypocrites said: "He likens his cousin to the Messiah and makes him his equal. Are our gods, which we used to worship, better, or is 'Ali?"

So Allah revealed concerning them:

﴿وَلَمَّا ضُرِبَ ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ مَثَلاً إِذَا قَوْمُكَ مِنْهُ يَصِدُّونَ ۞ وَقَالُوا أَآلِهَتُنَا خَيْرٌ أَمْ هُوَ مَا ضَرَبُوهُ لَكَ إِلاَّ جَدَلاً بَلْ هُمْ قَوْمٌ خَصِمُونَ﴾

"And when the son of Mary was cited as an example, at once your people raised an uproar at it, and they said: 'Are our gods better, or is he?' They cited him to you only for the sake of dispute. Indeed, they are a contentious people." [al-Zukhruf: 57–58]
