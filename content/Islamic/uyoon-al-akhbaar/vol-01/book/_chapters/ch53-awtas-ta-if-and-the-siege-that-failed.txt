# Awtas, Ta'if, and the Siege That Failed

Their formation collapsed, their order dissolved, and their affairs fell into disarray. Allah Almighty struck them in their faces and supported His Messenger, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, with a humiliation of the enemy the like of which had not been seen before, just as He has described in His Book. The last of those who had fled did not return until the captives were already sitting in the hands of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, and the spoils had been collected and were present.

'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, had on that day of tribulation a role that no one else matched. When the Ansar returned to the Messenger of Allah they stood well; and the Muhajirun were patient. The Messenger of Allah oversaw the men from his mount, watching as they fought, and said: "Now the furnace burns hot."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, turned to Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib during the rout, while he was holding the Prophet's mule, and asked: "Who is this?" He said: "I am your cousin, O Messenger of Allah." And he held firm at that hour, and his Islam proved good when he had embraced it.

He saw Umm Sulaym bint Milhan. She was with her husband Abu Talha, standing firm in the midst of the battle. She was pregnant with 'Abdullah ibn Abi Talhah, and Abu Talha's mount was with her. She was afraid the camel might throw her off, so she gripped its head, plunged her hand into the halter near the stern, and the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, spoke to her: She said: "Yes, by my father and mother, O Messenger of Allah — kill those who flee from you, as you kill those who fight you, for they deserve it."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "O mother, Allah is sufficient." It is said she had a dagger with her. Abu Talha asked her: "What is that dagger for, O Umm Sulaym?" She said: "I took it so that if any polytheist came near me, I would plunge it into him." Abu Talha then said: "Do you not hear, O Messenger of Allah, what Umm Sulaym al-Rumaysa' is saying?" — "Rumaysa'" referring to dirt that collects in the corner of the eye.

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, was advancing toward Hunayn, Banu Sulaym joined him under al-Dahhak ibn Sufyan al-Kilabi, and they were with him. When the people were routed, Malik ibn 'Awf said, struck by what had befallen him:

> *The oldest proof is that this was a day of denial —*
> *a man like me, a man like you: shield and shield.*
> *If the company loses one day, and the burden falls,*
> *then I drove back ranks upon ranks.*
> *Battalions in full sight of eye —*
> *I may yet drive the thrust to its resounding mark.*
> *When the poor outlast the prosperous,*
> *and the nobles howl and flee from the thrust of the spear,*
> *a falling machine gun sweeps from the flanks,*
> *Tarat screams while we erupt forward,*
> *and the fox working within it is broken.*
> *O Zayd, son of their concern — where do you flee?*
> *The tooth has worn down and the age has grown long;*
> *the white banners have yielded to the long red lances.*
> *I have known nothing like her except immersion,*
> *when the Guards emerge from beneath the shadow.*

Abu Qatadah al-Ansari said: On the day of Hunayn, I saw two men fighting — one a Muslim and the other a polytheist — and a man from among the polytheists was trying to get his polytheist companion the upper hand over the Muslim. So I went at him, struck his hand, and cut it off. He then seized me with his other hand and held me so tightly that I feared he would kill me, but the bleeding weakened him until he fell, and I struck him and killed him.

When Allah defeated the polytheists and the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, was able to —

> *I swear by God, the plant deserves it —*
> *I have been absent from God's horses, the horses of Allat.*

When Hawazin was defeated, Thaqif suffered losses at Banu Malik, with seventy men killed under their banner, among them 'Uthman ibn 'Abd-Allah ibn Rabi'ah ibn al-Harith ibn Habib. Their banner had been carried by Dhu al-Khimar, and when he was killed, 'Uthman ibn 'Abd-Allah took it up and fought under it until he too was killed. When he fell, the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "May God keep him away — he hated the Quraysh."

One of the Muslim women remarked on this.

The banner of the allied forces was held by the boat of Ibn al-Aswad. When the people were routed, he propped it against a tree, and then he, his cousins, and his kinsmen fled from the alliance. Of the allied forces, only two men were killed: a man from Jealousy, known as Wahb, and another from Banu Kubbah, known as al-Jalah. When word reached the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, that al-Jalah had been killed, he said: "Today the master of the youth of Thaqif was killed, except for the son of Hneida" — meaning Ibn Hunaida, al-Harith ibn Uwais.

When the polytheists were routed, they fell back to al-Ta'if with Malik ibn Auf. Some fled in haste while others headed toward a valley; those who made for the valley were none but Banu Jalah of Thaqif. The cavalry of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, pursued them. He pressed through the palm groves among the people and did not follow the well-worn path of the passes.

Rabi'ah ibn Rafi' al-Sulami caught up with Duraid ibn al-Samma, taking hold of the halter of his camel. He mistook Duraid for a woman, since Duraid was riding in a litter, and Rabi'ah intended to take him captive. But the man was an aged elder, and Rabi'ah did not recognize him.

Duraid said to him, "What do you want, young man?" He replied, "I will kill you." Duraid said, "And who are you?" He said, "I am Rabi'ah ibn Rafi' al-Sulami." He struck him with his sword, but it did nothing. Duraid told him, "Your mother has given you a poor weapon! Take that sword from behind the litter, strike with it, raise it above the bones, and bring it down on the brain — for that is how I used to strike men. But if you have lost your wits, then go back to your mother and tell her you have killed Duraid ibn al-Samma, and there will come a day when you will have kept your women from harm."

When Duraid fell and was exposed, his thighs were like parchment from a lifetime in the saddle.

When Rabi'ah returned to his mother and told her he had killed Duraid, she said, "By Allah, he freed three of your mothers."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, sent, in pursuit of those who had fled toward Awtaas, Abu 'Amir al-Ash'ari. He caught up with some of the defeated fighters and they battled him. Abu Amir was struck by an arrow and killed. Abu Musa al-Ash'ari then took up the banner and fought them, and they feared he would kill the Banu Nasr from Banu Riab.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, passed by a woman who had been killed by Khalid ibn al-Walid, and a crowd had gathered around her. He said, "Who killed this?" They said: Khalid ibn al-Walid. He sent word to Khalid through one of those with him, saying: "The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, forbids the killing of a child, a woman, or a hired laborer." And "al-Asif" means the hired man.

Al-Zubayr ibn al-'Awwam fought fiercely that day. Malik ibn 'Awf positioned himself and those with him at a mountain pass to cover the retreat of their vulnerable so that they might get away safely.

The Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said regarding Bajad: "If you are able to find him, do not let him escape you." Bajad was a man from Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr. The Muslims captured him along with his family, and with them was al-Shayma' bint al-Harith ibn 'Abd al-'Ari, the foster sister of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family. Those who captured her treated her roughly during the march, but she said: "I am your companion's foster sister." They did not believe her, and when she was brought before the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, she said: "I am your sister from breastfeeding." He said: "And what is the sign of that?" She said: "A bite you gave me on my back when I was carrying you."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, spread out his robe for her, seated her upon it, and treated her with kindness, and said: "If you wish, you may stay with me in honor and love, and I shall hold you dear, or if you wish, I will provide for you and return you to your people." She said: "Rather, provide for me and return me to my people." And she chose that. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, returned her to her people.

From the Quraysh: Ayman ibn Ubayd. Yazid ibn Zama'ah ibn al-Aswad ibn al-Muttalib ibn Asad ibn 'Abd al-'Uzza — he had a mare called "The Wing," and he was killed by her.

From the Ansar: Saraqa ibn al-Harith ibn 'Adi, from Banu al-'Ajlan.

From the Ash'aris: Abu 'Amir al-Ash'ari.

The captives of Hunayn were brought together before the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him. He ordered that the captives and the spoils be held at al-Ji'ranah, and they were kept there.

Then came the Battle of Ta'if:

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, marched to Ta'if after returning from Hunayn. Thaqif, upon the arrival of the Prophet at Ta'if, shut the city gates against him and prepared their fortifications for battle.

Ka'b ibn Malik composed the following verses when the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, assembled his forces for the march to Ta'if:

> *We have swept all doubt from Tihamah,*

Shaddad ibn 'Arid al-Jashmi said in commemoration of the journey of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, to Ta'if:

> *How can he be aided who finds no victory,*
> *and did not fight alongside her stones in vain?*
> *They are not human beings from among her people —*
> *do not support al-Lat; God will destroy it.*
> *The one that burned in the dam caught fire —*
> *when will the Prophet come down to your land?*

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, marched along the Palm of Yemen, then along a pass, then along the salt flats, then across the sea of foam from Layyah, where he built a mosque — one that remains a seasonal landmark.

At that time, a man from Banu Layth who had killed a man from Hudhayl was brought before him — and this was the first blood case adjudicated in Islam.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, ordered the fortress of Malik ibn Auf to be demolished, and the march continued until the army descended near a Sidra tree. It is said that it was forbidden to approach the property of a man who was close to the wealth of a man of Thaqif, and so the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, sent word to him: either surrender, or we will tear down your wall. He refused to accept Islam, and so the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, ordered his wall to be destroyed.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then advanced until he camped near Ta'if. He positioned his army there, and men accompanying him fell, because Thaqif's soldiers descended from the wall of Ta'if and shot arrows that reached them. When the fallen were among them, he moved his army to where his mosque in Ta'if stands today.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, besieged them for twenty nights — some say seventeen.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had two of his wives with him. One of them, Umm Salamah, was sheltered between two tent poles, and he prayed between the two intervals. When Thaqif later embraced Islam, they built a mosque over the prayer site of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family — the mosque of 'Amr ibn Ummah ibn Wahb ibn Mu'tab ibn Malik.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, fought fierce battles against them throughout the days of the siege, and they found themselves worn down by it.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, ordered a catapult to be constructed and stones were hurled at the people of Ta'if. When the stones began to pile up and break through part of the wall, a group of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, crept beneath a protective tank and pushed it toward the wall of Ta'if in order to breach it. Thaqif responded by hurling upon them an iron beam wrapped in fire. The men scrambled out from beneath the tank, and Thaqif killed a number of them. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then commanded — alongside his forces — that the grape vineyards of Thaqif be cut down, and the Muslims carried this out.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said, while the siege was still heavy upon him: "Permission has not been granted to conquer Ta'if." When this became known to the Muslims, they questioned him about it, and he confirmed it. They said: "O Messenger of Allah, then give the order to withdraw." And so the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, commanded the herald to call it out, and the call for withdrawal was made.

'Uyaynah ibn Husn al-Fazari said: Thaqif is a glorious and honorable people. Some Muslims said to him: "You praise the polytheists, yet you came to support the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family!" He replied: "I did not come to fight Thaqif alongside you. What I wanted was for Muhammad to open Ta'if, so that Thaqif might capture a slave girl, whom I would acquire, that she might bear me a son — for they are a people of noble stock."

While the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, were besieging Ta'if, some enslaved people came down to him, and the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, freed them.

Isma'il ibn Aban narrated, with his isnad, from Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah al-Ansari, who said:

"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, withdrew into his chamber while he was besieging Ta'if, and he prolonged his private counsel with 'Ali, while the people looked on. Many envied me for what he had singled him out with. Allah and His Messenger" — until Abu Bakr and 'Umar said: "O Messenger of Allah, you have prolonged since today your private counsel with 'Ali." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "It was not I who chose him — it was God who chose him."

All the people came to know the distinction that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and his God had accorded to his guardian 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, drawing him close above all other Companions — such that none among them had been granted his favor during the lifetime of the Messenger of Allah.

In the isnad of Abu Ghassan, from 'Ali, peace be upon him, he said:

"When Allah, exalted be He, revealed: 'O you who believe, when you consult the Messenger in private, offer charity before your consultation' (al-Mujadilah: 12) — 'Ali, peace be upon him, said: I possessed a dinar, which I exchanged for ten dirhams. Whenever I wished to consult the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, I would give a dirham in charity before doing so, until it was spent. No other Muslim did this. Then Allah, exalted be He, revealed: 'Are you fearful of having to give charity before your consultation? Then when you do not, and Allah has pardoned you' (al-Mujadilah: 12) — to the end of the verse. No one fulfilled the verse of al-Najwa other than me."

This is agreed upon among Muslims, and both al-Zamakhshari and al-Tha'labi mentioned in their commentary on this verse that no one among the Muslims acted upon the verse of al-Najwa except 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him.

His virtues are many and well-known, and those who deny his virtue, associated with it, have rejected most of them. Neither Abu Bakr, nor 'Umar, nor any other Companion is cited in connection with the verse of al-Najwa, nor is there any mention of them in it.

'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, said: The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, placed his mouth to my ear and opened to me a thousand doors of knowledge, and from each of those doors a thousand more doors were opened to me.

None of the Companions received anything like this. Yet Abu Bakr said: "I am your guardian, and I am not the best among you." And among you is the one who is "he" whose horizon is mine.

'Umar said: "Do not exaggerate in the dowries of women — the most the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, ever gave to any of his wives was ten ounces."

A woman who was not among the most learned stood up and said to him: "O Commander of the Faithful, have you not prevented us from a right that Allah has obligated for us? He said: 'And if you wish to take one wife in place of another, and you have given one of them a heap of gold, take nothing from it'" (al-Nisa': 20) — to the end of the verse. 'Umar ibn al-Khattab felt ashamed and turned to those around him, saying: "You hear such a thing from me and do not object — until a woman who knows little corrects me?"

Many such instances could be cited had we not already exceeded what we set out to recount — but we have mentioned them here in connection with the private counsel of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, with his cousin, and his distinction above others. The denial of those who deny this amounts to a challenge to the Prophet, and they dare to prefer over him one who belongs to the people of preference.

Those who were martyred at Ta'if numbered twelve Muslims: seven from Quraysh, four from the Ansar, and one from Banu Layth.

> *Hadal touches their longing, our sandals —*
> *from the weaving of David and the family of Muharraq.*

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, departed from Ta'if, and it was said to him: "Pray against Thaqif, O Messenger of Allah." He said: "O Allah, guide Thaqif and bring them to us."
