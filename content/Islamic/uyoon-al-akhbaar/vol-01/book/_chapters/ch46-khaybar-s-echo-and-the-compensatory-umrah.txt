# Khaybar's Echo and the Compensatory Umrah

Al-ʿAbbās pressed him for certainty and said, "أحقّاً ما تقولُ يا حجَّاج؟" ("Is it truly as you say, O Ḥajjāj?"). Al-Ḥajjāj answered, "إي والله، فاكتم عنّي، ولقد أسلمتُ وما جئتُ إلا لآخذ مالي، خوفاً من أن أُغْلَبَ عليه، فإذا مضت ثلاثٌ بعد مسيري، فأظهِر أمرَك، فإنَّه والله على ما تحبّ" ("Yes, by Allah, so keep my secret. I have accepted Islam, and I came only to take my wealth, fearing I would be overcome and lose it. When three days have passed after my departure, make the matter known, for by Allah it stands as you would wish").

On the third day after al-Ḥajjāj had set out, al-ʿAbbās ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib put on a fine robe of his, perfumed himself, took up his staff, and went out until he came to the Kaʿbah and circled it. When the people saw him they said to him, "يا أبا الفَضْل، إنّ هذا والله التَّجَلُّدُ لِحَرِّ المُصِيبة" ("O Abū al-Faḍl, this, by Allah, is fortitude under the heat of calamity"). He said, "كلا، والله لقد افتتح محمّدٌ خيبر وملكها عنوةً، ونزل على بنت ملكهم حُيَيّ بن أخطب عروساً، وصارت أموالهم وما فيها له ولأصحابه" ("Not at all. By Allah, Muhammad has conquered Khaybar and taken possession of it by force, and he has taken as bride the daughter of their king Ḥuyayy ibn Akhṭab, and their wealth and all that it holds has passed to him and to his companions"). They asked who had brought him this news. He answered that it was the very man who had brought them what he brought them, and that this man had entered upon them as a Muslim, had taken his wealth, and had set off to join Muhammad and his companions and to be with them. At this they cried out, "يا لَعبادَ الله! انفلتَ عدوُّ الله" ("O servants of Allah! The enemy of Allah has slipped away"), and said that had they known, there would have been a reckoning between them and him. It was not long before the news itself reached them and confirmed what al-ʿAbbās had said.

When the Messenger of Allah had finished with Khaybar he turned aside to Wādī al-Qurā, laid siege to its people for several nights, and then returned to Madinah.

Ibn Luqaym al-ʿAbsī said concerning the conquest of Khaybar, which took place in Ṣafar:

شَهْبَاءَ ذَاتِ مَنَاكِبٍ وَفِقَارِ
رُمِيَتْ نَطَاةُ مِنَ الرَّسُولِ بِفَيْلَقِ
وَاسْتَيْقَنَتْ بِالذُّلِّ لَمَّا شُيِّعَتْ
وَرِجَالُ أَسْلَمَ وَسْطَهَا وَغِفَارِ
وَالشَّقُّ أَظْلَمَ أَهْلُهُ بِنَهَارِ
صَبَحَتْ بَنِي عَمْرِو بْنِ زُرْعَةَ غُدْوَةً
إِلاَّ الدَّجَاجَ تَصِيْحُ فِي الأَسْحَارِ
جَرَّتْ بِأَبْطَحِهَا الذُّيُولَ فَلَمْ تَدَعْ
مِنْ عَبْدِ أَشْهَلَ أَوْ بَنِي النَّجَّارِ
وَلِكُلِّ حِصْنٍ شَاغِلٌ مِنْ خَيْلِهِمْ
فَوْقَ الْمَغَافِرِ لَمْ يَنُوا لِفِرَارِ
وَمُهَاجِرِيْنَ قَدْ أَعْلَمُوا سِيْمَاهُمُ
وَلَيْوِيَنَّ بِهَا إِلَى أَصْفَارِ
وَلَقَدْ عَلِمْتُ لَيَغْلِبَنَّ مُحَمَّدٌ
تَحْتَ العَجَاجِ غَمَائِمَ الأَبْصَارِ
فَرَّتْ يَهُودٌ يَوْمَ ذَلِكَ فِي الوَغَى

Naṭāt was struck by the Messenger with a legion, ash grey, of broad shoulders and firm spine. It grew certain of humiliation when it was escorted forth, the men of Aslam in its midst and Ghifār. He came upon the Banū ʿAmr ibn Zurʿah in the early morning, and al-Shiqq, whose people were darkened in broad daylight. The host trailed its skirts through their valley and left nothing there but the cocks crying in the hours before dawn. For every fortress there was one of their horsemen to keep it occupied, from ʿAbd Ashhal or the Banū al-Najjār, and Emigrants who had set their tokens above the helmets and never wavered toward flight. I knew that Muhammad would surely prevail, and that the matter would be brought to its end. The Jews fled on that day in the fray, beneath the dust that clouded all eyes.

Ḥassān ibn Thābit said on the day of Khaybar:

بِئْسَ مَا قَاتَلَتْ خَيَابِرُ عَمَّا
جَمَّعَتْ مِنْ مَزَارِعٍ وَنَخِيْلِ
وَأَقَامُوا فِعْلَ اللَّئِيمِ الذَّلِيْلِ
كَرِهُوا الْمَوْتَ فَاسْتُبِيحَ حِمَاهُمْ
أَمِنَ الْمَوْتِ تَهْرُبُونَ فَإِنَّ الْـمَوْتَ مَوْتُ الْهُزَالِ غَيْرُ جَمِيْلِ

Ill did the people of Khaybar fight for what they had gathered of fields and palms. They hated death, so their sanctuary was made lawful, and they behaved with the conduct of the base and abject. Is it from death that you flee? Death by wasting away is no fair death at all.

Kaʿb ibn Mālik said on the day of Khaybar:

بِكُلِّ فَتًى عَارِي الأَشَاجِعِ مِذْوَدِ
وَنَحْنُ وَرَدْنا خَيْبَراً وَقُرُوْضَهُ
جَرِيءٍ عَلَى الأَعْدَاءِ فِي كُلِّ مَشْهَدِ
جَوَادٍ لَدَى الْغَايَاتِ لاَ وَاهِنِ الْقُوَى
ضَرُوبٍ بِنَصْلِ الْمَشْرَفِيِّ الْمُهَنَّدِ
عَظِيْمِ رَمَادِ الْقِدْرِ فِي كُلِّ شَتْوَةٍ
مِنَ اللهِ يَرْجُوهَا وَفَوْزاً بِأَحْمَدِ
يَرَى الْقَتْلَ مَدْحاً إِنْ أَصَابَ شَهَادَةً
وَيَدْفَعُ عَنْهُ بِاللَّسَانِ وَبِالْيَدِ
يَذُودُ وَيَحْمِي عَنْ ذِمَارِ مُحَمَّدٍ
يَجُودُ بِنَفْسٍ دُونَ نَفْسِ مُحَمَّدٍ
وَيَنْصُرُهُ مِنْ كُلِّ أَمْرٍ يُرِيبُهُ
يُرِيْدُ بِذَاكَ الْفَوْزَ وَالْعِزَّ فِي غَدِ
يُصَدِّقُ بِالأَنْبَاءِ بِالْغَيْبِ مُخْلِصاً

We came down upon Khaybar and its outlying ground with every youth of bared sinews, a defender: generous when the goal is reached, not slack of strength, bold against the enemy in every field; great in the ashes of his cooking pot in every winter, a striker with the blade of the Mashrafī sword of Indian steel. He counts being slain a praise if he attains a martyrdom he hopes for from Allah, and triumph in the company of Aḥmad. He wards off and guards what Muhammad holds sacred, defending him with tongue and with hand. He gives his own life freely before the life of Muhammad, and aids him in every matter that troubles him. He affirms sincerely the tidings of the unseen, desiring thereby triumph and honour on the morrow.

Jaʿfar ibn Abī Ṭālib (ع) arrived on the day Khaybar was opened, and his coming gladdened the Messenger of Allah, who said:

"مَا أَدْرِي بِأَيِّهِمَا أَنَا أُسَرُّ: أَبِفَتْحِ خَيْبَرَ أَمْ بِقُدُومِ جَعْفَرَ؟"

"I do not know at which of the two I am the more delighted: at the opening of Khaybar or at the coming of Jaʿfar."

With him were his wife Asmāʾ bint ʿUmays and his son ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar, who had been born in the land of Abyssinia, together with a company of the Muslims who had emigrated to Abyssinia.

After his return from Khaybar the Messenger of Allah remained in Madinah through the two months of Rabīʿ, the two Jumādās, Rajab, Shaʿbān, Ramaḍān and Shawwāl. Then he went out to perform ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ in place of the ʿUmrah from which the polytheists had turned him back, and the Muslims went out with him, those who had been turned back along with him. When the people of Makkah heard of him they withdrew from his path, and Quraysh said among themselves that Muhammad and his companions were in hardship, strain and distress. They ranged themselves near Dār al-Nadwah in order to look upon him and his companions. When the Messenger of Allah entered the sanctuary he threw his cloak under his right arm, baring the upper arm, and said:

"رَحِمَ اللهُ امْرَأً أَرَاهُمُ اليَوْمَ مِنْ نَفْسِهِ قُوَّةً"

"May Allah have mercy on a man who shows them strength in himself today."

Then he touched the corner and went out at a trot, his companions trotting with him, until the House came between him and them; and when he had touched the Yemeni corner he walked until he touched the Black Stone. He trotted in this manner for three circuits and walked the rest of them.

The Muslims supposed that the Prophet had done this only to show Quraysh strength; yet when he did it again in the Farewell Pilgrimage he held to it, and so the practice remained.

It is said that the Prophet entered Makkah in that ʿUmrah while ʿAbd Allāh ibn Rawāḥah held the halter of his she camel, saying:

خُلُّوا بَنِي الْكُفَّارِ عَنْ سَبِيْلِهِ
خُلُّوا فَكُلُّ الْخَيْرِ فِي رَسُولِهِ
يَا رَبِّ إِنِّي مُؤْمِنٌ بِقِيْلِهِ
أَعْرِفُ حَقَّ اللهِ فِي قُبُولِهِ

Clear the way, sons of the unbelievers, from his path. Clear away, for all good lies in His Messenger. O my Lord, I am a believer in what he says. I acknowledge the right of Allah in accepting him.

The Messenger of Allah stayed in Makkah three days, and then Quraysh asked him to depart, and he departed.

It is said that in that ʿUmrah this verse was revealed to him:

﴿لَقَدْ صَدَقَ اللَّهُ رَسُولَهُ الرُّؤْيَا بِالْحَقِّ لَتَدْخُلُنَّ الْمَسْجِدَ الْحَرَامَ إِن شَاءَ اللَّهُ آمِنِينَ مُحَلِّقِينَ رُؤُوسَكُمْ وَمُقَصِّرِينَ لَا تَخَافُونَ فَعَلِمَ مَا لَمْ تَعْلَمُوا فَجَعَلَ مِن دُونِ ذَلِكَ فَتْحًا قَرِيبًا﴾ [الفتح: ٢٧]

"Allah has fulfilled the vision of His Messenger in truth: you shall certainly enter the Sacred Mosque, if Allah wills, in security, with your heads shaved and your hair shortened, without fear. For He knew what you did not know, and He appointed, besides that, a near victory."

The "near victory" here means the conquest of Khaybar.
