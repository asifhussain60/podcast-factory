# The Pure Line: Ishmael to Adnan

Mention is made here of some of the excellence of the fathers of the Prophet (ع), from Ishmael onward: what God bestowed upon them of great bounty, what He singled them out with of purity, and what has come down concerning that in explicit text and in indication.

With the help of God, exalted be He, it may be said that this is known to the elect and to the common alike, and that all the sects of Islam are agreed upon the soundness of the narration from the Prophet (ع) in his saying:

> «نُقِلْتُ أَنَا وَأَنْتَ يا عَلِيَّ بنَ أبي طالِب مِنْ كِرَامِ الأَصْلاَبِ إِلَى مُطَهَّرَاتِ الأَرْحَامِ»

"You and I, O Ali ibn Abi Talib, have been carried from the noble loins to the purified wombs."

And God, exalted be He, has said:

> ﴿إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِندَ اللَّهِ أَتْقَاكُمْ﴾ [الحجرات: ١٣]

"Surely the most honored of you in the sight of God is the most God-fearing of you."

And the Prophet (ع) said:

> «الْكَرَمُ: التَّقْوَى»

"Nobility is piety." The fuller wording of the saying runs: «الْكَرَمُ التَّقْوَى، والشَّرَفُ التَّواضُع، واليَقِيْنُ الغِنَى» — "Nobility is piety, honor is humility, and certainty is wealth."

And God, exalted be He, has said:

> ﴿إِنَّمَا الْمُشْرِكُونَ نَجَسٌ﴾ [التوبة: ٢٨]

"Indeed, the polytheists are impure."

Thus the Prophet's testimony to the nobility of his fathers stands disclosed, for they were upon piety and purity, and free of any association of partners with God, as the Messenger (ع) made plain in clear speech. Whoever ascribes shirk to them, having heard the words of the Prophet (ع), has come with a grave slander and a fabrication, and has deserved, unless he purifies himself by repentance, the punishment of the next abode.

God, exalted be He, has said upon the tongue of His prophet Ibrahim (ع):

> ﴿رَّبَّنَا إِنِّي أَسْكَنتُ مِن ذُرِّيَّتِي بِوَادٍ غَيْرِ ذِي زَرْعٍ عِندَ بَيْتِكَ الْمُحَرَّمِ رَبَّنَا لِيُقِيمُواْ الصَّلاَةَ فَاجْعَلْ أَفْئِدَةً مِّنَ النَّاسِ تَهْوِي إِلَيْهِمْ وَارْزُقْهُم مِّنَ الثَّمَرَاتِ﴾ [إبراهيم: ٣٧]

"Our Lord, I have settled some of my offspring in a valley without cultivation, near Your Sacred House, our Lord, that they may establish the prayer. So make hearts among the people incline toward them, and provide them with fruits."

This shows that the supplication of God's prophet and intimate friend was not turned back, for the Sacred House was never emptied of those of the offspring of the Friend of God who were deserving of that great bounty, even when they were among idolaters as men who kept their faith concealed, like the believer of the household of Pharaoh, and even so there appeared in them praiseworthy traits and righteous marks that were absent in others. It is glory enough for them that they were those through whom the light of prophethood was transmitted, and that they were singled out for the birth of the Seal of the Messengers, pure of root and pure of line, blessing be upon him and upon his pure forefathers.

When Ibrahim the Friend of God entrusted his charge to his two sons, Ishmael and Isaac (عليهم السلام), he settled Isaac and his offspring in the Holy House, and settled Ishmael and his family at the Sacred House of God, distinguished by Zamzam and the Station. That Sacred House was the qibla of Ibrahim toward which he prayed, toward which he proclaimed the pilgrimage, and to which he guided all who followed him.

This is sufficient as clarification, proof, evidence, and exposition of the greatness of station with which they were distinguished, and of the bounty and favor God, exalted be He, appointed for them, even though there mingled among them the people of shirk who worshipped idols while claiming kinship in lineage to Ibrahim (ع). Among them were the people of the deep Jahiliyya, who were more astray than cattle, and the polytheists of Quraysh and other Arabs besides them. For the believer may keep company with the disbeliever, and the righteous with the corrupt. God, exalted be He, has said in His noble Book, describing a believer who rebuked one of his companions:

> ﴿أَكَفَرْتَ بِالَّذِي خَلَقَكَ مِن تُرَابٍ﴾ [الكهف: ٣٧]

"Have you disbelieved in Him who created you from dust?"

He made Moses and Jesus and the other prophets (عليهم السلام) among the heirs of Isaac, and singled out Ishmael the intended sacrifice (ع) with this, that the Prophet Muhammad (ع) should come from his most noble offspring and from the choicest of his blessed sons.

Ishmael was the executor of Ibrahim's testament and his eldest son. God, exalted be He, has said in His noble Book:

> ﴿وَاذْكُرْ فِي الْكِتَابِ إِسْمَاعِيلَ إِنَّهُ كَانَ صَادِقَ الْوَعْدِ وَكَانَ رَسُولاً نَبِيًّا وَكَانَ يَأْمُرُ أَهْلَهُ بِالصَّلاَةِ وَالزَّكَاةِ وَكَانَ عِندَ رَبِّهِ مَرْضِيًّا﴾ [مريم: ٥٤-٥٥]

"And mention in the Book Ishmael. He was true to his promise, and he was a messenger, a prophet. He used to enjoin upon his family the prayer and the zakat, and he was pleasing to his Lord."

They are his sons who stood firm upon the religion of the Friend of God, and the bounty passed among them from one to another until it came to Muhammad the Messenger (ع).

The sons of Ishmael (ع) were twelve, the most excellent of them being Qaydhar ibn Ishmael, and their mother was Ra'la, daughter of Mudad ibn 'Amr al-Jurhumi. It is said that when Ishmael died he was buried at al-Hijr in Mecca together with his mother Hagar, his son Qaydhar having grown to manhood, and Qaydhar fathered offspring. The bounty in Qaydhar was manifest. He met his cousin Ya'qub ibn Ishaq ibn Ibrahim (ع), and Ya'qub saw in him the light of prophethood and gave him the glad tidings that the Prophet Muhammad (ع) would be of his posterity, and that the first signs of his light were appearing in the lines of his face.

The bounty did not cease to pass among those sons of Ishmael who gave birth to the line of the Prophet (ع), and not among the others who branched off from the offspring of Ishmael. Muhammad (ع) was of that offspring, and to him belongs the manifest excellence over his brethren and the well-known, evident honor.

So it continued until it reached Adnan, who lived in the time of the prophet Musa ibn Imran (ع). His story is well known and his excellence is great. He met Shu'ayb, Moses, and al-Khidr (عليهم السلام). From him came Ma'add and 'Akk, and Ma'add was the more excellent of the two. Concerning 'Akk, al-'Abbas ibn Mirdas al-Sulami says:

> وَعَكُّ بْنُ عَدْنَانَ الَّذِيْنَ تَلاَعَبُوا بِغَسَّانَ حَتَّى طُرِّدُوا كُلَّ مَطْرَدِ

"And 'Akk son of Adnan, they who made sport with Ghassan until they were driven off in every driving."

That is because 'Akk married among the Ash'arites and traced his lineage among them. To recount them exhaustively is not the purpose here, for what has been set down is the line of the fathers of the chosen Prophet (ع).

Ma'add fathered four: Nizar ibn Ma'add, who was their chief, and Qudā'ah, Qunus, and Iyad. The sons of Qudā'ah went south and traced their lineage to Himyar, and they have a story that is not for this place.

The sons of Nizar were Mudar, who was the chief of his sons, and Rabi'ah, and Anmar. The sons of Nizar are named accordingly: Mudar is called "the Red," Rabi'ah is called "Rabi'ah of the Horse," and Anmar is called "Anmar of the Donkey." That is because it is said that Nizar bequeathed to Mudar the red tents and the camels, to Rabi'ah the horse, and to Anmar the donkey.

Honor remained unbroken, and good continued to be transmitted, among the sons of Mudar who were the fathers of the Prophet (ع), until it came to Naṣr, who is the one named "Quraysh," and to him Quraysh are traced.

It is said that their name is derived from قَرَشَ، يَقْرُشُ (qarasha, yaqrushu), which is earning and gathering. And it is said that القِرْش (al-qirsh) is a creature in the sea that devours the smaller fish and gathers them into its belly. A poet has said:

> وَقُرَيْشٌ هِيَ الَّتِي تَسْكُنُ الْبَحْـرَ بِهَا سُمِّيَتْ قُرَيْشٌ قُرَيْشَا
> تَأْكُلُ الْغَثَّ وَالسَّمِيْنَ، وَلاَ تَتْـرُكُ فِيْهِ لِذِي جَنَاحَيْنِ رِيْشَا

"And Quraysh is the creature that dwells in the sea; by it Quraysh were named Quraysh. It devours the lean and the fat, and leaves in it for no winged thing a feather."

The name Quraysh was derived from this because they overcome the other tribes of the Arabs and hold mastery over them.

Abu Nasr al-Farabi said at the opening of كتاب الألفاظ والحروف:

> "Quraysh were the most discriminating of the Arabs in their choice of words, the easiest of them upon the tongue in utterance, the most pleasing of them to the ear, and the most exact of them in disclosing what is in the soul. Those from whom Arabic was transmitted, who were taken as models, and from whom the Arabic tongue was drawn from among the tribes of the Arabs, are Qays, Tamim, and Asad. It is from these that most of it was taken, and upon them that reliance was placed in Arabic, in inflection, and in derivation. Then Hudhayl, and some of Kinanah, and some of the Tayyi'; and nothing was taken from the rest of their tribes.
>
> In sum, nothing whatever was drawn from a town dweller, nor from those who dwelt in the outlying wastes and the frontiers of their lands bordering the other nations around them. Nothing was taken from Lakhm nor from Judham, for these were neighbors of the people of Mudar; nor from Qudā'ah, nor from Ghassan, nor from Iyad, for these were neighbors of the people of Syria and mingled with them, and most of them were Christians who recited in their prayers in other than Arabic; nor from Taghlib and al-Namir, for they were neighbors of the Greeks in the Jazirah and were Christians; nor from Bakr, for they were neighbors of the Persians and the Nabateans and mingled with them; nor from 'Abd al-Qays, for they were dwellers of Bahrain who mingled with the Persians and the Indians; nor from the people of Yemen at all, because of their mingling with the Indians and the Abyssinians, and the birth of Abyssinians among them, and then their mingling with the Persians; nor from Banu Hanifah and the dwellers of al-Yamamah, nor from Thaqif and the dwellers of Ta'if, because of their mingling with the merchants of the nations; nor from the settled folk of the Hijaz, because those who transmitted the language found them, when they first began to transmit the speech of the Arabs, already mingled with other nations, their tongues corrupted.
>
> Those who transmitted the language and the Arabic tongue from these, and fixed it in a book, and made of it a science and a craft, were the people of Kufa and Basra alone from among all the settlements of the Arabs. And the trades by which those Arabs lived were hunting, herding, and raiding. They were the strongest of them in soul, the hardest of them in heart, the fiercest of them in guile, the most impregnable of them in flank, the most united of them in force, the most desirous of them not to be overcome, the most difficult of them to lead in obedience to kings, the roughest of them in manners, and the least of them in endurance of oppression and humiliation."

This is the statement of al-Farabi.

Returning to the thread: from Naṣr the lineage of the fathers of the Messenger of God (ع) continued, one from another and a glorious man from a glorious man, until it reached Qusayy ibn Kilab.
