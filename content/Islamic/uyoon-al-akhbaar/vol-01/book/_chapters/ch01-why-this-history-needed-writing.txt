# Why This History Needed Writing

The book bears on its opening leaf the name of its author: عُيون الأخبار, composed by Idris ibn al-Hasan, who resided in Yemen. It begins as such works begin:

> بسم الله الرَّحمنِ الرَّحيم وبه أستعين
> "In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate, and in Him I seek help."

The praise that follows belongs to God, Lord of the worlds, Creator of all mankind, who raised the heaven and spread out the earths without support or helper, and who released the rain-bearing clouds with abundant water. He chose His Messenger Muhammad, master of the messengers, those who summon to the reward He has prepared in the Gardens of Bliss, bearers of glad tidings and warners of punishment to whoever disobeys God. He selected those among the prophets with whom He was pleased, and appointed them to guide and to make plain to all His servants.

The author bears witness that there is no god but God, One and Alone, who chose His prophets and His friends for guidance and direction and rescued His servants through them from destruction and ruin, then singled out from among them the one He set above them all by a distinction given to him alone, naming him in the Torah and the Gospel Muhammad and Ahmad. He was sent while people wandered aimlessly in their error, scattered in their idolatry and their ignorance, worshipping idols in place of the Merciful, straying in the wastelands of unbelief and tyranny, giving their obedience to other than the Creator of men and jinn, disobeying God and obeying Satan. Through him God guided to the best of paths, sending him after an interval in which no messenger had come, calling through him to the most excellent of laws and communities, and setting straight through him whoever had turned aside from the road of the hereafter.

He bears witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of God, through whom the best of religions was established, upon whom the verses of the Furqan were sent down, and whom God sent so that both mankind and jinn might be guided by him, addressing them on his tongue:

> ﴿يَا مَعْشَرَ الْجِنِّ وَالإِنسِ إِنِ اسْتَطَعْتُمْ أَن تَنفُذُوا مِنْ أَقْطَارِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالأَرْضِ فَانفُذُوا لَا تَنفُذُونَ إِلَّا بِسُلْطَانٍ﴾
> "O company of jinn and mankind, if you are able to pass beyond the regions of the heavens and the earth, then pass beyond. You shall not pass beyond except by an authority." (al-Rahman: 33)

He bears witness that God sent him as a guide, a bearer of good tidings, a warner, a summoner to God by His permission, and a shining lamp, a prophet through whom God shattered unbelief utterly, upon whom He bestowed abundant favour above all His prophets, and to whose followers He bequeathed gardens and silk, in which rivers of good things and of bliss flow forth, while those who opposed and denied him He consigned to the blaze.

God made his wasi, Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), his helper and supporter and partner in everything except prophethood, his minister and his brother, as Aaron was to Moses, and his backer. Concerning him, together with the Messenger, his wife, and their two sons, God sent down:

> ﴿إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ الرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًا﴾
> "God only desires to remove defilement from you, O People of the House, and to purify you with a thorough purification." (al-Ahzab: 33)

God made his excellence clear on the tongue of His Messenger and made it renowned, and sent down concerning him:

> ﴿وَيُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَى حُبِّهِ مِسْكِينًا وَيَتِيمًا وَأَسِيرًا﴾
> "And they give food, for the love of Him, to the needy, the orphan, and the captive." (al-Insan: 8)

He was the wasi who fought the unbelievers and the hypocrites, and through his walaya God completed His favour and perfected the religion. God made his offspring, in whom the Imamate rests until the Day of Resurrection, the abiding ones, and chose them knowingly above the worlds. Blessings rest also upon his two pure sons and shining stars, Abu Muhammad al-Hasan and Abu Abdallah al-Husayn (عليهم السلام), the two grandsons of the one to whom God revealed:

> ﴿لَا تَتَّخِذُوا إِلَٰهَيْنِ اثْنَيْنِ﴾
> "Do not take two gods." (al-Nahl: 51)

And upon the pure Imams from the offspring of al-Husayn ibn Ali (عليهم السلام), from whom God appointed in every age one who is His chosen and His friend, selected in his own time for the community of his grandfather. They renewed from the law of their grandfather whatever had worn thin, and each passed to his son, from father to son, the lofty station of the Imamate. Among them are those whose clear proof God caused to shine openly, and among them are those who were veiled as the sun is veiled in the blackest night, patient under what befell them. And blessings rest upon the inheritor of their pure station and their successor, in whose line the word of the Imamate remains until the last day, for from his seed is the master of the age and the time, the friend of the people of religion and faith, deliverance from punishment and safety for those who follow him. Upon them be the most excellent blessing and peace as long as a dark night grows dark and a blazing star rises, as long as the sky is adorned with stars and constellations, and the sun gives light and pours its rays over the earth with its broad passes.

**On the wasiyya and the Imamate**

When God sent His prophets and messengers and established His religion through them among the earliest peoples, it was not consistent with His wisdom, His justice, His mercy, and His bounty that He should appoint for His prophets successors, deputies, and guides after them, prophets and friends whom He Himself chose and selected, and then leave the community of Muhammad empty of anyone to guide them and to stand in his place after God took him to Himself. God had sealed the prophethoods with his prophethood and made him the last of His messengers who guide to salvation. After prophethood has come to an end, a community stands in greater need of guiding successors, of one who makes the straight road known to it and keeps it upright upon the purified Shariah and its path. This was never left to the community to choose. No right of decision in the religion of God was given to them.

The Imamate is the foundation of the religion by which the religion stands, and through the Imams of truth obedience to God, the Living, the Self-subsisting, is made sound. It was not even possible for the community to agree upon one man. No such agreement is known in any report or in any transmitted trace. How could it be, and from where, when disagreement and opposition fell out among them, when the Companions divided after their Prophet and struck at one another with sharp swords?

The Prophet said, in what has come down from him in sound transmission:

> "اخْتَلَفَتْ أُمَّة أخي مُوسى إحدى وسبعين فِرْقَة؛ منها فرقة ناجية، والباقون في النَّارِ. واختلفت أُمَّة أخي عِيْسَى اثنتين وسبعين فِرْقَة؛ منها فِرْقَة ناجية، والباقون في النَّارِ. وستختلف أُمَّتي ثلاثًا وسبعين فِرْقَةً؛ منها فِرْقَة ناجية، والباقون في النَّار"
> "The community of my brother Moses divided into seventy-one sects, one of them saved and the rest in the Fire. The community of my brother Jesus divided into seventy-two sects, one of them saved and the rest in the Fire. And my community will divide into seventy-three sects, one of them saved and the rest in the Fire."

Had they followed their Prophet they would not have differed, and had they obeyed him concerning the one he appointed over them they would not have turned away from the truth nor swerved from it. Far be it from him that he should say:

> "لَيْسَ يَنْبَغِي لمسلم أنْ يَبِيتَ لَيْلَتَيْنِ، إِلاَّ وَوَصِيَّتُهُ مَكْتُوبَةٌ عند رأسِهِ"
> "It is not fitting for a Muslim to pass two nights unless his will is written at his head."

and then leave the community itself in disorder and neglect, without appointing by wasiyya one who would take charge of their affair and stand as a marker for the people of religion. Far be it likewise from his wasi and the Imams of truth after him that they should abandon the community aimlessly and depart without appointing anyone for the guidance of creation.

The wasiyya passed from the Prophet to the Commander of the Faithful, Ali (ع), and from him to one friend of God after another, by texts that were made manifest and known, though many, both common and select, denied it. Most of the community rejected it out of insolence and arrogance, and they were as God said:

> ﴿وَجَحَدُوا بِهَا وَاسْتَيْقَنَتْهَا أَنفُسُهُمْ ظُلْمًا وَعُلُوًّا﴾
> "And they denied them, though their souls were convinced of them, out of wrongdoing and haughtiness." (al-Naml: 14)

The excellent scholars and the righteous guides have left behind, on this matter, works of composition and classification so numerous that they cannot be counted, and no one renowned for learning can exhaust them, however great his labour.

**The plan of the book**

The author resolved to run in their field with the pace of a lamed and weary beast, to give his utmost effort while confessing his shortcoming, and to compose this book. He begins it with a summary of the life of the Messenger, follows that with an account of the wasi (ع) and what befell him after the Messenger, his patience until God restored the affair to him, and his struggle against النَّاكِثين والقاسِطِين والْمَارِقِين, the oath-breakers, the deviators, and the renegades.

He then gives in brief a selection from the reports of the Imams of guidance from among his family, those guided by his guidance and holding to his way: those among them who appeared by the command of God and those who remained concealed, those who unsheathed the sword and those who kept to the shelter of taqiyya, those who were tried and were patient, and those of the Shia who contended with them and opposed them, parted from them and left their call. All of this in a concise account, without taking the road of long elaboration, for the best speech is what is brief and to the point.

> وحسبي الله ونِعْمَ الوكيل
> "God suffices me, and how excellent a guardian He is."

From Him he draws help and success and guidance to the straight road; from the lights of the blessings of His friends he takes his kindling, and through them he seeks direction to the path of right conduct.

He named this book: عُيون الأَخْبار وصَحيح الآثار, on the life of the chosen Prophet and his wasi Ali ibn Abi Talib, the slayer of the unbelievers, and their pure family, upon whom be the blessings of the Mighty, the Forgiving. Biographies had already been written with the reports of the master of mankind and of his wasi al-Murtada, who is called by the name حَيْدر (Haydar). Even so, he wished to seek blessing through mentioning them, and not to leave his book unperfumed by the fragrance of their remembrance.

At its beginning he mentions the excellence of the forefathers of the chosen Prophet, and how God kept them far above what the people of ignorance flung at them, claiming they were upon the way of the unbelievers.

> نعوذ بالله من الجهل المُرْدي
> "We take refuge with God from ruinous ignorance."

and from the way such people set upon the people of excellence with slander, insolence, and transgression.

> ولا حول ولا قوَّة إلاّ بالله العليّ العظيم
> "There is no strength and no power except with God, the High, the Great."

From Him he draws success and right direction, through the merit of His Prophet Muhammad and his pure family from among his descendants, upon whom be blessing and peace. He asks God to make his reward for what he intended full, and his striving in what pleases Him and brings him near to Him and to His friends accepted, for he made this purely for His noble face, seeking from none of His creation either recompense or thanks.
