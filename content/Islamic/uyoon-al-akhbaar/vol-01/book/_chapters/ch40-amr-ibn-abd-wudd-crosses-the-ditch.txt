# Amr ibn Abd Wudd Crosses the Ditch

They armed themselves, prepared for battle, and set out on horseback until they drew up before Banu Kinana and called out: "Prepare to fight, O Banu Kinana — today you will see true horsemen!"

Then they urged their horses forward until they reached a narrow point of the trench. Their horses leapt across it, and the riders spread out in the salt flat between the goods-storage and the trench.

'Amr ibn 'Abd Wudd was the mightiest of them, and he rode ahead of them as their leader, calling out for single combat. He said:

> *Calling them to gather — is there any swordsman?*
> *I have escaped from the frost of age*
> *at the station of great deeds across the ages,*
> *and I stood when they called me,*
> *rushing toward the shaker.*
> *Yes, I am still undefeated,*
> *and valor is among the finest of traits —*
> *courage in a man.*

'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, rose and sought permission from the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, to go out to him. Permission was granted, and he went out to face him, saying:

When the Prophet, peace be upon him and his family, sent Ali toward him, he said: He is Amr ibn 'Abd al-Wad. Ali, peace be upon him, replied: I gladly accept. By Allah, O Messenger of Allah. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, granted him permission and handed him his sword. He then raised his hands toward the sky and said: "O Allah, protect him from what is before him and behind him, from his right and his left, from above him and from below him." The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, then said: "All of faith has gone forth to meet all of disbelief."

Ali, peace be upon him, advanced, reciting:

> *When you called the lover, your voice was never helpless,*
> *and sincerity clears a path past every rival.*
> *You must mourn, you criminals,*
> *for the sting of the bees will reach you.*
> *I hope you will prove strong.*

'Amr said to him, "Who are you?" He replied: I am Ali ibn Abi Talib. 'Amr said: A worthy man of noble rank — yet I have no wish to kill you. Ali, peace be upon him, said to him: But I have been told about you. O 'Amr, I put to you two choices and you must answer one of them. He said: Then put them to me. He said: I invite you to Allah, to His Messenger, and to Islam. He said: I want none of that. He said: Then I invite you to single combat. 'Amr said: Your father Abu Talib was a dear friend to me, and I hate to kill you. Ali, peace be upon him, said: As for me, I would gladly kill you, since you have refused Islam.

'Amr then dismounted from his horse and charged at him, striking at his face. He came at Ali, peace be upon him, who gave ground and sidestepped. A fierce struggle broke out between them, and no one could see them through the dust, and the Muslims feared for Ali. 'Amr struck Ali on the head, peace be upon him, splitting his helmet. He then struck at his flank, but Ali, peace be upon him, delivered a blow that severed his head from his body.

The dust settled to reveal Ali, peace be upon him, wiping his sword on his garment and saying:

> *Against them I did not leave my friends without support,*
> *while the knights broke apart before me like this.*
> *I upheld the Lord of Muhammad with my righteousness —*
> *a victory wrested from the folly of his arrogance.*
> *Like the bond between your brother and my narrator,*
> *issued the moment I left him boasting.*
> *I was the one whom he called to challenge —*
> *and I shamed his deputies, though I did so alone.*
> *And O Prophet, O gathering of the confederates —*
> *do not imagine that Allah would abandon His religion.*

'Amr's cavalry fled in rout until they plunged back across the trench. 'Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl hurled his spear as he fled, and Hassan ibn Thabit said:

> *Was it not you, Ikrimah, who fell short?*
> *You barely escaped from the ditch.*
> *He fled and left his spear behind us.*

And it was said:

> *Would you not count yourself among the enemy of darkness?*
> *Or has your back grown tame — is your back like the back of a snake?*

Ali, peace be upon him, killed, together with 'Amr ibn 'Abd Wad, his son Hasal ibn 'Amr and Nuqal ibn 'Abd Allah al-Makhrumi, who had crossed the trench alongside 'Amr and become entangled in the fighting with him. The polytheists asked the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, to let them ransom 'Amr's body. He said: "We have no need of his body or its price." And he left them to it.

Because of what Ali, peace be upon him, achieved that day, Allah opened the way for the Muslims. When He opened it, their hearts were strengthened, and they knew that the polytheists had lost all hope of forcing the trench. Despair and dread descended upon the polytheists.

In the recitation of 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud: "And Allah has sufficed the believers in fighting" — with Ali, peace be upon him — "and Allah is Strong and Mighty." This was narrated by Fadl ibn al-Qasim from Sufyan al-Thawri, from Zubayd, from Murrah, from 'Abdullah. It was mentioned by Ibn Makula in his work "Al-Ikmal," in the chapter "Al-Fadl."

Sa'd ibn Mu'adh al-Ansari, may Allah have mercy on him, was struck by an arrow that severed the vein of his arm. The arrow was shot by a man from Banu 'Amir ibn Lu'ay, and Sa'd died after the Battle of the Trench and the affair of the Banu Qurayzah from that wound.

Safiyyah bint 'Abd al-Muttalib was in the fortress of Fara', where she had taken refuge with the women and children, and Hassan ibn Thabit was with them. A Jewish man came to the fortress, circled it, and began to probe its defenses, for the Banu Qurayza had cut off communication between the fortress and the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, and the Muslims, who were occupied facing their enemy. Safiyyah, may Allah have mercy on her, said: "I said to Hassan: 'This Jew — as you can see — is prowling around the fortress, and I fear that he will expose our position from behind us to the Jews, while the Messenger of Allah and the Muslims are unable to reach us. Go down and deal with him.' He said: 'O daughter of 'Abd al-Muttalib, you know I am not capable of this.' When he told me that, and I saw nothing of use in him, I took a tent pole, climbed down from the fortress, and struck him with it until I had killed him. When I finished, I returned and said, 'O Hassan, go down and strip him of his weapons, for it is not seemly for me to strip a man.' He said: 'I fear there may still be fight left in him.'"

When 'Amr ibn 'Abd Wad was killed, the feet of the polytheists faltered and their resolve collapsed.

Na'im ibn Mas'ud ibn 'Amir ibn Anif, a man from Ghatafan who had been among the polytheists, came to the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, and said: O Messenger of Allah, I have accepted Islam and my people do not yet know of it. I have come to you, so employ me however you see fit. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, said: "You are but one man among us — a single voice — but go to your people and sow discord among them if you can, for war is deception."

He went out on this mission. He was a friend of the Banu Qurayza, so he went to them as a visitor. They welcomed him and drew him close. After he had greeted them, he said: "You know my affection for you, and I have come to give you counsel, if you will accept it from me." They said: May Allah reward you well — we do not suspect you, and we trust your affection; give us your advice.

He said: "You have taken a course of action without thinking carefully about yourselves. You have given your backing to Muhammad's enemies — the Quraysh and Ghatafan — yet your situation is not like theirs. The Quraysh and Ghatafan came only to fight Muhammad and his companions on their own soil. If they gain what they want from him, well and good; but if the tide turns, they will ride away and leave you behind with him — and you know you cannot stand against Muhammad and his companions alone if he turns on you. Your confederates have grown worn down by failure and disagreement; their momentum has slackened and their resolve diminished. You have seen what became of 'Amr ibn 'Abd Wad and his companions — you heard what happened, and you understood the full weight of it. He was the one they depended on, and you saw what came of him when he stormed the trench. Now, with despair gripping people's hearts, a few more days and any shock will send them home. When they go, they will return to their lands and leave you to face Muhammad alone."

They said: You have spoken truthfully and counseled us well. But what stratagem is there after all this? He said: "The stratagem is not to fight alongside these people until you take hostages from them — nobles of their men held in your hands — so that they cannot simply abandon you while you hold their kinsmen." They said: You have pointed us to sound counsel and have done well. May Allah reward you.

Then 'Uyaynah ibn Husn and Abu Sufyan came separately to the Banu Qurayza and said: Between me and the Banu Qurayza there is something you have heard of, and I have met with them and learned their position. They said: And what is it? He said: "The people regret having broken their covenant with Muhammad now that they see where we stand. They have looked at the outcome of 'Amr ibn 'Abd Wad and his companions and fear that we may withdraw and leave them behind — and then Muhammad will reach out to them, and they will send to him wishing to reconcile, expressing their regret for what they did. They are proposing to demand hostages from both tribes — nobles from among our men — which they would then hand over to Muhammad to have their necks struck, or to deal with as he sees fit. After that they would join him against whatever remains of us. Do not be taken in by the Jews, lest they hand one of you over to the enemy."

So Abu Sufyan sent to the Banu Qurayza 'Uyaynah ibn Husn and 'Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl, along with a contingent of Quraysh and Ghatafan, to inquire into this and summon them to battle. They said: We cannot remain here any longer — the horses are exhausted, the provisions have run out, and Abu Muhammad and his companions are blocking us with that trench of theirs. You know the terrain, so come out with your forces and let us finish off Muhammad and his companions, and storm the trench together.

When the leaders of the Banu Qurayza received this appeal, they replied: We were bound to Muhammad by a covenant, and we did not break what stood between us and him without reason. We fear that when the tide of war turns against us, you will withdraw, and the war will have moved away from us — we will not fight alongside you unless you give us hostages from among your leading men to hold as a guarantee, so that Muhammad and his companions can be dealt with.

When word of this reached Abu Sufyan and 'Uyaynah, they recognized that it was exactly what Na'im ibn Mas'ud had engineered, and they refused to give anyone as a hostage. The Banu Qurayza said: This confirms precisely what Na'im ibn Mas'ud told us.

The confederates then held their positions, each faction distrustful of the other, their hearts estranged, and they had no course left but to withdraw to their homelands. Then Allah sent a fierce wind upon them that left them with no vessel upright, no cooking pot standing, and no shelter intact. Abu Sufyan rose when the night had grown long and said: O people of Quraysh — by Allah, this is no place to remain. The horses and camels are perishing, and we have left the Banu Qurayza behind us having gained nothing. Add to that the ferocity of this wind you see — so depart. And they departed.

He went to his own mount, which had been hobbled and was kneeling, and he sat upon it and spurred it, then struck it and set it off at a trot, and he did not release its headband until he was standing in the open. The Quraysh and Ghatafan departed each to their own lands, and not one of them reached Medina. Allah delivered the Muslims through the blessing of His Messenger, peace be upon him and his family, and the jihad of Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him.

It was narrated from Abu Harun al-'Abdi from Rabi'ah al-Sa'di, who said: "I came to Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman and said: 'O Abu 'Abdullah, relate to us something about Ali, peace be upon him, and his virtues, for the people of Basra say to us that we exaggerate concerning Ali, peace be upon him, and his merits. Will you not tell me a hadith about Ali, peace be upon him?' Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman said to me: 'O Rabi'ah, what do you ask me about a man who — if the deeds of all the companions of Muhammad, peace be upon him and his family, were placed together on one pan of the scale, and the deeds of Ali, peace be upon him, were placed on the other — his deeds would outweigh theirs?' Rabi'ah said: I said: 'Even Abu Bakr and 'Umar?' He said: 'Yes, and all the rest.' I said: 'Even Abu Bakr and 'Umar?' He said: 'O you — how could it be otherwise? Where were Abu Bakr and 'Umar — may your mother mourn you — and all the companions of Muhammad, peace be upon him and his family, on the day of 'Amr ibn 'Abd al-Wad, when he stormed the trench and called for a champion? Every single one of them held back — except Ali, who stepped forward and killed him with his own hand, and thereby caused all the confederate forces to scatter. By Him in Whose hand my soul rests, his deed on that day outweighs in reward the deeds of all the followers of Muhammad, peace be upon him and his family, until the Day of Judgment.'"
