# Shaybah al-Hamd: The Boy Brought Back from Yathrib

'Abd al-Muttalib inherited from his father Hashim his bounty, his generosity, and the noble qualities that had been his particular distinction. He was the grandfather of the Prophet. His given name was "Shaybah al-Hamd," and he was called Shaybah because he was born with a streak of white hair upon his head. The name by which the world came to know him, 'Abd al-Muttalib, came to him by another route, and the reason for it lies in the story of his mother, Salma bint 'Amr ibn Zayd ibn Labid ibn Khaddash ibn 'Amir ibn Ghannam ibn 'Adi ibn al-Najjar. The name of al-Najjar was Taym Allah ibn Tha'labah ibn 'Amr ibn al-Khazraj.

Hashim married her and brought her with him to Makkah, but her father had made it a condition of the marriage that if she conceived, she would return to give birth among her own people. She conceived 'Abd al-Muttalib in Makkah, and when her pregnancy grew heavy, Hashim ibn 'Abd Manaf took her to Yathrib on the very journey by which he was setting out for the Levant. He died there on that road.

Salma gave birth to 'Abd al-Muttalib in Yathrib and named him "Shaybah," for the reason already given. As the boy grew, there appeared in him completeness and beauty, virtue and mastery. A man of Banu al-Harith ibn 'Abd Manaf passed by him while he was among boys competing at archery. He saw that the boy was the most beautiful of them in face and the surest of them in aim, and that each time he shot and struck the mark he would cry out:

> أنا ابن هاشِم؛ أنا ابن سَيِّدِ البَطْحَاءِ

> "I am the son of Hashim! I am the son of the master of al-Batha'!"

The man marvelled at what he saw of him. On his return to Makkah he reported the boy's story to his uncle, al-Muttalib ibn 'Abd Manaf. Al-Muttalib went out to Yathrib and carried the boy back with him to Makkah, seating him behind himself upon his young she-camel. When the people saw them, they asked where he had come from. The boy said: "From Yathrib." They asked who was with him. Al-Muttalib said: "A slave I purchased." When he brought him to his own quarter he bought him a fine garment and clothed him in it, then brought him to the assembly of Banu 'Abd Manaf. They asked: "Who is this?" Al-Muttalib said: "He is the son of your brother Hashim." He told them the boy's story and spoke of his excellence, and they said: "This is the one you told us yesterday was your slave!"

So the name 'Abd al-Muttalib prevailed over him. Al-Muttalib himself died afterward in the land of Yemen, at a place called Radman, near Hajar.

Matrud al-Khuza'i composed an elegy mourning him and his brothers, the Banu 'Abd Manaf:

> واْكِي عَلَى السِّرِّ مِنْ كَعْبِ الْمُغِيْراتِ
> يَا عَيْنُ جُودِي وَأَذْرِي الدَّمْعَ والْهَمِرِي
> وابْكِي حَبِيْنَةَ نَفْسِي فِي الْمُلِمَّاتِ
> يَا عَيْنُ واسْحَتْفِرِي بِالدَّمْعِ وَاحْتَفِلِي
> ضَخْمِ الدَّسِيْعَةِ وَهَّابِ الْحَزِيْلاتِ
> وَابْكِي عَلَى كُلِّ فَيَّاضٍ أَخِي ثِقَةٍ
> جَلْدِ التَّحِيْزِةِ نَاءٍ بِالْعَظِيْمَاتِ
> مَحْضِ الضَّرِيْبَةِ عَالِي الْهَمِّ مُخْتَلَقٍ
> مَاضِي الْعَزِيْمَةِ مِتْلافِ الْكَرِيْماتِ
> صَعْبِ الْبَدِيْهَةِ لا نِكْسٍ وَلا وَكِلٍ
> بُحْبُوحَةَ الْمَحْدِ وَالشُّمِّ الرَّفِيْعَاتِ
> صَفْرٍ توسَّطَ مِنْ كَعْبٍ إِذا نُسِبُوا
> واسْتَخْرِطِي بَعْدَ فَيْضَاتٍ بِحَمَّاتِ
> ثُمَّ الْنُدِّي الْغَيْضَ وَالْفَيَّاضَ مُطَّلِباً
> يَا لَهَفَ نَفْسِي عَلَيْهِ بَيْنَ أَمْوَاتِ
> أَمْسَى بِرَدْمَانَ عَنَّا الْيَوْمَ مُغْتَرِباً

> I wait at the head of the road for those who come and go;
> O eye, weep with flowing tears and abundant grief!
> Weep, my beloved, and do not hold back your blame;
> O eye, shed tears and celebrate his memory.
> Mourn the great al-Dasiyah, the mother of the noble ones;
> Weep for every generous brother who was a pillar of trust.
> The skin of nobility is far from the base;
> Lofty in resolve, his ambitions fashioned from cream.
> He never shrank from what was difficult, nor turned away,
> Drawn by the love of the noble and the fragrance of high-minded women.
> A mediator, pure, if they call his lineage;
> He dug deep after the floods with generous hands.
> The tip of generosity and its excess were his demand;
> How my soul yearns for him among the dead!
> Yesterday in Radman he was an exile; today he is here no more.

'Abd al-Muttalib grew up upon the noblest traits of character, upon excellence, generosity, and mastery, and it was for this that he was named "Shaybah al-Hamd."

In this regard Hudhayfah ibn Ghanim ibn 'Awf ibn 'Ubayd ibn 'Uwayj ibn 'Adi ibn Ka'b praised the Banu 'Abd al-Muttalib:

> يُضِيْءُ ظَلاَمَ الْلَيْلِ كَالْقَمَرِ الْبُدْرِ
> عَلَى شَيْبَةِ الْحَمْدِ الَّذِي كَانَ وَجْهُهُ
> كُهُوْلُهُمُ خَيْرُ الْكُهُوْلِ وَنَسْلُهُمْ
> وَسَاقِي الْحَجِيْجِ ثُمَّ لِلْخَيْرِ هَاشِمِ
> كَنَسْلِ الْمُلُوْكِ لاَ يَبُورُ وَلاَ يَحْرِي
> وَعَبْدِ مَنَافٍ السَّيِّدِ الْقَمَرِ الْفِهْرِي
> مَتَى تَلْقَ مِنْهُمْ خَارِجاً فِي شَبَابِهِ
> تَجِدْهُ عَلَى إِجْرَاءِ وَالِدِهِ يَحْرِي
> هُمُ مَلَؤُوْا الْبَطْحَاءَ مَجْداً وَسُؤْدُداً
> وَهُمْ نَكَّبُوا عَنَّا غُوَاةَ بَنِي بَكْرٍ
> وَهُمْ تَرَكُوا رَأْيَ السَّفَاهَةِ وَالْحُرِّ
> وَهُمْ يَغْفِرُونَ الذَّنْبَ يُنْقَمُ مِثْلُهُ
> لَهُمْ شَاكِراً حَتَّى تُغَيَّبَ فِي الْقَبْرِ
> أَخَارِجُ إِمَّا أَهْلِكَنَّ فَلاَ تَزَلْ

> The darkness of the night shines bright as the full moon,
> upon the elder of praise whose face was radiant.
> I speak of the best among the ancients and their offspring,
> the pilgrims ever generous, and noble Hashim.
> The descendants of kings neither deceive nor dissemble,
> and 'Abd Manaf, the full moon of the Fihri tribe.
> He will not fail them in his youth;
> he is found walking his father's path.
> They filled al-Batha' with glory and honour,
> and they turned away from us the reckless of Bani Bakr.
> They abandoned foolishness and baseness,
> and they pardon the offence for which another seeks revenge.
> Give them thanks until you vanish into the grave;
> go, for among your own people you need not linger.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn 'Ali ibn al-Husayn narrated from his father (عليهم السلام):

> إِنّ سبب قول حُذَيْفَة هذه الأبيات أَنَّ رَكْباً مِنْ جُذَام خرجوا صادرين عن الحَجِّ مِنْ مكّة، ففقدوا رَجُلاً منهم غَالَتْهُ بِيوت مكّة، فتلقَّوْا حُذَيْفَة فأخذوه وربطوه، ثُمَّ انطلقوا به، فتلقّاهم عَبْد الْمُطَّلِب مُقْبِلاً مِنَ الطَّائِفِ، وقد كُفَّ بَصَرُهُ ومعه ابنه أبو لَهَبٍ يقود به، فلمَّا رَآهُ حُذَيْفَة هتف به، فأمر عَبْد الْمُطَّلِب أبا لهب، فافتَكَّه بعشرين أُوْقِيَّةً مِنْ ذَهَبٍ، وَعَشْرٍ مِنَ الإِبِلِ وَفَرَسٍ.

> "The reason Hudhayfah composed these verses is that a company of riders from Judham set out from Makkah on their return from the Hajj, and they lost one of their men, whom the houses of Makkah had swallowed up. They came upon Hudhayfah, seized him, bound him, and set off with him. 'Abd al-Muttalib met them as he was coming from al-Ta'if. His sight had been taken from him, and his son Abu Lahab was leading him. When Hudhayfah saw him, he cried out to him, and 'Abd al-Muttalib ordered Abu Lahab to ransom him, which he did, with twenty ounces of gold, ten camels, and a mare."
