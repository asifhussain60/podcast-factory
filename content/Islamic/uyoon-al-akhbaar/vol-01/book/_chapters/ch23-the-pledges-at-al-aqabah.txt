# The Pledges at al-Aqabah

When Allah willed to strengthen His religion and fulfill His promise, the Messenger of Allah would go out during some of the pilgrimage seasons and present himself to the Arab tribes, reciting to them the signs of his Lord, as he had done every season. While he was at al-Aqaba, he encountered a group from al-Khazraj, for whom Allah intended good. He said to them: "Who are you?" They said: "From the Khazraj." He said: "The allies of the Jews?" They said: "Yes." He said: "Will you not sit with me so we may speak?" They said: "Yes."

They sat with him and he called them to Allah Almighty and presented Islam to them, reciting the Quran. There were Jews nearby, and they had told the Ansar that a prophet was about to arise at that time. When the Messenger of Allah spoke to these people and called them to Allah, they said to one another: "This is the very prophet those Jews told us of. Let us not allow the Jews to reach him before us."

They believed in the Prophet and in Allah his Lord, then departed — the Messenger of Allah bidding them farewell — and returned to their land, where they brought others to belief.

They were six men from the Khazraj:

From Banu al-Najjar — who is the Tayyim of Allah — then from Banu Malik ibn al-Najjar ibn Tha'labah ibn 'Amr ibn al-Khazraj ibn Haritha ibn 'Amr ibn 'Amir: As'ad ibn Zurara ibn Adas ibn 'Ubayd ibn Tha'labah ibn Ghannam ibn Malik ibn al-Nahar, known as Abu Umama.

And 'Awf ibn al-Harith ibn Rifa'ah ibn Sawad ibn Malik ibn Ghannam ibn Malik ibn Malik ibn al-Najjar — he is the son of Afra'.

And from Banu Zurayq ibn 'Amir ibn Zurayq ibn 'Abd al-Haritha ibn Malik ibn Ghadab ibn Hashm ibn al-Khazraj: Rafi' ibn Malik ibn al-'Ajlan ibn 'Amr ibn 'Amir ibn Zurayq.

And from Banu Salamah ibn Sa'd ibn 'Ali ibn Sarda ibn Yazid ibn Jashim ibn al-Khazraj, then from Banu Sawad ibn Ghannam ibn Ka'b ibn Salamah: Qutbah ibn 'Amir ibn Hudaydah ibn 'Amr ibn Ghannam ibn Sawad.

And from Banu Haram ibn Ka'b ibn Ghannam ibn Ka'b ibn Salamah: 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir ibn Nabi ibn Zayd ibn Haram.

And from Banu 'Ubayd ibn 'Adi ibn Ghannam ibn Ka'b ibn Salamah: Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Ri'ab ibn al-Nu'man ibn Sinan ibn 'Ubayd.

When they embraced Islam, they said: "O Messenger of Allah, we have left behind a people among whom there is no greater evil and enmity than that which exists between them. May Allah unite them through you; if Allah unites them around you, there is no man more honored than you."

When the following year came, during the pilgrimage season, twelve men came to the Messenger of Allah at al-'Aqaba and pledged their allegiance to him after believing in Allah and accepting him. That was the first pledge of allegiance — likened to the pledge of women — in which fighting was not mentioned.

They included:

From Banu al-Najjar, then from Banu Malik ibn al-Najjar: As'ad ibn Zurara, and 'Awf and Mu'adh — the sons of al-Harith ibn Rifa'ah.

The Islam of As'ad ibn Zurara and 'Awf ibn al-Harith has already been mentioned.

And from Banu Zurayq ibn 'Amir: Rafi' ibn Malik ibn al-'Ajlan ibn 'Amr ibn 'Amir ibn Zurayq.

Dhakwan ibn 'Abd Qays ibn Khaldah ibn Mukhlad ibn 'Amir ibn Zurayq.

And from Banu 'Awf ibn al-Khazraj: 'Ubadah ibn al-Samit ibn Qays ibn Asram ibn Fahr ibn Tha'labah ibn Ghannam.

Abu 'Abd al-Rahman, who is Yazid ibn Tha'labah ibn Khazmah ibn Asram ibn 'Amr ibn 'Amara, from Banu Ghusayna, from Bali, as their ally.

And from Banu Salim ibn 'Awf ibn 'Amr ibn al-Khazraj, then from Banu al-'Ajlan ibn Zayd ibn Ghannam ibn Salim: al-'Abbas ibn 'Ubadah ibn Nadlah ibn Malik ibn al-'Ajlan.

And from Banu Salamah ibn 'Ali ibn Asad ibn Sarda ibn Yazid ibn Jashim ibn al-Khazraj, then from Banu Haram ibn Ka'b ibn Ghannam ibn Salamah: 'Uqbah ibn 'Amir ibn Nabi — his particulars have already been given.

And from Banu Sawad ibn Ghannam ibn Ka'b ibn Salamah: Qutbah ibn 'Amir ibn Hudaydah ibn 'Amr ibn Ghannam ibn Sawad.

Their embrace of Islam has already been mentioned.

And from al-Aws ibn Haritha ibn Tha'labah, then from Banu 'Abd al-Ashhal ibn Jashim ibn al-Harith ibn al-Khazraj: Abu al-Haytham ibn al-Tayhan, whose name is Malik.

And from Banu 'Amr ibn 'Awf ibn Malik ibn al-Aws: 'Uwaym ibn Sa'idah.

When the delegation departed from the Messenger of Allah, he sent with them Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr ibn Hashim ibn 'Abd Manaf ibn 'Abd al-Dar ibn Qusai. He instructed him to recite the Quran to them, to teach them Islam, and to instruct them in the religion. He thus became the first to be called one of the "reciters" in Medina. He would lead the Ansar in prayer, call them to Allah and His Messenger, and it was through his hands that Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Usayd ibn Hudayr came to Islam — and through them, all of Banu 'Abd al-Ashhal. After their conversion, not a single man or woman remained in the household of Banu 'Abd al-Ashhal except as a Muslim.

Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr then returned to Mecca, and the Ansar who had become Muslim set out for the pilgrimage season along with the polytheists of their people. The Messenger of Allah appointed a meeting with them at al-'Aqaba during the middle of the days of Tashreeq, for he desired his dignity, the victory of his Prophet, the honor of Islam and its people, and the humiliation of shirk and its people.

With the Messenger of Allah were 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, his uncle Hamza, and al-'Abbas — the sons of 'Abd al-Muttalib. Al-'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib said to the gathering: "O assembly of the Khazraj, Muhammad is from us, as you well know, and we have protected him from our people. He lives in honor among his people and in security in his land. He has, however, resolved to join you and attach himself to you. If you are prepared to be faithful to what you promise and to stand firm against those who oppose him, then so be it — but if you think you may abandon him and surrender him after he has come to you, then leave him now, for he is in a position of strength and protection among his people and his country."

The Ansar replied: "We have heard what you said. Speak now, O Messenger of Allah, and take for yourself what you wish."

The Messenger of Allah spoke, recited the Quran, and called to Allah; then he said: "I take your pledge on the condition that you protect me from what you protect your women and children." Al-Bara' ibn Ma'rur — one of the masters of the Ansar — took his hand and said: "Yes, by the One who sent you with the truth — we will protect you from all that we protect ourselves from. So take our pledge, O Messenger of Allah. By God, we are the sons of war and the people of the shield; we have inherited that from one generation to the next."

They then pledged allegiance to the Messenger of Allah at al-'Aqaba on the terms of Islam: that they would protect him as they protected their women and children, and that they would stand firmly by him.

The Messenger of Allah appointed twelve chieftains from among them. They were: As'ad ibn Zurara, 'Abdullah ibn Rawaha, Sa'd ibn al-Rabi', Rafi' ibn Malik, al-Bara' ibn Ma'rur, 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr ibn Haram, 'Ubadah ibn al-Samit, Sa'd ibn 'Ubadah, al-Mundhir ibn 'Amr, Usayd ibn Hudayr, Sa'd ibn Khayithama, and Abu al-Haytham ibn al-Tayhan.

He said to the chieftains: "You are the guarantors over your people — as the disciples of 'Isa ibn Maryam were guarantors for him — and I am the guarantor for those among my own people who have believed in me." They said: "Yes, O Messenger of Allah." Then they set out for the city.

Ka'b ibn Malik recited the following, remembering them and addressing the polytheists of Quraysh:

> *Tomorrow has come upon the people and is now a reality —*
> *he told my father that good sense had slipped away from him.*
> *Keeping watch over the affairs of the people, alert and attentive —*
> *O God, do not let yourself be destroyed;*
> *with Ahmad, a light from Allah's guidance shines forth.*
> *He told Abu Sufyan that he had appeared before us —*
> *you cannot crowd out something that has taken its place.*
> *Gather whatever force you are able to gather.*
> *Father, you will prove a fool if you pursue this path,*
> *know well that we have broken our covenants with you.*
>
> *For your nose, if you dare attempt that brave act —*
> *its venom seeps in without a remedy.*
> *As for Ibn Rawaha — he will not yield it to him,*
> *but will honor it and raise its standing,*
> *with a Muslim who neither covets nor is greedy.*
> *And what of Ibn al-Rabi' when you deal with his covenant?*
> *He strives to fulfill what he has bound himself to —*
> *meeting every pledge he has given.*
> *Abu al-Haytham also fulfilled his pledge in full —*
> *are you so foolish as to think of nullifying this dispute?*
> *And what of Ibn Hudayr when you come against him —*
> *wounds, when I tested him, did not weigh on me.*
> *And Sa'd, the brother of 'Amr ibn 'Awf, is*
> *one upon whom the night closes in around you.*
> *Your foremost ones — do not tire yourself of them.*
> *His father al-Bara' and Ibn 'Amr, both of them —*
> *Awsad, his father al-Sa'di, and al-Mundhir —*
> *fulfillment personified — and al-Qawqali ibn Samit.*
