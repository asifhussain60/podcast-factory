# A Light Born in the Year of the Elephant

'Abd Allah ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib ibn Hashim died after the Messenger of Allah had been born, two months after his birth according to one report; another report states that he died while Amina bint Wahb was still carrying him.

Amina bint Wahb mourned him, saying:

طَوْدًاً يُلاَذُ بِرُكْنِهِ فِي الْمَحْتِدِ
كَانَ ابْنُ هَاشِم سَيِّداً فِي قَوْمِهِ
جَهْراً يُؤْكَّلُ فَوْقَ ظَهْرِ الفَدْفَدِ
غَيْثاً إِذَا قَحَطَ الْغَمَامُ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ
عِنْدَ الْخَلاَءِ وَعِنْدَ جَمْعِ الْمَشْهَدِ
وَيُذِيْبُ إِيْمَاضَ السَّدِيْفِ الْمُجْمَدِ
يُعْلِي السَّدِيْفَ لِمَنْ أَلَمَّ بِرَحْلِهِ
فَقَدْ كَانَ مِفْضَالاً كَثِيْرَ الْمَكَارِمِ

"A mountain whose flank is sought for refuge in the place of noble descent. Ibn Hashim was a master among his people, openly honored upon the back of the open plain; a rain when the clouds withheld their water and there was none, in the empty land and at the gathering of the assembly. He melts down the frozen fat and heaps it high for whoever alights at his camp, for he was a man of abundant bounty and many noble deeds."

She said also, mourning him:

جَبَلاً يُعَزُّ اللاَّئِذُونَ بِرُكْنِهِ

"A mountain by whose flank those who seek shelter are made strong."

And she said also:

عَفَا جَانِبُ الْبَطْحَاءِ مِنْ اِبْنِ غَالِبٍ
وَجَاوَرَ لَحْداً مُدْرَجاً فِي الْغَمَائِمِ
وَمَا تَرَكَتْ فِي النَّاسِ مِثْلَ ابْنِ هَاشِمِ
دَعَتْهُ الْمَنَايَا دَعْوَةً فَأَجَابَهَا
عَشِيَّةَ رَاحُوا يَحْمِلُونَ سَرِيْرَهُ
تَعَاوَرَهُ أَصْحَابُهُ بِالتَّرَاحُمِ
فَإِنْ تَكُ أَبْلَتْهُ الْمَنُوْنُ وَرَيْبُهَا

Her lament then lengthened into a longer poem:

فِي حُفْرَةٍ بَيْنَ أَحْجَارٍ لِذِي الْحُفَرِ
وَارِي الزِّنَادِ لِذِي مُسْتَصْلِدِ الْفِقَرِ
كَانَ ابْنُ هَاشِم فِي أَبْيَاتِ مَحْضِهُمُ
كَرِيْماً طَيِّبَ الْخَبَرِ
سَبَّاقَ غَايَاتِهَا فِي كُلِّ لاَزِبَةٍ
مَا غَنَّتِ الْوُرْقُ فِي الأَغْصَانِ بالشَّجَرِ
يُعْطِي الْجَزِيْلَ ثَبِيْتَ الأَصْلِ فِي مُضَرِ
وَالْعَقْلُ حِيْنَ يُقَاسُ الْعَقْلُ بِالْعَرِ
الْجُودُ والْحِلْمُ كَانَا مِنْ سَجِيَّتِهِ
مُحَاوِرَ اللَّحْدِ طُولَ الدَّهْرِ لِلْقَدَرِ
فَإِنْ يَكُنْ قَدْ ثَوَى فِي قَعْرِ مُظْلِمَةٍ
فَرُبَّ شَيْحَانَةٍ وَجْنَاءَ مُحْفَرَةٍ
تَفْرِي الْحَمَائِلَ مُرْدَاةٍ عَلَى السَّفَرِ
لِلْعَقْلِ حِيْنَ يُخَافُ الْعَقْلُ فِي الْعُسُرِ
حَدِيْدِ حَمْسٍ غُذِيْ بِالْمَحْضِ فِي الضَّرَرِ
وَكُلُّ عَبْلِ الشَّوَى نَهْدٍ مَرَاكِلُهُ
غَيْثٌ أَحَمُّ الذُّرَا زَيْتٌ مِنَ الْمَطَرِ
سَقَى جَوَانِبَ قَبْرٍ أَنْتَ سَاكِنُهُ
وَلاَ تَمَلِّي عَلَى قَرْمٍ لَنَا سَنَدِ
يَا عَيْنُ أَذْرِي دُمُوعاً آخِرَ الأَبَدِ
وَمَا بِقَلْبِي مِنَ الْآلاَمِ والْكَمَدِ
أَشْكُو الَّذِي بِي مِنَ الْوَجْدِ الشَّدِيْدِ لَهُ
إِذْ كَانَ مِنْهَا مَكَانَ الرُّوحِ لِلْجَسَدِ
لَوْ عَاشَ كَانَ لِفِهْرٍ كُلِّهَا عَلَمًا
أَضْحَى ابْنُ هَاشِم فِي بَهْمَاءٍ مُظْلِمَةِ
فَإِنْ نَسُوهُ فَإِنِّي لَسْتُ نَاسِيَةً
سَهْلَ الْعَرِيْكَةِ قَرْماً ذَا مُحَافَظَةِ
أَعْطَيْتَهَا غَيْرَ مُلْتَاثٍ وَلاَ ضَجِرٍ

"He lies in a hollow among stones, in the place of graves, a man whose fire-stick struck light for one whose own was barren. Ibn Hashim was, among the tents of their purest stock, generous and of good report, foremost to the goal in every hardship for as long as the doves sing in the branches of the trees. He gives abundantly, firm of root in Mudar, and his judgment when judgment is weighed. Generosity and forbearance were of his nature, though now the grave is his companion for all time, by decree. And if he has come to rest in the depth of a dark place, still many a swift and sturdy she-camel cuts across the plain in travel for the sake of his counsel when counsel is feared in hardship, and every strong-limbed, full-flanked steed, sharp and fed on pure milk in time of scarcity. May rain, dark-crested and abundant, water the sides of the grave in which you dwell. Do not weary of a noble one who was our support. O eye, let your tears fall to the end of time, for the pains and the sorrow that are in my heart. I complain of the violent grief within me for him, for he was to his people what the spirit is to the body. Had he lived, he would have been a standard for all of Fihr; now Ibn Hashim has come to be in a dark and trackless waste. If they forget him, I am not one who forgets, gentle of temper, noble, a keeper of trusts. I gave it freely, neither faltering nor complaining."

His brother Abu Talib also spoke verses in his mourning.

It was said that Amina, the mother of the Messenger of Allah, when she was carrying him, saw in her sleep one who came to her and said:

"إِنَّكِ حَمَلْتِ بِسَيِّدِ هذه الأُمَّةِ، فإذا وضعتِه فقولي:
أَعِيْذُهُ بِالْوَاحِدِ
مِنْ شَرِّ كُلِّ حَاسِدٍ
وسمّيه محمّداً"

"You have conceived the master of this community. When you give birth to him, say: I place him in the refuge of the One, from the evil of every envier. And name him Muhammad."

She saw also that a light came forth from her by which the palaces of Busra were lit. She told his father 'Abd Allah of this, and it gladdened him, and he told his father 'Abd al-Muttalib, who awaited the birth. When she gave birth, she said what she had been told to say. 'Abd al-Muttalib came and took the child up in his joy, and carried him into the Ka'ba and prayed for him, saying:

الْحَمْدُ للهِ الَّذِي أَعْطَانِي
هَذَا الْغُلاَمَ الطَّيِّبَ الأَرْدَانِ
قَدْ سَادَ فِي الْمَهْدِ عَلَى الْغِلْمَانِ
أُعِيْذُهُ بِالْبَيْتِ ذِي الأَرْكَانِ
حَتَّى أَرَاهُ بَالِغَ الْبُنْيَانِ
أُعِيْذُهُ مِنْ كُلِّ شَنَآنِ

"Praise be to God who has given me this boy, sweet of garment, who has already mastered the other boys while still in the cradle. I place him in the refuge of the House with its corners, that I may see him come to his full stature. I place him in refuge from every hatred."

Then he went out with him to his mother and gave him back into her hands.

The birth of the Messenger of Allah fell on a Monday, twelve nights having passed of the month of Rabi' al-Awwal, in the Year of the Elephant. In this Allah, mighty and majestic, wrought a defense for Quraysh through his blessing, so that no enemy should prevail over them while he was among them, neither as a child nor while still unborn.

A nurse was engaged for him from Banu Sa'd ibn Abi Bakr, called Halima bint Abi Dhu'ayb. Her husband, in whose keeping he was suckled, was al-Harith ibn 'Abd al-'Uzza ibn Rifa'a, likewise of Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr ibn Hawazin. They continued to see in him the marks of excellence and the signs of prophecy; they were blessed through his good fortune, and they witnessed wonders of his great distinction.

In the hadith, a group of the companions of the Messenger of Allah said to him: "O Messenger of Allah, tell us about yourself." He said:

"أَنَا دَعْوَةُ أَبِي إِبْرَاهِيْمَ، وَأَنَا بُشْرَى أَخِي عِيْسَى، وَرَأَتْ أُمِّي حِيْنَ حَمَلَتْ بِي أَنَّهُ خَرَجَ مِنْهَا نُورٌ أَضَاءَتْ لَهُ قُصُورُ بُصْرَى الشَّامِ، وَاسْتُرْضِعْتُ فِي بَنِي سَعْدِ بْنِ بَكْرٍ، فَبَيْنَا أَنَا مَعَ أَخٍ لِي خَلْفَ بُيُوتِنَا نَرْعَى بَهْماً لَنَا، إِذْ أَتَانِي رَجُلانِ عَلَيْهِمَا ثِيَابٌ بِيْضٌ بِطَسْتٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ مَمْلُوءَةٍ ثَلْجاً، فَأَخَذَانِي فَشَقَّا بَطْنِي، ثُمَّ اسْتَخْرَجَا قَلْبِي فَشَقَّاهُ، ثُمَّ اسْتَخْرَجَا مِنْهُ عَلَقَةً سَوْدَاءَ فَطَرَحَاهَا، وَغَسَلاَ بَطْنِي وَقَلْبِي بِذَلِكَ الثَّلْجِ حَتَّى أَنْقَيَاهُ، وَقَالَ أَحَدُهُمَا لِصَاحِبِهِ: زِنْهُ بِعَشْرَةٍ مِنْ أُمَّتِهِ. فَوَزَنَنِي بِعَشْرَةٍ فَوَزَنْتُهُمْ، فَقَالَ: زِنْهُ بِمِئَةٍ مِنْ أُمَّتِهِ. فَوَزَنَنِي بِهِمْ فَوَزَنْتُهُمْ، فَقَالَ: زِنْهُ بِأَلْفٍ مِنْ أُمَّتِهِ. فَوَزَنَنِي بِهِمْ فَوَزَنْتُهُمْ، فَقَالَ: دَعْهُ عَنْكَ، فَوَاللهِ لَوْ وَزَنْتَهُ بِأُمَّتِهِ لَوَزَنَهَا"

"I am the answer to the prayer of my father Ibrahim, and I am the good tidings of my brother 'Isa. My mother saw, when she conceived me, that a light came forth from her by which the palaces of Busra of Syria were lit. I was suckled among Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr, and while I was with a brother of mine behind our tents, pasturing our young flock, two men came to me wearing white garments, with a golden basin filled with snow. They took hold of me and split open my belly, then drew out my heart and split it, then drew from it a black clot and cast it away, and washed my belly and my heart with that snow until they had made it clean. One of them said to his companion: Weigh him against ten of his community. He weighed me against ten and I outweighed them. Then he said: Weigh him against a hundred of his community. He weighed me against them and I outweighed them. Then he said: Weigh him against a thousand of his community. He weighed me against them and I outweighed them. Then he said: Let him be, for by Allah, were you to weigh him against his whole community, he would outweigh it."

The Messenger of Allah used to say to his companions:

"أنا أَعْرَبُكُمْ؛ أنا قُرَشِيٌّ وَاسْتُرْضِعْتُ في بني سَعْدِ بِن بَكْرٍ"

"I am the most Arab of you: I am of Quraysh, and I was suckled among Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr."

The people of the Sira mention that among the things which moved his foster-mother of Banu Sa'd to return him to his mother was this: a company of Abyssinian Christians saw him with her when she brought him back after his weaning. They looked at him closely and questioned her about him, and examined him, and said to her: "We shall take this boy and carry him to our king and our land, for this is a boy who will have a great matter before him." It was said that she barely escaped them with him, and she brought him to Mecca and delivered him to his mother and to his grandfather 'Abd al-Muttalib.

His mother, Amina bint Wahb, died when he was four years old; it has also been said that she died when he was six.

The Messenger of Allah then remained with his grandfather 'Abd al-Muttalib. A couch used to be spread for 'Abd al-Muttalib in the shade of the Ka'ba, and his sons would sit around it until he came out to it, and none of them would sit upon it, out of reverence for him. The Messenger of Allah, still a small boy, would come and sit upon it, and his uncles would take hold of him to move him away. Seeing this, 'Abd al-Muttalib would say: "Leave my son alone, for by Allah he has a great matter before him." Then he would seat him beside himself upon the couch and pass his hand over his back, and what he saw the boy do delighted him.

When death came upon 'Abd al-Muttalib, the Messenger of Allah being an orphan in his care, he entrusted him to his son Abu Talib ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, and said in that regard:

أُوْصِيْكَ يَا عَبْدَ مَنَافٍ بَعْدِيْ
بِمَوْحَدٍ بَعْدَ أَبِيْهِ فَرْدِ
فَارَقَهُ وَهْوَ ضَجِيعُ الْمَهْدِ
فَكُنْتُ كَالأُمِّ لَهُ فِي الْوَجْدِ
تُدْنِيْهِ مِنْ أَحْشَائِهَا وَالْكِبْدِ
حَتَّى إِذَا خِفْتُ الْمَدَى لِلْوَعْدِ

"I charge you, O 'Abd Manaf, after me, with one left alone, left single after his father, who parted from him while he was still a companion of the cradle. I was to him as a mother in tenderness, drawing him close to her breast and her heart, until I feared that the appointed term had drawn near."
