# The Bed and the Cave

Islam had by now taken firm root among the Ansar, and the Prophet granted his companions permission to migrate to Medina. They emigrated in groups, one after another, and settled in the homes of the Ansar, who sheltered them, shared their dwellings with them, and gave them preference over themselves. Concerning them Allah revealed:

> ﴿يُحِبُّونَ مَنْ هَاجَرَ إِلَيْهِمْ وَلاَ يَحِدُونَ فِي صُدُورِهِمْ حَاجَةً مِّمَّا أُوتُوا وَيُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَى أَنْفُسِهِمْ وَلَوْ كَانَ بِهِمْ خَصَاصَةٌ وَمَن يُوقَ شُحَّ نَفْسِهِ فَأُوْلَئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ﴾ [الحشر: ٩]

"They love those who emigrated to them, and find in their breasts no need for what the emigrants have been given, but prefer them over themselves even though they themselves are in want. And whoever is saved from the greed of his own soul, it is they who are the prosperous."

### The Gathering of Quraysh in Dar al-Nadwa

The Messenger of Allah remained in Mecca after his companions had emigrated. No one stayed behind with him except those who were imprisoned or otherwise prevented from leaving, apart from his cousin 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع) and Abu Bakr ibn Abi Quhafa.

When the disbelievers of Quraysh saw this and understood that the Messenger of Allah had gained a party and supporters, they feared that his authority would grow strong against them. They assembled in the house of the council, Dar al-Nadwa. Present among them, according to what is related, was Iblis, may Allah curse him, in the form of an aged shaykh. They did not know him and asked, "Where are you from?" He said, "I am a man from the people of Najd. Word reached me of the matter for which you have assembled, and I have come to attend it with you. You will not lack from me a counsel that may profit you."

They spoke and said: "The people of Yathrib have believed Muhammad, and they have given protection to those of his companions who came to them. We fear that he will go over to them, that his standing will grow formidable against us, that his supporters will multiply, and that he will make war upon us with those who follow him. Nor are we safe from the scattered tribes of the Arabs answering him as the people of Yathrib answered him. What is your view?"

Some of them said, "Let us seize him, bind him, and imprison him."

The Najdi shaykh said, "That is no counsel. If you do it, his people will rescue him from your hands and shield him."

They said, "The shaykh has spoken truly."

Another said, "Rather let us banish him from among us. He may do as he pleases, and we shall have rest from him."

The Najdi shaykh said, "This is a thing you have torn open and would then have to pursue."

Another said, "Let us kill him."

The shaykh said, "This is the best counsel you have offered. But bring out from every clan among you a man, and give each of them a sword, and let them strike him with the blow of a single man. When that is done, every group of Quraysh will be defending its own brother, and his blood will go unavenged, spread among all Quraysh, so that the Banu Hashim will not be able to stand against them."

They said, "This is the plan." They agreed upon it, and appointed a night on which they would come to him.

### The Departure of the Prophet and 'Ali in His Bed

Jibril came to the Prophet and informed him of their design. The Messenger of Allah then told his cousin 'Ali (ع) what they had resolved and commanded him to wrap himself in his cloak and sleep in his bed, so that whoever of the polytheists came upon him would think that it was he himself, until he could get far away. He informed 'Ali of his emigration, ordered him to remain with his family, and charged him to discharge the trusts that had been left with him and the debts owed by him, and then to follow after him.

He was still instructing him when the people became aware of them, having already surrounded the house. One of them was saying to the rest: "Muhammad claims that if you follow him in his affair you will be kings of the Arabs and the non-Arabs as long as you live, and then when you die you will be raised and brought into gardens like the gardens of Jordan. And if you do not, you will be slain, and then raised to a fire and burned in it. So hasten and give him that yourselves."

When the Messenger of Allah heard this from them, he commanded 'Ali (ع), who lay down upon his bed, and he covered him with the green Hadrami cloak in which he used to sleep. Then he began to recite Surah Ya-Sin, took a handful of dust in his hand, cast it in their faces, and went out. Allah sealed their eyes against him, and their number was not yet complete.

The Messenger of Allah made his way to the cave, having already arranged with Abu Bakr, 'Amir ibn Fuhayra, and 'Abdullah ibn Arqat that they should set out with him to Medina, carrying him and what he needed, and guiding him upon the road.

The people kept looking through the gaps of the door at 'Ali (ع), who lay upon the bed of the Messenger of Allah in his cloak, and they did not doubt that it was he. When they had gathered and were about to rise for what they had come to do, a man who had not been with them came to them and said, "What are you waiting for here?"

They said, "We mean to kill Muhammad."

He said, "Allah has disappointed you! By Allah, Muhammad has gone out past you, and he left not one of you who was present at the moment of his going out without scattering dust upon him."

They looked, and there was dust upon the heads of most of them. Then they looked again at 'Ali (ع) through the gaps of the door, and in the cloak he seemed to be the Messenger of Allah. They said, "This is Muhammad." They entered upon him with their swords, and when 'Ali (ع) sensed them he took hold of the swords and sprang up in their faces. When they saw him and recognized him, they drew back from him and said, "It was not you we wanted, O son of Abi Talib." And they said to one another, "There is no profit for us in killing this man and losing some of our own." So they turned away.

This was the first act of jihad in which 'Ali (ع) offered up his own life in place of the Messenger of Allah, settling his soul upon being killed instead of him, and standing in the faces of those who sought him, who were many in number, despite his youth and the newness of his years.

### The Descent of Jibril and Mika'il to Guard 'Ali from Every Harm

Muhammad ibn 'Isa al-Nakha'i related by his chain of transmission from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) that he said:

> "لَمَّا أَمَرَ الله - تعالى - نبيّه بالهجرة، وأعلمه بما عقد المشركون من أن يبيّتوه ليقتلوه، وأمر عليّاً - عليه السّلام - أن يضطجع مضجعه، ففعل، فأوحى الله - عزّ وجلّ - إلى جَبْرَائِيْل وميكائيل - عليهما السّلام -: أَنِّي قد آخيت بينكما، وأنّي قابض روح أحدكما، فاختارا أيُّكما أقبض روحه؟ فكلاهما أحبّ الحياة وكره الموت، فأوحى الله - عزّ وجلّ - إليهما: ما أنتما في مواساتكما كمواساة عليّ لمحمَّد. فانطلقا، فاحفظاه من كلّ سوء من عدوّي وعدوّه حتَّى يصبح. فهبطا، فقعد أحدهما عند رأسه، والآخر عند رجليه، وهما يقولان: بَخٍ بَخٍ؛ يا عليّ المحبوب المواسي بنفسه."

"When Allah Most High commanded His Prophet to emigrate, and informed him of what the polytheists had resolved, that they would fall upon him by night and kill him, he commanded 'Ali (ع) to lie down in his sleeping place, and he did so. Then Allah, Mighty and Majestic, revealed to Jibril and Mika'il (عليهم السلام): 'I have made the two of you brothers, and I am taking the soul of one of you. Choose which of you it shall be whose soul I take.' Both of them loved life and hated death. So Allah, Mighty and Majestic, revealed to them: 'Neither of you in your self-giving is like 'Ali in his self-giving for Muhammad. Go down, therefore, and guard him from every harm of My enemy and his enemy until morning.' So they descended, and one of them sat at his head and the other at his feet, saying: 'Well done, well done, O 'Ali, the beloved one who gives his own self as ransom.'"

### The Stay in the Cave

The Messenger of Allah entered the cave, and Abu Bakr with him. The polytheists sought him until they came to the mouth of the cave, and Allah turned them away from him and blinded their sight. Abu Bakr was afraid of them when they drew near the cave, and the Messenger of Allah kept calming his alarm and promising him what Allah, Mighty and Majestic, had promised of His help and the completion of His affair.

This is the most that those who place Abu Bakr before 'Ali (ع) can count among his virtues, for they say: he accompanied the Prophet in the cave. The scholars of the Shia have said that this is nearer to being a defect than a virtue, for he did not endure patiently with the Prophet, nor did he place his trust and certainty in what Allah, glorified be He, had promised in the Revelation. Rather he grieved and grew anxious, and Allah, glorified be He, withheld tranquility from him. He, Most High, said:

> ﴿إِذْ يَقُولُ لِصَاحِبِهِ لاَ تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ اللّهَ مَعَنَا فَأَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ سَكِيْنَتَهُ عَلَيْهِ وَأَيَّدَهُ بِجُنُودٍ لَّمْ تَرَوْهَا﴾ [التوبة: ٤٠]

"When he said to his companion, 'Do not grieve, Allah is with us.' Then Allah sent down His tranquility upon him and supported him with soldiers you did not see."

The Messenger of Allah was the one upon whom the tranquility was sent down. Had Abu Bakr possessed such merit, Allah would have included him in the tranquility, as He included the believers in another place, where He, glorified be He, said:

> ﴿ثُمَّ أَنَزلَ اللّهُ سَكِينَتَهُ عَلَى رَسُولِهِ وَعَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَأَلْزَمَهُمْ كَلِمَةَ التَّقْوَى وَكَانُوا أَحَقَّ بِهَا وَأَهْلَهَا﴾ [الفتح: ٢٦]

"Then Allah sent down His tranquility upon His Messenger and upon the believers, and bound them to the word of piety, and they were most deserving of it and worthy of it."

Companionship in itself is no proof. A believer may be the companion of a disbeliever, and a righteous man the companion of a corrupt one. They recited the word of Allah Most High:

> ﴿قَالَ لَهُ صَاحِبُهُ وَهُوَ يُحَاوِرُهُ أَكَفَرْتَ بِالَّذِي خَلَقَكَ مِن تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ مِن نُّطْفَةٍ ثُمَّ سَوَّاكَ رَجُلاً ۝ لَكِنَّا هُوَ اللهُ رَبِّي وَلاَ أُشْرِكُ بِرَبِّي أَحَدًا﴾ [الكهف: ٣٧-٣٨]

"His companion said to him, while he was conversing with him: 'Have you disbelieved in the One who created you from dust, then from a drop of fluid, then fashioned you into a man? But as for me, He is Allah, my Lord, and I associate none with my Lord.'"

The merit belongs rather to the one who ransomed the Prophet with his own life, who slept upon his bed with his soul settled upon martyrdom in the way of Allah and upon jihad in the place of his Prophet. The discussion of this is long and wide-ranging, and brevity has been the aim here.
