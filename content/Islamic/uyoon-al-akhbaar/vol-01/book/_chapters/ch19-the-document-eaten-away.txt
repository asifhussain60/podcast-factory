# The Document Eaten Away

The Messenger of Allah called upon his people day and night, openly and in secret, summoning them to the command of Allah without fearing anyone in doing so. The Quraysh, when Allah shielded him from them, found that his uncle and his kinsmen of Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib stood between them and their desire to oppress him. So they resorted to mocking him, his family, and his companions.

Among those who did so were Abu Lahab and his wife Umm Jamil bint Harb ibn Umayyah, who is described in the Quran as the "carrier of firewood." It was said that she used to gather thorny wood and scatter it along the path of the Messenger of Allah wherever he would walk. It was also said that she used to spread slander. Allah knows best.

Allah then revealed concerning them:

> ﴿ تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ ۝ مَا أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُ مَالُهُ وَمَا كَسَبَ ﴾
> "Perish the hands of Abu Lahab, and perish he. His wealth availed him nothing, nor what he earned." (al-Masad: 1–2)

— to the end of the surah.

When she heard what had been revealed about her, Umm Jamil came while the Messenger of Allah was sitting in the mosque near the Ka'bah, with Abu Bakr beside him. She had a piece of stone in her hand and said: "O Abu Bakr, I have heard what your companion has said about me":

> *We have disobeyed, and his son commanded him,*
> *and his religion is contemptible.*

"And I was kept from seeing the Messenger of Allah."

The Quraysh used to call the Messenger of Allah by an insulting name, cursing him. But the Messenger of Allah said to his companions: "Does it not amaze you how Allah has turned the harm of the Quraysh away from me? They curse and revile a man they call 'the reproachable one,' and I am Muhammad."

Abu Talib addressed Abu Lahab, reproaching him for withdrawing from Banu Hashim and appealing to him:

> *The dreams of nations have left you in foolishness —*
> *I marveled, O Ibn Shayba, at what has come to pass.*
> *They say whoever seeks Muhammad shall regret it —*
> *do something other than evil, or draw near.*
> *Yet close to you there is none who stands in rank,*
> *only the envious one who harbors treachery.*
> *A man has come from the finest of the sons of Manaf —*
> *do not ride against him from among them.*
> *Be a man of solidarity and honor,*
> *and do not abandon him so long as I live and breathe.*
> *Their loyalty among people is the finest of all —*
> *enemies defend the heights of the Hashimites.*
> *Not through an oath nor through addition —*
> *if he has kinship, you have a bond of honor.*
> *He sails across the encircling seas,*
> *yet he is of Hashim at its very core.*
>
> *A minister over enemies who goes unguarded —*
> *rally all people away from him and stand by him.*
> *Our cousins, are you too weak?*
> *If the Quraysh are angry with him, tell them:*
> *how do they deceive us under cover of darkness?*
> *And how the wombs among them hold a hollow.*
> *We did not wrong the people with injustice,*
> *nor were we light in what they did to them.*
> *But we were the hope of the guardians and the sanctuary,*
> *and glory in the sight of the great ones fills us.*

Abu Salamah ibn Abd al-Asad sought the protection of Abu Talib against his own people, Banu Makhzum, who wished to kill him. Abu Talib protected him, so they came to Abu Talib and said: "O Abu Talib, you have prevented us from your nephew Muhammad — so why do you also prevent us from our own man?" He said: "He has sought my protection, and he is my sister's son. If I did not protect my nephew, I would not protect my brother's son." They argued with him over this, and Abu Lahab rose and said to them: "O assembly of Quraysh, you have burdened this elder beyond measure — you keep pressing upon him in the presence of his own people. By Allah, either step back from this matter or stand with him in what he has done, until he reaches what he seeks." They said: "We will withdraw from you and from what you dislike, O Abu Utbah." And they had, in that, a guardian and a supporter for the Messenger of Allah.

When Abu Talib heard what he had heard, he was moved, and he composed verses inciting Abu Lahab to support the Messenger of Allah:

> *Turn around the nursling — for no sooner are grievances raised*
> *than Abu Mutab, your darkness stands firm.*
> *To him I say: where has my counsel gone?*
> *Do you dishonor it, or have the seasons fallen away?*
> *Do not accept a time when I have not devised a plan —*
> *you were not created for helplessness.*
> *The paths of weakness lie elsewhere.*
> *The brother of war gives regret until they find safety.*
> *Fight — war is ordained — and you shall not see*
> *them strip you as a spoiler or one who spoils.*
> *And how have they not gained anything great from you.*
> *And orphans — orphans in their stubbornness —*
> *may Allah requite Abd Shams and Nawfal*
> *for scattering our group after friendship and affection.*
> *They lingered and tasted corruption all together,*
> *obeying Ibn Dhakwan, Qays, and Disma.*
> *And when you see a day the people rise up,*
> *a woman — Abu Utayba is his uncle —*
> *you lied, and the House of God is offended by Muhammad.*

Hisham ibn Amr ibn al-Harith then moved to have the document annulled — the document the Quraysh had drawn up against Banu Hashim. He was a man of standing among his people and was among those who kept watch over Banu Hashim. Having done this for a long time, he went to Zuhayr ibn Abi Umayyah ibn al-Mughirah ibn Abd Allah ibn Umar ibn Makhzum, then to al-Mut'im ibn Adi ibn Nawfal ibn Abd Manaf, then to Abu al-Bakhtari ibn Hisham, then to Zama'ah ibn al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Asad.

They gathered and met at the head of the pilgrim track by night, and they said: "We eat food, we wear clothes, we take wives in marriage, yet the sons of Hashim — nothing is sold to them nor bought from them, they cannot marry nor be given in marriage." They then made a pact and agreed to repudiate the document. Zuhayr said: "I will start by speaking and be the first among you."

The next morning, Zuhayr ibn Abi Umayyah circled the Ka'bah seven times, then came to the people and said: "O people of Mecca — we eat food and wear clothes, while the Banu Hashim perish: nothing is sold to them, nothing is bought from them. I swear I will not sit until this unjust and contemptible document is torn apart."

Abu Jahl ibn Hisham, who was in a corner of the mosque, said: "You lie — by the House of God, it shall not be torn." Zama'ah ibn al-Aswad said: "You — by Allah — are the liar. We did not approve of this document when it was written." Abu al-Bakhtari said: "Zama'ah speaks the truth — we are not satisfied with what is written in it." Al-Mut'im ibn Adi said: "You both speak the truth, and whoever says otherwise lies. We disavow before Allah what is written in it." Hisham ibn Amr said the same. Abu Jahl said: "This is a matter that was decided overnight" — meaning it had been arranged without him.

The Messenger of Allah said to his uncle Abu Talib: "O uncle, my Lord has sent a small creature against the document of the Quraysh, and it has eaten away every name of Allah that was in it, leaving only the injustice, the severance of ties, and the falsehood in it. Allah has informed me of this."

When Abu Talib heard this, he went to where the Quraysh were gathered at the side of the mosque and said: "O assembly of Quraysh, my nephew has told me such-and-such — so bring out your document. If it is as he has described, then cease your boycott against us."

The people said: "We are satisfied." They agreed to this, then examined the document — and found it exactly as the Messenger of Allah had said.

When the document was torn and what it contained was annulled, Abu Talib composed verses about it:

> *By their faces — I swear by God — people resolve*
> *that they came to our sea for the work of our Lord.*
> *And all that does not please God is destined to corrupt —*
> *he tells them the document has been torn apart.*
> *Untangled, its knots undone, its spell assembled —*
> *those who would not crack it fell before it.*
> *The magic of the end of ages could not prevail;*
> *its strands wavered about its head.*
> *To cut from it an arm and sever its limb —*
> *it was enough that this befell it in full.*
> *Their quarry thunders in fear of misfortune,*
> *and they drive the people of the two sanctuaries to flight.*
> *Which of them at that moment shall be saved?*
> *Let the plower turn his course around.*
> *It has an arrow, a bow, and its string —*
> *and you rise between the two woods as scribes.*
> *So we fortified ourselves in the heart of Mecca —*
> *who will be driven from the civilization of Mecca?*
> *We did not yield; we shall increase in goodness and gratitude,*
> *we grew up in it while its people were few.*
> *If you would freeze the hands of those who would overflow,*
> *and feed until people leave behind their bounty,*
> *may God reward you — O pilgrims — in succession,*
> *may He guide firmly and lead aright.*
> *Bound together — yet they are stripped and united,*
> *sitting at the head of the pilgrim track, most resolute.*
> *If he does not walk in the fold of the shield, I am lost —*
> *every falcon helped her as though she were*
> *a burning brand, enough to blaze.*
> *Bold enough to make the engagements as though —*
> *if his face wearies, his face shall turn out*
> *from the most noble of Luay ibn Ghalib.*
> *Long and sharp, half his leg protruding —*
> *on his face the clouds water and are glad.*
> *He summons the summer quarters and mobilizes —*
> *shelter me, O clan companions of Salih.*
> *And he grows greatly wherever he stood against the enemy,*
> *so we ranged across the land and paved the way*
> *slowly, while the rest of the people slept.*
> *Repel the enemy — nothing else avails —*
> *the great commander has given his order, then praise.*
> *Preserve this reconciliation, every justification —*
> *and the best of people, Muhammad, was pleased with it.*
> *They returned — Sahl ibn Baydha among the pleased —*
> *and we used to be content before that.*
> *What is the association with nations in most of our affairs?*
> *Attain what you seek and do not hesitate —*
> *we are elders; we do not accept darkness.*
> *Do you have something to offer tomorrow?*
> *By al-Qusay — do you have something in your mouth?*
> *A great lord, master and son of a master —*
> *they spent what they spent in their nights, and became.*
> *You have milk — if you speak, it is pure.*
> *I and you are as he said.*

Abu Talib also said concerning the document:

> *And the people of steadfastness from your many people —*
> *are those who hold a station at the end of the night*
> *not to be crowded away from their rightful struggle.*
> *And look — see it from Luay ibn Ghalib:*
> *he is a wreck yet no guilty man.*
> *If the commander devised a plan within it,*
> *and an ancient religion whose people are not let down —*
> *and he who calls to Allah alone has not been deceived,*
> *and if he can nurture the people, he shall nurture them,*
> *and he who calls to righteousness and meets it has not withered.*
> *And there is no one who knows a matter like one who has tried it —*
> *those who have tried in the past know they are not in a position.*
> *When the absent one is reported, the people grow fond —*
> *the document held a lesson.*
> *And they did not take revenge from the one who speaks the truth, though he is vindicated —*
> *may God erase from it their disbelief and disobedience.*
> *And whoever invents what is not true is a liar,*
> *and what they said has been rendered void.*
> *Upon the disgruntled among our people — none to be blamed —*
> *so Ibn Abdullah has become a believer among us.*
> *We are estranged from us, yet not truly distant —*
> *so do not think, Muhammad, that we are losers.*
> *His standing among the people is the finest standing —*
> *a hand of Hashim will protect him from us.*
> *By the people of al-Uqair, or by the people of Yathrib,*
> *Talih is in the palm of the harvest.*
> *So the one who challenged every light —*
> *let us swear, a hero, with the ancient and veiled.*
> *By God the truth — He has confirmed us in it and we were not*
> *among those who spread lies about the close prophet.*
> *You leave him until you fight all around him —*
> *when we are unjustly driven from people, we grow angry.*
> *O our people, do not oppress us — for we*
> *do not depart from your counsel in every direction.*
> *And cease your curiosity about your dreams —*
> *so reward you with weakness in mother and father.*
> *And do not set upon us with darkness and harm,*
> *for God — who is his God — shall help him.*
