# Hamza, Umar, and the Parchment in the Ka'bah

Islam was spreading and growing in numbers. Then the Prophet's uncle, Hamza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, accepted Islam. The occasion was this: Abu Jahl had abused and cursed the Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, and news of this reached Hamza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib. He entered the mosque in a rage, not pausing for anything, until he stood over Abu Jahl as he sat among his people. Hamza raised his bow and struck him a severe blow on the head, then said: "You dare to curse my nephew while I follow his religion? I am the uncle of the Messenger of God." The Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, came and heard what had happened, and he declared his acceptance of Islam.

Hamza, may God be pleased with him, addressed Abu Jahl in verse about what he had done:

> *Abu Jahl was shaken by what we had brought upon him.*
> *By your unjust command — act on it if you will.*
>
> *You harmed the Messenger of God — we have forbidden*
> *your unjust decree, though you persist.*
>
> *You face our defiance in spite of what you have done;*
> *had you sought God, you would not have been brought low.*
>
> *You turned from truth when it was offered to you;*
> *you were destroyed before destruction came.*
>
> *You harmed the Messenger of God and have been led astray;*
> *I could not live after they returned.*
>
> *Until we made you taste humiliation,*
> *souls were healed, and we found our cure.*

Among the most harmful of the polytheists were Abu Jahl ibn Hisham and 'Umar ibn al-Khattab. The Prophet, peace and blessings of God be upon him, prayed that God would guide one of the two to Islam, so that religion would be strengthened and its corruptors diminished.

One day, 'Umar ibn al-Khattab set out with his sword, declaring that he intended to kill Muhammad — the man who, as he saw it, had split the Quraysh, corrupted their thinking, disparaged their religion, and reviled their gods. He came to where the Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, was sitting in a gathering of his companions.

He knocked at the door. When those inside heard his voice, one of the companions of the Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, rose and peered through the door. Seeing who it was, he went back to the Prophet, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, and told him. Hamza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib said: "Let him in — if he means harm, we will kill him with his own sword." The Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, said: "Admit him."

The Messenger of God rose to receive him in the room. He took hold of 'Umar by his garment, then seized him firmly and said: "What has brought you here, Ibn al-Khattab? I think God will send down a punishment upon you from heaven." 'Umar replied: "I have come to accept Islam, O Messenger of God." The color returned to the Prophet's face and his trembling ceased, and 'Umar submitted.

When news of 'Umar's Islam reached the Muslims in Abyssinia, they said: "'Umar would never surrender unless even his donkey submitted" — meaning they knew how deeply he had been set against Islam and how fiercely he had hated the Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family.

When the Quraysh saw that the Negus had shielded the emigrants from them, and that Abu Talib continued to protect the Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, and that his uncle Hamza had embraced Islam with all that had been mentioned, and that those who remained Muslim enjoyed protection such that no harm could easily reach them, they despaired of winning Arab support against him. They gathered, turned every scheme over, debated every option, and in the end saw no recourse but to draw up a document binding the Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib — pledging that no one would marry them, sell to them, or deal with them. They wrote this document, made a solemn pact upon what it contained, and hung it inside the hollow of the Ka'bah. Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib withdrew to Abu Talib ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, entered his quarter, and stood with him.

Abu Lahab 'Abd al-'Uzza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, however, broke from Banu Hashim and went over to Banu 'Abd Shams and those among the Quraysh who openly declared their disbelief. The Banu 'Abd Shams praised him for it, and Hind bint 'Utbah welcomed him, saying: "Well done, son of 'Utbah! You have supported the cause and distanced yourself from those who turned away." She said: "Yes, may God reward you well, O Abu 'Utbah."

Abu Talib said concerning their pact:

> *Have you not carried my message to Luwayy?*
> *Truly — carry a message sent in earnest.*
>
> *Our elder cousins are bound to us,*
> *and our brothers from 'Abd Shams and Nawfal —*
> *yet they rose against us,*
> *drawn by a seductive order of enticement and deceit.*
>
> *Nawasi Hashim yielded to humiliation?*
> *They say: if we kill Muhammad —*
> *in Mecca, the proclamation in every quarter —*
> *you have lied! By the House of God, he is in our keeping,*
> *with his blood-right and the corner of the manumission.*
>
> *By pilgrimage or by the one whose throat was cut at the Nayb —*
> *defenders who free every bone and joint.*
>
> *You will reach him only over our bodies;*
> *prayers in the hollow of the wide valley.*
>
> *You call for mercy while you have been unjust —*
> *either well, or in the end hastened.*
>
> *Wait, and when tomorrow's war brings its result,*
> *you will learn who it is you have wronged.*
>
> *When we pass through it with our swords*
> *on a rise at the head of the lofty mountain —*
>
> *and Muhammad received the spring of the Abtah,*
> *Ka'b's noble pair after the first —*
>
> *and Hashim sheltered him, for Hashim*
> *casts aside whatever would bend and wither —*
>
> *if you wish to kill Muhammad,*
> *and the fluid one is the rock of the sanctuary,*
> *we will defend him with all our might,*
> *and he is like the lightning-flash of a cloud, a cleaver.*
>
> *And all my fighting men thirst to follow his heels,*
> *Dalas, like Zahaz of the revolving springs,*
> *and every charging rider overflowing with resolve.*
>
> *I believe I smell from the wolves of Hashim*
> *men who meet heroes in every quarter,*
> *and the finest lords of men in every trial —*
> *they are the masters of chieftains in every land.*
>
> *Luwayy, a multitude from the sons of Ka'b —*
> *shall I not report it to my own soul among us?*
>
> *A prophet like Moses, inscribed at the opening of books,*
> *and better than what God has singled out with love.*
>
> *And for him is love among God's servants,*
> *and what you have fabricated in your document —*
> *you have a creature that writhes like a serpent,*
> *and one who has no longing becomes savage as a wolf.*
>
> *Awake, awake, before the wealthy are struck down;*
> *we were bound together in affection and closeness.*
>
> *Do not follow the command of the schemer who cuts ties —*
> *a command for those who have tasted the war of Aleppo,*
> *and you enter a war of allies and perhaps*
> *consolation from the bite of time, and no anguish.*
>
> *We and the Lord of the House surrender Ahmad.*

He also said in this regard:

> *Did you not know that we have found Muhammad?*
> *And the hands of the witnesses were filled with resolve,*
> *with it and the wall of flesh — they drink of it.*
> *It is as though the horses stand firm in their rocky ground,*
> *and the cavalry of fierce battle.*
>
> *He commended his daughter to stabbing and striking.*
> *Is not our father Hashim strong of arm?*
> *We do not lament what comes upon us in calamity,*
> *nor do we tire of war until it is satisfied.*
>
> *If the souls of the truffles flew away in terror,*
> *we would find the people of blood-right and sanctuary.*
>
> *And when you raise stories against us,*
> *in the battle of your wrongdoing you will see the breaking of the cane.*

Hamza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, peace be upon him, said concerning the parchment and what the people had unanimously agreed upon:

> *Time passes, and those we love depart —*
> *are the people not struck with wonder?*
>
> *With shamelessness and aggression without cause,*
> *and the words of people whose minds I have lost faith in —*
> *to speak a fool's word or a sign of defect.*
>
> *They say: we shall starve Ahmad out.*
> *Messages of truth and a revelation that is not false —*
> *he came with clear truth and established it.*
>
> *Worshippers who have right and love in God,*
> *letters from the One of Might, to stand in line.*
>
> *To you — and the word of the noble messengers:*
> *if you accept what has come from your Lord,*
> *and during war, the war of kinsmen is grievous,*
> *you will not be disgraced before any traveler.*
>
> *Do not think we will abandon Muhammad as Muslims*
> *without striking brow and forehead.*
>
> *May God have mercy on us, and exalt his neighborhood —*
> *be dazzled by our gathering.*
>
> *Noble Luwayy ibn Ghalib,*
> *and a branch from Hashim, by whom he was known.*
>
> *Wait, and when war breaks out in our house,*
> *the people of the quarter will scatter after their gathering,*
> *and some people will be humbled, left exposed.*
>
> *Basha'a means every leader and follower;*
> *Fayel reveals the servants of the Kaaba's keepers.*
> *They have been struck with an eternity of many trials.*

The Banu Hashim and the Banu al-Muttalib remained under the boycott for three years, without contracting marriages or conducting trade, with food reaching them only in secret.

Hakim ibn Hizam ibn Khuwaylid ibn Asad frequently sent food to Banu Hashim in general, and to the Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, in particular, out of regard for his aunt Khadijah bint Khuwaylid and her bond with the Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family. Hakim was a man of sound judgment, wealth, standing, and protection among the Quraysh. He would send caravan after caravan by night, and the people of Banu Hashim would meet him and take what he brought.

One night Abu Jahl ibn Hisham intercepted him and confronted him, saying: "Are you going with food to Banu Hashim? I swear, you and your food shall not pass until I have exposed you before all of Mecca." Abu al-Bakhtari ibn Hisham ibn al-Harith ibn Asad came upon them and said: "What is this?" Abu Jahl said: "He is carrying food to Banu Hashim." Abu al-Bakhtari said to him: "Rather, this is food belonging to his aunt that she had with him — he is sending it to her. Are you going to prevent a man from bringing food to his aunt? Get out of this man's way." Abu Jahl leapt at him, and Abu al-Bakhtari struck him with a camel's jawbone, landing a severe blow. Hamza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib witnessed this not long after, and Abu Jahl hated that the companions of the Messenger of God, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, should come to hear of it, so they kept it quiet and mocked him in silence.
