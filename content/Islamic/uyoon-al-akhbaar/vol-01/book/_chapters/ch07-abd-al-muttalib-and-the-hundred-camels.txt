# Abd al-Muttalib and the Hundred Camels

Ten children were born to 'Abd al-Muttalib, and the best of them were 'Abd Allah and Abu Talib. Abu Talib was the elder of the two by birth. He was raised on generosity and noble character, and the excellence of these two over the rest of their brothers was plain to all.

When 'Abd Allah came of age and his youth reached its full stature, he attained a great place in the heart of his father 'Abd al-Muttalib, who loved him with an intense love. Now when nine sons had been born to him, 'Abd al-Muttalib had vowed that if a tenth were born he would sacrifice him. Many narrations have been transmitted concerning this.

Al-Qadi al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad (رض) said:

"This narration is the soundest, and it has come from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام): he held back from the slaughter, because of the love for the boy that Allah had cast into his heart and into the hearts of those servants who looked upon him. Then he took him by the hand and went out to sacrifice him, and the Quraysh rose to prevent him and asked him to release the boy. They said: 'If you slaughter him, no man among us will cease to follow your example in this, and each will slaughter his own son.' He said: 'What am I to do, when I have already vowed it?' They said: 'Be deliberate in this matter, and reconsider it.'"

His maternal uncles of Banu Makhzum rose in the affair as well, and al-Mughira ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar ibn Makhzum said:

> وَاعَجَباً مِنْ قَتْلِ عَبْدِ الْمُطَّلِبْ
> وَذَبْحِهِ خِرْقاً كَتِمْثَالِ الذَّهَبْ
> يَا شَيْبَ لا تَعْجَل عَلَيْنَا بِالْعَجَبْ
> فَمَا ابْنُنَا بِشَرَطِ الْقَوْمِ النُّحُبْ
> وَلا ابْنُنَا بِالْمُسْتَذَلِّ الْمُعْتَصَبْ
> فَفَادِهِ بِالْمَالِ حَتَّى يَحْتَرِبْ
> فَسَوفَ أَفْدِيْهِ بِمَالِي وَالسَّلَبْ
> وَسَوْفَ أَلْقَى دُونَهُ مُرَّ الْعَطَبْ

"How strange a thing, that 'Abd al-Muttalib should kill, and slaughter a lad who is like a statue of gold. O Shayba, do not hasten upon us with such a wonder, for our son is no pledge given over to death, nor is our son one to be humbled and seized. Ransom him with wealth, though it strip you bare, for I shall ransom him with my property and my spoils, and I shall meet bitter ruin sooner than lose him."

Abu Talib defended him, he being his full brother, he and al-Zubayr ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, and Abu Talib said in this matter:

> كَلاَّ وَرَبِّ الْبَيْتِ ذِي الأَنْصَابِ
> وَرَبِّ مَا أَنْضَى مِنَ الرِّكَابِ
> كُلِّ قَرِيْبِ الدَّارِ أَوْ مُنْتَابٍ
> يَزُورُ بَيْتَ اللهِ ذَا الْحِجَابِ
> مَا ذَبْحُ عَبْدِ اللهِ بِاللَّعَابِ
> مِنْ بَيْنِ رَهْطِ عُصْبَةٍ شَبَابِ
> بَيْنَ نِسَاءِ شُطُنِ الأَنْسَابِ
> أَغَرَّ بَيَّنَ الْبَيْضِ مِنْ كِلاَبٍ
> وَبَيْنَ مَخْزُومٍ ذَوِي الأَنْسَابِ
> أَهْلِ الْجِيَادِ القُبِّ وَالْقِبَابِ
> لَسْتُمْ عَلَى ذَلِكَ بِالأَرْبَابِ

"No, by the Lord of the House with its sacred pillars, and by the Lord of all the mounts worn thin upon the road, every dweller near the House and every traveller who comes to visit the veiled House of Allah, the slaughter of 'Abd Allah is no light sport, from among a company of noble youths, born of women of far-reaching lineage, a radiant one, clear among the pure of Kilab and among Makhzum, the people of pedigree, the people of the short-haired steeds and the domed tents. In such a matter you are not the masters."

The poem is a long one.

Then 'Abd al-Muttalib said:

> اللهُ رَبِّي أَنَا مُوْفٍ نَذْرَهُ
> أَخَافُ رَبِّي إِنْ عَصَيْتُ أَمْرَهُ
> هَذَا بُنَيَّ قَد أَرَدْتُ نَحْرَهُ
> فَإِنْ يُؤَخِّرْهُ وَيَقْبَلْ عُذْرَهُ
> أَوْجَبْتُ لِلَّهِ الْعَظِيْمِ شُكْرَهُ

"Allah is my Lord, and I am one who fulfils his vow. I fear my Lord if I disobey His command. This is my little son, and I had resolved upon his slaughter. If He grants him respite and accepts the excuse, I bind myself to thank the Mighty God."

The account continues: "Then 'Abd al-Muttalib brought out ten camels and said: 'I dedicate these and slaughter them, and then let him go.' Then he said: 'What makes me certain that Allah is pleased with this from me, and that my vow is discharged? I shall rather cast lots between them and him.' He did so, and the arrow fell upon 'Abd Allah. So he added ten more and cast lots, and the arrow fell upon him again. He continued in this way, adding ten and ten, while the arrow kept falling upon 'Abd Allah, until the number reached a hundred, and then the arrow fell upon the camels. He said: 'No, by Allah, I will not seize this chance at a single throw, until the arrow falls upon the camels ten times, as it fell upon 'Abd Allah; otherwise I shall add more.' And the arrow fell upon the camels ten times. He said: 'Now I know that my Lord is pleased.' So he slaughtered them and said to the people: 'They are yours, take them.' And they fell upon the meat and carried it off."

Concerning this Murrah ibn Khulayf al-Fahmi says:

> كَمَا قُسِمَتْ نَهْباً دِيَاتُ ابْنِ هَاشِمٍ
> بِبَطْحَائِهَا فِي حَيْثُ تُعْتَصَبُ الْبُزْلُ

"As the blood-monies of the son of Hashim were divided out as spoil, in the open valley, there where the full-grown camels are bound and slaughtered."

What 'Abd al-Muttalib intended in the sacrifice of 'Abd Allah was an inspiration from Allah and a following of the example of Ibrahim (ع), who said:

> ﴿يَا بُنَيَّ إِنِّي أَرَى فِي الْمَنَامِ أَنِّي أَذْبَحُكَ فَانظُرْ مَاذَا تَرَى قَالَ يَا أَبَتِ افْعَلْ مَا تُؤْمَرُ سَتَجِدُنِي إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ مِنَ الصَّابِرِينَ﴾ [الصافات: ١٠٢]

"O my son, I see in a dream that I am sacrificing you, so look, what do you think? He said: O my father, do as you are commanded. You will find me, if Allah wills, among the patient." (al-Saffat: 102)

'Abd Allah was ransomed with a hundred camels, just as Isma'il ibn Ibrahim was ransomed with the great sacrifice: a favour from Allah and an exaltation of him, in that Allah joined him to Isma'il in excellence. And Allah bestows His bounty upon whom He wills.

Among the greatest of that excellence, and of the lofty station Allah granted him, is that He brought forth Muhammad (ع) from his loins and ennobled him through his birth. Neither he nor his forefathers were of those who rejected the divine Lordship or denied prophethood. They held fast to the law of Ibrahim (ع). Their virtues, and the reports transmitted of them, indicate this, together with the testimony the Prophet (ع) gave concerning their generosity and their purity.

'Abd Allah ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib was raised upon noble character, purity, generosity, sovereignty and honour. He was beautiful and comely, and the light of prophecy was seen between his eyes, clear and manifest, on account of the nearness of the birth of the Messenger of Allah (ع).

When he married his wife Amina bint Wahb, the mother of the Messenger of Allah (ع), a soothsayer of Khath'am named Fatima bint Murr, of the people of Tabala, looked upon him. She had read the scriptures, and she discerned the light of prophecy between his eyes. When she saw it she coveted a son by him, and called him to herself, but he refused. She offered him a hundred she-camels, and still he refused, saying:

> أَمَّا الْحَرَامُ فَالْمَمَاتُ دُوْنَهُ
> وَالْحِلُّ لَمْ يَأْتِ الَّذِيْ تَلِيْنَهُ
> يَحْمِي الْكَرِيْمُ عِرْضَهُ وَدِيْنَهُ
> فَكَيْفَ بِالأَمْرِ الَّذِيْ تَبْغِيَهُ

"As for the forbidden, death stands before it. And as for the lawful, that which you would make soft has not come about. The noble man guards his honour and his religion, so how then with the thing that you desire?"

She then asked him to marry her. When he had gone in to his wife Amina bint Wahb and had lain with her, he sought to marry Fatima in the manner proper to marriage in the religion of Ibrahim (ع), but she refused, and said: "I saw a light in your face, and I had knowledge of a prophet who is to be sent, whose time of birth had drawn near, so I hoped that he might be from me. But now I do not see that light, and it has departed from you. What has passed with you?" He informed her of what had occurred with his wife, and she said:

> إِنِّي رَأَيْتُ مَخِيْلَةً لَمَعَتْ
> فَتَلأْلأَتْ كَتَلأْلُؤِ الْقَطْرِ
> فِي وَجْهِهِ نُوْرٌ يُضِيْءُ لَهُ
> مَا حَوْلَهُ كَإِضَاءَةِ الْبَدْرِ
> فَرَجَوْتُهَا فَخْراً أَبُوءُ بِهِ
> مَا كُلُّ قَادِحِ زَنْدِهِ يُوْرِي
> لِلَّهِ مَا زُهْرِيَّةٌ سَلَبَتْ
> نُوْراً فَحَلَّ بِهَا وَمَا تَدْرِي

"I saw a cloud that flashed and glittered like the glittering of falling rain. In his face is a light that illumines all around him as the full moon gives light. I hoped for it as a glory I might carry away, yet not every man who strikes his fire-stick draws a flame. By Allah, what a woman of Zuhra it was who carried off that light, so that it settled in her while she did not know."

And she said concerning Banu Hashim:

> بَنِي هَاشِمٍ قَدْ غَادَرَتْ مِنْ أَخِيْكُمْ
> أُمَيْنَةُ إِذْ لِلْبَاهِ يَعْتَرِكَانِ
> كَمَا غَادَرَ الْمِصْبَاحُ عِنْدَ خُمُوْدِهِ
> فَتَائِلَ قَدْ تُبَّتْ لَهُ بِدِهَانِ
> أَوَمَا كُلُّ مَا يَحْوِي الْفَتَى مِنْ
> لِعَزْمٍ، وَلاَ مَا فَاتَهُ لِتَوَانِيْ
> فَأَجْمِلْ إِذَا طَالَبْتَ أَمْراً فَإِنَّهُ
> سَيَكْفِيْكَهُ جَدَّانِ يَعْتَلِجَانِ
> سَيَكْفِيْكَهُ إِمَّا يَدٌ مُقْفَعِلَّةٌ
> وَإِمَّا يَدٌ مَبْسُوطَةٌ بِبَنَانِ
> وَلَمَّا حَوَتْ مِنْهُ أَمِيْنَةُ مَا حَوَتْ
> حَوَتْ مِنْهُ فَخْراً مَا لِذَلِكَ ثَانِيْ

"O Banu Hashim, Amina has taken from your brother what she has taken, as a lamp at its quenching leaves behind wicks that were prepared for it with oil. Not everything the young man attains is by his resolve, nor is what escapes him by his slackness. So be moderate when you seek a matter, for two contending fortunes will suffice you in it: either a hand drawn back and closed, or a hand outstretched with open fingers. And when Amina had gained from him what she gained, she gained a glory that has no second."

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, preserved 'Abd Allah as He preserved Yusuf ibn Ya'qub (ع). He entered upon his wife and lay with her, and Allah settled that light in its rightful place through the purity of the birth, and Amina bint Wahb ibn 'Abd Manaf ibn Zuhra ibn Kilab ibn Murra ibn Ka'b ibn Lu'ayy ibn Ghalib ibn Fihr ibn Malik ibn al-Nadr ibn Kinana conceived by him the Messenger of Allah.

These are virtues that none denies and none rejects except one who denies prophethood and rejects it, and who has not known its secret in Allah's friends and in those He chooses from among His elect.

'Abd Allah ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib ibn Hashim died two months after the birth of the Messenger of Allah (ع). It is also said that his death occurred while his mother was still carrying him.

Amina bint Wahb said, lamenting him:

> كَانَ ابْنُ هَاشِمٍ سَيِّداً فِي قَوْمِهِ
> طَوْداً يُلاَذُ بِرُكْنِهِ فِي الْمَحْتِدِ
> غَيْثاً إِذَا قَحَطَ الْغَمَامُ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ
> جَهْراً يُؤَكِّلُ فَوْقَ ظَهْرِ الفَدْفَدِ
> يُعْلِي السَّدِيْفَ لِمَنْ أَلَمَّ بِرَحْلِهِ
> عِنْدَ الْخَلاَءِ وَعِنْدَ جَمْعِ الْمَشْهَدِ
> وَيُذِيْبُ إِيْمَاضَ السَّدِيْفِ الْمُجْمَدِ

"The son of Hashim was a lord among his people, a mountain to whose flank men clung in the place of noble descent, a rain when the clouds withheld their water. He set forth the fat of the hump for whoever alighted at his tent, in solitude and in the crowded assembly, and he melted the frozen fat for them."

And she said, also lamenting him:

> جَبَلاً يُعَزُّ اللائِذُونَ بِرُكْنِهِ

"A mountain by whose flank those who take refuge are made strong."

And she said, also lamenting him:

> عَفَا جَانِبُ الْبَطْحَاءِ مِنْ اِبْنِ غَالِبٍ
> وَجَاوَرَ لَحْداً مُدْرَجاً فِي الْغَمَائِمِ
> دَعَتْهُ الْمَنَايَا دَعْوَةً فَأَجَابَهَا
> وَمَا تَرَكَتْ فِي النَّاسِ مِثْلَ ابْنِ هَاشِمِ
> عَشِيَّةَ رَاحُوا يَحْمِلُونَ سَرِيْرَهُ
> تَعَاوَرَهُ أَصْحَابُهُ بِالتَّرَاحُمِ
> فَإِنْ تَكُ أَبْلَتْهُ الْمَنُوْنُ وَرَيْبُهَا
> فَقَدْ كَانَ مِفْضَالاً كَثِيْرَ الْمَكَارِمِ

"The side of the valley lies empty of the son of Ghalib, and he has come to dwell in a grave, wrapped in his shrouds. Death called him with a single call and he answered it, and it has left among mankind none like the son of Hashim. On the evening when they went out bearing his bier, his companions took turns beneath it in tenderness. And if fate and its calamities have worn him away, he was a man of abundant bounty and many noble deeds."
