# Sermons, Brotherhood, and the New Community

The first sermon delivered by the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, was at the first Friday prayer gathered in Madinah. As Ibn Hisham narrated, he praised Allah and extolled Him as He deserves, then said:

"As for what follows — O people, send forth provision for yourselves. By Allah, one of you will surely be struck unconscious with no shepherd and no interpreter and no intercessor between him and his Lord. Then his Lord will say to him: 'Did My Messenger not come to you and convey the message? I gave you wealth and favored you — what have you put forward for yourself?' He will look to his right and see nothing, then to his left and see nothing, then before him and see nothing but the Fire. Whoever can shield his face from the Fire, even with half a date, let him do so. And whoever cannot find even that, let him do so with a good word, for a good deed is rewarded ten times over — up to seven hundred times over. And peace be upon you, and the mercy and blessings of Allah."

He, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then delivered another sermon, in which he said:

"In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to Allah — we praise Him, seek His help, and take refuge in Allah from the evils of our own souls and the evils of our deeds. Whoever Allah guides, none can lead astray, and whoever He leaves to stray, none can guide. I testify that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner. The finest of speech is the Book of Allah — blessed and exalted is He. Whoever Allah has adorned with it in his heart, and who has entered Islam after disbelief, choosing it over all other discourse, has found the best of guidance. Love what Allah loves, and love Allah with all your hearts. Do not grow weary of the word of Allah and His remembrance, and do not let your hearts harden against Him, for Allah selects from all things the best of deeds, the best of servants, and the most righteous of speech. Observe what Allah has made lawful and forbidden for people; worship Allah and associate nothing with Him; fear Allah as He truly deserves to be feared; be sincere to Allah in what you profess with your mouths; love one another in the spirit of Allah. Allah is angered when His covenant is broken. And peace be upon you."

The Messenger of Allah, may peace be upon him, concluded a pact with the Jews, made peace between the Aws and the Khazraj, and abolished the blood feuds of the pre-Islamic era.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then established bonds of brotherhood among his companions. He made Hamzah, peace be upon him, and Zayd ibn Haritha brothers, and Ja'far ibn Abi Talib — who was at that time in the land of Abyssinia — was paired with Mu'adh ibn Jabal. He made Abu Bakr and 'Umar brothers, and in the same way established brotherhood between all of his companions, pairing each man with another.

Then he took the hand of Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, and said: "This is my brother, my guardian and the fulfillment of my pledge, and my successor after me."

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had settled, and Allah's religion had spread and taken root in the city of his dwelling, Abu Fais Sarmah ibn Abi Anas — a brother of the Banu 'Adi ibn al-Najjar — composed verses commemorating the honor Allah had bestowed upon his people through Islam, and the distinction of having the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, descend among them:

> *He recalls when he found a true and loyal friend —*
> *how few such supporters were among Quraysh.*
> *He found none to shelter him and none to heed his call,*
> *though he declared himself openly before the people of the seasons.*
> *He arrived content, and we received him gladly —*
> *when he came to us, Allah made His religion manifest.*
> *He was granted Allah's help through our hands,*
> *and found two true friends; the city was set at peace by him.*
> *He tells us what Moses said when he answered the caller,*
> *and what Noah said to his people —*
> *swift in proclamation, fearless of those far away,*
> *until he was no longer reckoned among the rejected.*
>
> *We gave him our wealth and our very selves in times of hardship and adversity,*
> *knowing that there is no god but Allah — and you know that Allah is the best of guides.*
>
> *All of them — even the most beloved — if you bear enmity toward what people hold as ordinary,*
> *you have come to your calling.*
>
> *I say, when I call out to you in every pledge of allegiance:*
> *do not expose yourself to your enemies.*
> *I say, when you cross fearsome ground*
> *and have no one to guard you —*
> *he treads an open plain whose edges spread wide;*
> *had Allah not appointed him a protector,*
> *the young man would not know how to remain pious.*
> *If you become arrogant and turn rebellious,*
> *like the palm trees that defy their Lord —*

Salman al-Farsi, may Allah be pleased with him, reached the Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, and embraced Islam. He was from Persia. He had come to love the Christian religion and abandoned his father's faith, for his father followed the religion of the Magi. He traveled with caravans to the Levant and served righteous monks one after another. The last of them, as he was dying, counseled him and said: "You will find that a prophet sent by Allah — the best of prophets — whose time has now come is near at hand; he accepts gifts but does not accept charity, and he bears the seal of prophethood between his shoulders."

Salman, may Allah be pleased with him, had been sold by compulsion into the ownership of a Jewish man. He thus came to the Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, and put him to the test: he brought him charity, but the Prophet did not eat from it and distributed it among his companions. Then Salman brought him a gift, and the Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, ate from it and shared it with them. Salman then looked at the seal of prophethood between his shoulders, recognized the signs, and submitted to Islam.

Salman went on to become one of the closest companions to the Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family. The Prophet would greet him warmly, draw him near, and speak highly of him. He said of him: "Salman is of us, the Ahl al-Bayt." He would pray for him and send him greetings.

Salman was among the Shi'a of Ali, peace be upon him. Ali, peace be upon him, had followers during the lifetime of the Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, who were known for their belief in his guardianship and were celebrated for it. Among them were: Salman al-Farsi, 'Ammar, Abu Dhar, al-Miqdad, 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, 'Abd Allah ibn 'Abbas, and others. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, would remind people of their virtue and call them the Shi'a of Ali.

This is a well-known matter, familiar to both the learned and the general public, and the narrations that have come down concerning it are too numerous to be contained within this book.

Many Jews converted to Islam, as did the remaining members of the Aws and Khazraj — some of them sincerely, while others concealed hypocrisy when they saw that Islam had grown strong and was taking hold.

Among the hypocrites were 'Abd Allah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul and others — many in number and well known by name. They would say one thing to the believers and another among themselves:

> ﴿ وَإِذَا لَقُوا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا قَالُوا آمَنَّا وَإِذَا خَلَوْا إِلَىٰ شَيَاطِينِهِمْ قَالُوا إِنَّا مَعَكُمْ إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُسْتَهْزِئُونَ ﴾
> "And when they meet those who believe, they say, 'We believe'; but when they are alone with their evil ones, they say, 'Indeed, we are with you; we were only mockers.'" (al-Baqarah: 14)

The Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, accepted from them what they outwardly displayed of Islam and left them to themselves, because verses had been revealed exposing their hypocrisy and making clear that even if they said they believed with their mouths, their hearts had not believed.

The Ansar said: "We used to recognize the hypocrites at the time of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, by their hatred of Ali ibn Abi Talib — peace be upon him." This is further confirmed by the Prophet's own words to Ali, peace be upon him: "No one hates you, O Ali, except a hypocrite" — or, in some narrations: "except one of illegitimate birth," or "except one whose mother conceived him while she was menstruating," or "except one who was conceived in transgression."

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had established himself in Madinah, news reached the polytheists of Quraysh of the unity between the Muhajireen and the Ansar and the growing strength of the Muslim community. When the religion became manifest, this filled them with revulsion and overwhelming grief. They yearned for the death of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, yet found themselves powerless to bring it about.
