# Ambush in the Valley of Hunayn

When the Hawazin tribes heard what had happened to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, and what Allah had granted him in the conquest of Makkah, Malik ibn 'Awf al-Nasri rallied them. He gathered together the Hawazin, all of Thaqif, all of Nasr and Jushm, Sa'd ibn Bakr, and a few from Banu Hilal. Of all the clans of Qays 'Aylan, only these few took part; the rest were absent. The Hawazin brought with them Ka'b and Kilab, though none of those clans distinguished themselves by name. Among Banu Jushm was a great elder, Duraid ibn al-Sammah, a man whose only remaining value was his judgment and experience in warfare; he was carried in a litter. Among Banu Thaqif there were Qarib ibn al-Aswad ibn Mas'ud ibn Mu'athib, and among Banu Malik there were Dhu al-Khimar Subay' ibn al-Harith ibn Malik and his brother Ahmar ibn al-Harith. Overall command of the people fell to Malik ibn 'Awf al-Nasri.

When Malik resolved to march against the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, he led the people out along with their wealth, their wives, and their children. When he descended upon Awtas, the people gathered around him, including Duraid ibn al-Sammah, who was carried in his litter to that place. Duraid asked: "In what valley are you camped?" They said: "In Awtas." He said: "Good terrain for horses — neither rocky enough to injure hooves nor so soft as to swallow them. But what is this I do not hear? No lowing of camels, no braying of donkeys, no crying of children, no wailing of women?" They told him: Malik ibn 'Awf had led the people out with their wealth, their wives, and their children.

He said: "Bring him to me." When Malik came before him, Duraid said: "O Malik, you have become the leader of your people, and this day will have days after it. Why do I hear no lowing of camels, no braying of donkeys, no crying of children, no wailing of women?" Malik replied: "I brought the people along with their wealth, their children, and their wives." Duraid said: "And why is that?" Malik said: "I wanted every man to feel that his family and his wealth were behind him, so that he would fight to defend them."

Duraid said: "By God, you think like a shepherd of sheep! Does a routed man ever save anything? In the end, nothing helps a man but his own sword and spear. If the battle goes against you, you will have exposed your family and your wealth."

Then he asked: "And what of the Kilab and Ka'b — did they witness this gathering?" They said: "None of them came." He said: "The men of resolve and seriousness were absent. Had this been the day of al-'Ala' and Rifa'a, neither Kilab nor Ka'b would have stayed away. Who from Amir's people is with you?" They said: "'Amr ibn 'Amir and 'Awf ibn 'Amir." He said: "Those two branches of Amir will neither help nor harm. O Malik, you have accomplished nothing by bringing the women and wealth before the horses — by 'egg' he meant property and women. Send them back to the highlands and put the fighting men on horseback. If you win, what lies behind you is yours; if you lose, at least you have preserved your family and your wealth."

Malik said: "By God, I will not do that. You have grown old and your mind has weakened with your age."

"Obey me, O assembly of Hawazin!" Malik called out. "Or I will fall upon this sword until it comes out through my back."

They disliked that Duraid ibn al-Sammah should have any say or influence in the matter, and so they said: "We obey you."

Duraid ibn al-Sammah said: "This is a day I have not witnessed before, and it has not stirred wonder in me." He said:

> *I wish it had a trunk*
> *I would hide within it*
> *I would lead and extinguish the impulse*
> *It is like a crack*

Then Malik said to the people: "When you see them, break off the hilts of your swords, then charge them as one man."

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, was preparing to march against Hawazin, he was reminded that Safwan ibn Umayyah possessed shields and weapons. He sent a messenger to Safwan ibn Umayyah, who was at that time still a polytheist. He said to him: "O Safwan, lend us what you have of weapons so that we may meet our enemy tomorrow."

Safwan said: "O Muhammad, is this by compulsion?" The Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "It is a guaranteed loan, to be returned to you in full." Safwan said: "In that case, there is no objection." It is said that the Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, borrowed from him a hundred shields, along with the weapons and equipment to carry them.

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, received word of Malik ibn 'Awf's movements, he departed Makkah on the twelfth — with ten thousand men, plus two thousand from the people of Makkah and ten thousand of his companions who had marched with him to the conquest of Makkah.

As the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, marched with the Muslims, 'Abbas ibn Mirdas al-Sulami said:

> *From me, a letter of counsel, plainly set forth —*
> *carry it to Hawazin, to great and small alike.*
> *His army fills the breadth of earth, its farthest corners:*
> *I say the Messenger of God shall meet you in the morning.*
> *And the Muslims are the garment of God — Ghassan among them —*
> *in them I shield you whole, yet you have turned away.*
> *And the Ajraban: Banu 'Abs and Dhubyan are with him,*
> *and on his right, the support of Banu Asad,*
> *in their vanguard, Aws and 'Uthman.*
> *The earth near trembled beneath his awe.*

When the Muslims reached a place called Dhat Anwat — a tree — it was a tree the Quraysh had venerated in the time of ignorance. They would come to it every year, hang their weapons upon it, and slaughter animals beside it, staying near it for a day. Some of those who had recently embraced Islam called out to the Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him: "O Messenger of Allah, make for us a Dhat Anwat as they have one."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Allah is greater! By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, you have said what the people of Moses said to Moses: 'Make for us a god as they have gods. He said: You are a people who are ignorant.'" (al-A'raf: 138) "It is the same pattern — you would follow the ways of those who came before you."

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, descended into the valley of Hunayn, he moved through a wide, hollow valley of the Tihama region in the darkness before dawn. The Hawazin and those who had joined them had arrived before him and lay in ambush in the ravines, bends, and narrow passages of the valley. They had gathered, drawn up their ranks, and made ready. The Muslims, advancing through that valley in loose order, were suddenly overwhelmed and broke into flight as one man; the men turned and retreated, and no one could be blamed.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, turned to the right and called out: "Come to me! Where are you going? I am the Messenger of Allah. I am Muhammad ibn 'Abdullah."

The people fled in defeat. Some of the hypocrites revealed what lay in their hearts. Abu Sufyan said: "This is a rout that will not be stopped until it reaches the sea." And another cried aloud: "Is not the magic defeated today?"

Five men of the Banu 'Abd al-Muttalib stood firm with the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him: 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, sword in hand, shielding and striking for him; al-'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, who held the bridle of the Messenger of Allah's mule — and the Prophet was riding his white mule — Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, al-Fadl ibn al-'Abbas, and al-'Abbas himself.

It was also said that Qatham was not among them, but rather Ja'far ibn Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said to al-'Abbas — who was a man with a powerful voice — "Call to the people and let them know where I am."

The people had been routed, as Allah the Almighty has described in His words: "Indeed, Allah has given you victory on many fields — and on the day of Hunayn, when your great numbers pleased you but availed you nothing, and the earth, vast as it is, seemed to close in upon you; then you turned back in flight. Then Allah sent down His tranquility upon His Messenger and upon the believers." (al-Tawbah: 25–26) — meaning those who remained steadfast with the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, when the people fled.

Shayba ibn 'Uthman ibn Abi Talha had resolved to kill the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him. He said: "Today I shall avenge my father." His father had been killed on the day of Uhud. He said: "I drew close to the Messenger of Allah, but my heart was overwhelmed — it was as though something closed over me and held me back."

'Ali ibn Abi Talib looked and saw the man carrying the polytheists' standard — mounted on a camel, banner in hand — driving into the Muslims and pressing them hard as they fell back before Hawazin, with the enemy behind him. 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, charged him, struck the camel's leg with his sword, felled the beast, and split the man's head open. The fallen camel became a barrier between the Muslims and the polytheists.

Al-'Abbas called out at the top of his voice: "O assembly of Muslims! O assembly of the Muhajirun! O assembly of the Ansar! O people of the Tree! O people of the Pledge of al-Ridwan — come to your Prophet!"

From every direction came the reply: "Labbaik! Labbaik!" Those who had fled were already beginning to realize that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had not been killed and had not abandoned those who had turned back. Each man who wished to wheel his horse or camel around found he could not do so in the press and crush of the crowd, so he would take his weapons, cast himself from his mount, and make his way on foot to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and his family.

When the polytheist standard-bearer was struck down, the enemy could no longer hold their position.
