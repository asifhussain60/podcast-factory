# The Caravan and the Dream of Atikah

Then came the Great Battle of Badr.

This is because the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, received word that Abu Sufyan ibn Harb was returning from the Levant with a great merchant caravan belonging to Quraysh — carrying their wealth and trade goods — accompanied by thirty or forty men of Quraysh. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, urged his companions forward, saying: "This is the caravan of Quraysh with their wealth — go out against it, and perhaps Allah will grant it to you as spoils." The people readied themselves, though some were reluctant, supposing that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, would not actually face a battle.

Abu Sufyan received word that Muhammad had roused his companions against his caravan. He hired Damdam ibn 'Amr al-Ghifari, sent him to Mecca, and ordered him to call Quraysh to arms in defense of their property.

'Atikah bint 'Abd al-Muttalib had a vision three days before Damdam arrived in Mecca. It terrified her. She saw a rider approaching on a camel until he halted, then called out at the top of his voice: "Hasten to your fallen warriors — in three days!" He then entered the mosque, and the people followed him. He made his camel kneel against the back of the Ka'bah and cried out in the same way: "Hasten to your fallen warriors — in three days!" Then he led his camel to the summit of Abu Qubays, called out as before, took a rock, and hurled it from the mountain. It crashed to the ground below, and as it rolled to the foot of the mountain it shattered, until there was not a single house or dwelling in Mecca that was not struck by a fragment of that rock.

She told her brother 'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib about this, and al-'Abbas in turn mentioned it to al-Walid ibn 'Utbah ibn Rabi'ah, who spread the news. Abu Jahl then said: "Since when has this prophetess arisen among you? Is it not enough that your men lay claim to prophethood — must your women do so as well?"

Al-'Abbas denied that she had seen anything, and the Quraysh weighed in heavily on this, none more so than Abu Jahl ibn Hisham.

Al-'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with him, said: The women of Banu 'Abd al-Muttalib said to me: "Abu Jahl is belittling your men with insults, and will not be satisfied until he turns to insulting your women!" So I went out in anger, intending to confront Abu Jahl with something he would detest. I entered the mosque and saw him, and moved toward him to expose him. He retreated from some of what he had been saying, and I cornered him — for he was a lean man with a face like iron, a tongue like iron, and a gaze like iron. When he moved toward the door of the mosque, I pressed him harder. I said to myself: "What does the curse of Allah matter — would this change anything for me if I were to curse him?"

Then something distracted me from him: the voice of Damdam ibn 'Amr al-Ghifari shouting from the belly of the valley, standing on his camel with his shirt torn, saying: "O people of Quraysh — the caravan! The caravan! Your wealth with Abu Sufyan has been intercepted by Muhammad and his companions — rescue it, rescue it!" And so that drew me away from Abu Jahl, and it drew him away from the matter between us.

The people prepared swiftly, saying: "Does Muhammad and his companions think this will be like the affair of Ibn al-Hadrami? No, by God, it will not." Not one of the nobles of Quraysh stayed behind, except that Abu Lahab remained and sent in his place al-'As ibn Hisham ibn al-Mughirah.

Quraysh took out al-'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib and Talib ibn Abi Talib and 'Aqil ibn Abi Talib with them to fight the Messenger of Allah, though they went out reluctantly. There arose a dispute between them and a companion of theirs, so they turned him back and said: "We know that your heart is with Muhammad." So Talib returned, and composed the following verses about it:

> *Oh Lord, whether they went forth with a companion*
> *in a league of sworn warriors*
> *in one of these positions —*
> *so make them the losers, not the victors,*
> *and let them be the despoiled, not the ones who despoil.*

Talib had in fact accepted Islam, and it is he who says of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him:

> *The place of sleep and flowers —*
> *the King's messenger, for a time.*
> *The glory of Banu Hashim has come,*
> *and pure Banu Hashim: Ahmad.*
>
> *Bold of heart, of clear voice —*
> *the great and honorable, the light of the land.*
> *If the one who sets limits ponders his power —*
> *generous of aspect, fair as Lebanon.*
> *Pure in conduct, both inwardly and outwardly,*
> *chaste, pious, immaculate in character.*
> *From the neighborhood of Raba and from Zahra —*
> *bountiful, springlike, noble, noble of birth.*
> *War brings rebuke upon those who deserve rebuke,*
> *and he is like a lion — relentless, unspent.*
> *How many of his cries have raised the fallen?*
> *Long moaning gives way to relief.*

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, set out in the early nights of Ramadan with his companions — it is said on the eighth. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had two black banners: one he gave to 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, and the other to the Ansar.

The companions of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had seventy camels, which they rode in turns. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and 'Ali ibn Abi Talib shared one camel between them in turn, and the Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, walked alongside the Muhajirun and the Ansar until they descended upon Sajjaj — which is the well of al-Ruh — then continued until, drawing close to al-Safra', he sent two men ahead to gather news of Abu Sufyan ibn Harb.

He pressed on until he came to a valley called Dhafran, where he made camp. There the news reached him that Quraysh had marched out to defend their honor. He consulted the people, and they responded well and gave good counsel. He said: "Advance and be of good cheer, for Allah has promised me one of the two parties, and by Allah, it is as though I can see right now where the enemy will fall."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then departed from Dhafran and soon descended upon Badr. When he had nearly arrived, he sent 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, with a group of Muslims to scout for news of Quraysh at the waters of Badr. They found two servants of Quraysh there: Aslam, a slave of Banu al-Hajjaj, and 'Arid, the slave of Abu Yasar of Banu al-'As ibn Sa'id. They captured them and brought them to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him. He said: "Tell me about Quraysh." They said: "They are behind that great dune you see — the Dune of 'Aqanqal."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said to them: "How many are the people?" They said: "Many." He said: "What is their number?" They then said: "We do not know their count." He said: "How many camels do they slaughter each day?" They said: "Nine one day and ten another." He said: "Then the people number between nine hundred and a thousand." He asked them: "Who among them are the nobles?"

The Quraysh recalled him. He said to his companions: "This is Makkah — she has cast her liver-pieces before you."

As for Abu Sufyan, he turned his camel away from the road and left Badr on his left. Abu Sufyan had learned that al-'Ir was safe, and he sent word to the Quraysh ordering them to turn back, but they refused.

This is the Quraysh: after falling into dispute among themselves, they pressed on until they had advanced as far as their most bitter enemies. From the valley, behind the Aqqul and the belly of the valley — that night — between Badr and the Aqqal, the dune was left behind by the Quraysh, and hearts lay full and exposed in the depths of the night, from the belly of the valley toward the city.

The Prophet, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, moved until he reached the nearest water of the people, and he descended there. He then commanded that a basin be built at the water source where he had descended, and it was filled with water.

When the Quraysh drew near, they were let through. When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, saw them advancing from al-'Aqqul, and from the dune through which they entered the valley, he said: "O Allah, here are the Quraysh — they have come in their arrogance and deceit, to challenge You and to give the lie to Your Messenger. O Allah, grant me the victory You have promised me. I am the one who stands against them."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, caught sight of 'Utbah ibn Rabi'ah among the Quraysh ranks and said: "If there is any good in any one of those people, it is in the rider of the red camel — if they follow him, they will be rightly guided." Yet 'Utbah was among those slain at Badr, and no good came of it.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, spoke truly, for Allah says:

> ﴿ وَمَا يَنطِقُ عَنِ الْهَوَىٰ ﴾
> "Nor does he speak out of desire." (al-Najm: 3)

Rather, it was with those who were from his children — the followers among the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family.

When the Quraysh had settled their position, they sent 'Umayr ibn Wahb al-Jumahi and said: "Assess Muhammad's companions for us." He rode his horse around the Muslim army and then returned to them and said: "About three hundred men, give or take — but let me look more carefully to see whether they have an ambush or reinforcements waiting." He swept through the valley as far as he could and found nothing, so he returned to them and said: "I found nothing, but I have seen, O assembly of Quraysh, calamities that carry their fates. Yathrib has sent you soaking death: a people who have no shield and no refuge except their swords. By Allah, I do not see that a single one of them will be killed without first killing one of you in return. If they kill among you a number equal to their own, what good is life after that?"

'Utbah ibn Rabi'ah then rose, pulled away from the Quraysh, and called on them to abandon the fighting. But Abu Jahl said: "He has swelled with cowardice, by God — his resolve has bewitched him." When he saw Muhammad and his companions, he said: "No, by God, we shall not turn back until God judges between us and Muhammad. Nothing stands before us but the sight of Muhammad and his companions — like the eaters of slaughter-camels — including his own son; perhaps you fear him." When word of 'Utbah's speech reached Abu Jahl, he replied: "He who is yellow with cowardice — that is he, not I. He will know which of us is swollen with magic." Then 'Utbah sought a helmet to place on his head but could find none to fit his large skull. Seeing this, he wrapped his head with a cloth.

Al-Aswad ibn 'Abd al-Asad al-Makhzumi came forward and declared: "I will drink from Muhammad's basin, or I will destroy it, or die without having done so." Hamza ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, may Allah be pleased with him, went out to meet him. When they clashed, Hamza struck him and severed half of his leg — cutting it clean — and he fell on his back with his leg streaming blood. He then dragged himself toward the basin and plunged into it, intending to fulfill his oath, as he claimed. Hamza, peace be upon him, followed him and struck him again in the basin until he was killed.

Then 'Utbah ibn Rabi'ah came forward, flanked by his brother Shaybah ibn Rabi'ah and his son al-Waleed ibn 'Utbah. When he stepped out from the line and called for single combat, three men came out to meet them: 'Awf and Mu'awwidh, the sons of al-Harith — their mother being 'Afra' — and another man; some say this third man was 'Abdullah ibn Rawaha. The Quraysh asked: "Who are you?" They replied: "Men of the Ansar." The Quraysh said: "We have no need of you."

A spokesman called out: "O Muhammad, send out to us men who are our equals from among our own people." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Rise, O 'Ubaydah ibn al-Harith; rise, O Hamzah; rise, O 'Ali."

When they rose and were asked who they were, they identified themselves and were recognized as worthy and honorable opponents.

'Ubaydah — the eldest of the three — faced 'Utbah ibn Rabi'ah. Hamza, peace be upon him, faced Shaybah ibn Rabi'ah. And 'Ali, peace be upon him, faced al-Waleed ibn 'Utbah. 'Ali, peace be upon him, was the youngest of the three — eighteen years old; some say he had not yet reached twenty. Al-Waleed wasted no time before 'Ali killed him, and Hamza, peace be upon him, wasted no time before he killed Shaybah. As for 'Ubaydah, he and 'Utbah exchanged two blows; each man struck the other severely, and 'Ubaydah had his leg severed. 'Ali and Hamza, peace be upon them, then turned their swords on 'Utbah and finished him, then carried 'Ubaydah back to the Muslim army.

Then the two sides advanced and engaged one another. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, commanded his companions not to charge until he gave the order, saying: "If the enemy surrounds you, drive them back with arrows."
