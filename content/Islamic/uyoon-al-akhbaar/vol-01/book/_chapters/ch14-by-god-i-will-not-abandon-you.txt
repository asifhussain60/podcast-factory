# By God, I Will Not Abandon You

By God, I Will Not Abandon You

The Messenger of Allah continued to call to Allah and to convey His message, and Allah commanded him to make that call openly:

> ﴿فَاصْدَعْ بِمَا تُؤْمَرُ وَأَعْرِضْ عَنِ الْمُشْرِكِينَ﴾ [الحجر: ٩٤]
> "So proclaim what you have been commanded and turn away from the polytheists."

He came forward and invited the Quraysh and the people of Makkah to Islam. He told them what he had been sent with, warned them of Allah's punishment, and spoke against their idols and what they attributed to them instead of Allah. His uncle Abu Talib stood up to defend him against those who sought to deny and obstruct him, and forbade the Quraysh from harming him. Yet the mission grew ever mightier upon them, for it challenged their cherished beliefs and condemned their gods.

The leaders of Quraysh gathered in resentment at what he had called them to, and in defiance against him. Those who took the lead in this, who stood up and sat down together in opposition and deliberation, were Abu Sufyan, who is Sakhr ibn Harb ibn Umayyah ibn Abd Shams ibn Abd Manaf; his kinsmen from Banu Umayyah; Utbah and Shayba, the sons of Rabi'a ibn Abd Shams; Abu al-Bakhtari, who is al-'Aas ibn Hisham; al-Walid ibn al-Mughira; al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Muttalib; al-'Aas ibn Wa'il; al-Hakam ibn Abi al-'Aas; and Abu Jahl ibn Hisham.

They gathered, deliberated, and concluded that they should go to Abu Talib and put their grievance before him. They came to him and said: "O Abu Talib, your nephew Muhammad has insulted our forefathers, denounced our gods, declared our fathers to have been misguided, and you have shielded him from us. Either you restrain him, or leave us to deal with him." Abu Talib replied to them gently and with dignity, and they departed.

The Messenger of Allah went on fulfilling his command, calling to Allah his Lord and conveying what Allah had sent him with. Many of the Quraysh accepted Islam, and this weighed heavily on Banu Umayyah and those among the Quraysh who followed them. So they came again to Abu Talib and said: "O Abu Talib, we have come to you before and thanked you for what you granted us, and you hold honor and standing among us. We have endured what your nephew has brought upon us, the cursing of our gods, the disparagement of our fathers, the dismissal of our dreams as foolishness, and now a group of Quraysh have gone over to him. Either you stop him from us, or we shall make war on him and on you and those who stand with him, until one of the two sides is destroyed." Abu Talib replied to them with a fair and measured answer and said: "I am considering what is best in this, God willing."

He then called the Messenger of Allah privately and told him what his people had resolved, that they had decided it was easier for them to strike first than to wait, and said to him: "O nephew, your people have come to me with what you have heard. Spare me and spare yourself, and do not burden me with more than I can bear."

The Messenger of Allah assumed that Abu Talib was wavering, and said:

> «يَا عَمِّ؛ إِنْ شِئْتَ فَلاَ تُكَلِّفْ نَفْسَكَ مَا لاَ تُطِيْقُهُ، فَأَمَّا أَنَا فَوَاللهِ لَوْ وَضَعُوا الشَّمْسَ فِي يَمِيْنِي، وَالقَمَرَ فِي شِمَالِي، لَمَا تَرَكْتُ هَذَا الأَمْرَ حَتَّى يُظْهِرَهُ اللهُ أَوْ أَهْلِكَ دُونَهُ»
> "O uncle, if you wish, do not be overwhelmed. But I, by Allah, even if they placed the sun on my right hand and the moon on my left, I would not abandon this matter until Allah caused it to prevail or I perished in its pursuit."

At that, Abu Talib stood firm for him, called him back, and said:

> «يَا ابْنَ أَخِي؛ اذْهَبْ فَقُلْ مَا أَحْبَبْتَ وَاصْنَعْ مَا شِئْتَ، فَوَاللهِ لاَ أَسْلَمْتُكَ لِشَيْءٍ أَبَداً»
> "O son of my brother, go and say what you love and do whatever you will, for by God, I will never abandon you for anything."

He said in this regard:

> وَاللهِ لَنْ يَصِلُوا إِلَيْكَ بِجَمْعِهِمْ * حَتَّى أُوَسَّدَ فِي التَّرَابِ دَفِيْنَا
> فَاجْهَدْ لِأَمْرِكَ مَا عَلَيْكَ غَضَاضَةٌ * وَابْشِرْ وَقَرَّ بِذَاكَ مِنْكَ غُيُونَا
> وَدَعَوْتَنِي وَزَعَمْتَ أَنَّكَ نَاصِحٌ * فَلَقَدْ صَدَقْتَ وَأَنْتَ كُنْتَ أَمِيْنَا
> وَعَرَضْتَ دِيْناً لاَ مَحَالَةَ إِنَّهُ * مِنْ خَيْرِ أَدْيَانِ الْبَرِيَّةِ دِيْنَا
> لَوْلاَ الْمَلاَمَةُ أَوْ حَذَارِي سُبَّةً * لَوَجَدْتَنِي سَمْحاً بِذَاكَ مُبِيْنَا
>
> *I swear they will not reach you while I am buried in the dirt, by gathering all their forces against you.*
> *Preach and let that not diminish you; work hard at your matter, and have no anger.*
> *You believed, and you were truthful, and you called, claiming to be a sincere adviser.*
> *The best of wilderness religions is our religion, and yet a religion has been offered that must inevitably come.*
> *Were it not for blame and my fear of reproach, I would have found myself free to follow it.*

This too is testimony from Abu Talib to the truthfulness of the Messenger of Allah, and that his religion is the finest of religions. What he endured in suppressing his own acceptance of that religion stands as the greatest proof of his conviction.

When the Quraysh learned that Abu Talib would not abandon the Messenger of Allah nor surrender him, and that they had united in their hostility toward him, they came to Abu Talib again, bringing 'Amarah ibn al-Walid ibn al-Mughira. They said: "O Abu Talib, this is 'Amarah ibn al-Walid, the finest and most vigorous young man among the Quraysh. We offer him to you: take him as your son, for he is yours. Hand over to us your nephew, who has opposed your religion and the religion of your fathers, sown division among your people, and made a mockery of their cherished beliefs, for he is but a man like any man." Abu Talib replied:

> «لَبِئْسَ وَاللهِ مَا تَسُومُونَنِي؛ أَتُعْطُونِي ابْنَكُمْ أَكْسُوهُ وَأَغْذُوهُ، وَأُعْطِيكُمُ ابْنِي لِتَقْتُلُوهُ! هَذَا وَاللهِ مَا لاَ يَكُونُ أَبَداً»
> "By Allah, what a terrible bargain you offer me, that you give me your son to clothe and feed, while I hand over my son to be killed! That, by God, will never happen."

Al-Mut'im ibn 'Adi ibn Nawfal ibn 'Abd Manaf then said to him: "Your people have been fair to you, O Abu Talib, and have gone to great lengths to spare you what you dislike. Yet I see you unwilling to accept anything from them." Abu Talib replied: «لاَ وَاللهِ مَا أَنْصَفُونِي» "By God, they have not been fair to me; rather you have all abandoned me and united against me. Do then whatever seems right to you."

Abu Talib then composed verses rousing Banu 'Abd Manaf to support him while rebuking Banu 'Abd Shams:

> أَلَا قُلْ لِعَمْرٍو وَالْوَلِيْدِ وَمُطْعِمِ
> أَلَا لَيْتَ حَظِّي مِنْ حَيَاطَتِكُمْ بَكْرُ
> مِنَ الْحُوْرِ حَبْحَابٌ كَثِيْرٌ رُغَاؤُهُ
> يَرُشُّ عَلَى السَّاقَيْنِ مِنْ بَوْلِهِ قَطْرُ
> إِذَا مَا عَلَا الْفِيْفَاءَ قِيْلَ لَهُ: وَبْرُ
> إِذَا سُئِلاَ قَالاَ: إِلَى غَيْرِنَا الْأَمْرُ
> كَمَا جُرْجِمَتْ مِنْ رَأْسٍ ذِي عَلَقٍ صَخْرُ
> بَلَى لَهُمَا أَمْرٌ وَلَكِنْ تَجَرْجَمَا
> هُمَا نَبَذَانَا مِثْلَ مَا يُنْبَذُ الْجَمْرُ
> أَخُصُّ خُصُوصاً عَبْدَ شَمْسٍ وَنَوْفَلاً
> فَقَدْ أَصْبَحَتْ مِنْهُمْ أَكُفُّهُمَا صِفْرُ
> هُمَا أَشْرَكَا فِي الأَمْرِ مَنْ لاَ أَبَا لَهُ
> مِنَ النَّاسِ إِلاَّ أَنْ يُرَسَّ لَهُ ذِكْرُ
> وَلِيْدٌ أَبُوهُ كَانَ عَبْداً لِجَدِّنَا
> إِلَى عِلْجَةٍ زَرْقَاءَ جَاشَ بِهَا الْبَحْرُ
> وَكَانُوا لَنَا مَوْلًى إِذَا ابْتُغِيَ النَّصْرُ
> وَتَيْمٌ وَمَخْزُومٌ وَزُهْرَةُ مِنْهُمُ
> وَكَانُوا كَجَفْرٍ بِئْسَ مَا صَنَعَتْ جَفْرُ
> فَقَدْ سَفُهَتْ أَحْلاَمُهُمْ وَعُقُولُهُمْ
> وَمَا مِنْهُمُ مَا كَانَ مِنْ نَسْلِنَا شَفْرُ
> فَوَاللهِ لاَ تَنْفَكُّ مِنَّا عَدَاوَةٌ
> تَخَلَّفَ خَلْفَ الْوِرْدِ لَيْسَ بِلاَحِقِ
> أَرَى أَخَوَيْنَا مِنْ أَبِيْنَا وَأُمِّنَا
> هُمَا أَغْمَرَا لِلْقَوْمِ فِي أَخَوَيْهِمَا
>
> *Do not tell 'Amr and al-Walid and al-Mut'im, that I would ever wish to be under your protection.*
> *From the poplars I love, great froth*
> *sprinkles on the legs from a drop of urine.*
> *If he stood above the heights, it was said to him: well done.*
> *If they were asked, they deferred to another.*
> *As though I were swept from the head of one who wore a desert cloak.*
> *Yes, they hold authority, yet they are dragged along.*
> *They cast us out as embers reject their own fire,*
> *most of all 'Abd Shams and their feeble ones.*
> *Some of them have amounted to nothing.*
> *They brought into the matter those of uncertain lineage.*
> *No one is mentioned among the people but that he is named,*
> *al-Walid, whose father was but a slave alone.*
> *They came to a lush meadow brought by the sea,*
> *yet they were our lords when we sought victory.*
> *And Tim, and Makhzum, and Zahrah among them,*
> *they were like cisterns I did not fill.*
> *Their dreams and reason have gone silent,*
> *and some of them were not of our line; they were bound.*
> *By God, enmity will not cease from us,*
> *lagging behind fragrant blossoms, not following after.*
> *I see our brothers from our shared mother and father*
> *as the most settled of people in their dwellings.*

This only deepened the enmity of Banu 'Abd Shams and their allies toward the Messenger of Allah. As for what came from the Banu Hashim and Banu 'Abd al-Muttalib, their hearts turned fully to supporting the Messenger of Allah, with none among them holding back except Abu Lahab. Banu al-Muttalib and Banu Hashim had from ancient times been as one. Abu Talib said to them:

> حَتَّى مَتَى نَحْنُ عَلَى فَتْرَةٍ * يَا هَاشِماً وَالقَوْمُ فِي جَحْفَلِ
> مِنَّا لَدَى الْخَوْفِ وَفِي مَعْزِلِ
> يَدْعُونَ بِالْخَيْلِ عَلَى رِقْبَةِ
> سَرْعَاتُهَا فِي سَبْسَبٍ مَجْهَلِ
> كَالْحَرَّةِ السَّوْدَاءِ تَغْلُو بِهَا
> مِثْلُ الْقَطَا السَّارِبِ لِلْمَنْهَلِ
> عَلَيْهِمُ الرَّكْضُ عَلَى رَعْلَةِ
> بِكُلِّ مِقْصَالٍ عَلَى مُسْبِلِ
> يَا قَوْمُ ذُودُوا عَنْ جَمَاهِيْرِكُمْ
> مَآرِثُ الأَفْضَلِ لِلأَفْضَلِ
> حَدِيْدِ خَمْسٍ لَهِزٌ حَدُّهُ
> يُصَانُ بِالتَّذْلِيْقِ فِي مِحْدَلِ
> كَمْ قَدْ شَهِدْتُ الْحَرْبَ فِي فِئَةٍ
> عِنْدَ الْوَغَى فِي عِثْيَرِ الْقَسْطَلِ
> وَفِي هِيَاجِ الْحَرْبِ كَالأَشْبُلِ
> لاَ مُتَنَحِّيْنَ إِذَا حِنْتَهُمْ
> عَرِيْضٍ سِتٍّ لَهَبٌ حُصْرُهُ
>
> *How long must we, O Hashim, remain while the people stand in one party*
> *and we in fear and isolation?*
> *They call their horses by the neck,*
> *speeds unknown,*
> *like the black one you exaggerate,*
> *like the cat that covers the opening.*
> *They must kick against the rock*
> *with every sharp blade upon a sable.*
> *O people, protect your masses,*
> *the finest inheritance from the finest.*
> *Iron, five-fold, to shake its limit,*
> *protected by the swift in the cradle.*
> *How many times has war been witnessed in it,*
> *at the time of rebellion in the wastelands,*
> *and in the fury of war like a cub;*
> *do not bow if they are rebuked.*
> *Wide, six flames.*
