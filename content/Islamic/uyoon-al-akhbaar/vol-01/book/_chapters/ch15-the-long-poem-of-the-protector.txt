# The Long Poem of the Protector

When the sons of Hashim and the sons of al-Muttalib rallied around Abu Talib, his confidence in his own position grew, and his resolve in supporting the Messenger of Allah was renewed. He set himself openly against Banu Umayya and Banu 'Abd Shams, and against those bellies of Quraysh who had joined them in enmity, and he declared his stand to them in verse:

> *Flashing like lightning,*
> *beware the whistle and the signal.*
> *We have protected the Prophet, the messenger of the King.*
> *Strike and melt without plundering,*
> *the protection of a tender uncle.*
> *I throw and shield the messenger of the King.*
> *The wolf of the herd, beware the phoenix.*
> *And though he be courteous to his enemies,*
> *the lion too visits the narrow pass.*
> *But I shall raise them high.*

And he said also:

> *following from among Banu Dhu Habb.*
> *I swear I have not abandoned the Prophet, nor*
> *when things grew heated and fierce.*
> *'Ali and Ja'far are my trust,*
> *summit and belonging to Takab.*
> *I see them ready to meet the encounter,*
> *my brother, son of my mother, among them, and my father.*
> *Do not let them down, and support your cousin.*

When Abu Talib saw in his people what pleased him, and found them steadfast at his side, he began to praise them, to recall their ancestry, and to speak of the virtue of the Messenger of Allah and of his standing among them, so that their resolve might harden and they might unite upon his cause. He said:

> *The slave of Manaf is its secret and its core.*
> *If the Quraysh gather one day in pride,*
> *in Hashim lies its honor and its antiquity.*
> *And if you take the honors of every tribe,*
> *he is the chosen one of her secrets and generosity.*
> *And if you depart one day, then Muhammad:*
> *over us she did not triumph, and swept away her illusions.*
> *Quraysh have squandered their wealth and their fullness.*
> *If they bend the stiff necks of the borders, we shall set them right,*
> *for we were not ones to accept darkness.*
> *And we protect its protectors every day like a crane,*
> *and we strike with our stones those who hurl at it.*
> *With our shoulders it calls and its throws grow,*
> *we have revived the oudh of the people.*

Then al-Walid ibn al-Mughirah gathered a group of Quraysh about him. He was one of their elders, and the season of pilgrimage had come. He said to them: "O assembly of Quraysh, the season has come, and the Arab delegations will arrive before you. They have heard of this matter concerning your companion, so agree upon one position, and do not differ, lest you give the lie to one another and your words contradict each other." They said: "O Abu 'Abd Shams, speak, and set out for us an opinion we may voice." He said: "No, you speak first, and I will listen to what you say." They said: "We shall say he is a soothsayer." He said: "By Allah, he is no soothsayer. We have seen soothsayers, and what he says bears no resemblance to their murmuring or their rhymed manner." They said: "We shall say he is mad." He said: "We have seen madness and know it well. What he does has nothing of madness's interference or its whispering." They said: "We shall say he is a poet." He said: "He is no poet. We have known poetry in all its kinds, its rajaz and its hazaj, its measured verse and its extended verse, and what he brings is not poetry." They said: "We shall say he is a sorcerer." He said: "He is no sorcerer. We have seen sorcerers and their sorcery, and what he does has nothing of their breath or their knotted cords." They said: "Then what shall we say, O Abu 'Abd Shams?" He replied: "By Allah, his speech has a sweetness in it, and you can say nothing about it whose falsehood will not be recognized. The nearest thing to say is this: he is a sorcerer who has come with a magic that divides a man from his wife, and a man from his brother and his clan."

So they dispersed upon that word, and they sat along the roads of the people and said it to them.

Concerning al-Walid ibn al-Mughirah, Allah, may He be exalted, revealed:

> ﴿ذَرۡنِي وَمَنۡ خَلَقۡتُ وَحِيدٗا ۝ وَجَعَلۡتُ لَهُۥ مَالٗا مَّمۡدُودٗا ۝ وَبَنِينَ شُهُودٗا ۝ وَمَهَّدتُّ لَهُۥ تَمۡهِيدٗا ۝ ثُمَّ يَطۡمَعُ أَنۡ أَزِيدَ ۝ كَلَّآ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ لِأٓيَٰتِنَا عَنِيدٗا ۝ سَأُرۡهِقُهُۥ صَعُودٗا ۝ إِنَّهُۥ فَكَّرَ وَقَدَّرَ ۝ فَقُتِلَ كَيۡفَ قَدَّرَ ۝ ثُمَّ قُتِلَ كَيۡفَ قَدَّرَ ۝ ثُمَّ نَظَرَ ۝ ثُمَّ عَبَسَ وَبَسَرَ ۝ ثُمَّ أَدۡبَرَ وَٱسۡتَكۡبَرَ ۝ فَقَالَ إِنۡ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا سِحۡرٞ يُؤۡثَرُ ۝ إِنۡ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا قَوۡلُ ٱلۡبَشَرِ ۝ سَأُصۡلِيهِ سَقَرَ ۝ وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا سَقَرُ ۝ لَا تُبۡقِي وَلَا تَذَرُ﴾ [Al-Muddaththir: 11–29]

"Leave Me with the one I created alone, and gave him extended wealth, and sons present with him, and smoothed the way before him; then he covets that I should add more. No indeed, he has been obstinate toward Our signs. I shall burden him with a steep ascent. He reflected and measured; so may he be slain, how he measured; again, may he be slain, how he measured. Then he looked, then he frowned and scowled, then he turned away and grew proud, and said: This is nothing but sorcery handed down, this is nothing but the word of a mortal. I shall roast him in Saqar. And what will make you know what Saqar is? It neither spares nor leaves." So the verses run to their close.

When Abu Talib feared the mass of the Arabs, he composed the poem in which he took refuge in the sanctuary of Mecca and in his own standing within it, and in which he drew near to the nobles of his people, while informing them and others in his verse that he would not surrender the Messenger of Allah, nor abandon him for anything ever, until he perished before him. Abu Talib said:

> *Mukhaysa, between Sudais and Basil,*
> *tattooed, or shortness in the sinews.*
> *Listening to truth or to falsehood,*
> *my dear friend, I do not lend my ear to the former.*
> *And there is no plunder when matters pile like hills.*
> *My dear friend, opinion is of no use in company.*
>
> *They have severed all bonds and means,*
> *and when I saw the people, I misliked them,*
> *they obeyed the enemy's command to cast us down.*
> *They opened upon us enmity and harm,*
> *biting in anger behind our backs with their fingers.*
> *They made an alliance, or so I think,*
> *and white fury from the contractor's heritage.*
> *I bore them patiently, my soul, a patient beauty,*
> *and I held fast to their garments with the reins.*
> *I brought my house and my shelter,*
> *I have where every supererogatory act is spent.*
> *Standing together and receiving its rejection*
> *with floods from Isaf and Na'il.*
> *And where the Ash'arites are their passengers,*
> *with their necks knotted like a snail.*
> *You see the trust within it, and the marble and its adornment,*
> *upon us with evil, or seasoned with falsehood.*
> *I seek refuge with the Lord of people from every stabber,*
> *and from whoever is beguiled in religion unless we are tried.*
> *And from whoever is a man who seeks us with harm,*
> *a man who would rejoice in Hira' as he descends.*
> *And by Him who established a thunderbolt in its place,*
> *by God, God is not heedless.*
> *And in the House of God, from the belly of Mecca,*
> *if they are satisfied with the sacrifice and the sacred rites,*
> *and with the Black Stone when they wipe it,*
> *on his feet, barefoot, without sandals.*
> *And the footprints of Ibrahim in the open ground,*
> *and the image and its likeness within them.*
> *And runs between the track to al-Safa,*
> *and from all who depart and from every traveler,*
> *and from the pilgrims to the House of God from every journeyer,*
> *except the free ones, unshackled.*
> *And in al-Masjid al-Aqsa when they consecrate it.*
>
> *They lift the bodies of the dead with their hands*
> *and halt them on the ropes at evening,*
> *and the night of the gathering, and the houses of Mina;*
> *is there any sanctity or house above it?*
>
> *If relatives were near, he would reward them,*
> *swiftly, as they emerge from a torrent.*
> *They pray that she will be burdened with the loads,*
> *and with the great redness when they hold fast to it.*
> *They are allied with Hajjaj, Bakr ibn Wa'il,*
> *and Kinda, when they camp in the gravel on the eve,*
>
> *in answer to him, the emotions of the means,*
> *and their resolve gathers every gain.*
> *Samar cut them down with the blade of the antelope,*
> *peacefully, and the ostriches unite.*
> *They are like him, surrounding Basal and his hair.*
>
> *Is there anyone who fears God rightly?*
> *Is there anyone after this who is a refuge for the seeker?*
> *We block the doors of Turk and Kabul;*
> *the enemies submit to us, and if they do not,*
> *we shall be safe, except by your command in Bulbul.*
>
> *You lie, by the House of God, we do not abandon Mecca,*
> *and when we obey him and fight.*
> *You lie, by the House of God, in disgracing Muhammad,*
> *and you marvel at our children and our women.*
>
> *Hand it over until we insist in circling it,*
> *the rise of generations under the same clay,*
> *from the stabbing, the act of the partisan.*
>
> *And by the life of God, I truly see*
> *in the palm of a boy like a shooting star,*
> *from the lineage of the two branches of Luay ibn Ghalib;*
> *do not let our bonds be severed.*
>
> *My brother is Thiqa, the guardian of truth, the steadfast,*
> *warding off calamity when the idle man goes without.*
> *Upon us comes an argument, after he met;*
> *he surrounds Dhamar without withholding provision.*
>
> *Two allies who bound the covenant they pledged;*
> *and a people clad in iron will rise against you.*
> *Until you see this grudge mounting as a deterrent,*
> *months, days, and years held sacred.*
>
> *And what remains of a people who hold you, master, in no regard?*
>
> *The protection of orphans is a shield for widows,*
> *and white clouds watering from his face.*
> *They are in mercy and virtue for him;*
> *destruction takes refuge in him from the House of Hashim,*
>
> *for one another, and our reward for all.*
> *By my life, I have run through hardship, and tomorrow*
> *brings an abiding punishment that does not delay the urgent.*
> *May God have mercy on us, as a master and as Khalid.*
>
> *But they obeyed the command of those tribes*
> *and did not watch us with an article to their name.*
> *They obeyed any, and the son of a slave will aid them,*
> *and everyone who took a pledge did not bear it,*
>
> *as we have received, from seven and beyond.*
> *We give them a measure for a measure of the vessel.*
> *If they are cast, or God is able to do so,*
> *let us be among the people of flock and camel.*
>
> *And as for Abu Amr, he is my father, but he hates us;*
> *Abu Amr came to us, then became an uncle.*
> *Yes, we may yet hear a voice other than one of welcome,*
> *from the ground between the trees, and he argued.*
>
> *We have a grievance like that of the wronged.*
> *The path of Abu al-Walid: what did you love in us?*
>
> *And his mercy is upon us, and I am not ignorant;*
> *I know a woman who lives by his opinion,*
> *an envious liar who hates the deceitful.*
>
> *Threshold, do not hear us utter a blatant word,*
> *so live gently, cousin, not bereft.*
> *And I do not care about him for his own sake.*
>
> *You will part from us and receive one of the nightingales,*
> *and I fear that if they do not increase their grazing,*
> *it will be as was said of the bones of the contracted.*
>
> *Abu Sufyan passed me with a grievance*
> *and claims that I am not unaware of you;*
> *he flees to a place where the water is cold.*
>
> *As the enemy is both right and wrong,*
> *and Uthman did not grieve for us, and he was negligent.*
> *We are greeted every evening and morning,*
> *and he swears by the Merciful that, as soon as he gives us life,*
> *each of us is constrained by him;*
> *and I know that he remains unaware through the evening.*
>
> *Whether upon us, and the wind brings rain,*
> *compassionate, yet concealing what lies within,*
> *nor are most affairs easily resolved.*
>
> *First dispute among litigants;*
> *and when I eat, I shall not be foolish.*
> *May God reward us, Abd Shams and Tofla,*
> *with punishment of evil sooner rather than later.*
> *He has a witness from within himself who provides no sustenance.*
>
> *Bani Khalaf, the leaders of the structure and al-Ghaital,*
> *and the family of Qusay in the first engagements,*
> *and the pride of the lineage from Ghalib and al-Kahl;*
> *they swore nothing but sparks among the tribes,*
> *so they did not harm you, and no blood was shed.*
>
> *Bu Hamah, Ubayd Fa'is ibn Aqil,*
> *Bani Ummah, gathered, Hind Kiya.*
> *We must shed all idleness and sloth,*
> *and an arrow, a piercing arrow, twisted and swift.*
>
> *Understanding where we stand in every small matter,*
> *and Shayez was among Luay ibn Ghalib,*
> *and the hardship is barefoot, from stomach to sandals;*
> *so do not bring into your affairs every passerby.*
>
> *"So you are the best of your people,*
> *be as Wa'il's gatherings were.*
> *I fear that God will not set your affairs right,*
> *and you have gone astray with a misguided command."*
>
> *By my life, you have grown weak and helpless.*
> *Hattab is more capable and more resolute,*
> *and you were a woodcutter when he completed the task,*
> *and he let us down and left us in the strongholds.*
>
> *Why do the sons of Abd Manaf defy us?*
> *Lean on all of you, if your inclination holds;*
> *and the counsel of advisors tells us:*
> *a provision I have never withheld from you for a single day,*
> *and not a day of shortage, if I brought you a child.*
>
> *I sense that the people have a plan,*
> *weighed with a premium that observes no ritual.*
> *The dreams of a people have changed,*
> *and we are the core of Hashim's wolf.*
>
> *We had a watering basin among them,*
> *and he repelled the evil of one who trod upon the pebbles.*
>
> *Dragging our elders through public forums,*
> *by God, if I were not alive, bound by a covenant,*
> *from a time long past, beyond mere mockery,*
> *but we sold it nonetheless.*
>
> *We have, and I do not mean to speak falsely,*
> *they knew that our word is no lie;*
> *they will make her fruitful only through a family.*
> *If a people keep secret what you have done,*
>
> *then proclaim it to Qusay after us, with vigor,*
> *so he told Qusay to spread our matter.*
> *If we do not take refuge in their words at the entrances,*
> *or I struck at night, Qusay, a great blow.*
>
> *But we grieved for the women left childless,*
> *and if they trusted the strike within their homes,*
> *then it must be so in ignorance,*
> *the heel of many peoples;*
> *one day it must be removed.*
>
> *And if you are a remnant of Luay, gathered,*
> *they slaughtered us with ranges and terrors.*
> *And we were sound before the darkening of the mat;*
> *we found it, in our time, useless folly.*
>
> *Every friend and nephew we count*
> *is absolved from the disabled and the deceitful,*
> *except one who is among the dogs of Ibn Murrah;*
> *if he does not speak truth, he speaks falsely.*
>
> *Bani Asad, do not be extremist in the dust;*
> *Zuhayr Hasama is one of the bearers.*
> *The uncle of the son of Ahat, the people do not lie,*
> *in the reckoning of Houmat al-Muhad, is foremost.*
>
> *I sense something stronger than the scent of the Bahalil belonging,*
> *and contain it, the devoted lover who persists.*
> *By my life, I have been committed to Ahmad,*
> *and we are, despite the enemy, like ropes.*
>
> *So beauty still abides in the world for its people,*
> *if rulers measured it when distinguishing;*
> *and who among the people is like him, for any hopeful soul?*
> *He is loyal to her, and nothing about him is strange.*
>
> *Forbearing, rightly guided, just, not reckless,*
> *and Dina showed his right without it being withheld;*
> *so the Lord of all servants supported him with His victory.*
>
> *To glory, honorable fathers of the gifted,*
> *a youth from al-Muteen and Hashim,*
> *who rebukes every seller and the ignorant,*
> *like the bright gleam of swords in the hands of the refined.*
>
> *Black predator over the mustard herd;*
> *by striking, you see the boys within it as though*
> *the people rise among them when they are transgressed,*
> *and we found the descendants of the noble men.*
>
> *It rises and rises within a few nights.*
> *The people of the grudge will come to know which is which;*
> *he finds, when the time comes to yield,*
> *who among me and them stands with his sword.*
>
> *And he praises the horizons with the saying:*
> *"And who is weary of war, from me and from them?"*
> *The long poem is drawn shorter from it,*
> *so Ahmad came among us in honor.*
>
> *And he was defended with shield and clay;*
> *I stood with him and protected him,*
> *and what harm is upon him in this world and on the Day of Solidarity?*
> *There is no doubt that God has elevated His command.*
>
> *His father saw them as no worse than they were,*
> *just as yesterday and today his grandfather saw,*
> *honorable men other than their inclination.*
>
> *We halted them until they scattered.*

This word of Abu Talib is like the word of the believer of Pharaoh's household, who concealed his faith. It suffices to recall what Abu Talib said and did, his struggle on behalf of the Messenger of Allah, his protection of him, his fighting in his defense, and his testimony to his prophethood and his truthfulness. This came from the signs that had become clear to him in the Prophet, and the virtues he had come to know in him.

Had Abu Talib openly declared his Islam, he would have found no supporter among his people, and he would have become merely one of those who accepted Islam while their people cast them off. But God, mighty and majestic, supported His Messenger through him, and established His religion by means of him at the first appearance of the religion and the massing of the disbelievers, out of God's regard for His religion and His aid to His Messenger.

This poetry of Abu Talib spread among the Arabs, and with it the affair of the Messenger of Allah, and the standing of Banu Hashim in his defense, and the standing of Banu 'Abd Shams and those allied with them against him. Those Arabs who had been incited against the Messenger of Allah held back; some of them inclined toward him, and most of them gave the lie to what was said about him.

The people of Yathrib recalled what the Jews had been telling them of the time of the appearance of a prophet among them, and of the nearness of the sending of a messenger, and their eyes turned toward him and their expectations inclined his way. The polytheists of Quraysh called upon those who had promised them help against the Messenger of Allah, but they held back from them and magnified the matter of the Haram, for the poetry of Abu Talib had reminded them of its sanctity. Some of them sent messages admonishing them and forbidding them from war with their own people. So what was in their hands fell away, since their plot did not prevail and their design was not completed.
