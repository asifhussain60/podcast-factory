# Khuza'ah Calls for Help

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, remained after the army had been sent to Mu'tah throughout Jumada al-Akhirah and Rajab.

Then the matter arose of nullifying the treaty between him and the Quraysh, because Banu Bakr ibn 'Abd Manat ibn Kinana had attacked Khuza'a.

Khuza'ah had entered into a treaty with the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and his covenant, and Banu Bakr had entered the era of the Quraysh and their covenant.

There had been old feuds and fighting between them from the pre-Islamic era, but Islam had held them in check. They were bound by the truce of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family.

When Banu al-Dail of Banu Bakr seized upon the truce to attack Khuza'a and sought to take revenge against them — Khuza'ah being encamped at al-Watir, a watering place of theirs — they fell upon them, wounding some of their men. Fighting broke out and men were killed. The Quraysh supplied Banu Bakr with weapons, and some of the Quraysh even joined the fighting secretly under cover of night, until one party took a position inside the Sacred Precinct.

Lufl ibn Mu'awiya al-Dili, of Banu Bakr, said: "We have entered the Haram — your God, your God!" He then replied: "There is no god for Him today, O Banu Bakr — take your revenge! For by my life, do you not steal even within the Haram? Will you not take your revenge there?"

So Khuza'ah took refuge in the house of Badil ibn Warqa', and in the house of their master, whose name was said to be Rafi', after the Quraysh and Banu Bakr had killed their men.

When Banu Bakr and the Quraysh moved against Khuza'a and wounded some of them, breaking the covenant between themselves and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and violating the covenant that gave Khuza'ah his protection and placed them under his treaty, 'Amr ibn Salim al-Khuza'i set out until he reached the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, in Medina, while he was sitting in his mosque among the Emigrants and the Ansar, and he said:

> *O Lord, I call upon Muhammad*
> *by the oath that is bound and firm.*
> *You were the young ones and we were the elders,*
> *then we submitted, and she did not withdraw her hand.*
> *So God granted you victory —*
> *let the servants of God come to our aid.*
> *The Messenger of Allah has been stripped bare by them;*
> *if he is left weak, his face will be brought low.*
> *Come with a host like the surging, foaming sea.*
> *The Quraysh have betrayed you at the appointed time.*
>
> *They have revoked your firmly confirmed covenant*
> *and left me in a state of bewilderment.*
> *They claimed that I call upon no one —*
> *they are humiliated and few in number.*
> *They seek to make us falter,*
> *and they have killed us while we were kneeling and prostrating.*
> *So grant us victory — may God guide you with a decisive, conquering victory.*

He said: "O 'Amr ibn Salim, you have been answered."

Then, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, a cloud appeared in the sky, and he said: "This cloud heralds the victory of Bani Ka'b."

Then Badil ibn Warqa' set out with a group of Khuza'a until they reached the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, in Medina. They told him what had been done to them and the attack that the Quraysh and Banu Bakr had launched against them. Then they turned back to Mecca, and the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, said to those around him: "It is as if Abu Sufyan has come to you to tighten the covenant and extend its term."

Badil ibn Warqa' and his companions went on until they encountered Abu Sufyan ibn Harb in 'Asfan, for the Quraysh had sent him to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, to reinforce the treaty, extend its term, and reassure those who had broken it.

Abu Sufyan came until he reached the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, in Medina. He entered upon his daughter, Umm Habiba bint Abi Sufyan, wife of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him. When he moved to sit on the bed of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, she folded it away from beneath him. He said: "O daughter, I do not know whether you found me unworthy of this bed, or whether you found the bed unworthy of me." She said: "It is the bed of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, and you are a polytheist and impure. I did not wish you to sit on the bed of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him." He said: "By Allah, evil has come upon you, O daughter, since leaving me."

Umm Habiba bint Abi Sufyan had emigrated to Abyssinia with her husband, who died there. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, then sent word to the Negus to betroth her to him, and she thereafter presented herself before the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family. She became one of his wives.

When Abu Sufyan went out, he came to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, and spoke to him, but he received no reply.

So he came to Abu Bakr and asked him to speak to him on his behalf. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "I shall not do so."

Then 'Umar ibn al-Khattab came, and Abu Sufyan appealed to him in the same way. 'Umar answered: "We cannot approach the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, with what he dislikes."

He went out to 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, peace be upon him, who was with Fatima, daughter of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, and al-Hasan ibn 'Ali was there as a small boy. He said: "O 'Ali, have mercy on me yesterday's guest, for I am in need. Let me not return as I came, empty-handed. Intercede for me with the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family."

He said, rubbing his hands: "O Abu Sufyan! By Allah, the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, has resolved upon something, and nothing we could say will change his mind."

He turned to Fatima, peace be upon her, and said: "O daughter of Muhammad, can you command your son" — meaning al-Hasan, peace be upon him — "to extend his protection to the people, so that he becomes a master of the Arabs until the end of time?"

She said: "By Allah, my son has not yet reached the point of extending protection over the people, and no one can intercede against the decision of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family."

He said: "O Abu al-Hasan, I see that matters have grown difficult for me — give me your counsel."

He said: "By Allah, I know of nothing that will serve you — yet you are the master of Banu Kinana. So stand among the people and extend your protection, and then make your way back to your land."

He said: "Do you think that this will avail me anything?"

He said: "No, by Allah, I do not think so — but I can find nothing else for you."

Abu Sufyan stood up in the mosque among the people and said: "O people, I hereby extend my protection among all people." Then he mounted his camel and set off. When he arrived in Mecca to his people, they asked: "What news do you bring?" He said: "Muhammad spoke not a word to me, and by Allah, he gave me no answer. Then I went to Ibn al-Khattab and found him the most hostile of enemies. Then I came to 'Ali and found him the most gentle of the people, and he pointed me toward something I did — though I do not know whether it will avail anything or not." They said: "And what did he order you?" He said: "He ordered me to extend my protection among the people, and I did so." They then said: "Woe to you! By God, he has done nothing but play with your mind." He said: "By Allah, I found nothing better than that."

Then the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, informed the people that he was setting out for Mecca. He ordered them to prepare and make ready, and he said: "O Allah, take from the eyes and news of the Quraysh and those with them, until they proclaimed it in their own country." So the people were prepared.

Hassan ibn Thabit spoke up, inciting the people and recalling the wounded men of Khuza'ah:

> *I have not seen the men of Bani Ka'b in the presence of Mecca,*
> *in the hands of men who did not draw their swords,*
> *and many people did not wear their armour.*
> *If only my hair could win you my victory?*
> *Suhail ibn 'Umar and her punishment —*
> *this is the time of war; he drew out its sinews.*
> *And Safwan — we long for the edge of his blade.*
> *If it is purely milked and I shave its fangs,*
> *then do not trust us, son of Umm Mahalid.*
> *She has a fall in death that opens her door,*
> *and do not fear us — for our swords are ready.*

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, assembled his forces to march on Mecca, Hatib ibn Abi Balta'ah wrote a letter to the Quraysh informing them of what the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, had resolved to do — namely, to march against them. He entrusted the letter to a woman — said to be a freed slave of some of the Banu 'Abd al-Muttalib — on the condition that she deliver it to the Quraysh. She concealed it in her hair, twisting her locks around it, and set out.

Then news of what Hatib had done came to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, from heaven. He sent 'Ali ibn Abi Talib and al-Zubayr ibn al-'Awwam, saying: "Go — you will find a woman who has with her a letter that Hatib ibn Abi Balta'ah wrote to the Quraysh, warning them of what we are gathering against them."

They set out and overtook her at a place known as the grove of Bani Abi Ahmad. They dismounted her from her mount, searched her baggage, and found nothing. Then 'Ali, peace be upon him, said to her: "By Allah, I have not lied and I have not been misled — bring out this letter, or we will strip your clothing." When she saw the resolve in him, she said, "I will show it." She turned away, loosened her braids, and drew out the letter from within them. She handed it over, and it was brought to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family.

He summoned Hatib and said, "What drove you to do this?"

Hatib said: "O Messenger of Allah, I am a believer in Allah and His Messenger — I have not changed nor wavered. But I was a man among the Quraysh who had no family or clan to protect his people and children there, so I sought to earn their favour through this."

'Umar ibn al-Khattab said: "O Messenger of Allah, let me strike his neck — he has committed treason." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, said: "And what do you know, O 'Umar? Perhaps Allah has already looked upon the people of Badr and said: 'Do as you wish, for I have forgiven you.'"

Then Allah revealed concerning Hatib: "O you who believe, do not take My enemy and your enemy as allies, offering them affection, when they have disbelieved in what has come to you of the truth, driving out the Messenger and yourselves because you believe in Allah, your Lord — if you have gone forth to strive in My cause and to seek My pleasure. You confide to them affection, while I know best what you have concealed and what you have revealed" — up to the words of the Almighty: "You have had a good example in Abraham and those with him, when they said to their people: 'We are innocent of you and of what you worship besides Allah. We disbelieve in you, and there has appeared between us and you enmity and hatred forever, until you believe in Allah alone.'" (al-Mumtahanah: 1–4)

And so on to the end of the passage.

Then the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, set out to conquer Mecca when ten nights of Ramadan had passed.
