# Freedom, Generosity, and the Division of Estates

When the heirs of a slain man are of differing condition, some present and some absent, some grown and some still children, the right of retribution belongs to those who have reached maturity. The killer is not held back until the young ones come of age, nor until those who are absent return. So it was that al-Hasan ibn Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him and his family, put to death the enemy of Allah, Ibn Muljam, may Allah curse him, the man who struck Ali, peace be upon him, although Ali's own children had not yet reached the age of puberty.

As for one who is slain, whether by an iron weapon or by anything else: if the killer intended the death of his victim and struck him deliberately with whatever he struck him, and the victim died of that blow, then the guardians of the slain may demand retaliation. Yet retaliation is carried out by no means other than the sword, for the Messenger of Allah, may the peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, said: "There is no execution except by the sword" (لَا قَوَدَ إِلَّا بِحَدِيدَةٍ). The condemned is to be struck upon the neck with the sword; he is neither to be tortured nor to be mutilated.

It is permitted to question the one accused of the killing and to draw out his confession gently, without striking him. If he is beaten and confesses under that duress, his confession does not bind him, for the admission of one compelled has no legal force. But if two upright witnesses testify that he committed the killing, or if he confesses of his own free will, then the killing is established against him. The testimony of women is not admitted in cases of bloodshed, nor in any of the prescribed punishments (hudud) or in retaliation (qisas), though it is admitted in matters where wealth is owed.

If a single man is slain by two, or by a company who joined together against him, each of them striking him until he died of their blows, then the guardians of the slain may choose one of them and put him to death for their kinsman, for Allah Almighty has said: *"A soul for a soul"* (النَّفْسَ بِالنَّفْسِ). They may not put more than one man to death in return for one. Thereafter the guardians of the slain turn to the remaining killers and take from each of them blood money in proportion to his share. If they were three and one was put to death, his guardians take from the remaining two two-thirds of the blood money. If they were two and one was put to death, his guardians take half the blood money from the other. If they were more than this, the shares are reckoned accordingly, and the rest are made to bear a punishment as well.

If one man restrains another while a third kills him, then the one who killed is put to death, and the one who held the victim is imprisoned until he dies, after he has been flogged. If a man kills his own slave, he is given a painful punishment. If the guardians pardon the killer, there is no claim against him, and whoever kills him after that pardon is himself put to death in return. Likewise, if they accept blood money from him, they are entitled to nothing beyond it, for Allah Almighty has said: *"And whoever transgresses after that, for him is a painful punishment"* (فَمَنِ اعْتَدَى بَعْدَ ذَلِكَ فَلَهُ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ).

Every wound for which retaliation can be reached without danger of excess and without harm to the one from whom it is taken, such as the surface wound (al-mudihah) and what lies below it, is subject to retaliation. The wound is measured along its length upon the skin, a mark is set, and the one taking retaliation cuts in the corresponding place to the like of it, and to the like of its depth in the flesh.

So too a tooth is drawn for a tooth, an eye put out for an eye, a nose severed for a nose, an ear for an ear, or whatever portion of them has been cut away, in accordance with the word of Allah Almighty: *"And the eye for the eye, and the nose for the nose, and the ear for the ear, and the tooth for the tooth, and for wounds is retaliation"* (وَالْعَيْنَ بِالْعَيْنِ وَالْأَنْفَ بِالْأَنْفِ وَالْأُذُنَ بِالْأُذُنِ وَالسِّنَّ بِالسِّنِّ وَالْجُرُوحَ قِصَاصٌ). This is when the act was deliberate. But if the wound was inflicted in error, or if its nature is such that retaliation would risk further harm to the offender, or if the wound has dried and healed, or if a bone has been broken, or if it is a wound greater than the surface wound, then in all of these blood money is owed, and we shall mention it in its proper place. Whoever inflicts such wounds deliberately is also punished, lest a man of means find it an easy thing to do and simply discharge the blood money for it.

Retaliation is taken from a woman for a man in wounds, and from a man for a woman in the surface wound and what is below it. As for retaliation in the eye, the eye that is not to suffer retaliation is bound, an iron or a mirror is heated, and it is brought near to the eye from which retaliation is to be taken; the eye is held open until it flows. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them all.

## On Blood Money and Its Categories

The blood money for a free Muslim man, whether young or old, is one thousand dinars upon those who deal in gold, or its equivalent value upon the people of every other kind of wealth. They are not compelled to sell what they possess in order to pay in gold; rather the equivalent of one thousand dinars is taken from them in the currency of their land: from the people of silver, the value of one thousand dinars in it; from the people of camels, one hundred camels; from the people of cattle, two hundred cows; from the people of sheep, one thousand sheep; and from the people of cloth, two hundred garments of the like value. By this the Sunnah has run, and this is the blood money for the soul.

Blood money is owed only in the case of error, which is that the killer did not intend the death of the one he killed, as when he meant to cast at another and struck this man instead. But if he acted deliberately with whatever he used, that is intentional killing, and he is subject to retaliation, unless the guardians of the slain accept blood money from him or pardon him. The one who killed in error must, after paying the blood money, expiate by freeing a believing slave; if he cannot find one, then by fasting two consecutive months; and if he is unable to do that, then by feeding sixty poor persons.

The blood money is borne by his 'aqila, who are the heirs that would inherit from him were he to die, save his maternal aunts, for they neither bear this responsibility nor inherit anything of the blood money. The deliberate acts of children and of the insane are treated as error, and the blood money for them falls upon the 'aqila, but nothing is laid upon the poor among them, nor upon one who possesses nothing. The 'aqila is not bound except by what is established through clear evidence; but as for one in good standing who confesses and acts upon it, and as for what involves a slave, the 'aqila bears nothing of it. The 'aqila does not bear unintentional offenses except where the amount reaches a third of the blood money or more; whatever is less than a third is paid from the offender's own wealth. As for the people of the dhimma, what they incur is from their own wealth, and their 'aqila bears nothing of it; whoever offends alone bears it alone.

Whoever strikes a woman who has been with her husband so that she casts forth the seed from the womb after it had settled there owes twenty dinars. If she casts forth a clot, which is the blood ('alaqah), there are forty dinars in it. For the morsel of flesh (mudghah), once it has become flesh, there are sixty dinars. If bone has formed within it, there are eighty dinars. If she casts it forth as a fully formed child before the spirit has been breathed into it, there are one hundred dinars in it; and once the spirit has been breathed into it, the full blood money is due.

Whoever offends against one already dead, severing his head or doing to him what, were it done to him alive, would have killed him, owes one hundred dinars; and for whatever wound or other injury is done to him, the offender owes the reckoned share of his blood money. That sum is spent on his behalf in the works of piety, and the Hajj is performed for him if he had not yet performed it, and his heirs inherit nothing of it. As for offenses against beasts, they are reckoned by their price: whoever kills a beast is fined its value, and whoever wounds it or breaks a limb of it owes the amount by which its value has fallen. If it breaks loose and causes harm, the harm is without redress; and likewise if one enters upon it in its place. But if its owners loose it or station it where they have no right, they are answerable for whatever harm it causes.

When a thief seeks a man's wealth and fights him, and the man so threatened kills him, the thief's blood is forfeit. Whoever is slain in defense of his wealth is a martyr; and if he yields his wealth and does not fight for it, there is no blame upon him. Whoever seeks to overcome a woman against herself, and she repels him and kills or wounds him, whatever she inflicts upon him is without redress. Whoever dies under a prescribed punishment (hadd) or under lawful retaliation, there is no claim for him. Whoever peers into the private quarters of a people and they put out his eye, his claim is forfeit.

The Messenger of Allah, may the peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, ruled by al-Qasamah, which applies when a man is killed and his killer is not known. If, before he dies, he says, "So-and-so killed me," or if his guardians come forward bearing a mark of accusation against the one they charge with his death, saying "He killed him," then fifty men of them swear oaths that he killed him, and so the accused is given over to them. If they do not number fifty, those who are present repeat their oaths until they complete fifty oaths. If they wish, they may have the accused swear oaths instead. And if the accused bring proof of their innocence, it is accepted from them.

When a slain man is found in a village and it is not known who killed him, and its people are the people of that one village with no others mixing among them, they swear that they did not kill him and do not know his killer, and then they are made to pay his blood money. If he is found between two villages, the responsibility falls upon the nearer of them to the place where he lay. But if the place in which he was struck down is a great city, or a village in which all manner of people mix, or upon a road along which travelers pass between villages, then the burden of proof falls upon whoever is accused among them, if no mark of accusation is found against them, and al-Qasamah is established.

If two horsemen collide, each having driven toward the other, and one of them dies, then half the blood money is due, for he died of his own act together with the act of his fellow. If the act came from one of them while the other was standing still or walking, and the one who charged died, then nothing is owed by the one who stood still; but if the one who was struck died, his blood money is upon the one who charged, if he did not intend it, and if he did intend it, retaliation is upon him.

The physician and the veterinarian are answerable for what they bring about if they were ignorant of their craft; and if they were knowledgeable yet intended harm, they are answerable likewise. The circumciser is answerable for whatever he cuts of the boy's flesh beyond the place of cutting, and the woman who performs the cutting upon girls is the same when she exceeds the place of the cut. Whoever digs a well or a pit in a road, or places something in it, when the road is a public thoroughfare, is answerable for whatever harm is suffered there; but if the road lies within his own right, there is nothing upon him. A rider is answerable for whatever his beast strikes with its foreleg or its chest, but not for what it strikes with its hind leg, since that is not within his control. When a wall leans and its owner comes to know of it, or it is brought to his notice, yet he neither sets it right nor pulls it down until it falls, he is answerable for whatever it strikes. When a man hires a laborer who then perishes at his work, nothing is owed for him. When a dog is kept in the dwelling of a people or within their right, and someone enters upon it without their leave and it bites him, there is nothing upon them; but if they gave him leave to enter, they are answerable.

When a woman severs a man's organ, or a man severs a woman's part, there is no retaliation between them, for there is in this no like of the offense to take from the offender; rather the blood money in full is upon him, and he is severely punished if he acted deliberately. When a man falls upon a woman whose like is not fit to be approached, or uses force upon a woman and tears her, so that, brought to such a state, she does not bear children, or loses control of her water or her bowels, or dies of his violence against her, then in all of this the full blood money is upon him. Whoever is found dead bearing no mark, and it is not known that anyone killed him, nothing is owed for him save the oath of the one accused of it. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them.

## On Offenses Against the Limbs and Wounds of the Head

We have mentioned above that retaliation is taken for the deliberate act in every case where retaliation can be reached and excess in it is guarded against, and where no harm is feared for the one from whom it is taken. But as for that which cannot be brought to true retaliation, and where the safety of the offender cannot be assured, the blood money for it is upon the offender if the act was deliberate, and upon the 'aqila if it was in error.

For every part of which a person has but one, the full blood money is owed; and for every part of which there are two, half the blood money is owed for each. For the hair of the head, if it is plucked, or falls out from injury, or is burned and does not grow back, the full blood money is owed; and likewise for the skin of the head if it is flayed. If the forehead is struck and the bone set again, there are one hundred dinars in it. If the nose or the tongue is severed, or an offense is committed against the beard so that it does not grow back, in each of these the full blood money is owed. For the eyebrow, the eye, the ear, the hand, the foot, and one of the two organs of generation, half the blood money is owed for each one of them, and for both together the full blood money. For the eye of a one-eyed man, the full blood money. For hearing, if it is lost, the full blood money; for speech, if it is lost, the full blood money; and for sight, the full blood money, even if the pupils themselves do not perish. For whatever is diminished of all of these, it is reckoned by its share.

If a man strikes another or offends against him so that some of the sight of one of his eyes is lost, the sound eye is bound and the other left, and an egg is taken, emptied of what is within it, and hung by a hair. The injured man is told to look at it, and it is drawn away from him until he says, "I no longer see the hair," and that place is marked. He is measured from where he does not know its measuring, and this is then done from four directions; and if the measurements agree, his truthfulness is known. Then his sound eye is bound and the injured eye left, and the same is done to him from four directions; and if his account agrees, well and good, but if not, he is told, "You have lied; return to the truth." If his account holds true and is consistent upon the measuring from four directions, one looks to what the injured eye has lost of its seeing as against the sound eye, and he is given of the blood money the like of what was lost: if it is a half, then a half; if a third, then a third; whether less or more, it is reckoned accordingly.

If what was lost of his sight is from both his eyes together, he is measured in the same way against the seeing of an upright man of middling sight, and of two and three such men. When they agree upon it, or come near to agreement, his own sight is measured against it from the four directions, as we have described; and when the measurements are even and his truthfulness is known, he is given of the blood money according to what was lost, as we have set forth. If the loss is in the hearing, the like of this is done to him: a dirham is struck near him until he says, "I do not hear it," and he is measured as we have described for the two eyes, for the one ear and for both ears. If it is in the sense of smell, something bearing a scent is brought near to him and then withdrawn in the same way, by the reckoning of what is done for sight and hearing, and he is given according to his share. And if the loss is in speech, one looks to what has fallen away from him of the letters, against the full count of the letters,

As for the man whose speech has been injured, the letters he can no longer pronounce are counted against the full alphabet, which numbers twenty-eight letters, and blood money is owed to him in proportion to what he has lost of speech. For the lips, half the blood money is due for the upper lip, and two-thirds of the blood money for the lower lip, that is, a half and a sixth, reckoned from its share in eating and in the softness of speech. For the teeth set at the front of the mouth, which are four incisors, four lateral incisors, and four canines, each tooth carries fifty dinars. For the teeth at the back of the mouth, which are the molars, each carries twenty-five dinars, and together they complete the full blood money. The molars number four in each jaw, four above and four below, which is the complete count of the teeth in the perfection of natural creation. Some people have five molars in each jaw, the additional one being superfluous; the blood money is apportioned according to the perfect original count, and whatever is lost of them, whether superfluous or not, carries twenty-five dinars. If part of a tooth is broken, blood money is owed in proportion to what is broken. If a tooth turns black, its full blood money is due, for it has been ruined. For a child's tooth knocked out before its natural time to fall, ten dinars are owed, and five dinars for his molar, if it grows back afterward; but if it does not grow back, the full blood money for it is due.

For the shoulder, the upper arm, and the elbow, if any one of them is broken or dislocated and then heals without defect, one hundred dinars are owed for each. For the forearm and the wrist, one-third of the blood money for each. For every finger of the hands and the feet, one hundred dinars. For the shoulder blade, forty dinars. For the ribs joined to the chest, twenty-five dinars for each rib; and for the ribs that are not joined to it and do not meet the breastbone, ten dinars for each rib. For the hip, the thigh, and the knee, one-fifth of the blood money for each. For the jā'ifa, which is a blow that penetrates into the inner cavity of the body, one-third of the blood money. For a blow to the face that leaves it red without drawing blood, and without turning black or green, two and a half dinars; and if it turns black or green, three dinars. If the blow is around the eye and turns green, three dinars; and if it turns black, six dinars.

For the dāmiya, the wound in which blood flows, ten dinars. For the fāqira, which breaks the skin and reaches the flesh without cutting it, twelve dinars. For the bādi'a, which cuts the skin and enters the flesh, severing some of it, twenty dinars. For the mutalāhima, which cuts deep into the flesh, thirty dinars. For the mishāq, also called the milṭāt, which cuts through the skin and all the flesh and reaches the membrane over the bone without cutting it, forty dinars. For the mūdiha, which cuts through all of that and lays the bone bare, fifty dinars. For the hāshima, which does all of that and breaks the bone, one hundred dinars. For the munaqqila, which breaks the bone and draws out a fragment of it, one hundred and fifty dinars. For the ma'mūma, in which the membrane of the brain becomes visible and is reached without further injury beyond it, one-third of the blood money. All of this applies to wounds of the head and the face. For wounds to the rest of the body, half of these amounts apply. As for an injury to one of the limbs, such as the fingers and their like, blood money is owed in proportion to its share of the limb's full blood money, as we have already mentioned. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the blessings of Allah be upon them all.

## On the Establishment of the Hudud

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: "And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah has wronged himself." (Surah al-Talaq, 65:1)

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, forbade the neglect of the prescribed limits, and said: "The Children of Israel perished only because, when a man of noble standing among them committed an offense that incurred a limit, they would not carry it out against him, but when a lowly man committed it, they would establish it upon him." A woman of Quraysh stole a garment in the time of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, and he ordered her hand to be cut off. A group of Quraysh gathered to him and said: "O Messenger of Allah, she is a woman of ours who holds honor among her people, and what she has stolen is a trifle." The Messenger of Allah said: "It was by the like of this that those before you were destroyed: they would establish the limit upon their weak and leave their strong and their nobles, and so they perished."

He, may Allah bless him and his family, forbade intercession in the prescribed limits, and said: "Whoever intercedes to nullify one of the limits of Allah has been insolent toward Allah and has striven to undo His limits; and whoever is insolent toward Allah and strives to undo His limits, Allah, Mighty and Majestic, will punish him on the Day of Resurrection." Safwan ibn Umayyah brought to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, a man who had stolen a cloak of his, and the Messenger of Allah ordered the man's hand to be cut off. Safwan said: "O Messenger of Allah, is his hand to be cut off for the sake of a cloak? I have given it to him." The Messenger of Allah said to him: "Why was that not before you brought him to me? Once the limit has been raised to the ruler, it is not fitting for him to leave it unenforced." And he ordered it, and the hand was cut off.

It is related from him, may Allah bless him and his family, that he said: "The back of the believer is inviolable, except for a limit that is due." He forbade that anyone exceed a limit from among the limits of Allah, whether by falling short of it or by going beyond what has been prescribed, and he said: "Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has made the limits clear, so whoever transgresses them has wronged himself, as He, exalted be His remembrance, has said." And he set a limit upon whoever transgresses a limit. He, may Allah bless him and his family, said: "The most hateful of creation to Allah is one who bares the back of a Muslim without right." He forbade the carrying out of the prescribed limits inside the mosques. It is related from him that he said: "Whoever commits a sin and is punished for it in this world, Allah is more just than to repeat the punishment upon His servant in the Hereafter; and whoever commits a sin and Allah conceals it for him in this world, He is more generous than to return to something He has already pardoned and concealed."

Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, wrote to Rifa'a ibn Shaddad, his judge over al-Ahwaz, a letter in which he said: "Establish the limits upon the near, that the distant may shun them; do not let blood be shed in vain, and do not let the limits be neglected." When Uthman was brought al-Waleed ibn Utbah, and witnesses testified against him, and the offense he had committed against the Muslims was made plain, the Muslims spoke of it and said that the limits of Allah had been neglected. So al-Waleed was placed in a room at the side of the house, and it was said: "Let whoever holds that the limit is due upon him rise and carry it out." Yet whenever a man came in to flog him, al-Waleed would say: "I adjure you by Allah not to sever my kinship, and not to expose me before the Commander of the Faithful," and the man would turn back from him. When Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, saw that a limit from among the limits of Allah was being neglected, he took up the whip and went in to him, and al-Waleed said the like to him. Al-Husayn ibn Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon them both, followed him in and said: "O my father, what is between you and him? Leave him be; you see what the people have made of his case." But Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, pushed against the chest of al-Husayn, peace be upon him, and spoke sharply to him so that he withdrew, and he took the whip and flogged al-Waleed until the limit was carried out in full.

It is related from him, peace be upon him, that he said: "Three things, if you do them, no affliction will descend upon you: that you wage struggle against your enemy; that you raise your limits to your imams and let them judge in them with justice; and that you submit to their command." Umar said to him: "Counsel me, O Abu al-Hasan." He said: "I counsel you to judge by the Book of Allah in contentment and in anger, to apportion with justice between the red and the black, and to establish the limits upon the distant and the near alike." Umar said to him: "You have been brief and you have reached the heart of it."

He, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, used to review the prisons every Friday, and whoever was found to have a limit due against him, he would carry it out and not delay it. Three men once testified before him against a man for adultery, and he asked: "Where is the fourth?" but the fourth was not present. They said: "We will bring him after this, O Commander of the Faithful." He said: "There is no waiting even an hour in the limits," and he flogged the three with the limit for false accusation. A people came to him and said: "O Commander of the Faithful, we have come to ask you for a need." He said: "Ask me for nothing that I possess but that I will give it to you." They had come to ask him concerning a man of Banu Asad upon whom a limit had become due, and they said: "So-and-so, grant him to us." But he brought the man out and carried out the limit upon him. They said: "Did you not promise us, O Commander of the Faithful, to release him?" He said: "I promised you what I possess; but this belongs to Allah, and I do not possess it." He, peace be upon him, ordered his servant Qanbar to flog a man the prescribed limit, and Qanbar, in his anger, gave him three lashes more. When this was raised to him, Ali, peace be upon him, granted the man retaliation against Qanbar, and flogged Qanbar three lashes. And he, peace be upon him, wrote to Rifa'a: "Shield the believer as much as you are able, for his back is a sanctuary of Allah, and his soul is honored before Allah, and to him belongs the reward of Allah."

It is related from him, peace be upon him, that he said concerning a people who held out in the territory of the enemy and asked to be given a covenant that they would not be called to account for anything that had passed: "Do not grant a covenant that would set aside a limit from among the limits of Allah. How can the limits be lifted from those to whom they apply, when it is the friends of Allah and those who strive in His path who go forth to establish them?" It is related from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, that he said: "Pardon nothing of the limits that belong to Allah, Mighty and Majestic. Whoever comes to know of one of them, nothing is fitting for him but to raise it to the Imam. As for the rights of people within the limits, there is no harm in pardoning them before the matter reaches the ruler, as with the thief before he is brought to the Imam; but it is not permissible to pardon after it has reached him. And whoever pardons a limit that is his and then wishes to enforce it has no right to do so." It is related from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, that he said: "Whoever seizes a thief and pardons him, there is no harm in it. But once the thief is raised to the Sultan, let his hand be cut off. If the one from whom he stole says, 'I grant him to him,' the limit is not averted from him, for the pardon would have held before the raising; but once it is raised to the ruler, the limit is not neglected, by the word of Allah, Mighty and Majestic: 'and those who keep the limits of Allah.' Once the limit has been raised to the Imam, it is not for anyone to leave it."

## On the Hadd Punishment for Zina

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: "And do not approach adultery; indeed it is an indecency and an evil way." (Surah al-Isra, 17:32) And He said: "And those who do not call upon another god alongside Allah, nor kill the soul that Allah has forbidden except in truth, nor commit adultery; and whoever does that will meet a penalty. The punishment will be multiplied for him on the Day of Resurrection, and he will abide in it humiliated, except for whoever repents." (Surah al-Furqan, 25:68-70)

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, said: "The wrath of Allah grows severe against a woman who brings into a people one who is not of them, so that he gazes upon what is sacred to them and treads upon their beds. And the most severely punished of people on the Day of Resurrection is the one who plants his seed in a womb forbidden to him." He, may Allah bless him and his family, also said: "The adulterer will stand on the Day of Resurrection above the people of the Fire, and a drop will fall from his private part by which the people of Hell will be tormented, and they will say to the keepers, 'What is this foul stench?' and it will be said to them, 'This is the smell of an adulterer.' And an adulteress will be brought, and a drop will fall from her, and the people of the Fire will be tormented by her stench." He, may Allah bless him and his family, said: "There is no sin greater before Allah, Mighty and Majestic, after associating partners with Him, than unlawful seed that a man places in a womb not lawful to him." And he said: "Adultery and goodness do not gather in a single house." And he delivered a sermon, may Allah bless him and his family, in which he said: "Three whom Allah will not speak to on the Day of Resurrection, nor purify, and for whom there is a painful punishment: an adulterous old man, a tyrant king, and a proud pauper." And he said: "The adulterer does not commit adultery while he is a believer."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, said: "When he lies upon her belly, the spirit of faith departs from him; and when he rises and seeks the forgiveness of Allah, it returns to him." Adultery is the placing of the private part in one who is neither a spouse nor lawfully possessed by a man's right hand. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: "And those who guard their private parts, except from their spouses or those whom their right hands possess, for then they are not to be blamed; but whoever seeks beyond that, those are the transgressors." (Surah al-Mu'minun, 23:5-7)

When an adulterer is found, and four men of just standing testify against him that they saw him commit adultery with a woman, having seen the organ enter her private part, while he had a wife in his lawful bond with whom he had consummated marriage and the woman had a husband in her lawful bond; or when the man and the woman both confess; or when one of them confesses and the testimony of witnesses is established against the other, then each of them, whether the one who confessed or the one against whom the proof was given, receives one hundred lashes. They are then taken out, and a pit is dug for each, reaching to the navel, and each is set into it standing and packed in. If the matter was a confession, the judge before whom the confession was made begins, and orders the stoning; then he orders the people, and they stone with stones of moderate size, such as fill the palm or less, until they die. They are then brought out, washed, prayed over, and buried.

The confession of an adulterer is not established until he confesses on four separate occasions, while of sound mind, willing, and not coerced. If the matter is the testimony of witnesses, the witnesses cast the first stones, then the judge, then the people. A man is not counted as protected until he has consummated marriage with his wife and she is with him; a bondwoman does not render a free man protected, nor a bondman a free woman. If one of the two is protected and the other is not, the protected one is stoned after being flogged, and the one who is not protected is flogged one hundred lashes. The most severe flogging is the flogging of the adulterer, then the flogging of the false accuser, then the flogging of the drinker, then the flogging of discretionary punishment. A pregnant woman is not flogged until she has delivered what is in her womb and is purified of her postnatal bleeding. There is no limit upon a child, neither boy nor girl; but if an adult commits this sin with one of them, the limit is established upon the adult.

# Freedom, Generosity, and the Division of Estates

When a single child is claimed by more than one man, the elder of the claimants takes precedence, and whoever withheld the child from one of them bears the responsibility for what was done. As for the act of the people of Lot, the one who performs the act upon another is stoned just as the two who commit adultery are stoned. If the act is intercrural, the hadd falls due upon them both. For the act committed between women, each of the two receives a hundred lashes.

Whoever commits adultery with a relative whom it is unlawful for him to marry is put to death, and she too is put to death if she consented to him, provided they are unmarried. If they are protected by marriage, the hadd is applied and they are stoned. Whoever forces a woman against her will is put to death if he is unmarried, and is flogged the hadd and stoned if he is protected by marriage. No hadd falls upon the woman who was coerced, and if he has taken her virginity she is owed a dowry equal to that of her peers. If she is a slave girl, she is owed a tenth of her price; if she is a non-virgin, half a tenth of her price.

There is no hadd upon one who has lost his reason, and no hadd in an unlawful union that bears the outward form of a marriage, such as a man who marries a woman during her waiting period, or upon a contract that does not meet the requirements of a valid marriage. If two men are found together under a single cover, or a man and a woman, the hadd is applied. If two women are found together in like manner, they are flogged with a punishment short of the hadd of stoning. If a slave or a slave girl commits adultery, each of them receives fifty lashes, whether or not they are protected by marriage.

When the adulterer is brought forward to be flogged, witnesses from among the believers are commanded to attend the punishment, as Allah Almighty says:

> ﴿وَلْيَشْهَدْ عَذَابَهُمَا طَائِفَةٌ مِّنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ﴾
>
> "And let a group of the believers witness their punishment." (al-Nur 2)

The adulterer is flogged in the condition in which he is found. If he is naked, he is left naked; if he is clothed, he is left in his clothing and struck over it. Nothing that would shield him from the effect of the blows, such as a thick fur or the like, is left upon him. The woman is seated and kept covered with a garment, and every limb of her is struck except the face, the private parts, and the vital organs. If striking the back alone is what is practiced, that is permissible, and it is what is customarily done. The blows shall not be such as to kill, and neither a woman in her post-natal bleeding nor a menstruating woman is struck until she is purified.

If the adulterer and the adulteress confess to adultery and then retract, they are left alone until they have confessed four times; but if they retract after that, their retraction is not accepted. Whoever commits adultery with his wife's mother receives the hadd. If a man has intercourse with a slave woman in whom he holds a share, he is flogged in proportion to the share that is not his, and the hadd is averted from him in proportion to his own share. Likewise, if she is his slave woman contracted to purchase her freedom, he is struck in proportion to the part of her that has been freed, and the hadd is averted from him in proportion to the part still owned.

Whoever among men imitates women in his manner is disciplined; and if he persists in that, he is banished from the dwellings. Whoever commits an act with an animal is punished for it, and the price of the beast is paid to its owner. If the beast is of a kind that is eaten, it is slaughtered and buried, and it is not lawful to eat it. If it is not of a kind that is eaten, it is sold and removed to where it will not be recognized, and the one who did this is reproached for it. A slave under a contract of manumission is flogged with the hadd of a free man in proportion to the part of him that has been freed, and with the hadd of a slave in proportion to the part still in bondage. All of this is the unanimous teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the blessings of Allah be upon them all.

### The Hadd Punishment for Slander

Allah Almighty said:

> ﴿إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَرْمُونَ الْمُحْصَنَاتِ الْغَافِلَاتِ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ لُعِنُوا فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ﴾
>
> "Indeed, those who accuse chaste, heedless, believing women are cursed in this world and the Hereafter, and for them is a great punishment." (al-Nur 23)

And He said:

> ﴿وَالَّذِينَ يَرْمُونَ الْمُحْصَنَاتِ ثُمَّ لَمْ يَأْتُوا بِأَرْبَعَةِ شُهَدَاءَ فَاجْلِدُوهُمْ ثَمَانِينَ جَلْدَةً وَلَا تَقْبَلُوا لَهُمْ شَهَادَةً أَبَدًا وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْفَاسِقُونَ ۝ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ تَابُوا مِن بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ وَأَصْلَحُوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ﴾
>
> "And those who accuse chaste women and then do not produce four witnesses, flog them with eighty lashes and never accept their testimony thereafter; it is they who are the defiantly disobedient, except those who repent after that and make amends, for indeed Allah is Forgiving and Merciful." (al-Nur 4 to 5)

It is narrated from Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, that he said: "The major sins are associating partners with Allah Almighty, killing a believer while under covenant, fleeing from the battle line except as one wheeling to fight or withdrawing to join his own troop, consuming usury after the proof of its prohibition has been established, devouring the wealth of the orphan unjustly, reverting to the life of the desert after the Emigration, and accusing chaste, heedless, believing women."

It is narrated from Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, that he said: "Whoever falsely accuses a believing man or a believing woman of something that is not in them, Allah Almighty casts him into the clay of corruption until he comes forth from what he said." He was asked about a man who slanders a chaste Muslim woman, and he said: "The hadd is established against him once it is proven against him, and his repentance is that he declare himself a liar before the people and turn back from what he did. If he does this, his testimony is accepted thereafter."

The slanderer is flogged eighty lashes. If he slanders a group and they come against him together, he receives a single hadd for all of them; but if they come one after another, he receives a hadd for each of them. If the one slandered, whether a man or a woman, confesses to what was said of them, or the evidence of it is established against them as Allah Almighty has indicated, then there is no hadd upon the slanderer. Whoever slanders a polytheist incurs no hadd, unless the polytheist has a Muslim child, in which case the hadd is applied for the sanctity of Islam. It does not befit a Muslim to slander a polytheist nor anyone else.

If a free man slanders a slave, he is disciplined but is not given the full hadd. If a slave slanders a free man, the full hadd is applied. If a dhimmi slanders a Muslim, the hadd is applied and he is made an example. If a man slanders another man and that man slanders him in return, and they bring the matter before the authority, both are given a discretionary punishment. Whoever says to his wife, "I did not find you a virgin," incurs no hadd.

Whoever has incurred one hadd and is then falsely accused of another, the one who slandered him is flogged the hadd. Whoever acknowledges a child and then denies him is flogged the hadd, the child's lineage is fixed upon him, and there is no hadd for mere insinuation. Whoever insults the Prophet, may the peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, is put to death. Whoever insults any of the Imams, may Allah's prayers be upon them, is likewise put to death. Whoever slanders in any tongue has the hadd imposed upon him. Whoever slanders another and then pardons the one he accused before raising the matter to the authority, his pardon is valid, and he no longer retains the right to pursue it; but if he has already raised the matter to the authority and petitioned against him, no pardon is accepted thereafter.

If someone slanders a child or one who has lost his reason, nothing is due from them, though the child is disciplined so that he will not grow bold in slander. If a man or a woman slanders their own child, there is no hadd upon them; but if the child slanders them, the hadd is applied for their sake. If a man says to another, "O sodomite," there is no hadd upon him, because he has only ascribed him to Lot, peace be upon him; but if he says, "O you who do the work of the people of Lot," then the hadd becomes obligatory upon him. If a man slanders an absent or a deceased woman, her guardian may rise against him on her behalf, and the hadd is for him.

Whoever denies a man his lineage incurs the hadd. Whoever reviles a man or a woman is given a discretionary punishment on their behalf, as when he says, "O immoral one," "O malicious one," "son of a Christian," "son of a Zoroastrian," and the like from cursing and reviling. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may Allah's prayers be upon them.

### The Punishment for Intoxicants and Wine, and Related Rulings of the Hudud

Allah Almighty said:

> ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنَّمَا الْخَمْرُ وَالْمَيْسِرُ وَالْأَنْصَابُ وَالْأَزْلَامُ رِجْسٌ مِّنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَانِ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ﴾
>
> "O you who believe, wine, gambling, idols, and divining arrows are only a defilement from the work of Satan, so avoid it, that you may be successful." (al-Ma'idah 90)

The Messenger of Allah, may the peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, said: "May Allah curse wine, the one who presses it, the one who has it pressed, its seller, its buyer, the one who pours it, the one who consumes its price, its drinker, the one who carries it, and the one to whom it is carried." And he said: "There is no one who drinks wine in this world and then drinks it in the Enclosure of Holiness." Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, said: "One addicted to wine meets Allah Almighty, when he meets Him, as one who worships an idol." And whoever drinks a single draught of it, Allah does not accept a prayer from him for forty nights. The Messenger of Allah, may the peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, said: "Every intoxicant is forbidden, and whatever intoxicates in a large quantity, a small quantity of it is forbidden as well."

So whoever drinks a little or a great deal of wine has the hadd applied to him. If he confesses to drinking it a second time, he is struck again; and if he drinks it a third time, he is put to death, though he is not put to death until the hadd has been administered upon him twice. Whoever drinks an intoxicant and becomes so intoxicated that he does not know his own state is flogged the hadd of eighty lashes. If he returns to it after being struck once, he is struck again; and if he returns after being struck twice, he is put to death on the third occasion. If he drinks of it but does not become intoxicated, he is given a discretionary punishment; and if he returns to it, he is punished with a painful chastisement. Whoever drinks wine or an intoxicant not knowing that it is forbidden incurs no hadd. Whoever acknowledges drinking wine and then denies it is still given the hadd, and no regard is paid to his denial. The hadd for a slave in the matter of wine is eighty lashes, the same as the hadd for a free man. If the people of the dhimma openly drink wine or an intoxicant, they are struck just as the Muslims are struck, for they were given their covenant only on the condition that they not openly display anything forbidden in the religion of Islam.

If a doubt arises concerning a hadd, it is suspended. There is no surety in a hadd, no testimony built upon testimony, no oath, and no judge's letter to another judge taken as a basis. No hadd is carried out on the strength of intimidation, nor on a confession extracted under duress, and for that purpose a man is neither imprisoned nor beaten. If several hadd punishments fall due upon a single person, all of them are carried out; and if death is among them, those short of death are carried out first, then he is put to death. A man may not carry out a hadd upon his own slave except by the permission of the authority. If a hadd becomes due upon someone and its measure is not specified, he is beaten until the one striking him desists. Whoever is testified against for a hadd is held until the evidence is duly established; and once it is established and the hadd is applied, he is not held in detention.

### The Rulings Concerning Thieves and Brigands

Allah Almighty said:

> ﴿وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا أَمْوَالَكُم بَيْنَكُم بِالْبَاطِلِ﴾
>
> "And do not consume one another's wealth among yourselves through falsehood." (al-Baqarah 188)

And He said:

> ﴿وَالسَّارِقُ وَالسَّارِقَةُ فَاقْطَعُوا أَيْدِيَهُمَا﴾
>
> "As for the thief, male and female, cut off their hands." (al-Ma'idah 38)

The Messenger of Allah, may the peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, said: "I have seen in the Fire the man with the crooked staff who used to steal from the pilgrims with it." And he said: "The thief does not steal while he is a believer."

Whoever enters the house of a people without their permission and steals from their wealth, or takes out from their secured place goods worth five dinars, and acknowledges this or has evidence established against him, has his right hand cut off at the joint of the four fingers of the palm, while the thumb and the palm are left to him for his use. If he steals a second time, his left foot is cut off at the middle of the foot, while the heel and what adjoins it are left to him so that he may walk upon it. If he steals after that, he is imprisoned for life; and if he steals while in prison, he is put to death. When the cutting is carried out, the place of amputation is cauterized with fire to hasten its healing and to make him safe from perishing. If the one performing the cutting errs and cuts off the left hand or the right foot, that suffices, and nothing else is cut from him. Whatever of the stolen goods is found still in his possession is returned to its owners, and he is held liable for whatever he has destroyed of them. After he is cut, he is banished to a land other than the one in which the cutting was carried out.

The guest is not subject to cutting if he steals from among those who hosted him, nor is the hired man if he steals from the house of the one who hired him and admitted him into his home, nor is one who entered with the permission of the household. There is no cutting for snatching, nor for open robbery declared within the town, such as one who seizes a thing and overpowers its owner openly in the town. The hand is not cut off for the one who openly grabs and flees, nor for the pickpocket; rather, both are made an example.

But if this is done upon the road, the one who does it is a brigand. When such a one is brought before the Imam, the ruler has discretion concerning him: if he wishes, he may put him to death; if he wishes, he may crucify him and leave him alive until he dies; if he wishes, he may cut off his hand and his foot from opposite sides; and if he wishes, he may banish him from one land to another. This is done with him when he has terrorized the road and waylaid the Muslims. If he repents before he is seized, he is pardoned. Whoever fights such an aggressor in defense of himself and kills or wounds him bears no liability for that. If the brigand is seized and wealth is found with him, it is returned to its owners if they are known; and if its owners are not known and he confesses and it is witnessed against him that this is among what he plundered, it is placed in the treasury of the Muslims until one who is entitled to it comes forward.

Whoever steals something in which he holds a share is not subject to cutting. There is no cutting upon one who bores a hole or opens a door but takes nothing out. There is no cutting for any stone other than a precious gem, nor for fruit when it is stolen, nor for the pith of the palm, nor for a bird of any kind, nor for what is stolen from places open to all, such as mosques, inns, bathhouses, open courts, and every place permitted for people to enter without leave, unless the stolen goods are taken out from a secured place within it. Whoever steals sheep from the open pasture is not subject to cutting. Whoever steals repeatedly and cannot be apprehended, then is finally apprehended, has his hand cut off and is held liable for all that he stole. Whoever is found with stolen property in his possession, but it is neither proven that he stole it nor acknowledged by him, is not subject to cutting, though the property is taken from him and returned to the one who rightfully owns it.

A madman who steals is not subject to cutting, nor is a boy who has not reached puberty, nor a minor; though the child is disciplined so that he will not grow bold in stealing. The grave robber has his hand cut off when he removes the shroud from the grave, for the grave is a secured place. If one whose hand has been cut dies from the cutting, there is no liability for it. The thief ought to be admonished after the cutting; for it is narrated that Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, cut the hands of a group who had stolen, then brought fire, then fed them and gave them drink until they were restored, then clothed them, and commanded them to take up their severed hands in their sound hands.

Whatever the case, the matter was carried further. He said to them, "Raise your hands toward the sky and say: O Allah, Ali has cut off our hands according to Your Book and the Sunnah of Your Messenger." When they had said it, he answered, "Yes, O Lord, upon Your Book and the Sunnah of Your Messenger." Then he said to them, "These are your hands. They have gone ahead of you to the Fire. Whoever among you repents, his hand will drag him to Paradise, and whoever does not repent, it will drag him to Hell."

There are cases in which no hand is cut at all. If a slave steals the property of his master, his hand is not cut off, but if he steals from someone other than his master, it is. If a father steals from his son's property, or a child from his father's, or a wife from her husband's, or a husband from his wife's, or a sister from her brother's, or a brother from his sister's, there is no cutting in any of these. And if a thief steals in a year of famine and hardship, his hand is not to be cut. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, may Allah's prayers be upon them.

**Mention of the rulings on apostates and innovators**

Allah the Almighty said: "How shall Allah guide a people who disbelieved after their faith?" And He said: "Whoever of you turns back from his religion and dies while he is a disbeliever, those are the ones whose deeds have become worthless in this world and the Hereafter." And He said: "It has been revealed to you and to those before you: if you associate partners with Allah, your deeds will surely become worthless, and you will surely be among the losers."

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him and his family, said: "It is not permissible to kill a believing man except for one of three things: disbelief after faith, fornication after the establishment of marriage, or the taking of a life for no life." And he said: "Every innovation is misguidance, and every misguidance is in the Fire." And he said: "Whoever changes his religion, kill him."

So whoever was born in Islam and then departed from it to any of the religions of disbelief is killed and is not invited to repent, except that if the apostate is a woman, she is imprisoned until she repents. As for the heretic who does not believe in the resurrection and the reckoning and who denies the prophethood of the Prophets, or one who held a religion other than Islam and then submitted and afterward reverted, Islam and repentance are presented to him over three days. If he repents, he is left alone. If he persists and remains upon what he is, he is killed on the fourth day and is not invited to repent at that point. And if he repents of his own accord, he is left; if he does not repent, he is killed. A slave is likewise killed if his case is such. As for a slave woman who apostatizes, the bounds upon her are tightened: she is put to the harshest service, she is not clothed except with what covers her nakedness, and she is not fed except with what keeps her alive, until she repents or dies. She is not killed, so that she is not destroyed for her family's loss. The same applies to the mother of a child.

When the apostate apostatizes, whatever property he leaves behind passes as inheritance to his heirs, for his apostasy is treated as his death. His wife observes her waiting period and may marry again if she wishes. His children remain Muslims. Whichever of the two parents submits to Islam, his minor children, and any born to him after that, are Muslims by virtue of that parent's Islam. If they grow up and refuse Islam, they are in the standing of apostates, and what is done to apostates is done to them.

Whoever works sorcery against the Muslims has disbelieved, for Allah, exalted is He, says concerning the two angels: "And they taught no one until they said: We are only a trial, so do not disbelieve." And He says: "And they followed what the devils recited during the reign of Solomon, and Solomon did not disbelieve, but the devils disbelieved." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, said: "The sorcerer among the Muslims is killed, but the sorcerer among the polytheists is not killed." It was asked, "O Messenger of Allah, and why is that?" He said, "Because the shirk he is upon is greater than the sorcery." Sorcery and shirk are thus bound together upon one tether. And whoever embraces Islam and then partakes of anything forbidden in Islam is beaten, and if it is something for which a prescribed penalty is due, the penalty is carried out upon him. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, may Allah's prayers be upon them.

**Mention of usurpation and transgression**

Allah, the most truthful of those who speak, said: "And do not transgress; indeed, Allah does not love those who transgress." And He said: "And do not consume your wealth among yourselves through falsehood." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, said: "The sanctity of your wealth among you is like the sanctity of this sacred day, in this sacred month, in this sacred land."

So whoever comes into unlawful wealth is obligated to repent and to free himself of it. There is no repentance for him except that he rid himself of it and return it to its owners if he knows them. If he does not know them, he places it in the treasury of the Muslims until the one entitled to it comes forward. If he finds no way to that, he gives it in charity, and if its owners later make themselves known to him, he makes restitution to them. It is not permissible for him to keep it with himself nor to benefit from it.

And whoever has been transgressed against, or had something usurped, or had something stolen from him, must not himself transgress, nor usurp, nor steal from the one who did this to him, even if he is able, because usurpation, betrayal, theft, and transgression are all forbidden. It does not befit one to whom a forbidden act has been done that he do the like of it to the one who did it to him. Rather, he must keep faith with his trust and must not betray nor steal. Al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him, said: "Render up your trusts, and do not betray the one who betrayed you."

Whoever usurps a slave woman and she bears him a child must return her to the one from whom he usurped her, and his child by her belongs as a slave to the one from whom he took her. If he usurped her and then sold her to a man, and the buyer, knowing that she was usurped, fathered a child by her, she is returned to the one from whom she was seized, and the value of the child is paid by him, and he then has recourse against the usurping seller for her price and for the value of the child he gave up. Whoever usurps a slave, a beast, or anything whatever, and it perishes in his possession, owes its value or its equivalent. If he usurped it and it increased in his hand, the increase belongs to its owner from whom it was usurped. If it diminished, the usurper is liable for what diminished from it. If he hired it out, the hire belongs to its owner. If what he seized was sheep or other animals and they bred, then they and their offspring belong to the one from whom they were usurped. And whoever assaults something belonging to the instruments of frivolity and breaks it, or pours out wine or any intoxicant, bears no liability for that. And whoever has wealth stolen or usurped from him and then comes upon the very thing itself may take it back, but it is not lawful for him to take some substitute in its place.

**On loans and deposits**

Allah, mighty and majestic, said: "And do not forget generosity among yourselves." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, said: "A loan of use is an act of affection." And Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him, said: "The loan, the lending of an article, and the hosting of the guest are obligations within the Sunnah." And Ali, peace be upon him, said: "Whoever guards the property of an orphan, Allah, mighty and majestic, makes Paradise obligatory for him, just as He has made the Fire obligatory for the one who devours it." And just as sin falls upon the one who betrays what was entrusted to him, so the reward belongs to the one who is given something in trust and renders his trust faithfully.

Whoever borrows an article and stands surety for it to the one who lent it, and it perishes or is lost with him, is liable to make it good, whether or not he was at fault in it. If he did not stand surety for it, but did wrong to it, or transgressed against it, or wasted it, he is liable for it. But if it perished or was damaged with no wrongdoing on his part and no wasting of it, there is no liability upon him, and he is held trustworthy if he is a man of trust. And if he borrows dinars or dirhams and they perish
