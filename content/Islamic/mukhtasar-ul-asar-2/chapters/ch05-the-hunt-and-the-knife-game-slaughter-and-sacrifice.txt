# The Hunt and the Knife: Game, Slaughter, and Sacrifice

The Hunt and the Knife: Game, Slaughter, and Sacrifice

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, was asked about a man whom fear of Allah Almighty had so overtaken that he abandoned women, abandoned wholesome food, and grew unable to lift his head toward heaven out of reverence for Allah Almighty. The one who put the question said, "As for his turning away from women, I know what the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him and his family, had of them. And as for his turning away from wholesome food, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to eat meat and honey. And as for the fear of Allah Almighty entering him until he could no longer raise his head toward the sky out of reverence, who was ever more fearful of Allah, or more humbled before Him, than the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him and his family, yet he did not do this?" For Allah Almighty has said, *"Indeed in the Messenger of Allah you have a good example,"* and true humility is in the heart.

This is the spirit in which the merits of marriage are to be understood, for there is no monasticism in Islam. Allah Almighty said: *"And marry the unmarried among you and the righteous among your male and female slaves. If they should be poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty, and Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing."*

It is narrated from the Imams of the House of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, that he said: "Whoever loves to follow my way among my nation, let him know that marriage is one of my Sunnahs." And he said: "Whoever loves to meet Allah pure and purified, let him meet Him with a wife." And he said: "There is no young man who marries in his youth except that his devil cries out, 'Woe to him, he has protected two-thirds of his religion!' So let the servant then fear Allah Almighty in the remaining third." Whenever he learned that one of his companions had married, he would say, "He has completed his religion." And he said, peace be upon him, "The more a believer increases in faith, the more he loves women." And he said, "There is no monasticism in Islam. Marry, for I shall boast of your numbers before the nations." He forbade celibacy and the forsaking of marriage.

He said: "When a believing man comes to his believing wife, two angels enfold him, and he is like one who has drawn his sword in the path of Allah. When he is done, his sins fall from him as the leaves fall from the tree, as though he had washed himself clean of sin. If she conceives by him, he has the reward of one who fasts and stands in prayer. When the pangs of labor seize her, no one knows her reward but Allah Almighty. When she delivers, with every draught of milk she suckles the child Allah Almighty writes for her a good deed and erases from her an evil one. And if she should die of her travail, she will rise on the Day of Resurrection without reckoning, for she died of her affliction."

And he said: "Whoever abandons marriage out of fear of household expense has thought ill of his Lord the Almighty, who says, *'If they should be poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty.'"* And he said: "Whoever amasses wealth and does not marry, so that the women left unwed because of him fall into sin, their sin rests upon him." And from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him: "No two believers are joined in a lawful marriage but that a caller from heaven cries out, 'Allah Almighty has wedded such a one to such a one'; and no two believing spouses are parted from a marriage but that a caller from heaven cries out, 'Allah Almighty has permitted the separation of such a one from such a one.'"

On what is recommended in a spouse, and from whose marriage one should turn away, it is narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, that he said: "Choose well for your seed, for the maternal uncle is one of the two who share the bed." By this he meant: do not place your seed except in purity, that the mother of the child be not of corrupt origin nor corrupt in herself. From this also he forbade that a child be suckled by the milk of a wicked woman; and if such a thing is disliked in nursing and forbidden, then in lineage it is graver still and more emphatic. He said: "Marry those who are your equals, and give your daughters in marriage to them, and choose well for your seed."

He related that Allah Almighty says: "When I wish to grant My servant the good of this world and the Hereafter, I give him a tongue that remembers Me, a humble heart, a body patient in affliction, and a believing wife who gladdens him when he looks upon her and guards herself and his wealth in his absence." And he said: "Five things are of happiness: a righteous wife, upright children, righteous companions, a man's provision in his own land, and love for the family of Muhammad, peace be upon him and his family."

He said: "A good woman among women is like the albino crow, and the albino crow is seldom found." By "good" here he meant the believing, chaste, beautiful woman, fitting in all her states; and rare is the woman found in such a condition, so he likened her to the albino crow, which is the rarest of birds. He said: "A woman of standing has no price, neither the good among them nor the bad. As for the good, she has no price in gold or silver or all the possessions of this world; and as for the bad, she has no price, not even of dust, for dust is better than she." And he said: "The world is but enjoyment, and the best enjoyment of the world is a righteous wife." And he said: "Of the believing man's happiness is a righteous wife, a spacious dwelling, an easy mount, and a righteous child."

He forbade that a woman be married for her wealth or her beauty alone, saying, "Her wealth makes her overbearing, and her beauty destroys her." And he said: "Take the one of religion." And he said, comparing what is most prized: "There is no horse finer than the deep black, and no woman like the daughter of one's age." And he said: "The best of your women are the women of Quraysh, the most tender toward a husband and the most devoted to a child."

He said: "Marry the one of clear, healthy complexion, for there is blessing in that. And when one of you would marry a woman, let him ask about her hair as he asks about her face, for the hair is one of the two beauties. And keep to those of shorter stature, for that is better suited to what you seek." He said: "A woman is but a necklace, so let each of you consider well with what he adorns himself." And he said: "If there is ill fortune in anything, it is in a woman, a dwelling, and a mount."

He said: "Women are four: a gathering that gathers, a spring that gives water, a war that subdues, and a galling fetter." As for the galling fetter, the people of the Age of Ignorance would shackle their captives and those they wished to torment with a thong of fresh hide stripped from a camel, turning the hair inward against the skin and binding it fast; as it dried it tightened and the skin contracted, and lice would breed within it and could not be reached because of its tightness, so the captive suffered greatly, a torment that struck without a blow. This the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, made a likeness of the bad wife and what her husband endures from her. The gathering that gathers is she in whom is gathered her goodness and her purity; the spring that gives water is she who has a child in her arms and another in her womb; and the war that subdues is she of ill manners toward her husband.

He said: "Beware of marrying the foolish woman, for her companionship is an affliction and her child is a loss." And he said: "The best women of my nation are those fairest of face and lightest in dowry." And he said: "Among the blessings of a woman is that her firstborn be a girl." He said again: "Women are four: a spring that gives water, a gathering that gathers, a war that subdues, and a barren woman." And he said: "Marry the dark-complexioned woman who is fertile, and do not marry the beautiful barren woman, for I will boast of your numbers before the nations on the Day of Resurrection. The children who die young are beneath the Throne of the Most Merciful, seeking forgiveness for their parents; Ibrahim, peace be upon him, takes them into his care, and Sarah raises them upon a mountain of musk and amber and saffron."

He said: "The best of your women is the ardent yet chaste one, ardent toward her husband and chaste from all besides him." And he said: "Marry the virgins, for they are sweetest of mouth, purest of womb, quickest of the women to learn, and most steadfast in affection." And he said: "Marry the ayyami," meaning Allah Almighty will better their character, broaden their provision, and increase their blessing. His command here is to marry the ayyami, those women who once had husbands who died or who divorced them, and it is a command of encouragement, not of obligation that may not be left, just as Allah's word, *"And marry the unmarried among you,"* is of this kind. Whoever marries one of the ayyami, desiring what Allah Almighty and His Messenger have urged, has done what he was called to; and whoever marries a virgin, there is no fault in that, for the virtue of marrying virgins has already been mentioned from the Messenger of Allah. Yet the ayyami should not be passed over out of mere preference or disdain on account of a prior marriage, for most of the wives of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, had been married before, and Allah Almighty says: *"Indeed in the Messenger of Allah you have a good example for those who hope for Allah and the Last Day."*

A Muslim should answer his Muslim brother who proposes to him, when he is satisfied with the man's religion; and he warned that if this is not done, there will be strife in the land and great corruption. He forbade a marriage sought for other than the face of Allah Almighty and chastity, and he forbade marriage undertaken for show and reputation. He said: "Whoever marries a woman for her standing or her wealth, Allah leaves him to that; but whoever marries her for her religion and her excellence, Allah Almighty grants him beauty and wealth besides." Then he recited the words of Allah Almighty: *"If they should be poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty, and Allah is all-Encompassing, Knowing."* And he said: "There is no calamity heavier upon a servant in his religion than that his Muslim kinsman should come to him and say, 'Marry me your daughter,' and he answer, 'I will not,' because he is wealthier than the suitor."

It is told of Ali ibn al-Husayn, peace be upon him, that he passed by a woman in one of the districts of Makkah and was struck by her beauty and character. He inquired about her, found her without a husband, proposed to her, married her, and consummated the marriage. Afterward a man of his kin came to him and said, "O son of the Messenger of Allah, something has weighed upon me concerning your marriage to this woman that I could not bear, for you married her without asking after her lineage. I could not rest until I inquired, and I found her to be of the Banu Shayban, of the noblest of her people, and so I was gladdened and set at ease." Ali ibn al-Husayn, peace be upon him, said, "And what was it you had feared?" The man said, "I feared she might not be your equal, and that people would speak of it." Ali ibn al-Husayn, peace be upon him, said to him, "I had thought you of sounder judgment than this. Do you not know that Allah Almighty brought Islam and by it raised the lowly, completed the deficient, and ennobled what was base? There is no reproach upon the affair of a Muslim; reproach belongs only to the Age of Ignorance."

He, peace be upon him, also married the freedwoman who had borne a son to a man now dead. When Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan heard of it, he wrote reproaching him, thinking he had found in it a fault, and saying, "Was there not enough for you among the women of Quraysh and the noble Arabs to keep you from the mother of another man's son?" Ali ibn al-Husayn, peace be upon him, wrote back to him: "To proceed: Allah Almighty has by Islam raised the lowly and completed the deficient, so there is no reproach upon the affair of a Muslim; reproach belongs only to the Age of Ignorance. And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, married such a one," meaning Safiyyah bint Hayy ibn Akhtab, "and in him is a good example." Were it permissible to disdain the mothers of children, the marriage bed would be abandoned and corruption would appear in the land; and the Imams, may Allah's prayers be upon them, are the most fit to make this plain, that the people might follow their example.

Allah Almighty said: *"O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most honored of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you. Indeed, Allah is Knowing and Aware."*

On the day of the conquest of Makkah he addressed the people, praised and glorified Allah, then said: "O people, Allah Almighty has taken from you the arrogance of the Age of Ignorance and its boasting in forefathers. You are all from Adam, and Adam was from clay. The best of Allah's servants before Allah are those most fearful of Him. Arabness is not a thing born of a father; it is a tongue that speaks. Whoever is held back by his deeds, his lineage will not advance him." And every claim of blood or rancor from the Age of Ignorance is beneath his feet until the Day of Resurrection.

It is told that he gave Dubaa bint al-Zubayr ibn Abd al-Muttalib in marriage to al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad, and said that he married her to al-Miqdad only that marriage might be made humble, that the people might follow the example of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, and that they might know that the most honored of them before Allah is the most God-fearing. Al-Zubayr was the brother of Abd Allah, the father of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, by both father and mother. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, said: "The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, married Qurayshi women to freedmen: he gave Dubaa bint al-Zubayr ibn Abd al-Muttalib to al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad, and he married Tamim al-Dari to a woman of the Banu Hashim ibn Abd Manaf."

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "When one whose religion and trustworthiness are pleasing to you comes seeking marriage, then marry him; for if you do not, there will be strife in the earth and great corruption."

It is narrated from Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him, that he was in Makkah when word reached him that Dawud ibn Ali had ordered the killing of al-Ma'ali ibn Hunaish, who was one of his own companions, and that he had been killed. Al-Sadiq, peace be upon him, rose in anger, dragging his garment behind him. His son Isma'il, peace be upon him, came to meet him and said: "O my father, where are you going? The people are defiant, and their insolence against Allah Almighty is such as you well know. Leave them be." But he struck him upon the chest and said: "Step aside from me. Were it a blazing fire, I would plunge into it." He went on until he came in upon Dawud and said: "O Dawud, you have committed a sin that Allah will never forgive you." Dawud said: "And what is that?" He said: "You have killed a believing man from among the people of Paradise."

Dawud grew angry and fell silent for a time, then said: "And you too have committed a sin that Allah Almighty will not forgive you." He said: "And what is that?" He said: "Your daughter has been given in marriage to a man from Banu Umayyah." Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him, answered that in this his example was the Messenger of Allah himself, peace and blessings be upon him, for he had given his daughter Zaynab in marriage to Abu al-As ibn Rabi'ah, and he had given Umm Kulthum in marriage to Uthman ibn Affan, and when she died he married him to Ruqayyah, both being his daughters, peace be upon him and his family.

Then he said: "You are the one who forbade the killing of believers unjustly." Dawud said: "I did not command that he be killed." Al-Sadiq said that al-Sarraqi had reported, "We have lost him," and that he, the killer, had said, "I have taken responsibility for him." So Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, had his companions seize the man and proceed with him to be put to death. The man cried out: "O servants of Allah, do you command me to kill men for them, and then exact retribution from me?" Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him, said: "As for you, O enemy of Allah, you confess to the killing, and your companion has denied that he commanded you to it. Have you any proof of that?" The man said: "And who will bear witness for me against Dawud?" He said: "Then retribution from you is obligatory and must be exacted." And he delivered him over to the kinsmen of al-Ma'ali, and they put him to death.

It was said to Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him: Is it permissible to give daughters in marriage to those of opposing views? He answered that the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, had given his daughters in marriage to certain men whom they knew, and that Umar had sought the hand of Ali's daughter Umm Kulthum, may Allah's prayers be upon him, and Ali had refused him. So Umar went to al-Abbas and complained of this to him, and pressed the Banu Abd al-Muttalib hard. Al-Abbas came to Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, and said: "O son of my brother, you see the situation we are in. Umar has sought her hand and has pressed us hard over your refusing him." He did not cease until Ali committed her affair to him, and al-Abbas gave her in marriage to Umar on his behalf. It is therefore better to give daughters in marriage to people of sound faith, or to those who do not stir up enmity against the family of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family. The marriage of a believer is better than the marriage of any other, yet when there is necessity there is no harm in marrying people of differing views from among the Muslims. Likewise to marry among them is not forbidden, as marriage to polytheists is forbidden, but the meritorious and preferred course is marriage among people of sound faith, and after them the weak ones who are neither loyal nor hostile.

A man asked Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, about a sister of his wife, a believing woman of knowledge who was in Basra and had no man among her kin to oversee her affair. He said to him, peace be upon him: "Do not give her in marriage to them. As for yourselves, there is no harm in your giving the weak women in marriage; but a Nasibiyyah, the daughter of a Nasib, no." Zurara ibn A'yan asked Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him, about the like of this, and he was permitted to give the weak women in marriage, but not a Nasibiyyah, the daughter of a Nasib. And Abu Ja'far, may Allah's prayers be upon him, said to Zurara ibn A'yan concerning the weak women: "Keep to the simple and weak among women." And Abu Ja'far, may Allah's prayers be upon him, said: "Marry into the people of doubt, but do not give your women to them in marriage, for a woman takes from her husband's manners, and he prevails over her in the matter of his religion."

He said, may Allah's prayers be upon him: "The marriage of people of knowledge, and marriage among them, is foremost and best and nearest to Allah Almighty. If that is not to be had, then the weak ones who are neither loyal nor hostile are better than the people of enmity." After them come the Muslims of the people of truce, whom the Shari'ah binds together: they marry one another and inherit from one another, and the testimony of one of them concerning another is admissible when they are upright and not suspect. As for the people of disbelief, such as Jews, Christians, and polytheists, it is not lawful for any of them to marry a Muslim woman, though their marrying one another is permissible, and Muslim men may marry their women when there is necessity and Muslim women are not to be found. Muslim women, however, are not lawful for them.

Abu Abd Allah, peace be upon him, was asked about the saying of Allah Almighty: ﴿الزَّانِي لَا يَنْكِحُ إِلَّا زَانِيَةً أَوْ مُشْرِكَةً وَالزَّانِيَةُ لَا يَنْكِحُهَا إِلَّا زَانٍ أَوْ مُشْرِكٌ وَحُرِّمَ ذَٰلِكَ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ﴾, "The adulterer shall not marry except an adulteress or a polytheist, and the adulteress shall not marry except an adulterer or a polytheist, and that is forbidden to the believers." He said: "This was revealed concerning women known for licentiousness in the time of ignorance, who were openly hired out for adultery and had set up houses marked for that purpose. The truce came with the provision that whoever wished might return to them; among them were Habibah, al-Rabab, and Sarah, whose blood the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, declared lawful on the day of the conquest of Makkah, because she had been inciting the polytheists to fight the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family. As for a slave girl who has fallen into fornication, there is no harm in purchasing her and having relations with her, and there is likewise no harm in marrying such a woman when there is necessity, provided one is fortified and guarded against her. Yet chaste women are better and more fitting."

A man came to the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, and said: "O Messenger of Allah, I have a woman who does not turn away the hand of one who touches her, and I love her." He said: "Then keep her." It was narrated of a man who saw his wife committing adultery yet was loath to part from her: he may keep her if he wishes. Peace be upon him was asked about a man's marrying an immoral woman, and he said: "What prevents him? But if he does so, let him fortify the door of his house." He was asked about a man who marries a slave girl born of adultery, and he said: "There is no harm in it, though to keep clear of that is dearer to me." Concerning a man who has a slave girl who has fallen into fornication, asking whether he may have relations with her, he said: "Yes, if he wishes." Concerning a man who marries a woman without full discernment, he said: "It is permissible, so long as no fault remains attached to his child." Concerning a child of adultery, whether one may have relations with her and sell her if she is a slave girl, and buy her, he said: "Yes; the forbidden does not render the lawful forbidden, nor corrupt it. Were people to turn away from the like of these and forbid their marriage, corruption in the land would multiply, and Allah may yet set right the corrupters and accept the repentance of the sinners."

Some of the Ahl al-Bayt, may Allah's prayers be upon them, were asked about the saying of Allah Almighty, "The adulterer shall not marry except an adulteress or a polytheist." He said: "The word 'marry' here may mean the act of union and intercourse, or it may mean the contract and marriage. If it means union and intercourse, then it may indeed signify adultery, not lawful marriage." He said: "As for His words, 'except an adulterer or a polytheist,' a man does not bring upon himself the wrath of Allah Almighty in this except while he is a polytheist." And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, said: "The adulterer does not commit adultery at the moment of his adultery while he is a believer."

**Mentioning the Seeking of Women's Hands in Marriage**

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and upon the Imams of his house, that he forbade a man to propose marriage over the proposal of his brother, and to bid over the bid of his brother. Some of the Ahl al-Bayt, peace be upon them, explained this and said: The meaning of the words of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, that a man should not propose over his brother's proposal, is that this is after there has come about mutual approach, contentment, inclination, attentiveness, and agreement, such that nothing remains but the contract itself. As for what falls short of that, there is no harm in two men, or a group, proposing to a woman until she inclines to one of them and answers him; only then is it disliked for another to oppose him with a proposal. In the same way it is permissible for a man to bid on a commodity and to purchase it, and for another to bid above him, so long as the two parties to the sale have not yet reached agreement.

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, commanded selling by open auction, and people have continued to this day to outbid one another in the call and otherwise, each raising upon the other until the commodity is settled and the seller accepts a buyer; after that no further bid should be made, nor any raising upon what has been commanded. It is a matter of recommendation, on account of the benefit in it. Should a suitor propose, or a bidder raise his bid upon a commodity, after agreement and before the contract of marriage or sale is concluded, that falls under the same ruling: if one surpasses another in merit before the contract is concluded, it does not invalidate the marriage or the sale, since nothing has become binding to another by contract. And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, said: "When one of you wishes to marry a woman, let him cast his gaze upon her, for he is but a buyer." And he said: "When one of you wishes to marry a woman, there is no harm in his looking at what draws him to her."

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, said: I mentioned this hadith to Jabir ibn Abd Allah al-Ansari, and he said to me: "I heard it from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him. I sought the hand of a young woman from among the women of the Ansar, and I concealed myself for her behind a wall of her father's garden, and I looked at her and saw what I wished to see and what I had not meant to see, and she pleased me, so I married her, and she was the best of women."

Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him, said: There is no harm for one who wishes to marry a woman in looking at her; but he should not look at a slave girl except with her master's permission, meaning that the master may not wish to sell his slave girl or to offer her. The command from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, permitting a man to look at the woman he intends to marry, extends the permission of that gaze, once he has come to know that they will give her to him and will not refuse him, when they have agreed and nothing remains but the marriage contract. He should look at her only to acquaint himself with her state, not out of desire to look at her or to take pleasure in her, nor should he deliberately direct his gaze toward her private parts or toward what it is not fitting to look upon. It is not lawful for him to look with the eye of contemplation at women who are not his, that is, women other than his wives, those forbidden to him by blood, and those his right hand possesses, whether they be bareheaded or unveiled, for Allah says, exalted be He, ﴿قُل لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ يَغُضُّوا مِنْ أَبْصَارِهِمْ﴾, "Say to the believing men that they lower their gaze."

Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him, was asked about a man past whom a woman passes, and he looks at her from behind. He said: "Would it please one of you that men should look upon his womenfolk and his kinswomen? Be content for people with what you are content with for yourselves." And he said, peace be upon him, concerning the saying of Allah Almighty in the story of Moses, recounting the words of the woman, ﴿قَالَتْ إِحْدَاهُمَا يَا أَبَتِ اسْتَأْجِرْهُ إِنَّ خَيْرَ مَنِ اسْتَأْجَرْتَ الْقَوِيُّ الْأَمِينُ﴾, "One of the two women said: O my father, hire him; indeed, the best one you can hire is the strong and the trustworthy," that she meant Moses, peace be upon him and his family. Her father said to her: "As for your saying that he is strong, that is from what you saw of his lifting the rock from the well; but how did you come to know his trustworthiness?" She said: "When he set out with me to come to you, I wished to walk before him to show him the road, but he came forward and said: Walk behind me and direct me to the road, for we are a people who do not look upon the backs of women."

It is narrated from Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, that he said: I heard the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, when looking at women was mentioned, and he said: "The first glance is for you," meaning the glance that falls unawares, "and the second is against you, and the third glance is a poisoned arrow from the arrows of Iblis. Whoever forsakes such glances for the sake of Allah Almighty, and for no other, Allah Almighty will requite him with a faith whose sweetness he will taste." And Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's prayers be upon him, said: "Do those who look upon the forms of women not fear that they may be afflicted with the like in their own wives?"

It is narrated from Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, that a man came to the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, and said: "O Messenger of Allah, should I ask my mother's leave before entering upon her?" He said: "Yes." He said: "And why, O Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family?" He said: "Would it please you to see her unclothed?" He said: "No." He said: "Then ask her leave." He said: "O Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, my sister uncovers her hair before me." He said: "No." He said: "Why?" He said: "I fear that, should she reveal one of her charms before you, Satan may invite you toward her." And he said, peace be upon him and his family: "When one of you would kiss a woman who is a mahram to him and has come of age, a mother, a sister, an aunt, or any who is forbidden to him in marriage, let him kiss her upon the head and between the eyes, or let him refrain from her cheeks and her mouth."

Concerning Fatimah, may Allah's prayers be upon her: a blind man sought leave to enter upon her, and she veiled herself from him. It was said to her: "He is blind, his sight is wholly gone; he sees nothing of her." She said: "Even if he does not see me, I see him, and he perceives the voice and senses the scent."

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, asked Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, and a company that was before him, what is best for women, and they did not know what to say. Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, withdrew to Fatimah, may Allah's prayers be upon her, and mentioned this to her, and she said: "What is best for women is that they should not see men and that men should not see them." Ali, may Allah's prayers be upon him, went to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, and said this to him. He said: "Did you arrive at this of yourself, or did another say it?" He said: "O Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family, it was Fatimah who said it." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, was pleased by it and said: "Fatimah is but a part of me."

# The Hunt and the Knife: Game, Slaughter, and Sacrifice

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, taught that whoever desires to marry should not approach the matter heedlessly, but should begin by turning to Allah, praising Him and extolling Him, and then asking Him for the kind of companion that makes a household whole. He said that such a person should pray:

"O Allah, grant me a wife who is affectionate and fruitful, grateful and jealous in her honor. If I do good, she thanks me; if I do wrong, she forgives me; if I remember Allah, she helps me; if I forget, she reminds me; if I go out from her, she guards herself; if I come in to her, she gladdens me; if I command her, she obeys me; if I swear by her, she fulfills my oath; if she becomes angry, You satisfy me. O Lord of majesty and honor, grant me what I have asked for, and what You have apportioned and given."

And he made a clear promise concerning this supplication. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, said that whoever says these words and acts upon them, Allah Almighty will give him what he has asked.

He also taught that marriage is not a thing to be entered without its proper preliminaries. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, said that every marriage in which there is no engagement is like a leprous hand.

This same care for the right beginning was kept by his descendants. Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn 'Ali, peace be upon them, when he gave a woman in marriage or when a marriage was contracted in his presence, would open with words of praise and witness, saying: "Praise be to Allah. We praise Him and seek refuge with Him from the evils of ourselves and the evils of our deeds. Whoever Allah guides, none can mislead, and whoever He leaves to stray, none can guide. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and His messenger."

Then he would recite the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ اتَّقُوا رَبَّكُمُ الَّذِي خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا رِجَالًا كَثِيرًا وَنِسَاءً ۚ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ الَّذِي تَسَاءَلُونَ بِهِ وَالْأَرْحَامَ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلَيْكُمْ رَقِيبًا ﴾

And he would recite the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ حَقَّ تُقَاتِهِ وَلَا تَمُوتُنَّ إِلَّا وَأَنتُم مُّسْلِمُونَ ﴾

And he would recite the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَقُولُوا قَوْلًا سَدِيدًا ۝ يُصْلِحْ لَكُمْ أَعْمَالَكُمْ وَيَغْفِرْ لَكُمْ ذُنُوبَكُمْ ۚ وَمَن يُطِعِ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ فَقَدْ فَازَ فَوْزًا عَظِيمًا ﴾
