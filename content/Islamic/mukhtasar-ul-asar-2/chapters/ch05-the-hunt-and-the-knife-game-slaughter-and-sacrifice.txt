### What is lawful in hunting and what is forbidden

Allah the Almighty said: ﴿أُحِلَّ لَكُمْ صَيْدُ الْبَحْرِ وَطَعَامُهُ مَتَاعًا لَكُمْ وَلِلسَّيَّارَةِ وَحُرِّمَ عَلَيْكُمْ صَيْدُ الْبَرِّ مَا دُمْتُمْ حُرُمًا﴾ "He has made permissible for you the hunting of the sea and its food, as a pleasure for you and for travelers, and forbidden for you is the hunting of land as long as you are in the sacred state" (al-Ma'idah: 96), and He said: ﴿وَإِذَا حَلَلْتُمْ فَاصْطَادُوا﴾ "But when you leave the sacred state, then hunt" (al-Ma'idah: 2). So the Almighty permitted the hunting of the sea even while one remains in the state of consecration, and forbade the hunting of the land while one is in it. He addressed this to the generality of the believers, except for what was excluded from it on the tongue of His Messenger and the Imams of his household, who make plain what He intends, as He says: ﴿وَأَنزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ الذِّكْرَ لِتُبَيِّنَ لِلنَّاسِ مَا نُزِّلَ إِلَيْهِمْ﴾ "And We have sent down to you the Remembrance that you may make clear to the people what has been revealed to them" (al-Nahl: 44).

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, that he forbade hunting birds in their nests, and said: "The bird in its nest is under the protection of Allah; but if it takes to the air upon its wings, then hunt it if you wish. Set a trap for it and shoot at it." It was narrated from him, and from Ali, (ع), and from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (عليهم السلام), that pigeons in the towns should not be hunted, because they are among what belongs to their owners, even when they fly free. We have already mentioned, in the chapters on food, what is lawful to eat among the beasts of land and sea and what is forbidden. So whatever is caught of that which is lawful to eat is eaten, and whatever is caught of that which is forbidden to eat is left and not eaten, except in dire necessity for the one who is compelled.

### What hunting animals seize and kill

Allah the Almighty said: ﴿يَسْأَلُونَكَ مَاذَا أُحِلَّ لَهُمْ قُلْ أُحِلَّ لَكُمُ الطَّيِّبَاتُ وَمَا عَلَّمْتُم مِّنَ الْجَوَارِحِ مُكَلِّبِينَ تُعَلِّمُونَهُنَّ مِمَّا عَلَّمَكُمُ اللَّهُ فَكُلُوا مِمَّا أَمْسَكْنَ عَلَيْكُمْ وَاذْكُرُوا اسْمَ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ سَرِيعُ الْحِسَابِ﴾ "They ask you what has been made lawful for them. Say: Lawful for you are all good things, and what you have trained of hunting animals, training them as Allah has taught you, so eat of what they have caught for you, and mention the name of Allah over it, and fear Allah. Indeed, Allah is swift in reckoning" (al-Ma'idah: 4).

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, from Ali, (ع) and upon them all, that he said: If a hunting animal seizes prey and it dies before its slaughter can be performed, then if the animal is trained, and the Muslim sent it and invoked the name of Allah at its release, it may be eaten. If he forgot to invoke the name at the moment of release, he invokes it when he remembers. But if he deliberately omitted it, it is not to be eaten.

The word الجوارح in the tongue of the Arabs, the tongue in which the Qur'an was revealed, means the earners. The Arabs say, "So-and-so is the جارحة of his household," meaning the one who earns for them. So dogs and their like, and the birds of prey with which one hunts, are earners that earn the catch for the one who has trained them. If such an animal is untrained yet seizes the prey, and the prey is reached while still alive and is then slaughtered, it is eaten; but if its slaughter is not reached in time, it is not eaten. And they, (عليهم السلام), said: Whatever the trained hunting animals seize may be eaten, even if its slaughter was not reached, and even if the animal has eaten from it. They, (عليهم السلام), said: Every trained hunting creature, such as cheetahs and the other predators of the land, and the birds of prey, may have its catch eaten.

They narrated from the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, concerning the hunting of the black dog, that he disliked it for them and ordered it to be killed. And they, (عليهم السلام), said: Dogs are all one in their ruling, apart from this, whether the Kurdish kind or the greyhound kind: what they seize may be eaten when they are trained. They, (عليهم السلام), said: If the prey is reached while still alive and the hunting animal has wounded it, and he does not then slaughter it, and it dies in his hands from the act of the hunting animal, it is eaten. But if the hunting animal did not wound it, and it was carried off and died afterward, he does not eat it.

From Ali, (ع), that he forbade the eating of whatever dies before its slaughter can be reached from the game of the dogs of the Magians and of the Christians of the Arabs, and of whatever hunting animals they have trained, unless the Muslims train them afterward.

### What hunters bring down with a throw

Allah the Almighty said: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَيَبْلُوَنَّكُمُ اللَّهُ بِشَيْءٍ مِّنَ الصَّيْدِ تَنَالُهُ أَيْدِيكُمْ وَرِمَاحُكُمْ لِيَعْلَمَ اللَّهُ مَن يَخَافُهُ بِالْغَيْبِ فَمَنِ اعْتَدَى بَعْدَ ذَلِكَ فَلَهُ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ﴾ "O you who believe, Allah will surely test you with something of the game that your hands and your spears can reach, that Allah may know who fears Him in the unseen. So whoever transgresses after that, for him is a painful punishment" (al-Ma'idah: 94).

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, (ع), that one of them said to a man who asked him about hunting, when he throws at prey, strikes it, and it dies: "Eat what you have killed outright (al-isma), and do not eat what you have driven off (al-inma)." Al-isma is that a man throws at the quarry and strikes it, and it dies on the spot. Al-inma is that he strikes it and it moves off and goes out of his sight until it is hidden from him and disappears, and then it dies and he finds it dead, and he does not know whether it died from his blow or from something else.

It has been narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), that he said: If he shoots the game with an arrow and strikes it in a fatal spot, then it disappears from him, and he later finds his arrow in it and knows that it died from his shot, he may eat it. But if he does not know that, he does not eat it. From Ali, (ع), that he said: If he shoots the game and strikes it, and then it falls from a high place or falls into water and dies, he does not eat it, unless he knows that it died from the blow that struck it.

Everything we have mentioned of what game is shot or struck with, so that it dies, is lawful to eat only when it has been struck with a sword, a spear, an arrow, or an iron implement. As for whatever is struck of that with a stick, a stone, or a pellet, it is not eaten according to the word of the people of the household, (عليهم السلام), unless its slaughter is reached while it is alive. They permitted the مِعْراض when one has no other arrow: the مِعْراض is an arrow without feathers, which is thrown and strikes the game with its body. From Ali, (ع), that he said: Whatever the snare takes of game, and it dies in it before it is slaughtered, is not eaten. But if it severs a hand, a leg, or a limb and cuts it off, and its slaughter is then reached, it is eaten, except for the severed limb. The الحبالة is what is set up to take game, such as nets, traps, and the like.

### The Comprehensive Doors of Slaughter

### The acts of those who slaughter

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, that they narrated from him, (ع), that he said: "Whoever slaughters an animal, let him sharpen his blade and let him give his animal ease."

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: When you intend to slaughter an animal, do not torment the beast. Sharpen the blade, face the qiblah, and invoke the name of Allah, and do not sever it through until it dies, meaning do not cut off its head. Likewise he forbade that it be skinned until it has died and grown still. They, (عليهم السلام), said: Whoever slaughters and forgets the invocation of the name, or to face the animal toward the qiblah, or is unaware of it, invokes the name of Allah the Almighty when he remembers, and it may be eaten. But if he deliberately omits that, his slaughtered animal is not to be eaten. And if he says "There is no god but Allah," or "Allah is the Greatest," or remembers Allah the Almighty with whatever he may remember Him, that suffices him in place of the invocation. What they recommended to say at the slaughter is, "In the name of Allah, and Allah is the Greatest."

They narrated from the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, that he forbade mutilating beasts and ordered that they be slaughtered as is due, and forbade المجثمة and المصبورة, which is the beast that is held, or buried, or tied up, and shot at until it dies. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: Whoever kills a sparrow in vain, Allah the Almighty will bring it before him on the Day of Resurrection, and it will cry out, saying, "My Lord, ask this one why he killed me without slaughter." So beware of mutilation.

He, (ع), said: Slaughter is at the place of slaughter, that is, below the غلصمة. The غلصمة is the knot in the windpipe, and it should be left in the head. The slaughterer cuts the windpipe, the gullet, and the two jugular veins, falling short of none of that, and not cutting into the bone. That is the Sunnah of slaughter. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع) and upon his family, was asked about breaking the neck of the animal before it dies, and he disliked it. They, (عليهم السلام), said: Whoever the knife outruns, or who out of ignorance cuts off the head of the animal without intending it and without knowing that it is not permitted, there is nothing against him and his animal may be eaten. But if he intends that, he has done wrong, and his animal is not to be eaten.

They, (عليهم السلام), said: If the beast bolts, that is, flees or refuses, so that one cannot slaughter it, or it falls into a well and cannot be lifted out, nor can one reach the place of slaughter, then let it be stabbed with a spear, or struck with a sword, or struck with an iron implement until it dies, and it is eaten. They, (عليهم السلام), said: There is no slaughter except with iron. Do not slaughter with a stone, nor a tooth, nor a nail, nor a bone, nor anything else, but only with iron.

### What may be eaten of slaughtered animals and what may not

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, and upon them all, that he disliked the slaughter of the milk-giving animal and of the pregnant one, though he did not forbid it; rather he disliked it for the benefit in them to one who has nothing else.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), was asked about the slaughtered animals of the Jews, the Christians, the Magians, and the people of divergent belief, and he recited the word of Allah the Almighty: ﴿فَكُلُوا مِمَّا ذُكِرَ اسْمُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ إِن كُنتُم بِآيَاتِهِ مُؤْمِنِينَ﴾ "So eat of that over which the name of Allah has been mentioned, if you are believers in His signs" (al-An'am: 118), and he said: When you hear them invoking the name of Allah, then eat; and what they do not invoke the name of Allah over, do not eat. And whoever among them is suspected of abandoning the invocation, holding it lawful to do so, his slaughtered animal need not be eaten, unless one sees him at the time he slaughters it and hears from him that he has invoked the name of Allah the Almighty over it. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: There is no harm in the meat sold in the markets of the Muslims, as long as it is not known of the slaughterers that they depart from the Sunnah in slaughter, and as long as that is not witnessed of their practice.

From them, (عليهم السلام), that they disliked the slaughtered animals of the Magians and the Christians of the Arabs. They permitted the slaughter of the blind man when he is directed toward the qiblah and slaughters well; and likewise the woman when she does it well; and the boy before he reaches maturity when he does it well and there is need of him. They saw no harm in slaughter performed without ritual purity. They permitted the slaughter of the mute when he understands the invocation and gestures with it.

### Valid and invalid slaughter

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, from Ali, (ع), that he was asked about the foetuses of livestock whose mothers are slaughtered while they are in their wombs, whether they are made lawful when they are brought out. He said: "Their slaughter is the slaughter of their mothers, for the foetus is a limb of her limbs. But if it comes out alive, it is left until it dies, and then it is eaten, provided it has grown hair or fur or has something of that upon it, even on its tail, for the tail is the first place where hair appears." And he disliked whatever has not yet grown hair or fur.

He, (ع), was asked about certain limbs of an animal that are cut while it is alive, such as the fatty tails of sheep, the humps of camels, and the like, and he said: "Whatever is cut from them and separated from them while they are alive, before slaughter, is carrion and is not eaten, and the rest of the animal is not eaten when it is slaughtered." And he, (ع), said: Valid slaughter is known by the eye that flickers, the leg that kicks, the tail that moves, or the ear. So whoever slaughters his animal and it moves, or thrashes after the slaughter, or flickers its eye, or any part of it stirs, it is validly slaughtered. And whatever of it falls and is slaughtered, and nothing of it moves, is not eaten, even if blood came out of it.

He, (ع), was asked about the animal that is slaughtered and then falls from a height or falls into water and dies, and he said: "If you have done the slaughter well and fulfilled what is required in it, then eat." From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), that he said: The Sunnah for the large animals is that they be sacrificed standing with one leg bound, and thus did the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, sacrifice his gift-offering. There is no harm, if that is not possible, in its kneeling and being hobbled. He disliked that the sheep be slaughtered standing, except for a reason, and said the Sunnah is that it be laid down; but if it is sacrificed in the manner of the large animals, there is no harm. He, (ع), was asked about the animal slaughtered from behind the nape, and he said: If one is unaware of that, there is no harm; but if he intends it while knowing the Sunnah, his animal is not eaten and he is to be corrected. He, (ع), was asked about two sheep, one validly slaughtered and the other not, when the valid one of the two is not known, and he said: Neither of them is eaten until it is known which is validly slaughtered.

### The sacrificial offerings

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, and from him, (ع), that he addressed the people before the Day of Sacrifice and said: "O people, whoever has the means, let him sacrifice and let him magnify the rites of Allah. And whoever does not have the means, then Allah the Almighty does not burden a soul beyond its capacity."

From him, (ع), that a man of the Ansar met him on the Day of Sacrifice after he had finished the prayer and said, "O Messenger of Allah, if you would honor me with your presence today, for I have prepared food for you." So the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, went with him, and the man brought him the food and said, "O Messenger of Allah, I slaughtered my sacrifice before I went out to the place of prayer, and I ordered my family and they prepared this food, that I might be foremost in bringing it to you." The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said to him: "Your sheep is a sheep of meat. If you have another besides it, sacrifice with it." He said, "I have nothing but a young goat of mine." He said: "Sacrifice with it, though it will not be lawful for anyone after you."

So the Sunnah in the sacrifice is that it is not slaughtered except after the Eid prayer on the Day of Sacrifice. Whoever slaughters before the prayer, that does not count for him as a sacrifice, and he must repeat it when he is able. The best slaughter is on the Day of Sacrifice, and it is valid during the days of Mina, which are the Day of Sacrifice, the Day of al-Adha, or the three days after it. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: Among camels and cattle nothing suffices as a sacrifice except the mature animal, and likewise among goats; but the yearling of the sheep suffices. And he, (ع), said: The sacrifice is an obligation upon every Muslim except one who cannot find the means, and a man's sacrifice suffices for his household.

They narrated from the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, that he addressed the people on the Day of Sacrifice and said: "This is the day of العَجّ and الثَّجّ. الثَّجّ is the blood you pour out, and whoever's intention is sincere, its first drop is an expiation for every sin. And العَجّ is supplication, so cry out to Allah the Almighty. By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, no one will leave this standing-place except that he is forgiven, save the one persisting in a great sin among the great sins, who does not bring himself to forsake it."

From him, may Allah bless him and his family, that he entered upon Fatimah, (ع), on the Day of Sacrifice and said: "O Fatimah, arise and witness your sacrifice, for its first dropping drop is an expiation for every sin that is yours. Its flesh, its dung, its bones, its wool, and everything of it will be placed in your balance and multiplied for you seventy times over." Al-Miqdad heard that and said: "May my father and mother be your ransom, O Messenger of Allah, is this something singled out for the family of Muhammad, or is it general?" He said: "Rather it is for the Muslims in general."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: What is recommended for a man is that he undertake the slaughter of his sacrifice with his own hand. If he is unable, let him place his hand with the hand of the slaughterer. If he is unable to do that, let him stand over it, invoking the name of Allah upon it until it is slaughtered. From Ali, (ع), that he said: The sacrifice of a Muslim should not be slaughtered except by a Muslim, and he says when slaughtering it: "In the name of Allah, and Allah is the Greatest. I have turned my face toward the One who created the heavens and the earth, upright and submitting, and I am not among the polytheists. Truly my prayer, my sacrifice, my living, and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds, who has no partner. By this I have been commanded, and I am the first of those who submit."

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), that he was asked about the best of sacrificial animals and said: "The females of camels, then their males, then the females of cattle, then their males, then the rams of the sheep, then the wethers of them," meaning those whose testicles have been crushed or bound until they spoil, "then the ewes, then the male of the goats, then the females of them." He disliked the one with cut-off ears. The best of all these kinds is the fattest and the largest of them. The best of the rams is the horned ram that eats amid blackness, drinks amid blackness, walks amid blackness, looks amid blackness, urinates amid blackness, and drops dung amid blackness. With the like of this the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, used to sacrifice. It is the الأملح, the black-and-white. It has come down that he used to sacrifice with two such black-and-white rams, and it is said that this is the description of the ram that was sent down to Ibrahim, (ع) and upon his family.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), that he permitted sharing in the sacrifice for one who cannot find an animal to offer alone but is able to take part. From Ali, (ع), that he disliked sacrificing the animal with a broken horn, the clearly lame, the clearly emaciated, and the one whose ear has been wholly cut away. He permitted the slit in the ear when it is a mark, and the aged animal when there is no defect or leanness in it. He, (ع), said: Whoever buys a sacrifice and it falls ill or dies before the Day of Sacrifice, it has sufficed for him; but if he obtains another to sacrifice in its place and does so, that is better.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), that he was asked about the word of Allah the Almighty, ﴿فَكُلُوا مِنْهَا وَأَطْعِمُوا الْقَانِعَ وَالْمُعْتَرَّ﴾ "So eat of it and feed the contented and the petitioner" (al-Hajj: 36), and His word, ﴿فَكُلُوا مِنْهَا وَأَطْعِمُوا الْبَائِسَ الْفَقِيرَ﴾ "So eat of it and feed the wretched poor" (al-Hajj: 28). He said: "The قانع is the beggar who is content with what he is given and does not deem it too little; the مُعْتَرّ is the one who presents himself for charity but does not ask; the poor man is the one who finds nothing, and the مسكين is harder pressed than he in his condition; and the wretched poor man is the worst of them in condition and the most hard pressed."

He said: My father, (رض), when he slaughtered his sacrifice at Mina or sacrificed his gift-offering, would place the head before him. When a beggar passed by, he would stop him and say, "Take the head." If he saw that the man took it with gladness and went on his way, he would give it to him; but he would say, "Leave it," and give him of the meat instead. And if the man turned away from it and did not accept it, he would leave it and give him nothing, testing thereby the conditions of the poor.

From Ali, (ع), that he said concerning the word of Allah the Almighty, ﴿فَكُلُوا مِنْهَا﴾ "So eat of it" (al-Hajj: 36): "This is a command of recommendation, not of obligation. Whoever wishes may eat of his sacrifice, and whoever wishes need not eat of it, and to eat of it is better." It has come down from the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, that he made Ali a partner in a gift-offering, and they were a hundred sacrificial camels. He ordered that a piece be taken from each camel and put in a pot and cooked. Then he called Ali, (ع), and the two of them ate of the meat as much as they could and were able, and sipped of the broth, that they might partake of all their flesh. And the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said: "The broth is one of the two meats, for the strength of the meat comes out into it."
