# Binding Words: Oaths, Vows, and Their Atonement

If a man says, "If Allah heals my sickness, or returns to me the one who is absent, or grants me wealth or a child, then I owe such and such a vow," and he names that vow, then he is bound to fulfill it once Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has done for him what he longed for, since he laid the vow upon himself in the matter. But if he does not name the vow, saying only, "If such a thing comes to pass, of what I have mentioned, then I owe a vow to Allah," without specifying what that vow is, then nothing is required of him. If he vowed some particular thing, it suffices him, and it stands as a voluntary act, not an obligation laid upon him. And if he makes his vow after the measure of the expiation of an oath, that is good and seemly.

### Concerning the Expiations

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: "Allah will not take you to task for the thoughtless word in your oaths, but He will take you to task for the oaths you have deliberately sworn. Its expiation is the feeding of ten poor persons from the average of that with which you feed your own families, or the clothing of them, or the freeing of a slave. Whoever does not find the means, then a fast of three days. That is the expiation of your oaths when you have sworn. And guard your oaths. Thus does Allah make clear to you His signs, that you may be grateful" (al-Ma'idah: 89).

So whoever swears by Allah and then breaks his oath with a breach that makes expiation obligatory upon him owes the expiation set down above. If he is able, he feeds ten poor persons, or clothes them, or frees a slave, and he is free to do whichever of these he wishes, for he has the choice among them.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: of everything in the Qur'an stated with "or," giving the choice among options, the one to whom it applies has that choice; but wherever it is stated, "if he does not find" or "if he is not able," then he must perform the first whenever he finds it and is able, and the second does not suffice him until he can neither find the first nor perform it. So if the one who swore can find nothing with which to feed ten poor persons, and has the means neither to free a slave, whether of small worth or great, nor to clothe ten poor persons, then he must fast three days. Fasting does not stand in place of the rest until none of what has been named, neither food, nor clothing, nor manumission, can be found.

The feeding in the expiation of an oath is not discharged by a single day's provision given to the poor; the least of it is a mudd of food, together with what supports it. The middle grade of seasoning, the idam, is vinegar and oil; meat is the highest grade; and salt alone is the lowest, which does not suffice. This rests upon the word of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, "from the average of that with which you feed your families" (al-Ma'idah: 89). The average is the most balanced: whatever the one making expiation feeds his household on most of his days and in the general run of his circumstances is what he ought to feed in the expiation of an oath. If he feeds them better than that, it is the more excellent for him.

As for the clothing in the expiation of an oath, each poor person is to receive two garments.

In the expiation of an oath, the freeing of a slave born into bondage suffices. The freeing of a mudabbar slave, one whose release has been tied to his master's death, is not permitted unless the master first revokes that arrangement, the tadbir, returns him to the condition of a slave, and then frees him. The fast in the expiation of an oath is three consecutive days.

The feeding and clothing of the people of allegiance from the expiation of an oath is what is enjoined upon those who are commanded to it. If such persons are not to be found, then those of the weak who harbor no enmity are entitled to it. But one must not give to a Nasib, who is known for his hostility toward the family of Muhammad, (ع), anything from expiation, nor from charity, nor from anything else of the sort, even if no one but him can be found.

Whoever lays a vow upon himself concerning some matter, in case it should come to pass, when in truth it had already come to pass before he bound himself to it, and he had no knowledge that it had occurred, then nothing is required of him. A man questioned Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), about the like of this, saying: "I have a slave girl by whom I am loath to have a child. Her menses ceased to come on time, and I feared that she had conceived, so I bound myself before Allah to free a slave, to fast, and to give charity if she should menstruate. Then she menstruated, but before I had bound myself to all this, and I did not know it." He said: "Nothing is required of you."

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: if a man swears that he will not give another man dinars, and then gives him goods in their place, he has not broken his oath. If a man swears that he will not sell his wares at a price he has named, and then sells them for goods worth that same price, he has not broken his oath. And if a man is owed dinars by another and swears that he will not receive them except in full, and is then given part of them in coin and part in goods of equal value, he has not broken his oath.

He, (ع), was asked about a man who swears to slaughter his son, and he said: "There is nothing upon him in that, and it is among the steps of Satan."

And he said, (ع): the expiation comes after the breaking of the oath.
