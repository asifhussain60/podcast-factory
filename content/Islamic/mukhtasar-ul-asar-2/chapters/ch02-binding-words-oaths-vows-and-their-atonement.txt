# Binding Words: Oaths, Vows, and Their Atonement

When a man swears by Allah and then breaks his oath, an expiation falls due upon him, as Allah says, "Allah makes clear to you His signs, that you may be grateful" (al-Ma'idah 89). What follows is the mention of the expiations.

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: ﴿لَا يُؤَاخِذُكُمُ اللَّهُ بِاللَّغْوِ فِي أَيْمَانِكُمْ وَلَٰكِن يُؤَاخِذُكُم بِمَا عَقَّدتُّمُ الْأَيْمَانَ ۖ فَكَفَّارَتُهُ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ أَوْ كِسْوَتُهُمْ أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ ۖ فَمَن لَّمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ كَفَّارَةُ أَيْمَانِكُمْ إِذَا حَلَفْتُمْ ۚ وَاحْفَظُوا أَيْمَانَكُمْ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ آيَاتِهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ﴾, "Allah will not take you to task for the thoughtless word in your oaths, but He will take you to task for the oaths you have bound in earnest. Its expiation is the feeding of ten poor persons with the average of what you feed your families, or their clothing, or the freeing of a slave. Whoever cannot find the means, let him fast three days. That is the expiation of your oaths when you have sworn. And guard your oaths. Thus does Allah make His signs clear to you, that you may be grateful" (al-Ma'idah 89).

So whoever swears by Allah and then commits perjury, the expiation set out above becomes obligatory upon him. If he has the means, he feeds ten poor persons, or clothes them, or frees a slave. He is free to choose whichever of these he wishes, for he is capable of any of them. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: everything in the Qur'an that is stated as "or" leaves its doer free to choose, while everything stated as "if he cannot find" or "if he is unable" obliges him to the first once he finds it and is able. The second does not suffice for him unless he cannot find the first or is incapable of it.

If the one who swore can find nothing with which to feed ten poor persons, and has the means neither to free a slave, small or large, nor to clothe ten poor persons, then he must fast three days. Fasting does not stand in place of feeding until none of what has been named is available, neither food, nor clothing, nor manumission. The feeding required in the expiation of an oath is not satisfied by a single day's provision alone; the least of it is a mudd of food, and what upholds a person by it. Its middle measure is vinegar and oil, its higher measure is meat, and its lowest is salt alone, which does not suffice. This rests upon the words of Allah Almighty, ﴿مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ﴾, "from the average of what you feed your families." The average is the most balanced. So whatever a man feeds his household on most of his days and in the general run of his circumstances is what he ought to feed in the expiation of an oath. If he feeds them better than that, it is more excellent for him.

As for clothing in the expiation of an oath, each poor person is to receive two garments. In this expiation the freeing of a slave born into bondage suffices, but it may not be discharged by freeing a mukatab slave unless he returns to his original condition of bondage and is then set free. The fasting in the expiation of an oath is three consecutive days. Feeding and clothing the people of allegiance (ahl al-walāya) from the expiation of an oath is what a man is commanded to do; and if he does not find them, then it is due to the weak who bear no enmity. One must not give to a Nāṣib, the man known for hostility toward the household of Muhammad (ع) and his family, anything from expiation, nor charity, nor any other religious disbursement, even if he can find no one else.

Whoever binds himself with a vow concerning something that had already come to pass before he laid the vow upon himself, and he did not know of it, then nothing is required of him. A man questioned Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) about the like of this, saying: "I have a slave girl. I am unwilling to have a child by her, and her menstruation grew irregular, and I feared she might have conceived. So I made it binding upon myself, for Allah, to free a slave, to fast, and to give charity, if she should menstruate. She then menstruated before I made this vow, and I knew nothing of it." He said: nothing is required of you.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) also said: whoever swears not to give a certain man dinars, and then gives him goods in their place, has not committed perjury. Whoever swears not to sell his merchandise for a stated price, and then sells it for goods equal to that price, has not committed perjury. And if a man is owed dinars and swears he will not receive them except in full, and is then given part in coin and part in goods of equal worth, he has not committed perjury.

He (ع) was asked about a man who swore an oath to slaughter his son, and he said: nothing is required of him in that, and it is among the steps of Satan (khuṭuwāt al-shayṭān). And he (ع) said: the expiation comes after the perjury.
