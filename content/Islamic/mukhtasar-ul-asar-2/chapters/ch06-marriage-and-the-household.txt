# Marriage and the Household

The Hunt and the Knife: Game, Slaughter, and Sacrifice

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), was asked about a man whom fear of Allah Almighty had so overtaken that he abandoned women, abandoned wholesome food, and grew unable to lift his head toward heaven out of reverence for Allah Almighty. The one who put the question said, "As for his turning away from women, I know what the Messenger of Allah, (ع), had of them. And as for his turning away from wholesome food, the Messenger of Allah, (ع), used to eat meat and honey. And as for the fear of Allah Almighty entering him until he could no longer raise his head toward the sky out of reverence, who was ever more fearful of Allah, or more humbled before Him, than the Messenger of Allah, (ع), yet he did not do this?" For Allah Almighty has said, *"Indeed in the Messenger of Allah you have a good example,"* and true humility is in the heart.

This is the spirit in which the merits of marriage are to be understood, for there is no monasticism in Islam. Allah Almighty said: *"And marry the unmarried among you and the righteous among your male and female slaves. If they should be poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty, and Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing."*

It is narrated from the Imams of the House of the Messenger of Allah, (ع), that he said: "Whoever loves to follow my way among my nation, let him know that marriage is one of my Sunnahs." And he said: "Whoever loves to meet Allah pure and purified, let him meet Him with a wife." And he said: "There is no young man who marries in his youth except that his devil cries out, Woe to him, he has protected two-thirds of his religion! So let the servant then fear Allah Almighty in the remaining third." Whenever he learned that one of his companions had married, he would say, "He has completed his religion." And he said, (ع), "The more a believer increases in faith, the more he loves women." And he said, "There is no monasticism in Islam. Marry, for I shall boast of your numbers before the nations." He forbade celibacy and the forsaking of marriage.

He said: "When a believing man comes to his believing wife, two angels enfold him, and he is like one who has drawn his sword in the path of Allah. When he is done, his sins fall from him as the leaves fall from the tree, as though he had washed himself clean of sin. If she conceives by him, he has the reward of one who fasts and stands in prayer. When the pangs of labor seize her, no one knows her reward but Allah Almighty. When she delivers, with every draught of milk she suckles the child Allah Almighty writes for her a good deed and erases from her an evil one. And if she should die of her travail, she will rise on the Day of Resurrection without reckoning, for she died of her affliction."

And he said: "Whoever abandons marriage out of fear of household expense has thought ill of his Lord the Almighty, who says, *If they should be poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty."* And he said: "Whoever amasses wealth and does not marry, so that the women left unwed because of him fall into sin, their sin rests upon him." And from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع): "No two believers are joined in a lawful marriage but that a caller from heaven cries out, Allah Almighty has wedded such a one to such a one; and no two believing spouses are parted from a marriage but that a caller from heaven cries out, Allah Almighty has permitted the separation of such a one from such a one."

On what is recommended in a spouse, and from whose marriage one should turn away, it is narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, that he said: "Choose well for your seed, for the maternal uncle is one of the two who share the bed." By this he meant: do not place your seed except in purity, that the mother of the child be not of corrupt origin nor corrupt in herself. From this also he forbade that a child be suckled by the milk of a wicked woman; and if such a thing is disliked in nursing and forbidden, then in lineage it is graver still and more emphatic. He said: "Marry those who are your equals, and give your daughters in marriage to them, and choose well for your seed."

He related that Allah Almighty says: "When I wish to grant My servant the good of this world and the Hereafter, I give him a tongue that remembers Me, a humble heart, a body patient in affliction, and a believing wife who gladdens him when he looks upon her and guards herself and his wealth in his absence." And he said: "Five things are of happiness: a righteous wife, upright children, righteous companions, a man's provision in his own land, and love for the family of Muhammad, (ع) and his family."

He said: "A good woman among women is like the albino crow, and the albino crow is seldom found." By "good" here he meant the believing, chaste, beautiful woman, fitting in all her states; and rare is the woman found in such a condition, so he likened her to the albino crow, which is the rarest of birds. He said: "A woman of standing has no price, neither the good among them nor the bad. As for the good, she has no price in gold or silver or all the possessions of this world; and as for the bad, she has no price, not even of dust, for dust is better than she." And he said: "The world is but enjoyment, and the best enjoyment of the world is a righteous wife." And he said: "Of the believing man's happiness is a righteous wife, a spacious dwelling, an easy mount, and a righteous child."

He forbade that a woman be married for her wealth or her beauty alone, saying, "Her wealth makes her overbearing, and her beauty destroys her." And he said: "Take the one of religion." And he said, comparing what is most prized: "There is no horse finer than the deep black, and no woman like the daughter of one's age." And he said: "The best of your women are the women of Quraysh, the most tender toward a husband and the most devoted to a child."

He said: "Marry the one of clear, healthy complexion, for there is blessing in that. And when one of you would marry a woman, let him ask about her hair as he asks about her face, for the hair is one of the two beauties. And keep to those of shorter stature, for that is better suited to what you seek." He said: "A woman is but a necklace, so let each of you consider well with what he adorns himself." And he said: "If there is ill fortune in anything, it is in a woman, a dwelling, and a mount."

He said: "Women are four: a gathering that gathers, a spring that gives water, a war that subdues, and a galling fetter." As for the galling fetter, the people of the Age of Ignorance would shackle their captives and those they wished to torment with a thong of fresh hide stripped from a camel, turning the hair inward against the skin and binding it fast; as it dried it tightened and the skin contracted, and lice would breed within it and could not be reached because of its tightness, so the captive suffered greatly, a torment that struck without a blow. This the Prophet, (ع), made a likeness of the bad wife and what her husband endures from her. The gathering that gathers is she in whom is gathered her goodness and her purity; the spring that gives water is she who has a child in her arms and another in her womb; and the war that subdues is she of ill manners toward her husband.

He said: "Beware of marrying the foolish woman, for her companionship is an affliction and her child is a loss." And he said: "The best women of my nation are those fairest of face and lightest in dowry." And he said: "Among the blessings of a woman is that her firstborn be a girl." He said again: "Women are four: a spring that gives water, a gathering that gathers, a war that subdues, and a barren woman." And he said: "Marry the dark-complexioned woman who is fertile, and do not marry the beautiful barren woman, for I will boast of your numbers before the nations on the Day of Resurrection. The children who die young are beneath the Throne of the Most Merciful, seeking forgiveness for their parents; Ibrahim, (ع), takes them into his care, and Sarah raises them upon a mountain of musk and amber and saffron."

He said: "The best of your women is the ardent yet chaste one, ardent toward her husband and chaste from all besides him." And he said: "Marry the virgins, for they are sweetest of mouth, purest of womb, quickest of the women to learn, and most steadfast in affection." And he said: "Marry the ayyami," meaning Allah Almighty will better their character, broaden their provision, and increase their blessing. His command here is to marry the ayyami, those women who once had husbands who died or who divorced them, and it is a command of encouragement, not of obligation that may not be left, just as Allah's word, *"And marry the unmarried among you,"* is of this kind. Whoever marries one of the ayyami, desiring what Allah Almighty and His Messenger have urged, has done what he was called to; and whoever marries a virgin, there is no fault in that, for the virtue of marrying virgins has already been mentioned from the Messenger of Allah. Yet the ayyami should not be passed over out of mere preference or disdain on account of a prior marriage, for most of the wives of the Messenger of Allah, (ع), had been married before, and Allah Almighty says: *"Indeed in the Messenger of Allah you have a good example for those who hope for Allah and the Last Day."*

A Muslim should answer his Muslim brother who proposes to him, when he is satisfied with the man's religion; and he warned that if this is not done, there will be strife in the land and great corruption. He forbade a marriage sought for other than the face of Allah Almighty and chastity, and he forbade marriage undertaken for show and reputation. He said: "Whoever marries a woman for her standing or her wealth, Allah leaves him to that; but whoever marries her for her religion and her excellence, Allah Almighty grants him beauty and wealth besides." Then he recited the words of Allah Almighty: *"If they should be poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty, and Allah is all-Encompassing, Knowing."* And he said: "There is no calamity heavier upon a servant in his religion than that his Muslim kinsman should come to him and say, Marry me your daughter, and he answer, I will not, because he is wealthier than the suitor."

It is told of Ali ibn al-Husayn, (ع), that he passed by a woman in one of the districts of Makkah and was struck by her beauty and character. He inquired about her, found her without a husband, proposed to her, married her, and consummated the marriage. Afterward a man of his kin came to him and said, "O son of the Messenger of Allah, something has weighed upon me concerning your marriage to this woman that I could not bear, for you married her without asking after her lineage. I could not rest until I inquired, and I found her to be of the Banu Shayban, of the noblest of her people, and so I was gladdened and set at ease." Ali ibn al-Husayn, (ع), said, "And what was it you had feared?" The man said, "I feared she might not be your equal, and that people would speak of it." Ali ibn al-Husayn, (ع), said to him, "I had thought you of sounder judgment than this. Do you not know that Allah Almighty brought Islam and by it raised the lowly, completed the deficient, and ennobled what was base? There is no reproach upon the affair of a Muslim; reproach belongs only to the Age of Ignorance."

He, (ع), also married the freedwoman who had borne a son to a man now dead. When Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan heard of it, he wrote reproaching him, thinking he had found in it a fault, and saying, "Was there not enough for you among the women of Quraysh and the noble Arabs to keep you from the mother of another man's son?" Ali ibn al-Husayn, (ع), wrote back to him: "To proceed: Allah Almighty has by Islam raised the lowly and completed the deficient, so there is no reproach upon the affair of a Muslim; reproach belongs only to the Age of Ignorance. And the Messenger of Allah, (ع), married such a one," meaning Safiyyah bint Hayy ibn Akhtab, "and in him is a good example." Were it permissible to disdain the mothers of children, the marriage bed would be abandoned and corruption would appear in the land; and the Imams, (عليهم السلام), are the most fit to make this plain, that the people might follow their example.

Allah Almighty said: *"O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most honored of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you. Indeed, Allah is Knowing and Aware."*

On the day of the conquest of Makkah he addressed the people, praised and glorified Allah, then said: "O people, Allah Almighty has taken from you the arrogance of the Age of Ignorance and its boasting in forefathers. You are all from Adam, and Adam was from clay. The best of Allah's servants before Allah are those most fearful of Him. Arabness is not a thing born of a father; it is a tongue that speaks. Whoever is held back by his deeds, his lineage will not advance him." And every claim of blood or rancor from the Age of Ignorance is beneath his feet until the Day of Resurrection.

It is told that he gave Dubaa bint al-Zubayr ibn Abd al-Muttalib in marriage to al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad, and said that he married her to al-Miqdad only that marriage might be made humble, that the people might follow the example of the Messenger of Allah, (ع), and that they might know that the most honored of them before Allah is the most God-fearing. Al-Zubayr was the brother of Abd Allah, the father of the Prophet, (ع), by both father and mother. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: "The Messenger of Allah, (ع), married Qurayshi women to freedmen: he gave Dubaa bint al-Zubayr ibn Abd al-Muttalib to al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad, and he married Tamim al-Dari to a woman of the Banu Hashim ibn Abd Manaf."

The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said: "When one whose religion and trustworthiness are pleasing to you comes seeking marriage, then marry him; for if you do not, there will be strife in the earth and great corruption."

It is narrated from Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), that he was in Makkah when word reached him that Dawud ibn Ali had ordered the killing of al-Ma'ali ibn Hunaish, who was one of his own companions, and that he had been killed. Al-Sadiq, (ع), rose in anger, dragging his garment behind him. His son Isma'il, (ع), came to meet him and said: "O my father, where are you going? The people are defiant, and their insolence against Allah Almighty is such as you well know. Leave them be." But he struck him upon the chest and said: "Step aside from me. Were it a blazing fire, I would plunge into it." He went on until he came in upon Dawud and said: "O Dawud, you have committed a sin that Allah will never forgive you." Dawud said: "And what is that?" He said: "You have killed a believing man from among the people of Paradise."

Dawud grew angry and fell silent for a time, then said: "And you too have committed a sin that Allah Almighty will not forgive you." He said: "And what is that?" He said: "Your daughter has been given in marriage to a man from Banu Umayyah." Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), answered that in this his example was the Messenger of Allah himself, (ع), for he had given his daughter Zaynab in marriage to Abu al-As ibn Rabi'ah, and he had given Umm Kulthum in marriage to Uthman ibn Affan, and when she died he married him to Ruqayyah, both being his daughters, (ع) and his family.

Then he said: "You are the one who forbade the killing of believers unjustly." Dawud said: "I did not command that he be killed." Al-Sadiq said that al-Sarraqi had reported, "We have lost him," and that he, the killer, had said, "I have taken responsibility for him." So Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), had his companions seize the man and proceed with him to be put to death. The man cried out: "O servants of Allah, do you command me to kill men for them, and then exact retribution from me?" Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: "As for you, O enemy of Allah, you confess to the killing, and your companion has denied that he commanded you to it. Have you any proof of that?" The man said: "And who will bear witness for me against Dawud?" He said: "Then retribution from you is obligatory and must be exacted." And he delivered him over to the kinsmen of al-Ma'ali, and they put him to death.

It was said to Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع): Is it permissible to give daughters in marriage to those of opposing views? He answered that the Messenger of Allah, (ع), had given his daughters in marriage to certain men whom they knew, and that Umar had sought the hand of Ali's daughter Umm Kulthum, (ع), and Ali had refused him. So Umar went to al-Abbas and complained of this to him, and pressed the Banu Abd al-Muttalib hard. Al-Abbas came to Ali, (ع), and said: "O son of my brother, you see the situation we are in. Umar has sought her hand and has pressed us hard over your refusing him." He did not cease until Ali committed her affair to him, and al-Abbas gave her in marriage to Umar on his behalf. It is therefore better to give daughters in marriage to people of sound faith, or to those who do not stir up enmity against the family of the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family. The marriage of a believer is better than the marriage of any other, yet when there is necessity there is no harm in marrying people of differing views from among the Muslims. Likewise to marry among them is not forbidden, as marriage to polytheists is forbidden, but the meritorious and preferred course is marriage among people of sound faith, and after them the weak ones who are neither loyal nor hostile.

A man asked Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), about a sister of his wife, a believing woman of knowledge who was in Basra and had no man among her kin to oversee her affair. He said to him, (ع): "Do not give her in marriage to them. As for yourselves, there is no harm in your giving the weak women in marriage; but a Nasibiyyah, the daughter of a Nasib, no." Zurara ibn A'yan asked Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), about the like of this, and he was permitted to give the weak women in marriage, but not a Nasibiyyah, the daughter of a Nasib. And Abu Ja'far, (ع), said to Zurara ibn A'yan concerning the weak women: "Keep to the simple and weak among women." And Abu Ja'far, (ع), said: "Marry into the people of doubt, but do not give your women to them in marriage, for a woman takes from her husband's manners, and he prevails over her in the matter of his religion."

He said, (ع): "The marriage of people of knowledge, and marriage among them, is foremost and best and nearest to Allah Almighty. If that is not to be had, then the weak ones who are neither loyal nor hostile are better than the people of enmity." After them come the Muslims of the people of truce, whom the Shari'ah binds together: they marry one another and inherit from one another, and the testimony of one of them concerning another is admissible when they are upright and not suspect. As for the people of disbelief, such as Jews, Christians, and polytheists, it is not lawful for any of them to marry a Muslim woman, though their marrying one another is permissible, and Muslim men may marry their women when there is necessity and Muslim women are not to be found. Muslim women, however, are not lawful for them.

Abu Abd Allah, (ع), was asked about the saying of Allah Almighty: ﴿الزَّانِي لَا يَنْكِحُ إِلَّا زَانِيَةً أَوْ مُشْرِكَةً وَالزَّانِيَةُ لَا يَنْكِحُهَا إِلَّا زَانٍ أَوْ مُشْرِكٌ وَحُرِّمَ ذَٰلِكَ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ﴾, "The adulterer shall not marry except an adulteress or a polytheist, and the adulteress shall not marry except an adulterer or a polytheist, and that is forbidden to the believers." He said: "This was revealed concerning women known for licentiousness in the time of ignorance, who were openly hired out for adultery and had set up houses marked for that purpose. The truce came with the provision that whoever wished might return to them; among them were Habibah, al-Rabab, and Sarah, whose blood the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, declared lawful on the day of the conquest of Makkah, because she had been inciting the polytheists to fight the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family. As for a slave girl who has fallen into fornication, there is no harm in purchasing her and having relations with her, and there is likewise no harm in marrying such a woman when there is necessity, provided one is fortified and guarded against her. Yet chaste women are better and more fitting."

A man came to the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, and said: "O Messenger of Allah, I have a woman who does not turn away the hand of one who touches her, and I love her." He said: "Then keep her." It was narrated of a man who saw his wife committing adultery yet was loath to part from her: he may keep her if he wishes. (ع) was asked about a man's marrying an immoral woman, and he said: "What prevents him? But if he does so, let him fortify the door of his house." He was asked about a man who marries a slave girl born of adultery, and he said: "There is no harm in it, though to keep clear of that is dearer to me." Concerning a man who has a slave girl who has fallen into fornication, asking whether he may have relations with her, he said: "Yes, if he wishes." Concerning a man who marries a woman without full discernment, he said: "It is permissible, so long as no fault remains attached to his child." Concerning a child of adultery, whether one may have relations with her and sell her if she is a slave girl, and buy her, he said: "Yes; the forbidden does not render the lawful forbidden, nor corrupt it. Were people to turn away from the like of these and forbid their marriage, corruption in the land would multiply, and Allah may yet set right the corrupters and accept the repentance of the sinners."

Some of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام), were asked about the saying of Allah Almighty, "The adulterer shall not marry except an adulteress or a polytheist." He said: "The word marry here may mean the act of union and intercourse, or it may mean the contract and marriage. If it means union and intercourse, then it may indeed signify adultery, not lawful marriage." He said: "As for His words, except an adulterer or a polytheist, a man does not bring upon himself the wrath of Allah Almighty in this except while he is a polytheist." And the Messenger of Allah, (ع), said: "The adulterer does not commit adultery at the moment of his adultery while he is a believer."

**Mentioning the Seeking of Women's Hands in Marriage**

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and upon the Imams of his house, that he forbade a man to propose marriage over the proposal of his brother, and to bid over the bid of his brother. Some of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام), explained this and said: The meaning of the words of the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, that a man should not propose over his brother's proposal, is that this is after there has come about mutual approach, contentment, inclination, attentiveness, and agreement, such that nothing remains but the contract itself. As for what falls short of that, there is no harm in two men, or a group, proposing to a woman until she inclines to one of them and answers him; only then is it disliked for another to oppose him with a proposal. In the same way it is permissible for a man to bid on a commodity and to purchase it, and for another to bid above him, so long as the two parties to the sale have not yet reached agreement.

The Messenger of Allah, (ع), commanded selling by open auction, and people have continued to this day to outbid one another in the call and otherwise, each raising upon the other until the commodity is settled and the seller accepts a buyer; after that no further bid should be made, nor any raising upon what has been commanded. It is a matter of recommendation, on account of the benefit in it. Should a suitor propose, or a bidder raise his bid upon a commodity, after agreement and before the contract of marriage or sale is concluded, that falls under the same ruling: if one surpasses another in merit before the contract is concluded, it does not invalidate the marriage or the sale, since nothing has become binding to another by contract. And the Messenger of Allah, (ع), said: "When one of you wishes to marry a woman, let him cast his gaze upon her, for he is but a buyer." And he said: "When one of you wishes to marry a woman, there is no harm in his looking at what draws him to her."

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: I mentioned this hadith to Jabir ibn Abd Allah al-Ansari, and he said to me: "I heard it from the Messenger of Allah, (ع). I sought the hand of a young woman from among the women of the Ansar, and I concealed myself for her behind a wall of her father's garden, and I looked at her and saw what I wished to see and what I had not meant to see, and she pleased me, so I married her, and she was the best of women."

Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: There is no harm for one who wishes to marry a woman in looking at her; but he should not look at a slave girl except with her master's permission, meaning that the master may not wish to sell his slave girl or to offer her. The command from the Messenger of Allah, (ع), permitting a man to look at the woman he intends to marry, extends the permission of that gaze, once he has come to know that they will give her to him and will not refuse him, when they have agreed and nothing remains but the marriage contract. He should look at her only to acquaint himself with her state, not out of desire to look at her or to take pleasure in her, nor should he deliberately direct his gaze toward her private parts or toward what it is not fitting to look upon. It is not lawful for him to look with the eye of contemplation at women who are not his, that is, women other than his wives, those forbidden to him by blood, and those his right hand possesses, whether they be bareheaded or unveiled, for Allah says, exalted be He, ﴿قُل لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ يَغُضُّوا مِنْ أَبْصَارِهِمْ﴾, "Say to the believing men that they lower their gaze."

Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), was asked about a man past whom a woman passes, and he looks at her from behind. He said: "Would it please one of you that men should look upon his womenfolk and his kinswomen? Be content for people with what you are content with for yourselves." And he said, (ع), concerning the saying of Allah Almighty in the story of Moses, recounting the words of the woman, ﴿قَالَتْ إِحْدَاهُمَا يَا أَبَتِ اسْتَأْجِرْهُ إِنَّ خَيْرَ مَنِ اسْتَأْجَرْتَ الْقَوِيُّ الْأَمِينُ﴾, "One of the two women said: O my father, hire him; indeed, the best one you can hire is the strong and the trustworthy," that she meant Moses, (ع) and his family. Her father said to her: "As for your saying that he is strong, that is from what you saw of his lifting the rock from the well; but how did you come to know his trustworthiness?" She said: "When he set out with me to come to you, I wished to walk before him to show him the road, but he came forward and said: Walk behind me and direct me to the road, for we are a people who do not look upon the backs of women."

It is narrated from Ali, (ع), that he said: I heard the Messenger of Allah, (ع), when looking at women was mentioned, and he said: "The first glance is for you," meaning the glance that falls unawares, "and the second is against you, and the third glance is a poisoned arrow from the arrows of Iblis. Whoever forsakes such glances for the sake of Allah Almighty, and for no other, Allah Almighty will requite him with a faith whose sweetness he will taste." And Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: "Do those who look upon the forms of women not fear that they may be afflicted with the like in their own wives?"

It is narrated from Ali, (ع), that a man came to the Messenger of Allah, (ع), and said: "O Messenger of Allah, should I ask my mother's leave before entering upon her?" He said: "Yes." He said: "And why, O Messenger of Allah, (ع)?" He said: "Would it please you to see her unclothed?" He said: "No." He said: "Then ask her leave." He said: "O Messenger of Allah, (ع), my sister uncovers her hair before me." He said: "No." He said: "Why?" He said: "I fear that, should she reveal one of her charms before you, Satan may invite you toward her." And he said, (ع) and his family: "When one of you would kiss a woman who is a mahram to him and has come of age, a mother, a sister, an aunt, or any who is forbidden to him in marriage, let him kiss her upon the head and between the eyes, or let him refrain from her cheeks and her mouth."

Concerning Fatimah, (ع): a blind man sought leave to enter upon her, and she veiled herself from him. It was said to her: "He is blind, his sight is wholly gone; he sees nothing of her." She said: "Even if he does not see me, I see him, and he perceives the voice and senses the scent."

The Messenger of Allah, (ع), asked Ali, (ع), and a company that was before him, what is best for women, and they did not know what to say. Ali, (ع), withdrew to Fatimah, (ع), and mentioned this to her, and she said: "What is best for women is that they should not see men and that men should not see them." Ali, (ع), went to the Messenger of Allah, (ع), and said this to him. He said: "Did you arrive at this of yourself, or did another say it?" He said: "O Messenger of Allah, (ع), it was Fatimah who said it." The Messenger of Allah, (ع), was pleased by it and said: "Fatimah is but a part of me."

### The Hunt and the Knife: Game, Slaughter, and Sacrifice

The Messenger of Allah, (ع), taught that whoever desires to marry should not approach the matter heedlessly, but should begin by turning to Allah, praising Him and extolling Him, and then asking Him for the kind of companion that makes a household whole. He said that such a person should pray:

"O Allah, grant me a wife who is affectionate and fruitful, grateful and jealous in her honor. If I do good, she thanks me; if I do wrong, she forgives me; if I remember Allah, she helps me; if I forget, she reminds me; if I go out from her, she guards herself; if I come in to her, she gladdens me; if I command her, she obeys me; if I swear by her, she fulfills my oath; if she becomes angry, You satisfy me. O Lord of majesty and honor, grant me what I have asked for, and what You have apportioned and given."

And he made a clear promise concerning this supplication. The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said that whoever says these words and acts upon them, Allah Almighty will give him what he has asked.

He also taught that marriage is not a thing to be entered without its proper preliminaries. The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said that every marriage in which there is no engagement is like a leprous hand.

This same care for the right beginning was kept by his descendants. Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn Ali, (عليهم السلام), when he gave a woman in marriage or when a marriage was contracted in his presence, would open with words of praise and witness, saying: "Praise be to Allah. We praise Him and seek refuge with Him from the evils of ourselves and the evils of our deeds. Whoever Allah guides, none can mislead, and whoever He leaves to stray, none can guide. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and His messenger."

Then he would recite the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ اتَّقُوا رَبَّكُمُ الَّذِي خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا رِجَالًا كَثِيرًا وَنِسَاءً ۚ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ الَّذِي تَسَاءَلُونَ بِهِ وَالْأَرْحَامَ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلَيْكُمْ رَقِيبًا ﴾

And he would recite the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ حَقَّ تُقَاتِهِ وَلَا تَمُوتُنَّ إِلَّا وَأَنتُم مُّسْلِمُونَ ﴾

And he would recite the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَقُولُوا قَوْلًا سَدِيدًا ۝ يُصْلِحْ لَكُمْ أَعْمَالَكُمْ وَيَغْفِرْ لَكُمْ ذُنُوبَكُمْ ۚ وَمَن يُطِعِ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ فَقَدْ فَازَ فَوْزًا عَظِيمًا ﴾

Once the marriage has been thus commended and the standing of each party made known to the other, the contract is concluded. The one who performs it praises Allah for what He deserves, prays upon the Prophet ((ع) and his family), recalls the virtue of marriage and what is desired in it, and only then ties the knot. It is fitting that the husband make known to his prospective companion whatever he can of his situation, so that she enters the union with knowledge of his circumstances. And if the contract is not preceded by a formal engagement, it remains permissible, though the khutbah is the better course.

The scholars ((عليهم السلام)) forbade a formal proposal during the iddah, yet they permitted a discreet hint at marriage, in keeping with the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ وَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِيمَا عَرَّضْتُم بِهِ مِنْ خِطْبَةِ النِّسَاءِ أَوْ أَكْنَنتُمْ فِي أَنفُسِكُمْ ۚ عَلِمَ اللَّهُ أَنَّكُمْ سَتَذْكُرُونَهُنَّ وَلَٰكِن لَّا تُوَاعِدُوهُنَّ سِرًّا إِلَّا أَن تَقُولُوا قَوْلًا مَّعْرُوفًا ۚ وَلَا تَعْزِمُوا عُقْدَةَ النِّكَاحِ حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ الْكِتَابُ أَجَلَهُ ﴾

*There is no blame upon you for what you hint at regarding a proposal to women, or what you conceal within yourselves. Allah knows that you will remember them, but do not make a secret commitment with them except in the form of honorable speech. And do not resolve the marriage contract until the prescribed term reaches its end.* (al-Baqarah: 235)

Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said concerning the words of Allah Almighty, "do not make a secret commitment with them": this refers to a secret pledge of marriage, while exposing them gently to the matter and speaking goodly words is the permitted hinting at the khutbah.

It was narrated from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((عليهم السلام)) that he entered upon Sakina bint Handhalah, who had been separated from a cousin of hers who had married her and then died. Abu Ja'far greeted her and said, "How have you been, O daughter of Handhalah?" She said, "May I be your ransom, O son of the Messenger of Allah." He said, "You know my kinship to the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family), and my standing with Ali ((ع)), and my right in Islam, and my honor among the Arabs." She said, "May Allah forgive you, O Abu Ja'far. You propose to me while I am in my iddah!" He said, "I have done no such thing. I have only informed you of my status and my standing. The Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) once entered upon Umm Salamah, the daughter of Umayyah ibn al-Mughirah al-Makhzumiyyah, while she was in her iddah from Abu Salamah, who was her cousin. He continued to mention his standing to her until the impression of the mat showed upon his palm from the force with which he leaned upon his hand, and yet that was not a formal proposal." They said ((عليهم السلام)): it is recommended for one who wishes to marry to acquaint his prospective companion with his situation, so that she may know his circumstances.

It was related that when the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) proposed to her, Umm Salamah ((رض)) sent word to him saying, "O Messenger of Allah, I am an elderly woman, I have children, and I am a woman of great jealousy, and I fear there may be something in me of which you are unaware." The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) sent back to her, "As for your age, you have mentioned it, and I am older than you. As for your children, they shall be among the children of the Messenger of Allah. And as for your jealousy, I shall pray to Allah Almighty to remove it from you." And he married her ((ع) and his family).

**The Wedding Feast and the Consummation of Marriage**

We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah (may the prayers of Allah be upon him and upon his family and upon them all) that the feast is of four kinds: the walimah, the khurs, the i'dhar, and the wakirah. The walimah is the food prepared when a man enters upon his wife. The khurs is the food of the aqiqah prepared for the newborn on the seventh day, which has already been mentioned along with what is obligatory in it. The i'dhar is the food prepared at the circumcision of a boy. The wakirah is when a man returns from a journey, prepares food, and invites people to it. He said ((ع) and his family): it is among the Sunnah of the Messengers to provide food upon marriage. The Prophet ((ع) and his family) held a wedding feast for his wives when he consummated marriage with them, and at some of his feasts he served al-hays, a food made of dates, ghee, and condensed milk. He said ((ع)) to one of his companions who had married, "Give a feast, even if it be only with a sheep." And he said ((ع) and his family), "The feast of the first day is a duty, the feast of the second day is a kindness known among people, and what is beyond that is ostentation and reputation-seeking." Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (may the prayers of Allah be upon them) was invited to a feast on the first day and he answered it; he was invited on the second day and he answered it; then a messenger came to him on the third day to invite him, and he ordered the messenger turned away until he was out of sight.

It was narrated from the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) that he passed by Banu Zurayq and heard the sound of instruments and singing, and he asked, "What is this?" They said, "O Messenger of Allah, so-and-so has married so-and-so." He said, "He has completed his religion. This is marriage, not a clandestine affair, and there is no marriage in secret, until its smoke is seen and the sound of the tambourine is heard." And he said ((ع) and his family), "What distinguishes marriage from fornication is the beating of the tambourine." It was also narrated from him ((ع) and his family) that on the night Fatimah was brought to Ali ((عليهم السلام)) he heard the beat of the tambourine, and he asked, "What is this?" Umm Salamah said, "O Messenger of Allah, this is Asma bint Umays beating the tambourine for Fatimah, lest she say within herself, My mother has died and there is no one to rejoice with me." The Messenger of Allah raised his hands to the heavens and said, "O Allah, bring joy to Asma bint Umays as she has gladdened the daughter of Your Prophet." Then he called her and said, "O Asma, what do you say when you beat the tambourine?" She said, "O Messenger of Allah, by Allah I do not know what to say; I only wished to gladden her." He said, "Then do not say anything indecent, but say: We have come to you, we have come to you; so greet us and we will greet you."

This kind of speech and its like, together with the beating of the tambourine and what resembles it, is what permission has been granted for at weddings and banquets. As for other speech that carries vulgarity, and the singing that accompanies it, and the instruments of diversion and the entrancing flutes that the people of frivolity take up, that is disliked.

Al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((عليهم السلام)) was asked about such things, and he recited the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ وَمَا خَلَقْنَا السَّمَاءَ وَالْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا لَاعِبِينَ ۝ لَوْ أَرَدْنَا أَن نَّتَّخِذَ لَهْوًا لَّاتَّخَذْنَاهُ مِن لَّدُنَّا إِن كُنَّا فَاعِلِينَ ۝ بَلْ نَقْذِفُ بِالْحَقِّ عَلَى الْبَاطِلِ فَيَدْمَغُهُ فَإِذَا هُوَ زَاهِقٌ ۚ وَلَكُمُ الْوَيْلُ مِمَّا تَصِفُونَ ﴾

*We did not create the heaven and the earth and what is between them in play. Had We intended to take a pastime, We would have taken it from near Us, were We to do so. Rather, We hurl the truth against falsehood, and it crushes it, and at once it vanishes. And woe to you for what you describe.* (al-Anbiya: 16–18)

It was narrated from him that he said, "Singing is a gathering upon which Allah Almighty does not look." It was narrated from him that he was asked about the flute, and he said, "The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said: there are two voices that Allah Almighty detests: a wailing at the time of calamity, and a flute at the time of blessing." And it was narrated from him regarding the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ فَاجْتَنِبُوا الرِّجْسَ مِنَ الْأَوْثَانِ وَاجْتَنِبُوا قَوْلَ الزُّورِ ﴾

*So avoid the abomination of idols, and avoid the false statement* (al-Hajj: 30)

that "the abomination of idols" includes chess, and that "the false statement" includes singing. He said concerning the words of Allah Almighty, ﴿ وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَن يَشْتَرِي لَهْوَ الْحَدِيثِ ﴾ *And among the people is he who purchases idle talk* (Luqman: 6), that this too refers to singing.

One of his companions said, "I was in Madinah and came upon a place where there was singing, so I sat and listened. The next day I entered upon Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((عليهم السلام)), and when I sat before him he began by reciting the words of Allah, exalted be His mention:

﴿ إِنَّ السَّمْعَ وَالْبَصَرَ وَالْفُؤَادَ كُلُّ أُولَٰئِكَ كَانَ عَنْهُ مَسْئُولًا ﴾

*Indeed, the hearing, the sight, and the heart: about all those one will be questioned.* (al-Isra: 36)

Then he looked at me and said, You will be asked of your hearing what it heard, of your heart what it understood, and of your sight what it beheld."

It was narrated from him that he said, "The sale of singing is not lawful, nor its purchase; listening to it is hypocrisy, and teaching it is disbelief." It was also narrated from him that one day some of his Shi'a, who had come as a delegation from various towns, entered upon him, and he asked them, "Where have you lodged?" They said, "O son of the Messenger of Allah, we lodged in a house, and when we drew near to the place we heard singing within it, so we sought another lodging but could not find one." He said, "Even so, be noble: keep your hearing far from listening to it and lending it your ear, for Allah, exalted is the One who speaks, says:

﴿ وَالَّذِينَ لَا يَشْهَدُونَ الزُّورَ وَإِذَا مَرُّوا بِاللَّغْوِ مَرُّوا كِرَامًا ﴾

*And those who do not bear witness to falsehood, and when they pass by idle talk, they pass by with dignity.* (al-Furqan: 72)"

It was narrated from him that he said, "My father ((ع)) passed by me one day when I was a boy, and I had stopped to listen to two pipers. He took me by the hand and said, Step away; do not be of those who gloated over Adam ((ع)). I said, How is that, O my father? He said, All the amusement, play, and singing that you see was devised by Iblis to gloat over Adam ((ع)) when he was expelled from Paradise." He said ((ع)), "The house in which singing takes place is a house in which no calamity is met with comfort, no supplication is answered, and the angel does not enter." It was narrated from him that he said, "Singing causes hypocrisy to grow in the heart as water causes the date palm to bring forth its spathe." And it was narrated from him that he said, "Whoever has the lute played in his house for forty days, Allah sets over him a devil called al-Anqad, who leaves no limb of his without sitting upon it; and when he is thus afflicted, his sense of shame is stripped from him, and he no longer cares what he says or what is said to him."

It was mentioned to him ((ع)) that a man of his Shi'a had entered upon someone he knew, and that man brought out a slave-girl of his who played and sang, and she sang and played before him. Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((عليهم السلام)) said, "Did he not fear that the command of Allah Almighty might come upon him while he was in such a state, in a gathering upon which Allah does not look?" It was also narrated from him that a man asked him about singing, and he said to him, "Tell me: if Allah Almighty separates truth from falsehood, where do you see singing falling?" The man said, "O son of the Messenger of Allah, I see it only with falsehood." He said, "Then I reckon you have judged it. Leave it, for there is no good in it, and it is among that for which Allah Almighty has promised the Fire." Then he recited the words of Allah Almighty, ﴿ وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَن يَشْتَرِي لَهْوَ الْحَدِيثِ ﴾ *And among the people is he who purchases idle talk* (Luqman: 6). It was also narrated from him that singing bequeaths hypocrisy and brings on poverty.

And he said ((ع)) that the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) forbade his community al-zafn, the flute, the drums, and the reed-pipes; and that a man who had broken a lute was once brought before Ali ((ع)), and Ali approved his deed and ruled that there was no liability for breaking the lute. All of this points to the prohibition of frivolity and of listening to singing, flutes, lyres, and the like that are taken up for diversion. As for what is used in war, in the marshaling and parading of troops, and the like, such as what people raise up before the Imams and the commanders of armies by beating drums and shields and by blowing horns and pipes that are not taken for enjoyment and amusement, that is not among what was forbidden, nor of the kind whose prohibition has just been mentioned. Rather it is among what is recommended in its proper place. For it has come from the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) that he passed by a company of Abyssinians beating their drums, and he said, "Carry on, O sons of Arfidah, that the Jews may know there is breadth in our religion."

If someone should ask, "Why then do we hear and see, in the lands where the Imams hold authority (may the prayers of Allah be upon them), the very instruments of diversion that they have forbidden?" the answer is that this is no greater than the indecencies that Allah Almighty has forbidden and made unlawful, which people nonetheless commit in their homes, behind their doors and their curtains. Allah has commanded that the prescribed penalties be carried out for those indecencies upon which He has commanded such penalties, in what appears openly and is witnessed by witnesses. As for what remains hidden, He alone, glorified be He, holds the knowledge of it and has reserved to Himself both its punishment and its pardon, advancing of that what He wills and deferring what He defers. It is well known and widely established among all people that the Imams ((عليهم السلام)) denounced these things upon those who did them and withheld any license for them, holding that this is not a matter for which the hadd is due, but rather one calling for denunciation and prohibition.

It was narrated from the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) that he said, "Bring your brides in by night, and give the feast by day." And it was narrated from him ((ع)) that he said, "There is no staying awake save in three: a vigil with the Quran, the seeking of knowledge, or the bringing of a bride to her husband." It was also narrated from him ((ع)) that a man should make himself ready for his wife as he would wish her to make herself ready for him; Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((عليهم السلام)) said that this means cleanliness.

He said: when a man's wife is brought to him and she enters upon him, let him pray two rak'ahs, then place his hand upon her forelock and say, "O Allah, bless me in my wife and bless her in me; in whatever You have joined between us, join us in goodness, felicity, and blessing; and if You decree a parting, make it a parting toward every good." Then, when he sits beside her, let him wipe her forelock and say, "Praise be to Allah, who guided my straying, enriched my poverty, raised up my burden, gave might to my lowliness, sheltered my dependents, gave a spouse to my solitude, bore up my step, served my need, brought company to my loneliness, and lifted my standing; a praise wholesome, abundant, and blessed, for what You have granted, O Lord, and for what You have apportioned, and for what You have honored."

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((عليهم السلام)) said: the woman too is commanded to be in a state of ablution on the night she is brought as a bride; when her husband prays, she prays, and when he supplicates, she says amen. He added that one should increase in supplication, sending prayers upon the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) at the beginning, the middle, and the end, and saying within it, "O Allah, grant me her affection, her warmth, her contentment with me and my contentment with her, and join us in the best of unions and the gentlest of bonds, for You love the lawful and hate the unlawful."

And Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((عليهم السلام)) said: when a man wishes to come to his wife, let him say, "O Allah, if You decree from me this day a child, then make him purely Yours, and grant the devil no share in him, no portion and no part; indeed You are powerful over all things." It was narrated from the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) that he said, "When one of you comes to his wife, let him not hasten her," and that when he is with her, let him deal kindly and truly with her. And he ((ع) and his family) forbade that a man be intimate with a free woman while another woman is in the room with her. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)) said there is no harm in that with a bondwoman, and likewise said Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)). And it was narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he said it does not befit a man to be intimate with his wife

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (عليهم السلام), said that when a man wishes to come to his wife, let him say: «اللهم إن قضيت مني اليوم خلقاً فاجعله لكَ خالصاً، ولا تجعل للشيطان فيه شركاً ولا حظاً ولا نصيباً، إنك على كل شيء قدير» "O Allah, if You decree from me today a creation, make it purely for You, and grant the devil in it no share, no portion, and no part, for You are able to do all things." And from the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, it was related that he said: «إذا أتى أحدكم امرأته فلا يعجلها، وإذا واقعها فليصدقها» "When one of you comes to his wife, let him not hasten her, and when he is with her, let him deal truthfully with her."

He, (ع) and his family, permitted a man to come to a free wife while another wife is present in the house. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, said there is no objection to this in the case of a bondwoman, and likewise said Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع). From Ali, (ع), it was related that a man should not come to his wife while facing the qibla. He also said that to gaze during intercourse brings on blindness. The Prophet, (ع) and his family, and the Imams of his progeny, would turn the face away from any animal being mounted upon the road, and he would avert his face, (ع).

He, (ع), named a hidden form of infanticide, الوأد الخفي: that a man comes to a woman, and when he senses the issuing of the fluid he withdraws from her and spills it elsewhere. Of this he said, "So do not do that." He said that the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, forbade withdrawal from a free woman except with her leave, and from a bondwoman taken in marriage except with the leave of her master. Ali, (ع), said that a man may withdraw from his own bondwoman as he wishes, and likewise said Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (عليهم السلام).

From Ali, (ع), by way of Ja'far ibn Muhammad: a man should not lie between two free women, though he may lie between two bondwomen; and he should not look upon the awrah of a free woman, nor expose his own awrah to her. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said that intercourse is disliked from the breaking of dawn until sunrise, and from the setting of the sun until the fading of twilight; and on the night in which the moon is eclipsed, and on the day in which the sun is eclipsed; and on the day and night in which the earth quakes; and when the yellow, the black, and the red winds blow.

He, (ع), said: The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, passed a night with one of his wives on a night in which the moon was eclipsed, and nothing passed between him and her. It was said, "O Messenger of Allah, what is this coldness?" He said, "It is no coldness, but this was a sign, and I was loath to take my pleasure on such a night, that I might not be among those whom Allah, mighty and majestic, has rebuked in His Book where He says: «وَإِن يَرَوْا كِسْفًا مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ سَاقِطًا يَقُولُوا سَحَابٌ مَّرْكُومٌ» And were they to see a fragment falling from the sky, they would say: a heaped-up cloud." Then Abu Ja'far, may Allah's blessings be upon him, said: By Him who sent Muhammad with prophethood, singled him out with the message, and honored him with nobility, none of you comes to his wife at any of these disliked times and is then granted offspring in whom he sees the delight of his eye.

From Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him: a slave girl under nine years of age is not to be approached; if a man does so and she is harmed by it, he is liable. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family, said: If a man marries a young girl, he does not enter upon her until she completes nine years from the day of her birth.

From Ali, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, it was related that he disliked the coming to women in their hinder parts, and he used to forbid it, citing the words of Allah, mighty and majestic, that one may come to them only «في الفروج من قبل أو دبر» in the place of tilth, whether from before or from behind. And from the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, it was related that he said: «لا يخلو رجل بامرأة غير ذات محرم منه إلا كان الشيطان ثالثهما» "No man is alone with a woman who is not his mahram but that the devil is the third of them." He forbade conversing with women other than one's wives and one's mahrams. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's blessings be upon him, said: «حديث النساء يورث النسيان ويزيغ القلب وهو من مصائد الشيطان» "Idle talk with women breeds forgetfulness and makes the heart swerve, and it is among the snares of the devil."

The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: «اتقوا الله في النساء، فإنهن عيٌّ وعورة، وإنكم استحللتموهن بأمانة الله» "Fear Allah concerning women, for they are weak of speech and are a thing to be covered, and you have made them lawful to you by the trust of Allah." He said: They have their weakness, so treat their weakness with restraint of speech, and shelter that which is to be covered within the homes; do not lodge them in the upper rooms, do not teach them to write, but teach them Surat al-Nur. And from him, (ع) and his family, he said: «نِعم شغل المرأة المؤمنة المغزل» "How excellent an occupation for a believing woman is the spindle." From him also, (ع) and his family, when he took the pledge from women he would take upon them that they would not speak with men other than their husbands and their mahrams.

It was related from him, (ع) and his family, that he forbade women to look upon men, and that a woman should not leave her house without her husband's leave, not even for the bath save out of necessity. And it was related that he said one day to his companions, "What is the woman?" They said, "An awrah, O Messenger of Allah." He said, "Yes." Then he said, "And when is she nearest to her Lord?" They had no answer to give. Ali mentioned this to Fatimah, may Allah's blessings be upon her, and she said, "Return to him and say: O Messenger of Allah, the nearest a woman is to her Lord is when she keeps to the innermost of her house." He went and informed the Prophet, (ع) and his family, who said, "This is from you, O Ali." He said, "No, O Messenger of Allah, rather it was Fatimah who said it." The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said, "She has spoken truly; «إنها بضعة مني» indeed she is a part of me."

From the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family: he forbade a woman to walk unclothed before her husband, and forbade the man to be unclothed before her. He forbade women to comb their hair with wine or to apply its dregs, and he forbade women to imitate men and men to imitate women, and he cursed whoever did so. From him, (ع): "Any woman who perfumes herself, let her not leave her house, nor attend the prayer with us." And from him: he forbade women, when they go out behind men, to walk in the middle of the road with them, and he said, "Women have no portion of the middle of the road." He forbade, (ع), that a woman should wear, when she goes out, a garment that draws notice, or adorn herself with what makes a sound, or strike with her foot so that the chime of her anklet is heard. He forbade women from open display, from wailing, and from raising the voice except out of necessity, and from conversing with men, and from passing the night away from their homes.

He forbade, (ع), the giving of greeting to women. And from him, may Allah's blessings be upon him, he said that a man should not touch a man unless there is a garment between them, nor a woman a woman unless there is a garment between them. From him, (ع) and his family: a woman came to him and said, "O Messenger of Allah, I have a husband who is harsh of nature, and I have worked something to turn his affection toward me." The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said to her, "Fie upon you! You have sullied your religion, and the angels of heaven and the angels of earth have cursed you." From him, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, he said: «لو كنت آمراً أحداً أن يسجد لأحد لأمرت المرأة أن تسجد لزوجها» "Had I been commanding anyone to prostrate to another, I would have commanded the woman to prostrate to her husband." He forbade a woman to deny her husband herself when he called her, and to deny him her help and her work. He said, "The best thing with which a woman adorns herself is the fear of Allah and obedience to her husband."

He said, (ع) and his family: When a woman knows her Lord and believes in Him and in His Messenger, and acknowledges the merit of the People of the House of her Prophet, and prays the five prayers, and fasts the month of Ramadan, and guards her chastity, and obeys her husband, she enters by whichever of the gates of Paradise she wishes. From him, (ع): a woman sent to him saying, "O Messenger of Allah, my husband has ordered me not to go out, near or far, until he returns from his journey; and my father is in the market. Shall I go out to him?" The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said to her, "Sit in your house and obey your husband." So she did, and her father died. The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, sent to her saying, "Truly Allah, mighty and majestic, has forgiven your father on account of your obedience to your husband."

A woman asked him, may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family, "O Messenger of Allah, what is the right of the husband over the wife?" He said, "She must not give in charity from his house except by his leave, nor deny him herself even upon the saddle's back, nor fast a voluntary day except by his leave, nor leave her house except by his leave; and if she goes out without his leave, the angels of heaven, the angels of earth, the angels of mercy, and the angels of wrath curse her until she returns." She said, "O Messenger of Allah, who has the greatest right over a man?" He said, "His parents." "And who has the greatest right over a woman?" He said, "Her husband." She said, "O Messenger of Allah, then have I no right over him to match what he has over me?" He said, "No, not even one in a hundred." And it was related that he, (ع), assigned to his daughter Fatimah, may Allah's blessings be upon her, the service of what was within the house, and assigned to Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, the service of what was outside it.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's blessings be upon him, was asked about the right of the woman over her husband, the fulfilling of which makes him a doer of good. He said: He feeds her and clothes her, and when she wrongs him he pardons her. He said: My father, may Allah's blessings be upon him, had a wife who used to vex him, and he would forgive her and would say: The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: «المرأة كالضلع، إن تركتها انتفعت بها، وإن أقمتها كسرتها» "The woman is like a rib: if you leave it be, you take benefit from it, and if you try to straighten it, you break it." It was said to him, "But the woman is jealous over her husband." He said, "That is love."

He was asked, (ع), about a man who turns away from his wife because worship, fasting, and prayer occupy him from her. He said: The wife of Uthman ibn Maz'un came to the Messenger of Allah, (ع), and said, "O Messenger of Allah, Uthman fasts the day and stands the night in prayer." He understood, (ع) and his family, what she intended, and he went out to the mosque, where he found Uthman ibn Maz'un standing in prayer, and he said, "O Uthman, Allah, mighty and majestic, did not send me with monasticism; He sent me with the upright way. Whoever loves my nature, let him follow my Sunnah, and among my Sunnah is marriage." And the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, forbade the striking of women where it is not warranted.

From Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him: a man of the Ansar came to the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, with his daughter and said, "O Messenger of Allah, her husband has struck her; grant her retaliation against him." The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, granted her that. Then Allah, mighty and majestic, sent down: «الرِّجَالُ قَوَّامُونَ عَلَى النِّسَاءِ بِمَا فَضَّلَ اللَّهُ بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ وَبِمَا أَنفَقُوا مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ ۚ فَالصَّالِحَاتُ قَانِتَاتٌ حَافِظَاتٌ لِّلْغَيْبِ بِمَا حَفِظَ اللَّهُ ۚ وَاللَّاتِي تَخَافُونَ نُشُوزَهُنَّ فَعِظُوهُنَّ وَاهْجُرُوهُنَّ فِي الْمَضَاجِعِ وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ ۖ فَإِنْ أَطَعْنَكُمْ فَلَا تَبْغُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ سَبِيلًا ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيًّا كَبِيرًا» "Men are the protectors and maintainers of women, because Allah has favored some of them over others, and because they spend of their wealth. So the righteous women are devoutly obedient, guarding in the husband's absence what Allah would have them guard. As for those from whom you fear discord, admonish them, then leave them apart in their beds, then strike them. But if they obey you, seek no way against them. Surely Allah is Most High, Most Great." (al-Nisa: 34) And the Messenger of Allah, (ع), then said: «أردت أمراً وأراد الله غيره» "I intended one thing, and Allah intended another."

From him, (ع), he said that jealousy is a part of faith, and a careless yielding is a part of hypocrisy, meaning jealousy in what is forbidden, and the yielding that brings men and women together for that. In another report from Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, forbade al-madhal, which is the abandonment of due jealousy. Its root is from a man's making known his secret, that is, exposing it, and from this is taken a man's exposing of his womenfolk to other men. As for jealousy in what is lawful, it is disliked and forbidden. Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, said: There is no jealousy in the lawful, in light of the saying of the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, on the night he brought Fatimah, may Allah's blessings be upon her, in to her husband, after she had come to be his: «لا تُحدثا شيئاً حتى آتيكما» "Do not do anything until I come to you." He said this to them after they had lain down. So Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, held back, and the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, entered and prayed for them and blessed them, and then departed.

From the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, he said: «كُتب الجهاد على رجال أمتي والغيرة على نسائها، فمن صبرت منهن واحتسبت أعطاها الله أجر شهيد» "Striving has been prescribed upon the men of my community, and jealousy upon its women; so whichever of them is patient and seeks her reward, Allah, mighty and majestic, grants her the reward of a martyr." From him, (ع), he mentioned women and what they ask of men, and said: They burden men, when they deck themselves in gold and wear silk; they burden the rich and wear out the poor. And from Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, he said: Whoever obeys his wife in four things, Allah casts him upon his face into the Fire: that he obey her in going to wedding feasts, to gatherings of wailing, to visitings, and to the bathhouses.

**On Marriage Through Guardians**

We have related from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family: «لا نكاح إلا بوليٍّ وشاهدَي عدل» "There is no marriage save with a guardian and two upright witnesses." And he forbade that a woman be given in marriage until she is consulted. From Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, he said: None of you should give his daughter in marriage until he consults her, for she knows best her own self; if she keeps silent, or weeps, or laughs, that is her leave, and if she refuses, he does not marry her off. The arranging by fathers of the marriage of their minor sons and daughters is permissible, and they have no right of dissolution when they come of age; and when an agent is authorized to contract a marriage, it is valid. From him, may Allah's blessings be upon him, he said: If a Muslim woman, being of age, authorizes her Christian father to give her in marriage, and he does so, the marriage is valid; but if he gives her in marriage while she is a child, it is not valid, for a disbeliever has no guardianship over a Muslim.

When a woman entrusts two guardians and delegates the matter to both of them, and each of them marries her to a different man, the marriage belongs to the first of the two, provided that the first was unaware of the other's contract. If the two contracts are concluded at one and the same time, one of them is annulled and the matter is begun anew thereafter. From Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), and from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), it is related that they said: A paternal grandfather may contract the marriage of his son's minor daughter just as the father contracts it, and he has the prior right in this, even if the two of them should contract at the same moment. All of them are bound by the first contract concluded among them, even if the one whose contract is dissolved was unaware of the other and only learns of it afterward, in which case the matter is resumed.

From Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), it is related that he said: Whatever testimony is admissible in financial matters is admissible in marriage, and the testimony of women and of slaves is accepted in it. From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع): He was asked about two witnesses who testified in the marriage of a woman that her father had given her in marriage while she was unwilling, and another witness testified that he had given her in marriage with her consent. He said: The marriage is valid with her consent. He was also asked about two witnesses, one of whom testified that the dowry was one thousand and the other that it was two thousand. He said: If she claims the greater amount, she takes an oath together with her witness, and it is decreed for her. If one of them testifies that her father gave her in marriage while she was a child, and the other testifies that he gave her in marriage when she was of age and without her consent, the testimony is void.

**Mentioning the Witnesses in Marriage**

I have mentioned in what came before this chapter that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said: There is no marriage save with a guardian and two witnesses. This is what is commanded, and upon it action is taken, and by it the marriage is established. It is related from Ali, (ع), that he said: A marriage is not valid except with a guardian and witnesses. Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdullah, (عليهم السلام), said: Witnesses were appointed in marriage only for the sake of the child, so that it may be known who his father is, and as a safeguard against either of the spouses denying the marriage, and out of caution before the authorities, lest the two be charged with being a man and a woman united unlawfully. For this reason, and for whatever resembles it, the seeking of witnesses in marriage is obligatory. As for what lies between a person and Allah, Mighty and Majestic, there is nothing upon him if he does not call witnesses, for Allah, Mighty and Majestic, mentioned witnessing only in the matter of divorce and did not mention it in the matter of marriage. Yet witnesses in marriage cannot be dispensed with, because through them the marriage is established, lineage is confirmed, and doubt is removed, and upright persons bear witness to it. Among the witnesses, women and slaves are admissible, provided they are upright.

**Mentioning the Dowries**

We narrate concerning the Commander of the Believers, (ع). Allah, exalted be the One who speaks, said: ﴿ وَآتُوا النِّسَاءَ صَدُقَاتِهِنَّ نِحْلَةً ﴾. Ali, (ع), said concerning the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, ﴿ وَآتُوا النِّسَاءَ صَدُقَاتِهِنَّ نِحْلَةً ﴾: Allah, Mighty and Majestic, is saying, give them the gift by which you have made their private parts lawful to you. So whoever wrongs a woman in her dowry has made her private part lawful to himself through fornication. And the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said: Allah, Mighty and Majestic, forgives every sin save the sin of a man who has defrauded a hired laborer of his wage, or a woman of her dowry, or who has sold a free person.

It is related from him, (ع), that he said: The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, did not marry any of his wives, nor did he give any of his daughters in marriage, for more than twelve uqiyyahs and a nashsh of silver. An uqiyyah is forty dirhams, and a nashsh is half an uqiyyah, so that is five hundred dirhams in all, together with a woven cloak and a bed of ram's hide. This is the dowry of the Sunnah. There is no fixed limit set upon dowries, and whatever is named, be it little or much, the marriage is contracted upon it. It is recommended that it be made easy. Ali, (ع), said: Among the blessings of a woman is the ease of her marriage and the ease of her womb, so do not be excessive in the dowries of women, for that breeds enmity.

He, (ع), said: A man came to the Prophet, may Allah bless him and his family, and said: O Messenger of Allah, I wish to marry a certain woman. He said: Do you have anything to give her? The man said: No, by Allah, I have nothing. So he looked at a ring upon his hand and said: This ring that is on your hand. The man said: Yes, O Messenger of Allah. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said: Then you have married her with this ring of yours. So ease in marriage is a merit, as has been related, and there is no harm in going beyond it either. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, says: ﴿ وَآتَيْتُمْ إِحْدَاهُنَّ قِنطَارًا فَلَا تَأْخُذُوا مِنْهُ شَيْئًا ﴾. And it has come down from al-Husayn ibn Ali, (عليهم السلام) both, that he married a woman and sent to her one hundred slave girls, with each slave girl a thousand dirhams.

We have narrated from Abu Abdullah, (ع), that he was asked about the dowry, and he said: It is whatever the two spouses agree upon, be it little or much. But a dowry there must be, and no marriage is permissible without a dowry, though there is no harm in contracting it without one being named, and he may not consummate it until he gives her something, whatever it may be. This is for the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic: ﴿ لَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِن طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ مَا لَمْ تَمَسُّوهُنَّ أَوْ تَفْرِضُوا لَهُنَّ فَرِيضَةً ﴾. So a contract without a named dowry is, on this basis, permissible, but he may not consummate it until he gives her something. Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), was asked about this and said: My father, (ع), married a woman and did not name a dowry for her, and when he wished to consummate the marriage he took a garment from me and handed it to her.

He, (ع), was asked about the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic: ﴿ وَامْرَأَةً مُؤْمِنَةً إِن وَهَبَتْ نَفْسَهَا لِلنَّبِيِّ ﴾. He said: That is something singled out for the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace. No marriage is sound except with a dowry. If he does not name a dowry for her and then dies before her, or divorces her before consummating the marriage, she has no dowry, yet he inherits from her and she from him, and she must observe the waiting period. If he has consummated the marriage, she is entitled to the like of the dowry of the women of her family, according to her condition, her wealth, and her beauty, measured against one of his like in condition and in wealth, provided that this does not exceed the dowry of the Sunnah. If it does exceed it, the excess is returned to him, and the dowry of the Sunnah, as we have mentioned, is five hundred dirhams.

It is related from him, (ع), concerning a man who married a woman upon her own determination, that he said: The marriage is valid; and if the two disagree as to the amount of the dowry, she is entitled to the like of the dowry of the women of her family, neither falling short nor going to excess, and it is not permissible for it to exceed the dowry of the Sunnah.

It is related from him, (ع), that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, forbade the marriage of exchange. Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: The marriage of exchange, shighar, is that a man give his daughter in marriage to another man on the condition that the other give him his daughter in marriage, with no dowry between them. He said: Two men came to Ali, (ع), and each married the other's daughter without a dowry, so he rejected it and said: No, there must be a dowry. This applies when the contract in such a marriage is made on the basis that the marriage of the one is in return for the marriage of the other, each being set as the recompense for the other, and they oblige themselves to this. But if they contract it as a marriage is contracted without a named dowry, and do not stipulate within it what we have described, then the contract is valid, and each of the two women is entitled to the like of the dowry of the women of her family, in the manner we have described.

They, (عليهم السلام), permitted the contracting of marriage upon described and known goods. They narrated from Ali, (ع), that he said concerning a man who married a woman in return for a young servant: neither falling short nor going to excess. Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: Whoever marries a woman for an unknown dowry has not thereby corrupted the marriage; she is entitled to the like of the dowry of her peers, and it shall be no more than the dowry of the Sunnah.

He, (ع), was asked about a man who married a woman in return for a slave girl of his who had been pledged her freedom upon his death, and who then divorced her before consummating the marriage. He said: The woman is entitled to half the service of the pledged slave girl, so that the woman has one day of her service and her master one day; and if the master revokes the pledge of freedom or sells her, the woman is entitled to half her value. They, (عليهم السلام), said: The dowry belongs to the woman. They narrated from Ali, (ع), that he said: Marriage in return for a wage paid to the father is not lawful in Islam, in the manner that Moses hired himself out. The dowry belongs only to the wife. If a man marries a woman on the condition that he perform some work for her, the marriage is valid, and the wage in that case stands in place of the dowry; he may not consummate the marriage until he has done for her the work for which he was engaged, or has given her something.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: A woman came to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, and said: O Messenger of Allah, give me in marriage. He said: Who will take this woman? A man among the people said: I, O Messenger of Allah. He said: What will you give her? He said: I have no wealth. He said: With no wealth at all? So the woman turned to go. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, repeated his words, and no one rose but that man, and the woman turned away again. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said to the man a third time: Do you know anything of the Qur'an? He said: Yes. He said: I have given her to you in marriage for what you know of the Qur'an. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: If a man marries a woman on the condition that he teach her a chapter from the Book of Allah, I would not have him consummate the marriage until he has taught her or given her something.

From Ali, (ع), it is related that he said: Once a woman has been brought in to her husband, she has no claim to her dowry after she has entered upon him except by clear proof, meaning, if she claims that he gave her nothing and that something of her dowry remains owing to her. Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: If he marries her with a portion paid at once and a portion deferred, the immediate portion falls due before consummation, and he may not consummate the marriage if she withholds herself from him until he pays it to her. The deferred portion runs until death or separation, that is, when no time of payment has been stipulated upon him. But if she stipulates that it become due after consummation, it has been related from Ali, (ع), that a woman brought before him a man who had married her, consummated the marriage, and named a term for her dowry, and Ali, (ع), said: There is no term for you in her dowry once you have consummated the marriage; her right has fallen due, so pay her her right. This, and Allah knows best, is when he had paid her nothing in advance, or had named for her neither an immediate nor a deferred portion. But if he named that and paid her at once what he had obligated upon himself, then the deferred remainder runs to the time he stipulated, for the words of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace: People are bound by their conditions. Likewise, if he named no term but named a dowry for her, and she refused that he consummate the marriage until he discharge it, that is her right.

From Ali, (ع), it is related that he said concerning a man who marries a woman and witnesses are called secretly the first time upon one dowry and openly another time upon a greater dowry: the first is the marriage contract, and by it the matter is taken. From him, (ع), that he said: When the curtains are let down and the doors are shut, the dowry has fallen due. This is the saying of Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdullah, (عليهم السلام), and that is when the woman claims that the union took place. But if she acknowledges that he did not touch her, even though he had let down his curtain and shut his door upon them, and he then divorces her, she is entitled to no more than half the dowry, and she must observe the waiting period, for the waiting period is not waived from her by her claim that he did not touch her once he has shut his door and let down his curtain over her.

From Ali and Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdullah, (عليهم السلام), it is related that they said concerning a man who makes the freeing of his slave woman her dowry: that this is permissible. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: That is when the condition is set before the freeing, that is, he says: I marry you on the condition that I have made your dowry your freedom, and then he frees her. But if he frees her first and says to her: I free you for the sake of Allah on the condition that your freedom is your dowry and I marry you upon that, then the choice is hers: if she wishes she marries him, and if she wishes she does not, because she has been freed and has come to own herself apart from him. If he marries her after that, let him marry her with a dowry.

From Ali, (ع), it is related that he was asked about a man who steals wealth and marries with it, or buys a slave girl with it. He said: The union is lawful, but the liability for the wealth remains upon him, and he is a sinner for what he has done, and he must make restitution for what he stole.

**Mentioning the Conditions in Marriage**

We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, and from the Imams of his household, that he said: Every condition that contradicts the Book of Allah is to be rejected. From Ali, (ع), it is related that he ruled concerning a man who married a woman and stipulated to her family that if he were to take another wife over her she would be divorced, or if he were to take a concubine over her the concubine would be free. He said: The condition of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, comes before their conditions. If he wishes, he fulfills his promise; and if he wishes, he marries another over her or takes a concubine, and no wife is divorced from him should he marry her, nor is any concubine freed should he take her. From Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdullah, (عليهم السلام), the like of this is related.

It was narrated from Abu Ja'far and Abu Abd-Allah ((عليهم السلام)), as likewise from Ali ((ع)), that he gave judgment concerning a man who married a woman and stipulated that she should hold authority over her own affairs. Ali said: This departs from the Sunnah and assigns a right to one who is not its rightful holder. The husband is the guardian; intimacy and divorce rest in his hand. He therefore ruled that the dowry was owed by the husband, that intimacy and divorce remained his, and he nullified the condition.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: A condition that is lawful, one that does not contradict the Book of Allah, is binding upon the man who married the woman on its basis. Among such valid conditions is that she shall remain with her family in a known land, or in a specified dwelling. This is permissible.

It was also narrated from him ((ع)) that he said: Whoever marries a woman on the condition that he will provide her a fixed daily maintenance and not be absent from her during the day, that condition does not invalidate the marriage, but neither is it itself binding; she retains the right to the division of nights and to the maintenance due to wives generally. Yet if a man marries a woman who fears that he may harm her or divorce her, and he agrees with her to forgo some of his rights regarding her maintenance and the division of nights, that is permissible, in accordance with the words of Allah the Almighty: "If a woman fears discord or desertion from her husband, there is no blame upon them if they come to an agreement between themselves" (al-Nisa 128).

Abu Abd-Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: If he stipulates for her that, should he take another wife or take a concubine, she shall have one hundred dinars or whatever sum he has bound himself to, the marriage is permissible. If the sum he has committed is drawn from the original dowry, it is binding; if it rests only upon the added stipulation, it is not binding. This is the meaning of the saying of Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)): A man should not make the divorce of one wife a condition for marrying another. The Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) forbade a woman to seek the divorce of her sister wife so that she might take her place, for Allah the Almighty is the One who provides for her. And the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) forbade the marriage of pleasure (mut'ah).

Concerning the marriage of pleasure which some had allowed: it is reported that a man would say to a woman, "Give yourself to me in marriage," and would say, "I marry you for this dirham," or two dirhams, or whatever sum was named, for a single union, or a day, or two days, or whatever term they agreed upon. They said that once they agreed on this it became permissible for him until the end of the agreed term; and when the term expired the separation occurred of itself, without divorce. They claimed there was no iddah upon her, that she did not inherit from him nor he from her, and that intimacy by this arrangement was not permissible with a virgin, though some of them claimed it was permissible even with women already married. This is pure fornication, in which there is no ambiguity and which admits of no covering. Allah the Almighty has permitted intimacy only through two paths, through the bond of marriage and through the right hand of possession (milk al-yamin), for the Almighty said: "Those who guard their private parts, except with their wives or what their right hands possess, for they are not blameworthy; but whoever seeks beyond that, those are the transgressors" (al-Mu'minun 5–7).

For if this pleasure union were a marriage, the woman could not be parted from the man without divorce and without iddah, since Allah the Almighty says: "O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the beginning of their waiting period" (al-Talaq 1); and she would inherit and be inherited from, since Allah says: "And you shall have half of what your wives leave" (al-Nisa 12), and "They shall have a fourth of what you leave" (al-Nisa 12); and the child would be attached to them both, for the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) said: "The child belongs to the bed," meaning to the owner of the bed. But in this arrangement there is neither marriage nor right of possession, and what remains is forbidden. The reports forbidding the marriage of pleasure from the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) are many, as is his saying, "There is no marriage except with a guardian and two upright witnesses." Those who uphold the marriage of pleasure themselves claim that it is contracted without a guardian and without witnesses, and so it cannot stand. It was narrated from Ali, Abu Ja'far, and Abu Abd-Allah ((عليهم السلام)) that they declared the marriage of pleasure void, forbade it, and prohibited it.

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he gave judgment concerning a slave-woman who had fled from her master and deceived a man by claiming to be free, so that he married her on that basis and she bore him children; then her master established his claim over her. He ruled that she be returned to her master, and that the man who had been deceived owed her master the value of his children by her, unless the master chose to grant the children to their father. If the man had bought her and she bore him a child, and her master then established his claim, she was to be returned to the one whose right was vindicated, and the value of the child was owed by the father, who could in turn recover it from the one who had sold her to him. But if he had taken her by force, or had known when he married her that she was owned and that her master had not given her in marriage, then both she and her child belonged to the master.

From Ali ((ع)) it was narrated concerning a free woman whom a male slave had approached, claiming to be free, so that she married him on that basis and then learned that he was owned: they may be separated if the woman wishes. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)) said: She holds authority over her own decision; if she wishes she may remain with him, and if she wishes she may leave him. If she chooses to separate and the marriage has not been consummated, she is owed nothing; but if it has been consummated, she is entitled to her dowry, and the liability falls upon whoever deceived her, whether the slave himself or another. When the slave is freed, if his lord does not give him in marriage to her, the dowry rests with the slave once he is free; and if the lord gave him in marriage, or gave him leave to marry her, then her dowry is owed by the lord.

It was narrated from Ali, Abu Ja'far, and Abu Abd-Allah ((عليهم السلام)) concerning a man to whom another woman was brought as a bride, so that he consummated the marriage believing her to be his wife and only afterward learned otherwise: he must return that woman, and she is entitled to a dowry for what was done to her, claimed against the one who deceived him, and his true wife, the one he had actually married, is to be restored to him.

It was narrated from them concerning a man who is impotent and unable to approach women, yet conceals this from the woman he marries: she has the right of choice once she comes to know, and she is entitled to the dowry if she exercises that choice and the marriage has been consummated. They also said concerning a man who marries a woman and finds in her leprosy, or vitiligo, or blindness, or insanity, or a condition in the private parts that prevents intercourse, or a manifest lameness: the husband has the right of choice; if he wishes he may keep her, and if he wishes he may return her. She is entitled to the dowry if he has had intercourse with her, and his claim for it is against the one who deceived him with her, while the woman herself owes nothing in return. For defects other than these she is not to be returned.

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)) concerning a man who marries a woman as a virgin and finds her otherwise: he saw nothing objectionable in this, and said that virginity may be lost through a leap, through ablution, or through the passing of long years.

Mentioning the Forbidden Marriage and the Permissible Marriage

Allah the Almighty said: "And do not marry those whom your fathers married of women, except what has already passed; it is an indecency and a hateful thing and an evil way. Forbidden to you are your mothers, your daughters..." (al-Nisa 22–23). Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has thus forbidden in the plain text of His Book the marriage of those He named explicitly, and the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) and the Imams of his house ((عليهم السلام)) made clear what required clarification.

It came from Ali and Abu Abd-Allah ((عليهم السلام)) concerning the mention of the mothers of wives that the matter is unrestricted: when a man marries a woman, her mother becomes forbidden to him, whether or not he has consummated the marriage with the daughter. They said: if a man marries a woman and then divorces her, or she dies before he has consummated the marriage, the marriage of her daughter remains lawful for him, in keeping with the words of Allah: "And your stepdaughters under your guardianship, born of your wives with whom you have consummated marriage; but if you have not consummated with them, there is no blame upon you" (al-Nisa 23). The "guardianship" here is the bond of inviolability. But if he has consummated the marriage with the woman, her daughter becomes forbidden to him; and likewise, if he has seen of her what is forbidden to other men, he may not marry her daughter.

It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) concerning a man who marries a woman and then dies leaving her, or divorces her before consummation: she is forbidden to his son for all the generations that descend, and to his fathers however far they ascend. And if a man looks upon his bondswoman with desire, or touches her, or looks upon her nakedness, she becomes forbidden to his sons and to his fathers. But if one of them owned her and did not approach her, nor touch her, nor look upon her with desire, and then the other came to own her, she is lawful to him. They permitted the owning of two sisters together, while forbidding their combination in marriage. They said: if a man has approached one of the two, the other becomes forbidden to him, and he may not approach the second until the first has left his ownership through manumission, sale, or gift; and it does not suffice for him to give her to his son. If she dies, the other becomes lawful to him; what is forbidden is only their combination in marriage, for Allah says: "And that you combine two sisters" (al-Nisa 23), making no exception there between the owned woman and the free.

It was narrated from Ali, Abu Ja'far, and Abu Abd-Allah ((عليهم السلام)) that a man should not marry the sister of a woman he has divorced until her iddah has elapsed and his right of revocation over her is extinguished; for so long as that right remains, he has effectively joined the two sisters together. But if the divorce was a final and irrevocable one, he may marry her sister, or another, provided he has no more than three other free wives besides her. They also said that a woman may not be joined with her paternal aunt, nor with her maternal aunt. Ali ((ع)) said: The Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) forbade that, and made it unlawful. The daughter of Hamza was offered to him in marriage, and he said: Do you not know that she is the daughter of my brother through suckling, for I was nursed together with Hamza.

They ((عليهم السلام)) saw no harm in joining the daughters of paternal uncles with the daughters of maternal uncles, nor in a man's marrying both his wife and a daughter of his by another. Likewise, when a man divorces a woman or dies and another man marries her thereafter, there is no harm in the son of her first husband, by another wife, marrying the daughter of her second husband by another wife. They said: if a man commits fornication with the mother of his wife, or her sister, or her daughter, this does not corrupt his marriage, for the unlawful does not corrupt the lawful. But if he commits fornication with a woman and she then bears a child, it is not proper for his son by another wife to marry that woman's daughter. They granted permission regarding the woman with whom a man had committed fornication once both have repented.

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)) concerning a woman who commits adultery before her husband has consummated the marriage with her: they are separated, she is owed no dowry, and the prescribed punishment (hadd) is carried out upon her. Her dowry is forfeited because the offense arose from her side. Yet the separation rests upon the husband's wish: if he holds to her, she remains his wife.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)), when asked about a sick man who contracts a marriage and then dies, said: the marriage is valid, and she inherits from him.

It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) concerning a man who marries two sisters, or five women, in a single marriage contract: the one whose name was mentioned first among the sisters at the time of the contract has a valid marriage, and four of the five women have valid marriages, while the marriage of the second sister, and of the fifth woman after the four, is annulled. If it cannot be known which was named first, the whole contract is void. They ((عليهم السلام)) said concerning a man who marries a woman during her iddah, or marries her while one of the two is in a state of consecration, both knowing that this is not lawful: they are separated, and she is never again lawful to him. But if they were ignorant, they are separated, and they may contract anew once she becomes lawful to suitors.

Mentioning the Missing Husband

We have narrated from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)) that he said: if a man is reported dead to his family, so that his wife observes her iddah and then remarries, and afterward the first husband returns, the first husband has the greater right to her, whether or not the second husband has consummated the marriage. If the second husband has consummated it, she is entitled to her dowry for what was made lawful from her.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: the wife of the missing man is to be left in her state so long as she keeps silent. But if she brings her case before the governor, he sets for her a term of four years and writes to inquire after her husband. If four years pass and no news of him comes, the governor summons the guardian of the missing man and asks whether the missing man has wealth. If he has wealth from which she may be maintained, and the guardian spends upon her from it, or the guardian spends upon her from his own means, then she has no way to remarry. But if the guardian will not maintain her and the missing man has no wealth, the governor compels the guardian to divorce her with a single pronouncement at the onset of her iddah while she is in a state of purity, and the guardian's divorce stands as the husband's divorce. If her husband returns before her iddah is complete, he has the greater right to her; and if her iddah has ended, she is lawful to suitors.

It was said to him: O son of the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family), what is to be done if the guardian provides for her and she says, "I cannot be patient; I want what other women have"? He answered that she has no such recourse, and no claim to it, so long as the guardian provides for her.

The missing man (al-mafqud) is one who has departed his home and whose whereabouts are unknown. He may have been seen between the two battle lines in time of war, after which the people scattered and he was lost; or he may have set sail on a ship that capsized; or it may simply be that no one on the journey knows where he settled. As for a man whose location is known, his ruling is not that of the missing man. The ruling concerning his wife is that of a woman whose husband is merely absent (gha'ib): she may not remarry unless he dies or divorces her. He ((ع)) said: if the whereabouts of the absent man are known, his wife may not remarry.

On Breastfeeding (al-Rida)

I have mentioned above the saying of Allah Almighty: ﴿وَأُمَّهَاتُكُمُ اللَّاتِي أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ وَأَخَوَاتُكُم مِّنَ الرَّضَاعَةِ﴾, "and your mothers who have suckled you, and your sisters through breastfeeding." We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah ((ع), and (عليهم السلام)) that what is forbidden through lineage is forbidden through breastfeeding. I have already set out which of the women are forbidden in marriage, and whoever comes, through breastfeeding, to occupy the place of one of these holds the same status of prohibition.

It is permissible for a man to marry a woman who has breastfed his sons, and to marry from among her daughters those she did not nurse him alongside, because she is not his mother through breastfeeding and is not a wife to his father. He may likewise marry her daughter when the woman is among the daughters of a woman who breastfed his brother or his sister. In the same way, a man may marry the daughters of women who breastfed his son, and their daughters, for they did not nurse his son, and there is no bond between them and him, whether through breastfeeding or otherwise. What is forbidden is to marry the nursing woman herself; but the daughters of paternal uncles and aunts, and of maternal uncles and aunts, through breastfeeding are lawful to marry, just as they are lawful through lineage. A man may marry a woman while his father marries her daughter by another man, and a father and son may each marry one of two sisters.

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he said: breastfeeding makes forbidden whether it be little or much; even a single suckle creates prohibition, by the saying of Allah Almighty, ﴿وَأُمَّهَاتُكُمُ اللَّاتِي أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ﴾, "and your mothers who have suckled you." Breastfeeding applies to the little and the much alike, for even a small quantity nourishes, and from it flesh and blood grow and bone is strengthened in proportion.

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he said: the breastfeeding that creates prohibition is that which occurs within the two years, for Allah says: ﴿وَالْوَالِدَاتُ يُرْضِعْنَ أَوْلَادَهُنَّ حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَن يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ﴾, "and mothers shall suckle their children for two complete years, for whoever wishes to complete the suckling." What occurs after the two years is not breastfeeding and creates no prohibition. The Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) said: "لا رضاع بعد فطام," "There is no breastfeeding after weaning."

It is narrated from Ali, Abu Ja'far, and Abu Abd Allah ((عليهم السلام)) concerning a woman who gives her husband her milk to drink in order, by that pretense, to make herself forbidden to him, or who gives it to her bondswoman, or nurses her, to make her forbidden to him: they said ((عليهم السلام)) that the man should discipline his wife, but that this creates no prohibition for him, because the breastfeeding of an adult is not breastfeeding. If a man is suckled after two years have passed from his birth, or a wife gives her husband her milk to drink, nothing is thereby made forbidden. But whatever occurs before the two years, whether by suckling, or by drinking a woman's milk, or by its being administered into the throat (wajur) or the nose (sa'ut), is breastfeeding.

They said ((عليهم السلام)): the milk of the stallion creates prohibition, meaning the milk attributed to the man who fathered it. This is when a man has several wives, or umm-walad mothers, in his household, and one of them nurses a slave boy while another nurses a slave girl: the two become siblings through breastfeeding, even though they did not nurse at the breast of one woman, because the one father unites them and they were nursed by the milk of his wives, so the milk is attributed to him.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)) was asked about the wife of a man who nursed a slave girl: is it lawful for his son by another woman to marry her? He said: No. It was said to him: does she then stand to him as a sister through breastfeeding? He said: Yes, from the father's side. He said: a man had two wives, each of whom bore a son, and one of the two wives nursed a slave girl belonging to other people. He said: the other son may not marry this slave girl, for she was nursed by the milk of the same father, and what lineage forbids, breastfeeding forbids.

He ((ع)) was asked about a slave girl belonging to a man, whom his wife, or the mother of his child, claimed to have nursed. He said: she is not to be believed, for she is drawing something to herself. Concerning a woman who said she had nursed a slave boy and a slave girl and then denied it, he said: if she denies it, that stands. It was said to him: and if she afterward retracts her denial and claims again that she nursed them? He said: she is not to be believed. The testimony of a single upright woman is accepted in matters of breastfeeding; but if no upright woman is available, her testimony alone does not suffice.

They ((عليهم السلام)) forbade seeking a wet-nurse whose milk comes of fornication, as with a woman who commits adultery and bears a child by it: such a woman should not be sought as a wet-nurse, nor taken as a foster-nurse (zi'r), neither she nor her daughter born of adultery. Yet they permitted seeking a wet-nurse among polytheist women if they are within a marriage, while disliking the seeking of a wet-nurse from the milk of those known for enmity toward the family of Muhammad ((ع) and his family). Breastfeeding by the milk of fornication makes nothing forbidden. Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: unlawful milk does not render the lawful unlawful, as with a woman nursed first by lawful milk and afterward by the milk of fornication. He said: whoever nurses a girl with the milk of fornication does not thereby make it forbidden to marry her, because unlawful milk does not render the lawful forbidden.

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he ruled concerning a man who married a woman, gave her her dowry, and had not yet consummated the marriage, after which it was established that there was a nursing relationship between them: she must return to him what she had taken. And it was narrated from the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) that he forbade women to nurse freely those boys whom they do not know.

On the Marriage of Bondswomen (Nikah al-Ima)

Allah Almighty said: ﴿وَمَن لَّمْ يَسْتَطِعْ مِنكُمْ طَوْلًا أَن يَنكِحَ الْمُحْصَنَاتِ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ فَمِن مَّا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُم مِّن فَتَيَاتِكُمُ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ﴾, up to His saying ﴿ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنْ خَشِيَ الْعَنَتَ مِنكُمْ ۚ وَأَن تَصْبِرُوا خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ﴾: "And whoever among you cannot afford to marry free believing women, then from those whom your right hands possess, of your believing bondswomen," up to His saying, "That is for him among you who fears falling into sin; and that you be patient is better for you, and Allah is Forgiving, Merciful."

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)), commenting on the words of Allah Almighty ﴿ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنْ خَشِيَ الْعَنَتَ مِنكُمْ﴾, "That is for him among you who fears falling into sin," that he said: this means adultery, nothing else. So a free Muslim should not marry a bondswoman until he fears being overcome by desire and cannot find the means to marry a free woman. If he has the means to a free woman, he should not marry a bondswoman; and if he does not fear falling into sin, he likewise should not marry her, for Allah Almighty has permitted free men the marriage of bondswomen only upon these two conditions.

Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: a free man may not marry a bondswoman until two conditions are joined in him, the fear of sin and the lack of means. If he is driven to it, let him marry her. When a free man marries a bondswoman, the children she bears him belong to her master as slaves, unless he stipulates at the marriage that whatever children she bears him shall be free; if he stipulates that, they are free, and the ruling on the child follows the ruling on the mother, in freedom and in bondage. If a slave marries a free woman, their children are free, and his master may not stipulate that the free woman's children be slaves, as he might with the children of a bondswoman, because the freeing of a bondswoman's children rests with their master, who frees them if he wishes. A woman may not take ownership of her own children while they are free, nor may anyone else.

A bondswoman may not be given in marriage except with the permission of her master, for Allah says: ﴿فَانكِحُوهُنَّ بِإِذْنِ أَهْلِهِنَّ﴾, "So marry them with the permission of their families." If a bondswoman is jointly owned by two men, she is not lawful to either of them, even if the other permits it, because the part of a person cannot be divided, nor made lawful by gift. If one of the two gives her in marriage without the permission of his partner, the marriage is not valid unless the other ratifies it, and the husband may not consummate it until the second owner permits the marriage; if he does not permit it, the marriage is dissolved. Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) was asked about a bondswoman jointly owned by two men, one of whom gives her in marriage while the other is absent: is the marriage valid? He said: if the absent owner is averse to it, it is not valid.

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he said: it is not lawful for a free Muslim to marry a Jewish or a Christian bondswoman, for Allah Almighty says ﴿مِّن فَتَيَاتِكُمُ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ﴾, "of your believing bondswomen." Likewise a free man should not marry a Magian or a polytheist bondswoman; for were that lawful, the polytheists would come to enslave the children of the Muslims. If a man wishes to marry a bondswoman, let him free her first, and then marry her if he wishes and she wishes. The Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) married Safiyyah bint Huyayy ibn Akhtab and drew the veil (hijab) over her. Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: any man who frees his slave woman and wishes to marry her, let it be with witnesses and an appointed dower; and if he makes her emancipation her dower, that is permitted, as I have explained in the foregoing.

A woman under a contract of emancipation (mukatabah) may be married with the permission of her master. Ali ((ع)) was asked about the marriage of a mukatabah, and he said: she may marry; and whatever child is born to her during her contract of emancipation stands in her own position, so that of that child what is freed in her is freed, and what remains in bondage in her remains in bondage. We shall mention in the chapter on those under emancipation contracts how this is reckoned, if Allah wills.

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he said concerning a man who marries a bondswoman over a free wife: they are to be separated, and he is charged her dower for what he made lawful of her person if he consummated the marriage; if he did not consummate it, she is owed nothing. Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: if a man marries a free woman alongside a bondswoman and the free woman knows of it, he may divide his time, two portions to the free woman and one portion to the bondswoman. If the free woman did not know that he had a bondswoman when he married her, and afterward learns of it, she has the choice: if she wishes she may remain, and if she wishes she may leave his bond, and she is owed the dower for what he made lawful of her person if he consummated the marriage; but if he did not consummate it and she chooses to part from him, she is owed nothing.

It was narrated from him that he said: it is not lawful for a free man to marry a bondswoman by herself. If he marries a free woman and a bondswoman in a single contract, one looks to whose name began the contract: if the free woman's name began it, the marriage of the bondswoman is void; but if the bondswoman's name began it and the free woman accepts to remain alongside her, the marriage of both is established, provided the man is one for whom the marriage of bondswomen is lawful, as we have described. A man may contract marriage upon a free woman while lacking the means for her dower; but if he does have the means, the marriage of the bondswoman is void, whether he began with her or named her second. Likewise, if it is not known with which of them the marriage of the bondswoman began, the marriage of the bondswoman is invalid.

He was asked about the maintenance (nafaqah) of a bondswoman wife. He said: when a free man marries a bondswoman, she serves her people by day and comes to her husband by night, and he owes her maintenance when they do this; but if they bar her from him by night, he owes her no maintenance.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) was asked about a man who has young children who own a slave girl: may he lie with her? He said: if he desires her, let him set a just value upon himself for her, so that her price stands against him as a debt to his children; then he owns her and may lie with her if he wishes. But if the children are grown, she is not lawful to him unless they sell her to him, and it is not lawful for him to do otherwise. A man has no right to any part of his child's wealth except by the child's own goodwill, save that, if he is driven to it, he may eat of it what sustains him, in the customary measure and without indulgence, when he is in hardship and the child is wealthy.

It was narrated from him ((ع)) that he was asked about a man who had a slave girl, and his wife, fearing he would take her for himself, ordered her son to lie with her, and he did. He said: she has sinned, and her son has sinned, and the son is liable to the prescribed punishment (hadd) if he is an adult. And he disliked that men should approach her, even though she is thereby forbidden to the master, because the unlawful does not render the lawful forbidden.

It was narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he disliked a man's lying with a bondswoman over whom another holds a stipulated right. The mudabbarah may be married with the permission of her master, and her ruling remains that of a bondswoman until her master dies, whereupon she is freed. If a bondswoman is freed while she has a husband: if her husband is free, she remains his wife and has no choice; but if he is a slave, she has the choice, upon being freed, to remain with him or to leave.

The matter of the freed slave woman is settled by what the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, did with Barirah. She was a slave woman married to a slave man, and she was grieved by her situation. When she was set free, the Messenger of Allah, (ع), gave her the choice, and she chose herself rather than her husband. He then ordered her to observe a waiting period of three menstrual cycles. And he said, (ع): if her master assists her to fulfill her emancipation contract, even if she is elderly, she has no right of choice against her husband on account of that assistance.

**The marriage of slaves**

Allah, exalted be the One who speaks, said: ﴿ عَبْدًا مَمْلُوكًا لَا يَقْدِرُ عَلَىٰ شَيْءٍ ﴾, "A slave who is owned and has no power over anything" (al-Nahl: 75). The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, forbade a slave to marry without the permission of his master. He said: any free woman who gives herself in marriage to a slave without the permission of her guardian has made her person unlawful and is owed no dower, meaning if the master denies the marriage. But if the master permits his slave to marry, then it is valid, and the dower falls upon the master when he is content with it.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), was asked about a man who marries his own slave woman to one of his male slaves. He said: he says, "I have given her to you in marriage," and he gives her some small thing as a dower. The right of separation remains in the hand of the master: if he wishes he may part them without a divorce, and if he sells the slave man, that selling is itself a separation between them; and if he sells them together, the buyer may part them if he wishes.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: if a slave woman is sold and she has a free husband, she remains his wife, and her person is not lawful to the buyer until her husband divorces her or dies and she completes her waiting period. From Ali, (ع): if a woman comes to own her husband, or any portion of him, the marriage between them is dissolved by the ownership itself, and she becomes the owner of her own person. It is not lawful for a woman to marry her own slave unless she first frees him; if she wishes, she may free him and then marry him afterward.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع): the slave may purchase and may approach of the slave women whatever his master has permitted him, and he may not marry more than two free women. He may marry four slave women, and the divorce of those he marries rests in the hand of his master, who may divorce them on his behalf whenever he wishes.

**The marriage of polytheists**

The marriages of polytheists among themselves, whether they belong to a single religious community or to differing ones, are valid when contracted according to what their own religion requires, and it is not fitting that such marriages be annulled. We have narrated from Ali, (ع), that he said: if a man and his wife are taken among the spoils, their marriage holds, and even if her husband is captured some days after her, they remain as they were, unless one of them is carried off and brought into the House of Islam. If that occurs, their marriage is dissolved, and she is not lawful to him except by a new contract, even if one of them had been taken before the other. So long as either of them remains a captive among the spoils, for as long as they remain there, their marriage stands between them. But once one of them enters the House of Islam, their marriage is dissolved. If she embraces Islam and her husband is then captured after her acceptance of Islam, and she has passed three menstrual cycles, yet she had not been carried off and her husband had not embraced Islam, then they remain upon their marriage. The one who owns them both may separate them if he wishes, as he may do with the rest of his slaves, unless at the time of their capture they were granted safe conduct on the condition that they not be parted, and that was contracted for them, in which case their separation is not obligatory.

From Ali, (ع): it is not lawful for Muslims to marry polytheist women in the abode of war while the rulings of the polytheists hold sway over them. And from him, (ع): if a woman of the people of war seeks safe conduct and comes out to the House of Islam while she has a polytheist husband, his marital bond to her is severed, and she may marry if she wishes after her womb has been cleared. Should her husband then come as a Muslim under safe conduct, he has no claim upon her except by a renewed marriage, because when she came out to the House of Islam his bond to her was already severed.

If a man embraces Islam while he has a polytheist wife, she remains his wife. But if a woman embraces Islam while her husband remains a polytheist, a separation is made between them. If her husband embraces Islam before her waiting period has ended, they remain upon their marriage; if her waiting period has expired, she becomes the owner of her own person.

From Ali and Abu Ja'far, (عليهم السلام) both: they forbade marriage with the Christian women of the Arabs and the eating of their slaughtered animals. From Ali, (ع): an apostate who renounces his faith and joins the abode of war is in the standing of a dead man. His wealth is divided among his heirs, his wives observe the waiting period of widows, and they may then marry if they wish.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع): if a woman embraces Islam while her husband remains a polytheist, she is separated from him should he not embrace Islam, and she keeps her full dower; if he had not consummated the marriage with her, she is entitled to half the dower. And if polytheists embrace Islam, they remain upon their existing marriages. The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: "Confirm the people of the age of ignorance upon whatever state they entered Islam, whether of marriage, divorce, or inheritance."

From Ali, (ع), concerning the polytheist who embraces Islam while he has two free sisters as wives, or more than four free wives: he is left with the first of the two sisters he married, and a separation is made between him and the second, and between him and whatever wives he took beyond the four free women. And from him, (ع): Allah made the women of the People of the Book lawful for the Muslims only when Muslim women were few; and when Muslim women became many, Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ وَلَا تَنْكِحُوا الْمُشْرِكَاتِ حَتَّى يُؤْمِنَّ ﴾, "And do not marry polytheist women until they believe" (al-Baqarah: 221). And He said: ﴿ وَلَا تُمْسِكُوا بِعِصَمِ الْكَوَافِرِ ﴾, "And do not hold to the marriage bonds of disbelieving women" (al-Mumtahanah: 10). The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, forbade a Muslim man to marry a non-Muslim woman while he is able to find a Muslim woman, and forbade a polytheist to marry a Muslim woman.

If a polytheist embraces Islam while he has a polytheist wife, he may keep her with him if he desires her, in the hope that Allah, mighty and majestic, will guide her, and he may marry above her up to three Muslim women, if she is aware of her standing. If he marries a Muslim woman while he still has a polytheist wife, and the Muslim woman does not know of this, and she learns of it after consummation, then the matter is placed in her own hand: if she wishes to remain with him she may stay, and if she wishes to depart she may go. She is entitled to her dower for what was made lawful of her person, and she may marry whomever she wishes once her waiting period has elapsed, without need of a divorce. But if he divorces the polytheist wife before the waiting period has elapsed, counted from the day the Muslim wife made her choice, then he has the greater right to her.

**The division of turns among co-wives**

Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ وَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعُوا أَنْ تَعْدِلُوا بَيْنَ النِّسَاءِ وَلَوْ حَرَصْتُمْ فَلَا تَمِيلُوا كُلَّ الْمَيْلِ فَتَذَرُوهَا كَالْمُعَلَّقَةِ ﴾, "You will never be able to deal justly between wives, even though you are eager to do so; yet do not incline altogether away, so as to leave her as one suspended" (al-Nisa: 129). Thus the Exalted in praise made known that justice between wives in matters of affection, desire, and ardor, apart from intercourse, cannot be attained, while in matters of maintenance, lodging, gifts, and the like, justice can indeed be practiced. So it is upon a man, if he has several wives, to be with each of them by day and night in turn.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), was asked about a man who has wives and is intimate with some of them and not others. He said: it is upon him to spend with each one her night, and to remain with her through her morning. If he is not stirred to intercourse, nothing is held against him; but if a man is stirred to intercourse while he is with one of his wives, it is not fitting that he abandon this and withhold himself for another.

From Ali, (ع), when he was asked about a man who has wives and then sets out on a journey: he said, when he returns let him begin with the one whose right of a turn is due. And from him, (ع): if a man has only one wife, it is not fitting that he leave her without spending the night with her one night out of four, and in the remaining three he may do what Allah has made lawful for him.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: if a man has two wives, he may set apart one of them for the three nights that are his own and assign to the other her single night. Likewise, if he has three wives, he assigns to each her night and devotes the fourth to whichever of them he wishes. If he has four, he may not favor one of them above another.

From Ali, (ع), concerning a man who has a wife or several wives and then marries again: if he marries a virgin, he stays with her seven nights, and if he marries a woman previously married, he stays with her three nights; thereafter he divides the nights equally among his wives. He was asked about the words of Allah, mighty and majestic: ﴿ وَإِنِ امْرَأَةٌ خَافَتْ مِنْ بَعْلِهَا نُشُوزًا أَوْ إِعْرَاضًا فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا أَنْ يُصْلِحَا بَيْنَهُمَا صُلْحًا وَالصُّلْحُ خَيْرٌ ﴾, "If a woman fears ill treatment or aversion from her husband, there is no blame upon them if they reach a reconciliation between themselves, and reconciliation is best" (al-Nisa: 128). He said: this concerns a man who has two wives and becomes unable to fulfill the rights of one of them, or she is a dhimmi woman, so he inclines toward the other and wishes to divorce the first, while she dislikes that. So she reconciles with him on the condition that he come to her from time to time, or that she yield to him her share of that.

**Maintenance owed to wives**

Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ قَدْ عَلِمْنَا مَا فَرَضْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ فِي أَزْوَاجِهِمْ وَمَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُهُمْ ﴾, "We have known what We have made obligatory upon them concerning their wives and those whom their right hands possess" (al-Ahzab: 50). And the Blessed and Exalted said: ﴿ وَارْزُقُوهُمْ فِيهَا وَاكْسُوهُمْ وَقُولُوا لَهُمْ قَوْلًا مَعْرُوفًا ﴾, "And provide for them therein and clothe them, and speak to them words of kindness" (al-Nisa: 5).

It is narrated from the family of the Messenger of Allah, (ع), that he addressed the people in the Farewell Pilgrimage, mentioned the women, and enjoined the people to treat them well, saying: "They have over you their sustenance and their clothing in a fitting manner." He forbade, (ع) and his family, that a man eat his fill while his family goes hungry, and said: "It is enough sin for a man that he neglect those whom he supports." It is narrated from him, (ع) and his family, that he said: "There are seven among the foremost of deeds, so keep to them," and he mentioned among them maintenance for one's dependents. And from him, (ع) and his family: "The severest of trials is many dependents with little wealth, and few dependents is one of the two forms of ease." And from him, (ع) and his family: "Whoever is moderate in his livelihood, Allah provides for him, and whoever is wasteful, Allah deprives him." And from him, (ع) and his family: "When Allah, mighty and majestic, intends good for a household, He grants them understanding in religion, provides them gentleness in their livelihood, and grants them moderation in their affairs." And from him, (ع) and his family: "Gentle moderation is half of livelihood; no man who lives in moderation is ever in want."

From Ali, (ع): if a man finds nothing to spend upon his wife, he is given a respite; if he brings her something, meaning what suffices to feed her, they are not separated, and if he finds nothing at all he is granted a term, and then they are separated. And from him, (ع): a woman complained against her husband that he would not maintain her, intending harm to her, so he imprisoned him on account of her maintenance. And he said, (ع): any woman who leaves her husband's house without his permission is owed no maintenance until she returns.

And from him, (ع), concerning the man who divorces his wife while she is nursing his child by her: she has the better right to the nursing, and she takes from him what would be given to a wet nurse. And if her husband dies and leaves with her a child of his whom she nurses, she is entitled to the like of a nurse's wage drawn from the child's wealth, if the child has wealth from inheritance or otherwise, and the guardian has no right to remove the child from her lap.

Maintenance owed to wives, and to those relatives upon whom it is obligatory, is according to one's means and capacity. Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ لِيُنْفِقْ ذُو سَعَةٍ مِنْ سَعَتِهِ وَمَنْ قُدِرَ عَلَيْهِ رِزْقُهُ فَلْيُنْفِقْ مِمَّا آتَاهُ اللَّهُ ﴾, "Let a man of ample means spend out of his ampleness, and whoever has his provision straitened, let him spend out of what Allah has given him" (al-Talaq: 7). So the ruler imposes upon the one obliged the maintenance of his like, without extravagance and without stinting. Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ وَالَّذِينَ إِذَا أَنْفَقُوا لَمْ يُسْرِفُوا وَلَمْ يَقْتُرُوا وَكَانَ بَيْنَ ذَلِكَ قَوَامًا ﴾, "And those who, when they spend, are neither extravagant nor stinting, but hold a just balance between the two" (al-Furqan: 67).

This is a thing well known and not obscure, requiring no recourse to opinion or independent reasoning, for its measure is known to people from their customary provisions in every place and time, and according to want and plenty, the means at hand, cheapness and dearness, poverty and wealth. Every poor person who is unable to manage or to work, his maintenance falls upon the one who would inherit from him were he to die, in proportion to what that heir would receive of his estate, except the wife, for her maintenance is upon her husband and he owes her no return. If the one upon whom the maintenance is obligatory is himself poor and has nothing to spend, then his case is like that of one who has no guardian to provide for him: he seeks his provision from the provision of Allah, glorified be His mention. A man is not bound to provide for one distant from him who would not inherit from him were he to die, when his own heir is poor and without wealth, because his maintenance is owed only there, and Allah, mighty and majestic, has excused him, for His words: ﴿ لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا ﴾, "Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity" (al-Baqarah: 286), and His words: ﴿ لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا مَا آتَاهَا ﴾, "Allah does not burden a soul except with what He has given it" (al-Talaq: 7). If he should afterward come into ease, he spends upon the one in his charge from that time onward, and he has no right to be held to account for what has already passed.

**Al-istibra, the clearing of the womb**

We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, (ع), that a man of the Ansar invited the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, to a meal, and he accepted. At the Ansari's house there was a slave woman, great with child, going to and fro serving the food. The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said to him: "What is this?" He said: "A slave woman of mine, whom I bought some days ago, and she is pregnant." He said: "Perhaps you have approached her?" He said: "Yes, O Messenger of Allah, I have done so." Then the Messenger of Allah said to him: "Were it not for the sanctity of your food, I would have invoked upon you a curse that would enter upon you in your grave. Free what is in her womb, for your seed has already nourished its hearing, its sight, its flesh, its blood, its hair, and its skin."

And Ali, (ع), said: it is not lawful for a man to approach a woman pregnant by another until she delivers what is in her womb and becomes pure; and if he purchases a slave woman who is pregnant, he must not approach her until she gives birth and becomes pure. And from the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family: a slave woman, when a man would approach her, is to be cleared by one menstrual cycle.

On istibra

Ali, (ع), said: whoever buys a young slave girl who has not yet reached puberty and has shown no sign of it, and over whom there is no fear of pregnancy, may approach her if he wishes and is under no obligation to perform istibra upon her. He said: whoever buys a slave girl who has despaired of menstruation should set her apart for two months and then approach her if he wishes. This applies when it is doubted that she may still menstruate, even though her menses appear to have ceased. Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, the prayers of Allah be upon him, said: whoever buys an old woman and knows with certainty that she does not menstruate, there being no doubt in her case, may approach her if he wishes and need not perform istibra.

He said: whoever buys a slave girl from a woman may approach her before performing istibra, and there is no harm in that; yet if he performs istibra as a safeguard for himself, that is better. Istibra is, however, an obligation upon the seller: if he has approached the slave woman and then wishes to sell her, he must not sell her until he has performed istibra upon her by one menstrual cycle, lest she be carrying his child and his offspring be sold along with her.

It was narrated from Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, that he said: if a man buys a slave woman who does not menstruate, or one who has reached puberty without having menstruated, he must perform istibra upon her for forty-five nights.

Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, was asked about a man who bought a slave girl who had reached the age of discernment but had not menstruated and was a virgin, whose seller had taken her back and then sold her. He said, (ع): her case is grave; he must perform istibra upon her for nine months, and then he may approach her after the nine months if no pregnancy has appeared, taking the cautious course.

It was related from him, (ع), concerning a man who buys a slave girl while she is in a state of purity, and her seller informs him that he has not approached her since she last menstruated nor during the present purity in which she is. He said, (ع): if the seller is trustworthy in his eyes, he may approach her if he wishes; and if he performs istibra, that is the more cautious course for him. He said, (ع): whoever buys a slave girl who has had no husband, and whose master has not approached her, must nonetheless perform istibra upon her by one menstrual cycle.

He, (ع), was asked about a man who bought a slave girl, then freed her and married her, whether he might approach her. He said: let him perform istibra of her womb by one menstrual cycle; and if he approaches her before performing istibra, there is no harm in it. It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), that he was asked about a man who had lain with a slave woman of his and then wished to give her in marriage before performing istibra. He said: No. It was said to him: by how much should he perform istibra? He said: by one menstrual cycle, though two are more beloved to me. A man must likewise perform istibra of the slave woman when he wishes to sell her, just as he performs it when he buys her.

It was narrated from Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, that he said: when a man buys a slave woman, there is no harm in his enjoying of her what is short of intercourse before he performs istibra, meaning fondling, kissing, and approach short of the private part. It was narrated from him, (ع), that he was asked about a man who bought a slave woman and feared she might be pregnant; he said: let him perform istibra upon her for forty-five nights.

He, (ع), was asked about a slave woman who had committed adultery, whether her master might approach her. He said: he does not approach her until he has performed istibra upon her. And if a man commits adultery with a slave woman and then buys her, he ought not to approach her until he has performed istibra upon her; and if she has conceived by him through that adultery, the child is not attributed to him, for the Messenger of Allah said: "الولد للفراش والعاهر الحجر," meaning the child belongs to the marriage bed, and for the fornicator is the stone.

It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), that he said: whoever buys a slave girl while she is menstruating, when she becomes pure let him approach her if he wishes. And it was narrated from Ali, (ع), that he said concerning a woman taken captive who has a husband: she performs istibra by one menstrual cycle, and when she is pure her master may approach her. And if a woman is coerced and violated, her husband does not approach her until she has performed istibra by one menstrual cycle.
