# Marriage and the Household

Once the marriage has been thus commended and the standing of each party made known to the other, the contract is concluded. The one who performs it praises Allah for what He deserves, prays upon the Prophet (peace be upon him and his family), recalls the virtue of marriage and what is desired in it, and only then ties the knot. It is fitting that the husband make known to his prospective companion whatever he can of his situation, so that she enters the union with knowledge of his circumstances. And if the contract is not preceded by a formal engagement, it remains permissible, though the khutbah is the better course.

The scholars (peace be upon them) forbade a formal proposal during the 'iddah, yet they permitted a discreet hint at marriage, in keeping with the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ وَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِيمَا عَرَّضْتُم بِهِ مِنْ خِطْبَةِ النِّسَاءِ أَوْ أَكْنَنتُمْ فِي أَنفُسِكُمْ ۚ عَلِمَ اللَّهُ أَنَّكُمْ سَتَذْكُرُونَهُنَّ وَلَٰكِن لَّا تُوَاعِدُوهُنَّ سِرًّا إِلَّا أَن تَقُولُوا قَوْلًا مَّعْرُوفًا ۚ وَلَا تَعْزِمُوا عُقْدَةَ النِّكَاحِ حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ الْكِتَابُ أَجَلَهُ ﴾

*There is no blame upon you for what you hint at regarding a proposal to women, or what you conceal within yourselves. Allah knows that you will remember them, but do not make a secret commitment with them except in the form of honorable speech. And do not resolve the marriage contract until the prescribed term reaches its end.* (al-Baqarah: 235)

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him) said concerning the words of Allah Almighty, "do not make a secret commitment with them": this refers to a secret pledge of marriage, while exposing them gently to the matter and speaking goodly words is the permitted hinting at the khutbah.

It was narrated from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (peace be upon them) that he entered upon Sakina bint Handhalah, who had been separated from a cousin of hers who had married her and then died. Abu Ja'far greeted her and said, "How have you been, O daughter of Handhalah?" She said, "May I be your ransom, O son of the Messenger of Allah." He said, "You know my kinship to the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family), and my standing with Ali (peace be upon him), and my right in Islam, and my honor among the Arabs." She said, "May Allah forgive you, O Abu Ja'far. You propose to me while I am in my 'iddah!" He said, "I have done no such thing. I have only informed you of my status and my standing. The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) once entered upon Umm Salamah, the daughter of Umayyah ibn al-Mughirah al-Makhzumiyyah, while she was in her 'iddah from Abu Salamah, who was her cousin. He continued to mention his standing to her until the impression of the mat showed upon his palm from the force with which he leaned upon his hand, and yet that was not a formal proposal." They said (peace be upon them): it is recommended for one who wishes to marry to acquaint his prospective companion with his situation, so that she may know his circumstances.

It was related that when the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) proposed to her, Umm Salamah (may Allah be pleased with her) sent word to him saying, "O Messenger of Allah, I am an elderly woman, I have children, and I am a woman of great jealousy, and I fear there may be something in me of which you are unaware." The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) sent back to her, "As for your age, you have mentioned it, and I am older than you. As for your children, they shall be among the children of the Messenger of Allah. And as for your jealousy, I shall pray to Allah Almighty to remove it from you." And he married her (peace be upon him and his family).

**The Wedding Feast and the Consummation of Marriage**

We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah (may the prayers of Allah be upon him and upon his family and upon them all) that the feast is of four kinds: the walimah, the khurs, the i'dhar, and the wakirah. The walimah is the food prepared when a man enters upon his wife. The khurs is the food of the 'aqiqah prepared for the newborn on the seventh day, which has already been mentioned along with what is obligatory in it. The i'dhar is the food prepared at the circumcision of a boy. The wakirah is when a man returns from a journey, prepares food, and invites people to it. He said (peace be upon him and his family): it is among the Sunnah of the Messengers to provide food upon marriage. The Prophet (peace be upon him and his family) held a wedding feast for his wives when he consummated marriage with them, and at some of his feasts he served al-hays, a food made of dates, ghee, and condensed milk. He said (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) to one of his companions who had married, "Give a feast, even if it be only with a sheep." And he said (peace be upon him and his family), "The feast of the first day is a duty, the feast of the second day is a kindness known among people, and what is beyond that is ostentation and reputation-seeking." Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (may the prayers of Allah be upon them) was invited to a feast on the first day and he answered it; he was invited on the second day and he answered it; then a messenger came to him on the third day to invite him, and he ordered the messenger turned away until he was out of sight.

It was narrated from the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) that he passed by Banu Zurayq and heard the sound of instruments and singing, and he asked, "What is this?" They said, "O Messenger of Allah, so-and-so has married so-and-so." He said, "He has completed his religion. This is marriage, not a clandestine affair, and there is no marriage in secret, until its smoke is seen and the sound of the tambourine is heard." And he said (peace be upon him and his family), "What distinguishes marriage from fornication is the beating of the tambourine." It was also narrated from him (peace be upon him and his family) that on the night Fatimah was brought to Ali (peace be upon them) he heard the beat of the tambourine, and he asked, "What is this?" Umm Salamah said, "O Messenger of Allah, this is Asma' bint 'Umays beating the tambourine for Fatimah, lest she say within herself, 'My mother has died and there is no one to rejoice with me.'" The Messenger of Allah raised his hands to the heavens and said, "O Allah, bring joy to Asma' bint 'Umays as she has gladdened the daughter of Your Prophet." Then he called her and said, "O Asma', what do you say when you beat the tambourine?" She said, "O Messenger of Allah, by Allah I do not know what to say; I only wished to gladden her." He said, "Then do not say anything indecent, but say: 'We have come to you, we have come to you; so greet us and we will greet you.'"

This kind of speech and its like, together with the beating of the tambourine and what resembles it, is what permission has been granted for at weddings and banquets. As for other speech that carries vulgarity, and the singing that accompanies it, and the instruments of diversion and the entrancing flutes that the people of frivolity take up, that is disliked.

Al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon them) was asked about such things, and he recited the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ وَمَا خَلَقْنَا السَّمَاءَ وَالْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا لَاعِبِينَ ۝ لَوْ أَرَدْنَا أَن نَّتَّخِذَ لَهْوًا لَّاتَّخَذْنَاهُ مِن لَّدُنَّا إِن كُنَّا فَاعِلِينَ ۝ بَلْ نَقْذِفُ بِالْحَقِّ عَلَى الْبَاطِلِ فَيَدْمَغُهُ فَإِذَا هُوَ زَاهِقٌ ۚ وَلَكُمُ الْوَيْلُ مِمَّا تَصِفُونَ ﴾

*We did not create the heaven and the earth and what is between them in play. Had We intended to take a pastime, We would have taken it from near Us, were We to do so. Rather, We hurl the truth against falsehood, and it crushes it, and at once it vanishes. And woe to you for what you describe.* (al-Anbiya': 16–18)

It was narrated from him that he said, "Singing is a gathering upon which Allah Almighty does not look." It was narrated from him that he was asked about the flute, and he said, "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said: there are two voices that Allah Almighty detests: a wailing at the time of calamity, and a flute at the time of blessing." And it was narrated from him regarding the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿ فَاجْتَنِبُوا الرِّجْسَ مِنَ الْأَوْثَانِ وَاجْتَنِبُوا قَوْلَ الزُّورِ ﴾

*So avoid the abomination of idols, and avoid the false statement* (al-Hajj: 30)

that "the abomination of idols" includes chess, and that "the false statement" includes singing. He said concerning the words of Allah Almighty, ﴿ وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَن يَشْتَرِي لَهْوَ الْحَدِيثِ ﴾ *And among the people is he who purchases idle talk* (Luqman: 6), that this too refers to singing.

One of his companions said, "I was in Madinah and came upon a place where there was singing, so I sat and listened. The next day I entered upon Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon them), and when I sat before him he began by reciting the words of Allah, exalted be His mention:

﴿ إِنَّ السَّمْعَ وَالْبَصَرَ وَالْفُؤَادَ كُلُّ أُولَٰئِكَ كَانَ عَنْهُ مَسْئُولًا ﴾

*Indeed, the hearing, the sight, and the heart: about all those one will be questioned.* (al-Isra': 36)

Then he looked at me and said, 'You will be asked of your hearing what it heard, of your heart what it understood, and of your sight what it beheld.'"

It was narrated from him that he said, "The sale of singing is not lawful, nor its purchase; listening to it is hypocrisy, and teaching it is disbelief." It was also narrated from him that one day some of his Shi'a, who had come as a delegation from various towns, entered upon him, and he asked them, "Where have you lodged?" They said, "O son of the Messenger of Allah, we lodged in a house, and when we drew near to the place we heard singing within it, so we sought another lodging but could not find one." He said, "Even so, be noble: keep your hearing far from listening to it and lending it your ear, for Allah, exalted is the One who speaks, says:

﴿ وَالَّذِينَ لَا يَشْهَدُونَ الزُّورَ وَإِذَا مَرُّوا بِاللَّغْوِ مَرُّوا كِرَامًا ﴾

*And those who do not bear witness to falsehood, and when they pass by idle talk, they pass by with dignity.* (al-Furqan: 72)"

It was narrated from him that he said, "My father (peace be upon him) passed by me one day when I was a boy, and I had stopped to listen to two pipers. He took me by the hand and said, 'Step away; do not be of those who gloated over Adam (peace be upon him).' I said, 'How is that, O my father?' He said, 'All the amusement, play, and singing that you see was devised by Iblis to gloat over Adam (peace be upon him) when he was expelled from Paradise.'" He said (peace be upon him), "The house in which singing takes place is a house in which no calamity is met with comfort, no supplication is answered, and the angel does not enter." It was narrated from him that he said, "Singing causes hypocrisy to grow in the heart as water causes the date palm to bring forth its spathe." And it was narrated from him that he said, "Whoever has the lute played in his house for forty days, Allah sets over him a devil called al-'Anqad, who leaves no limb of his without sitting upon it; and when he is thus afflicted, his sense of shame is stripped from him, and he no longer cares what he says or what is said to him."

It was mentioned to him (peace be upon him) that a man of his Shi'a had entered upon someone he knew, and that man brought out a slave-girl of his who played and sang, and she sang and played before him. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon them) said, "Did he not fear that the command of Allah Almighty might come upon him while he was in such a state, in a gathering upon which Allah does not look?" It was also narrated from him that a man asked him about singing, and he said to him, "Tell me: if Allah Almighty separates truth from falsehood, where do you see singing falling?" The man said, "O son of the Messenger of Allah, I see it only with falsehood." He said, "Then I reckon you have judged it. Leave it, for there is no good in it, and it is among that for which Allah Almighty has promised the Fire." Then he recited the words of Allah Almighty, ﴿ وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَن يَشْتَرِي لَهْوَ الْحَدِيثِ ﴾ *And among the people is he who purchases idle talk* (Luqman: 6). It was also narrated from him that singing bequeaths hypocrisy and brings on poverty.

And he said (peace be upon him) that the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) forbade his community al-zafn, the flute, the drums, and the reed-pipes; and that a man who had broken a lute was once brought before Ali (peace be upon him), and Ali approved his deed and ruled that there was no liability for breaking the lute. All of this points to the prohibition of frivolity and of listening to singing, flutes, lyres, and the like that are taken up for diversion. As for what is used in war, in the marshaling and parading of troops, and the like, such as what people raise up before the Imams and the commanders of armies by beating drums and shields and by blowing horns and pipes that are not taken for enjoyment and amusement, that is not among what was forbidden, nor of the kind whose prohibition has just been mentioned. Rather it is among what is recommended in its proper place. For it has come from the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) that he passed by a company of Abyssinians beating their drums, and he said, "Carry on, O sons of Arfidah, that the Jews may know there is breadth in our religion."

If someone should ask, "Why then do we hear and see, in the lands where the Imams hold authority (may the prayers of Allah be upon them), the very instruments of diversion that they have forbidden?" the answer is that this is no greater than the indecencies that Allah Almighty has forbidden and made unlawful, which people nonetheless commit in their homes, behind their doors and their curtains. Allah has commanded that the prescribed penalties be carried out for those indecencies upon which He has commanded such penalties, in what appears openly and is witnessed by witnesses. As for what remains hidden, He alone, glorified be He, holds the knowledge of it and has reserved to Himself both its punishment and its pardon, advancing of that what He wills and deferring what He defers. It is well known and widely established among all people that the Imams (peace be upon them) denounced these things upon those who did them and withheld any license for them, holding that this is not a matter for which the hadd is due, but rather one calling for denunciation and prohibition.

It was narrated from the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) that he said, "Bring your brides in by night, and give the feast by day." And it was narrated from him (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) that he said, "There is no staying awake save in three: a vigil with the Quran, the seeking of knowledge, or the bringing of a bride to her husband." It was also narrated from him (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) that a man should make himself ready for his wife as he would wish her to make herself ready for him; Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon them) said that this means cleanliness.

He said: when a man's wife is brought to him and she enters upon him, let him pray two rak'ahs, then place his hand upon her forelock and say, "O Allah, bless me in my wife and bless her in me; in whatever You have joined between us, join us in goodness, felicity, and blessing; and if You decree a parting, make it a parting toward every good." Then, when he sits beside her, let him wipe her forelock and say, "Praise be to Allah, who guided my straying, enriched my poverty, raised up my burden, gave might to my lowliness, sheltered my dependents, gave a spouse to my solitude, bore up my step, served my need, brought company to my loneliness, and lifted my standing; a praise wholesome, abundant, and blessed, for what You have granted, O Lord, and for what You have apportioned, and for what You have honored."

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (peace be upon them) said: the woman too is commanded to be in a state of ablution on the night she is brought as a bride; when her husband prays, she prays, and when he supplicates, she says amen. He added that one should increase in supplication, sending prayers upon the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) at the beginning, the middle, and the end, and saying within it, "O Allah, grant me her affection, her warmth, her contentment with me and my contentment with her, and join us in the best of unions and the gentlest of bonds, for You love the lawful and hate the unlawful."

And Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon them) said: when a man wishes to come to his wife, let him say, "O Allah, if You decree from me this day a child, then make him purely Yours, and grant the devil no share in him, no portion and no part; indeed You are powerful over all things." It was narrated from the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) that he said, "When one of you comes to his wife, let him not hasten her," and that when he is with her, let him deal kindly and truly with her. And he (peace be upon him and his family) forbade that a man be intimate with a free woman while another woman is in the room with her. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (peace be upon him) said there is no harm in that with a bondwoman, and likewise said Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him). And it was narrated from Ali (peace be upon him) that he said it does not befit a man to be intimate with his wife

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon them, said that when a man wishes to come to his wife, let him say: «اللهم إن قضيت مني اليوم خلقاً فاجعله لكَ خالصاً، ولا تجعل للشيطان فيه شركاً ولا حظاً ولا نصيباً، إنك على كل شيء قدير» "O Allah, if You decree from me today a creation, make it purely for You, and grant the devil in it no share, no portion, and no part, for You are able to do all things." And from the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, it was related that he said: «إذا أتى أحدكم امرأته فلا يعجلها، وإذا واقعها فليصدقها» "When one of you comes to his wife, let him not hasten her, and when he is with her, let him deal truthfully with her."

He, peace be upon him and his family, permitted a man to come to a free wife while another wife is present in the house. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, said there is no objection to this in the case of a bondwoman, and likewise said Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him. From Ali, peace be upon him, it was related that a man should not come to his wife while facing the qibla. He also said that to gaze during intercourse brings on blindness. The Prophet, peace be upon him and his family, and the Imams of his progeny, would turn the face away from any animal being mounted upon the road, and he would avert his face, peace be upon him.

He, peace be upon him, named a hidden form of infanticide, الوأد الخفي: that a man comes to a woman, and when he senses the issuing of the fluid he withdraws from her and spills it elsewhere. Of this he said, "So do not do that." He said that the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, forbade withdrawal from a free woman except with her leave, and from a bondwoman taken in marriage except with the leave of her master. Ali, peace be upon him, said that a man may withdraw from his own bondwoman as he wishes, and likewise said Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon them.

From Ali, peace be upon him, by way of Ja'far ibn Muhammad: a man should not lie between two free women, though he may lie between two bondwomen; and he should not look upon the 'awrah of a free woman, nor expose his own 'awrah to her. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, peace be upon him, said that intercourse is disliked from the breaking of dawn until sunrise, and from the setting of the sun until the fading of twilight; and on the night in which the moon is eclipsed, and on the day in which the sun is eclipsed; and on the day and night in which the earth quakes; and when the yellow, the black, and the red winds blow.

He, peace be upon him, said: The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, passed a night with one of his wives on a night in which the moon was eclipsed, and nothing passed between him and her. It was said, "O Messenger of Allah, what is this coldness?" He said, "It is no coldness, but this was a sign, and I was loath to take my pleasure on such a night, that I might not be among those whom Allah, mighty and majestic, has rebuked in His Book where He says: «وَإِن يَرَوْا كِسْفًا مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ سَاقِطًا يَقُولُوا سَحَابٌ مَّرْكُومٌ» 'And were they to see a fragment falling from the sky, they would say: a heaped-up cloud.'" Then Abu Ja'far, may Allah's blessings be upon him, said: By Him who sent Muhammad with prophethood, singled him out with the message, and honored him with nobility, none of you comes to his wife at any of these disliked times and is then granted offspring in whom he sees the delight of his eye.

From Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him: a slave girl under nine years of age is not to be approached; if a man does so and she is harmed by it, he is liable. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family, said: If a man marries a young girl, he does not enter upon her until she completes nine years from the day of her birth.

From Ali, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, it was related that he disliked the coming to women in their hinder parts, and he used to forbid it, citing the words of Allah, mighty and majestic, that one may come to them only «في الفروج من قبل أو دبر» in the place of tilth, whether from before or from behind. And from the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, it was related that he said: «لا يخلو رجل بامرأة غير ذات محرم منه إلا كان الشيطان ثالثهما» "No man is alone with a woman who is not his mahram but that the devil is the third of them." He forbade conversing with women other than one's wives and one's mahrams. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's blessings be upon him, said: «حديث النساء يورث النسيان ويزيغ القلب وهو من مصائد الشيطان» "Idle talk with women breeds forgetfulness and makes the heart swerve, and it is among the snares of the devil."

The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, said: «اتقوا الله في النساء، فإنهن عيٌّ وعورة، وإنكم استحللتموهن بأمانة الله» "Fear Allah concerning women, for they are weak of speech and are a thing to be covered, and you have made them lawful to you by the trust of Allah." He said: They have their weakness, so treat their weakness with restraint of speech, and shelter that which is to be covered within the homes; do not lodge them in the upper rooms, do not teach them to write, but teach them Surat al-Nur. And from him, peace be upon him and his family, he said: «نِعم شغل المرأة المؤمنة المغزل» "How excellent an occupation for a believing woman is the spindle." From him also, peace be upon him and his family, when he took the pledge from women he would take upon them that they would not speak with men other than their husbands and their mahrams.

It was related from him, peace be upon him and his family, that he forbade women to look upon men, and that a woman should not leave her house without her husband's leave, not even for the bath save out of necessity. And it was related that he said one day to his companions, "What is the woman?" They said, "An 'awrah, O Messenger of Allah." He said, "Yes." Then he said, "And when is she nearest to her Lord?" They had no answer to give. Ali mentioned this to Fatimah, may Allah's blessings be upon her, and she said, "Return to him and say: O Messenger of Allah, the nearest a woman is to her Lord is when she keeps to the innermost of her house." He went and informed the Prophet, peace be upon him and his family, who said, "This is from you, O Ali." He said, "No, O Messenger of Allah, rather it was Fatimah who said it." The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, said, "She has spoken truly; «إنها بضعة مني» indeed she is a part of me."

From the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family: he forbade a woman to walk unclothed before her husband, and forbade the man to be unclothed before her. He forbade women to comb their hair with wine or to apply its dregs, and he forbade women to imitate men and men to imitate women, and he cursed whoever did so. From him, peace be upon him: "Any woman who perfumes herself, let her not leave her house, nor attend the prayer with us." And from him: he forbade women, when they go out behind men, to walk in the middle of the road with them, and he said, "Women have no portion of the middle of the road." He forbade, peace be upon him, that a woman should wear, when she goes out, a garment that draws notice, or adorn herself with what makes a sound, or strike with her foot so that the chime of her anklet is heard. He forbade women from open display, from wailing, and from raising the voice except out of necessity, and from conversing with men, and from passing the night away from their homes.

He forbade, peace be upon him, the giving of greeting to women. And from him, may Allah's blessings be upon him, he said that a man should not touch a man unless there is a garment between them, nor a woman a woman unless there is a garment between them. From him, peace be upon him and his family: a woman came to him and said, "O Messenger of Allah, I have a husband who is harsh of nature, and I have worked something to turn his affection toward me." The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said to her, "Fie upon you! You have sullied your religion, and the angels of heaven and the angels of earth have cursed you." From him, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, he said: «لو كنت آمراً أحداً أن يسجد لأحد لأمرت المرأة أن تسجد لزوجها» "Had I been commanding anyone to prostrate to another, I would have commanded the woman to prostrate to her husband." He forbade a woman to deny her husband herself when he called her, and to deny him her help and her work. He said, "The best thing with which a woman adorns herself is the fear of Allah and obedience to her husband."

He said, peace be upon him and his family: When a woman knows her Lord and believes in Him and in His Messenger, and acknowledges the merit of the People of the House of her Prophet, and prays the five prayers, and fasts the month of Ramadan, and guards her chastity, and obeys her husband, she enters by whichever of the gates of Paradise she wishes. From him, peace be upon him: a woman sent to him saying, "O Messenger of Allah, my husband has ordered me not to go out, near or far, until he returns from his journey; and my father is in the market. Shall I go out to him?" The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said to her, "Sit in your house and obey your husband." So she did, and her father died. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, sent to her saying, "Truly Allah, mighty and majestic, has forgiven your father on account of your obedience to your husband."

A woman asked him, may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family, "O Messenger of Allah, what is the right of the husband over the wife?" He said, "She must not give in charity from his house except by his leave, nor deny him herself even upon the saddle's back, nor fast a voluntary day except by his leave, nor leave her house except by his leave; and if she goes out without his leave, the angels of heaven, the angels of earth, the angels of mercy, and the angels of wrath curse her until she returns." She said, "O Messenger of Allah, who has the greatest right over a man?" He said, "His parents." "And who has the greatest right over a woman?" He said, "Her husband." She said, "O Messenger of Allah, then have I no right over him to match what he has over me?" He said, "No, not even one in a hundred." And it was related that he, peace be upon him, assigned to his daughter Fatimah, may Allah's blessings be upon her, the service of what was within the house, and assigned to Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, the service of what was outside it.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may Allah's blessings be upon him, was asked about the right of the woman over her husband, the fulfilling of which makes him a doer of good. He said: He feeds her and clothes her, and when she wrongs him he pardons her. He said: My father, may Allah's blessings be upon him, had a wife who used to vex him, and he would forgive her and would say: The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, said: «المرأة كالضلع، إن تركتها انتفعت بها، وإن أقمتها كسرتها» "The woman is like a rib: if you leave it be, you take benefit from it, and if you try to straighten it, you break it." It was said to him, "But the woman is jealous over her husband." He said, "That is love."

He was asked, peace be upon him, about a man who turns away from his wife because worship, fasting, and prayer occupy him from her. He said: The wife of 'Uthman ibn Maz'un came to the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, and said, "O Messenger of Allah, 'Uthman fasts the day and stands the night in prayer." He understood, peace be upon him and his family, what she intended, and he went out to the mosque, where he found 'Uthman ibn Maz'un standing in prayer, and he said, "O 'Uthman, Allah, mighty and majestic, did not send me with monasticism; He sent me with the upright way. Whoever loves my nature, let him follow my Sunnah, and among my Sunnah is marriage." And the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, forbade the striking of women where it is not warranted.

From Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him: a man of the Ansar came to the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, with his daughter and said, "O Messenger of Allah, her husband has struck her; grant her retaliation against him." The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, granted her that. Then Allah, mighty and majestic, sent down: «الرِّجَالُ قَوَّامُونَ عَلَى النِّسَاءِ بِمَا فَضَّلَ اللَّهُ بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ وَبِمَا أَنفَقُوا مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ ۚ فَالصَّالِحَاتُ قَانِتَاتٌ حَافِظَاتٌ لِّلْغَيْبِ بِمَا حَفِظَ اللَّهُ ۚ وَاللَّاتِي تَخَافُونَ نُشُوزَهُنَّ فَعِظُوهُنَّ وَاهْجُرُوهُنَّ فِي الْمَضَاجِعِ وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ ۖ فَإِنْ أَطَعْنَكُمْ فَلَا تَبْغُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ سَبِيلًا ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيًّا كَبِيرًا» "Men are the protectors and maintainers of women, because Allah has favored some of them over others, and because they spend of their wealth. So the righteous women are devoutly obedient, guarding in the husband's absence what Allah would have them guard. As for those from whom you fear discord, admonish them, then leave them apart in their beds, then strike them. But if they obey you, seek no way against them. Surely Allah is Most High, Most Great." (al-Nisa: 34) And the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, then said: «أردت أمراً وأراد الله غيره» "I intended one thing, and Allah intended another."

From him, peace be upon him, he said that jealousy is a part of faith, and a careless yielding is a part of hypocrisy, meaning jealousy in what is forbidden, and the yielding that brings men and women together for that. In another report from Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, forbade al-madhal, which is the abandonment of due jealousy. Its root is from a man's making known his secret, that is, exposing it, and from this is taken a man's exposing of his womenfolk to other men. As for jealousy in what is lawful, it is disliked and forbidden. Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, said: There is no jealousy in the lawful, in light of the saying of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, on the night he brought Fatimah, may Allah's blessings be upon her, in to her husband, after she had come to be his: «لا تُحدثا شيئاً حتى آتيكما» "Do not do anything until I come to you." He said this to them after they had lain down. So Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, held back, and the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, entered and prayed for them and blessed them, and then departed.

From the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, he said: «كُتب الجهاد على رجال أمتي والغيرة على نسائها، فمن صبرت منهن واحتسبت أعطاها الله أجر شهيد» "Striving has been prescribed upon the men of my community, and jealousy upon its women; so whichever of them is patient and seeks her reward, Allah, mighty and majestic, grants her the reward of a martyr." From him, peace be upon him, he mentioned women and what they ask of men, and said: They burden men, when they deck themselves in gold and wear silk; they burden the rich and wear out the poor. And from Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, he said: Whoever obeys his wife in four things, Allah casts him upon his face into the Fire: that he obey her in going to wedding feasts, to gatherings of wailing, to visitings, and to the bathhouses.

**On Marriage Through Guardians**

We have related from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family: «لا نكاح إلا بوليٍّ وشاهدَي عدل» "There is no marriage save with a guardian and two upright witnesses." And he forbade that a woman be given in marriage until she is consulted. From Ali, may Allah's blessings be upon him, he said: None of you should give his daughter in marriage until he consults her, for she knows best her own self; if she keeps silent, or weeps, or laughs, that is her leave, and if she refuses, he does not marry her off. The arranging by fathers of the marriage of their minor sons and daughters is permissible, and they have no right of dissolution when they come of age; and when an agent is authorized to contract a marriage, it is valid. From him, may Allah's blessings be upon him, he said: If a Muslim woman, being of age, authorizes her Christian father to give her in marriage, and he does so, the marriage is valid; but if he gives her in marriage while she is a child, it is not valid, for a disbeliever has no guardianship over a Muslim.

When a woman entrusts two guardians and delegates the matter to both of them, and each of them marries her to a different man, the marriage belongs to the first of the two, provided that the first was unaware of the other's contract. If the two contracts are concluded at one and the same time, one of them is annulled and the matter is begun anew thereafter. From Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali, peace be upon him, and from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, it is related that they said: A paternal grandfather may contract the marriage of his son's minor daughter just as the father contracts it, and he has the prior right in this, even if the two of them should contract at the same moment. All of them are bound by the first contract concluded among them, even if the one whose contract is dissolved was unaware of the other and only learns of it afterward, in which case the matter is resumed.

From Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali, peace be upon him, it is related that he said: Whatever testimony is admissible in financial matters is admissible in marriage, and the testimony of women and of slaves is accepted in it. From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him: He was asked about two witnesses who testified in the marriage of a woman that her father had given her in marriage while she was unwilling, and another witness testified that he had given her in marriage with her consent. He said: The marriage is valid with her consent. He was also asked about two witnesses, one of whom testified that the dowry was one thousand and the other that it was two thousand. He said: If she claims the greater amount, she takes an oath together with her witness, and it is decreed for her. If one of them testifies that her father gave her in marriage while she was a child, and the other testifies that he gave her in marriage when she was of age and without her consent, the testimony is void.

**Mentioning the Witnesses in Marriage**

I have mentioned in what came before this chapter that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said: There is no marriage save with a guardian and two witnesses. This is what is commanded, and upon it action is taken, and by it the marriage is established. It is related from 'Ali, peace be upon him, that he said: A marriage is not valid except with a guardian and witnesses. Abu Ja'far and Abu 'Abdullah, peace be upon them, said: Witnesses were appointed in marriage only for the sake of the child, so that it may be known who his father is, and as a safeguard against either of the spouses denying the marriage, and out of caution before the authorities, lest the two be charged with being a man and a woman united unlawfully. For this reason, and for whatever resembles it, the seeking of witnesses in marriage is obligatory. As for what lies between a person and Allah, Mighty and Majestic, there is nothing upon him if he does not call witnesses, for Allah, Mighty and Majestic, mentioned witnessing only in the matter of divorce and did not mention it in the matter of marriage. Yet witnesses in marriage cannot be dispensed with, because through them the marriage is established, lineage is confirmed, and doubt is removed, and upright persons bear witness to it. Among the witnesses, women and slaves are admissible, provided they are upright.

**Mentioning the Dowries**

We narrate concerning the Commander of the Believers, peace be upon him. Allah, exalted be the One who speaks, said: ﴿ وَآتُوا النِّسَاءَ صَدُقَاتِهِنَّ نِحْلَةً ﴾. 'Ali, peace be upon him, said concerning the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, ﴿ وَآتُوا النِّسَاءَ صَدُقَاتِهِنَّ نِحْلَةً ﴾: Allah, Mighty and Majestic, is saying, give them the gift by which you have made their private parts lawful to you. So whoever wrongs a woman in her dowry has made her private part lawful to himself through fornication. And the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said: Allah, Mighty and Majestic, forgives every sin save the sin of a man who has defrauded a hired laborer of his wage, or a woman of her dowry, or who has sold a free person.

It is related from him, peace be upon him, that he said: The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, did not marry any of his wives, nor did he give any of his daughters in marriage, for more than twelve uqiyyahs and a nashsh of silver. An uqiyyah is forty dirhams, and a nashsh is half an uqiyyah, so that is five hundred dirhams in all, together with a woven cloak and a bed of ram's hide. This is the dowry of the Sunnah. There is no fixed limit set upon dowries, and whatever is named, be it little or much, the marriage is contracted upon it. It is recommended that it be made easy. 'Ali, peace be upon him, said: Among the blessings of a woman is the ease of her marriage and the ease of her womb, so do not be excessive in the dowries of women, for that breeds enmity.

He, peace be upon him, said: A man came to the Prophet, may Allah bless him and his family, and said: O Messenger of Allah, I wish to marry a certain woman. He said: Do you have anything to give her? The man said: No, by Allah, I have nothing. So he looked at a ring upon his hand and said: This ring that is on your hand. The man said: Yes, O Messenger of Allah. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said: Then you have married her with this ring of yours. So ease in marriage is a merit, as has been related, and there is no harm in going beyond it either. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, says: ﴿ وَآتَيْتُمْ إِحْدَاهُنَّ قِنطَارًا فَلَا تَأْخُذُوا مِنْهُ شَيْئًا ﴾. And it has come down from al-Husayn ibn 'Ali, peace be upon them both, that he married a woman and sent to her one hundred slave girls, with each slave girl a thousand dirhams.

We have narrated from Abu 'Abdullah, peace be upon him, that he was asked about the dowry, and he said: It is whatever the two spouses agree upon, be it little or much. But a dowry there must be, and no marriage is permissible without a dowry, though there is no harm in contracting it without one being named, and he may not consummate it until he gives her something, whatever it may be. This is for the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic: ﴿ لَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِن طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ مَا لَمْ تَمَسُّوهُنَّ أَوْ تَفْرِضُوا لَهُنَّ فَرِيضَةً ﴾. So a contract without a named dowry is, on this basis, permissible, but he may not consummate it until he gives her something. Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, was asked about this and said: My father, peace be upon him, married a woman and did not name a dowry for her, and when he wished to consummate the marriage he took a garment from me and handed it to her.

He, peace be upon him, was asked about the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic: ﴿ وَامْرَأَةً مُؤْمِنَةً إِن وَهَبَتْ نَفْسَهَا لِلنَّبِيِّ ﴾. He said: That is something singled out for the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace. No marriage is sound except with a dowry. If he does not name a dowry for her and then dies before her, or divorces her before consummating the marriage, she has no dowry, yet he inherits from her and she from him, and she must observe the waiting period. If he has consummated the marriage, she is entitled to the like of the dowry of the women of her family, according to her condition, her wealth, and her beauty, measured against one of his like in condition and in wealth, provided that this does not exceed the dowry of the Sunnah. If it does exceed it, the excess is returned to him, and the dowry of the Sunnah, as we have mentioned, is five hundred dirhams.

It is related from him, peace be upon him, concerning a man who married a woman upon her own determination, that he said: The marriage is valid; and if the two disagree as to the amount of the dowry, she is entitled to the like of the dowry of the women of her family, neither falling short nor going to excess, and it is not permissible for it to exceed the dowry of the Sunnah.

It is related from him, peace be upon him, that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, forbade the marriage of exchange. Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, said: The marriage of exchange, shighar, is that a man give his daughter in marriage to another man on the condition that the other give him his daughter in marriage, with no dowry between them. He said: Two men came to 'Ali, peace be upon him, and each married the other's daughter without a dowry, so he rejected it and said: No, there must be a dowry. This applies when the contract in such a marriage is made on the basis that the marriage of the one is in return for the marriage of the other, each being set as the recompense for the other, and they oblige themselves to this. But if they contract it as a marriage is contracted without a named dowry, and do not stipulate within it what we have described, then the contract is valid, and each of the two women is entitled to the like of the dowry of the women of her family, in the manner we have described.

They, peace be upon them, permitted the contracting of marriage upon described and known goods. They narrated from 'Ali, peace be upon him, that he said concerning a man who married a woman in return for a young servant: neither falling short nor going to excess. Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, said: Whoever marries a woman for an unknown dowry has not thereby corrupted the marriage; she is entitled to the like of the dowry of her peers, and it shall be no more than the dowry of the Sunnah.

He, peace be upon him, was asked about a man who married a woman in return for a slave girl of his who had been pledged her freedom upon his death, and who then divorced her before consummating the marriage. He said: The woman is entitled to half the service of the pledged slave girl, so that the woman has one day of her service and her master one day; and if the master revokes the pledge of freedom or sells her, the woman is entitled to half her value. They, peace be upon them, said: The dowry belongs to the woman. They narrated from 'Ali, peace be upon him, that he said: Marriage in return for a wage paid to the father is not lawful in Islam, in the manner that Moses hired himself out. The dowry belongs only to the wife. If a man marries a woman on the condition that he perform some work for her, the marriage is valid, and the wage in that case stands in place of the dowry; he may not consummate the marriage until he has done for her the work for which he was engaged, or has given her something.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali, peace be upon him, said: A woman came to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, and said: O Messenger of Allah, give me in marriage. He said: Who will take this woman? A man among the people said: I, O Messenger of Allah. He said: What will you give her? He said: I have no wealth. He said: With no wealth at all? So the woman turned to go. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, repeated his words, and no one rose but that man, and the woman turned away again. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, said to the man a third time: Do you know anything of the Qur'an? He said: Yes. He said: I have given her to you in marriage for what you know of the Qur'an. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali, peace be upon him, said: If a man marries a woman on the condition that he teach her a chapter from the Book of Allah, I would not have him consummate the marriage until he has taught her or given her something.

From 'Ali, peace be upon him, it is related that he said: Once a woman has been brought in to her husband, she has no claim to her dowry after she has entered upon him except by clear proof, meaning, if she claims that he gave her nothing and that something of her dowry remains owing to her. Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, said: If he marries her with a portion paid at once and a portion deferred, the immediate portion falls due before consummation, and he may not consummate the marriage if she withholds herself from him until he pays it to her. The deferred portion runs until death or separation, that is, when no time of payment has been stipulated upon him. But if she stipulates that it become due after consummation, it has been related from 'Ali, peace be upon him, that a woman brought before him a man who had married her, consummated the marriage, and named a term for her dowry, and 'Ali, peace be upon him, said: There is no term for you in her dowry once you have consummated the marriage; her right has fallen due, so pay her her right. This, and Allah knows best, is when he had paid her nothing in advance, or had named for her neither an immediate nor a deferred portion. But if he named that and paid her at once what he had obligated upon himself, then the deferred remainder runs to the time he stipulated, for the words of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace: People are bound by their conditions. Likewise, if he named no term but named a dowry for her, and she refused that he consummate the marriage until he discharge it, that is her right.

From 'Ali, peace be upon him, it is related that he said concerning a man who marries a woman and witnesses are called secretly the first time upon one dowry and openly another time upon a greater dowry: the first is the marriage contract, and by it the matter is taken. From him, peace be upon him, that he said: When the curtains are let down and the doors are shut, the dowry has fallen due. This is the saying of Abu Ja'far and Abu 'Abdullah, peace be upon them, and that is when the woman claims that the union took place. But if she acknowledges that he did not touch her, even though he had let down his curtain and shut his door upon them, and he then divorces her, she is entitled to no more than half the dowry, and she must observe the waiting period, for the waiting period is not waived from her by her claim that he did not touch her once he has shut his door and let down his curtain over her.

From 'Ali and Abu Ja'far and Abu 'Abdullah, peace be upon them all, it is related that they said concerning a man who makes the freeing of his slave woman her dowry: that this is permissible. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali, peace be upon him, said: That is when the condition is set before the freeing, that is, he says: I marry you on the condition that I have made your dowry your freedom, and then he frees her. But if he frees her first and says to her: I free you for the sake of Allah on the condition that your freedom is your dowry and I marry you upon that, then the choice is hers: if she wishes she marries him, and if she wishes she does not, because she has been freed and has come to own herself apart from him. If he marries her after that, let him marry her with a dowry.

From 'Ali, peace be upon him, it is related that he was asked about a man who steals wealth and marries with it, or buys a slave girl with it. He said: The union is lawful, but the liability for the wealth remains upon him, and he is a sinner for what he has done, and he must make restitution for what he stole.

**Mentioning the Conditions in Marriage**

We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, and from the Imams of his household, that he said: Every condition that contradicts the Book of Allah is to be rejected. From 'Ali, peace be upon him, it is related that he ruled concerning a man who married a woman and stipulated to her family that if he were to take another wife over her she would be divorced, or if he were to take a concubine over her the concubine would be free. He said: The condition of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, comes before their conditions. If he wishes, he fulfills his promise; and if he wishes, he marries another over her or takes a concubine, and no wife is divorced from him should he marry her, nor is any concubine freed should he take her. From Abu Ja'far and Abu 'Abdullah, peace be upon them, the like of this is related.

It was narrated from Abu Ja'far and Abu 'Abd-Allah (may Allah's prayers be upon them), as likewise from 'Ali (may Allah's peace be upon him), that he gave judgment concerning a man who married a woman and stipulated that she should hold authority over her own affairs. 'Ali said: This departs from the Sunnah and assigns a right to one who is not its rightful holder. The husband is the guardian; intimacy and divorce rest in his hand. He therefore ruled that the dowry was owed by the husband, that intimacy and divorce remained his, and he nullified the condition.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (may Allah's prayers be upon him) said: A condition that is lawful, one that does not contradict the Book of Allah, is binding upon the man who married the woman on its basis. Among such valid conditions is that she shall remain with her family in a known land, or in a specified dwelling. This is permissible.

It was also narrated from him (may Allah's prayers be upon him) that he said: Whoever marries a woman on the condition that he will provide her a fixed daily maintenance and not be absent from her during the day, that condition does not invalidate the marriage, but neither is it itself binding; she retains the right to the division of nights and to the maintenance due to wives generally. Yet if a man marries a woman who fears that he may harm her or divorce her, and he agrees with her to forgo some of his rights regarding her maintenance and the division of nights, that is permissible, in accordance with the words of Allah the Almighty: "If a woman fears discord or desertion from her husband, there is no blame upon them if they come to an agreement between themselves" (al-Nisa' 128).

Abu 'Abd-Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (may Allah's prayers be upon him) said: If he stipulates for her that, should he take another wife or take a concubine, she shall have one hundred dinars or whatever sum he has bound himself to, the marriage is permissible. If the sum he has committed is drawn from the original dowry, it is binding; if it rests only upon the added stipulation, it is not binding. This is the meaning of the saying of Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (may Allah's prayers be upon him): A man should not make the divorce of one wife a condition for marrying another. The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) forbade a woman to seek the divorce of her sister wife so that she might take her place, for Allah the Almighty is the One who provides for her. And the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) forbade the marriage of pleasure (mut'ah).

Concerning the marriage of pleasure which some had allowed: it is reported that a man would say to a woman, "Give yourself to me in marriage," and would say, "I marry you for this dirham," or two dirhams, or whatever sum was named, for a single union, or a day, or two days, or whatever term they agreed upon. They said that once they agreed on this it became permissible for him until the end of the agreed term; and when the term expired the separation occurred of itself, without divorce. They claimed there was no 'iddah upon her, that she did not inherit from him nor he from her, and that intimacy by this arrangement was not permissible with a virgin, though some of them claimed it was permissible even with women already married. This is pure fornication, in which there is no ambiguity and which admits of no covering. Allah the Almighty has permitted intimacy only through two paths, through the bond of marriage and through the right hand of possession (milk al-yamin), for the Almighty said: "Those who guard their private parts, except with their wives or what their right hands possess, for they are not blameworthy; but whoever seeks beyond that, those are the transgressors" (al-Mu'minun 5–7).

For if this pleasure union were a marriage, the woman could not be parted from the man without divorce and without 'iddah, since Allah the Almighty says: "O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the beginning of their waiting period" (al-Talaq 1); and she would inherit and be inherited from, since Allah says: "And you shall have half of what your wives leave" (al-Nisa' 12), and "They shall have a fourth of what you leave" (al-Nisa' 12); and the child would be attached to them both, for the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) said: "The child belongs to the bed," meaning to the owner of the bed. But in this arrangement there is neither marriage nor right of possession, and what remains is forbidden. The reports forbidding the marriage of pleasure from the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) are many, as is his saying, "There is no marriage except with a guardian and two upright witnesses." Those who uphold the marriage of pleasure themselves claim that it is contracted without a guardian and without witnesses, and so it cannot stand. It was narrated from 'Ali, Abu Ja'far, and Abu 'Abd-Allah (may Allah's prayers be upon them all) that they declared the marriage of pleasure void, forbade it, and prohibited it.

It was narrated from 'Ali (may Allah's peace be upon him) that he gave judgment concerning a slave-woman who had fled from her master and deceived a man by claiming to be free, so that he married her on that basis and she bore him children; then her master established his claim over her. He ruled that she be returned to her master, and that the man who had been deceived owed her master the value of his children by her, unless the master chose to grant the children to their father. If the man had bought her and she bore him a child, and her master then established his claim, she was to be returned to the one whose right was vindicated, and the value of the child was owed by the father, who could in turn recover it from the one who had sold her to him. But if he had taken her by force, or had known when he married her that she was owned and that her master had not given her in marriage, then both she and her child belonged to the master.

From 'Ali (may Allah's prayers be upon him) it was narrated concerning a free woman whom a male slave had approached, claiming to be free, so that she married him on that basis and then learned that he was owned: they may be separated if the woman wishes. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (may Allah's prayers be upon him) said: She holds authority over her own decision; if she wishes she may remain with him, and if she wishes she may leave him. If she chooses to separate and the marriage has not been consummated, she is owed nothing; but if it has been consummated, she is entitled to her dowry, and the liability falls upon whoever deceived her, whether the slave himself or another. When the slave is freed, if his lord does not give him in marriage to her, the dowry rests with the slave once he is free; and if the lord gave him in marriage, or gave him leave to marry her, then her dowry is owed by the lord.

It was narrated from 'Ali, Abu Ja'far, and Abu 'Abd-Allah (may Allah's prayers be upon them) concerning a man to whom another woman was brought as a bride, so that he consummated the marriage believing her to be his wife and only afterward learned otherwise: he must return that woman, and she is entitled to a dowry for what was done to her, claimed against the one who deceived him, and his true wife, the one he had actually married, is to be restored to him.

It was narrated from them concerning a man who is impotent and unable to approach women, yet conceals this from the woman he marries: she has the right of choice once she comes to know, and she is entitled to the dowry if she exercises that choice and the marriage has been consummated. They also said concerning a man who marries a woman and finds in her leprosy, or vitiligo, or blindness, or insanity, or a condition in the private parts that prevents intercourse, or a manifest lameness: the husband has the right of choice; if he wishes he may keep her, and if he wishes he may return her. She is entitled to the dowry if he has had intercourse with her, and his claim for it is against the one who deceived him with her, while the woman herself owes nothing in return. For defects other than these she is not to be returned.

It was narrated from 'Ali (may Allah's peace be upon him) concerning a man who marries a woman as a virgin and finds her otherwise: he saw nothing objectionable in this, and said that virginity may be lost through a leap, through ablution, or through the passing of long years.

Mentioning the Forbidden Marriage and the Permissible Marriage

Allah the Almighty said: "And do not marry those whom your fathers married of women, except what has already passed; it is an indecency and a hateful thing and an evil way. Forbidden to you are your mothers, your daughters..." (al-Nisa' 22–23). Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has thus forbidden in the plain text of His Book the marriage of those He named explicitly, and the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him and his family) and the Imams of his house (may Allah's prayers be upon them all) made clear what required clarification.

It came from 'Ali and Abu 'Abd-Allah (may Allah's prayers be upon them) concerning the mention of the mothers of wives that the matter is unrestricted: when a man marries a woman, her mother becomes forbidden to him, whether or not he has consummated the marriage with the daughter. They said: if a man marries a woman and then divorces her, or she dies before he has consummated the marriage, the marriage of her daughter remains lawful for him, in keeping with the words of Allah: "And your stepdaughters under your guardianship, born of your wives with whom you have consummated marriage; but if you have not consummated with them, there is no blame upon you" (al-Nisa' 23). The "guardianship" here is the bond of inviolability. But if he has consummated the marriage with the woman, her daughter becomes forbidden to him; and likewise, if he has seen of her what is forbidden to other men, he may not marry her daughter.

It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (may Allah's prayers be upon him) concerning a man who marries a woman and then dies leaving her, or divorces her before consummation: she is forbidden to his son for all the generations that descend, and to his fathers however far they ascend. And if a man looks upon his bondswoman with desire, or touches her, or looks upon her nakedness, she becomes forbidden to his sons and to his fathers. But if one of them owned her and did not approach her, nor touch her, nor look upon her with desire, and then the other came to own her, she is lawful to him. They permitted the owning of two sisters together, while forbidding their combination in marriage. They said: if a man has approached one of the two, the other becomes forbidden to him, and he may not approach the second until the first has left his ownership through manumission, sale, or gift; and it does not suffice for him to give her to his son. If she dies, the other becomes lawful to him; what is forbidden is only their combination in marriage, for Allah says: "And that you combine two sisters" (al-Nisa' 23), making no exception there between the owned woman and the free.

It was narrated from 'Ali, Abu Ja'far, and Abu 'Abd-Allah (may Allah's prayers be upon them) that a man should not marry the sister of a woman he has divorced until her 'iddah has elapsed and his right of revocation over her is extinguished; for so long as that right remains, he has effectively joined the two sisters together. But if the divorce was a final and irrevocable one, he may marry her sister, or another, provided he has no more than three other free wives besides her. They also said that a woman may not be joined with her paternal aunt, nor with her maternal aunt. 'Ali (peace be upon him) said: The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) forbade that, and made it unlawful. The daughter of Hamza was offered to him in marriage, and he said: Do you not know that she is the daughter of my brother through suckling, for I was nursed together with Hamza.

They (peace be upon them) saw no harm in joining the daughters of paternal uncles with the daughters of maternal uncles, nor in a man's marrying both his wife and a daughter of his by another. Likewise, when a man divorces a woman or dies and another man marries her thereafter, there is no harm in the son of her first husband, by another wife, marrying the daughter of her second husband by another wife. They said: if a man commits fornication with the mother of his wife, or her sister, or her daughter, this does not corrupt his marriage, for the unlawful does not corrupt the lawful. But if he commits fornication with a woman and she then bears a child, it is not proper for his son by another wife to marry that woman's daughter. They granted permission regarding the woman with whom a man had committed fornication once both have repented.

It was narrated from 'Ali (may Allah's peace be upon him) concerning a woman who commits adultery before her husband has consummated the marriage with her: they are separated, she is owed no dowry, and the prescribed punishment (hadd) is carried out upon her. Her dowry is forfeited because the offense arose from her side. Yet the separation rests upon the husband's wish: if he holds to her, she remains his wife.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (peace be upon him), when asked about a sick man who contracts a marriage and then dies, said: the marriage is valid, and she inherits from him.

It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him) concerning a man who marries two sisters, or five women, in a single marriage contract: the one whose name was mentioned first among the sisters at the time of the contract has a valid marriage, and four of the five women have valid marriages, while the marriage of the second sister, and of the fifth woman after the four, is annulled. If it cannot be known which was named first, the whole contract is void. They (peace be upon them) said concerning a man who marries a woman during her 'iddah, or marries her while one of the two is in a state of consecration, both knowing that this is not lawful: they are separated, and she is never again lawful to him. But if they were ignorant, they are separated, and they may contract anew once she becomes lawful to suitors.

Mentioning the Missing Husband

We have narrated from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (peace be upon him) that he said: if a man is reported dead to his family, so that his wife observes her 'iddah and then remarries, and afterward the first husband returns, the first husband has the greater right to her, whether or not the second husband has consummated the marriage. If the second husband has consummated it, she is entitled to her dowry for what was made lawful from her.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: the wife of the missing man is to be left in her state so long as she keeps silent. But if she brings her case before the governor, he sets for her a term of four years and writes to inquire after her husband. If four years pass and no news of him comes, the governor summons the guardian of the missing man and asks whether the missing man has wealth. If he has wealth from which she may be maintained, and the guardian spends upon her from it, or the guardian spends upon her from his own means, then she has no way to remarry. But if the guardian will not maintain her and the missing man has no wealth, the governor compels the guardian to divorce her with a single pronouncement at the onset of her 'iddah while she is in a state of purity, and the guardian's divorce stands as the husband's divorce. If her husband returns before her 'iddah is complete, he has the greater right to her; and if her 'iddah has ended, she is lawful to suitors.

It was said to him: O son of the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family), what is to be done if the guardian provides for her and she says, "I cannot be patient; I want what other women have"? He answered that she has no such recourse, and no claim to it, so long as the guardian provides for her.

The missing man (al-mafqūd) is one who has departed his home and whose whereabouts are unknown. He may have been seen between the two battle lines in time of war, after which the people scattered and he was lost; or he may have set sail on a ship that capsized; or it may simply be that no one on the journey knows where he settled. As for a man whose location is known, his ruling is not that of the missing man. The ruling concerning his wife is that of a woman whose husband is merely absent (ghāʾib): she may not remarry unless he dies or divorces her. He (peace be upon him) said: if the whereabouts of the absent man are known, his wife may not remarry.

On Breastfeeding (al-Riḍāʿ)

I have mentioned above the saying of Allah Almighty: ﴿وَأُمَّهَاتُكُمُ اللَّاتِي أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ وَأَخَوَاتُكُم مِّنَ الرَّضَاعَةِ﴾, "and your mothers who have suckled you, and your sisters through breastfeeding." We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him and his family, and peace be upon them all) that what is forbidden through lineage is forbidden through breastfeeding. I have already set out which of the women are forbidden in marriage, and whoever comes, through breastfeeding, to occupy the place of one of these holds the same status of prohibition.

It is permissible for a man to marry a woman who has breastfed his sons, and to marry from among her daughters those she did not nurse him alongside, because she is not his mother through breastfeeding and is not a wife to his father. He may likewise marry her daughter when the woman is among the daughters of a woman who breastfed his brother or his sister. In the same way, a man may marry the daughters of women who breastfed his son, and their daughters, for they did not nurse his son, and there is no bond between them and him, whether through breastfeeding or otherwise. What is forbidden is to marry the nursing woman herself; but the daughters of paternal uncles and aunts, and of maternal uncles and aunts, through breastfeeding are lawful to marry, just as they are lawful through lineage. A man may marry a woman while his father marries her daughter by another man, and a father and son may each marry one of two sisters.

It was narrated from ʿAlī (peace be upon him) that he said: breastfeeding makes forbidden whether it be little or much; even a single suckle creates prohibition, by the saying of Allah Almighty, ﴿وَأُمَّهَاتُكُمُ اللَّاتِي أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ﴾, "and your mothers who have suckled you." Breastfeeding applies to the little and the much alike, for even a small quantity nourishes, and from it flesh and blood grow and bone is strengthened in proportion.

It was narrated from ʿAlī (peace be upon him) that he said: the breastfeeding that creates prohibition is that which occurs within the two years, for Allah says: ﴿وَالْوَالِدَاتُ يُرْضِعْنَ أَوْلَادَهُنَّ حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَن يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ﴾, "and mothers shall suckle their children for two complete years, for whoever wishes to complete the suckling." What occurs after the two years is not breastfeeding and creates no prohibition. The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) said: "لا رضاع بعد فطام," "There is no breastfeeding after weaning."

It is narrated from ʿAlī, Abū Jaʿfar, and Abū ʿAbd Allāh (peace be upon them all) concerning a woman who gives her husband her milk to drink in order, by that pretense, to make herself forbidden to him, or who gives it to her bondswoman, or nurses her, to make her forbidden to him: they said (peace be upon them) that the man should discipline his wife, but that this creates no prohibition for him, because the breastfeeding of an adult is not breastfeeding. If a man is suckled after two years have passed from his birth, or a wife gives her husband her milk to drink, nothing is thereby made forbidden. But whatever occurs before the two years, whether by suckling, or by drinking a woman's milk, or by its being administered into the throat (wajūr) or the nose (saʿūṭ), is breastfeeding.

They said (peace be upon them): the milk of the stallion creates prohibition, meaning the milk attributed to the man who fathered it. This is when a man has several wives, or umm-walad mothers, in his household, and one of them nurses a slave boy while another nurses a slave girl: the two become siblings through breastfeeding, even though they did not nurse at the breast of one woman, because the one father unites them and they were nursed by the milk of his wives, so the milk is attributed to him.

Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī (peace be upon him) was asked about the wife of a man who nursed a slave girl: is it lawful for his son by another woman to marry her? He said: No. It was said to him: does she then stand to him as a sister through breastfeeding? He said: Yes, from the father's side. He said: a man had two wives, each of whom bore a son, and one of the two wives nursed a slave girl belonging to other people. He said: the other son may not marry this slave girl, for she was nursed by the milk of the same father, and what lineage forbids, breastfeeding forbids.

He (peace be upon him) was asked about a slave girl belonging to a man, whom his wife, or the mother of his child, claimed to have nursed. He said: she is not to be believed, for she is drawing something to herself. Concerning a woman who said she had nursed a slave boy and a slave girl and then denied it, he said: if she denies it, that stands. It was said to him: and if she afterward retracts her denial and claims again that she nursed them? He said: she is not to be believed. The testimony of a single upright woman is accepted in matters of breastfeeding; but if no upright woman is available, her testimony alone does not suffice.

They (peace be upon them) forbade seeking a wet-nurse whose milk comes of fornication, as with a woman who commits adultery and bears a child by it: such a woman should not be sought as a wet-nurse, nor taken as a foster-nurse (ẓiʾr), neither she nor her daughter born of adultery. Yet they permitted seeking a wet-nurse among polytheist women if they are within a marriage, while disliking the seeking of a wet-nurse from the milk of those known for enmity toward the family of Muḥammad (peace be upon him and his family). Breastfeeding by the milk of fornication makes nothing forbidden. Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) said: unlawful milk does not render the lawful unlawful, as with a woman nursed first by lawful milk and afterward by the milk of fornication. He said: whoever nurses a girl with the milk of fornication does not thereby make it forbidden to marry her, because unlawful milk does not render the lawful forbidden.

It was narrated from ʿAlī (peace be upon him) that he ruled concerning a man who married a woman, gave her her dowry, and had not yet consummated the marriage, after which it was established that there was a nursing relationship between them: she must return to him what she had taken. And it was narrated from the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) that he forbade women to nurse freely those boys whom they do not know.

On the Marriage of Bondswomen (Nikāḥ al-Imāʾ)

Allah Almighty said: ﴿وَمَن لَّمْ يَسْتَطِعْ مِنكُمْ طَوْلًا أَن يَنكِحَ الْمُحْصَنَاتِ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ فَمِن مَّا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُم مِّن فَتَيَاتِكُمُ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ﴾, up to His saying ﴿ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنْ خَشِيَ الْعَنَتَ مِنكُمْ ۚ وَأَن تَصْبِرُوا خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ﴾: "And whoever among you cannot afford to marry free believing women, then from those whom your right hands possess, of your believing bondswomen," up to His saying, "That is for him among you who fears falling into sin; and that you be patient is better for you, and Allah is Forgiving, Merciful."

It was narrated from ʿAlī (peace be upon him), commenting on the words of Allah Almighty ﴿ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنْ خَشِيَ الْعَنَتَ مِنكُمْ﴾, "That is for him among you who fears falling into sin," that he said: this means adultery, nothing else. So a free Muslim should not marry a bondswoman until he fears being overcome by desire and cannot find the means to marry a free woman. If he has the means to a free woman, he should not marry a bondswoman; and if he does not fear falling into sin, he likewise should not marry her, for Allah Almighty has permitted free men the marriage of bondswomen only upon these two conditions.

Abū ʿAbd Allāh Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) said: a free man may not marry a bondswoman until two conditions are joined in him, the fear of sin and the lack of means. If he is driven to it, let him marry her. When a free man marries a bondswoman, the children she bears him belong to her master as slaves, unless he stipulates at the marriage that whatever children she bears him shall be free; if he stipulates that, they are free, and the ruling on the child follows the ruling on the mother, in freedom and in bondage. If a slave marries a free woman, their children are free, and his master may not stipulate that the free woman's children be slaves, as he might with the children of a bondswoman, because the freeing of a bondswoman's children rests with their master, who frees them if he wishes. A woman may not take ownership of her own children while they are free, nor may anyone else.

A bondswoman may not be given in marriage except with the permission of her master, for Allah says: ﴿فَانكِحُوهُنَّ بِإِذْنِ أَهْلِهِنَّ﴾, "So marry them with the permission of their families." If a bondswoman is jointly owned by two men, she is not lawful to either of them, even if the other permits it, because the part of a person cannot be divided, nor made lawful by gift. If one of the two gives her in marriage without the permission of his partner, the marriage is not valid unless the other ratifies it, and the husband may not consummate it until the second owner permits the marriage; if he does not permit it, the marriage is dissolved. Abū ʿAbd Allāh Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) was asked about a bondswoman jointly owned by two men, one of whom gives her in marriage while the other is absent: is the marriage valid? He said: if the absent owner is averse to it, it is not valid.

It was narrated from ʿAlī (peace be upon him) that he said: it is not lawful for a free Muslim to marry a Jewish or a Christian bondswoman, for Allah Almighty says ﴿مِّن فَتَيَاتِكُمُ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ﴾, "of your believing bondswomen." Likewise a free man should not marry a Magian or a polytheist bondswoman; for were that lawful, the polytheists would come to enslave the children of the Muslims. If a man wishes to marry a bondswoman, let him free her first, and then marry her if he wishes and she wishes. The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him and his family) married Ṣafiyyah bint Ḥuyayy ibn Akhṭab and drew the veil (ḥijāb) over her. Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) said: any man who frees his slave woman and wishes to marry her, let it be with witnesses and an appointed dower; and if he makes her emancipation her dower, that is permitted, as I have explained in the foregoing.

A woman under a contract of emancipation (mukātabah) may be married with the permission of her master. ʿAlī (peace be upon him) was asked about the marriage of a mukātabah, and he said: she may marry; and whatever child is born to her during her contract of emancipation stands in her own position, so that of that child what is freed in her is freed, and what remains in bondage in her remains in bondage. We shall mention in the chapter on those under emancipation contracts how this is reckoned, if Allah wills.

It was narrated from ʿAlī (peace be upon him) that he said concerning a man who marries a bondswoman over a free wife: they are to be separated, and he is charged her dower for what he made lawful of her person if he consummated the marriage; if he did not consummate it, she is owed nothing. Abū ʿAbd Allāh Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) said: if a man marries a free woman alongside a bondswoman and the free woman knows of it, he may divide his time, two portions to the free woman and one portion to the bondswoman. If the free woman did not know that he had a bondswoman when he married her, and afterward learns of it, she has the choice: if she wishes she may remain, and if she wishes she may leave his bond, and she is owed the dower for what he made lawful of her person if he consummated the marriage; but if he did not consummate it and she chooses to part from him, she is owed nothing.

It was narrated from him that he said: it is not lawful for a free man to marry a bondswoman by herself. If he marries a free woman and a bondswoman in a single contract, one looks to whose name began the contract: if the free woman's name began it, the marriage of the bondswoman is void; but if the bondswoman's name began it and the free woman accepts to remain alongside her, the marriage of both is established, provided the man is one for whom the marriage of bondswomen is lawful, as we have described. A man may contract marriage upon a free woman while lacking the means for her dower; but if he does have the means, the marriage of the bondswoman is void, whether he began with her or named her second. Likewise, if it is not known with which of them the marriage of the bondswoman began, the marriage of the bondswoman is invalid.

He was asked about the maintenance (nafaqah) of a bondswoman wife. He said: when a free man marries a bondswoman, she serves her people by day and comes to her husband by night, and he owes her maintenance when they do this; but if they bar her from him by night, he owes her no maintenance.

Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) was asked about a man who has young children who own a slave girl: may he lie with her? He said: if he desires her, let him set a just value upon himself for her, so that her price stands against him as a debt to his children; then he owns her and may lie with her if he wishes. But if the children are grown, she is not lawful to him unless they sell her to him, and it is not lawful for him to do otherwise. A man has no right to any part of his child's wealth except by the child's own goodwill, save that, if he is driven to it, he may eat of it what sustains him, in the customary measure and without indulgence, when he is in hardship and the child is wealthy.

It was narrated from him (peace be upon him) that he was asked about a man who had a slave girl, and his wife, fearing he would take her for himself, ordered her son to lie with her, and he did. He said: she has sinned, and her son has sinned, and the son is liable to the prescribed punishment (ḥadd) if he is an adult. And he disliked that men should approach her, even though she is thereby forbidden to the master, because the unlawful does not render the lawful forbidden.

It was narrated from ʿAlī (peace be upon him) that he disliked a man's lying with a bondswoman over whom another holds a stipulated right. The mudabbarah may be married with the permission of her master, and her ruling remains that of a bondswoman until her master dies, whereupon she is freed. If a bondswoman is freed while she has a husband: if her husband is free, she remains his wife and has no choice; but if he is a slave, she has the choice, upon being freed, to remain with him or to leave.

The matter of the freed slave woman is settled by what the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, did with Barirah. She was a slave woman married to a slave man, and she was grieved by her situation. When she was set free, the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, gave her the choice, and she chose herself rather than her husband. He then ordered her to observe a waiting period of three menstrual cycles. And he said, peace be upon him: if her master assists her to fulfill her emancipation contract, even if she is elderly, she has no right of choice against her husband on account of that assistance.

**The marriage of slaves**

Allah, exalted be the One who speaks, said: ﴿ عَبْدًا مَمْلُوكًا لَا يَقْدِرُ عَلَىٰ شَيْءٍ ﴾, "A slave who is owned and has no power over anything" (al-Nahl: 75). The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, forbade a slave to marry without the permission of his master. He said: any free woman who gives herself in marriage to a slave without the permission of her guardian has made her person unlawful and is owed no dower, meaning if the master denies the marriage. But if the master permits his slave to marry, then it is valid, and the dower falls upon the master when he is content with it.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, was asked about a man who marries his own slave woman to one of his male slaves. He said: he says, "I have given her to you in marriage," and he gives her some small thing as a dower. The right of separation remains in the hand of the master: if he wishes he may part them without a divorce, and if he sells the slave man, that selling is itself a separation between them; and if he sells them together, the buyer may part them if he wishes.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, said: if a slave woman is sold and she has a free husband, she remains his wife, and her person is not lawful to the buyer until her husband divorces her or dies and she completes her waiting period. From Ali, peace be upon him: if a woman comes to own her husband, or any portion of him, the marriage between them is dissolved by the ownership itself, and she becomes the owner of her own person. It is not lawful for a woman to marry her own slave unless she first frees him; if she wishes, she may free him and then marry him afterward.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him: the slave may purchase and may approach of the slave women whatever his master has permitted him, and he may not marry more than two free women. He may marry four slave women, and the divorce of those he marries rests in the hand of his master, who may divorce them on his behalf whenever he wishes.

**The marriage of polytheists**

The marriages of polytheists among themselves, whether they belong to a single religious community or to differing ones, are valid when contracted according to what their own religion requires, and it is not fitting that such marriages be annulled. We have narrated from Ali, peace be upon him, that he said: if a man and his wife are taken among the spoils, their marriage holds, and even if her husband is captured some days after her, they remain as they were, unless one of them is carried off and brought into the House of Islam. If that occurs, their marriage is dissolved, and she is not lawful to him except by a new contract, even if one of them had been taken before the other. So long as either of them remains a captive among the spoils, for as long as they remain there, their marriage stands between them. But once one of them enters the House of Islam, their marriage is dissolved. If she embraces Islam and her husband is then captured after her acceptance of Islam, and she has passed three menstrual cycles, yet she had not been carried off and her husband had not embraced Islam, then they remain upon their marriage. The one who owns them both may separate them if he wishes, as he may do with the rest of his slaves, unless at the time of their capture they were granted safe conduct on the condition that they not be parted, and that was contracted for them, in which case their separation is not obligatory.

From Ali, peace be upon him: it is not lawful for Muslims to marry polytheist women in the abode of war while the rulings of the polytheists hold sway over them. And from him, peace be upon him: if a woman of the people of war seeks safe conduct and comes out to the House of Islam while she has a polytheist husband, his marital bond to her is severed, and she may marry if she wishes after her womb has been cleared. Should her husband then come as a Muslim under safe conduct, he has no claim upon her except by a renewed marriage, because when she came out to the House of Islam his bond to her was already severed.

If a man embraces Islam while he has a polytheist wife, she remains his wife. But if a woman embraces Islam while her husband remains a polytheist, a separation is made between them. If her husband embraces Islam before her waiting period has ended, they remain upon their marriage; if her waiting period has expired, she becomes the owner of her own person.

From Ali and Abu Ja'far, peace be upon them both: they forbade marriage with the Christian women of the Arabs and the eating of their slaughtered animals. From Ali, peace be upon him: an apostate who renounces his faith and joins the abode of war is in the standing of a dead man. His wealth is divided among his heirs, his wives observe the waiting period of widows, and they may then marry if they wish.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him: if a woman embraces Islam while her husband remains a polytheist, she is separated from him should he not embrace Islam, and she keeps her full dower; if he had not consummated the marriage with her, she is entitled to half the dower. And if polytheists embrace Islam, they remain upon their existing marriages. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, said: "Confirm the people of the age of ignorance upon whatever state they entered Islam, whether of marriage, divorce, or inheritance."

From Ali, peace be upon him, concerning the polytheist who embraces Islam while he has two free sisters as wives, or more than four free wives: he is left with the first of the two sisters he married, and a separation is made between him and the second, and between him and whatever wives he took beyond the four free women. And from him, peace be upon him: Allah made the women of the People of the Book lawful for the Muslims only when Muslim women were few; and when Muslim women became many, Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ وَلَا تَنْكِحُوا الْمُشْرِكَاتِ حَتَّى يُؤْمِنَّ ﴾, "And do not marry polytheist women until they believe" (al-Baqarah: 221). And He said: ﴿ وَلَا تُمْسِكُوا بِعِصَمِ الْكَوَافِرِ ﴾, "And do not hold to the marriage bonds of disbelieving women" (al-Mumtahanah: 10). The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, forbade a Muslim man to marry a non-Muslim woman while he is able to find a Muslim woman, and forbade a polytheist to marry a Muslim woman.

If a polytheist embraces Islam while he has a polytheist wife, he may keep her with him if he desires her, in the hope that Allah, mighty and majestic, will guide her, and he may marry above her up to three Muslim women, if she is aware of her standing. If he marries a Muslim woman while he still has a polytheist wife, and the Muslim woman does not know of this, and she learns of it after consummation, then the matter is placed in her own hand: if she wishes to remain with him she may stay, and if she wishes to depart she may go. She is entitled to her dower for what was made lawful of her person, and she may marry whomever she wishes once her waiting period has elapsed, without need of a divorce. But if he divorces the polytheist wife before the waiting period has elapsed, counted from the day the Muslim wife made her choice, then he has the greater right to her.

**The division of turns among co-wives**

Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ وَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعُوا أَنْ تَعْدِلُوا بَيْنَ النِّسَاءِ وَلَوْ حَرَصْتُمْ فَلَا تَمِيلُوا كُلَّ الْمَيْلِ فَتَذَرُوهَا كَالْمُعَلَّقَةِ ﴾, "You will never be able to deal justly between wives, even though you are eager to do so; yet do not incline altogether away, so as to leave her as one suspended" (al-Nisa': 129). Thus the Exalted in praise made known that justice between wives in matters of affection, desire, and ardor, apart from intercourse, cannot be attained, while in matters of maintenance, lodging, gifts, and the like, justice can indeed be practiced. So it is upon a man, if he has several wives, to be with each of them by day and night in turn.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, was asked about a man who has wives and is intimate with some of them and not others. He said: it is upon him to spend with each one her night, and to remain with her through her morning. If he is not stirred to intercourse, nothing is held against him; but if a man is stirred to intercourse while he is with one of his wives, it is not fitting that he abandon this and withhold himself for another.

From Ali, peace be upon him, when he was asked about a man who has wives and then sets out on a journey: he said, when he returns let him begin with the one whose right of a turn is due. And from him, peace be upon him: if a man has only one wife, it is not fitting that he leave her without spending the night with her one night out of four, and in the remaining three he may do what Allah has made lawful for him.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, said: if a man has two wives, he may set apart one of them for the three nights that are his own and assign to the other her single night. Likewise, if he has three wives, he assigns to each her night and devotes the fourth to whichever of them he wishes. If he has four, he may not favor one of them above another.

From Ali, peace be upon him, concerning a man who has a wife or several wives and then marries again: if he marries a virgin, he stays with her seven nights, and if he marries a woman previously married, he stays with her three nights; thereafter he divides the nights equally among his wives. He was asked about the words of Allah, mighty and majestic: ﴿ وَإِنِ امْرَأَةٌ خَافَتْ مِنْ بَعْلِهَا نُشُوزًا أَوْ إِعْرَاضًا فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا أَنْ يُصْلِحَا بَيْنَهُمَا صُلْحًا وَالصُّلْحُ خَيْرٌ ﴾, "If a woman fears ill treatment or aversion from her husband, there is no blame upon them if they reach a reconciliation between themselves, and reconciliation is best" (al-Nisa': 128). He said: this concerns a man who has two wives and becomes unable to fulfill the rights of one of them, or she is a dhimmi woman, so he inclines toward the other and wishes to divorce the first, while she dislikes that. So she reconciles with him on the condition that he come to her from time to time, or that she yield to him her share of that.

**Maintenance owed to wives**

Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ قَدْ عَلِمْنَا مَا فَرَضْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ فِي أَزْوَاجِهِمْ وَمَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُهُمْ ﴾, "We have known what We have made obligatory upon them concerning their wives and those whom their right hands possess" (al-Ahzab: 50). And the Blessed and Exalted said: ﴿ وَارْزُقُوهُمْ فِيهَا وَاكْسُوهُمْ وَقُولُوا لَهُمْ قَوْلًا مَعْرُوفًا ﴾, "And provide for them therein and clothe them, and speak to them words of kindness" (al-Nisa': 5).

It is narrated from the family of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, that he addressed the people in the Farewell Pilgrimage, mentioned the women, and enjoined the people to treat them well, saying: "They have over you their sustenance and their clothing in a fitting manner." He forbade, peace be upon him and his family, that a man eat his fill while his family goes hungry, and said: "It is enough sin for a man that he neglect those whom he supports." It is narrated from him, peace be upon him and his family, that he said: "There are seven among the foremost of deeds, so keep to them," and he mentioned among them maintenance for one's dependents. And from him, peace be upon him and his family: "The severest of trials is many dependents with little wealth, and few dependents is one of the two forms of ease." And from him, peace be upon him and his family: "Whoever is moderate in his livelihood, Allah provides for him, and whoever is wasteful, Allah deprives him." And from him, peace be upon him and his family: "When Allah, mighty and majestic, intends good for a household, He grants them understanding in religion, provides them gentleness in their livelihood, and grants them moderation in their affairs." And from him, peace be upon him and his family: "Gentle moderation is half of livelihood; no man who lives in moderation is ever in want."

From Ali, peace be upon him: if a man finds nothing to spend upon his wife, he is given a respite; if he brings her something, meaning what suffices to feed her, they are not separated, and if he finds nothing at all he is granted a term, and then they are separated. And from him, peace be upon him: a woman complained against her husband that he would not maintain her, intending harm to her, so he imprisoned him on account of her maintenance. And he said, peace be upon him: any woman who leaves her husband's house without his permission is owed no maintenance until she returns.

And from him, peace be upon him, concerning the man who divorces his wife while she is nursing his child by her: she has the better right to the nursing, and she takes from him what would be given to a wet nurse. And if her husband dies and leaves with her a child of his whom she nurses, she is entitled to the like of a nurse's wage drawn from the child's wealth, if the child has wealth from inheritance or otherwise, and the guardian has no right to remove the child from her lap.

Maintenance owed to wives, and to those relatives upon whom it is obligatory, is according to one's means and capacity. Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ لِيُنْفِقْ ذُو سَعَةٍ مِنْ سَعَتِهِ وَمَنْ قُدِرَ عَلَيْهِ رِزْقُهُ فَلْيُنْفِقْ مِمَّا آتَاهُ اللَّهُ ﴾, "Let a man of ample means spend out of his ampleness, and whoever has his provision straitened, let him spend out of what Allah has given him" (al-Talaq: 7). So the ruler imposes upon the one obliged the maintenance of his like, without extravagance and without stinting. Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿ وَالَّذِينَ إِذَا أَنْفَقُوا لَمْ يُسْرِفُوا وَلَمْ يَقْتُرُوا وَكَانَ بَيْنَ ذَلِكَ قَوَامًا ﴾, "And those who, when they spend, are neither extravagant nor stinting, but hold a just balance between the two" (al-Furqan: 67).

This is a thing well known and not obscure, requiring no recourse to opinion or independent reasoning, for its measure is known to people from their customary provisions in every place and time, and according to want and plenty, the means at hand, cheapness and dearness, poverty and wealth. Every poor person who is unable to manage or to work, his maintenance falls upon the one who would inherit from him were he to die, in proportion to what that heir would receive of his estate, except the wife, for her maintenance is upon her husband and he owes her no return. If the one upon whom the maintenance is obligatory is himself poor and has nothing to spend, then his case is like that of one who has no guardian to provide for him: he seeks his provision from the provision of Allah, glorified be His mention. A man is not bound to provide for one distant from him who would not inherit from him were he to die, when his own heir is poor and without wealth, because his maintenance is owed only there, and Allah, mighty and majestic, has excused him, for His words: ﴿ لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا ﴾, "Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity" (al-Baqarah: 286), and His words: ﴿ لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا مَا آتَاهَا ﴾, "Allah does not burden a soul except with what He has given it" (al-Talaq: 7). If he should afterward come into ease, he spends upon the one in his charge from that time onward, and he has no right to be held to account for what has already passed.

**Al-istibra', the clearing of the womb**

We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, that a man of the Ansar invited the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family, to a meal, and he accepted. At the Ansari's house there was a slave woman, great with child, going to and fro serving the food. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family, said to him: "What is this?" He said: "A slave woman of mine, whom I bought some days ago, and she is pregnant." He said: "Perhaps you have approached her?" He said: "Yes, O Messenger of Allah, I have done so." Then the Messenger of Allah said to him: "Were it not for the sanctity of your food, I would have invoked upon you a curse that would enter upon you in your grave. Free what is in her womb, for your seed has already nourished its hearing, its sight, its flesh, its blood, its hair, and its skin."

And Ali, peace be upon him, said: it is not lawful for a man to approach a woman pregnant by another until she delivers what is in her womb and becomes pure; and if he purchases a slave woman who is pregnant, he must not approach her until she gives birth and becomes pure. And from the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family: a slave woman, when a man would approach her, is to be cleared by one menstrual cycle.

On istibra'

'Ali, peace be upon him, said: whoever buys a young slave girl who has not yet reached puberty and has shown no sign of it, and over whom there is no fear of pregnancy, may approach her if he wishes and is under no obligation to perform istibra' upon her. He said: whoever buys a slave girl who has despaired of menstruation should set her apart for two months and then approach her if he wishes. This applies when it is doubted that she may still menstruate, even though her menses appear to have ceased. Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, the prayers of Allah be upon him, said: whoever buys an old woman and knows with certainty that she does not menstruate, there being no doubt in her case, may approach her if he wishes and need not perform istibra'.

He said: whoever buys a slave girl from a woman may approach her before performing istibra', and there is no harm in that; yet if he performs istibra' as a safeguard for himself, that is better. Istibra' is, however, an obligation upon the seller: if he has approached the slave woman and then wishes to sell her, he must not sell her until he has performed istibra' upon her by one menstrual cycle, lest she be carrying his child and his offspring be sold along with her.

It was narrated from 'Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, that he said: if a man buys a slave woman who does not menstruate, or one who has reached puberty without having menstruated, he must perform istibra' upon her for forty-five nights.

'Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, was asked about a man who bought a slave girl who had reached the age of discernment but had not menstruated and was a virgin, whose seller had taken her back and then sold her. He said, peace be upon him: her case is grave; he must perform istibra' upon her for nine months, and then he may approach her after the nine months if no pregnancy has appeared, taking the cautious course.

It was related from him, peace be upon him, concerning a man who buys a slave girl while she is in a state of purity, and her seller informs him that he has not approached her since she last menstruated nor during the present purity in which she is. He said, peace be upon him: if the seller is trustworthy in his eyes, he may approach her if he wishes; and if he performs istibra', that is the more cautious course for him. He said, peace be upon him: whoever buys a slave girl who has had no husband, and whose master has not approached her, must nonetheless perform istibra' upon her by one menstrual cycle.

He, peace be upon him, was asked about a man who bought a slave girl, then freed her and married her, whether he might approach her. He said: let him perform istibra' of her womb by one menstrual cycle; and if he approaches her before performing istibra', there is no harm in it. It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, that he was asked about a man who had lain with a slave woman of his and then wished to give her in marriage before performing istibra'. He said: No. It was said to him: by how much should he perform istibra'? He said: by one menstrual cycle, though two are more beloved to me. A man must likewise perform istibra' of the slave woman when he wishes to sell her, just as he performs it when he buys her.

It was narrated from 'Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, that he said: when a man buys a slave woman, there is no harm in his enjoying of her what is short of intercourse before he performs istibra', meaning fondling, kissing, and approach short of the private part. It was narrated from him, peace be upon him, that he was asked about a man who bought a slave woman and feared she might be pregnant; he said: let him perform istibra' upon her for forty-five nights.

He, peace be upon him, was asked about a slave woman who had committed adultery, whether her master might approach her. He said: he does not approach her until he has performed istibra' upon her. And if a man commits adultery with a slave woman and then buys her, he ought not to approach her until he has performed istibra' upon her; and if she has conceived by him through that adultery, the child is not attributed to him, for the Messenger of Allah said: "الولد للفراش والعاهر الحجر," meaning the child belongs to the marriage bed, and for the fornicator is the stone.

It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him, that he said: whoever buys a slave girl while she is menstruating, when she becomes pure let him approach her if he wishes. And it was narrated from 'Ali, peace be upon him, that he said concerning a woman taken captive who has a husband: she performs istibra' by one menstrual cycle, and when she is pure her master may approach her. And if a woman is coerced and violated, her husband does not approach her until she has performed istibra' by one menstrual cycle.

Chapters of Divorce

Allah Almighty has said: ﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ إِذَا طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَطَلِّقُوهُنَّ لِعِدَّتِهِنَّ وَأَحْصُوا الْعِدَّةَ ﴾ "O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the onset of their waiting period, and count the 'iddah." And He, glorified and exalted, has said: ﴿ وَإِن يَتَفَرَّقَا يُغْنِ اللَّهُ كُلًّا مِّن سَعَتِهِ ﴾ "And if the two separate, Allah will enrich each of them out of His abundance."

So Allah, mighty and majestic, has permitted divorce in His Book and placed it in the hands of men rather than women. A man may divorce his wife when he wishes, whether for a cause or for no cause. Yet it has come down from 'Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, that he disliked divorce without cause, though he did not forbid it, nor did he invalidate the divorce of one who divorces without cause.

Umm Sa'id, his servant, related: one day I was pouring water over his hands, peace be upon him, when he raised his head toward me and said, "O Umm Sa'id." I said, "At your service, O Commander of the Faithful." He said, "I have longed to be a bridegroom." I said, "And what prevents the Commander of the Faithful from that?" He said, "Woe to you, after four, and in al-Rahbah?" I said, "Then divorce one and bring another in her place." He said, "Woe to you, divorce is an ugly thing, and I dislike it."

Al-Hasan, peace be upon him, was much given to divorce, and he divorced a great number of women without cause. It is said that he once passed by a group of women, who sent word to him asking, "Do you know this company of women?" He said, "No." One of them said, "There is not one among us but that you have married her and then divorced her." The most that 'Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, said concerning this was, "O people of Kufa, do not give your daughters in marriage to al-Hasan, for he is a man much given to divorce."

The divorce that is forbidden and the divorce that is permitted

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family and upon the Imams of his household, that it was brought to him that 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar had divorced his wife three times while she was menstruating. He said to 'Umar: command him to take her back, then to divorce her according to the Sunnah if he wishes.

So whoever divorces his wife after he has consummated the marriage, while she is in a purity in which he has touched her, or while she is menstruating, or divorces her without calling witnesses to the divorce as Allah Almighty has commanded, or in the blood of her postnatal bleeding, or swears by her divorce and then breaks the oath, his divorce is no divorce, and she remains his wife. This is the divorce of innovation, because it is contrary to what Allah, mighty and majestic, has commanded and to the Sunnah of His Messenger regarding divorce according to the Sunnah. Both forms of divorce are nonetheless one in substance, even though their wording differs.

That is, when a man wishes to divorce his wife, and she is among those who menstruate, he leaves her until she menstruates and then becomes pure. When she has been cleansed of the blood and has bathed from her menses, he divorces her before touching her, while she is pure in a purity in which he has not touched her, with a single pronouncement, and he calls two witnesses of just character to it, as Allah Almighty has commanded. He retains the greater right to take her back and may return her if he wishes, and she remains with him with two pronouncements counted against her, until she has menstruated three times. When she has menstruated three full times, she has the greater right over herself, and he has no return to her unless he wishes and she also wishes; then he may marry her by a fresh contract, with a dowry, a guardian, and witnesses, and she remains with him with two pronouncements. This is the Sunnah divorce, and it is the one that is recommended, on account of the words of Allah, mighty and majestic: ﴿ لَا تَدْرِي لَعَلَّ اللَّهَ يُحْدِثُ بَعْدَ ذَٰلِكَ أَمْرًا ﴾ "You do not know; perhaps Allah will bring about, after that, some new matter," meaning the taking back.

If he divorces her, in the manner we first described, with a single pronouncement, and then takes her back before her waiting period has elapsed, or after it has elapsed by a fresh contract, he may not divorce her again until he has touched her. When he then wishes to divorce her a second time, he leaves her until she menstruates and becomes pure, and divorces her while she is pure in a purity in which he has not touched her, calling two witnesses of just character. If he wishes to take her back, he returns her in the same way as we described first, at any time before her waiting period elapses. If her waiting period has elapsed, she has the greater right over herself; and if he wishes and she wishes, he may marry her by a renewed contract as we described first. Whether he takes her back or marries her anew in this way, she is his wife, and there now remains against her with him a single pronouncement. If he wishes to divorce her, he may not do so until he has touched her; then he leaves her until she menstruates and becomes pure, and then divorces her while she is pure in a purity in which he has not touched her, with a third pronouncement. She then becomes irrevocably separated from him and is not lawful to him until after another husband, as Allah, mighty and majestic, has said. This is the divorce of the 'iddah: when he divorces her, then takes her back and is intimate with her, then divorces her three times in the manner we have described, this is the sound and permissible divorce in which there is no disagreement.

If he divorces her twice or thrice in a single purity, it is not valid; for when he has divorced her once she has become divorced, and the second pronouncement does not fall upon her until he has taken her back and she is again his wife. Whoever pronounces divorce upon a woman already divorced, his divorce is not valid and does not fall upon her until she is his wife, just as, were he to pronounce the divorce of a woman who is not his wife, without the authorization of her husband, his divorce of her would not be valid. Likewise, if he divorces her three times, or by whatever number of pronouncements he names, in a single sitting and in a single purity, only one falls upon her, if she was pure in a purity in which he had not touched her. And if he divorces her three times, or by whatever pronouncement he wishes, while she is menstruating, or in a purity in which he has touched her, or divorces her without calling witnesses to the divorce, it is not valid, because it is contrary to what Allah, mighty and majestic, has commanded concerning divorce and to the Sunnah of His Messenger.

Such also is the position of those who permit this divorce and call it a Sunnah of the general body of Muslims, in contradiction of the Book and the Sunnah; and the one who pronounces it is a transgressor who has overstepped, yet they claim that the divorce takes effect and is binding. It is said to them: what then do you say if a man were to marry a woman contrary to the Book and the Sunnah, as when he marries her while she is in her waiting period, or while she has a husband? Their own position is that such a marriage is not permitted, and they forbid it. So when they are asked about permitting divorce contrary to the Book and the Sunnah while forbidding such a marriage, they too have no proof in the matter. It is then said to them: this divorce, which you have permitted though it is contrary to the Book and the Sunnah, is itself a thing that makes lawful a marriage contrary to the Book and the Sunnah, the very marriage whose permissibility you denied in your own doctrine. For this divorce renders the woman forbidden to the one who divorced her by it and makes her lawful to another who would marry her; and were this divorce not valid, that marriage would not be valid either, since she would still be a woman with a husband. So your annulling of her marriage by this divorce is what, in your view, establishes her marriage to the second husband. Thus there has come to be one who makes a marriage lawful contrary to the Book and the Sunnah, which is the very thing you reject. The arguments in a matter like this run long and pass beyond the bounds of this book.

The testimony of women is not admissible in divorce, for Allah, mighty and majestic, said concerning it: ﴿ وَأَشْهِدُوا ذَوَيْ عَدْلٍ مِّنكُمْ ﴾ "And call to witness two of just character from among you." Divorce falls upon five kinds of women, who are divorced in every circumstance, no menstruation or purity being awaited for them: the pregnant woman whose pregnancy has become apparent; the young girl who has not reached the age of menstruation; the woman past her courses, whose menstruation has ceased so that she no longer menstruates; the woman whose husband has not consummated the marriage with her; and the woman whose husband is absent from her on a distant journey. Whenever one of these is divorced, the divorce falls upon her, and she observes her waiting period by the months, counting each month in place of a menstrual cycle, except the woman with whom the marriage has not been consummated; for if he divorces her, she has no waiting period to observe, on account of the words of Allah, mighty and majestic: ﴿ فَمَا لَكُمْ عَلَيْهِنَّ مِنْ عِدَّةٍ تَعْتَدُّونَهَا ﴾ "You have against them no waiting period that you may count."

Metaphors for divorce, and the word of divorce

One who intends to divorce his wife says to her, "You are divorced," or names her and says, "So-and-so is divorced," or she is named to him and he says, "She is divorced," or he says to her, "Reckon your 'iddah," intending divorce by it, or he sends a messenger to her with that, or he says, "Let so-and-so reckon her 'iddah." There is no metaphor for divorce and no wording for it other than these two, for they are the two that Allah, mighty and majestic, mentioned in His Book, when He said: ﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ إِذَا طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَطَلِّقُوهُنَّ لِعِدَّتِهِنَّ ﴾ "O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the onset of their waiting period." He named nothing but the divorce and the 'iddah. So if he says to her, "You are set loose," or "a wilderness," or "severed," or "cut off," or "forbidden," or whatever else he may say to her apart from divorce and 'iddah, it is nothing, just as anything else he says to her, from the rest of speech, is not divorce. And if he says to her, "You are forbidden to me," or "your private part is forbidden to me," or whatever of her he declares forbidden to himself, no consequence falls upon him in that, nor is she made forbidden to him.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, said concerning a man who said to his wife, "You are forbidden to me": had I authority over him, I would have struck his head soundly, and I would say to him, Allah made her lawful to you, and then you forbid her? He has done no more than lie, for he has claimed that what Allah made lawful to him is forbidden to him. No divorce falls upon him in that, nor is any expiation binding. It was said to him: but the words of Allah, mighty and majestic, ﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ لِمَ تُحَرِّمُ مَا أَحَلَّ اللَّهُ لَكَ ﴾ "O Prophet, why do you forbid what Allah has made lawful to you," placed an expiation in it. He said, peace be upon him: this very thing confirms what we have said, for Allah, mighty and majestic, reproached His Prophet, and He made the expiation binding upon him only because he had, along with that, sworn an oath. This is shown by His words: ﴿ قَدْ فَرَضَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ تَحِلَّةَ أَيْمَانِكُمْ ﴾ "Allah has ordained for you the absolution of your oaths."

He said: that was because the Messenger of Allah had been alone with Mariyah the Copt, his slave woman, before she bore Ibrahim, peace be upon him. 'A'ishah came upon this and it grieved her, and he wished to please her, so he forbade Mariyah to himself and swore to her an oath that he would not approach her thereafter. He confided this to her as a secret and bade her conceal it, but she told it to Hafsah, and the two of them backed each other against the Messenger of Allah. So Allah, mighty and majestic, sent down: "O Prophet, why do you forbid what Allah has made lawful to you, seeking the approval of your wives? And Allah is Forgiving, Merciful. Allah has already ordained for you the absolution of your oaths; and Allah is your Protector, and He is the All-Knowing, the All-Wise. And when the Prophet confided a matter to one of his wives, and she then disclosed it, and Allah made it known to him, he made known part of it and turned away from part; and when he informed her of it, she said, 'Who told you this?' He said, 'The All-Knowing, the All-Aware told me.'"

When a man pronounces divorce upon his wife, the only words that carry weight are those Allah Almighty named in His Book, where He said:

﴿يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ إِذَا طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَطَلِّقُوهُنَّ لِعِدَّتِهِنَّ﴾

"O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the beginning of their waiting period."

He named nothing but the divorce (al-talaq) and the waiting period (al-idda). So if a man says to his wife, "You are loosed," or "You are quit of me," or "You are severed," or "You are cut off," or "You are forbidden," or any such word other than divorce and the waiting period, it is nothing at all. Such speech is like any other words he might say to her apart from divorce. And if he says to her, "You are forbidden to me," or "Your body is forbidden to me," or makes forbidden upon himself anything of her, no consequence falls upon him in that, and she does not become unlawful to him.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (peace be upon him) said concerning a man who told his wife, "You are forbidden to me": "Had I any authority over him, I would strike his head and say, Allah made her lawful for you, and then you forbid her? He has done no more than tell a lie, claiming that what Allah made lawful for him is forbidden to him. No divorce enters upon him through that, and no expiation is binding."

It was said to him: "But Allah Almighty says, ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ لِمَ تُحَرِّمُ مَا أَحَلَّ اللَّهُ لَكَ﴾, 'O Prophet, why do you forbid yourself what Allah has made lawful for you?' and He placed an expiation in it." He (peace be upon him) said: "This confirms what we have said, for Allah Almighty was reproaching His Prophet (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family). The expiation was laid upon him only because he had joined an oath to it. The proof of this is His saying, ﴿قَدْ فَرَضَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ تَحِلَّةَ أَيْمَانِكُمْ﴾, 'Allah has already ordained for you the absolution of your oaths.'"

He explained that the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family) had withdrawn in private with Mariya the Copt, his bondwoman, before she gave birth to Ibrahim (peace be upon him). Aisha came upon them, and the matter grieved her. Wishing to please her, he forbade Mariya to himself and swore to her that he would not approach Mariya thereafter. He confided this to Aisha and asked her to keep it secret, but she disclosed it to Hafsa, and the two of them aided one another against the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family). Then Allah Almighty revealed concerning the matter that He set right between the Prophet and his wives, declaring that Jibril, the righteous among the believers, and the angels were his supporters, and that if he were to divorce his wives, his Lord would replace them with wives better than they, as He said:

﴿عَسَىٰ رَبُّهُ إِن طَلَّقَكُنَّ أَن يُبْدِلَهُ أَزْوَاجًا خَيْرًا مِّنكُنَّ مُسْلِمَاتٍ مُّؤْمِنَاتٍ قَانِتَاتٍ تَائِبَاتٍ عَابِدَاتٍ سَائِحَاتٍ ثَيِّبَاتٍ وَأَبْكَارًا﴾

"obedient, believing, devoutly worshipping, repentant, given to worship, given to fasting, both previously married and virgins."

So He commanded him to expiate the oath he had sworn. He expiated it and returned to her, and Ibrahim was born of her. She thus became the mother of his child (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family).

**The option and the transfer of authority**

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him and his family) that his wives showed discontent with him and asked him for more of the goods of this world, as women are wont to do, and that the most severe among them in this was Hafsa. She said to him, "If you divorced us, we would find among our own people men of equal standing." The Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family) bore with them patiently, withdrew from them, and the revelation was withheld for twenty days. Then Allah Almighty revealed:

﴿يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ قُل لِّأَزْوَاجِكَ إِن كُنتُنَّ تُرِدْنَ الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا وَزِينَتَهَا فَتَعَالَيْنَ أُمَتِّعْكُنَّ وَأُسَرِّحْكُنَّ سَرَاحًا جَمِيلًا ۝ وَإِن كُنتُنَّ تُرِدْنَ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ وَالدَّارَ الْآخِرَةَ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ أَعَدَّ لِلْمُحْسِنَاتِ مِنكُنَّ أَجْرًا عَظِيمًا﴾

"O Prophet, say to your wives: If you desire the life of this world and its adornment, then come, I will provide for you and release you graciously. But if you desire Allah, His Messenger, and the abode of the Hereafter, then indeed Allah has prepared for the doers of good among you a great reward."

He withdrew from all of them for twenty-nine nights in the chamber of Umm Ibrahim. Then he called them, recited to them what Allah had revealed concerning them, and gave them the option, and they chose him (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family).

Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdillah (peace be upon them) said that this was not a divorce, but that had they chosen themselves, it would have been a single, irrevocable divorce. Abu Abdillah (peace be upon him) also said: when a man wishes to give his wife the option, let him first withdraw from her, as the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family) did, until she menstruates and then purifies herself. Then he gives her the option while she is in a state of purity in which he has not touched her, and he calls witnesses to it. If she then chooses herself, she is divorced by an irrevocable divorce, and he has no right of return over her; he may court her again, and if he wishes and she wishes, they may marry afresh. If she chooses him, it is no divorce. The option is exercised by her saying, "I have chosen myself," or "I have chosen you." If she chooses herself, the matter is settled; but if she stays silent until he or she rises and they part from where they were, the option that was granted to her has lapsed. Likewise, if he withdraws the option before she has exercised it, she has no right to choose. And if he comes to her and lays his hand upon her, or kisses her, or is intimate with her before she speaks, the option granted to her has lapsed. If he gives her the option and she says, "I do not accept your option," then she has no option.

If he gives her the option while she is menstruating, or in a state of purity in which he has touched her, or without two witnesses testifying, then it counts for nothing. He (peace be upon him) said: "No divorce, no khul', no mutual release, and no granting of the option takes effect except by the testimony of two witnesses, in a state of purity untouched by intercourse."

He (peace be upon him) said: "If a man entrusts the option to his wife, or to her father, or her brother, or her guardian, and she is content that the one to whom he entrusts the matter should decide it, that is permissible." The transfer of authority is of the same standing as the option: it is that he says to her, "I have placed you in command of yourself," or "I have placed your affair in your hands." This stands to him in the place of his saying to her, "Choose."

**What is binding in divorce and what is not**

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him) that he said, "There is no divorce except after marriage, and no divorce for one who has not married." And Ali (peace be upon him) said: "There is no divorce except after marriage, no emancipation except after ownership, no continuous fasting that joins two days without breaking, no fasting that is silence from speech from morning until night, no suckling after weaning, and no orphanhood after a child reaches maturity."

A man asked Abu Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him), saying, "I have a kinswoman who has been offered to me, and I wished to marry her, but before this day I had said, 'The day I marry her, she is divorced.'" He said, "Had you married her?" The man said, "No." He said, "Then marry her if you wish, for your divorce of one whose marriage bond you do not hold is no divorce."

Abu Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him) was asked about the man who says, "If I marry such a woman, or marry in such a land, or buy such a slave, or take a concubine, or purchase such-and-such, then the one I marry is divorced, and the one I buy or take as concubine is free, and what I buy is charity." He (peace be upon him) said: "None of this is anything. A man's divorce of what is not in his marriage bond is invalid, and so is his emancipation and his charity of what he does not yet own."

He (peace be upon him) was asked about a man who divorced his wife while he was ill, and her waiting period either expired or did not, and one of the two died. He said: "If her waiting period expires while he is ill or after he has recovered, he does not inherit from her, but she inherits from him if he dies of that illness, whether her waiting period has expired or not, unless he recovers."

From Ali (peace be upon him): "Every divorce is valid," meaning as it is duly required, "except the divorce of one bereft of reason." And from Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdillah (peace be upon them): "The one bereft of reason, whose mind is gone, his divorce is not valid, nor is his emancipation."

Ali (peace be upon him) was asked about a man who saw in his sleep that he had divorced his wife. He said: "That is from Satan. The divorce of a sleeping man is nothing, even if his tongue utters it while he sleeps, until he wakes and pronounces divorce as it is required." No divorce is valid from one bereft of reason, nor from one who raves, nor from one in delirium, nor from a boy who has not reached puberty, nor from a drunkard who does not comprehend.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him): "Whoever is compelled to divorce his wife, his divorce is not valid. There is no divorce and no emancipation under compulsion."

He (peace be upon him) said concerning a man who says to his wife, divorce being duly due, "You are divorced one and a half times": "She is divorced once, for divorce admits no fractions." Likewise, if he says, "You are divorced three quarters, or a quarter, or a sixth of a divorce," or names some other fraction of a divorce, or leaves it unnamed, she is divorced once. If a man addresses four wives or fewer, divorce being duly permissible, and says, "I have divorced you, one divorce shared among you," each of them is divorced once. Likewise, if he says, "I have divorced all of you, two divorces shared among you," or three, or four, each of them is divorced once. And if he says to them, "I have divorced you, five divorces shared among you," each of them is bound by only a single divorce. This rests upon the principle we set down before, that in a single period of purity only one divorce is binding, and whatever exceeds it counts for nothing.

**Khul' and mutual release (mubarat)**

Allah Almighty said:

﴿وَلَا يَحِلُّ لَكُمْ أَن تَأْخُذُوا مِمَّا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ شَيْئًا إِلَّا أَن يَخَافَا أَلَّا يُقِيمَا حُدُودَ اللَّهِ ۖ فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ أَلَّا يُقِيمَا حُدُودَ اللَّهِ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا فِيمَا افْتَدَتْ بِهِ﴾

"It is not lawful for you to take back anything you have given them, unless both fear that they will not maintain the limits of Allah. But if you fear that they will not maintain the limits of Allah, there is no blame upon either of them in what she gives to ransom herself."

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Prophet of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family), from Ali (peace be upon him) and the Imams among his descendants, that khul' is permissible when the man puts it in its proper place. That is, when his wife says to him, "I fear that I will not maintain the limits of Allah toward you, so I give you such-and-such, and set me free," or when her husband says the like to her, it is permissible for them to act upon what they agree.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him) said that the husband has no right to take anything from her unless she transgresses in her speech and says to him, "I will not obey any command of yours, I will not honor any oath to you, I will not purify myself from impurity for you, I will not let you share my bed, I will admit to your house one whom you dislike, I will enter upon you without your leave," or she says, "I will not maintain the limits of Allah toward you," and makes that plain. When she says such a thing, it becomes lawful for him to take what she ransoms herself with, even beyond what he gave her. But if she does not say such a thing, he ought not to take from her more than what he gave her.

He (peace be upon him) said: "Khul' and mutual release take effect only as divorce does, in a state of purity in which he has not touched her, and before witnesses." When he releases her in this way, he divorces her with a single divorce, as we described above. She thereby becomes clearly separated from him, and he has no right of return over her unless she too wishes it, so that they reconcile by a fresh marriage. She remains with him according to what is left of her divorce count, and he may marry her if he wishes and she wishes, whether before her waiting period expires or after.

As for the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ شِقَاقَ بَيْنِهِمَا فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا مِّنْ أَهْلِهِ وَحَكَمًا مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا إِن يُرِيدَا إِصْلَاحًا يُوَفِّقِ اللَّهُ بَيْنَهُمَا ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيمًا خَبِيرًا﴾

"And if you fear a breach between the two, appoint an arbitrator from his family and an arbitrator from her family. If they both desire reconciliation, Allah will bring about agreement between them. Indeed, Allah is All-Knowing, All-Aware."

It has come from Ali, Abu Ja'far, and Abu Abdillah (peace be upon them) that this applies to a husband and wife who fall into discord and cannot agree, and whose quarrel is prolonged, so that they bring the matter before the ruler. He appoints an arbitrator from the man's family and an arbitrator from the woman's family. If it is granted to the two arbitrators to reconcile the couple, they do so. But if that is not possible and they separate the couple, the arbitrators have no right to separate them until the man and the woman are first consulted. If the couple do not consent to the separation, the two are not parted.

**Al-Ila' (the oath of abstention)**

Allah Almighty said:

﴿لِّلَّذِينَ يُؤْلُونَ مِن نِّسَائِهِمْ تَرَبُّصُ أَرْبَعَةِ أَشْهُرٍ ۖ فَإِن فَاءُوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ ۝ وَإِنْ عَزَمُوا الطَّلَاقَ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ﴾

"For those who swear off their wives there is a waiting of four months. Then if they return, Allah is Forgiving, Merciful. But if they are resolved on divorce, then Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing."

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family), from Ali (peace be upon him) and the Imams of his household, that al-Ila' is for a man to swear to his wife that he will not have intercourse with her, and then to keep apart from her until four months pass.

Abu Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: "It is not for the woman to bring her husband to account before four months have passed from the day of his oath. Once four months or more have elapsed, she may bring him to account whenever she wishes thereafter, taking the matter to the ruler. When she does so, he gives the husband the choice between returning to her, that is, to resume relations with her in the proper manner, or divorcing her. He must do one of the two. And if he merely keeps apart from her without an oath, abstaining as long as he wishes, that is not al-Ila' and is not counted a divorce, until the woman takes the matter to the ruler and the ruler halts him: either he returns or he divorces then and there. Al-Ila' takes effect only in a state of purity untouched by intercourse, and with witnesses, as divorce does. It falls only upon a wife whose marriage has been consummated; it does not fall upon one with whom he has not yet been intimate."

A man is at liberty if he swears off his wife for four months or less; once four months pass and she brings him to account, nothing is lawful for him but to return to her in the proper manner or to divorce her. The return is intercourse. If he is unable to it through illness, or a chronic complaint, or travel, and he affirms his return with his tongue and calls witnesses, that suffices and is permissible. When he is able, he returns to her in the proper manner.

From Ali (peace be upon him): "When the one bound by al-Ila' is halted and resolves upon divorce, let him leave her until she menstruates and purifies herself. When she is pure, he divorces her and calls two upright witnesses. He is then the more entitled to take her back so long as three menstrual cycles have not elapsed. Once they have elapsed, he is but one suitor among others, and she is in command of herself. If he wishes and she wishes, he may marry her afresh, and she remains with him according to what is left of her divorce count."

A man asked Abu Ja'far (peace be upon him), saying, "My wife bore a boy, and I feared that if I were intimate with her she would conceive and the child would perish, so I swore by Allah that I would not approach her until she had weaned him." Ali (peace be upon him) said, "There is no al-Ila' upon you in seeking the child's welfare."

By this he (peace be upon him) meant that whoever swears to his wife that he will not approach her for a month, or for less than four months, that is not al-Ila'; and if he approaches her before the four months, it is no al-Ila' either, for al-Ila' is only with an oath, and the oath is the binding of it. If he persists until four months pass, even then it is not al-Ila', for there is no al-Ila' except by oath, and al-Ila' is the swearing itself.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him): "If the one bound by al-Ila' says, 'I have already had intercourse with her,' his word is accepted along with his oath," meaning if he had been intimate with her before that. For when a man has been intimate with his wife and then becomes incapable of it, no separation is made between them; but if he is incapable, or has not been intimate with her, he is given a term, and then a separation is made between them. He said: "And if he swears off her for more than four months, he is one bound by al-Ila'."

**Al-Dhihar**

Allah, exalted be His remembrance, said:

﴿الَّذِينَ يُظَاهِرُونَ مِنكُم مِّن نِّسَائِهِم﴾

"Those among you who pronounce dhihar against their wives," to the end of the passage on al-Dhihar.

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family) that a man came to him and said, "O Messenger of Allah, I have pronounced dhihar against my wife." He said, "Go and free a slave." The man said, "I have none." He said, "Then fast two consecutive months." He said, "I am not able." He said, "Then go and feed sixty poor persons."

Ali (peace be upon him) said: "When the one who has pronounced dhihar possesses the means to free a slave, he frees one, and fasting does not suffice for him. If he is able to fast, he fasts, and feeding does not suffice for him. And if he finds nothing to free and is unable to fast, he feeds."

Abu Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: "Everything in the Quran that uses 'or' leaves its doer free to do whichever of them he wishes, as in the saying of Him who is the Speaker:

﴿لَا يُؤَاخِذُكُمُ اللَّهُ بِاللَّغْوِ فِي أَيْمَانِكُمْ وَلَٰكِن يُؤَاخِذُكُم بِمَا عَقَّدتُّمُ الْأَيْمَانَ ۖ فَكَفَّارَتُهُ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ أَوْ كِسْوَتُهُمْ أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ﴾

'Allah will not call you to account for what is thoughtless in your oaths, but He will call you to account for the oaths you have deliberately taken. Its expiation is the feeding of ten poor persons with the average of what you feed your families, or clothing them, or the freeing of a slave.'

Here the one expiating is free to expiate by whichever of them he wishes. But where His words are 'if he does not find such-and-such,' or 'if he is not able to do such-and-such,' the latter is not lawful until he fails to find the former or is unable to it."

He (peace be upon him) said: "Al-Dhihar takes effect only in a state of purity untouched by intercourse, and with the testimony of two witnesses, as is the case with divorce. If it is otherwise, it is nothing. Al-Dhihar does not occur within an oath, just as divorce does not. It occurs only when a man intends it and says to her, while she is in a state of purity and in the presence of two upright witnesses, 'You are to me as my mother's back,' and he does not say, 'if you do such-and-such.' Al-Dhihar does not occur in a single period of purity more than once, in a single sitting, and then there is a single expiation. If he pronounces it repeatedly, as it is duly done, then for each time there is an expiation. And if he pronounces dhihar against four wives, then for each one of them there is an expiation. Al-Dhihar is binding with respect to every woman within the forbidden degrees, as when he says, 'You are to me as my mother's back.'"

When a man has pronounced ẓihār (ظهار) against his wife and looks for the means to free himself of its consequence, the order of the expiation is fixed. If he possesses a slave he can free, he frees the slave, and fasting is not permitted to him so long as freeing is within his power. If he is able to fast, he fasts, and feeding is not permitted to him. Only if he finds nothing to free and cannot fast does he feed the poor. Abū ʿAbdullāh Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) said: "Everything in the Qurʾān in which there is an 'or' offering a choice, its owner may do whichever of them he wishes." This is like the saying of Allah, exalted be His mention:

﴿ لَا يُؤَاخِذُكُمُ اللَّهُ بِاللَّغْوِ فِي أَيْمَانِكُمْ وَلَٰكِن يُؤَاخِذُكُم بِمَا عَقَّدتُّمُ الْأَيْمَانَ ۖ فَكَفَّارَتُهُ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ أَوْ كِسْوَتُهُمْ أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ ﴾

"Allah will not take you to task for what is unintended in your oaths, but He will take you to task for the oaths you have deliberately sworn. Its expiation is the feeding of ten of the poor with the average of what you feed your own families, or clothing them, or freeing a slave" (al-Māʾidah: 89). Here the one expiating may choose whichever of the three he wishes. But where the wording runs, "and whoever does not find such-and-such, or is unable," the second option is not opened to him until he has first failed to find, or been unable to perform, the option that precedes it.

He (peace be upon him) said: "Ẓihār is valid only in a state of purity in which he has not touched her, and with the testimony of two witnesses, just as is required for divorce. If it is otherwise, it counts for nothing. There is no ẓihār within an oath, just as there is no divorce within one." It comes about only when the man intends it and says to her, while she is in purity and in the presence of two upright witnesses, "You are to me as my mother's back," أنتِ عليَّ كظهر أمي, without his adding, "if I do such-and-such." Ẓihār does not occur more than once in a single sitting within one period of purity, and a single expiation suffices for it even if he repeats the pronouncement several times. But if he pronounces it on separate occasions, there is an expiation for each occasion, and if he pronounces it against four wives, there is an expiation for each one of them. Ẓihār binds a man with respect to every woman who is unmarriageable to him, as when he says, "You are to me as my mother's back," or names his sister, his paternal aunt, his maternal aunt, or any other of his unmarriageable kin.

The ẓihār remains binding until he has intercourse with her, and there is no ẓihār between a man and his slave-woman. If he wishes to approach his wife after pronouncing it, he must first make the expiation, and he must not draw near her until he has expiated. Should he approach her before that, he has done wrong; he then expiates afterward, seeks forgiveness of Allah, and turns to Him in repentance. If he divorces her after pronouncing ẓihār but before approaching her, no expiation is required of him. If he takes her back after divorcing her, he may not approach her until he has performed the ẓihār expiation. But if he leaves her until she is fully separated from him and then marries her afresh, no ẓihār clings to the new marriage, for she had already become separate from him.

A slave incurs ẓihār just as a free man does when he pronounces it against his wife, yet upon him there is no freeing of a slave and no feeding of the poor unless his master donates it to him. If his master does not, he fasts one month, half of what the free man fasts. The fast of ẓihār for the free man is two consecutive months, as Allah, exalted be His mention, has commanded. If he fasts a month or less and then breaks his fast, he begins the fast again from its start, and what he has already fasted does not count for him. But if he fasts a full month and a day or two, or more, into the second month, and then breaks his fast before completing it, he makes up only what remains, because he has joined the two months in succession. As for the freeing that expiates ẓihār, any slave suffices, whether young or old, Muslim or non-Muslim, male or female, because Allah, mighty and majestic, said ﴿ فَتَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ ﴾, "then the freeing of a slave," and laid down no condition upon it. It is not permitted in ẓihār to free an insane slave, nor one with a corrupting defect, nor a mudabbar, since his manumission may be revoked, nor a mukātab who is still paying off his installments.

It was related from ʿAlī (peace be upon him) that he said: If the one who has pronounced ẓihār cannot find a slave to free and so fasts part of the fast, then obtains the means to free a slave before his fast is finished, he frees the slave and the fast is annulled. But if he completes his fast and only afterward finds that he has the means, nothing further is required of him. It was related from him also (peace be upon him) that he said: He feeds sixty of the poor, each poor person receiving half a ṣāʿ. Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) said that it suffices to feed each poor person a mudd. What ʿAlī (peace be upon him) said is what is commanded of the one who is able, and what Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) said suffices for the one who is unable; his ruling enters by way of the latitude of relief. What ʿAlī (peace be upon him) said is what is commanded, and whoever feeds each poor person a mudd has discharged the duty, as Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him) said. Neither ẓihār nor expiation falls due except where there has been intercourse with her, and so it is even if he has not approached even one of his wives. If a man approaches his wife without having expiated, he has done wrong and committed a grave sin, so let him seek the forgiveness of Allah and turn to Him in repentance.

Mention of Liʿān

Allah, exalted be His mention, said:

﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يَرْمُونَ أَزْوَاجَهُمْ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُمْ شُهَدَاءُ إِلَّا أَنفُسُهُمْ فَشَهَادَةُ أَحَدِهِمْ أَرْبَعُ شَهَادَاتٍ بِاللَّهِ ۙ إِنَّهُ لَمِنَ الصَّادِقِينَ ۝ وَالْخَامِسَةُ أَنَّ لَعْنَتَ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ إِن كَانَ مِنَ الْكَاذِبِينَ ۝ وَيَدْرَأُ عَنْهَا الْعَذَابَ أَن تَشْهَدَ أَرْبَعَ شَهَادَاتٍ بِاللَّهِ ۙ إِنَّهُ لَمِنَ الْكَاذِبِينَ ۝ وَالْخَامِسَةَ أَنَّ غَضَبَ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهَا إِن كَانَ مِنَ الصَّادِقِينَ ۝ وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُ وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ تَوَّابٌ حَكِيمٌ ﴾

"And those who accuse their wives and have no witnesses except themselves, the testimony of one of them shall be four testimonies by Allah that he is among the truthful, and the fifth that the curse of Allah be upon him if he is among the liars. And it averts the punishment from her that she testify four testimonies by Allah that he is among the liars, and the fifth that the wrath of Allah be upon her if he is among the truthful. And were it not for the grace of Allah upon you and His mercy, and that Allah is Relenting, Wise" (al-Nūr: 6 to 10).

ʿAlī (peace be upon him) said: Whoever accuses his wife, there is no liʿān between him and her until he claims to have seen the act, saying, "I saw a man between her legs committing adultery with her," or until he disavows her child. According to the word of Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (peace be upon him), this takes place only before the judge, once the man has held firmly to his statement and not retracted it, and she has denied what he said. Then the judge orders him to perform liʿān. The liʿān is that he says, "I bear witness by Allah that I have indeed seen a man committing adultery with her," or, "I bear witness that this child is not mine." He says this four times, then says in the fifth, "If I am lying, then the curse of Allah be upon me," and the judge says, "Āmīn." Then the judge questions the woman. If she affirms what he said, she is stoned. If she denies it, she says, "I bear witness by Allah that he is among the liars in what he has accused me of," saying this four times, then says in the fifth, "If he is truthful, then the wrath of Allah be upon me," and the judge says, "Āmīn." When this has been done, the judge separates the two, and she is never again lawful to him. The child is not joined to the one who disavowed him and is not ascribed to him; rather the child is ascribed to his mother and her maternal kin, and his inheritance passes to them. The woman's dowry rests upon the one who performed liʿān against her.

Liʿān is carried out only before the judge, and there is no liʿān between a man and his wife until he has had intercourse with her, even if he accuses her of adultery or disavows the child she bore. If he refuses to perform liʿān and she raises the matter to the authority, he is given the ḥadd lashing for slandering her, unless he produces proof of what he said. If he has proof, and four upright men bear witness to the adultery against her, she is stoned and the liʿān falls away. Likewise, if he disavows the child she bore and will not perform liʿān, he receives the ḥadd lashing when she raises the matter to the authority, and she remains his wife unless he divorces her. If he performs liʿān and then retracts what he said and pronounces himself a liar, he receives the ḥadd lashing, she does not return to him, and the child inherits from him, though he inherits nothing from the child. Liʿān is valid between a Muslim and a dhimmī woman, between a free man and a slave-woman, between two slaves, and between every married pair who are of age.

There is no liʿān between two who are minors until they reach maturity, and once they reach it he may not perform liʿān against her for what he charged while they were minors. There is no liʿān between a mute man and a mute woman, for liʿān is enacted only with the tongue; if a man accuses his wife while she is mute, the two are separated and he does not perform liʿān. He does not perform liʿān against her while she is pregnant, but waits until she gives birth, and when she has given birth he performs the liʿān. If a man slanders his wife only through insinuation, there is no liʿān for that. If he says to her, "I did not find you a virgin," there is nothing upon him, and no liʿān, because virginity may be lost by something other than intercourse. If he accuses her before he has had intercourse with her, he receives the ḥadd lashing and she remains his wife. The two who perform liʿān do so only while standing, not seated. If a man accuses his wife and then divorces her, and she raises the matter while she is still within her ʿiddah, he performs liʿān; but if she raises it after her ʿiddah has elapsed, he receives the ḥadd lashing unless he brings proof of what he said. If he accuses her and she dies before he performs liʿān, and a man from among her guardians rises to perform liʿān against him on her behalf, the husband takes no inheritance from her; but if no one rises to do this, he inherits from her. If a man divorces his wife before consummation and she then claims to be pregnant by him while he denies it, then if she establishes that he had let down a veil over her in seclusion, he performs liʿān; otherwise he swears that he never let a veil down over her nor approached her, and her claim is set aside. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt (peace be upon them).

Mention of the ʿIddah, the Maintenance Within It, and the Mourning

Allah, mighty and majestic, said:

﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا ﴾

"And those of you who die and leave wives behind, they shall wait by themselves four months and ten days" (al-Baqarah: 234). And He said:

﴿ وَالْمُطَلَّقَاتُ يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ ثَلَاثَةَ قُرُوءٍ ﴾

"And divorced women shall wait by themselves three menstrual cycles" (al-Baqarah: 228). And He said:

﴿ وَأُولَاتُ الْأَحْمَالِ أَجَلُهُنَّ أَن يَضَعْنَ حَمْلَهُنَّ ﴾

"And as for those who are pregnant, their term is that they deliver their burden" (al-Ṭalāq: 4). And He said:

﴿ ثُمَّ طَلَّقْتُمُوهُنَّ مِن قَبْلِ أَن تَمَسُّوهُنَّ فَمَا لَكُمْ عَلَيْهِنَّ مِنْ عِدَّةٍ تَعْتَدُّونَهَا ﴾

"Then you divorce them before you have touched them, you have no waiting period over them to count" (al-Aḥzāb: 49).

So when a man dies leaving a wife, she observes a waiting period of four months and ten nights, whether he had consummated the marriage with her or not, and whether she is an adult or a child, because Allah, mighty and majestic, said, ﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا ﴾, and made no exception in this between one who has been consummated with and one who has not, nor between a child and an adult. She may keep this waiting period wherever she wishes, in her husband's house or elsewhere.

If a man divorces his wife while she is menstruating, her ʿiddah is three menstrual cycles, and she keeps it only in her husband's house. A pregnant woman whose husband dies observes the longer of the two terms. If she gives birth before the four months and ten nights have passed, she completes the four months and ten; and if the four months and ten pass and she has not yet given birth, she waits patiently until she gives birth, because Allah, mighty and majestic, said, ﴿ وَأُولَاتُ الْأَحْمَالِ أَجَلُهُنَّ أَن يَضَعْنَ حَمْلَهُنَّ ﴾, and said, ﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا ﴾, making no exception in either. If he divorces her while she is pregnant, her term is that she delivers her burden; whenever she delivers it she becomes separate from him, and everything she brings forth that is known to be a child separates her. But if her womb holds two children or more, she is not separated until she delivers the last of what is in her womb.

Whoever divorces his wife with one or two revocable pronouncements and then dies while she is in this state observes the ʿiddah of death, for she had not become separate from him and he died while she was still within his bond, and Allah, mighty and majestic, says, ﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا ﴾. Whoever divorces his wife while he is absent from her, her ʿiddah is reckoned from the day he pronounced the divorce, even if its news reaches her only some time later, and after it passes she becomes lawful for husbands. But if he dies while absent from her, she must begin her ʿiddah from the time his news reaches her, and what came before that is not counted, because mourning is obligatory upon the widow, while there is no mourning upon the divorced woman.

If a man divorces his wife before consummating the marriage, there is no ʿiddah upon her, and she may marry whenever she wishes. The divorced woman who suffers from istiḥāḍa counts her ʿiddah by the coming on of her menstruation. We have already mentioned how the blood of menstruation is distinguished from the blood of istiḥāḍa: menstrual blood is thick, dark, foul-smelling, and warm, while the blood of istiḥāḍa is thin and clear, neither warm nor foul. When she sees the blood of istiḥāḍa, she counts her ʿiddah accordingly; but if she is ignorant of this and does not know how to tell the one from the other, she counts her ʿiddah by the months. So too, if a woman is divorced and menstruates once or twice and then her menstruation ceases, she begins her ʿiddah afresh by the months and does not count what has already passed of the menstruation, because Allah, mighty and majestic, has imposed three cycles upon the woman who menstruates and three months upon the woman who has despaired of menstruation. Since this woman did not complete the three cycles and her menstruation stopped short of them, it falls upon her to observe a ʿiddah of three months.
