# The Body's Portion: Food, Drink, and Healing

Turning from oaths to what the body itself takes in, the law marks out clearly what may be eaten and what must be set aside. Among the parts of a slaughtered animal that are not eaten are the male glands, the penis, the female organ, and the inside of the kidneys.

It is narrated from him, (ع), that he said: "Nothing of the creatures of the sea is to be eaten except what is caught alive and has scales. As for what dies in the water before it is taken, or what has no scales, eating it is forbidden."

He forbade eating the cheese made by the Magians and the polytheists when they put into it rennet (al-anfaha) taken from carrion or from an animal over which the name of Allah was not pronounced. But if the cheese is of unknown origin, and it is not known that any such thing is in it, then eating it is permitted when it is sold in the market of the Muslims, just as one may eat the slaughtered meat sold there.

Everything whose eating is forbidden becomes permissible for the one in dire necessity, when he can find nothing else and fears for his life; and the one compelled by need may eat of it until he is satisfied, if he wishes.

Everything that serves as nourishment for man, the grains, the green herbs, what the earth brings forth, and all the fruits that neither harm nor kill, is lawful to eat. The flesh of camels, cattle, sheep, and all wild animals, together with their milk, is lawful, except for the beasts of prey that possess fangs and seize and devour other animals; their flesh, and nothing of them, is lawful to eat. All birds are lawful when slaughtered, except the birds of prey that possess talons and seize and devour other birds; eating them is not lawful, nor is eating their eggs.

If an egg is found and it is not known what creature it is from, it is examined: if its two ends differ, one pointed and one rounded, it is eaten, for that is the egg of a creature whose flesh is eaten; but if its two ends are alike, it is not eaten, for that is the egg of a creature whose flesh is not eaten. The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) permitted the eating of the rabbit, but disliked the lizard (al-dabb), the hedgehog, and the other creeping things of the earth.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) it is narrated that he permitted the use of vessels touched by impurity or wine once they have been washed and cleaned; and concerning food in which the small creatures of the earth, flies, and whatever has no blood happen to die, he said that this does not render the food forbidden to eat, nor does it make it impure. Whoever is repelled by it may leave it, and whoever wishes may eat. But if a creature possessing blood dies in it, then if the food is liquid it is spoiled, and if it is solid only what surrounds the dead creature is spoiled; whoever wishes may eat the rest, and there is no harm in it, once what lay around the dead thing has been removed and thrown away.

**On the Etiquette of Drinking**

It has been narrated from the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) that he forbade drinking in one continuous draught and commanded that one drink in sips, that the drinker name Allah at the first sip and praise Him when he has finished, and that he not breathe into the vessel. He should say "Bismillah" when beginning and "Alhamdulillah" when pausing. Ali ((ع)) said: "I observed the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) drinking water more than once, and he would breathe three times during the drink, taking the vessel away from his mouth at each breath, saying Bismillah when he began and Alhamdulillah when he paused." Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)) said: "Three breaths in a drink are better than one."

He forbade drinking from the mouth of a waterskin, that is, bending back its opening and drinking directly from it, saying that this fouls it and carries the risk that a creature in the water, unseen by the drinker, might slip down his throat.

The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) used to drink both standing and sitting, yet he forbade lapping water straight from a river, a basin, a pond, or the like. When he saw a man doing so, he said to him: "You lap as a beast laps. If you find no vessel, drink from your hand, for it is the best of your vessels."

He also forbade gulping water down, saying: "Suck the water as it is sucked, and do not gulp it down as a burden upon yourself, for that brings on disease of the liver (al-kubad)." Yet he would gulp milk. Ali ibn al-Husayn ((ع)) was seen gulping milk, and when he was asked, "Will you not take it in sips?" he answered: "Only the people of the Fire take it in sips." When he drank milk he would say: "O Allah, bless us in it and increase us from it." When he drank water he would say: "Praise be to Allah, who has given us fresh, sweet water by His mercy, and has not given us bitter, briny water on account of our sins."

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) it is narrated that he forbade drinking, or performing ablution, from the side of the handle (urwa) of the vessel.

**On What Is Permissible to Drink**

It has been narrated from the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) that he said: "Water is the master of drinks in this world and the Hereafter." Any naturally occurring water, in which no human labor has any part, is lawful and permitted to drink, provided it is not mixed with impurity and is neither harmful nor injurious. One may likewise drink the milk of every animal whose flesh is lawful to eat. It has been narrated from the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) that he said of camel's milk: "It draws from every tree." And he said ((ع)): "Cow's ghee is a cure, and its milk is medicine." A prophet once complained of a weakness he found in his body, and Allah, Blessed and Exalted, revealed to him: "The finest meat is that taken with milk; eat them both, for I have placed strength in both of them." And it is said: "Meat and milk are the broth of the prophets." He said ((ع)): "Honey is a healing." Ali ((ع)) said: "No sick person was ever healed by the like of honey." And he said: "Can any of you not, when he falls ill, ask his wife to give him a dirham or two from her dowry, and buy honey with it, and drink it dissolved in rainwater?"

Allah, Blessed and Exalted, says regarding the dowry: ﴿فَإِنْ طِبْنَ لَكُمْ عَنْ شَيْءٍ مِنْهُ نَفْسًا فَكُلُوهُ هَنِيئًا مَرِيئًا﴾, "If they willingly give up to you any part of it, then consume it in satisfaction and ease" (al-Nisa: 4); and He says regarding honey: ﴿فِيهِ شِفَاءٌ لِلنَّاسِ﴾, "In it is healing for people" (al-Nahl: 69); and He says regarding rainwater: ﴿وَنَزَّلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً مُبَارَكًا﴾, "And We sent down from the sky blessed water" (Qaf: 9).

Water mixed with honey, milk, or anything similar is lawful to drink, as are drinks made from dates, raisins, or other sweeteners, so long as they have not changed. The same holds for everything pressed from grape juice, dates, raisins, or the fruits and herbs that are lawful to eat: drinking such juice is lawful as long as it has not turned, whether taken pure or mixed with water, and selling it, buying it, and benefiting from it are lawful likewise. So too if grape juice is cooked until it thickens without turning; it is lawful to drink, pure or mixed with water, as long as it does not change. It has been narrated of Ali ((ع)) that he used to provide the people with al-tila, which is grape juice boiled until it gains body and, if left, does not turn but becomes like honey; that and its like are lawful to drink, pure or mixed with water, as long as they do not change. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)) was asked about drinking pressed juice, and he said: "There is no harm in it. Drink from a clean vessel that is not seasoned with wine; drink it as long as a large quantity does not intoxicate. But if a large quantity intoxicates, then a small quantity of it is forbidden. Do not drink what has stood a long while, for after an hour, or after a night, the pleasure of wine departs and only its sin remains. So fear Allah and call yourselves to account."

It was the mark of the Shi'a of Ali that they were known for piety, diligence, restraint, the shunning of sins great and small, and love for the friends of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. It has been narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he said: "We used to soak raisins or dates in water in a clean vessel for the Messenger of Allah ((ع)). He would drink it that day and the next; and if it had changed by the third day, he ordered it poured away." This is the lawful drink that is called nabidh only because of what is cast, that is, thrown, into the water, of dates, raisins, or the like, which sweetens it when the water is brackish. But if it is left to ferment, so that it boils, intensifies, and throws up froth, then it is wine, and a large quantity of it is forbidden once it intoxicates.

**On What Is Forbidden to Drink**

Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, said: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنَّمَا الْخَمْرُ وَالْمَيْسِرُ وَالْأَنْصَابُ وَالْأَزْلَامُ رِجْسٌ مِنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَانِ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ﴾, "O you who believe, intoxicants, gambling, stone altars, and divining arrows are but an abomination of Satan's handiwork, so avoid it, that you may be successful" (al-Ma'idah: 90). By this He forbade wine, just as He forbade all things unlawful.

It has been narrated from the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) that he said: "Wine is forbidden; no one drinks it in this world and then drinks it in the Hereafter, in the sacred enclosure of holiness (hazirat al-quds)." And he said: "Allah has cursed wine: its presser, the one who has it pressed, its seller, its buyer, its drinker, the one who serves it, the one who consumes its price, the one who carries it, and the one to whom it is carried." He said: "That which intoxicates in large quantity is forbidden even in a small quantity." He forbade treating illness with wine and intoxicants, and forbade that they be given to children or animals, saying that the sin falls upon whoever gives it to them.

It has been narrated from Ali ibn al-Husayn ((ع)) that he said: "Intoxicating drink comes from five things: dates, raisins, wheat, barley, and honey, that is, besides grapes." And from Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)), and upon his forefathers be peace, it is narrated: "The one addicted to wine will meet Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, on the day he meets Him as one who worships an idol; and whoever drinks a single draught of it, Allah, Blessed and Exalted, will not accept a prayer from him for forty nights."

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) it is narrated that he said: "Paradise is forbidden to three: the one addicted to wine, the worshipper of idols, and the enemy of the family of Muhammad." From Ali ((ع)) it is narrated: "Whoever drinks wine and dies within forty days of having drunk it meets Allah, Blessed and Exalted, as one who worships an idol." And he said ((ع)): "The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) forbade every intoxicant from every drink, and whatever the Messenger of Allah has forbidden, Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, has forbidden. Every intoxicant is forbidden, and that which intoxicates in large quantity is forbidden even in a small quantity."

A man from the people of Kufa said to him: "May Allah set you right; the jurists of our land say that only wine made from grapes is forbidden." He said to him: "O Sheikh, I do not know what the jurists of your land say. My father narrated to me, from his father, from his grandfather, from Ali ibn Abi Talib ((ع)), that the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said: That which intoxicates in large quantity is forbidden even in a small quantity." Ja'far ibn Muhammad then said ((ع)): "Taqiyya is my religion and the religion of my forefathers in everything except three matters: the prohibition of intoxicants, the removing of the two leather socks at ablution, and the saying aloud of Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim, meaning that it be recited aloud in the prayer."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: "One must not treat illness with wine or intoxicants, nor may women use such a thing to dress their hair, for my father narrated to me, from his father, from his grandfather, that Ali ((ع)) said: Allah, Blessed and Exalted, has placed no cure in any impurity that He has forbidden." He ((ع)) was also asked about drinking al-fuqqa, and when the questioner had described its nature, he said: "It is forbidden; do not drink it." And he ((ع)) said, concerning the seasoned vessels, that wine was not forbidden on account of such vessels, but that he had forbidden the intoxicant in little and in much alike; he only disliked the seasoned vessels because they alter the drinks.

**On What Makes Medicine Beneficial**

In this chapter we set down what has come from the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام), concerning medicine, the treatment and remedy permitted with it, and what they forbade in that regard, all of which falls within the gates of the lawful and the unlawful. It may be that one who doubts what has come from them, (عليهم السلام), will deny it when he hears it; and if he uses it without true belief, he will find no benefit in it and so cast it aside. For Allah, Blessed and Exalted, brings benefit through it only to the people who affirm them and submit to their command, who receive what has come from them with acceptance, not to one who takes it as a mere trial, without belief, without intention, and without conviction.

What confirms this is the report narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)): Muhammad ibn Khalid, the governor of Medina, was recovering from an illness that had befallen him, and complained, in a gathering of people, of a pain he felt in his abdomen. Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said to him: "Shall I not tell you a hadith my father narrated to me, from his father, from his grandfather, from Ali, from the Messenger of Allah ((ع))?" He said: "Yes." He said: "Abu Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Husayn told me, from his father, from his grandfather, (عليهم السلام), that a man complained to the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) of a pain he felt in his abdomen. The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said to him: Take a drink of honey and cast into it three grains of black seed (shuniz), or five, or seven, and you will be cured by the leave of Allah. He did so and was cured. So take this yourself."

A man of Medina who was present in the gathering objected and said: "O Abu Abd Allah, the hadith is sound, and we have heard it, but we tried it and gained no benefit from it." At this Abu Abd Allah ((ع)) grew very angry and said: "Allah brings benefit through this only to those who believe in Him and affirm His Messenger ((ع)), the Muslims who submit to his command and receive it from him with acceptance. As for the hypocrites who doubt it and do not affirm him, Allah, Blessed and Exalted, will not benefit them by it. Perhaps you are a hypocrite, and you took it without affirming the Messenger of Allah ((ع)), and so you were denied its benefit." The man fell silent, was overcome with shame, and bowed his head, and all who were present in the gathering affirmed the truth of Abu Abd Allah's words. We, for our part, cannot reckon how much of this we have used with sincere intention and certainty and have indeed benefited from it. So whoever turns to anything mentioned in the chapters of this section, from the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) and from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah ((ع)), seeking treatment and cure by it, let him turn to it with intention, sincerity, and certainty, for it will benefit him, if Allah wills. But if doubt over it has crossed his heart, he should not treat himself with its like; and if he inclines to the word of the physicians where they have spoken against it, then he will gain no benefit from it. It is Allah who grants the success of affirming His friends and submitting to their command, and of receiving and accepting from them, to those of His servants whom He is pleased with and chooses for their guardianship.

**On Seeking Healing Through Acts of Righteousness**

We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) that he said: "In the black seed (al-habba al-sawda) is a cure for every disease except al-sam," that is, death. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)) said this to some of his companions, telling them this hadith, and it pleased them, and they said: "O son of the Messenger of Allah, how easy a remedy is this, that heals every disease but death!" He said: "Yes. Shall I not tell you of something easier still, in which the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) excluded neither death nor anything else?" They said: "What is it, O son of the Messenger of Allah ((ع))?" He said: "Supplication, for it turns back the decree even after it has been firmly sealed." And he joined his fingers together, all of them, bringing the two little fingers side by side, depicting the firm sealing of the decree.

It is narrated of the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) that he missed a man of the Ansar, who came to him after a time, and he said: "What has kept you from us?" He said: "Sickness and poverty, O Messenger of Allah ((ع))." The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said to him: "Shall I not teach you words to call upon Allah with, by which Allah, Blessed and Exalted, will lift that from you?" He said: "Yes, by my father and my mother, O Messenger of Allah ((ع))." He said: "Say: There is no power and no strength except through Allah; I place my trust in the Living who does not die. Praise be to Allah, who has taken no son, who has no partner in His dominion, and who has no protector from humiliation; and magnify Him greatly."

From Ali ((ع)) it is narrated that he said: "I complained to the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) of a forgetfulness that had come over me, on account of which much of the Qur'an slipped away from me. He said to me: O Ali, I will teach you words that will drive that from you and fix the Qur'an firmly in your heart. Say: O Allah, have mercy on me through the forsaking of disobedience to You for as long as You keep me alive; have mercy on me by sparing me what does not concern me; grant me good discernment in what pleases You with me; bind my heart to the keeping of Your Book as You taught it to me; and grant that I recite it in the manner that pleases You."

Among the words he taught for the firm holding of revelation was this: by Your Book give light to my sight, release my tongue, open my chest with it, employ my body in it, and grant me strength through it, for none can grant strength in it but You. He said: I prayed with those words, and Allah established the Qur'an firmly in my chest.

It was narrated from him that he said: If one of you sets before him a vessel containing food or drink and fears that something in it may harm him, let him name Allah over it and take from it, for nothing harms alongside the name of Allah.

It was narrated from Abu Abd-Allah, (ع), that a man among his companions complained to him of a corruption he found in his stomach, saying that he could eat no food without it harming and distressing him. He said to him: Say the name of Allah over every food you eat, and over every variety of it as you eat it, and it will not harm you. The man did so and was cured.

It was narrated from him that a man among his companions was struck with a whiteness between his eyes and complained of it, for the affliction had taken hold of him and grieved him. He said to him: You must make du'a while you are in prostration. The man said: O son of the Messenger of Allah, (ع), teach me the words to pray with. He said: Say: O Allah, O Most Merciful, O Compassionate, O Hearer of prayers, O Giver of good things, grant me the good of the Hereafter and of this world, turn away from me the evil of them both, and remove from me this affliction that is between my eyes, for it has grieved and saddened me. He did so and was healed.

It was narrated from him that he said: Be eager for charity and give it early in the day, for there is no believer who gives a charity sincerely, desiring by it what is with Allah, except that Allah wards off from him the evil of what descends from the sky on that day, and shields him from it. Then he said: And do not think little of the prayers of the poor for the sick among you, for they are answered on your behalf, though they may not be answered for the poor themselves.

Isma'il ibn Ali entered Medina while he was ill, and Abu Abd-Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), came to visit him. When he saw his condition, he said to him: Call for a basket, put wheat in it, and set it before you. Then instruct your servants that when a beggar comes, they bring him in to you, and hand him some of that wheat with your own hand, and ask him to pray to Allah for you. He said: Shall I not rather give them dirhams and dinars? Abu Abd-Allah, (ع), said: Do what I have commanded you. He did so, and was granted health.

It was narrated from Abu Abd-Allah, (ع), that he said: Three things drive away forgetfulness and bring on remembrance: reciting the Qur'an, using the siwak, and fasting.

It was narrated from him that a man among his companions complained to him of how heavily he was overtaken by worry and grief. He said: When anything of that befalls you, pass your hand over the place of your prostration, then draw your hand across your face from the left side of your cheek, and across your forehead to the right side of your cheek, then say: In the name of Allah, there is no god but He, the Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, He is the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate. O Allah, remove from me worry and grief. Let him do this three times.

It was narrated from him, (ع), that he said: Whoever says every day, thirty times, In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful; praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds; and blessed be Allah, the best of creators; and there is no power and no strength except through Allah, the Most High, the Most Great, Allah will ward off from him ninety-nine kinds of affliction, the least of which is madness.

Mention of bearing illness with patience for its reward:

We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, (ع), that the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: The groaning of the sick man, if he is patient and counts it for reward, is recorded for him as good deeds; but if it is in panic, he is called one given to panic, and there is no reward for him. The full treatment of this matter, and of the reward in illness for the one who is patient and counts it for reward, has its place among the chapters on funerals. It is also narrated from them, (عليهم السلام), that a woman came to the Messenger of Allah, (ع), with a son of hers who was afflicted in his mind, and said: O Messenger of Allah, pray to Allah for this son of mine that He may heal him. He said: As you wish. If you wish, I will pray to Allah for him and He will heal him; and if you wish, I will leave him, and there will be no reckoning or punishment upon him. She said: Then leave him, O Messenger of Allah, may Allah's prayers and (ع).

Mention of seeking refuge and the ruqyah:

We have narrated from the Imams of the family of the Messenger of Allah, (ع), that the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, would seat al-Hasan and al-Husayn, (عليهم السلام), upon his thighs and seek refuge for them, saying: I seek refuge for you both in the perfect words of Allah from every devil and every crawling thing, and from every envious eye. And he would say: Thus did my father Ibrahim, (ع) and his family, seek refuge for his two sons Isma'il and Ishaq, (عليهم السلام).

It was narrated from him, (ع), that Labid ibn al-A'sam the Jew, and the Jewish woman Umm Abd-Allah, worked sorcery against him in a cord of red and yellow threads in which they had tied eleven knots, then placed it inside the sheath of a date-palm and lowered it into one of the steps of a well in Medina. So the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, remained unable to hear, to see, to stand, to speak, to eat, or to drink. Then Jibril, (ع), descended with the two chapters of refuge and said to him: Labid ibn al-A'sam and the Jewish woman Umm Abd-Allah have worked sorcery against you. He told him where the sorcery lay, and recited over him: In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Say, I seek refuge in the Lord of the Falaq, and Say, I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, to the end of the two chapters. As the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, recited, one knot was loosened and he sat up; he recited again and another knot was loosened, until he had recited eleven times and eleven knots were loosened, and the Prophet, (ع) and his family, sat up free of it. He ordered Ali, who brought him the sorcery from the well in which it had been placed, and he summoned Labid and Umm Abd-Allah and said: What moved you to do what you have done? Then he said to Labid: May Allah not bring you out of this world safe and sound. Labid was a man of wealth and great means. A boy passed by him wearing in his ear an earring worth a dinar, so he snatched it and tore the boy's ear and took it; and for this his hand was cut off and cauterized, yet the bleeding would not stop, and he bled until he died.

Ali, (ع), said: I fell gravely ill, so that I could find no rest upon my bed, and the Prophet, (ع) and his family, came to visit me. He said to me: O Ali, the most sorely tried of people in this world are the prophets, then those who come after them, then those who come after them. Rejoice, for this is your share of the chastisement of Allah the Mighty and Majestic, together with the reward laid up for you. Then he said to me: Would you like Allah to lift from you what afflicts you? I said: Yes, O Messenger of Allah. He said: Say: O Allah, have mercy upon my thin skin and my fragile bone, and I seek refuge in You from the surge of the burning. O Umm Maldam, if you have believed in Allah, then do not eat flesh, do not drink blood, and do not boil over upon the mouth; depart instead to one who claims that there is another god alongside Allah. And I bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and His messenger. Ali, (ع), said: I said it, and I was healed at that very hour. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: I never turned to it in distress but that I found it answered; and they used to teach it even to women and children.

It was narrated from Ali, (ع), that the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, gave license for ruqyah by the Book of Allah the Mighty and Majestic, and by what is known of His remembrance and His names. He said: These incantations are among what Sulayman ibn Dawud, (ع), took as authority over the jinn.

It was narrated from him, (ع), that the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: There is no ruqyah except in three things: a festering wound, an evil eye, or blood. People hold that the ruqyah is for the sting of the scorpion and the sting of the wasp, but that is a mistake. The hummah is the venom in such things, from the scorpion and the serpent and others; the hummah is a venomous creature, while the scorpion's sting is a needle.

It was narrated of the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, that he sent out a company, and they passed by an Arab tribe whose chief had been stung. They asked whether anyone among them could perform a ruqyah, and one of them recited over him the Opening of the Book, and he was cured. When the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, was told of it, he approved it. One of his wives also performed a ruqyah with the two chapters of refuge, and he approved that, saying to her: And how did you know that it is a ruqyah?

He, may Allah bless him and his family, said: There is no contagion, no ill omen from birds, and no ham; but the evil eye is real and the good omen is real. And he, (ع) and his family, said: When one of you looks upon a person, or a beast, or anything pleasing that delights him, let him say: I have believed in Allah; may Allah bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad; then his eye will not harm what he looked upon. It was narrated from him, (ع), that when an eye struck him or a headache came upon him, he would spread out his two hands, recite into them the Opening of the Book and the two chapters of refuge, then wipe his face with them, and what he had been feeling would depart.

He, (ع) and his family, forbade sorcery, soothsaying, amulets, and tiwalah. Amulets are what is hung upon a person bearing other than the names of Allah, Blessed and Exalted; and tiwalah is what women make to draw the love of their husbands to them. Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: There is no harm in a charm that is hung if it contains something of the Qur'an.

A man complained to Abu Abd-Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), of a pain he found in himself. He said: Say In the name of Allah, and wipe over the place, then say: I seek refuge in the might of Allah, I seek refuge in the power of Allah, I seek refuge in the majesty of Allah, I seek refuge in the greatness of Allah, I seek refuge in all the bounds of Allah, I seek refuge in the names of Allah, and I seek refuge in the names of the Messenger, may Allah bless him and his family, from the evil of what I find. Let him say this seven times. The man said: I said it, and what I had been suffering left me.

It was narrated from him, (ع) and his family, that he said: When you wish to seek refuge, clasp your two palms together, recite into them the Opening of the Book and Say, He is Allah, One three times, then lay them upon the place of pain; then clasp them again, recite into them the Opening of the Book and Say, I seek refuge in the Lord of the Falaq three times, and lay them upon the pain a second time; then clasp them again, recite into them the Opening of the Book and Say, I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind three times, and lay them upon the pain. Three times in all.

It was narrated from Ali, (ع), that he said: Whoever is ill-tempered, let the call to prayer be given in his ear. And Abu Abd-Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said of the sick man who has a ruqyah performed for him: There is no harm in it, so long as the ruqyah is by something known. It was narrated from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), that he said: When you wish to perform a ruqyah upon a wound, against pain, or blood, or what is feared for it, place your hand upon the wound and say: In the name of Allah I perform your ruqyah; in the name of Allah the Greatest, against iron and stone, and the dark fang and the running of sweat, so let it not deceive, and against the eye, so let it not keep vigil. Repeat it three times.

Mention of treatment and medicine:

We have narrated from the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام), that the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, was asked whether the sick should be treated and given remedies. He said: Yes. Allah, Blessed and Exalted, has sent down no disease without sending down a remedy with it, so seek treatment, except for the sam, which is death, for it has no cure.

A group of the Ansar asked him about a man among them who complained of his stomach, and about a Jewish physician who treated such a thing by opening the abdomen. The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, was reluctant and did not permit them. They returned to him about it two or three times, and each time he refused them, until at last he said to them: Do as you wish. So the Jew was called for, and he opened the man's abdomen, removed a great deal of foul matter from it, then washed the abdomen, stitched it, and treated it, and the man became well. When the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, was told of it, he said: He who created the disease has appointed for it a remedy; and the best of remedies are cupping, phlebotomy, and the black seed, which is the shuneez. And the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, forbade imposing strict diets upon the sick.

He looked at Salman, who was eating dates while suffering from sore eyes, and said: O Salman, you eat dates while you have sore eyes? Salman said: O Messenger of Allah, it is only my right eye that is sore, and I am chewing with my left teeth. So the Messenger of Allah smiled and did not stop him.

It was narrated from him, (ع) and his family, that he said: To forgo the evening meal is a cause of weakness in old age. And he said, (ع) and his family: Do not force your sick to eat, for Allah the Mighty and Majestic feeds them and gives them drink. It was narrated from him, (ع) and his family, that there is no harm in an enema, were it not that it enlarges the belly. And he said, (ع) and his family: When blood becomes excessive in one of you, let him draw it off, even with a small blade, meaning when the blood overcomes him. And he said, (ع) and his family: Cupping of the head is a cure for every disease, and remedy lies in four things: cupping, the enema, the depilatory, and vomiting.

Ali, (ع), said: The Messenger of Allah was never seized by fever but that he turned to cupping. And he used to say: When blood becomes excessive in one of you, let him be cupped on whatever day it may be, and let him recite Ayat al-Kursi, seek the forgiveness of Allah the Mighty and Majestic, and send blessings upon the Prophet, may Allah bless him and his family. Do not hold certain days to be against you, lest they be set against you; be cupped on whichever of them you wish; and let him name Allah over the cupping vessel.

Among the things narrated concerning the body and its remedies is the matter of cupping: the cupping of the head brings relief, and the cupping of the upper back, the kahil, is beneficial. As for fever, it is from the heat of Hell, so it is to be cooled with water; and when the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) was taken with fever, he would call for water and place his hand in it.

Al-Hasan and al-Husayn ((عليهم السلام)) once fell ill with fever, and Fatima came to the Messenger of Allah seeking help and refuge. She said, "O Messenger of Allah, pray to Allah for your son that He heal him, for he is gravely ill." So the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) rose and came to him, sat at his head, and found him in a heavy state. He said, "O Fatima, it is Allah the Almighty who bestowed him upon you, and He is able to heal him." Then Jibril ((ع)) came to him and said, "O Muhammad, Allah the Almighty has not sent down upon you a sura of the Qur'an in which there is not a fa, and every fa is an affliction, an afa, save al-Hamd, for there is no fa in it. So call for a cup of water, recite al-Hamd over it forty times, then pour it over him, and Allah the Almighty will heal him." The Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) did so, and it was as though the boy had been loosed from a tether.

It is narrated from him ((ع)) that he was asked about cauterisation, and he forbade it. Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said, "There is no objection to cauterisation. As for what was forbidden, that concerns what is feared to cause death or what disfigures the creation. As for what one hopes will bring a cure, there is nothing wrong with it."

The Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) used to command applying kohl with ithmid before sleep, and he would say, "Apply kohl an odd number of times." And he said, "You must use ithmid, for it makes the lashes grow, removes the motes from the eye, and clears the sight." He said ((ع) and his family), "Al-Ajwa is from Paradise, and in it is a cure for poison." Some of the Ahl al-Bayt ((عليهم السلام)) described its preparation thus: one takes Ajwa dates, removes their stones, pounds them thoroughly, and kneads them with aged cow's ghee; then it is set aside, and when it is needed by one who has taken poison, it is eaten and benefits him.

It is narrated from him ((ع)) that a scorpion stung the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family). He struck it down and said, "May Allah curse you, for you spare neither believer nor disbeliever from your sting." Then he called for salt, placed it upon the place of the sting, and pressed it with his thumb until it dissolved, and said, "If people knew what is in salt, they would have no need of any antidote alongside it."

It is narrated from him ((ع)) that he said, "Whoever eats twenty-one seedless raisins each morning on an empty stomach will not fall ill except with the illness in which he dies." And, "Whoever eats seven dates at his bedtime will be spared colic, and the worms in his belly will be killed." He said ((ع)) to Ja'far ibn Abi Talib ((ع)), "Eat quince, for it strengthens the heart and emboldens the coward."

He said, "Honey is a healing." Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said that Allah the Almighty has mentioned it in His Book, where He said of it, "فيه شفاء للناس," that is, "In it is a healing for people." He said ((ع) and his family), "Beware of al-shabram, for it is hot; and you must take senna, so treat yourselves with it, for if anything could repel death, senna would repel it. And treat yourselves with fenugreek, for if my nation knew what it holds for them, they would treat themselves with it even were it to cost its weight in gold." He forbade ((ع) and his family) the eating of clay and charcoal, and said that Allah the Almighty created Adam from clay and therefore forbade the eating of clay to his descendants. "Whoever eats clay has aided in his own harm, and whoever persists in eating clay until he dies of it, I will not pray over him." Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said that the eating of clay breeds hypocrisy.

The Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) said that eating fresh, soft fish dissolves the body, so one should not become accustomed to it; and when he ate it he would say, "O Allah, bless it for us and give us in its place what is better than it." Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said, "Whoever eats fish, let him eat dates afterward, for that removes its harm." The Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) forbade the drinking of al-hamim. Al-hamim is the rain that falls in the severity of heat, and al-hamim is also hot water; likewise the rainwater that falls in intense heat and runs along the ground until it grows hot and harmful. For this reason he forbade its drinking, not because it is unlawful or impure, but because it was disliked for the harm it causes.

It is narrated from him ((ع)) that he said, "Whoever is a physician, or one who undertakes treatment, whether Arab or non-Arab, let him not treat anyone until he has obtained a written release from him, witnessed by witnesses. Let him fear Allah the Almighty, strive diligently, and counsel sincerely." By the release he meant that the physician should be learned in medicine and should not treat people while ignorant; for if he does, he is liable for what he destroys.

It is narrated from him ((ع)) that he used to say, "Whoever desires to endure, though there is no enduring, let him lighten his burden, keep to his sandals, take his morning meal early, draw near his evening meal, and lessen intercourse." It was said to him, "O Commander of the Faithful, what is the lightening of the burden?" He replied, "It is debt."

He said ((ع)), "The truffle is from the manna, and its water is a cure for the eye." Some of the Ahl al-Bayt ((عليهم السلام)) described its preparation: one takes a truffle and washes it until it is clean, then squeezes its water into a vessel and kindles a fire beneath it until it thickens; then musk is added to it, and it is applied as kohl for all the ailments of the eye. When its moisture dries, it is moistened with rainwater from the sky and applied as kohl. And it is narrated from Ali ((ع)) that he said, "Nothing restores the woman in childbirth like the eating of fresh, moist dates. Allah the Almighty fed Maryam fresh dates in her childbirth." He said ((ع)), "Eat the pomegranate with its pith, for it is a tanner for the stomach. And you must eat apples, for they cleanse the stomach."

It is narrated from him ((ع) and his family) that the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) said, "Celery is the herb of the prophets, and there is no leaf of endive but that upon it is water from Paradise. You must take the gourd, for it strengthens the heart and the mind." He ((ع) and his family) once trod upon the scorching ground and it burned him, so he stepped onto the purslane, which is the foolish herb, and the heat of the scorching ground subsided for him; so he blessed it, and he used to eat it and was fond of it. Ali ((ع)) said, "The sick are healed by nothing like the drinking of honey."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) was asked about a man who seeks treatment from a Jew or a Christian. He said, "There is nothing wrong with that, for healing is in the hand of Allah the Almighty." He was also asked: if a woman has a disease in her body, or a fracture or a wound in a place that is not proper to look upon, may she uncover it to a man who treats her? He said, "There is nothing wrong with that when she is compelled to it." And Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) was asked about a man who treats people with medicine and takes a fee for it, and he said, "There is nothing wrong with it."

It is narrated from him ((ع)) that he said, "If people were moderate in their food and drink, their bodies would remain sound." He said ((ع)), "Abandoning the evening meal is the ruin of the body." He said, "Allah the Almighty says, ولهم رزقهم فيها بكرة وعشيا, that is, and they shall have their provision therein, morning and evening." A man complained to him of pains and indigestion that he was suffering, and he said, "Take your midday meal and your evening meal, and eat nothing between them, for that is the corruption of the body." He said, "Abandoning the evening meal diminishes a strength that does not return," and, "Let not the aged man abandon the evening meal, even if it be only a morsel." He said ((ع)), "Eating meat with milk makes the flesh grow and strengthens the bone." And he said ((ع)), "A prophet among the prophets complained to Allah the Almighty of his few children, so He commanded him to eat meat with eggs."

It is narrated from him ((ع) and his family) that the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) counselled Ali ((ع)), saying, "O Ali, sleep upon your back, for it slims your belly; and drink water by sipping, for thereby your food is made wholesome. Wear a ring, and apply kohl an odd number of times, for it will brighten your sight. Anoint yourself every other day, in keeping with the Sunnah of your Prophet. Renew your sandals, for they are the anklets of a man, and turbans are the crowns of the Arabs. When you cook a pot, make its broth plentiful, for if your neighbours do not receive some of its meat, they will receive some of its broth. Wear a ring of ruby and carnelian, for it is fortunate and blessed; whenever a man looks at his face within it, it increases him in light, and the prayer offered wearing it is worth seventy prayers. Wear the ring upon your right hand, for that is the practice of the Messengers, and whoever turns away from my Sunnah is not of me. Do not wear a ring upon the left, nor of anything but ruby and carnelian."

It is narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) that a man among his companions complained to him of pain in his loin. He said to him, "Take what falls from the table among the food, follow after it, and eat it." The man said, "I did so, and I was healed." Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said, "Eat of the food that falls from the table, for in it is a healing, by the leave of Allah the Almighty, for whoever seeks healing by it, that is, for every disease." A man looked at apples in the hands of Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)), and he said to him ((ع)), "The apple cools the heat and quenches it, and removes the fever." He mentioned vinegar ((ع)) and said, "It firms the mouth and kills the worms of the belly." He said ((ع)), "Rice sawiq, when it is parched and ground, binds the belly; if part of it is made into sawiq and part into a gruel, it binds the belly. Sawiq makes the flesh grow and strengthens the bone, and it benefits the feverish one when it is moistened and washed three times. Take sawiq dry on an empty stomach, for it dispels phlegm and bile." And it is narrated from Ali ibn al-Husayn ((ع)) that he permitted treatment with the milk of she-asses, and that if one finds the milk of the wild ass, that is better.
