# When a Marriage Ends: Divorce and Separation

Beyond the reckoning of the waiting period itself lies the question of what a woman is owed once the marriage has come apart, and here the law weighs carefully the different ways in which a marriage may end.

A pregnant woman who has been divorced is entitled to both maintenance and accommodation. The pregnant widow whose husband has died, however, has no maintenance. A woman divorced revocably, who is passing her ʿiddah, is entitled to maintenance and accommodation until her waiting period is complete. But the woman who has been divorced irrevocably has neither maintenance nor accommodation.

Mourning belongs to the widow whose husband has died. It is not required of the divorced woman, nor of the woman parted from her husband by khulʿ, nor of the woman parted by mubārāh, nor of the woman parted by liʿān. Mourning means that the widow, throughout the period she is counting, does not comb her hair and does not dye it, does not perfume herself and does not apply kohl, does not adorn herself and does not wear a dyed garment, unless that garment is black. She does not leave her house during the day, nor does she pass the night away from her house. If she wishes, she may go out in the last part of the night and return before evening. This is the essence of mourning.

## On the Provision of Divorce and Making the Divorced Woman Lawful Again

Allah, mighty and majestic, said:

> لَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِنْ طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ مَا لَمْ تَمَسُّوهُنَّ أَوْ تَفْرِضُوا لَهُنَّ فَرِيضَةً ۚ وَمَتِّعُوهُنَّ عَلَى الْمُوسِعِ قَدَرُهُ وَعَلَى الْمُقْتِرِ قَدَرُهُ مَتَاعًا بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۖ حَقًّا عَلَى الْمُحْسِنِينَ

"There is no blame upon you if you divorce women whom you have not touched, or for whom you have not specified an obligation. And make provision for them, the wealthy according to his means and the straitened according to his means, a provision in kindness, a duty upon those who do good." (Al-Baqarah 236)

And so the provision is obligatory. He also said:

> وَلِلْمُطَلَّقَاتِ مَتَاعٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۖ حَقًّا عَلَى الْمُتَّقِينَ

"And for divorced women there is a provision in kindness, a duty upon the God-fearing." (Al-Baqarah 241)

The provision of mutʿa is due to the woman divorced according to the Sunnah and to the woman passing her ʿiddah, whether or not the marriage was consummated. There is no mutʿa for the woman parted by khulʿ, nor for the woman parted by mubārāh. There is no fixed limit upon the amount of the provision. It rests upon the man of means, as Allah, mighty and majestic, said, "upon the wealthy according to his means and upon the straitened according to his means." Whatever the divorcing husband provides discharges the obligation. It is recommended, when a man has means, that he be generous in what he provides for her, whether with dinars or dirhams, a slave or a slave-girl, goods or food, all of which has been narrated from the Imams, the blessings of Allah be upon them. The provision is not given until after the ʿiddah has ended and she has become fully separated from him. So long as he still hopes for her and she still hopes for him, he does not give her the provision.

If a man divorces his wife in the manner we have described, for the ʿiddah or according to the Sunnah, with three pronouncements, she does not become lawful to him, as Allah, mighty and majestic, said, except after another husband has married her and consummated the marriage with her.

The second husband may then divorce her, or he may die. After her waiting period from the second husband has expired, the first husband may marry her again if he wishes and she wishes, in a fresh and renewed marriage, and she stands with him facing the full count of divorce anew. If he then divorces her three times in this way, and she marries another, and he afterward takes her back, she does not become lawful to him again after those three, even if she remarries. She is lawful to him only if she contracts a marriage of genuine desire, nikāḥ ghibṭa, with an adult man who consummates the marriage and has intercourse with her. A castrated man does not make her lawful for him, nor does a boy who has not yet reached puberty, unless he has intercourse with her after reaching puberty. A marriage of mutʿa does not make her lawful to him. Nor is it lawful for him to collude with a man so that the man will marry her and make her lawful for him, nor to arrange for some other man to do the same.

If, however, he divorces her once or twice, so that she becomes separated from him, and she then marries another who either dies or divorces her, and the first husband marries her again after her waiting period from the second husband has expired, she stands with him for whatever remained of her count of divorces, up to the full three, as set out above. She does not become lawful to him except after a husband. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, the blessings of Allah be upon them.

## On the Return, the Status of Slaves, and the Dowries Binding upon Husbands

We have mentioned above, in describing divorce, that when a man divorces his wife he may take her back so long as her waiting period has not ended. Once it has ended, he is but one suitor among suitors. If she wishes and he wishes, he marries her in a fresh and renewed marriage, and she stands with him for whatever remains of her count of divorces. If he takes her back before her waiting period expires, that is his right whether she consents or refuses, and she stands with him for whatever remains of her count of divorces. The return must be witnessed by two just witnesses. When he takes her back before her waiting period has ended, he does so without any compensation and without a new dowry. But if her waiting period has passed and they agree upon a return, he may not take her back except with a new dowry that he names for her.

A man does not need to seek his wife's permission to return to her so long as he still has a right of return over her. If he divorces her a third time, there is no return to her. The divorce pronounced by a slave against a free woman counts as three, and the divorce pronounced by a free man or a slave against a slave-woman counts as two.

If a man divorces his wife before consummating the marriage, she receives half the dowry, as Allah said. If he had given her an animal as her dowry and it bore young while in her keeping, and he then divorced her before consummation, the matter is examined. If the animal was already pregnant at the time he gave it to her as dowry, he takes back half of it and half of its offspring. But if the pregnancy occurred while the animal was in her keeping, the offspring are entirely hers, and she returns half of the mother-animals. If he had given her a dowry and she then gave it back to him as a gift, or bestowed it upon him as charity, and he afterward divorced her before consummation, he takes back half of it from her. And if she was his own slave-woman, and he had made her freedom her dowry, as we have described before, and he then divorced her before consummation, he takes back half of her value as assessed on the day he married her. If he dies without having named a dowry for her and without having consummated the marriage, she has no claim to a dowry, yet she inherits from him.

# Gifts, Bequests, and the Shares of Inheritance

## On Gifts and Generosity

Gifts, good works, and beneficence offered for the sake of God Almighty and in pursuit of His reward hold within them virtue, righteousness, and recompense. The Messenger of God, peace be upon him and his family, said: "All of creation are the children of God, and the most beloved of creation to God Almighty is the one who benefits His children, who brings joy to a household, and who walks with a Muslim brother in his time of need." Such a person, he said, is more beloved to God Almighty than two months of iʿtikāf in the Sacred Mosque.

ʿAlī, prayers of God be upon him, said that the people of goodness stand in greater need of having their goodness accepted than the needy stand in need of them, because through that goodness come praise, reward, and remembrance for them from God. Whoever performs a kind act toward one of God's creatures has in truth ennobled himself by it and adorned his own honor.

Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī, prayers of God be upon him, said: "When God raises the believer from his grave, there goes forth with him a beautiful figure. When he passes through those terrors, it says to him, 'Do not fear, no harm shall come to you,' and it does not cease to reassure him and give him glad tidings until it brings him before God, who calls him to an easy reckoning and then commands that he be taken to Paradise. The believer says to it, 'Who are you, may God have mercy upon you? You promised me and were truthful to me and gave me security.' It replies, 'I am a creation your Lord fashioned out of the joy you used to bring to the believers. Today it is I who gladden you.'"

Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad, prayers of God be upon him, said: "There is nothing more excellent than beneficence except its reward, and beneficence is a gift from God Almighty to His believing servant. Not everyone who loves to do good to people is opened to it, nor is everyone who desires it able to do it, nor is everyone who is able to do it given leave to do it. So when God Almighty bestows His grace upon a servant, He gathers together for him the desire for beneficence, the ability for it, and the leave to perform it. There, happiness and honor are complete, both for the one who seeks the good and the one to whom it is directed."

He said, peace be upon him: "Beneficence is not made complete except by three qualities: making little of it, making it easy, and hastening it. When you make little of it, you have made it great in the eyes of the one to whom you do it; when you make it easy, you have rendered it complete for him; and when you hasten it, you have made it a cause of joy for him. But if you do not do these things, you have spoiled it and rendered it thankless."

He said, peace be upon him: "The best of Muslims is the one who joins, helps, and gives." And he said, peace be upon him: "I heard my father, peace be upon him, say: the doing of good wards off the calamities of evil. Every kindness is a charity, and the people of goodness in this world are the people of goodness in the Hereafter. The first to enter Paradise are the people of goodness."

The Messenger of God, peace be upon him and his family, said: "Whoever has a kindness done to him, let him repay it; if he is unable, let him praise the one who did it; and if he does not, then he has been ungrateful for the blessing."

We have narrated from the Imāms of the People of the House of the Messenger of God, prayers of God be upon them, that they permitted a man to favor some of his children over others with his wealth and his gifts if he wished. A gift or a charity is valid whether or not it has been taken into the recipient's possession. Whoever gives a gift for the sake of God Almighty, or to a blood relative or a kinsman, has no right to take it back. But if he gives a gift or grant intending to be compensated for it, that is disliked, and he may take it back if he is not compensated. And whoever gives a gift, or gives a charity, to a blood relative or kinsman who then dies, so that it returns to him by inheritance, in that there is no harm.

The ʿumrā, the ruqbā, and the suknā are among the things in which there is merit and reward. The ʿumrā is that a man grants another man a house, an estate, or anything he may benefit from for the duration of his life. The ruqbā is of the same meaning, except that it carries the condition that whichever of the two dies first, the property returns to the original grantor or to his heirs. If a man grants something for his own lifetime, then it is his while he lives. If the condition is set upon the life of the grantor and he dies, it returns to his heirs. If it is conditioned upon the lifetime of the recipient, then it is the recipient's for his life, and if the grantor dies, his heirs may not take it from the recipient's hands; he may dwell in it and inhabit it until he dies. And if the grantor made it for the recipient and his heirs, then it belongs to him and to them after him, according to how the grantor settled it.

Presents are commended when they are for the sake of God Almighty and for His face. There is no good in any present given to curry favor in a falsehood, or to seek a like return, or to obtain the fulfillment of a need, or to secure attention from the one to whom it is given. The fulfilling of a believer's needs and care for him in what is right is an obligation upon his believing brother; so if he takes payment for that, it is unlawful gain. Likewise is whatever he takes for collusion in falsehood.

As for what is an imposed obligation, such as zakāt and the like, the merit is to give it openly and to announce it. As for voluntary charity, the merit is to keep it secret, though if it is announced there is no harm in that. God, exalted be He, has said: ﴿إِنْ تُبْدُوا الصَّدَقَاتِ فَنِعِمَّا هِيَ وَإِنْ تُخْفُوهَا وَتُؤْتُوهَا الْفُقَرَاءَ فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ﴾ — "If you disclose your charities, that is good; but if you conceal them and give them to the poor, that is better for you." (Al-Baqarah: 271)

So it is with all the acts of righteousness: the disclosure and mention of their obligatory portions is good, while the concealment and secrecy of their voluntary portions is more excellent. The obligatory prayer is more excellent in the mosques and in congregation, while the voluntary prayer is more excellent in seclusion. If a man were to say, "I have prayed the noon prayer," or the afternoon prayer, or whatever obligatory prayer he names, there is no harm in his saying so; and likewise if he says, "I have fasted the month of Ramaḍān." But if he says, "I have risen for the night prayer," or "I have fasted such-and-such" of the voluntary acts, then that saying of his is disliked, on account of the ostentation it admits. Upon this principle run all deeds.

Whoever owns something but has not yet taken it into his possession may still give it in charity or as a gift. Whoever gives a charity is not permitted to take it back. Whoever sets something aside as an endowment for his child, his freed clients, or particular people he names, and removes it from his own hand and delivers it to them, that is valid, and it stands according to whatever condition he stipulates and the terms upon which he restricts it. If they later fall into dispute over it and corruption among them is feared, they may sell it if they wish and divide its price in proportion to what was assigned to each, provided it was set aside for them by name. But if he set it aside for them and their descendants, or said, "When they die out, it becomes a charity," then it is a charity and they are not permitted to sell it. If he made its final destination a charity, then when those for whom it was set aside die out it passes to the poor and the needy. If he did not make its final destination a charity, and those for whom it was endowed die out, it returns to him if he is living, or to whoever is entitled to his inheritance if he has died. If he stipulated that any of the women for whom the endowment was made who marries forfeits her right in it, and then they are widowed and return to it, the condition holds. And if he said, "If I am in need, I have the better right to it," then the endowment is void; it is part of his estate for his heirs if he dies, and he may take it back whenever he wishes.

## On Bequests

God, exalted be He, has said: ﴿كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ إِنْ تَرَكَ خَيْرًا الْوَصِيَّةُ﴾ — "It is prescribed for you, when death approaches one of you and he leaves wealth behind, that he make a bequest." (Al-Baqarah: 180) And death is present for every living soul, for none knows when it will come to him. He also said: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا شَهَادَةُ بَيْنِكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ حِينَ الْوَصِيَّةِ﴾ — "O you who believe, let there be testimony among you when death approaches one of you, at the time of the bequest." (Al-Māʾidah: 106)

The Messenger of God, peace be upon him and his family, said: "It does not befit a Muslim to pass two nights except that his will is written and kept at his head." And he said, peace be upon him and his family: "Whoever does not make his bequest well at the time of death has fallen short in his manliness and his reason." They asked, "O Messenger of God, how does the dying man make his bequest?" He said that the people are to be gathered when death comes to him, and he bears witness and makes his bequest; and he spoke of this at length.

Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī, prayers of God be upon him, said: "The bequest is a duty upon every Muslim." And Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad, prayers of God be upon him, said: "The bequest is obligatory upon every Muslim, and it does not befit anyone to pass two nights except that his will is written. No dying person dies except that God Almighty restores to him his reason, his hearing, and his sight before death so that he may make his bequest, whether he takes that opportunity or leaves it."

We have narrated from the Imāms of the House of the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, that they permitted a bequest of up to one-third, and no more, of what the testator leaves behind, and they did not allow anything beyond the third except with the consent of the heirs.

Whoever makes a set of bequests in which he directs that the Hajj be performed on his behalf, that a slave be freed, and that expiation be made for his oaths: if he had not performed the Hajj and is unable to, and the bequest falls short of the third, then one begins with the Hajj, then the expiation, then the manumission, and whatever falls short is met from what remains. This is when he has framed the bequest by saying, "Let my third be brought out and spent on such-and-such." But if he made a bequest without naming a third or anything else, and directed that the Hajj be performed for him when he had not performed it, then the Hajj is brought out from the whole of what he left. Likewise, if he states that he owes zakāt or any other obligation and directs that it be discharged on his behalf, it is brought out from the entire estate, while whatever is voluntary is brought out from the third. So if he had already performed the Hajj and then directs that a Hajj be performed for him, that is voluntary and is brought out only from the third.

There is no bequest for an heir unless the other heirs permit it, for God Almighty has fixed the heirs' inheritance and nothing is added to it, unless the testator makes a bequest for a parent or a relative who does not inherit from him, as when his parents are enslaved or polytheists, or related to him through nursing, or are foster parents. If a man, during the illness in which he dies, acknowledges a debt before his heirs or one of them, his case is examined: if he is suspected, his acknowledgment is not accepted except by clear proof testifying to the origin of what he acknowledged, unless the rest of the heirs permit it; but if he is sick yet upright and trustworthy, his acknowledgment is accepted. When a man has made his bequest, he may revoke it, and he may add to or subtract from his bequest so long as he is alive and understands what he bequeaths. And if he makes bequests exceeding the third, and his heirs come to know of this in his lifetime and approve and allow it to him, then they have no right to retract it after his death.

## On the Obligatory Shares of Inheritance

This concerns the shares of the child, the parents, the spouses, the grandfather, the grandmother, and the brothers.

God, mighty and majestic, has said: ﴿يُوصِيكُمُ اللَّهُ فِي أَوْلَادِكُمْ لِلذَّكَرِ مِثْلُ حَظِّ الْأُنْثَيَيْنِ فَإِنْ كُنَّ نِسَاءً فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ فَلَهُنَّ ثُلُثَا مَا تَرَكَ وَإِنْ كَانَتْ وَاحِدَةً فَلَهَا النِّصْفُ وَلِأَبَوَيْهِ لِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ مِمَّا تَرَكَ إِنْ كَانَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ فَإِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَوَرِثَهُ أَبَوَاهُ فَلِأُمِّهِ الثُّلُثُ فَإِنْ كَانَ لَهُ إِخْوَةٌ فَلِأُمِّهِ السُّدُسُ مِنْ بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِي بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ﴾ — "God charges you concerning your children: to the male the like of the share of two females. If they are women, more than two, theirs is two-thirds of what he left; and if there is only one, hers is the half. To each of his parents a sixth of what he left, if he has a child; but if he has no child and his parents inherit from him, then to his mother a third; and if he has brothers, then to his mother a sixth, after any bequest he makes or any debt." (Al-Nisāʾ: 11)

We have narrated from the People of the House, prayers of God be upon them, that whoever dies leaving children, male and female, with no heir besides them, his inheritance is divided among them so that the male among them receives the like of the share of two females. So too if he leaves a single son and a single daughter, the son receives two-thirds and the daughter one-third. If he leaves a single son with no other heir, the whole inheritance is his. If he leaves two sons, or a number of sons, the inheritance is divided among them equally.

If he leaves a single daughter with no other heir, she takes the half by the share God Almighty has named for her in what we have recited, and the other half is returned to her by the tie of kinship, in accordance with the word of God, mighty and majestic: ﴿وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنْ بَعْدُ وَهَاجَرُوا وَجَاهَدُوا مَعَكُمْ فَأُولَئِكَ مِنْكُمْ وَأُولُو الْأَرْحَامِ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلَى بِبَعْضٍ فِي كِتَابِ اللَّهِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ﴾ — "And those who believed afterward and emigrated and struggled along with you, they are of you; and those who are bound by blood are nearer to one another in the Book of God. Truly God has knowledge of all things." (Al-Anfāl: 75) And if they are two daughters, or a group of daughters, the two-thirds is theirs, divided among them equally by the named share, and the remaining third is returned to them by the tie of kinship.

When the children are present together with those entitled to fixed shares, such as the husband, the wife, the grandmother, and the parents, these receive their fixed shares which God Almighty has named for them, and what remains goes to the child or the children, if they are a group, as we have described. If there is no child but there is a child's child, they stand in the place of the child, the males among them as the males and the females as the females, even if they descend and become remote, so long as there is none nearer to the deceased among them; for whoever is nearer of them screens out whoever is more remote. And everyone for whom God Almighty has named a share in the Book among the heirs has a better right than the one He has not named. So when something is left over beyond what He named for him, it is returned to him, and to the sons and the group, if they are of them, in proportion to their inheritance, except for the husband and the wife: nothing is returned to those two unless they have a kinship that entitles them to the return.

An example: a man dies and leaves his two parents and a daughter. The parents take the two sixths, and the daughter takes the half by her named share, and a sixth remains, which is returned to them in proportion to their shares. This is because one share remains out of six, since the two sixths went to the parents, which are two shares, and the half went to the daughter, which is three sixths, making five shares, with one share remaining. That remaining share must likewise be divided over the five, so the estate reverts to five: three-fifths of the inheritance to the daughter and two-fifths to the parents.

If he leaves a daughter, a father, and a mother, the daughter takes the half as we mentioned, and the father, or the mother, takes the sixth, so the estate reverts, in the manner we have described, to fourths: a quarter to the father or the mother, and three-quarters to the daughter. Upon this pattern runs whatever follows of the same kind.

# When a Marriage Ends: Divorce and Separation

When the parents alone survive a person and there is no other heir, the father receives two thirds and the mother receives one third. If a child is also present, the rule proceeds as we have already described, even when the children are many, both male and female: they take what remains after the fixed shares, the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females, however few or however many they may be. If two sons survive, they share what remains between them; and if two children survive together with the parents, they receive two thirds between them. We have already mentioned what falls to a single child.

If a person leaves behind two parents together with brothers, whether full brothers or brothers through the father, the brothers receive nothing. The mother receives one sixth and the father takes what remains, as Allah, mighty and majestic, has stated in His Book. The brothers through the mother do not deprive the mother of her third by way of inheriting for themselves; they merely reduce her share, so that the father receives two thirds and the mother one third. The father inherits alongside the child and alongside the parents, and none inherits together with the child and the parents except the husband, the wife, and the grandmother.

It is narrated from the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, that he assigned an inheritance to the grandmother and ruled that one sixth be fixed for her, looking to her children as they divided among themselves. So the grandmother inherits one sixth alongside the parents, and alongside either parent alone. If she is alone with no one to exclude her, and no other heir survives, she takes one sixth by the naming of the share and the remainder is returned to her, so that she gathers the whole inheritance. If there are two grandmothers, a grandmother on the father's side and a grandmother on the mother's side, the father's grandmother and the mother's mother share two thirds, and the mother's mother alone takes one third. Among the grandmothers, whichever is closer in degree excludes the one more remote.

The paternal grandfather stands in the position of the full brothers, and the brothers through the father inherit as they inherit. He is reckoned as one of them, and whoever excludes them excludes him. The maternal grandfather stands in the position of the brother through the mother, and he too inherits as they inherit and is excluded as they are excluded. This is because the grandfather's kinship to the deceased is like the kinship of the brother, each of them being joined to the deceased through his father, and their kinship through the father is a single kinship: the grandfather is that father's father, and the brother is that father's son.

If the deceased leaves behind no father, no mother, and no other ascendant, but is survived by brothers, the inheritance belongs to those brothers. The full brothers exclude the brothers through the father; and where there are no full brothers, the brothers through the father stand in their place. When the brothers are both full and paternal together, the inheritance is divided among them as with male and female shares. If they are through the mother alone, the inheritance is divided equally between male and female, as Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿وَإِن كَانَ رَجُلٌ يُورَثُ كَلَالَةً أَوِ امْرَأَةٌ وَلَهُ أَخٌ أَوْ أُخْتٌ فَلِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِّنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ فَإِن كَانُوا أَكْثَرَ مِن ذَٰلِكَ فَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ فِي الثُّلُثِ﴾ "If a man dies leaving no children, and he has a brother or a sister, each of them receives one sixth; but if there are more than that, they share one third" (al-Nisaʾ 12). It is narrated that this verse was revealed concerning a brother and a sister through the mother.

When full brothers and brothers through the mother are present together, along with a brother through the father, the full brothers receive two thirds divided among them, the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females, whether they are few or many. If maternal siblings are also present with them, male or female, two or more, whether two sisters, or a brother and sister, or brothers and sisters, then one third belongs to those maternal siblings, divided equally among them regardless of sex, however many they may be, and the brothers through the father are set aside. Likewise, if a person leaves behind one maternal brother or one maternal sister, that single one receives one sixth; and if there are several, they receive one third. If there is a full sister, she receives one half, and the remainder is returned to her through kinship.

Brothers and sisters inherit alongside the spouses. The spouse's share is given first, and what remains goes to the brothers according to what we have described. The husband receives half of his wife's inheritance if she has no child, and a quarter if she has a child, whether male, female, or several. The wife receives a quarter of her husband's inheritance if he has no child, and one eighth if he has a child, whether male, female, or several, according to the explicit text of the Book. Two, three, or four wives share equally in that same portion, one eighth where there is a child and a quarter where there is none, dividing it equally among them and adding nothing to it.

If a man dies leaving two parents and a wife, or a woman dies leaving two parents and a husband, the husband receives half of his wife's estate, the wife receives a quarter of her husband's estate, the mother receives one third, and the father takes what remains, according to the text of the Book. Just as the father has precedence over the mother when brothers are present, so here he bears the deficiency, because Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿فَإِن كَانَ لَهُ إِخْوَةٌ فَلِأُمِّهِ السُّدُسُ﴾ "If he has brothers, his mother receives one sixth" (al-Nisaʾ 11). Since the brothers do not inherit alongside the parents, the remaining wealth here is inherited by the parents. So the mother receives one third when there are no brothers, and one sixth when there are brothers. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them all.

## On the inheritance of kindred and connections, and on the apportioning of shares without ʿawl and without exclusion

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them, that whoever dies leaving behind those who hold fixed shares named in the Book, they inherit according to the shares Allah, mighty and majestic, has named in the Book. If he leaves behind none of the named sharers, his inheritance passes to those of his kindred who are nearest to him; and if they are equal in nearness, it is shared among them. Whoever draws near to him through one who holds a named share is more deserving than one who draws near through one who has no named share. Those who are near exclude those who are remote. Whoever is related through the deceased's father inherits the father's share as if the father were alive; whoever is related through the mother inherits the mother's share; whoever is related through a brother inherits that brother's share; and whoever is related through a sister inherits her share.

For example: a man dies leaving behind his son's son and his son's daughter. The inheritance between them is divided with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females. If he leaves behind his daughter's son and his son's daughter, the son's daughter receives two thirds, which is her father's share, and the daughter's son receives one third, which is his mother's share. And if he leaves behind sons and daughters of a son who predeceased him, together with a son or daughter of a daughter who predeceased him, one third goes to the single son or single daughter, or whatever children there are of the daughter, and two thirds go to the children of the son, divided among them with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females, whether few or many. These inherit their father's share as if he were alive, and those inherit their mother's share as if she were alive, whether each group is few or many.

Those who draw near through the mother's kinship, among the kindred, inherit as the brothers through the mother inherit, the male and the female alike. Those who draw near through the father's kinship inherit with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females. The sons of brothers and their daughters, and the sisters, inherit in the manner we have described when there is no direct child or grandchild, just as the grandchildren inherit as we explained before. If they are through one father or one mother, the sons of the brother divide the wealth among themselves with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females. Likewise, the sons of paternal uncles and the sons of maternal uncles divide the wealth, the sons of maternal uncles equally between male and female, each group inheriting what belonged to its father or mother had they been alive, whether they are few or many in number. So too the maternal uncle and the maternal aunt share the wealth equally between them, while the paternal uncle and the paternal aunt share it with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females, and their children likewise. To the sons of the maternal uncle and the sons of the maternal aunt belongs one third, shared among them, their males and their children alike; and to the sons of the paternal uncle and the paternal aunt belong the two thirds, divided among them with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females.

Whoever among the kindred and the connections is nearer to the deceased excludes the one more remote. The paternal uncle excludes the cousin; the son of the maternal uncle and the maternal aunt excludes the cousin and the son of the maternal uncle; the paternal aunt excludes the cousin and the son of the maternal uncle; and the maternal aunt excludes the cousin and the son of the maternal aunt, because they have come to the deceased by one degree sooner. Whoever draws near through a full brother is more deserving than one who draws near through a brother through the father. To the kindred through the mother belongs their portion of the sixth that was the share of the one through whom they are related, divided among them, their males and their females alike. Whoever draws near through one who holds a share excludes whoever draws near through one who holds no share. When several groups each draw near through a different person who holds a share, each group receives the share of the one through whom they are related, as has already been said.

There is no ʿawl in any fixed apportionment. One begins with the one whom Allah, mighty and majestic, has placed first. Whoever Allah, mighty and majestic, has named a fixed share when alone, and then is reduced in the presence of another, nothing is deducted from that second named share. So it is with the husband, the wife, the mother, and the father: nothing is deducted from the husband's quarter, nor from the wife's eighth, nor from either parent's sixth. Upon the one who holds the remainder falls the deficiency, and to him belongs the increase.

An example of this is a woman who dies leaving her husband, her brothers through the mother, and her sister through the father. Allah, mighty and majestic, named one half for the husband, since she has no child, and one half for the sister, and one third for the two maternal brothers, by the apparent reading of what He named. When the highest of the fixed shares were examined here, the matter was found bewildering, so some inflated it and turned the six shares into eight. But Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿إِنِ امْرُؤٌ هَلَكَ لَيْسَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَلَهُ أُخْتٌ فَلَهَا نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ وَهُوَ يَرِثُهَا إِن لَّمْ يَكُن لَّهَا وَلَدٌ﴾ "If a man dies having no child, and he has a sister, she receives half of what he left; and he inherits from her if she has no child" (al-Nisaʾ 176). The Almighty thus made clear that this is so only in the case of the sister alone, not when others share with her. He also said: ﴿وَإِن كَانَ رَجُلٌ يُورَثُ كَلَالَةً أَوِ امْرَأَةٌ وَلَهُ أَخٌ أَوْ أُخْتٌ فَلِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِّنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ فَإِن كَانُوا أَكْثَرَ مِن ذَٰلِكَ فَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ فِي الثُّلُثِ﴾ "If a man dies leaving no children, and he has a brother or a sister, each of them receives one sixth; but if there are more than that, they are partners in one third" (al-Nisaʾ 12). And they themselves say that the brother for whom Allah named the whole, were he in the place of the sister, would be given only one sixth. So in this case the husband receives one half, the maternal brothers one third, and the sister through the father one sixth.

Likewise, if she leaves a husband, two parents, and a daughter, the husband receives a quarter, the two parents receive two sixths, and what remains goes to the daughter, because were she a son she would receive no more than this. The deficiency falls upon the one to whom the surplus belongs. Upon this basis all the questions of ʿawl proceed. As for those of them that mention brothers and sisters together with the mother, we have already said that they inherit nothing in her presence; so whatever questions of ʿawl are reported are carried upon this meaning. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them all.

## On the inheritance of the son repudiated by liʿān, the child of adultery, the foundling, the captive of unknown kin, the fetus, the Muslim, the disbeliever, the Magian, the apostate, and the bondservant

We have mentioned earlier the liʿān and how it is carried out, and that whoever disavows a child his wife has borne, and who undertakes the liʿān against her concerning it, the child is not attributed to him. The child's lineage is the lineage of his mother, and his inheritance belongs to his kindred through her. The one who disavowed him does not inherit from him, nor does anyone inherit through him, unless he has acknowledged him after the liʿān. For we have mentioned that if a man undertakes the liʿān against a child and then acknowledges him afterward, the child inherits from him, but the one who undertook the liʿān against his mother does not inherit the child, nor does anyone inherit through that man; rather the child inherits, and those who draw near through his kinship inherit.

The child of adultery is not joined to the adulterer who lay with his mother and from whom she bore him. He is not attributed to him, and the two do not inherit from one another, whether he acknowledges him or denies him. His mother inherits from him and he inherits from her, and he draws near through her, and those related to her draw near to him through her; but no one draws near to him through his father, nor does he draw near to anyone through his father. As for the foundling who is found with neither known mother nor known father, he does not inherit and is not inherited from, and no one draws near to him through his parents.

The captive of unknown kin is one who is taken from the land of disbelief, and another is taken with him, or before him, or after him, and the two come to recognize one another in the abode of Islam and affirm their kinship, and they continue thus, affirming it, until one of them dies. The survivor then inherits from the deceased by the kinship that they affirmed during their lives, and they inherit from one another and draw near to one another by their kinship.

It is narrated from the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, that he said, "The people of two different faiths do not inherit from one another." So no people of two different faiths inherit from one another. The meaning of mutual inheritance is that each of these inherits from the other; in the language its form is reciprocal, and reciprocal action in the speech of the Arabs proceeds only from two agents, though the simple act may proceed from one. It is said that two men struck one another when each struck his companion, and that a man struck another when one struck the other. The Jew does not inherit from the Christian, nor the Christian from the Jew, nor the Christian and the Jew and the Magian from the Muslim. The Muslim, however, inherits from them by his kinship, as has come from the Imams, may the prayers of Allah be upon them, and the disbeliever does not exclude him. The narration upon this meaning is sound, for they do not inherit from one another in that the polytheists do not inherit from the Muslims, while the Muslims do inherit from them. The people of each faith inherit among themselves according to how the fixed shares run, and that is judged among them. If they bring their case before the Muslims in a matter of inheritance, it is judged according to what their religion and their faith prescribe; and if they do not raise it to the consideration of the Muslims, they are left alone and not interfered with.

The bondservant does not inherit from the free person, and the free person likewise does not inherit from the bondservant through kinship, and the wealth of the bondservant belongs to his master. Whoever embraces Islam from disbelief, or is freed from bondage, before the one who would inherit from him dies and before his inheritance is divided, has his portion of it; but if he is freed after the division, he has no portion in it. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them all.

## On the inheritance of the killer, the blood money, the intersexed person, those who drown together, the freed bondservant and his patron, the divorced woman, and the one who has no heir

Among these are questions of the fixed shares, and of one heir who acknowledges another heir. Whoever kills another, whether deliberately or by mistake, does not inherit from him, nor from his descendants, even if he is among those who would have deserved his inheritance had he not killed him. As for the parents, if they do not kill him, they inherit from him by the shares Allah, mighty and majestic, has fixed. A man inherits from his wife's blood money and a woman inherits from her husband's blood money, except for the brothers through the mother, for they inherit nothing from the blood money.

The intersexed person is one who has both a male organ like that of a man and a female organ like that of a woman. Such a one inherits and is inherited from, marries and is married, and is judged according to the passing of urine. If urine passes from the male organ, the person is a man, and the rulings of men apply: he marries women and men do not marry him, and he inherits and is inherited from according to the inheritance of men. If urine passes from the female organ, the person's ruling is the ruling of women, and she follows the course of women and does not follow the course of men in anything. If urine passes from both, one looks to which of the two it passes from first: if it precedes from the male organ, the ruling is the ruling of men; and if it precedes from the female organ, the ruling is the ruling of women. If it passes from both at once, or there is neither a male organ nor a female organ, then the matter is uncertain, and the judge casts lots over it. He writes on one card "the servant of Allah" and on another card "the handmaid of Allah," and he sets each card within a pellet of clay, and turns them about in his hand, and says, "O Allah, You know best this naming and how You created it; so bring forth the lot."

When a matter of inheritance cannot be settled by sight, recourse is had to the lot. The one who casts it takes the piece that lies in his hand and says: "O Allah, You know best about this person and how You created them, so bring out the arrow for the one You have made heir among them and the one You have appointed as ruler over the rulings." He then draws one of the two pieces of clay, neither of which has been seen, nor is it known what is enclosed within them; and whatever emerges determines the ruling that is applied. After this the urine is examined. The person is made to stand facing a wall, with the hands extended, and is told to stand back from the wall as far as can be managed. If the urine passes through to the wall, it is known to have issued from the male organ; if it runs down between the legs, it is known to have issued from the female organ. Neither men nor women look upon the person beforehand, so that no judgment is formed as to what the person is in advance of the test.

If a company of people drown together in a ship, or a house collapses upon them, or they are consumed together in a fire, or they are slain together in battle, and it is not known which of them died before his companion, and they were kin to one another, then each of them inherits from the other according to what he would have inherited, and the other in turn inherits from him according to his own portion. They inherit from one another whatever each has left behind. An illustration of this is two brothers, one the freed client (mawla) of one people and the other the freed client of another, who die together in such a manner, having no heir besides their respective patrons. One of them leaves a thousand dinars, the other a hundred. The owner of the hundred inherits from the thousand as though he had remained alive and his brother had died before him, taking his share from it; and the owner of the thousand inherits the hundred as though he had remained alive and his brother had died before him, taking his share from it. Thus there falls to the patron of the one who left the thousand a hundred, and to the patron of the one who left the hundred a thousand. In this manner the like of it proceeds: each patron inherits his deceased client, and is himself inherited from in turn, and thereafter the heirs of each inherit from him.

As for one who pronounces upon his wife an irrevocable divorce, after which he has no power of return over her, there is no inheritance between the two of them. The exception is the man who divorces her while he is ill: she inherits from him for as long as he remains in that same illness, unless he recovers from it or she herself remarries. If he becomes well again, or she takes another husband, she has no inheritance from him. As for one who divorces his wife with a divorce in which he retains the right of return, he inherits from her and she inherits from him, so long as her waiting period has not yet expired.

Whoever dies leaving no heir except the patrons (mawali) who freed him, or those who became patrons through their agency, his inheritance belongs to those patrons. But if he has a blood relative, his inheritance passes to that relative by right of kinship and not by right of patronage. The bond of patronage (al-wala') is inherited by men and women alike, and through the father who freed the slave its closeness is established, as is the case with kinship among men generally. Whoever is nearer in the degree of patronage has the better claim to it than one who is more remote, just as we mentioned in the chapter on patronage concerning the elder, which is to say the man who frees a slave. The freed man then dies leaving two sons; one of the two sons dies leaving a son of his own; and thereafter the patron dies with no near relative. His inheritance goes to the son of his patron's son, who has preceded the other by a degree, rather than to the son who is more remote. So too is it with inheritance by kinship. And whoever leaves no heir in the degree of an inheritor except a slave whom he had freed is inherited by that freed slave just as the freed slave would have been inherited by him. But if he leaves both a slave whom he had freed and a patron who had freed him, his inheritance goes to the patron who freed him rather than to the slave whom he freed.

If some of the heirs acknowledge another heir who is otherwise unknown, and for whom there stands no attested proof, and the remaining heirs deny him, then the one who is nearer inherits according to what he would have inherited. Where a portion of the heirs draw the claimant near, or judge him to be a relative, they hand over to him whatever remains in excess of their own shares, that is, of what has come to them; while those who do not acknowledge him owe him nothing, and their own inheritance remains complete. An illustration is a man who dies leaving two sons. One of them acknowledges a third brother, and the other denies him. The one who denies inherits one half from his father. The one who acknowledges inherits one third, and pays over one sixth to the one he has acknowledged, corresponding to what that one would have inherited had they been three brothers sharing in thirds.

The shroud of the deceased is taken from the principal of his estate, from whatever he has left, before any division is made, and likewise his debt, if a debt lies against him. A bequest, if he has made one, is taken from a third of the estate or less; beyond that it is not carried out without the consent of the heirs, unless they elect to discharge it in full. Whatever remains thereafter is divided according to the ordained obligations of inheritance. As for the mukatab, the slave bound by a written contract of manumission (kitabah), and the one part of whom has been set free, such persons inherit and are inherited from in proportion to the measure of freedom they have attained, as we have already mentioned. The ruling upon the soundness of the kitabah, and how the intention of manumission takes effect, has been set out in the chapter on the mukatab. Whoever has no known heir, his inheritance goes to the treasury of the Muslims.

If some of the heirs acknowledge a debt, each who acknowledges it is bound to pay it from his share of the inheritance, in proportion to what he has acknowledged. And if he bears witness to it, being a man of just standing, his testimony is admitted on behalf of all the heirs. And if a man acknowledges a child from his slave-woman and his wife, and then afterward disowns him, his disavowal is not permitted: the child is his son. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the blessings of Allah be upon them all.
