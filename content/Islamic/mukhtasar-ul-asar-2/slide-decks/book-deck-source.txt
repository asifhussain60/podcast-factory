# Mukhtasar al-Athar, Volume 2: The Law of the Market, the Table, and the Household

This deck holds the strongest visual structures of the whole book, one section per chapter, in book order. Each structure is rendered as STRUCTURE rather than prose, so that a slide generator draws a diagram rather than a bullet list. The audio episodes carry the reasoning and the narratives; these structures capture only what the eye must hold all at once.

## The Marketplace: Honest Earning, Trade, and Property

### What may be sold and what may not
Diagram type: Hierarchy tree.

- Goods brought to market
  - Forbidden absolutely (no sale, no earning, no taking of the price)
    - carrion, blood, swine
    - wine and every intoxicant
    - the instruments of idle amusement
    - the free person
  - Lawful in itself, though the buyer may later corrupt it
    - grapes, pressed juice, raisins, honey, dates, figs
    - the sin falls only on the one who turns them into wine
  - Ownership decides the ruling
    - water, pasture, and fire lying unowned in the wild: no sale
    - the same once dug, stored, gathered, or kindled within property: sale permitted
  - Disliked yet permitted
    - the price of the biting dog and of the cat: disliked
    - the hunting dog, the herding dog, and copies of the Qur'an: permitted

### The forms of gharar, the sale upon the unknown
Diagram type: Annotated structure.

Whole: gharar is every sale concluded upon a thing that is unknown.

Parts dissected, each with its one-line flaw:
- habal al-habalah: the offspring of an offspring, sold before either is born.
- al-madamin and al-malaqih: what is in the loins of the stallions and the embryos in the wombs.
- al-mulamasah: the folded garment sold by a touch, with sight waived.
- al-munabadhah: the sale sealed by casting the garment from hand to hand.
- the pebble sale: each party throws a pebble, and the throw is made the binding act.
- wool on the back, fish still in the water: sold before the shearing or the catch.

Callout: the binding act of a sale is the spoken word and the parting in mutual consent, never a touch and never a throw.

### The riba rule for measured and weighed goods
Diagram type: Comparison matrix.

| Exchange | Hand to hand | On deferred terms |
|---|---|---|
| Same kind for its own kind (gold for gold, wheat for wheat, dates for dates) | Equal measure or equal weight only; any increase is riba | Not permitted at all |
| Different kinds (wheat for dates, gold for silver) | Disparity permitted: a sa' of wheat for two sa' of dates | Not permitted |

> Gold for gold, and silver for silver, hand to hand and weight for weight; whoever increases or seeks increase has dealt in riba.
> Attributed to the Messenger of Allah, narrated through the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt.

### Two structures of joint venture: shirka and mudaraba
Diagram type: Contrast pair.

Column A: Shirka, the partnership of mingled capital
- Capital: both partners contribute and mingle it into one indistinguishable fund.
- Profit: divided as the two stipulate.
- Loss: borne in proportion to each one's capital.
- Action: neither buys nor sells without the other's leave.

Column B: Mudaraba, money from one and labor from the other
- Capital: belongs to one party alone; the worker is the mudarib.
- Profit: divided by the contract.
- Loss: falls on the owner of the money alone, never on the worker.
- Breach: if the worker violates the terms, he guarantees whatever is lost.

Shared row: a fixed guaranteed return turns either arrangement into a loan, and a fixed proportional return demanded as profit becomes riba.

## Binding Words: Oaths, Vows, and Their Atonement

### The expiation of a broken oath
Diagram type: Process flow with a fallback branch.

Start: an oath deliberately sworn, then broken.
  Choose any ONE of the three, the choice being free:
  - feed ten poor persons, from the average of what you feed your own family
  - clothe ten poor persons, two garments to each
  - free a slave
  Branch, only when none of the three can be found or afforded:
  - fast three consecutive days
End: the fast never substitutes until food, clothing, and manumission are all beyond reach.

> Of everything in the Qur'an stated with "or," the one to whom it applies has the choice; but wherever it says "if he does not find," he must perform the first whenever he is able.
> Ja'far ibn Muhammad, peace be upon him.

### The binding oath and the empty word
Diagram type: Contrast pair.

Column A: the deliberate oath, al-yamin al-mun'aqida
- The heart is set upon it.
- Breaking it makes expiation obligatory.
- "the oaths your hearts have deliberately bound."

Column B: the thoughtless word, al-laghw
- "No, by Allah" and "Yes, by Allah" let fall in passing, the heart not upon it.
- No expiation: it is a lie to seek forgiveness for, not an oath to expiate.
- "Allah will not take you to task for the thoughtless word in your oaths."

Shared row: the oath is only by Allah; there is no oath by divorce, by manumission, by Hajj, or by anything other than Allah.

### What is sworn, and whether it binds
Diagram type: Hierarchy tree.

- An oath upon an act
  - to do an obligatory act, or to leave a forbidden one: binding, it must be kept
  - to do a permitted act, then the contrary proves better: do the better and expiate
  - in disobedience, as to slaughter one's son or sever kin: counts for nothing, no perjury and no expiation
- A vow, al-nadhr
  - named, tied to a condition that then comes to pass: binding
  - unnamed, merely "I owe a vow": nothing is required
  - in disobedience or in the severing of kinship: void

## The Body's Portion: Food, Drink, and Healing

### The lawful and the forbidden among the animals
Diagram type: Hierarchy tree.

- Land beasts
  - lawful: camels, cattle, sheep, the grazing wild animals, with their milk
  - forbidden: the beasts of prey with fangs that seize and devour
- Birds
  - lawful: all birds when slaughtered
  - forbidden: the birds of prey with talons that seize, and their eggs
- Creatures of the sea
  - lawful: what is caught alive and bears scales
  - forbidden: what dies in the water, and what has no scales

Callout, the test of the unknown egg: two unlike ends, one pointed and one round, then it is eaten; two like ends, then it is not.

### The single drink that crosses the line into wine
Diagram type: Process flow.

Start: juice freshly pressed from grapes, dates, or raisins, unchanged: lawful.
  Cooked to a body, al-tila', thickened but not turned: lawful, pure or mixed with water.
  Soaked as nabidh, dates or raisins cast into water, drunk the first day and the second: lawful.
  By the third day, if it has changed: poured away.
  Branch into the forbidden: left to ferment, so it boils, intensifies, and throws up froth: it is now wine.
End: what intoxicates in much is forbidden even in little.

### The Prophetic pharmacy: healing by substance and by act
Diagram type: Annotated structure.

Whole: the remedy that the Imams transmitted, which profits only the one who receives it with belief and certainty.

Branch one, healing by substance:
- honey, "in it is a healing for people"
- the black seed, al-shuniz, a cure for every disease except death
- senna and fenugreek; cupping, phlebotomy; salt pressed on a sting; fresh dates for the woman in childbirth

Branch two, healing by act and word:
- supplication, which turns back the decree even after it is sealed
- charity given early in the day, which wards off the day's affliction
- ruqya by al-Fatiha and the two chapters of refuge
- the name of Allah pronounced over food and over the place of pain

## What We Wear: Dress, Adornment, and Fragrance

### Two eras, one principle: the plainness of Ali and the fine dress of al-Sadiq
Diagram type: Contrast pair.

Column A: Ali, peace be upon him
- Wore coarse cloth and rough fabric.
- Lived in a time of hardship and scarcity.
- "Wear cotton, for that was the clothing of the Messenger of Allah."

Column B: Ja'far al-Sadiq, peace be upon him
- Wore the fine al-Quhi, al-Muwri, and khazz.
- "Allah has widened our means, and the trace of His bounty should be seen upon His servant."
- Recited, "Who has forbidden the adornment of Allah which He has brought forth for His servants?"

Shared row: clothing fine or plain is permitted; plain dress worn in humility is no fault, and fine dress within one's means is a showing of the grace of Allah.

### What is lawful, disliked, and forbidden in dress and ornament
Diagram type: Hierarchy tree.

- Garments
  - lawful: whatever the earth grows; the skin, wool, hair, and fur of a lawfully slaughtered animal; white, the best and the cloth of shrouds; dyed cloth, for no dye forbids a garment
  - disliked: pure silk for men, though permitted woven with other fibers and displayed before the enemy; the deep saturated red, "the dye of the Banu Umayyah"; black as adornment, the garb the Banu al-Abbas took
- Ornament
  - lawful: gold and silver for women; the silver seal-ring; the carnelian and the ruby; the ring upon the right hand
  - forbidden: the gold seal-ring for men; the ring of iron, "the ornament of the people of the Fire"

### The fragrance of men and the fragrance of women
Diagram type: Contrast pair.

Column A: the fragrance of men
- Scent apparent, color hidden, such as musk.
- Worn openly, even to the Friday prayer.

Column B: the fragrance of women
- Color apparent, scent concealed, such as khaluq and henna.
- Kept within the home; disliked when she goes out so that it can be detected from her.

Shared row: fragrance was given to the prophets together with marriage and the siwak, and a sweet scent strengthens the heart.

## The Hunt and the Knife: Game, Slaughter, and Sacrifice

### The clean kill and the lost quarry: al-isma' and al-inma'
Diagram type: Contrast pair.

Column A: al-isma', struck dead on the spot
- Thrown at, struck, and it dies where it falls.
- Known with certainty to have died of the blow.
- Lawful to eat.

Column B: al-inma', driven off and lost
- Struck, then it moves out of sight and is later found dead.
- Unknown whether the blow or some other cause killed it.
- Not eaten.

Shared row: only an iron weapon, the sword, the spear, the arrow, makes the kill lawful, never the stick, the stone, or the pellet.

### The trained hunting animal, al-jawarih
Diagram type: Process flow.

Start: a trained dog or bird of prey is released, the name of Allah invoked at its release.
  It seizes the prey.
  Branch:
  - the prey is reached alive: slaughter it, then eat
  - the prey dies before slaughter, the animal being trained and the name invoked: eaten, even if the animal ate from it
  - the animal is untrained, or the name was deliberately omitted, or it killed without wounding: not eaten
End: the jawarih are the earners, who earn the catch for the one who trained them.

### The Sunnah of slaughter: the cut and its conditions
Diagram type: Annotated structure.

Whole: lawful slaughter at the place of slaughter, below the ghalsama, the knot of the windpipe.

Parts, what the blade severs: the windpipe, the gullet, and the two jugular veins, falling short of none, not cutting into the bone.

Conditions called out around the cut:
- the blade sharpened, the beast given ease, faced toward the qibla, the name invoked
- iron only, never stone, tooth, nail, or bone
- do not sever the head through, do not skin until the beast is still
- the name forgotten: invoke it when remembered and eat; the name deliberately left: it is not eaten

Sign of valid slaughter: the eye flickers, the leg kicks, or the tail moves.

### The ranking of the sacrificial animals
Diagram type: Hierarchy tree.

- The order of preference, best first, as al-Sadiq set it
  - female camels → male camels
  - female cattle → male cattle
  - rams → wethers → ewes
  - male goats → female goats

Callout: the best of every kind is the fattest and the largest; the most prized ram is the horned black-and-white, al-amlah; nothing is slaughtered as a sacrifice before the Eid prayer.

## Marriage and the Household

### The order of preference in a spouse, by faith
Diagram type: Hierarchy tree.

- Most preferred, descending to least
  - the people of knowledge and sound faith, foremost and nearest to Allah
  - the weak who are neither loyal nor hostile
  - the Muslims of the people of truce, who marry, inherit, and testify among themselves
  - the people of disbelief: a Muslim man may marry their women only in necessity, and a Muslim woman is never lawful to them

Callout on direction: marry INTO the people of doubt, but do not give your women to them, for a woman takes on her husband's manners; and never a Nasibiyya, the daughter of one hostile to the family of the Prophet.

### The four feasts of the household
Diagram type: Annotated structure.

Whole: the feast prepared at the turning points of a household.

Parts:
- al-walima: the food when a man enters upon his wife.
- al-khurs: the aqiqa food for the newborn on the seventh day.
- al-i'dhar: the food at the circumcision of a boy.
- al-wakira: the food on returning from a journey.

Callout: the first day's feast is a duty, the second a kindness known among people, and what is beyond that is ostentation.

### The dowry: the Sunnah, the floor, and the ceiling
Diagram type: Comparison matrix.

| Kind of dowry | The amount | When it falls due |
|---|---|---|
| The dowry of the Sunnah | five hundred dirhams, the Prophet's own practice | the measure against which excess is returned |
| A named dowry | whatever the two agree, little or much | the immediate portion before consummation, the deferred to death or separation |
| An unnamed dowry | the contract is valid; she gets the like of her peers, not above the Sunnah dowry | nothing consummated until he gives her something |

> When the curtains are let down and the doors are shut, the dowry has fallen due.
> Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdullah, peace be upon them.

### Lawful marriage against the marriage of pleasure, mut'a
Diagram type: Contrast pair.

Column A: the lawful marriage
- A guardian and two upright witnesses.
- A dowry, however small.
- Ends only by divorce, with a waiting period.
- The spouses inherit; the child belongs to the bed.

Column B: the pleasure union, rejected
- No guardian and no witnesses.
- A named sum for a named term.
- Ends of itself when the term expires, with no divorce.
- No waiting period claimed, and no inheritance.

Shared row: intimacy is permitted by two paths only, the bond of marriage and the right hand of possession; whatever lies outside the two is forbidden.

## When a Marriage Ends: Divorce and Separation

### What each parted woman is owed
Diagram type: Comparison matrix.

| The woman | Maintenance | Accommodation | Mourning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Divorced revocably, in her waiting period | yes | yes | no |
| Divorced irrevocably | no | no | no |
| Divorced while pregnant | yes | yes | no |
| Widowed while pregnant | no | no | yes |

Callout: mourning is the widow's alone; throughout her count she does not comb or dye her hair, does not perfume herself or apply kohl, does not adorn herself or wear a dyed garment unless it is black, and does not leave her house by day.

### The count of divorce and the path back to lawfulness
Diagram type: Process flow.

Start: a man divorces his wife.
  Within the waiting period: he may take her back, witnessed, with no new dowry.
  After the waiting period ends: he is one suitor among suitors, by a fresh marriage and a new dowry.
  Branch on how many pronouncements have fallen:
  - one or two: after a fresh marriage she returns to him for whatever remains of the count
  - the third: she is not lawful to him until she marries another in a marriage of genuine desire, he consummates it, and that marriage then ends
End: collusion with a hired husband, a mut'a, a boy, or a castrated man does NOT make her lawful again.

### The provision of parting, mut'a
Diagram type: Annotated structure.

Whole: the parting gift, "upon the wealthy according to his means and upon the straitened according to his means."

Parts, entitled and not:
- the woman divorced by the Sunnah, in her waiting period: entitled, whether or not the marriage was consummated
- the woman parted by khul': not entitled
- the woman parted by mubarah: not entitled

Callout: there is no fixed amount, and it is given only after the waiting period ends and hope between the two is gone.

## Freedom, Generosity, and the Division of Estates

### Three lifetime grants: al-umra, al-ruqba, al-sukna
Diagram type: Annotated structure.

Whole: grants of use offered for the sake of Allah.

Parts:
- al-umra: a house or estate granted for the lifetime of the recipient.
- al-ruqba: the same grant, returning to whichever of the two parties outlives the other.
- al-sukna: a grant of dwelling.

Callout, branching on the condition: conditioned on the grantor's life, it returns to his heirs at his death; conditioned on the recipient's life, it is his until he dies and his heirs cannot reclaim it; granted to him and his heirs, it passes to them after him.

### The obligatory gift and the voluntary gift: open or hidden
Diagram type: Contrast pair.

Column A: the obligatory, such as zakat and the fixed dues
- The merit lies in giving openly and announcing it.
- So too the obligatory prayer is best in congregation and the mosque.

Column B: the voluntary, such as sadaqa and the night prayer
- The merit lies in secrecy.
- So too the voluntary prayer is best in seclusion, and to boast of it admits ostentation.

> If you disclose your charities, that is good; but if you conceal them and give them to the poor, that is better for you.
> al-Baqarah 271.

### The order of the estate: what is taken before the heirs
Diagram type: Process flow.

Start: a person dies leaving wealth.
  First, the shroud, from the principal of the estate.
  Then the debts, from the principal.
  Then the bequest, from one third or less; beyond the third only with the heirs' consent.
  Then what remains: divided by the ordained shares.
End: there is no bequest for an heir unless the other heirs permit, and the testator may revoke or alter his bequest while he lives.

### The fixed shares, al-fara'id
Diagram type: Comparison matrix.

| Heir | With a child | Without a child |
|---|---|---|
| Husband | one quarter | one half |
| Wife | one eighth | one quarter |
| Mother | one sixth | one third, but one sixth if there are brothers |
| Father | one sixth, then the remainder | the remainder |
| A single daughter | one half, with the rest returned by kinship if she is alone | one half |
| Two or more daughters | two thirds, shared | two thirds, shared |

Callout: to the male the like of the share of two females; there is no awl, for the deficiency falls upon the one who takes the remainder, never upon the named shares.

## Wrong and Redress: Crime, Property Disputes, and the Judge

### The escalating hadd of theft
Diagram type: Process flow.

Start: theft of goods worth five dinars from a secured place, proven or freely confessed.
  First theft: the right hand is cut at the four fingers, the thumb and palm left for use.
  Second theft: the left foot is cut at the middle, the heel left to walk upon.
  Third theft: imprisonment for life.
  Theft while imprisoned: death.
  Branch, where no hand is cut: the guest, the hired man, the father from the son, the snatcher and the pickpocket, a year of famine, the part-owner, fruit and birds. The highway robber is judged apart, at the Imam's discretion.

### The prescribed punishments, the hudud
Diagram type: Comparison matrix.

| The offense | The punishment | Escalation or note |
|---|---|---|
| Zina, the protected (muhsan) | one hundred lashes, then stoning | the witnesses cast the first stones, or the judge if it was a confession |
| Zina, the unprotected | one hundred lashes | a confession must be repeated four times |
| Slander of the chaste, qadhf | eighty lashes | testimony never accepted again unless he repents |
| Wine and intoxicants | eighty lashes | death upon the third offense |

Callout: the order of severity in flogging runs from the adulterer, to the false accuser, to the drinker, to the discretionary; and where a doubt arises, the hadd is suspended.

### Blood money, the soul and its parts
Diagram type: Comparison matrix.

The full diya of a free Muslim, taken in the currency of the offender's land:

| Wealth | The full blood money |
|---|---|
| Gold | one thousand dinars |
| Silver | its equivalent value |
| Camels | one hundred |
| Cattle | two hundred |
| Sheep | one thousand |
| Cloth | two hundred garments |

The graded scale for the unborn, by stage, ascending:
seed cast after settling, 20 dinars → the clot, al-alaqa, 40 → the morsel, al-mudgha, 60 → once bone has formed, 80 → fully formed before the spirit, 100 → once the spirit is breathed in, the full blood money.

Callout: retaliation for the deliberate act where it can be taken without excess; blood money for the error, borne by the aqila when it reaches a third or more.

### Evidence and oath: who carries which
Diagram type: Contrast pair.

Column A: the claimant
- Bears the burden of evidence, al-bayyina.
- May swear with a single witness, in matters of wealth alone.

Column B: the one against whom the claim is made
- Bears the oath, al-yamin.
- May refer the oath back to the claimant.

Shared row: the judge rules by the outward, while Allah knows the hidden, so the ruling of a judge makes nothing lawful that was unlawful.

> I judge between you only by evidence and oaths; so whoever I award something out of his brother's wealth, I have only cut off for him a piece of the Fire.
> The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him and his family.
