Whoever borrows a piece of property and then mortgages it or sells it is subject to this rule: if its owner is at hand, the owner may recover it, and the buyer may seek restitution from the seller for the price he paid. Whoever pledges borrowed property is liable for an equivalent pledge, or for what that pledge was worth, unless the owner of the borrowed item had given the borrower permission to pledge or to sell it. If the property is still in his possession, no obligation falls upon him.

Whoever receives a deposit must preserve it and safeguard it as a person of comparable standing would. If it is destroyed or lost through no act of wrongdoing on his part, after he has taken proper care of it, no liability falls upon him. But if he loses it through negligence, misuse, or transgression, he is liable for it. If the owner of the deposit instructs him to deliver it to a certain man, and he claims to have done so, but that man denies receiving it and the owner of the deposit does not accept his word, then he must pay for it after all of them have sworn oaths.

Whoever holds a deposit is permitted to draw upon it if he carries a corresponding debt and can attest to that; the deposit then becomes his responsibility. If a child or an insane person entrusts a deposit and it is lost, there is no recourse against the one who held it, for he was misled through no fault of his own. If a slave receives a deposit without the permission of his master and the deposit is lost, the slave bears responsibility for it, but the claim is made against him only when he is freed, not against his master. If two men jointly entrust something to a third party, and one of them comes to claim it while the other is absent, the holder should give the one present only half, since it was deposited jointly, and he may not hand the whole of it to one without the other being present. Nor may the one present take any portion until his companion arrives, unless the absent party has died and his heirs come in his stead, in which case it may be released to them. All of this is the position of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام).

**On Found Property, the Foundling, and Related Matters**

Whoever finds lost property within the Haram of Makkah, or in any place where he believes it safe and does not fear it will be taken, should make it known and leave it where it lies until its owner comes for it. If it is not within the Haram and he fears for its safety, he should take it up if he is able, for he cannot simply abandon it, since the Messenger of Allah, (ع), forbade the wasting of wealth. Once he takes it, he should announce it. If its owner comes and can testify to it or describe it, it is returned to him. If no claimant comes forward within the period specified in the Sunnah, while he continues to announce it, he places it in the treasury of the Muslims if he finds a way to do so. Otherwise he may, if he wishes, give it in charity on the owner's behalf, on the understanding that if the owner later comes he restores it to him, or he may keep it with him if he was unable to place it in the treasury of the Muslims.

It is disliked to take up stray camels found in a safe place, because they can endure thirst and hunger until they reach their owners. But if the time grows long and he fears for the animal, he may take it and care for it until its owners come, spending on it and being reimbursed by them when they reclaim it. If the matter is prolonged and no claimant comes, it is referred to the governing authority, who sells it, keeps the proceeds, and deals with the price as one deals with found property, whether the animal is a sheep, cattle, a slave, or any other beast. No stray animal is to be left where it is found; rather one does as has been described. If a claimant comes and asks the finder for a fee, this is not obligatory upon the finder, because Allah Almighty has made it a duty to safeguard the wealth of the believers, a duty the believers owe one another. The finder earns a reward if his intention was righteous, and whatever he spent in caring for the animal is to be repaid to him.

Whoever finds perishable property that would spoil if left should sell it and deal with the proceeds as he would with found property. Whoever finds property buried in someone's home: it belongs to the people of that home if they claim it, even if it is a ruin that people pass through, in which case the procedure for found property applies. If it is found buried and appears to be from the wealth of a prior age, it is taken to the treasury of the Muslims. We have already mentioned what is obligatory for the finder in such cases.

A foundling is free if his parents are unknown. His inheritance passes to the treasury of the Muslims, and no relative inherits from him by lineage; he neither inherits nor is inherited from except through marriage. When he grows up and his circumstances improve, and the one who took him in and raised him asks to be repaid the cost of that upbringing, he is obliged to make it good. Whoever sets a reward for one who searches out a runaway slave or a stray and returns it to him is bound to pay that reward, as he stipulated it upon himself. All of this is the position of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام).

**On Division and Property Structures**

Whatever lies between dwelling walls, and whatever wealth is held between partners that admits of division and is not ruined by being divided, is to be divided among them if the partners, or some of them, request it. If it is of a kind that would be ruined by division, or that would bring harm upon them or upon one of them when divided, it is sold and the proceeds are divided. There is no objection to dividing a house so that its lower portion belongs to some of the partners and its upper portion to others. The roof of the lower level and its walls are the responsibility of the owner of the lower portion, while what lies above belongs to the owner of the upper level, and the ground beneath the upper level is the land of the upper owner. If the lower portion falls into disrepair without any fault on the part of the upper owner, the owner below is responsible for its repair, as he is for the lower walls upon which the upper level rests. Whoever causes damage to it is bound to repair what he has damaged. Everything belonging to the lower level must be repaired by the lower owner, because it is his property, even though the upper level is borne upon it and rests upon it, and on that basis the house is valued at the time of division.

When a wall stands between two men and it falls, both are obliged to rebuild it. If one of them rebuilds it, the other may not benefit from it until he pays half the cost. If the wall belongs to one of them and it falls, he is obliged to rebuild it; and if he leaves it to the other, who then rebuilds it, the one who abandoned it retains no right in it. No one may open in his wall an aperture through which he can look upon his neighbour's private quarters. But if he opens it for light and air, and it does not give him a view into his neighbour's private quarters, he is entitled to do so, and his neighbour is entitled to build a wall within his own house to block it if he wishes. No one may be prevented from building within his own property in a way that screens his neighbours view, so long as it is within his right. Whoever opens a door onto a thoroughfare, or digs a well within the bounds of his own wall, is not to be prevented from doing so if it is upon a passable, through-running public street. If it is upon a dead-end lane, he may not do so except with the permission of the people whose path runs through it. If it is at the very end of the lane, where none of them has a path past it, he may do as he wishes in the portion adjoining him in which no one else has a path. No one should divert a public, through-running road from its place; but if the road belongs to him and stands at his own disposal, and he diverts it, that is his to do. All of this is the position of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام).

**On Testimony**

Allah Almighty said, "Establish the testimony for the sake of Allah" (Al-Talaq: 2), and "Call to witness two witnesses from among your men; and if there are not two men, then one man and two women from among those whom you approve as witnesses" (Al-Baqarah: 282), and "Let two just men from among you bear witness" (Al-Talaq: 2). And He said, "Do not conceal testimony; whoever conceals it, his heart is sinful" (Al-Baqarah: 283). And He said, "That is nearer to their bearing witness in its proper form" (Al-Ma'idah: 108). And He said, "And shun the word of falsehood" (Al-Hajj: 30).

The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said: whoever conceals a knowledge he possesses will come on the Day of Resurrection bridled with a bridle of fire. He, (ع), also said that the false witness will be raised on the Day of Resurrection lapping at his tongue in the Fire as a dog laps its tongue in a vessel. And he said: the false witness is among the reviled. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: let the witness deliver what he was asked to witness, and let him fear Allah his Lord; for it is among the forms of false witness that a man should bear witness to what he does not know and deny what he does know. Whoever bears false witness, and this is known of him, is given a painful flogging, is paraded about, and his deed is made public, and he is liable for whatever was lost through his testimony.

The testimony of the just believer is admissible for everyone who is testified for on his behalf, whether near of kin or distant. The testimony of a just slave is admissible, except in favour of his masters, or in a matter on account of which he would be set free; but if he gives testimony after he has been freed from the bond by which he was held, his testimony in that matter is admissible. The testimony of boys in a case of bloodshed stands as a presumption with which the qasama oath may be administered, but their testimony is not admissible in anything other than that until they reach maturity. The testimony of two suspect persons is not admissible, nor that of one under accusation, nor that of a child of adultery, nor that of a partner against his partner in a matter that lies between the two of them; though such a partner's testimony is admissible in a matter outside their shared dealings, if he is just. A follower, a hired man, or a servant, if he is just, may testify on behalf of the one he serves, works for, or follows, except in a matter that would draw a benefit to himself, or in which he is suspect. Nor may a neighbour testify in his own favour, nor is the testimony of an adversary against his opponent admissible, nor is the testimony of the people of deviant inclinations admissible against the believers. If a slanderer repents, his testimony becomes admissible. If a witness's testimony has been established and the one testified against then challenges him, bringing forward just witnesses who discredit him on grounds of something objectionable, or of suspicion, or of conduct unbecoming of just people, his testimony is set aside. Whoever prays in the congregational mosque and shows forth good, and nothing evil is known of him, his testimony is admissible, unless his discrediting is established against him.

The testimony of a Muslim is admissible against the people of shirk, but the testimony of a polytheist is not admissible against Muslims, except in the matter of a bequest made during travel when no one else is available, provided he swears an oath upon it. The testimony of non-Muslims against one another is admissible if it is sound within their religion and nothing discrediting is known of them. If a person gives testimony while in a state of disbelief, or while still a minor, and then the disbeliever submits to Islam and the minor comes of age, their testimony is admissible if they are just at the time they deliver it, unless they had already given that testimony earlier and it was rejected, in which case it must be given anew.

The testimony of women is admissible in marriage and in matters of wealth, but it is not admissible in divorce, nor in the prescribed punishments, nor in what lies between them. If three men and two women testify to adultery, stoning becomes obligatory; but if there are two men and four women, their testimony to adultery is not admissible. The testimony of women is admissible in the matter of istihlal, and the testimony of the midwife alone is accepted if she is just and there is no other woman present. Istihlal is the cry of the newborn; if it cries and then dies, it is thereby known that it was born alive. The testimony of women is not admissible regarding the crescent of Ramadan, so that fasting should begin or end upon it, but their testimony is admissible in matters of the iddah, of the postpartum period, and of those things concerning women upon which men are not permitted to look.

No testimony may be given upon another's testimony in a matter of a prescribed punishment, of killing, or of retaliation. Testimony upon the testimony of a single man is not admissible except by way of two men, and only when the original witness is absent in a place to which the prayer is shortened and the fasting traveller breaks his fast, or is sick or infirm and unable to attend the session of the judge, or has died after entrusting his testimony to others. If he is present and sound, no one may testify in his stead, but he must testify in person. One does not bear witness except upon his own knowledge.

If the witnesses differ over a divorce, one of them saying that he divorced her for the iddah and the other saying otherwise, their testimony is not admissible. If they differ over the dowry, one testifying to the lesser amount and the other to the greater, the woman may, if she wishes, swear an oath alongside the witness who testified for the greater amount and take it, or take the lesser amount, if she prefers, without an oath, unless her adversary in the matter wishes to swear against her. A man may swear an oath together with his two witnesses, just as such an oath is admissible on behalf of one for whom two witnesses have testified, for rights are sometimes forfeited in ways unknown to the witnesses and to the one testified against. No one bears witness upon a written document unless the witness can establish his own testimony to it and knows it. Allah, the most truthful of speakers, said: "Except for those who bear witness to the truth while they know" (Al-Zukhruf: 86).

If the witnesses give their testimony and then ask to withdraw it before the judgment is issued, they are released; but if they ask to withdraw it after the judgment, they are not released. If they retract before the judgment, their retraction is accepted and no ruling is made upon their testimony. If they retract after the judgment, they are liable for whatever was lost through their testimony. Whoever is summoned to give his testimony should not refuse, for Allah, exalted be He who said it, declared: "And let not the witnesses refuse when they are called" (Al-Baqarah: 282). Whoever has witnessed something and is called upon to deliver his testimony must deliver it, in keeping with the words of Allah Almighty: "Do not conceal testimony; whoever conceals it, his heart is sinful" (Al-Baqarah: 283). But whoever overhears a people settling their accounts, or hears one man acknowledge a debt to another without himself having been asked to witness it, then if the one who made the acknowledgement asks him to deliver testimony to what he heard, he is given the choice: if he wishes, he may bear witness to what he heard.

When a man is asked to give what he has heard, he must testify truthfully; and if he chooses not to testify, this is no fault in him, for he was not formally called to bear witness. Testimony in matters of death, lineage, ancestral standing, and long-established possession is admissible on the basis of hearing and report, and through what has become widely known among the people. By such report people come to know one another. They testify to the death of one who dies, and to a man's attribution to his father, though most of them never witnessed these things with their own eyes and never heard the acknowledgment firsthand; in such matters the reported acknowledgment is accepted. All of this is the position of the Ahl al-Bayt, may Allah's blessings be upon them.

### The Case and the Evidence

Allah, exalted is He, said: "And do not consume one another's wealth unjustly, nor proffer it to the judges that you may sinfully consume a portion of the people's wealth while you know" ﴿وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا أَمْوَالَكُم بَيْنَكُم بِالْبَاطِلِ وَتُدْلُوا بِهَا إِلَى الْحُكَّامِ لِتَأْكُلُوا فَرِيقًا مِّنْ أَمْوَالِ النَّاسِ بِالْإِثْمِ وَأَنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ﴾ (Al-Baqarah: 188). And He said: "Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to their rightful owners, and when you judge between people to judge with justice" ﴿إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تُؤَدُّوا الْأَمَانَاتِ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهَا وَإِذَا حَكَمْتُم بَيْنَ النَّاسِ أَن تَحْكُمُوا بِالْعَدْلِ﴾ (Al-Nisa: 58).

The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: "I judge between you only by evidence and oaths. Some of you may be more persuasive in his plea than others; so whoever I award something out of his brother's wealth, I have only cut off for him a piece of the Fire." And he forbade, (ع) and his family, the seizing of Muslims wealth by a false oath. The ruling of a judge does not make lawful what is otherwise unlawful for the one in whose favor it is given. Whoever is awarded what is not rightfully his may not lawfully take it, for the judge rules by what is outwardly apparent, while Allah knows the unseen and the hidden.

The burden of evidence in claims over wealth lies upon the claimant, and the oath lies upon the one against whom the claim is made. The oath is taken by Allah, mighty and majestic, and by none other. The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: "Whoever swears, let him swear by Allah; and whoever has an oath sworn to him by Allah, let him accept it; and whoever does not do so is not a Muslim."

If the claimant presses his claim and the defendant denies it, the judge asks the defendant to swear. Should the defendant refer the oath back to the claimant, saying, "Let him swear to what he claims against me, and I will give it to him," that is his right; for had the claimant wished to swear his oath alongside his evidence, that too would have been his right. If the claimant then swears, judgment is given in his favor on the strength of his claim together with his oath. But if he declines to swear, and the defendant is relieved of swearing, and rather than receiving what was sworn to him nothing further is taken, then nothing more lies against the defendant than this.

When the evidence has been established, the defendant has the right to ask the claimant to swear that his right over him remains exactly as the evidence testified, and that nothing of it, little or much, has lapsed. This is his right, because rights, as we have said, may lapse in ways the witnesses do not know of. If the defendant has nothing by which to be charged unless the claimant swears, and the claimant does not ask for that oath, the judge who knows of this right should make it known to him, for most people do not know what they are entitled to. Once it is made known, if he asks, the oath is administered for him; if he does not ask, the matter is left as it stands. If the testimony concerns a minor or an absent party, the judge does not rule for the claimant until the claimant swears alongside the evidence. And if the claimant swears and judgment is given for him, and the defendant afterward brings upright witnesses, the judge gives him his right and pays no regard to the claimant's earlier oath.

Judgment on the testimony of a single witness together with the claimant's oath is valid in matters of wealth alone; it is not given in anything other than wealth.

When the proofs are evenly matched over a single object, and the object is in the hand of one of the two adversaries, or in the hands of both together, or in the hand of neither, and each brings witnesses, all of them upright, then the two adversaries swear. If one of them declines the oath, the object is awarded to the one who swore upon his evidence. If both swear, the matter is decided between them by lot, and whichever one's lot is drawn, the object is his. If both swear and the object is in the hands of both, the lot is likewise cast between them. If it is in the hand of a third party and both decline the oath, it is awarded to neither of them. Likewise, if it is in the hand of one of them and the other declines the oath, it is not awarded to that other, but remains with the one who holds it; and if the holder also declines the oath it still remains in his hand, and if he swears it is left with him in the same way. If the evidence of one of them testifies that the origin of the object is his, or that it was bred in his possession, while the evidence of the other testifies only that it is his without establishing its origin, then the object is awarded to the one whose evidence establishes both its origin and its increase.

Whoever willingly acknowledges that he is a slave is a slave; and if he afterward claims his freedom, or freedom is claimed for him, this is not accepted except by evidence. But if he acknowledged servitude under compulsion and then states that he is free, the burden of evidence falls upon the one who claims his servitude.

Whoever receives dinars or dirhams from a man and then returns to him saying he found some of them defective, while the giver denies this, the giver must swear that he gave him only sound coin and that he knows of no defect in what he gave. If the giver declines this oath, the one who received the coin swears that the defect was indeed in what was given to him, and the giver must then replace it for him.

When a man and a woman dispute, or their heirs, or one of them disputes with the heirs of the other, over the furnishings of the household, then whoever among them has evidence for what he claims is awarded it. If neither has evidence, they swear against each other; whoever declines the oath is ruled against on his opponent's claim in what was claimed. If both decline, or both swear, and neither has evidence, then the man receives what is customarily recognized as belonging to men, and the woman receives what is customarily recognized as belonging to women.

When two men, or a group, lie with one woman in a single term, whether they are free men, or slaves, or free polytheists, and she conceives, and each of them claims the child, they cast lots over it; whoever's lot is drawn takes the child, and it is attributed to him. If the lot falls to a polytheist while the child's mother is a Muslim, the child is a Muslim, and the polytheist has no claim over the child of a Muslim woman. If the mother is herself a polytheist and the lot of the polytheist is drawn, the child follows the religion of the two of them. And if the lot falls to a Muslim or to a freed slave, the child is a free Muslim.

Whoever sends something with a man to another, and the bearer says, "I delivered it to him," while the one it was sent to denies receiving it, the word of the one claiming delivery is not accepted except by evidence. But if the bearer delivered it and the sender said to him, "Give it as charity," and the bearer says, "I have already given it as charity," then his word is accepted together with his oath, if he is suspected. All of this is the position of the Ahl al-Bayt, may (عليهم السلام).

### The Etiquette of Judges

Allah, mighty and majestic, said: "O David, We have made you a vicegerent upon the earth, so judge between the people in truth" ﴿يَا دَاوُودُ إِنَّا جَعَلْنَاكَ خَلِيفَةً فِي الْأَرْضِ فَاحْكُم بَيْنَ النَّاسِ بِالْحَقِّ﴾ (Sad: 26). And He said: "And judge between them by what Allah has revealed" ﴿وَأَنِ احْكُم بَيْنَهُم بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ﴾ (Al-Ma'idah: 49). And He said: "And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed, those are the disbelievers" ﴿وَمَن لَّمْ يَحْكُم بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْكَافِرُونَ﴾ (Al-Ma'idah: 44). And He said: "And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed, those are the wrongdoers" ﴿وَمَن لَّمْ يَحْكُم بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الظَّالِمُونَ﴾ (Al-Ma'idah: 45). And He said: "And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed, those are the defiantly disobedient" ﴿وَمَن لَّمْ يَحْكُم بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْفَاسِقُونَ﴾ (Al-Ma'idah: 47). And He said: "Have you not seen those who claim to believe in what was revealed to you and what was revealed before you, yet wish to take their disputes to the Taghut, though they were commanded to reject it; and Satan wishes to lead them far astray" ﴿أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى الَّذِينَ يَزْعُمُونَ أَنَّهُمْ آمَنُوا بِمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ وَمَا أُنزِلَ مِن قَبْلِكَ يُرِيدُونَ أَن يَتَحَاكَمُوا إِلَى الطَّاغُوتِ وَقَدْ أُمِرُوا أَن يَكْفُرُوا بِهِ وَيُرِيدُ الشَّيْطَانُ أَن يُضِلَّهُمْ ضَلَالًا بَعِيدًا﴾ (Al-Nisa: 60).

The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: "Do not ask for command, for whoever asks for it is left to it, and whoever is given it without asking is helped in it." He forbade anyone from putting himself forward for the office of judge. Yet for one whom a just imam has summoned to it, and who is competent in it, the proper course is not to hold back from it. It is not permitted to him to judge for a tyrannical imam, nor to judge while he has no knowledge of the science of judgment.

The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: "Whoever gives a legal ruling without knowledge is cursed by the angels of heaven and the angels of the earth; and whoever judges and errs will come on the Day of Resurrection with his hands shackled to his neck." He forbade, (ع) and his family, that one who has no knowledge be appointed to judge. And Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, wrote to Rifa'a: "Do not employ anyone whose word concerning us you cannot trust; otherwise Allah will be your adversary and will demand from you." It was said to him concerning Mu'awiyah, "Were you to employ him, then once he has affirmed your authority you could dismiss him." He replied: "Where then is the word of Allah, mighty and majestic: And I would not take the misleaders as helpers ﴿وَمَا كُنتُ مُتَّخِذَ الْمُضِلِّينَ عَضُدًا﴾ (Al-Kahf: 51)?"

He, (ع), delivered a sermon and said within it: "As for what follows: my pledge is held in surety, and I stand answerable for it. For one who takes warning, godliness keeps watch, so that no people's crop of piety is left to wither, and no rooted stock that rests upon piety is left to thirst. Truth and good belong to the one who knows his own measure, and it is ignorance enough in a man that he does not know his own measure. Among the most hateful of creatures to Allah, mighty and majestic, are two men. One is a man whom Allah has abandoned to himself, who has swerved from the straight path, enamored of an innovation, given over to fasting and prayer within it, so that he is a trial for whoever is dazzled by his worship; he has strayed from the guidance of those before him and leads astray those who follow him after him, bearing the burdens of the sins of others whom he led astray by his error. And another, a man who has heaped up ignorance and moves among the rabble of the people, plunging into the murk of discord, whom the ignorant have named a scholar though he has not passed even a single sound day in knowledge. He hoards up much of that whose little is better than its much, until he has drunk his fill from stagnant water and amassed what is of no worth. He sits among the people as a judge, taking it upon himself to resolve what has confounded others; if he differs from a judge before him, he gives no security in his own ruling, and if one of the difficult cases comes before him, he stuffs together for it some padding of his own opinion and then cuts it short. So he is upon the confusion of doubts as upon a spider's web, not knowing whether he has hit the mark or missed it. If he is right, he fears that he has erred; and if he errs, he hopes that he was right. He does not reckon with knowledge in anything he has rejected, and he does not see that beyond what he has reached there is any further doctrine. If he measures one thing by another, he does not call his own view false; and if a matter darkens over him, he conceals it, knowing his own ignorance, lest it be said that he does not know; then he sits and gives his judgment. He is a key to obscurities, a rider of doubts, one who flounders through ignorance. He does not excuse himself from what he does not know, that he might be safe; nor does he bite down with a firm tooth in knowledge, that he might gain. He scatters narrations as the wind scatters dry chaff. The inheritances weep because of him, and the bloods cry out because of him, and through his judgment what is lawful in marriage is made forbidden. By Allah, he is not competent to issue what comes before him, nor is he fit for what has been entrusted to him. O people, look closely at the flaw of these veins of injustice, and hold fast to the obedience of one whose ignorance you cannot excuse; for the knowledge with which Adam, (ع), came down, and all that with which the prophets, (عليهم السلام), were favored, is found in the Seal of the Prophets, Muhammad, (ع) and his family, and in his pure progeny. If you are led astray, then where are you going?"

He, (ع), said: "When the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, sent me to Yemen, I said: O Messenger of Allah, you send me to judge between them while I am a young man and there are men of years among them, and I have no knowledge of judgment. He struck me upon my chest and said: O Allah, guide his heart and make his tongue firm. By the One who splits the seed and creates the soul, never after that did I doubt in judging between two parties."

He, (ع), said: "Discords arise only from passions that are followed and rulings that are newly invented, in which the judgment of Allah is opposed and men give their loyalty to other men. Were truth pure and unmixed, there would be no disagreement; and were falsehood pure and unmixed, it would not be hidden from those of discernment. But a handful is taken from this and a handful from that, and the two are mingled together; and there it is that Satan masters his followers, while those for whom the best reward has already been decreed are saved."

He, (ع), said: "The Muslims should not be placed under one who is greedy, so that he covets their wealth; nor under the ignorant, who would destroy them by his ignorance; nor under the miser, who would withhold their rights from them; nor under the harsh man, who by his harshness would drive them to harshness; nor under one who betrays his trust over the public funds, taking from one people and giving to another; nor under one who takes bribes in giving judgment, so that the rights of the people are lost; nor under one who suspends the Sunnah, for he would ruin the community."

The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, said: "Whoever acts wrongfully as a judge, whether deliberately or in error, is in the Fire." And Ali, (ع), wrote to Rifa'a, his judge over Ahvaz: "Know that this office of command is a trust; so whoever turns it into a betrayal, upon him is the curse of Allah until the Day of Resurrection."

The Messenger of Allah, (ع) and his family, forbade the judge from favoring one of the two parties by giving him more of his attention and the presence of his mind, and he forbade the judge from speaking before he has heard the statements of both adversaries.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: "Whoever among you knows anything of our rulings, make him a judge among you, for I have made him a judge; so bring your disputes before him." And he said, (ع): "Whoever consumes unlawful gain consumes the bribe given in judgment."

The judge should deal equitably between the two adversaries in his ruling, treating them alike in his speech, his attention, and his regard, and he should not speak until he has heard the statements of both. He should not give judgment in the mosque, nor carry out a prescribed punishment within it, though there is no harm in his administering correction there to one who deserves it. He should possess dignity, deliberateness, and care in examining the condition of those whom he relies upon and admits near him. He should restrain himself from mixing with the common traders and from personally engaging in buying and selling, entrusting that instead to one in whose faith and trustworthiness he has confidence, and warning him against any breach of it.

As for the one entrusted to judge between the Muslims, he keeps himself apart from mingling with the market folk and from engaging personally in buying and selling. He delegates such matters to those he trusts in their religion and honesty, and he warns them against underestimating or neglecting their duties.

He rules according to the Book of Allah the Almighty. What he does not find there he seeks in what is authentically established from the Messenger of Allah ((ع) and his family) and from the Imams of his lineage ((عليهم السلام)). If he does not know the ruling, he refers the matter to the Imam of his time and asks him. He does not rule by his own opinion, nor by his own desire, nor by analogy, nor by approbation. He accepts no intercession, nor is the intercessor to be received with favor by the one on whose behalf he intercedes, so that the judge would then incline toward him. He treats the noble and the humble, the virtuous and the questionable, the strong and the weak as equals before him. He does not hear one party alone in the absence of the other, nor does he instruct one party in his argument, nor assist him, nor lean toward either side in anything whatsoever.

If he decides a case and it afterward becomes clear to him that his ruling has violated the right, he overturns it and rules by the truth. He does the same with cases that were brought before him earlier and upon which he had already ruled.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)) said: Among the Children of Israel there was a judge who judged them with truth. When death came upon him, his wife began to weep at his side. He said to her: Why do you weep for me, when I am passing, Allah willing, to what is good? For I know that I once judged unjustly. She asked: And what is the sign of that? He said: When I am laid in my grave, come down to me and uncover my face, and you will see the sign of it. She did so, and she saw a great worm crossing within his nostril. She was frightened, covered him again, and left him. When night came she saw him in a dream, and he said to her: Did what you saw of me alarm you? She said: Yes, I was indeed alarmed. He said: What you saw befell me only on account of you. Your brother once brought a dispute against a man, and I wished that you might be the one in the right, so that I would rule in your favor; and so it was. That affliction reached me because of my desire that the right should be yours.

Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (may the (ع)) was asked about the words of Allah the Almighty and Exalted: ﴿And do not consume your wealth among yourselves unjustly, and offer it as a bribe to the rulers so that you may consume a portion of the wealth of the people in sin, while you know﴾ (al-Baqarah 188). He said: Allah, mighty and exalted, knew that there would be in the nation rulers who act unjustly. He did not mean by this the rulers among the people of justice; rather He meant the rulers among the people of injustice. If a man holds a right against another and summons him before the rulers of the people of justice, the man refuses and instead raises the matter to the rulers of the people of injustice, then he is among those who have sought judgment from al-Taghut (الطاغوت). That is the saying of Allah the Almighty: ﴿Have you not seen those who claim to believe in what has been revealed to you and what has been revealed before you, yet they wish to take their disputes to al-Taghut﴾ (al-Nisa 60).

The people of injustice are those who oppose you in your affair. So beware of taking your disputes, one against another, to the people of injustice. Rather, look to a man among you who knows something of our rulings, and appoint him among you, for I have made him a judge. Bring your disputes to him. As for whatever was decreed against the household of the wrongdoers by those who gave rulings against them in the days of their power, leave it and do not pursue it.

The judge does not rule in a way that lets one party profit from the arguments of the party convicted against him. It is permissible for him to accept what comes to him from the public treasury, but it is not permissible for him to take anything for himself in the matter he is deciding between the parties. He may inspect the markets of the Muslims, their measures and their weights, and he delegates this to one he trusts. He does not imprison anyone on mere suspicion except in a matter of blood. He strikes with the durrah in cases of slander, in discretionary punishment, and in discipline; and when the prescribed punishments fall due, the flogging in them is carried out with the whip. He does not imprison a man who is in straitened circumstances over a debt. But if the debtor is found to be solvent and possesses the means to satisfy what has been ruled against him, and that wealth is in his hands, the judge orders him to hand it over; if he refuses, he is beaten until he discharges what has been adjudged against him. If the debtor is absent, he is detained until he produces what is owed, and the judgment is then carried out against him. If, after his return, he persists in withholding it, his property is sold on his behalf and his debt is settled from it.

The judge does not accept a written ruling sent to him from another judge in a matter of a prescribed punishment, nor in divorce, nor does he accept testimony upon testimony, nor does he take a guarantor in a matter of a prescribed punishment. He carries out the judgment of a just judge, but he does not accept a judge's letter unless someone he trusts bears witness to it. Whatever stood in the judge's court from a prior judge as testimony that had not been carried into effect, if the witnesses against the defendant deny it, he has the defendant take an oath for the one in whose favor the testimony stood, should that party request it. If the defendant swears that the claim had not been discharged against him, the judge asks the claimant to bring his evidence. He accepts the appointment of an agent, and testimony in such matters is heard whether the litigant is present or absent. He rules against the absent party and holds him to presenting his argument when he appears. And he judges between the People of the Book with truth when they bring their dispute before him. All of this is the position of the Ahl al-Bayt (may (عليهم السلام)).
