# When a Marriage Ends: Divorce and Separation

Chapters of Divorce

Allah Almighty has said: ﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ إِذَا طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَطَلِّقُوهُنَّ لِعِدَّتِهِنَّ وَأَحْصُوا الْعِدَّةَ ﴾ "O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the onset of their waiting period, and count the iddah." And He, glorified and exalted, has said: ﴿ وَإِن يَتَفَرَّقَا يُغْنِ اللَّهُ كُلًّا مِّن سَعَتِهِ ﴾ "And if the two separate, Allah will enrich each of them out of His abundance."

So Allah, mighty and majestic, has permitted divorce in His Book and placed it in the hands of men rather than women. A man may divorce his wife when he wishes, whether for a cause or for no cause. Yet it has come down from Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, that he disliked divorce without cause, though he did not forbid it, nor did he invalidate the divorce of one who divorces without cause.

Umm Sa'id, his servant, related: one day I was pouring water over his hands, (ع), when he raised his head toward me and said, "O Umm Sa'id." I said, "At your service, O Commander of the Faithful." He said, "I have longed to be a bridegroom." I said, "And what prevents the Commander of the Faithful from that?" He said, "Woe to you, after four, and in al-Rahbah?" I said, "Then divorce one and bring another in her place." He said, "Woe to you, divorce is an ugly thing, and I dislike it."

Al-Hasan, (ع), was much given to divorce, and he divorced a great number of women without cause. It is said that he once passed by a group of women, who sent word to him asking, "Do you know this company of women?" He said, "No." One of them said, "There is not one among us but that you have married her and then divorced her." The most that Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, said concerning this was, "O people of Kufa, do not give your daughters in marriage to al-Hasan, for he is a man much given to divorce."

The divorce that is forbidden and the divorce that is permitted

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah, (ع) and upon the Imams of his household, that it was brought to him that Abdullah ibn Umar had divorced his wife three times while she was menstruating. He said to Umar: command him to take her back, then to divorce her according to the Sunnah if he wishes.

So whoever divorces his wife after he has consummated the marriage, while she is in a purity in which he has touched her, or while she is menstruating, or divorces her without calling witnesses to the divorce as Allah Almighty has commanded, or in the blood of her postnatal bleeding, or swears by her divorce and then breaks the oath, his divorce is no divorce, and she remains his wife. This is the divorce of innovation, because it is contrary to what Allah, mighty and majestic, has commanded and to the Sunnah of His Messenger regarding divorce according to the Sunnah. Both forms of divorce are nonetheless one in substance, even though their wording differs.

That is, when a man wishes to divorce his wife, and she is among those who menstruate, he leaves her until she menstruates and then becomes pure. When she has been cleansed of the blood and has bathed from her menses, he divorces her before touching her, while she is pure in a purity in which he has not touched her, with a single pronouncement, and he calls two witnesses of just character to it, as Allah Almighty has commanded. He retains the greater right to take her back and may return her if he wishes, and she remains with him with two pronouncements counted against her, until she has menstruated three times. When she has menstruated three full times, she has the greater right over herself, and he has no return to her unless he wishes and she also wishes; then he may marry her by a fresh contract, with a dowry, a guardian, and witnesses, and she remains with him with two pronouncements. This is the Sunnah divorce, and it is the one that is recommended, on account of the words of Allah, mighty and majestic: ﴿ لَا تَدْرِي لَعَلَّ اللَّهَ يُحْدِثُ بَعْدَ ذَٰلِكَ أَمْرًا ﴾ "You do not know; perhaps Allah will bring about, after that, some new matter," meaning the taking back.

If he divorces her, in the manner we first described, with a single pronouncement, and then takes her back before her waiting period has elapsed, or after it has elapsed by a fresh contract, he may not divorce her again until he has touched her. When he then wishes to divorce her a second time, he leaves her until she menstruates and becomes pure, and divorces her while she is pure in a purity in which he has not touched her, calling two witnesses of just character. If he wishes to take her back, he returns her in the same way as we described first, at any time before her waiting period elapses. If her waiting period has elapsed, she has the greater right over herself; and if he wishes and she wishes, he may marry her by a renewed contract as we described first. Whether he takes her back or marries her anew in this way, she is his wife, and there now remains against her with him a single pronouncement. If he wishes to divorce her, he may not do so until he has touched her; then he leaves her until she menstruates and becomes pure, and then divorces her while she is pure in a purity in which he has not touched her, with a third pronouncement. She then becomes irrevocably separated from him and is not lawful to him until after another husband, as Allah, mighty and majestic, has said. This is the divorce of the iddah: when he divorces her, then takes her back and is intimate with her, then divorces her three times in the manner we have described, this is the sound and permissible divorce in which there is no disagreement.

If he divorces her twice or thrice in a single purity, it is not valid; for when he has divorced her once she has become divorced, and the second pronouncement does not fall upon her until he has taken her back and she is again his wife. Whoever pronounces divorce upon a woman already divorced, his divorce is not valid and does not fall upon her until she is his wife, just as, were he to pronounce the divorce of a woman who is not his wife, without the authorization of her husband, his divorce of her would not be valid. Likewise, if he divorces her three times, or by whatever number of pronouncements he names, in a single sitting and in a single purity, only one falls upon her, if she was pure in a purity in which he had not touched her. And if he divorces her three times, or by whatever pronouncement he wishes, while she is menstruating, or in a purity in which he has touched her, or divorces her without calling witnesses to the divorce, it is not valid, because it is contrary to what Allah, mighty and majestic, has commanded concerning divorce and to the Sunnah of His Messenger.

Such also is the position of those who permit this divorce and call it a Sunnah of the general body of Muslims, in contradiction of the Book and the Sunnah; and the one who pronounces it is a transgressor who has overstepped, yet they claim that the divorce takes effect and is binding. It is said to them: what then do you say if a man were to marry a woman contrary to the Book and the Sunnah, as when he marries her while she is in her waiting period, or while she has a husband? Their own position is that such a marriage is not permitted, and they forbid it. So when they are asked about permitting divorce contrary to the Book and the Sunnah while forbidding such a marriage, they too have no proof in the matter. It is then said to them: this divorce, which you have permitted though it is contrary to the Book and the Sunnah, is itself a thing that makes lawful a marriage contrary to the Book and the Sunnah, the very marriage whose permissibility you denied in your own doctrine. For this divorce renders the woman forbidden to the one who divorced her by it and makes her lawful to another who would marry her; and were this divorce not valid, that marriage would not be valid either, since she would still be a woman with a husband. So your annulling of her marriage by this divorce is what, in your view, establishes her marriage to the second husband. Thus there has come to be one who makes a marriage lawful contrary to the Book and the Sunnah, which is the very thing you reject. The arguments in a matter like this run long and pass beyond the bounds of this book.

The testimony of women is not admissible in divorce, for Allah, mighty and majestic, said concerning it: ﴿ وَأَشْهِدُوا ذَوَيْ عَدْلٍ مِّنكُمْ ﴾ "And call to witness two of just character from among you." Divorce falls upon five kinds of women, who are divorced in every circumstance, no menstruation or purity being awaited for them: the pregnant woman whose pregnancy has become apparent; the young girl who has not reached the age of menstruation; the woman past her courses, whose menstruation has ceased so that she no longer menstruates; the woman whose husband has not consummated the marriage with her; and the woman whose husband is absent from her on a distant journey. Whenever one of these is divorced, the divorce falls upon her, and she observes her waiting period by the months, counting each month in place of a menstrual cycle, except the woman with whom the marriage has not been consummated; for if he divorces her, she has no waiting period to observe, on account of the words of Allah, mighty and majestic: ﴿ فَمَا لَكُمْ عَلَيْهِنَّ مِنْ عِدَّةٍ تَعْتَدُّونَهَا ﴾ "You have against them no waiting period that you may count."

Metaphors for divorce, and the word of divorce

One who intends to divorce his wife says to her, "You are divorced," or names her and says, "So-and-so is divorced," or she is named to him and he says, "She is divorced," or he says to her, "Reckon your iddah," intending divorce by it, or he sends a messenger to her with that, or he says, "Let so-and-so reckon her iddah." There is no metaphor for divorce and no wording for it other than these two, for they are the two that Allah, mighty and majestic, mentioned in His Book, when He said: ﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ إِذَا طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَطَلِّقُوهُنَّ لِعِدَّتِهِنَّ ﴾ "O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the onset of their waiting period." He named nothing but the divorce and the iddah. So if he says to her, "You are set loose," or "a wilderness," or "severed," or "cut off," or "forbidden," or whatever else he may say to her apart from divorce and iddah, it is nothing, just as anything else he says to her, from the rest of speech, is not divorce. And if he says to her, "You are forbidden to me," or "your private part is forbidden to me," or whatever of her he declares forbidden to himself, no consequence falls upon him in that, nor is she made forbidden to him.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, the prayers of Allah be upon him, said concerning a man who said to his wife, "You are forbidden to me": had I authority over him, I would have struck his head soundly, and I would say to him, Allah made her lawful to you, and then you forbid her? He has done no more than lie, for he has claimed that what Allah made lawful to him is forbidden to him. No divorce falls upon him in that, nor is any expiation binding. It was said to him: but the words of Allah, mighty and majestic, ﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ لِمَ تُحَرِّمُ مَا أَحَلَّ اللَّهُ لَكَ ﴾ "O Prophet, why do you forbid what Allah has made lawful to you," placed an expiation in it. He said, (ع): this very thing confirms what we have said, for Allah, mighty and majestic, reproached His Prophet, and He made the expiation binding upon him only because he had, along with that, sworn an oath. This is shown by His words: ﴿ قَدْ فَرَضَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ تَحِلَّةَ أَيْمَانِكُمْ ﴾ "Allah has ordained for you the absolution of your oaths."

He said: that was because the Messenger of Allah had been alone with Mariyah the Copt, his slave woman, before she bore Ibrahim, (ع). A'ishah came upon this and it grieved her, and he wished to please her, so he forbade Mariyah to himself and swore to her an oath that he would not approach her thereafter. He confided this to her as a secret and bade her conceal it, but she told it to Hafsah, and the two of them backed each other against the Messenger of Allah. So Allah, mighty and majestic, sent down: "O Prophet, why do you forbid what Allah has made lawful to you, seeking the approval of your wives? And Allah is Forgiving, Merciful. Allah has already ordained for you the absolution of your oaths; and Allah is your Protector, and He is the All-Knowing, the All-Wise. And when the Prophet confided a matter to one of his wives, and she then disclosed it, and Allah made it known to him, he made known part of it and turned away from part; and when he informed her of it, she said, Who told you this? He said, The All-Knowing, the All-Aware told me."

When a man pronounces divorce upon his wife, the only words that carry weight are those Allah Almighty named in His Book, where He said:

﴿يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ إِذَا طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَطَلِّقُوهُنَّ لِعِدَّتِهِنَّ﴾

"O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the beginning of their waiting period."

He named nothing but the divorce (al-talaq) and the waiting period (al-idda). So if a man says to his wife, "You are loosed," or "You are quit of me," or "You are severed," or "You are cut off," or "You are forbidden," or any such word other than divorce and the waiting period, it is nothing at all. Such speech is like any other words he might say to her apart from divorce. And if he says to her, "You are forbidden to me," or "Your body is forbidden to me," or makes forbidden upon himself anything of her, no consequence falls upon him in that, and she does not become unlawful to him.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ((ع)) said concerning a man who told his wife, "You are forbidden to me": "Had I any authority over him, I would strike his head and say, Allah made her lawful for you, and then you forbid her? He has done no more than tell a lie, claiming that what Allah made lawful for him is forbidden to him. No divorce enters upon him through that, and no expiation is binding."

It was said to him: "But Allah Almighty says, ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ لِمَ تُحَرِّمُ مَا أَحَلَّ اللَّهُ لَكَ﴾, O Prophet, why do you forbid yourself what Allah has made lawful for you? and He placed an expiation in it." He ((ع)) said: "This confirms what we have said, for Allah Almighty was reproaching His Prophet (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family). The expiation was laid upon him only because he had joined an oath to it. The proof of this is His saying, ﴿قَدْ فَرَضَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ تَحِلَّةَ أَيْمَانِكُمْ﴾, Allah has already ordained for you the absolution of your oaths."

He explained that the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family) had withdrawn in private with Mariya the Copt, his bondwoman, before she gave birth to Ibrahim ((ع)). Aisha came upon them, and the matter grieved her. Wishing to please her, he forbade Mariya to himself and swore to her that he would not approach Mariya thereafter. He confided this to Aisha and asked her to keep it secret, but she disclosed it to Hafsa, and the two of them aided one another against the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family). Then Allah Almighty revealed concerning the matter that He set right between the Prophet and his wives, declaring that Jibril, the righteous among the believers, and the angels were his supporters, and that if he were to divorce his wives, his Lord would replace them with wives better than they, as He said:

﴿عَسَىٰ رَبُّهُ إِن طَلَّقَكُنَّ أَن يُبْدِلَهُ أَزْوَاجًا خَيْرًا مِّنكُنَّ مُسْلِمَاتٍ مُّؤْمِنَاتٍ قَانِتَاتٍ تَائِبَاتٍ عَابِدَاتٍ سَائِحَاتٍ ثَيِّبَاتٍ وَأَبْكَارًا﴾

"obedient, believing, devoutly worshipping, repentant, given to worship, given to fasting, both previously married and virgins."

So He commanded him to expiate the oath he had sworn. He expiated it and returned to her, and Ibrahim was born of her. She thus became the mother of his child (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family).

**The option and the transfer of authority**

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) that his wives showed discontent with him and asked him for more of the goods of this world, as women are wont to do, and that the most severe among them in this was Hafsa. She said to him, "If you divorced us, we would find among our own people men of equal standing." The Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family) bore with them patiently, withdrew from them, and the revelation was withheld for twenty days. Then Allah Almighty revealed:

﴿يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ قُل لِّأَزْوَاجِكَ إِن كُنتُنَّ تُرِدْنَ الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا وَزِينَتَهَا فَتَعَالَيْنَ أُمَتِّعْكُنَّ وَأُسَرِّحْكُنَّ سَرَاحًا جَمِيلًا ۝ وَإِن كُنتُنَّ تُرِدْنَ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ وَالدَّارَ الْآخِرَةَ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ أَعَدَّ لِلْمُحْسِنَاتِ مِنكُنَّ أَجْرًا عَظِيمًا﴾

"O Prophet, say to your wives: If you desire the life of this world and its adornment, then come, I will provide for you and release you graciously. But if you desire Allah, His Messenger, and the abode of the Hereafter, then indeed Allah has prepared for the doers of good among you a great reward."

He withdrew from all of them for twenty-nine nights in the chamber of Umm Ibrahim. Then he called them, recited to them what Allah had revealed concerning them, and gave them the option, and they chose him (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family).

Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdillah ((عليهم السلام)) said that this was not a divorce, but that had they chosen themselves, it would have been a single, irrevocable divorce. Abu Abdillah ((ع)) also said: when a man wishes to give his wife the option, let him first withdraw from her, as the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family) did, until she menstruates and then purifies herself. Then he gives her the option while she is in a state of purity in which he has not touched her, and he calls witnesses to it. If she then chooses herself, she is divorced by an irrevocable divorce, and he has no right of return over her; he may court her again, and if he wishes and she wishes, they may marry afresh. If she chooses him, it is no divorce. The option is exercised by her saying, "I have chosen myself," or "I have chosen you." If she chooses herself, the matter is settled; but if she stays silent until he or she rises and they part from where they were, the option that was granted to her has lapsed. Likewise, if he withdraws the option before she has exercised it, she has no right to choose. And if he comes to her and lays his hand upon her, or kisses her, or is intimate with her before she speaks, the option granted to her has lapsed. If he gives her the option and she says, "I do not accept your option," then she has no option.

If he gives her the option while she is menstruating, or in a state of purity in which he has touched her, or without two witnesses testifying, then it counts for nothing. He ((ع)) said: "No divorce, no khul, no mutual release, and no granting of the option takes effect except by the testimony of two witnesses, in a state of purity untouched by intercourse."

He ((ع)) said: "If a man entrusts the option to his wife, or to her father, or her brother, or her guardian, and she is content that the one to whom he entrusts the matter should decide it, that is permissible." The transfer of authority is of the same standing as the option: it is that he says to her, "I have placed you in command of yourself," or "I have placed your affair in your hands." This stands to him in the place of his saying to her, "Choose."

**What is binding in divorce and what is not**

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) that he said, "There is no divorce except after marriage, and no divorce for one who has not married." And Ali ((ع)) said: "There is no divorce except after marriage, no emancipation except after ownership, no continuous fasting that joins two days without breaking, no fasting that is silence from speech from morning until night, no suckling after weaning, and no orphanhood after a child reaches maturity."

A man asked Abu Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)), saying, "I have a kinswoman who has been offered to me, and I wished to marry her, but before this day I had said, The day I marry her, she is divorced." He said, "Had you married her?" The man said, "No." He said, "Then marry her if you wish, for your divorce of one whose marriage bond you do not hold is no divorce."

Abu Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) was asked about the man who says, "If I marry such a woman, or marry in such a land, or buy such a slave, or take a concubine, or purchase such-and-such, then the one I marry is divorced, and the one I buy or take as concubine is free, and what I buy is charity." He ((ع)) said: "None of this is anything. A man's divorce of what is not in his marriage bond is invalid, and so is his emancipation and his charity of what he does not yet own."

He ((ع)) was asked about a man who divorced his wife while he was ill, and her waiting period either expired or did not, and one of the two died. He said: "If her waiting period expires while he is ill or after he has recovered, he does not inherit from her, but she inherits from him if he dies of that illness, whether her waiting period has expired or not, unless he recovers."

From Ali ((ع)): "Every divorce is valid," meaning as it is duly required, "except the divorce of one bereft of reason." And from Abu Ja'far and Abu Abdillah ((عليهم السلام)): "The one bereft of reason, whose mind is gone, his divorce is not valid, nor is his emancipation."

Ali ((ع)) was asked about a man who saw in his sleep that he had divorced his wife. He said: "That is from Satan. The divorce of a sleeping man is nothing, even if his tongue utters it while he sleeps, until he wakes and pronounces divorce as it is required." No divorce is valid from one bereft of reason, nor from one who raves, nor from one in delirium, nor from a boy who has not reached puberty, nor from a drunkard who does not comprehend.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)): "Whoever is compelled to divorce his wife, his divorce is not valid. There is no divorce and no emancipation under compulsion."

He ((ع)) said concerning a man who says to his wife, divorce being duly due, "You are divorced one and a half times": "She is divorced once, for divorce admits no fractions." Likewise, if he says, "You are divorced three quarters, or a quarter, or a sixth of a divorce," or names some other fraction of a divorce, or leaves it unnamed, she is divorced once. If a man addresses four wives or fewer, divorce being duly permissible, and says, "I have divorced you, one divorce shared among you," each of them is divorced once. Likewise, if he says, "I have divorced all of you, two divorces shared among you," or three, or four, each of them is divorced once. And if he says to them, "I have divorced you, five divorces shared among you," each of them is bound by only a single divorce. This rests upon the principle we set down before, that in a single period of purity only one divorce is binding, and whatever exceeds it counts for nothing.

**Khul and mutual release (mubarat)**

Allah Almighty said:

﴿وَلَا يَحِلُّ لَكُمْ أَن تَأْخُذُوا مِمَّا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ شَيْئًا إِلَّا أَن يَخَافَا أَلَّا يُقِيمَا حُدُودَ اللَّهِ ۖ فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ أَلَّا يُقِيمَا حُدُودَ اللَّهِ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا فِيمَا افْتَدَتْ بِهِ﴾

"It is not lawful for you to take back anything you have given them, unless both fear that they will not maintain the limits of Allah. But if you fear that they will not maintain the limits of Allah, there is no blame upon either of them in what she gives to ransom herself."

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Prophet of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family), from Ali ((ع)) and the Imams among his descendants, that khul is permissible when the man puts it in its proper place. That is, when his wife says to him, "I fear that I will not maintain the limits of Allah toward you, so I give you such-and-such, and set me free," or when her husband says the like to her, it is permissible for them to act upon what they agree.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said that the husband has no right to take anything from her unless she transgresses in her speech and says to him, "I will not obey any command of yours, I will not honor any oath to you, I will not purify myself from impurity for you, I will not let you share my bed, I will admit to your house one whom you dislike, I will enter upon you without your leave," or she says, "I will not maintain the limits of Allah toward you," and makes that plain. When she says such a thing, it becomes lawful for him to take what she ransoms herself with, even beyond what he gave her. But if she does not say such a thing, he ought not to take from her more than what he gave her.

He ((ع)) said: "Khul and mutual release take effect only as divorce does, in a state of purity in which he has not touched her, and before witnesses." When he releases her in this way, he divorces her with a single divorce, as we described above. She thereby becomes clearly separated from him, and he has no right of return over her unless she too wishes it, so that they reconcile by a fresh marriage. She remains with him according to what is left of her divorce count, and he may marry her if he wishes and she wishes, whether before her waiting period expires or after.

As for the words of Allah Almighty:

﴿وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ شِقَاقَ بَيْنِهِمَا فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا مِّنْ أَهْلِهِ وَحَكَمًا مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا إِن يُرِيدَا إِصْلَاحًا يُوَفِّقِ اللَّهُ بَيْنَهُمَا ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيمًا خَبِيرًا﴾

"And if you fear a breach between the two, appoint an arbitrator from his family and an arbitrator from her family. If they both desire reconciliation, Allah will bring about agreement between them. Indeed, Allah is All-Knowing, All-Aware."

It has come from Ali, Abu Ja'far, and Abu Abdillah ((عليهم السلام)) that this applies to a husband and wife who fall into discord and cannot agree, and whose quarrel is prolonged, so that they bring the matter before the ruler. He appoints an arbitrator from the man's family and an arbitrator from the woman's family. If it is granted to the two arbitrators to reconcile the couple, they do so. But if that is not possible and they separate the couple, the arbitrators have no right to separate them until the man and the woman are first consulted. If the couple do not consent to the separation, the two are not parted.

**Al-Ila (the oath of abstention)**

Allah Almighty said:

﴿لِّلَّذِينَ يُؤْلُونَ مِن نِّسَائِهِمْ تَرَبُّصُ أَرْبَعَةِ أَشْهُرٍ ۖ فَإِن فَاءُوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ ۝ وَإِنْ عَزَمُوا الطَّلَاقَ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ﴾

"For those who swear off their wives there is a waiting of four months. Then if they return, Allah is Forgiving, Merciful. But if they are resolved on divorce, then Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing."

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family), from Ali ((ع)) and the Imams of his household, that al-Ila is for a man to swear to his wife that he will not have intercourse with her, and then to keep apart from her until four months pass.

Abu Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: "It is not for the woman to bring her husband to account before four months have passed from the day of his oath. Once four months or more have elapsed, she may bring him to account whenever she wishes thereafter, taking the matter to the ruler. When she does so, he gives the husband the choice between returning to her, that is, to resume relations with her in the proper manner, or divorcing her. He must do one of the two. And if he merely keeps apart from her without an oath, abstaining as long as he wishes, that is not al-Ila and is not counted a divorce, until the woman takes the matter to the ruler and the ruler halts him: either he returns or he divorces then and there. Al-Ila takes effect only in a state of purity untouched by intercourse, and with witnesses, as divorce does. It falls only upon a wife whose marriage has been consummated; it does not fall upon one with whom he has not yet been intimate."

A man is at liberty if he swears off his wife for four months or less; once four months pass and she brings him to account, nothing is lawful for him but to return to her in the proper manner or to divorce her. The return is intercourse. If he is unable to it through illness, or a chronic complaint, or travel, and he affirms his return with his tongue and calls witnesses, that suffices and is permissible. When he is able, he returns to her in the proper manner.

From Ali ((ع)): "When the one bound by al-Ila is halted and resolves upon divorce, let him leave her until she menstruates and purifies herself. When she is pure, he divorces her and calls two upright witnesses. He is then the more entitled to take her back so long as three menstrual cycles have not elapsed. Once they have elapsed, he is but one suitor among others, and she is in command of herself. If he wishes and she wishes, he may marry her afresh, and she remains with him according to what is left of her divorce count."

A man asked Abu Ja'far ((ع)), saying, "My wife bore a boy, and I feared that if I were intimate with her she would conceive and the child would perish, so I swore by Allah that I would not approach her until she had weaned him." Ali ((ع)) said, "There is no al-Ila upon you in seeking the child's welfare."

By this he ((ع)) meant that whoever swears to his wife that he will not approach her for a month, or for less than four months, that is not al-Ila; and if he approaches her before the four months, it is no al-Ila either, for al-Ila is only with an oath, and the oath is the binding of it. If he persists until four months pass, even then it is not al-Ila, for there is no al-Ila except by oath, and al-Ila is the swearing itself.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)): "If the one bound by al-Ila says, I have already had intercourse with her, his word is accepted along with his oath," meaning if he had been intimate with her before that. For when a man has been intimate with his wife and then becomes incapable of it, no separation is made between them; but if he is incapable, or has not been intimate with her, he is given a term, and then a separation is made between them. He said: "And if he swears off her for more than four months, he is one bound by al-Ila."

**Al-Dhihar**

Allah, exalted be His remembrance, said:

﴿الَّذِينَ يُظَاهِرُونَ مِنكُم مِّن نِّسَائِهِم﴾

"Those among you who pronounce dhihar against their wives," to the end of the passage on al-Dhihar.

We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah (may Allah's blessings be upon him and his family) that a man came to him and said, "O Messenger of Allah, I have pronounced dhihar against my wife." He said, "Go and free a slave." The man said, "I have none." He said, "Then fast two consecutive months." He said, "I am not able." He said, "Then go and feed sixty poor persons."

Ali ((ع)) said: "When the one who has pronounced dhihar possesses the means to free a slave, he frees one, and fasting does not suffice for him. If he is able to fast, he fasts, and feeding does not suffice for him. And if he finds nothing to free and is unable to fast, he feeds."

Abu Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: "Everything in the Quran that uses or leaves its doer free to do whichever of them he wishes, as in the saying of Him who is the Speaker:

﴿لَا يُؤَاخِذُكُمُ اللَّهُ بِاللَّغْوِ فِي أَيْمَانِكُمْ وَلَٰكِن يُؤَاخِذُكُم بِمَا عَقَّدتُّمُ الْأَيْمَانَ ۖ فَكَفَّارَتُهُ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ أَوْ كِسْوَتُهُمْ أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ﴾

Allah will not call you to account for what is thoughtless in your oaths, but He will call you to account for the oaths you have deliberately taken. Its expiation is the feeding of ten poor persons with the average of what you feed your families, or clothing them, or the freeing of a slave.

Here the one expiating is free to expiate by whichever of them he wishes. But where His words are if he does not find such-and-such, or if he is not able to do such-and-such, the latter is not lawful until he fails to find the former or is unable to it."

He ((ع)) said: "Al-Dhihar takes effect only in a state of purity untouched by intercourse, and with the testimony of two witnesses, as is the case with divorce. If it is otherwise, it is nothing. Al-Dhihar does not occur within an oath, just as divorce does not. It occurs only when a man intends it and says to her, while she is in a state of purity and in the presence of two upright witnesses, You are to me as my mother's back, and he does not say, if you do such-and-such. Al-Dhihar does not occur in a single period of purity more than once, in a single sitting, and then there is a single expiation. If he pronounces it repeatedly, as it is duly done, then for each time there is an expiation. And if he pronounces dhihar against four wives, then for each one of them there is an expiation. Al-Dhihar is binding with respect to every woman within the forbidden degrees, as when he says, You are to me as my mother's back."

When a man has pronounced zihar (ظهار) against his wife and looks for the means to free himself of its consequence, the order of the expiation is fixed. If he possesses a slave he can free, he frees the slave, and fasting is not permitted to him so long as freeing is within his power. If he is able to fast, he fasts, and feeding is not permitted to him. Only if he finds nothing to free and cannot fast does he feed the poor. Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said: "Everything in the Qur'an in which there is an or offering a choice, its owner may do whichever of them he wishes." This is like the saying of Allah, exalted be His mention:

﴿ لَا يُؤَاخِذُكُمُ اللَّهُ بِاللَّغْوِ فِي أَيْمَانِكُمْ وَلَٰكِن يُؤَاخِذُكُم بِمَا عَقَّدتُّمُ الْأَيْمَانَ ۖ فَكَفَّارَتُهُ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ أَوْ كِسْوَتُهُمْ أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ ﴾

"Allah will not take you to task for what is unintended in your oaths, but He will take you to task for the oaths you have deliberately sworn. Its expiation is the feeding of ten of the poor with the average of what you feed your own families, or clothing them, or freeing a slave" (al-Ma'idah: 89). Here the one expiating may choose whichever of the three he wishes. But where the wording runs, "and whoever does not find such-and-such, or is unable," the second option is not opened to him until he has first failed to find, or been unable to perform, the option that precedes it.

He ((ع)) said: "Zihar is valid only in a state of purity in which he has not touched her, and with the testimony of two witnesses, just as is required for divorce. If it is otherwise, it counts for nothing. There is no zihar within an oath, just as there is no divorce within one." It comes about only when the man intends it and says to her, while she is in purity and in the presence of two upright witnesses, "You are to me as my mother's back," أنتِ عليَّ كظهر أمي, without his adding, "if I do such-and-such." Zihar does not occur more than once in a single sitting within one period of purity, and a single expiation suffices for it even if he repeats the pronouncement several times. But if he pronounces it on separate occasions, there is an expiation for each occasion, and if he pronounces it against four wives, there is an expiation for each one of them. Zihar binds a man with respect to every woman who is unmarriageable to him, as when he says, "You are to me as my mother's back," or names his sister, his paternal aunt, his maternal aunt, or any other of his unmarriageable kin.

The zihar remains binding until he has intercourse with her, and there is no zihar between a man and his slave-woman. If he wishes to approach his wife after pronouncing it, he must first make the expiation, and he must not draw near her until he has expiated. Should he approach her before that, he has done wrong; he then expiates afterward, seeks forgiveness of Allah, and turns to Him in repentance. If he divorces her after pronouncing zihar but before approaching her, no expiation is required of him. If he takes her back after divorcing her, he may not approach her until he has performed the zihar expiation. But if he leaves her until she is fully separated from him and then marries her afresh, no zihar clings to the new marriage, for she had already become separate from him.

A slave incurs zihar just as a free man does when he pronounces it against his wife, yet upon him there is no freeing of a slave and no feeding of the poor unless his master donates it to him. If his master does not, he fasts one month, half of what the free man fasts. The fast of zihar for the free man is two consecutive months, as Allah, exalted be His mention, has commanded. If he fasts a month or less and then breaks his fast, he begins the fast again from its start, and what he has already fasted does not count for him. But if he fasts a full month and a day or two, or more, into the second month, and then breaks his fast before completing it, he makes up only what remains, because he has joined the two months in succession. As for the freeing that expiates zihar, any slave suffices, whether young or old, Muslim or non-Muslim, male or female, because Allah, mighty and majestic, said ﴿ فَتَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ ﴾, "then the freeing of a slave," and laid down no condition upon it. It is not permitted in zihar to free an insane slave, nor one with a corrupting defect, nor a mudabbar, since his manumission may be revoked, nor a mukatab who is still paying off his installments.

It was related from Ali ((ع)) that he said: If the one who has pronounced zihar cannot find a slave to free and so fasts part of the fast, then obtains the means to free a slave before his fast is finished, he frees the slave and the fast is annulled. But if he completes his fast and only afterward finds that he has the means, nothing further is required of him. It was related from him also ((ع)) that he said: He feeds sixty of the poor, each poor person receiving half a sa. Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said that it suffices to feed each poor person a mudd. What Ali ((ع)) said is what is commanded of the one who is able, and what Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said suffices for the one who is unable; his ruling enters by way of the latitude of relief. What Ali ((ع)) said is what is commanded, and whoever feeds each poor person a mudd has discharged the duty, as Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)) said. Neither zihar nor expiation falls due except where there has been intercourse with her, and so it is even if he has not approached even one of his wives. If a man approaches his wife without having expiated, he has done wrong and committed a grave sin, so let him seek the forgiveness of Allah and turn to Him in repentance.

Mention of Li'an

Allah, exalted be His mention, said:

﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يَرْمُونَ أَزْوَاجَهُمْ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُمْ شُهَدَاءُ إِلَّا أَنفُسُهُمْ فَشَهَادَةُ أَحَدِهِمْ أَرْبَعُ شَهَادَاتٍ بِاللَّهِ ۙ إِنَّهُ لَمِنَ الصَّادِقِينَ ۝ وَالْخَامِسَةُ أَنَّ لَعْنَتَ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ إِن كَانَ مِنَ الْكَاذِبِينَ ۝ وَيَدْرَأُ عَنْهَا الْعَذَابَ أَن تَشْهَدَ أَرْبَعَ شَهَادَاتٍ بِاللَّهِ ۙ إِنَّهُ لَمِنَ الْكَاذِبِينَ ۝ وَالْخَامِسَةَ أَنَّ غَضَبَ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهَا إِن كَانَ مِنَ الصَّادِقِينَ ۝ وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُ وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ تَوَّابٌ حَكِيمٌ ﴾

"And those who accuse their wives and have no witnesses except themselves, the testimony of one of them shall be four testimonies by Allah that he is among the truthful, and the fifth that the curse of Allah be upon him if he is among the liars. And it averts the punishment from her that she testify four testimonies by Allah that he is among the liars, and the fifth that the wrath of Allah be upon her if he is among the truthful. And were it not for the grace of Allah upon you and His mercy, and that Allah is Relenting, Wise" (al-Nur: 6 to 10).

Ali ((ع)) said: Whoever accuses his wife, there is no li'an between him and her until he claims to have seen the act, saying, "I saw a man between her legs committing adultery with her," or until he disavows her child. According to the word of Ja'far ibn Muhammad ((ع)), this takes place only before the judge, once the man has held firmly to his statement and not retracted it, and she has denied what he said. Then the judge orders him to perform li'an. The li'an is that he says, "I bear witness by Allah that I have indeed seen a man committing adultery with her," or, "I bear witness that this child is not mine." He says this four times, then says in the fifth, "If I am lying, then the curse of Allah be upon me," and the judge says, "Amin." Then the judge questions the woman. If she affirms what he said, she is stoned. If she denies it, she says, "I bear witness by Allah that he is among the liars in what he has accused me of," saying this four times, then says in the fifth, "If he is truthful, then the wrath of Allah be upon me," and the judge says, "Amin." When this has been done, the judge separates the two, and she is never again lawful to him. The child is not joined to the one who disavowed him and is not ascribed to him; rather the child is ascribed to his mother and her maternal kin, and his inheritance passes to them. The woman's dowry rests upon the one who performed li'an against her.

Li'an is carried out only before the judge, and there is no li'an between a man and his wife until he has had intercourse with her, even if he accuses her of adultery or disavows the child she bore. If he refuses to perform li'an and she raises the matter to the authority, he is given the hadd lashing for slandering her, unless he produces proof of what he said. If he has proof, and four upright men bear witness to the adultery against her, she is stoned and the li'an falls away. Likewise, if he disavows the child she bore and will not perform li'an, he receives the hadd lashing when she raises the matter to the authority, and she remains his wife unless he divorces her. If he performs li'an and then retracts what he said and pronounces himself a liar, he receives the hadd lashing, she does not return to him, and the child inherits from him, though he inherits nothing from the child. Li'an is valid between a Muslim and a dhimmi woman, between a free man and a slave-woman, between two slaves, and between every married pair who are of age.

There is no li'an between two who are minors until they reach maturity, and once they reach it he may not perform li'an against her for what he charged while they were minors. There is no li'an between a mute man and a mute woman, for li'an is enacted only with the tongue; if a man accuses his wife while she is mute, the two are separated and he does not perform li'an. He does not perform li'an against her while she is pregnant, but waits until she gives birth, and when she has given birth he performs the li'an. If a man slanders his wife only through insinuation, there is no li'an for that. If he says to her, "I did not find you a virgin," there is nothing upon him, and no li'an, because virginity may be lost by something other than intercourse. If he accuses her before he has had intercourse with her, he receives the hadd lashing and she remains his wife. The two who perform li'an do so only while standing, not seated. If a man accuses his wife and then divorces her, and she raises the matter while she is still within her iddah, he performs li'an; but if she raises it after her iddah has elapsed, he receives the hadd lashing unless he brings proof of what he said. If he accuses her and she dies before he performs li'an, and a man from among her guardians rises to perform li'an against him on her behalf, the husband takes no inheritance from her; but if no one rises to do this, he inherits from her. If a man divorces his wife before consummation and she then claims to be pregnant by him while he denies it, then if she establishes that he had let down a veil over her in seclusion, he performs li'an; otherwise he swears that he never let a veil down over her nor approached her, and her claim is set aside. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt ((عليهم السلام)).

Mention of the Iddah, the Maintenance Within It, and the Mourning

Allah, mighty and majestic, said:

﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا ﴾

"And those of you who die and leave wives behind, they shall wait by themselves four months and ten days" (al-Baqarah: 234). And He said:

﴿ وَالْمُطَلَّقَاتُ يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ ثَلَاثَةَ قُرُوءٍ ﴾

"And divorced women shall wait by themselves three menstrual cycles" (al-Baqarah: 228). And He said:

﴿ وَأُولَاتُ الْأَحْمَالِ أَجَلُهُنَّ أَن يَضَعْنَ حَمْلَهُنَّ ﴾

"And as for those who are pregnant, their term is that they deliver their burden" (al-Talaq: 4). And He said:

﴿ ثُمَّ طَلَّقْتُمُوهُنَّ مِن قَبْلِ أَن تَمَسُّوهُنَّ فَمَا لَكُمْ عَلَيْهِنَّ مِنْ عِدَّةٍ تَعْتَدُّونَهَا ﴾

"Then you divorce them before you have touched them, you have no waiting period over them to count" (al-Ahzab: 49).

So when a man dies leaving a wife, she observes a waiting period of four months and ten nights, whether he had consummated the marriage with her or not, and whether she is an adult or a child, because Allah, mighty and majestic, said, ﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا ﴾, and made no exception in this between one who has been consummated with and one who has not, nor between a child and an adult. She may keep this waiting period wherever she wishes, in her husband's house or elsewhere.

If a man divorces his wife while she is menstruating, her iddah is three menstrual cycles, and she keeps it only in her husband's house. A pregnant woman whose husband dies observes the longer of the two terms. If she gives birth before the four months and ten nights have passed, she completes the four months and ten; and if the four months and ten pass and she has not yet given birth, she waits patiently until she gives birth, because Allah, mighty and majestic, said, ﴿ وَأُولَاتُ الْأَحْمَالِ أَجَلُهُنَّ أَن يَضَعْنَ حَمْلَهُنَّ ﴾, and said, ﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا ﴾, making no exception in either. If he divorces her while she is pregnant, her term is that she delivers her burden; whenever she delivers it she becomes separate from him, and everything she brings forth that is known to be a child separates her. But if her womb holds two children or more, she is not separated until she delivers the last of what is in her womb.

Whoever divorces his wife with one or two revocable pronouncements and then dies while she is in this state observes the iddah of death, for she had not become separate from him and he died while she was still within his bond, and Allah, mighty and majestic, says, ﴿ وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا ﴾. Whoever divorces his wife while he is absent from her, her iddah is reckoned from the day he pronounced the divorce, even if its news reaches her only some time later, and after it passes she becomes lawful for husbands. But if he dies while absent from her, she must begin her iddah from the time his news reaches her, and what came before that is not counted, because mourning is obligatory upon the widow, while there is no mourning upon the divorced woman.

If a man divorces his wife before consummating the marriage, there is no iddah upon her, and she may marry whenever she wishes. The divorced woman who suffers from istihada counts her iddah by the coming on of her menstruation. We have already mentioned how the blood of menstruation is distinguished from the blood of istihada: menstrual blood is thick, dark, foul-smelling, and warm, while the blood of istihada is thin and clear, neither warm nor foul. When she sees the blood of istihada, she counts her iddah accordingly; but if she is ignorant of this and does not know how to tell the one from the other, she counts her iddah by the months. So too, if a woman is divorced and menstruates once or twice and then her menstruation ceases, she begins her iddah afresh by the months and does not count what has already passed of the menstruation, because Allah, mighty and majestic, has imposed three cycles upon the woman who menstruates and three months upon the woman who has despaired of menstruation. Since this woman did not complete the three cycles and her menstruation stopped short of them, it falls upon her to observe a iddah of three months.

Beyond the reckoning of the waiting period itself lies the question of what a woman is owed once the marriage has come apart, and here the law weighs carefully the different ways in which a marriage may end.

A pregnant woman who has been divorced is entitled to both maintenance and accommodation. The pregnant widow whose husband has died, however, has no maintenance. A woman divorced revocably, who is passing her iddah, is entitled to maintenance and accommodation until her waiting period is complete. But the woman who has been divorced irrevocably has neither maintenance nor accommodation.

Mourning belongs to the widow whose husband has died. It is not required of the divorced woman, nor of the woman parted from her husband by khul, nor of the woman parted by mubarah, nor of the woman parted by li'an. Mourning means that the widow, throughout the period she is counting, does not comb her hair and does not dye it, does not perfume herself and does not apply kohl, does not adorn herself and does not wear a dyed garment, unless that garment is black. She does not leave her house during the day, nor does she pass the night away from her house. If she wishes, she may go out in the last part of the night and return before evening. This is the essence of mourning.

### On the Provision of Divorce and Making the Divorced Woman Lawful Again

Allah, mighty and majestic, said:

> لَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِنْ طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ مَا لَمْ تَمَسُّوهُنَّ أَوْ تَفْرِضُوا لَهُنَّ فَرِيضَةً ۚ وَمَتِّعُوهُنَّ عَلَى الْمُوسِعِ قَدَرُهُ وَعَلَى الْمُقْتِرِ قَدَرُهُ مَتَاعًا بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۖ حَقًّا عَلَى الْمُحْسِنِينَ

"There is no blame upon you if you divorce women whom you have not touched, or for whom you have not specified an obligation. And make provision for them, the wealthy according to his means and the straitened according to his means, a provision in kindness, a duty upon those who do good." (Al-Baqarah 236)

And so the provision is obligatory. He also said:

> وَلِلْمُطَلَّقَاتِ مَتَاعٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۖ حَقًّا عَلَى الْمُتَّقِينَ

"And for divorced women there is a provision in kindness, a duty upon the God-fearing." (Al-Baqarah 241)

The provision of mut'a is due to the woman divorced according to the Sunnah and to the woman passing her iddah, whether or not the marriage was consummated. There is no mut'a for the woman parted by khul, nor for the woman parted by mubarah. There is no fixed limit upon the amount of the provision. It rests upon the man of means, as Allah, mighty and majestic, said, "upon the wealthy according to his means and upon the straitened according to his means." Whatever the divorcing husband provides discharges the obligation. It is recommended, when a man has means, that he be generous in what he provides for her, whether with dinars or dirhams, a slave or a slave-girl, goods or food, all of which has been narrated from the Imams, (عليهم السلام). The provision is not given until after the iddah has ended and she has become fully separated from him. So long as he still hopes for her and she still hopes for him, he does not give her the provision.

If a man divorces his wife in the manner we have described, for the iddah or according to the Sunnah, with three pronouncements, she does not become lawful to him, as Allah, mighty and majestic, said, except after another husband has married her and consummated the marriage with her.

The second husband may then divorce her, or he may die. After her waiting period from the second husband has expired, the first husband may marry her again if he wishes and she wishes, in a fresh and renewed marriage, and she stands with him facing the full count of divorce anew. If he then divorces her three times in this way, and she marries another, and he afterward takes her back, she does not become lawful to him again after those three, even if she remarries. She is lawful to him only if she contracts a marriage of genuine desire, nikah ghibta, with an adult man who consummates the marriage and has intercourse with her. A castrated man does not make her lawful for him, nor does a boy who has not yet reached puberty, unless he has intercourse with her after reaching puberty. A marriage of mut'a does not make her lawful to him. Nor is it lawful for him to collude with a man so that the man will marry her and make her lawful for him, nor to arrange for some other man to do the same.

If, however, he divorces her once or twice, so that she becomes separated from him, and she then marries another who either dies or divorces her, and the first husband marries her again after her waiting period from the second husband has expired, she stands with him for whatever remained of her count of divorces, up to the full three, as set out above. She does not become lawful to him except after a husband. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام).

### On the Return, the Status of Slaves, and the Dowries Binding upon Husbands

We have mentioned above, in describing divorce, that when a man divorces his wife he may take her back so long as her waiting period has not ended. Once it has ended, he is but one suitor among suitors. If she wishes and he wishes, he marries her in a fresh and renewed marriage, and she stands with him for whatever remains of her count of divorces. If he takes her back before her waiting period expires, that is his right whether she consents or refuses, and she stands with him for whatever remains of her count of divorces. The return must be witnessed by two just witnesses. When he takes her back before her waiting period has ended, he does so without any compensation and without a new dowry. But if her waiting period has passed and they agree upon a return, he may not take her back except with a new dowry that he names for her.

A man does not need to seek his wife's permission to return to her so long as he still has a right of return over her. If he divorces her a third time, there is no return to her. The divorce pronounced by a slave against a free woman counts as three, and the divorce pronounced by a free man or a slave against a slave-woman counts as two.

If a man divorces his wife before consummating the marriage, she receives half the dowry, as Allah said. If he had given her an animal as her dowry and it bore young while in her keeping, and he then divorced her before consummation, the matter is examined. If the animal was already pregnant at the time he gave it to her as dowry, he takes back half of it and half of its offspring. But if the pregnancy occurred while the animal was in her keeping, the offspring are entirely hers, and she returns half of the mother-animals. If he had given her a dowry and she then gave it back to him as a gift, or bestowed it upon him as charity, and he afterward divorced her before consummation, he takes back half of it from her. And if she was his own slave-woman, and he had made her freedom her dowry, as we have described before, and he then divorced her before consummation, he takes back half of her value as assessed on the day he married her. If he dies without having named a dowry for her and without having consummated the marriage, she has no claim to a dowry, yet she inherits from him.
