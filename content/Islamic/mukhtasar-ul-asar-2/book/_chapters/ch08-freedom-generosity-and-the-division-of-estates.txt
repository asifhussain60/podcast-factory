# Freedom, Generosity, and the Division of Estates

### Gifts, Bequests, and the Shares of Inheritance

### On Gifts and Generosity

Gifts, good works, and beneficence offered for the sake of God Almighty and in pursuit of His reward hold within them virtue, righteousness, and recompense. The Messenger of God, (ع) and his family, said: "All of creation are the children of God, and the most beloved of creation to God Almighty is the one who benefits His children, who brings joy to a household, and who walks with a Muslim brother in his time of need." Such a person, he said, is more beloved to God Almighty than two months of i'tikaf in the Sacred Mosque.

Ali, prayers of God be upon him, said that the people of goodness stand in greater need of having their goodness accepted than the needy stand in need of them, because through that goodness come praise, reward, and remembrance for them from God. Whoever performs a kind act toward one of God's creatures has in truth ennobled himself by it and adorned his own honor.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, prayers of God be upon him, said: "When God raises the believer from his grave, there goes forth with him a beautiful figure. When he passes through those terrors, it says to him, Do not fear, no harm shall come to you, and it does not cease to reassure him and give him glad tidings until it brings him before God, who calls him to an easy reckoning and then commands that he be taken to Paradise. The believer says to it, Who are you, may God have mercy upon you? You promised me and were truthful to me and gave me security. It replies, I am a creation your Lord fashioned out of the joy you used to bring to the believers. Today it is I who gladden you."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, prayers of God be upon him, said: "There is nothing more excellent than beneficence except its reward, and beneficence is a gift from God Almighty to His believing servant. Not everyone who loves to do good to people is opened to it, nor is everyone who desires it able to do it, nor is everyone who is able to do it given leave to do it. So when God Almighty bestows His grace upon a servant, He gathers together for him the desire for beneficence, the ability for it, and the leave to perform it. There, happiness and honor are complete, both for the one who seeks the good and the one to whom it is directed."

He said, (ع): "Beneficence is not made complete except by three qualities: making little of it, making it easy, and hastening it. When you make little of it, you have made it great in the eyes of the one to whom you do it; when you make it easy, you have rendered it complete for him; and when you hasten it, you have made it a cause of joy for him. But if you do not do these things, you have spoiled it and rendered it thankless."

He said, (ع): "The best of Muslims is the one who joins, helps, and gives." And he said, (ع): "I heard my father, (ع), say: the doing of good wards off the calamities of evil. Every kindness is a charity, and the people of goodness in this world are the people of goodness in the Hereafter. The first to enter Paradise are the people of goodness."

The Messenger of God, (ع) and his family, said: "Whoever has a kindness done to him, let him repay it; if he is unable, let him praise the one who did it; and if he does not, then he has been ungrateful for the blessing."

We have narrated from the Imams of the People of the House of the Messenger of God, prayers of God be upon them, that they permitted a man to favor some of his children over others with his wealth and his gifts if he wished. A gift or a charity is valid whether or not it has been taken into the recipient's possession. Whoever gives a gift for the sake of God Almighty, or to a blood relative or a kinsman, has no right to take it back. But if he gives a gift or grant intending to be compensated for it, that is disliked, and he may take it back if he is not compensated. And whoever gives a gift, or gives a charity, to a blood relative or kinsman who then dies, so that it returns to him by inheritance, in that there is no harm.

The umra, the ruqba, and the sukna are among the things in which there is merit and reward. The umra is that a man grants another man a house, an estate, or anything he may benefit from for the duration of his life. The ruqba is of the same meaning, except that it carries the condition that whichever of the two dies first, the property returns to the original grantor or to his heirs. If a man grants something for his own lifetime, then it is his while he lives. If the condition is set upon the life of the grantor and he dies, it returns to his heirs. If it is conditioned upon the lifetime of the recipient, then it is the recipient's for his life, and if the grantor dies, his heirs may not take it from the recipient's hands; he may dwell in it and inhabit it until he dies. And if the grantor made it for the recipient and his heirs, then it belongs to him and to them after him, according to how the grantor settled it.

Presents are commended when they are for the sake of God Almighty and for His face. There is no good in any present given to curry favor in a falsehood, or to seek a like return, or to obtain the fulfillment of a need, or to secure attention from the one to whom it is given. The fulfilling of a believer's needs and care for him in what is right is an obligation upon his believing brother; so if he takes payment for that, it is unlawful gain. Likewise is whatever he takes for collusion in falsehood.

As for what is an imposed obligation, such as zakat and the like, the merit is to give it openly and to announce it. As for voluntary charity, the merit is to keep it secret, though if it is announced there is no harm in that. God, exalted be He, has said: ﴿إِنْ تُبْدُوا الصَّدَقَاتِ فَنِعِمَّا هِيَ وَإِنْ تُخْفُوهَا وَتُؤْتُوهَا الْفُقَرَاءَ فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ﴾ — "If you disclose your charities, that is good; but if you conceal them and give them to the poor, that is better for you." (Al-Baqarah: 271)

So it is with all the acts of righteousness: the disclosure and mention of their obligatory portions is good, while the concealment and secrecy of their voluntary portions is more excellent. The obligatory prayer is more excellent in the mosques and in congregation, while the voluntary prayer is more excellent in seclusion. If a man were to say, "I have prayed the noon prayer," or the afternoon prayer, or whatever obligatory prayer he names, there is no harm in his saying so; and likewise if he says, "I have fasted the month of Ramadan." But if he says, "I have risen for the night prayer," or "I have fasted such-and-such" of the voluntary acts, then that saying of his is disliked, on account of the ostentation it admits. Upon this principle run all deeds.

Whoever owns something but has not yet taken it into his possession may still give it in charity or as a gift. Whoever gives a charity is not permitted to take it back. Whoever sets something aside as an endowment for his child, his freed clients, or particular people he names, and removes it from his own hand and delivers it to them, that is valid, and it stands according to whatever condition he stipulates and the terms upon which he restricts it. If they later fall into dispute over it and corruption among them is feared, they may sell it if they wish and divide its price in proportion to what was assigned to each, provided it was set aside for them by name. But if he set it aside for them and their descendants, or said, "When they die out, it becomes a charity," then it is a charity and they are not permitted to sell it. If he made its final destination a charity, then when those for whom it was set aside die out it passes to the poor and the needy. If he did not make its final destination a charity, and those for whom it was endowed die out, it returns to him if he is living, or to whoever is entitled to his inheritance if he has died. If he stipulated that any of the women for whom the endowment was made who marries forfeits her right in it, and then they are widowed and return to it, the condition holds. And if he said, "If I am in need, I have the better right to it," then the endowment is void; it is part of his estate for his heirs if he dies, and he may take it back whenever he wishes.

### On Bequests

God, exalted be He, has said: ﴿كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ إِنْ تَرَكَ خَيْرًا الْوَصِيَّةُ﴾ — "It is prescribed for you, when death approaches one of you and he leaves wealth behind, that he make a bequest." (Al-Baqarah: 180) And death is present for every living soul, for none knows when it will come to him. He also said: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا شَهَادَةُ بَيْنِكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ حِينَ الْوَصِيَّةِ﴾ — "O you who believe, let there be testimony among you when death approaches one of you, at the time of the bequest." (Al-Ma'idah: 106)

The Messenger of God, (ع) and his family, said: "It does not befit a Muslim to pass two nights except that his will is written and kept at his head." And he said, (ع) and his family: "Whoever does not make his bequest well at the time of death has fallen short in his manliness and his reason." They asked, "O Messenger of God, how does the dying man make his bequest?" He said that the people are to be gathered when death comes to him, and he bears witness and makes his bequest; and he spoke of this at length.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, prayers of God be upon him, said: "The bequest is a duty upon every Muslim." And Ja'far ibn Muhammad, prayers of God be upon him, said: "The bequest is obligatory upon every Muslim, and it does not befit anyone to pass two nights except that his will is written. No dying person dies except that God Almighty restores to him his reason, his hearing, and his sight before death so that he may make his bequest, whether he takes that opportunity or leaves it."

We have narrated from the Imams of the House of the Messenger of God, (ع), that they permitted a bequest of up to one-third, and no more, of what the testator leaves behind, and they did not allow anything beyond the third except with the consent of the heirs.

Whoever makes a set of bequests in which he directs that the Hajj be performed on his behalf, that a slave be freed, and that expiation be made for his oaths: if he had not performed the Hajj and is unable to, and the bequest falls short of the third, then one begins with the Hajj, then the expiation, then the manumission, and whatever falls short is met from what remains. This is when he has framed the bequest by saying, "Let my third be brought out and spent on such-and-such." But if he made a bequest without naming a third or anything else, and directed that the Hajj be performed for him when he had not performed it, then the Hajj is brought out from the whole of what he left. Likewise, if he states that he owes zakat or any other obligation and directs that it be discharged on his behalf, it is brought out from the entire estate, while whatever is voluntary is brought out from the third. So if he had already performed the Hajj and then directs that a Hajj be performed for him, that is voluntary and is brought out only from the third.

There is no bequest for an heir unless the other heirs permit it, for God Almighty has fixed the heirs inheritance and nothing is added to it, unless the testator makes a bequest for a parent or a relative who does not inherit from him, as when his parents are enslaved or polytheists, or related to him through nursing, or are foster parents. If a man, during the illness in which he dies, acknowledges a debt before his heirs or one of them, his case is examined: if he is suspected, his acknowledgment is not accepted except by clear proof testifying to the origin of what he acknowledged, unless the rest of the heirs permit it; but if he is sick yet upright and trustworthy, his acknowledgment is accepted. When a man has made his bequest, he may revoke it, and he may add to or subtract from his bequest so long as he is alive and understands what he bequeaths. And if he makes bequests exceeding the third, and his heirs come to know of this in his lifetime and approve and allow it to him, then they have no right to retract it after his death.

### On the Obligatory Shares of Inheritance

This concerns the shares of the child, the parents, the spouses, the grandfather, the grandmother, and the brothers.

God, mighty and majestic, has said: ﴿يُوصِيكُمُ اللَّهُ فِي أَوْلَادِكُمْ لِلذَّكَرِ مِثْلُ حَظِّ الْأُنْثَيَيْنِ فَإِنْ كُنَّ نِسَاءً فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ فَلَهُنَّ ثُلُثَا مَا تَرَكَ وَإِنْ كَانَتْ وَاحِدَةً فَلَهَا النِّصْفُ وَلِأَبَوَيْهِ لِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ مِمَّا تَرَكَ إِنْ كَانَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ فَإِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَوَرِثَهُ أَبَوَاهُ فَلِأُمِّهِ الثُّلُثُ فَإِنْ كَانَ لَهُ إِخْوَةٌ فَلِأُمِّهِ السُّدُسُ مِنْ بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِي بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ﴾ — "God charges you concerning your children: to the male the like of the share of two females. If they are women, more than two, theirs is two-thirds of what he left; and if there is only one, hers is the half. To each of his parents a sixth of what he left, if he has a child; but if he has no child and his parents inherit from him, then to his mother a third; and if he has brothers, then to his mother a sixth, after any bequest he makes or any debt." (Al-Nisa: 11)

We have narrated from the People of the House, prayers of God be upon them, that whoever dies leaving children, male and female, with no heir besides them, his inheritance is divided among them so that the male among them receives the like of the share of two females. So too if he leaves a single son and a single daughter, the son receives two-thirds and the daughter one-third. If he leaves a single son with no other heir, the whole inheritance is his. If he leaves two sons, or a number of sons, the inheritance is divided among them equally.

If he leaves a single daughter with no other heir, she takes the half by the share God Almighty has named for her in what we have recited, and the other half is returned to her by the tie of kinship, in accordance with the word of God, mighty and majestic: ﴿وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنْ بَعْدُ وَهَاجَرُوا وَجَاهَدُوا مَعَكُمْ فَأُولَئِكَ مِنْكُمْ وَأُولُو الْأَرْحَامِ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلَى بِبَعْضٍ فِي كِتَابِ اللَّهِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ﴾ — "And those who believed afterward and emigrated and struggled along with you, they are of you; and those who are bound by blood are nearer to one another in the Book of God. Truly God has knowledge of all things." (Al-Anfal: 75) And if they are two daughters, or a group of daughters, the two-thirds is theirs, divided among them equally by the named share, and the remaining third is returned to them by the tie of kinship.

When the children are present together with those entitled to fixed shares, such as the husband, the wife, the grandmother, and the parents, these receive their fixed shares which God Almighty has named for them, and what remains goes to the child or the children, if they are a group, as we have described. If there is no child but there is a child's child, they stand in the place of the child, the males among them as the males and the females as the females, even if they descend and become remote, so long as there is none nearer to the deceased among them; for whoever is nearer of them screens out whoever is more remote. And everyone for whom God Almighty has named a share in the Book among the heirs has a better right than the one He has not named. So when something is left over beyond what He named for him, it is returned to him, and to the sons and the group, if they are of them, in proportion to their inheritance, except for the husband and the wife: nothing is returned to those two unless they have a kinship that entitles them to the return.

An example: a man dies and leaves his two parents and a daughter. The parents take the two sixths, and the daughter takes the half by her named share, and a sixth remains, which is returned to them in proportion to their shares. This is because one share remains out of six, since the two sixths went to the parents, which are two shares, and the half went to the daughter, which is three sixths, making five shares, with one share remaining. That remaining share must likewise be divided over the five, so the estate reverts to five: three-fifths of the inheritance to the daughter and two-fifths to the parents.

If he leaves a daughter, a father, and a mother, the daughter takes the half as we mentioned, and the father, or the mother, takes the sixth, so the estate reverts, in the manner we have described, to fourths: a quarter to the father or the mother, and three-quarters to the daughter. Upon this pattern runs whatever follows of the same kind.

### When a Marriage Ends: Divorce and Separation

When the parents alone survive a person and there is no other heir, the father receives two thirds and the mother receives one third. If a child is also present, the rule proceeds as we have already described, even when the children are many, both male and female: they take what remains after the fixed shares, the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females, however few or however many they may be. If two sons survive, they share what remains between them; and if two children survive together with the parents, they receive two thirds between them. We have already mentioned what falls to a single child.

If a person leaves behind two parents together with brothers, whether full brothers or brothers through the father, the brothers receive nothing. The mother receives one sixth and the father takes what remains, as Allah, mighty and majestic, has stated in His Book. The brothers through the mother do not deprive the mother of her third by way of inheriting for themselves; they merely reduce her share, so that the father receives two thirds and the mother one third. The father inherits alongside the child and alongside the parents, and none inherits together with the child and the parents except the husband, the wife, and the grandmother.

It is narrated from the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, that he assigned an inheritance to the grandmother and ruled that one sixth be fixed for her, looking to her children as they divided among themselves. So the grandmother inherits one sixth alongside the parents, and alongside either parent alone. If she is alone with no one to exclude her, and no other heir survives, she takes one sixth by the naming of the share and the remainder is returned to her, so that she gathers the whole inheritance. If there are two grandmothers, a grandmother on the father's side and a grandmother on the mother's side, the father's grandmother and the mother's mother share two thirds, and the mother's mother alone takes one third. Among the grandmothers, whichever is closer in degree excludes the one more remote.

The paternal grandfather stands in the position of the full brothers, and the brothers through the father inherit as they inherit. He is reckoned as one of them, and whoever excludes them excludes him. The maternal grandfather stands in the position of the brother through the mother, and he too inherits as they inherit and is excluded as they are excluded. This is because the grandfather's kinship to the deceased is like the kinship of the brother, each of them being joined to the deceased through his father, and their kinship through the father is a single kinship: the grandfather is that father's father, and the brother is that father's son.

If the deceased leaves behind no father, no mother, and no other ascendant, but is survived by brothers, the inheritance belongs to those brothers. The full brothers exclude the brothers through the father; and where there are no full brothers, the brothers through the father stand in their place. When the brothers are both full and paternal together, the inheritance is divided among them as with male and female shares. If they are through the mother alone, the inheritance is divided equally between male and female, as Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿وَإِن كَانَ رَجُلٌ يُورَثُ كَلَالَةً أَوِ امْرَأَةٌ وَلَهُ أَخٌ أَوْ أُخْتٌ فَلِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِّنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ فَإِن كَانُوا أَكْثَرَ مِن ذَٰلِكَ فَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ فِي الثُّلُثِ﴾ "If a man dies leaving no children, and he has a brother or a sister, each of them receives one sixth; but if there are more than that, they share one third" (al-Nisa 12). It is narrated that this verse was revealed concerning a brother and a sister through the mother.

When full brothers and brothers through the mother are present together, along with a brother through the father, the full brothers receive two thirds divided among them, the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females, whether they are few or many. If maternal siblings are also present with them, male or female, two or more, whether two sisters, or a brother and sister, or brothers and sisters, then one third belongs to those maternal siblings, divided equally among them regardless of sex, however many they may be, and the brothers through the father are set aside. Likewise, if a person leaves behind one maternal brother or one maternal sister, that single one receives one sixth; and if there are several, they receive one third. If there is a full sister, she receives one half, and the remainder is returned to her through kinship.

Brothers and sisters inherit alongside the spouses. The spouse's share is given first, and what remains goes to the brothers according to what we have described. The husband receives half of his wife's inheritance if she has no child, and a quarter if she has a child, whether male, female, or several. The wife receives a quarter of her husband's inheritance if he has no child, and one eighth if he has a child, whether male, female, or several, according to the explicit text of the Book. Two, three, or four wives share equally in that same portion, one eighth where there is a child and a quarter where there is none, dividing it equally among them and adding nothing to it.

If a man dies leaving two parents and a wife, or a woman dies leaving two parents and a husband, the husband receives half of his wife's estate, the wife receives a quarter of her husband's estate, the mother receives one third, and the father takes what remains, according to the text of the Book. Just as the father has precedence over the mother when brothers are present, so here he bears the deficiency, because Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿فَإِن كَانَ لَهُ إِخْوَةٌ فَلِأُمِّهِ السُّدُسُ﴾ "If he has brothers, his mother receives one sixth" (al-Nisa 11). Since the brothers do not inherit alongside the parents, the remaining wealth here is inherited by the parents. So the mother receives one third when there are no brothers, and one sixth when there are brothers. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them all.

### On the inheritance of kindred and connections, and on the apportioning of shares without awl and without exclusion

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them, that whoever dies leaving behind those who hold fixed shares named in the Book, they inherit according to the shares Allah, mighty and majestic, has named in the Book. If he leaves behind none of the named sharers, his inheritance passes to those of his kindred who are nearest to him; and if they are equal in nearness, it is shared among them. Whoever draws near to him through one who holds a named share is more deserving than one who draws near through one who has no named share. Those who are near exclude those who are remote. Whoever is related through the deceased's father inherits the father's share as if the father were alive; whoever is related through the mother inherits the mother's share; whoever is related through a brother inherits that brother's share; and whoever is related through a sister inherits her share.

For example: a man dies leaving behind his son's son and his son's daughter. The inheritance between them is divided with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females. If he leaves behind his daughter's son and his son's daughter, the son's daughter receives two thirds, which is her father's share, and the daughter's son receives one third, which is his mother's share. And if he leaves behind sons and daughters of a son who predeceased him, together with a son or daughter of a daughter who predeceased him, one third goes to the single son or single daughter, or whatever children there are of the daughter, and two thirds go to the children of the son, divided among them with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females, whether few or many. These inherit their father's share as if he were alive, and those inherit their mother's share as if she were alive, whether each group is few or many.

Those who draw near through the mother's kinship, among the kindred, inherit as the brothers through the mother inherit, the male and the female alike. Those who draw near through the father's kinship inherit with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females. The sons of brothers and their daughters, and the sisters, inherit in the manner we have described when there is no direct child or grandchild, just as the grandchildren inherit as we explained before. If they are through one father or one mother, the sons of the brother divide the wealth among themselves with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females. Likewise, the sons of paternal uncles and the sons of maternal uncles divide the wealth, the sons of maternal uncles equally between male and female, each group inheriting what belonged to its father or mother had they been alive, whether they are few or many in number. So too the maternal uncle and the maternal aunt share the wealth equally between them, while the paternal uncle and the paternal aunt share it with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females, and their children likewise. To the sons of the maternal uncle and the sons of the maternal aunt belongs one third, shared among them, their males and their children alike; and to the sons of the paternal uncle and the paternal aunt belong the two thirds, divided among them with the male receiving the equivalent of the share of two females.

Whoever among the kindred and the connections is nearer to the deceased excludes the one more remote. The paternal uncle excludes the cousin; the son of the maternal uncle and the maternal aunt excludes the cousin and the son of the maternal uncle; the paternal aunt excludes the cousin and the son of the maternal uncle; and the maternal aunt excludes the cousin and the son of the maternal aunt, because they have come to the deceased by one degree sooner. Whoever draws near through a full brother is more deserving than one who draws near through a brother through the father. To the kindred through the mother belongs their portion of the sixth that was the share of the one through whom they are related, divided among them, their males and their females alike. Whoever draws near through one who holds a share excludes whoever draws near through one who holds no share. When several groups each draw near through a different person who holds a share, each group receives the share of the one through whom they are related, as has already been said.

There is no awl in any fixed apportionment. One begins with the one whom Allah, mighty and majestic, has placed first. Whoever Allah, mighty and majestic, has named a fixed share when alone, and then is reduced in the presence of another, nothing is deducted from that second named share. So it is with the husband, the wife, the mother, and the father: nothing is deducted from the husband's quarter, nor from the wife's eighth, nor from either parent's sixth. Upon the one who holds the remainder falls the deficiency, and to him belongs the increase.

An example of this is a woman who dies leaving her husband, her brothers through the mother, and her sister through the father. Allah, mighty and majestic, named one half for the husband, since she has no child, and one half for the sister, and one third for the two maternal brothers, by the apparent reading of what He named. When the highest of the fixed shares were examined here, the matter was found bewildering, so some inflated it and turned the six shares into eight. But Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿إِنِ امْرُؤٌ هَلَكَ لَيْسَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَلَهُ أُخْتٌ فَلَهَا نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ وَهُوَ يَرِثُهَا إِن لَّمْ يَكُن لَّهَا وَلَدٌ﴾ "If a man dies having no child, and he has a sister, she receives half of what he left; and he inherits from her if she has no child" (al-Nisa 176). The Almighty thus made clear that this is so only in the case of the sister alone, not when others share with her. He also said: ﴿وَإِن كَانَ رَجُلٌ يُورَثُ كَلَالَةً أَوِ امْرَأَةٌ وَلَهُ أَخٌ أَوْ أُخْتٌ فَلِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِّنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ فَإِن كَانُوا أَكْثَرَ مِن ذَٰلِكَ فَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ فِي الثُّلُثِ﴾ "If a man dies leaving no children, and he has a brother or a sister, each of them receives one sixth; but if there are more than that, they are partners in one third" (al-Nisa 12). And they themselves say that the brother for whom Allah named the whole, were he in the place of the sister, would be given only one sixth. So in this case the husband receives one half, the maternal brothers one third, and the sister through the father one sixth.

Likewise, if she leaves a husband, two parents, and a daughter, the husband receives a quarter, the two parents receive two sixths, and what remains goes to the daughter, because were she a son she would receive no more than this. The deficiency falls upon the one to whom the surplus belongs. Upon this basis all the questions of awl proceed. As for those of them that mention brothers and sisters together with the mother, we have already said that they inherit nothing in her presence; so whatever questions of awl are reported are carried upon this meaning. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them all.

### On the inheritance of the son repudiated by li'an, the child of adultery, the foundling, the captive of unknown kin, the fetus, the Muslim, the disbeliever, the Magian, the apostate, and the bondservant

We have mentioned earlier the li'an and how it is carried out, and that whoever disavows a child his wife has borne, and who undertakes the li'an against her concerning it, the child is not attributed to him. The child's lineage is the lineage of his mother, and his inheritance belongs to his kindred through her. The one who disavowed him does not inherit from him, nor does anyone inherit through him, unless he has acknowledged him after the li'an. For we have mentioned that if a man undertakes the li'an against a child and then acknowledges him afterward, the child inherits from him, but the one who undertook the li'an against his mother does not inherit the child, nor does anyone inherit through that man; rather the child inherits, and those who draw near through his kinship inherit.

The child of adultery is not joined to the adulterer who lay with his mother and from whom she bore him. He is not attributed to him, and the two do not inherit from one another, whether he acknowledges him or denies him. His mother inherits from him and he inherits from her, and he draws near through her, and those related to her draw near to him through her; but no one draws near to him through his father, nor does he draw near to anyone through his father. As for the foundling who is found with neither known mother nor known father, he does not inherit and is not inherited from, and no one draws near to him through his parents.

The captive of unknown kin is one who is taken from the land of disbelief, and another is taken with him, or before him, or after him, and the two come to recognize one another in the abode of Islam and affirm their kinship, and they continue thus, affirming it, until one of them dies. The survivor then inherits from the deceased by the kinship that they affirmed during their lives, and they inherit from one another and draw near to one another by their kinship.

It is narrated from the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, that he said, "The people of two different faiths do not inherit from one another." So no people of two different faiths inherit from one another. The meaning of mutual inheritance is that each of these inherits from the other; in the language its form is reciprocal, and reciprocal action in the speech of the Arabs proceeds only from two agents, though the simple act may proceed from one. It is said that two men struck one another when each struck his companion, and that a man struck another when one struck the other. The Jew does not inherit from the Christian, nor the Christian from the Jew, nor the Christian and the Jew and the Magian from the Muslim. The Muslim, however, inherits from them by his kinship, as has come from the Imams, may the prayers of Allah be upon them, and the disbeliever does not exclude him. The narration upon this meaning is sound, for they do not inherit from one another in that the polytheists do not inherit from the Muslims, while the Muslims do inherit from them. The people of each faith inherit among themselves according to how the fixed shares run, and that is judged among them. If they bring their case before the Muslims in a matter of inheritance, it is judged according to what their religion and their faith prescribe; and if they do not raise it to the consideration of the Muslims, they are left alone and not interfered with.

The bondservant does not inherit from the free person, and the free person likewise does not inherit from the bondservant through kinship, and the wealth of the bondservant belongs to his master. Whoever embraces Islam from disbelief, or is freed from bondage, before the one who would inherit from him dies and before his inheritance is divided, has his portion of it; but if he is freed after the division, he has no portion in it. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them all.

### On the inheritance of the killer, the blood money, the intersexed person, those who drown together, the freed bondservant and his patron, the divorced woman, and the one who has no heir

Among these are questions of the fixed shares, and of one heir who acknowledges another heir. Whoever kills another, whether deliberately or by mistake, does not inherit from him, nor from his descendants, even if he is among those who would have deserved his inheritance had he not killed him. As for the parents, if they do not kill him, they inherit from him by the shares Allah, mighty and majestic, has fixed. A man inherits from his wife's blood money and a woman inherits from her husband's blood money, except for the brothers through the mother, for they inherit nothing from the blood money.

The intersexed person is one who has both a male organ like that of a man and a female organ like that of a woman. Such a one inherits and is inherited from, marries and is married, and is judged according to the passing of urine. If urine passes from the male organ, the person is a man, and the rulings of men apply: he marries women and men do not marry him, and he inherits and is inherited from according to the inheritance of men. If urine passes from the female organ, the person's ruling is the ruling of women, and she follows the course of women and does not follow the course of men in anything. If urine passes from both, one looks to which of the two it passes from first: if it precedes from the male organ, the ruling is the ruling of men; and if it precedes from the female organ, the ruling is the ruling of women. If it passes from both at once, or there is neither a male organ nor a female organ, then the matter is uncertain, and the judge casts lots over it. He writes on one card "the servant of Allah" and on another card "the handmaid of Allah," and he sets each card within a pellet of clay, and turns them about in his hand, and says, "O Allah, You know best this naming and how You created it; so bring forth the lot."

When a matter of inheritance cannot be settled by sight, recourse is had to the lot. The one who casts it takes the piece that lies in his hand and says: "O Allah, You know best about this person and how You created them, so bring out the arrow for the one You have made heir among them and the one You have appointed as ruler over the rulings." He then draws one of the two pieces of clay, neither of which has been seen, nor is it known what is enclosed within them; and whatever emerges determines the ruling that is applied. After this the urine is examined. The person is made to stand facing a wall, with the hands extended, and is told to stand back from the wall as far as can be managed. If the urine passes through to the wall, it is known to have issued from the male organ; if it runs down between the legs, it is known to have issued from the female organ. Neither men nor women look upon the person beforehand, so that no judgment is formed as to what the person is in advance of the test.

If a company of people drown together in a ship, or a house collapses upon them, or they are consumed together in a fire, or they are slain together in battle, and it is not known which of them died before his companion, and they were kin to one another, then each of them inherits from the other according to what he would have inherited, and the other in turn inherits from him according to his own portion. They inherit from one another whatever each has left behind. An illustration of this is two brothers, one the freed client (mawla) of one people and the other the freed client of another, who die together in such a manner, having no heir besides their respective patrons. One of them leaves a thousand dinars, the other a hundred. The owner of the hundred inherits from the thousand as though he had remained alive and his brother had died before him, taking his share from it; and the owner of the thousand inherits the hundred as though he had remained alive and his brother had died before him, taking his share from it. Thus there falls to the patron of the one who left the thousand a hundred, and to the patron of the one who left the hundred a thousand. In this manner the like of it proceeds: each patron inherits his deceased client, and is himself inherited from in turn, and thereafter the heirs of each inherit from him.

As for one who pronounces upon his wife an irrevocable divorce, after which he has no power of return over her, there is no inheritance between the two of them. The exception is the man who divorces her while he is ill: she inherits from him for as long as he remains in that same illness, unless he recovers from it or she herself remarries. If he becomes well again, or she takes another husband, she has no inheritance from him. As for one who divorces his wife with a divorce in which he retains the right of return, he inherits from her and she inherits from him, so long as her waiting period has not yet expired.

Whoever dies leaving no heir except the patrons (mawali) who freed him, or those who became patrons through their agency, his inheritance belongs to those patrons. But if he has a blood relative, his inheritance passes to that relative by right of kinship and not by right of patronage. The bond of patronage (al-wala) is inherited by men and women alike, and through the father who freed the slave its closeness is established, as is the case with kinship among men generally. Whoever is nearer in the degree of patronage has the better claim to it than one who is more remote, just as we mentioned in the chapter on patronage concerning the elder, which is to say the man who frees a slave. The freed man then dies leaving two sons; one of the two sons dies leaving a son of his own; and thereafter the patron dies with no near relative. His inheritance goes to the son of his patron's son, who has preceded the other by a degree, rather than to the son who is more remote. So too is it with inheritance by kinship. And whoever leaves no heir in the degree of an inheritor except a slave whom he had freed is inherited by that freed slave just as the freed slave would have been inherited by him. But if he leaves both a slave whom he had freed and a patron who had freed him, his inheritance goes to the patron who freed him rather than to the slave whom he freed.

If some of the heirs acknowledge another heir who is otherwise unknown, and for whom there stands no attested proof, and the remaining heirs deny him, then the one who is nearer inherits according to what he would have inherited. Where a portion of the heirs draw the claimant near, or judge him to be a relative, they hand over to him whatever remains in excess of their own shares, that is, of what has come to them; while those who do not acknowledge him owe him nothing, and their own inheritance remains complete. An illustration is a man who dies leaving two sons. One of them acknowledges a third brother, and the other denies him. The one who denies inherits one half from his father. The one who acknowledges inherits one third, and pays over one sixth to the one he has acknowledged, corresponding to what that one would have inherited had they been three brothers sharing in thirds.

The shroud of the deceased is taken from the principal of his estate, from whatever he has left, before any division is made, and likewise his debt, if a debt lies against him. A bequest, if he has made one, is taken from a third of the estate or less; beyond that it is not carried out without the consent of the heirs, unless they elect to discharge it in full. Whatever remains thereafter is divided according to the ordained obligations of inheritance. As for the mukatab, the slave bound by a written contract of manumission (kitabah), and the one part of whom has been set free, such persons inherit and are inherited from in proportion to the measure of freedom they have attained, as we have already mentioned. The ruling upon the soundness of the kitabah, and how the intention of manumission takes effect, has been set out in the chapter on the mukatab. Whoever has no known heir, his inheritance goes to the treasury of the Muslims.

If some of the heirs acknowledge a debt, each who acknowledges it is bound to pay it from his share of the inheritance, in proportion to what he has acknowledged. And if he bears witness to it, being a man of just standing, his testimony is admitted on behalf of all the heirs. And if a man acknowledges a child from his slave-woman and his wife, and then afterward disowns him, his disavowal is not permitted: the child is his son. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may (عليهم السلام).

When the heirs of a slain man are of differing condition, some present and some absent, some grown and some still children, the right of retribution belongs to those who have reached maturity. The killer is not held back until the young ones come of age, nor until those who are absent return. So it was that al-Hasan ibn Ali, (ع), put to death the enemy of Allah, Ibn Muljam, may Allah curse him, the man who struck Ali, (ع), although Ali's own children had not yet reached the age of puberty.

As for one who is slain, whether by an iron weapon or by anything else: if the killer intended the death of his victim and struck him deliberately with whatever he struck him, and the victim died of that blow, then the guardians of the slain may demand retaliation. Yet retaliation is carried out by no means other than the sword, for the Messenger of Allah, may the (ع), said: "There is no execution except by the sword" (لَا قَوَدَ إِلَّا بِحَدِيدَةٍ). The condemned is to be struck upon the neck with the sword; he is neither to be tortured nor to be mutilated.

It is permitted to question the one accused of the killing and to draw out his confession gently, without striking him. If he is beaten and confesses under that duress, his confession does not bind him, for the admission of one compelled has no legal force. But if two upright witnesses testify that he committed the killing, or if he confesses of his own free will, then the killing is established against him. The testimony of women is not admitted in cases of bloodshed, nor in any of the prescribed punishments (hudud) or in retaliation (qisas), though it is admitted in matters where wealth is owed.

If a single man is slain by two, or by a company who joined together against him, each of them striking him until he died of their blows, then the guardians of the slain may choose one of them and put him to death for their kinsman, for Allah Almighty has said: *"A soul for a soul"* (النَّفْسَ بِالنَّفْسِ). They may not put more than one man to death in return for one. Thereafter the guardians of the slain turn to the remaining killers and take from each of them blood money in proportion to his share. If they were three and one was put to death, his guardians take from the remaining two two-thirds of the blood money. If they were two and one was put to death, his guardians take half the blood money from the other. If they were more than this, the shares are reckoned accordingly, and the rest are made to bear a punishment as well.

If one man restrains another while a third kills him, then the one who killed is put to death, and the one who held the victim is imprisoned until he dies, after he has been flogged. If a man kills his own slave, he is given a painful punishment. If the guardians pardon the killer, there is no claim against him, and whoever kills him after that pardon is himself put to death in return. Likewise, if they accept blood money from him, they are entitled to nothing beyond it, for Allah Almighty has said: *"And whoever transgresses after that, for him is a painful punishment"* (فَمَنِ اعْتَدَى بَعْدَ ذَلِكَ فَلَهُ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ).

Every wound for which retaliation can be reached without danger of excess and without harm to the one from whom it is taken, such as the surface wound (al-mudihah) and what lies below it, is subject to retaliation. The wound is measured along its length upon the skin, a mark is set, and the one taking retaliation cuts in the corresponding place to the like of it, and to the like of its depth in the flesh.

So too a tooth is drawn for a tooth, an eye put out for an eye, a nose severed for a nose, an ear for an ear, or whatever portion of them has been cut away, in accordance with the word of Allah Almighty: *"And the eye for the eye, and the nose for the nose, and the ear for the ear, and the tooth for the tooth, and for wounds is retaliation"* (وَالْعَيْنَ بِالْعَيْنِ وَالْأَنْفَ بِالْأَنْفِ وَالْأُذُنَ بِالْأُذُنِ وَالسِّنَّ بِالسِّنِّ وَالْجُرُوحَ قِصَاصٌ). This is when the act was deliberate. But if the wound was inflicted in error, or if its nature is such that retaliation would risk further harm to the offender, or if the wound has dried and healed, or if a bone has been broken, or if it is a wound greater than the surface wound, then in all of these blood money is owed, and we shall mention it in its proper place. Whoever inflicts such wounds deliberately is also punished, lest a man of means find it an easy thing to do and simply discharge the blood money for it.

Retaliation is taken from a woman for a man in wounds, and from a man for a woman in the surface wound and what is below it. As for retaliation in the eye, the eye that is not to suffer retaliation is bound, an iron or a mirror is heated, and it is brought near to the eye from which retaliation is to be taken; the eye is held open until it flows. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them all.

### On Blood Money and Its Categories

The blood money for a free Muslim man, whether young or old, is one thousand dinars upon those who deal in gold, or its equivalent value upon the people of every other kind of wealth. They are not compelled to sell what they possess in order to pay in gold; rather the equivalent of one thousand dinars is taken from them in the currency of their land: from the people of silver, the value of one thousand dinars in it; from the people of camels, one hundred camels; from the people of cattle, two hundred cows; from the people of sheep, one thousand sheep; and from the people of cloth, two hundred garments of the like value. By this the Sunnah has run, and this is the blood money for the soul.

Blood money is owed only in the case of error, which is that the killer did not intend the death of the one he killed, as when he meant to cast at another and struck this man instead. But if he acted deliberately with whatever he used, that is intentional killing, and he is subject to retaliation, unless the guardians of the slain accept blood money from him or pardon him. The one who killed in error must, after paying the blood money, expiate by freeing a believing slave; if he cannot find one, then by fasting two consecutive months; and if he is unable to do that, then by feeding sixty poor persons.

The blood money is borne by his aqila, who are the heirs that would inherit from him were he to die, save his maternal aunts, for they neither bear this responsibility nor inherit anything of the blood money. The deliberate acts of children and of the insane are treated as error, and the blood money for them falls upon the aqila, but nothing is laid upon the poor among them, nor upon one who possesses nothing. The aqila is not bound except by what is established through clear evidence; but as for one in good standing who confesses and acts upon it, and as for what involves a slave, the aqila bears nothing of it. The aqila does not bear unintentional offenses except where the amount reaches a third of the blood money or more; whatever is less than a third is paid from the offender's own wealth. As for the people of the dhimma, what they incur is from their own wealth, and their aqila bears nothing of it; whoever offends alone bears it alone.

Whoever strikes a woman who has been with her husband so that she casts forth the seed from the womb after it had settled there owes twenty dinars. If she casts forth a clot, which is the blood (alaqah), there are forty dinars in it. For the morsel of flesh (mudghah), once it has become flesh, there are sixty dinars. If bone has formed within it, there are eighty dinars. If she casts it forth as a fully formed child before the spirit has been breathed into it, there are one hundred dinars in it; and once the spirit has been breathed into it, the full blood money is due.

Whoever offends against one already dead, severing his head or doing to him what, were it done to him alive, would have killed him, owes one hundred dinars; and for whatever wound or other injury is done to him, the offender owes the reckoned share of his blood money. That sum is spent on his behalf in the works of piety, and the Hajj is performed for him if he had not yet performed it, and his heirs inherit nothing of it. As for offenses against beasts, they are reckoned by their price: whoever kills a beast is fined its value, and whoever wounds it or breaks a limb of it owes the amount by which its value has fallen. If it breaks loose and causes harm, the harm is without redress; and likewise if one enters upon it in its place. But if its owners loose it or station it where they have no right, they are answerable for whatever harm it causes.

When a thief seeks a man's wealth and fights him, and the man so threatened kills him, the thief's blood is forfeit. Whoever is slain in defense of his wealth is a martyr; and if he yields his wealth and does not fight for it, there is no blame upon him. Whoever seeks to overcome a woman against herself, and she repels him and kills or wounds him, whatever she inflicts upon him is without redress. Whoever dies under a prescribed punishment (hadd) or under lawful retaliation, there is no claim for him. Whoever peers into the private quarters of a people and they put out his eye, his claim is forfeit.

The Messenger of Allah, may the (ع), ruled by al-Qasamah, which applies when a man is killed and his killer is not known. If, before he dies, he says, "So-and-so killed me," or if his guardians come forward bearing a mark of accusation against the one they charge with his death, saying "He killed him," then fifty men of them swear oaths that he killed him, and so the accused is given over to them. If they do not number fifty, those who are present repeat their oaths until they complete fifty oaths. If they wish, they may have the accused swear oaths instead. And if the accused bring proof of their innocence, it is accepted from them.

When a slain man is found in a village and it is not known who killed him, and its people are the people of that one village with no others mixing among them, they swear that they did not kill him and do not know his killer, and then they are made to pay his blood money. If he is found between two villages, the responsibility falls upon the nearer of them to the place where he lay. But if the place in which he was struck down is a great city, or a village in which all manner of people mix, or upon a road along which travelers pass between villages, then the burden of proof falls upon whoever is accused among them, if no mark of accusation is found against them, and al-Qasamah is established.

If two horsemen collide, each having driven toward the other, and one of them dies, then half the blood money is due, for he died of his own act together with the act of his fellow. If the act came from one of them while the other was standing still or walking, and the one who charged died, then nothing is owed by the one who stood still; but if the one who was struck died, his blood money is upon the one who charged, if he did not intend it, and if he did intend it, retaliation is upon him.

The physician and the veterinarian are answerable for what they bring about if they were ignorant of their craft; and if they were knowledgeable yet intended harm, they are answerable likewise. The circumciser is answerable for whatever he cuts of the boy's flesh beyond the place of cutting, and the woman who performs the cutting upon girls is the same when she exceeds the place of the cut. Whoever digs a well or a pit in a road, or places something in it, when the road is a public thoroughfare, is answerable for whatever harm is suffered there; but if the road lies within his own right, there is nothing upon him. A rider is answerable for whatever his beast strikes with its foreleg or its chest, but not for what it strikes with its hind leg, since that is not within his control. When a wall leans and its owner comes to know of it, or it is brought to his notice, yet he neither sets it right nor pulls it down until it falls, he is answerable for whatever it strikes. When a man hires a laborer who then perishes at his work, nothing is owed for him. When a dog is kept in the dwelling of a people or within their right, and someone enters upon it without their leave and it bites him, there is nothing upon them; but if they gave him leave to enter, they are answerable.

When a woman severs a man's organ, or a man severs a woman's part, there is no retaliation between them, for there is in this no like of the offense to take from the offender; rather the blood money in full is upon him, and he is severely punished if he acted deliberately. When a man falls upon a woman whose like is not fit to be approached, or uses force upon a woman and tears her, so that, brought to such a state, she does not bear children, or loses control of her water or her bowels, or dies of his violence against her, then in all of this the full blood money is upon him. Whoever is found dead bearing no mark, and it is not known that anyone killed him, nothing is owed for him save the oath of the one accused of it. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may the prayers of Allah be upon them.

### On Offenses Against the Limbs and Wounds of the Head

We have mentioned above that retaliation is taken for the deliberate act in every case where retaliation can be reached and excess in it is guarded against, and where no harm is feared for the one from whom it is taken. But as for that which cannot be brought to true retaliation, and where the safety of the offender cannot be assured, the blood money for it is upon the offender if the act was deliberate, and upon the aqila if it was in error.

For every part of which a person has but one, the full blood money is owed; and for every part of which there are two, half the blood money is owed for each. For the hair of the head, if it is plucked, or falls out from injury, or is burned and does not grow back, the full blood money is owed; and likewise for the skin of the head if it is flayed. If the forehead is struck and the bone set again, there are one hundred dinars in it. If the nose or the tongue is severed, or an offense is committed against the beard so that it does not grow back, in each of these the full blood money is owed. For the eyebrow, the eye, the ear, the hand, the foot, and one of the two organs of generation, half the blood money is owed for each one of them, and for both together the full blood money. For the eye of a one-eyed man, the full blood money. For hearing, if it is lost, the full blood money; for speech, if it is lost, the full blood money; and for sight, the full blood money, even if the pupils themselves do not perish. For whatever is diminished of all of these, it is reckoned by its share.

If a man strikes another or offends against him so that some of the sight of one of his eyes is lost, the sound eye is bound and the other left, and an egg is taken, emptied of what is within it, and hung by a hair. The injured man is told to look at it, and it is drawn away from him until he says, "I no longer see the hair," and that place is marked. He is measured from where he does not know its measuring, and this is then done from four directions; and if the measurements agree, his truthfulness is known. Then his sound eye is bound and the injured eye left, and the same is done to him from four directions; and if his account agrees, well and good, but if not, he is told, "You have lied; return to the truth." If his account holds true and is consistent upon the measuring from four directions, one looks to what the injured eye has lost of its seeing as against the sound eye, and he is given of the blood money the like of what was lost: if it is a half, then a half; if a third, then a third; whether less or more, it is reckoned accordingly.

If what was lost of his sight is from both his eyes together, he is measured in the same way against the seeing of an upright man of middling sight, and of two and three such men. When they agree upon it, or come near to agreement, his own sight is measured against it from the four directions, as we have described; and when the measurements are even and his truthfulness is known, he is given of the blood money according to what was lost, as we have set forth. If the loss is in the hearing, the like of this is done to him: a dirham is struck near him until he says, "I do not hear it," and he is measured as we have described for the two eyes, for the one ear and for both ears. If it is in the sense of smell, something bearing a scent is brought near to him and then withdrawn in the same way, by the reckoning of what is done for sight and hearing, and he is given according to his share. And if the loss is in speech, one looks to what has fallen away from him of the letters, against the full count of the letters,

As for the man whose speech has been injured, the letters he can no longer pronounce are counted against the full alphabet, which numbers twenty-eight letters, and blood money is owed to him in proportion to what he has lost of speech. For the lips, half the blood money is due for the upper lip, and two-thirds of the blood money for the lower lip, that is, a half and a sixth, reckoned from its share in eating and in the softness of speech. For the teeth set at the front of the mouth, which are four incisors, four lateral incisors, and four canines, each tooth carries fifty dinars. For the teeth at the back of the mouth, which are the molars, each carries twenty-five dinars, and together they complete the full blood money. The molars number four in each jaw, four above and four below, which is the complete count of the teeth in the perfection of natural creation. Some people have five molars in each jaw, the additional one being superfluous; the blood money is apportioned according to the perfect original count, and whatever is lost of them, whether superfluous or not, carries twenty-five dinars. If part of a tooth is broken, blood money is owed in proportion to what is broken. If a tooth turns black, its full blood money is due, for it has been ruined. For a child's tooth knocked out before its natural time to fall, ten dinars are owed, and five dinars for his molar, if it grows back afterward; but if it does not grow back, the full blood money for it is due.

For the shoulder, the upper arm, and the elbow, if any one of them is broken or dislocated and then heals without defect, one hundred dinars are owed for each. For the forearm and the wrist, one-third of the blood money for each. For every finger of the hands and the feet, one hundred dinars. For the shoulder blade, forty dinars. For the ribs joined to the chest, twenty-five dinars for each rib; and for the ribs that are not joined to it and do not meet the breastbone, ten dinars for each rib. For the hip, the thigh, and the knee, one-fifth of the blood money for each. For the ja'ifa, which is a blow that penetrates into the inner cavity of the body, one-third of the blood money. For a blow to the face that leaves it red without drawing blood, and without turning black or green, two and a half dinars; and if it turns black or green, three dinars. If the blow is around the eye and turns green, three dinars; and if it turns black, six dinars.

For the damiya, the wound in which blood flows, ten dinars. For the faqira, which breaks the skin and reaches the flesh without cutting it, twelve dinars. For the badi'a, which cuts the skin and enters the flesh, severing some of it, twenty dinars. For the mutalahima, which cuts deep into the flesh, thirty dinars. For the mishaq, also called the miltat, which cuts through the skin and all the flesh and reaches the membrane over the bone without cutting it, forty dinars. For the mudiha, which cuts through all of that and lays the bone bare, fifty dinars. For the hashima, which does all of that and breaks the bone, one hundred dinars. For the munaqqila, which breaks the bone and draws out a fragment of it, one hundred and fifty dinars. For the ma'muma, in which the membrane of the brain becomes visible and is reached without further injury beyond it, one-third of the blood money. All of this applies to wounds of the head and the face. For wounds to the rest of the body, half of these amounts apply. As for an injury to one of the limbs, such as the fingers and their like, blood money is owed in proportion to its share of the limb's full blood money, as we have already mentioned. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may (عليهم السلام).

### On the Establishment of the Hudud

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: "And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah has wronged himself." (Surah al-Talaq, 65:1)

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family and grant them peace, forbade the neglect of the prescribed limits, and said: "The Children of Israel perished only because, when a man of noble standing among them committed an offense that incurred a limit, they would not carry it out against him, but when a lowly man committed it, they would establish it upon him." A woman of Quraysh stole a garment in the time of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, and he ordered her hand to be cut off. A group of Quraysh gathered to him and said: "O Messenger of Allah, she is a woman of ours who holds honor among her people, and what she has stolen is a trifle." The Messenger of Allah said: "It was by the like of this that those before you were destroyed: they would establish the limit upon their weak and leave their strong and their nobles, and so they perished."

He, may Allah bless him and his family, forbade intercession in the prescribed limits, and said: "Whoever intercedes to nullify one of the limits of Allah has been insolent toward Allah and has striven to undo His limits; and whoever is insolent toward Allah and strives to undo His limits, Allah, Mighty and Majestic, will punish him on the Day of Resurrection." Safwan ibn Umayyah brought to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, a man who had stolen a cloak of his, and the Messenger of Allah ordered the man's hand to be cut off. Safwan said: "O Messenger of Allah, is his hand to be cut off for the sake of a cloak? I have given it to him." The Messenger of Allah said to him: "Why was that not before you brought him to me? Once the limit has been raised to the ruler, it is not fitting for him to leave it unenforced." And he ordered it, and the hand was cut off.

It is related from him, may Allah bless him and his family, that he said: "The back of the believer is inviolable, except for a limit that is due." He forbade that anyone exceed a limit from among the limits of Allah, whether by falling short of it or by going beyond what has been prescribed, and he said: "Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has made the limits clear, so whoever transgresses them has wronged himself, as He, exalted be His remembrance, has said." And he set a limit upon whoever transgresses a limit. He, may Allah bless him and his family, said: "The most hateful of creation to Allah is one who bares the back of a Muslim without right." He forbade the carrying out of the prescribed limits inside the mosques. It is related from him that he said: "Whoever commits a sin and is punished for it in this world, Allah is more just than to repeat the punishment upon His servant in the Hereafter; and whoever commits a sin and Allah conceals it for him in this world, He is more generous than to return to something He has already pardoned and concealed."

Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, wrote to Rifa'a ibn Shaddad, his judge over al-Ahwaz, a letter in which he said: "Establish the limits upon the near, that the distant may shun them; do not let blood be shed in vain, and do not let the limits be neglected." When Uthman was brought al-Waleed ibn Utbah, and witnesses testified against him, and the offense he had committed against the Muslims was made plain, the Muslims spoke of it and said that the limits of Allah had been neglected. So al-Waleed was placed in a room at the side of the house, and it was said: "Let whoever holds that the limit is due upon him rise and carry it out." Yet whenever a man came in to flog him, al-Waleed would say: "I adjure you by Allah not to sever my kinship, and not to expose me before the Commander of the Faithful," and the man would turn back from him. When Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, saw that a limit from among the limits of Allah was being neglected, he took up the whip and went in to him, and al-Waleed said the like to him. Al-Husayn ibn Ali, may (عليهم السلام) both, followed him in and said: "O my father, what is between you and him? Leave him be; you see what the people have made of his case." But Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, pushed against the chest of al-Husayn, (ع), and spoke sharply to him so that he withdrew, and he took the whip and flogged al-Waleed until the limit was carried out in full.

It is related from him, (ع), that he said: "Three things, if you do them, no affliction will descend upon you: that you wage struggle against your enemy; that you raise your limits to your imams and let them judge in them with justice; and that you submit to their command." Umar said to him: "Counsel me, O Abu al-Hasan." He said: "I counsel you to judge by the Book of Allah in contentment and in anger, to apportion with justice between the red and the black, and to establish the limits upon the distant and the near alike." Umar said to him: "You have been brief and you have reached the heart of it."

He, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, used to review the prisons every Friday, and whoever was found to have a limit due against him, he would carry it out and not delay it. Three men once testified before him against a man for adultery, and he asked: "Where is the fourth?" but the fourth was not present. They said: "We will bring him after this, O Commander of the Faithful." He said: "There is no waiting even an hour in the limits," and he flogged the three with the limit for false accusation. A people came to him and said: "O Commander of the Faithful, we have come to ask you for a need." He said: "Ask me for nothing that I possess but that I will give it to you." They had come to ask him concerning a man of Banu Asad upon whom a limit had become due, and they said: "So-and-so, grant him to us." But he brought the man out and carried out the limit upon him. They said: "Did you not promise us, O Commander of the Faithful, to release him?" He said: "I promised you what I possess; but this belongs to Allah, and I do not possess it." He, (ع), ordered his servant Qanbar to flog a man the prescribed limit, and Qanbar, in his anger, gave him three lashes more. When this was raised to him, Ali, (ع), granted the man retaliation against Qanbar, and flogged Qanbar three lashes. And he, (ع), wrote to Rifa'a: "Shield the believer as much as you are able, for his back is a sanctuary of Allah, and his soul is honored before Allah, and to him belongs the reward of Allah."

It is related from him, (ع), that he said concerning a people who held out in the territory of the enemy and asked to be given a covenant that they would not be called to account for anything that had passed: "Do not grant a covenant that would set aside a limit from among the limits of Allah. How can the limits be lifted from those to whom they apply, when it is the friends of Allah and those who strive in His path who go forth to establish them?" It is related from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, that he said: "Pardon nothing of the limits that belong to Allah, Mighty and Majestic. Whoever comes to know of one of them, nothing is fitting for him but to raise it to the Imam. As for the rights of people within the limits, there is no harm in pardoning them before the matter reaches the ruler, as with the thief before he is brought to the Imam; but it is not permissible to pardon after it has reached him. And whoever pardons a limit that is his and then wishes to enforce it has no right to do so." It is related from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, that he said: "Whoever seizes a thief and pardons him, there is no harm in it. But once the thief is raised to the Sultan, let his hand be cut off. If the one from whom he stole says, I grant him to him, the limit is not averted from him, for the pardon would have held before the raising; but once it is raised to the ruler, the limit is not neglected, by the word of Allah, Mighty and Majestic: and those who keep the limits of Allah. Once the limit has been raised to the Imam, it is not for anyone to leave it."

### On the Hadd Punishment for Zina

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: "And do not approach adultery; indeed it is an indecency and an evil way." (Surah al-Isra, 17:32) And He said: "And those who do not call upon another god alongside Allah, nor kill the soul that Allah has forbidden except in truth, nor commit adultery; and whoever does that will meet a penalty. The punishment will be multiplied for him on the Day of Resurrection, and he will abide in it humiliated, except for whoever repents." (Surah al-Furqan, 25:68-70)

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, said: "The wrath of Allah grows severe against a woman who brings into a people one who is not of them, so that he gazes upon what is sacred to them and treads upon their beds. And the most severely punished of people on the Day of Resurrection is the one who plants his seed in a womb forbidden to him." He, may Allah bless him and his family, also said: "The adulterer will stand on the Day of Resurrection above the people of the Fire, and a drop will fall from his private part by which the people of Hell will be tormented, and they will say to the keepers, What is this foul stench? and it will be said to them, This is the smell of an adulterer. And an adulteress will be brought, and a drop will fall from her, and the people of the Fire will be tormented by her stench." He, may Allah bless him and his family, said: "There is no sin greater before Allah, Mighty and Majestic, after associating partners with Him, than unlawful seed that a man places in a womb not lawful to him." And he said: "Adultery and goodness do not gather in a single house." And he delivered a sermon, may Allah bless him and his family, in which he said: "Three whom Allah will not speak to on the Day of Resurrection, nor purify, and for whom there is a painful punishment: an adulterous old man, a tyrant king, and a proud pauper." And he said: "The adulterer does not commit adultery while he is a believer."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad, may the blessings of Allah be upon him, said: "When he lies upon her belly, the spirit of faith departs from him; and when he rises and seeks the forgiveness of Allah, it returns to him." Adultery is the placing of the private part in one who is neither a spouse nor lawfully possessed by a man's right hand. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: "And those who guard their private parts, except from their spouses or those whom their right hands possess, for then they are not to be blamed; but whoever seeks beyond that, those are the transgressors." (Surah al-Mu'minun, 23:5-7)

When an adulterer is found, and four men of just standing testify against him that they saw him commit adultery with a woman, having seen the organ enter her private part, while he had a wife in his lawful bond with whom he had consummated marriage and the woman had a husband in her lawful bond; or when the man and the woman both confess; or when one of them confesses and the testimony of witnesses is established against the other, then each of them, whether the one who confessed or the one against whom the proof was given, receives one hundred lashes. They are then taken out, and a pit is dug for each, reaching to the navel, and each is set into it standing and packed in. If the matter was a confession, the judge before whom the confession was made begins, and orders the stoning; then he orders the people, and they stone with stones of moderate size, such as fill the palm or less, until they die. They are then brought out, washed, prayed over, and buried.

The confession of an adulterer is not established until he confesses on four separate occasions, while of sound mind, willing, and not coerced. If the matter is the testimony of witnesses, the witnesses cast the first stones, then the judge, then the people. A man is not counted as protected until he has consummated marriage with his wife and she is with him; a bondwoman does not render a free man protected, nor a bondman a free woman. If one of the two is protected and the other is not, the protected one is stoned after being flogged, and the one who is not protected is flogged one hundred lashes. The most severe flogging is the flogging of the adulterer, then the flogging of the false accuser, then the flogging of the drinker, then the flogging of discretionary punishment. A pregnant woman is not flogged until she has delivered what is in her womb and is purified of her postnatal bleeding. There is no limit upon a child, neither boy nor girl; but if an adult commits this sin with one of them, the limit is established upon the adult.

When a single child is claimed by more than one man, the elder of the claimants takes precedence, and whoever withheld the child from one of them bears the responsibility for what was done. As for the act of the people of Lot, the one who performs the act upon another is stoned just as the two who commit adultery are stoned. If the act is intercrural, the hadd falls due upon them both. For the act committed between women, each of the two receives a hundred lashes.

Whoever commits adultery with a relative whom it is unlawful for him to marry is put to death, and she too is put to death if she consented to him, provided they are unmarried. If they are protected by marriage, the hadd is applied and they are stoned. Whoever forces a woman against her will is put to death if he is unmarried, and is flogged the hadd and stoned if he is protected by marriage. No hadd falls upon the woman who was coerced, and if he has taken her virginity she is owed a dowry equal to that of her peers. If she is a slave girl, she is owed a tenth of her price; if she is a non-virgin, half a tenth of her price.

There is no hadd upon one who has lost his reason, and no hadd in an unlawful union that bears the outward form of a marriage, such as a man who marries a woman during her waiting period, or upon a contract that does not meet the requirements of a valid marriage. If two men are found together under a single cover, or a man and a woman, the hadd is applied. If two women are found together in like manner, they are flogged with a punishment short of the hadd of stoning. If a slave or a slave girl commits adultery, each of them receives fifty lashes, whether or not they are protected by marriage.

When the adulterer is brought forward to be flogged, witnesses from among the believers are commanded to attend the punishment, as Allah Almighty says:

> ﴿وَلْيَشْهَدْ عَذَابَهُمَا طَائِفَةٌ مِّنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ﴾
>
> "And let a group of the believers witness their punishment." (al-Nur 2)

The adulterer is flogged in the condition in which he is found. If he is naked, he is left naked; if he is clothed, he is left in his clothing and struck over it. Nothing that would shield him from the effect of the blows, such as a thick fur or the like, is left upon him. The woman is seated and kept covered with a garment, and every limb of her is struck except the face, the private parts, and the vital organs. If striking the back alone is what is practiced, that is permissible, and it is what is customarily done. The blows shall not be such as to kill, and neither a woman in her post-natal bleeding nor a menstruating woman is struck until she is purified.

If the adulterer and the adulteress confess to adultery and then retract, they are left alone until they have confessed four times; but if they retract after that, their retraction is not accepted. Whoever commits adultery with his wife's mother receives the hadd. If a man has intercourse with a slave woman in whom he holds a share, he is flogged in proportion to the share that is not his, and the hadd is averted from him in proportion to his own share. Likewise, if she is his slave woman contracted to purchase her freedom, he is struck in proportion to the part of her that has been freed, and the hadd is averted from him in proportion to the part still owned.

Whoever among men imitates women in his manner is disciplined; and if he persists in that, he is banished from the dwellings. Whoever commits an act with an animal is punished for it, and the price of the beast is paid to its owner. If the beast is of a kind that is eaten, it is slaughtered and buried, and it is not lawful to eat it. If it is not of a kind that is eaten, it is sold and removed to where it will not be recognized, and the one who did this is reproached for it. A slave under a contract of manumission is flogged with the hadd of a free man in proportion to the part of him that has been freed, and with the hadd of a slave in proportion to the part still in bondage. All of this is the unanimous teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, may (عليهم السلام).

### The Hadd Punishment for Slander

Allah Almighty said:

> ﴿إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَرْمُونَ الْمُحْصَنَاتِ الْغَافِلَاتِ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ لُعِنُوا فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ﴾
>
> "Indeed, those who accuse chaste, heedless, believing women are cursed in this world and the Hereafter, and for them is a great punishment." (al-Nur 23)

And He said:

> ﴿وَالَّذِينَ يَرْمُونَ الْمُحْصَنَاتِ ثُمَّ لَمْ يَأْتُوا بِأَرْبَعَةِ شُهَدَاءَ فَاجْلِدُوهُمْ ثَمَانِينَ جَلْدَةً وَلَا تَقْبَلُوا لَهُمْ شَهَادَةً أَبَدًا وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْفَاسِقُونَ ۝ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ تَابُوا مِن بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ وَأَصْلَحُوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ﴾
>
> "And those who accuse chaste women and then do not produce four witnesses, flog them with eighty lashes and never accept their testimony thereafter; it is they who are the defiantly disobedient, except those who repent after that and make amends, for indeed Allah is Forgiving and Merciful." (al-Nur 4 to 5)

It is narrated from Ali, (ع), that he said: "The major sins are associating partners with Allah Almighty, killing a believer while under covenant, fleeing from the battle line except as one wheeling to fight or withdrawing to join his own troop, consuming usury after the proof of its prohibition has been established, devouring the wealth of the orphan unjustly, reverting to the life of the desert after the Emigration, and accusing chaste, heedless, believing women."

It is narrated from Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), that he said: "Whoever falsely accuses a believing man or a believing woman of something that is not in them, Allah Almighty casts him into the clay of corruption until he comes forth from what he said." He was asked about a man who slanders a chaste Muslim woman, and he said: "The hadd is established against him once it is proven against him, and his repentance is that he declare himself a liar before the people and turn back from what he did. If he does this, his testimony is accepted thereafter."

The slanderer is flogged eighty lashes. If he slanders a group and they come against him together, he receives a single hadd for all of them; but if they come one after another, he receives a hadd for each of them. If the one slandered, whether a man or a woman, confesses to what was said of them, or the evidence of it is established against them as Allah Almighty has indicated, then there is no hadd upon the slanderer. Whoever slanders a polytheist incurs no hadd, unless the polytheist has a Muslim child, in which case the hadd is applied for the sanctity of Islam. It does not befit a Muslim to slander a polytheist nor anyone else.

If a free man slanders a slave, he is disciplined but is not given the full hadd. If a slave slanders a free man, the full hadd is applied. If a dhimmi slanders a Muslim, the hadd is applied and he is made an example. If a man slanders another man and that man slanders him in return, and they bring the matter before the authority, both are given a discretionary punishment. Whoever says to his wife, "I did not find you a virgin," incurs no hadd.

Whoever has incurred one hadd and is then falsely accused of another, the one who slandered him is flogged the hadd. Whoever acknowledges a child and then denies him is flogged the hadd, the child's lineage is fixed upon him, and there is no hadd for mere insinuation. Whoever insults the Prophet, may the (ع), is put to death. Whoever insults any of the Imams, (عليهم السلام), is likewise put to death. Whoever slanders in any tongue has the hadd imposed upon him. Whoever slanders another and then pardons the one he accused before raising the matter to the authority, his pardon is valid, and he no longer retains the right to pursue it; but if he has already raised the matter to the authority and petitioned against him, no pardon is accepted thereafter.

If someone slanders a child or one who has lost his reason, nothing is due from them, though the child is disciplined so that he will not grow bold in slander. If a man or a woman slanders their own child, there is no hadd upon them; but if the child slanders them, the hadd is applied for their sake. If a man says to another, "O sodomite," there is no hadd upon him, because he has only ascribed him to Lot, (ع); but if he says, "O you who do the work of the people of Lot," then the hadd becomes obligatory upon him. If a man slanders an absent or a deceased woman, her guardian may rise against him on her behalf, and the hadd is for him.

Whoever denies a man his lineage incurs the hadd. Whoever reviles a man or a woman is given a discretionary punishment on their behalf, as when he says, "O immoral one," "O malicious one," "son of a Christian," "son of a Zoroastrian," and the like from cursing and reviling. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام).

### The Punishment for Intoxicants and Wine, and Related Rulings of the Hudud

Allah Almighty said:

> ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنَّمَا الْخَمْرُ وَالْمَيْسِرُ وَالْأَنْصَابُ وَالْأَزْلَامُ رِجْسٌ مِّنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَانِ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ﴾
>
> "O you who believe, wine, gambling, idols, and divining arrows are only a defilement from the work of Satan, so avoid it, that you may be successful." (al-Ma'idah 90)

The Messenger of Allah, may the (ع), said: "May Allah curse wine, the one who presses it, the one who has it pressed, its seller, its buyer, the one who pours it, the one who consumes its price, its drinker, the one who carries it, and the one to whom it is carried." And he said: "There is no one who drinks wine in this world and then drinks it in the Enclosure of Holiness." Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali, (ع), said: "One addicted to wine meets Allah Almighty, when he meets Him, as one who worships an idol." And whoever drinks a single draught of it, Allah does not accept a prayer from him for forty nights. The Messenger of Allah, may the (ع), said: "Every intoxicant is forbidden, and whatever intoxicates in a large quantity, a small quantity of it is forbidden as well."

So whoever drinks a little or a great deal of wine has the hadd applied to him. If he confesses to drinking it a second time, he is struck again; and if he drinks it a third time, he is put to death, though he is not put to death until the hadd has been administered upon him twice. Whoever drinks an intoxicant and becomes so intoxicated that he does not know his own state is flogged the hadd of eighty lashes. If he returns to it after being struck once, he is struck again; and if he returns after being struck twice, he is put to death on the third occasion. If he drinks of it but does not become intoxicated, he is given a discretionary punishment; and if he returns to it, he is punished with a painful chastisement. Whoever drinks wine or an intoxicant not knowing that it is forbidden incurs no hadd. Whoever acknowledges drinking wine and then denies it is still given the hadd, and no regard is paid to his denial. The hadd for a slave in the matter of wine is eighty lashes, the same as the hadd for a free man. If the people of the dhimma openly drink wine or an intoxicant, they are struck just as the Muslims are struck, for they were given their covenant only on the condition that they not openly display anything forbidden in the religion of Islam.

If a doubt arises concerning a hadd, it is suspended. There is no surety in a hadd, no testimony built upon testimony, no oath, and no judge's letter to another judge taken as a basis. No hadd is carried out on the strength of intimidation, nor on a confession extracted under duress, and for that purpose a man is neither imprisoned nor beaten. If several hadd punishments fall due upon a single person, all of them are carried out; and if death is among them, those short of death are carried out first, then he is put to death. A man may not carry out a hadd upon his own slave except by the permission of the authority. If a hadd becomes due upon someone and its measure is not specified, he is beaten until the one striking him desists. Whoever is testified against for a hadd is held until the evidence is duly established; and once it is established and the hadd is applied, he is not held in detention.

### The Rulings Concerning Thieves and Brigands

Allah Almighty said:

> ﴿وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا أَمْوَالَكُم بَيْنَكُم بِالْبَاطِلِ﴾
>
> "And do not consume one another's wealth among yourselves through falsehood." (al-Baqarah 188)

And He said:

> ﴿وَالسَّارِقُ وَالسَّارِقَةُ فَاقْطَعُوا أَيْدِيَهُمَا﴾
>
> "As for the thief, male and female, cut off their hands." (al-Ma'idah 38)

The Messenger of Allah, may the (ع), said: "I have seen in the Fire the man with the crooked staff who used to steal from the pilgrims with it." And he said: "The thief does not steal while he is a believer."

Whoever enters the house of a people without their permission and steals from their wealth, or takes out from their secured place goods worth five dinars, and acknowledges this or has evidence established against him, has his right hand cut off at the joint of the four fingers of the palm, while the thumb and the palm are left to him for his use. If he steals a second time, his left foot is cut off at the middle of the foot, while the heel and what adjoins it are left to him so that he may walk upon it. If he steals after that, he is imprisoned for life; and if he steals while in prison, he is put to death. When the cutting is carried out, the place of amputation is cauterized with fire to hasten its healing and to make him safe from perishing. If the one performing the cutting errs and cuts off the left hand or the right foot, that suffices, and nothing else is cut from him. Whatever of the stolen goods is found still in his possession is returned to its owners, and he is held liable for whatever he has destroyed of them. After he is cut, he is banished to a land other than the one in which the cutting was carried out.

The guest is not subject to cutting if he steals from among those who hosted him, nor is the hired man if he steals from the house of the one who hired him and admitted him into his home, nor is one who entered with the permission of the household. There is no cutting for snatching, nor for open robbery declared within the town, such as one who seizes a thing and overpowers its owner openly in the town. The hand is not cut off for the one who openly grabs and flees, nor for the pickpocket; rather, both are made an example.

But if this is done upon the road, the one who does it is a brigand. When such a one is brought before the Imam, the ruler has discretion concerning him: if he wishes, he may put him to death; if he wishes, he may crucify him and leave him alive until he dies; if he wishes, he may cut off his hand and his foot from opposite sides; and if he wishes, he may banish him from one land to another. This is done with him when he has terrorized the road and waylaid the Muslims. If he repents before he is seized, he is pardoned. Whoever fights such an aggressor in defense of himself and kills or wounds him bears no liability for that. If the brigand is seized and wealth is found with him, it is returned to its owners if they are known; and if its owners are not known and he confesses and it is witnessed against him that this is among what he plundered, it is placed in the treasury of the Muslims until one who is entitled to it comes forward.

Whoever steals something in which he holds a share is not subject to cutting. There is no cutting upon one who bores a hole or opens a door but takes nothing out. There is no cutting for any stone other than a precious gem, nor for fruit when it is stolen, nor for the pith of the palm, nor for a bird of any kind, nor for what is stolen from places open to all, such as mosques, inns, bathhouses, open courts, and every place permitted for people to enter without leave, unless the stolen goods are taken out from a secured place within it. Whoever steals sheep from the open pasture is not subject to cutting. Whoever steals repeatedly and cannot be apprehended, then is finally apprehended, has his hand cut off and is held liable for all that he stole. Whoever is found with stolen property in his possession, but it is neither proven that he stole it nor acknowledged by him, is not subject to cutting, though the property is taken from him and returned to the one who rightfully owns it.

A madman who steals is not subject to cutting, nor is a boy who has not reached puberty, nor a minor; though the child is disciplined so that he will not grow bold in stealing. The grave robber has his hand cut off when he removes the shroud from the grave, for the grave is a secured place. If one whose hand has been cut dies from the cutting, there is no liability for it. The thief ought to be admonished after the cutting; for it is narrated that Ali, (ع), cut the hands of a group who had stolen, then brought fire, then fed them and gave them drink until they were restored, then clothed them, and commanded them to take up their severed hands in their sound hands.

Whatever the case, the matter was carried further. He said to them, "Raise your hands toward the sky and say: O Allah, Ali has cut off our hands according to Your Book and the Sunnah of Your Messenger." When they had said it, he answered, "Yes, O Lord, upon Your Book and the Sunnah of Your Messenger." Then he said to them, "These are your hands. They have gone ahead of you to the Fire. Whoever among you repents, his hand will drag him to Paradise, and whoever does not repent, it will drag him to Hell."

There are cases in which no hand is cut at all. If a slave steals the property of his master, his hand is not cut off, but if he steals from someone other than his master, it is. If a father steals from his son's property, or a child from his father's, or a wife from her husband's, or a husband from his wife's, or a sister from her brother's, or a brother from his sister's, there is no cutting in any of these. And if a thief steals in a year of famine and hardship, his hand is not to be cut. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام).

**Mention of the rulings on apostates and innovators**

Allah the Almighty said: "How shall Allah guide a people who disbelieved after their faith?" And He said: "Whoever of you turns back from his religion and dies while he is a disbeliever, those are the ones whose deeds have become worthless in this world and the Hereafter." And He said: "It has been revealed to you and to those before you: if you associate partners with Allah, your deeds will surely become worthless, and you will surely be among the losers."

The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said: "It is not permissible to kill a believing man except for one of three things: disbelief after faith, fornication after the establishment of marriage, or the taking of a life for no life." And he said: "Every innovation is misguidance, and every misguidance is in the Fire." And he said: "Whoever changes his religion, kill him."

So whoever was born in Islam and then departed from it to any of the religions of disbelief is killed and is not invited to repent, except that if the apostate is a woman, she is imprisoned until she repents. As for the heretic who does not believe in the resurrection and the reckoning and who denies the prophethood of the Prophets, or one who held a religion other than Islam and then submitted and afterward reverted, Islam and repentance are presented to him over three days. If he repents, he is left alone. If he persists and remains upon what he is, he is killed on the fourth day and is not invited to repent at that point. And if he repents of his own accord, he is left; if he does not repent, he is killed. A slave is likewise killed if his case is such. As for a slave woman who apostatizes, the bounds upon her are tightened: she is put to the harshest service, she is not clothed except with what covers her nakedness, and she is not fed except with what keeps her alive, until she repents or dies. She is not killed, so that she is not destroyed for her family's loss. The same applies to the mother of a child.

When the apostate apostatizes, whatever property he leaves behind passes as inheritance to his heirs, for his apostasy is treated as his death. His wife observes her waiting period and may marry again if she wishes. His children remain Muslims. Whichever of the two parents submits to Islam, his minor children, and any born to him after that, are Muslims by virtue of that parent's Islam. If they grow up and refuse Islam, they are in the standing of apostates, and what is done to apostates is done to them.

Whoever works sorcery against the Muslims has disbelieved, for Allah, exalted is He, says concerning the two angels: "And they taught no one until they said: We are only a trial, so do not disbelieve." And He says: "And they followed what the devils recited during the reign of Solomon, and Solomon did not disbelieve, but the devils disbelieved." The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said: "The sorcerer among the Muslims is killed, but the sorcerer among the polytheists is not killed." It was asked, "O Messenger of Allah, and why is that?" He said, "Because the shirk he is upon is greater than the sorcery." Sorcery and shirk are thus bound together upon one tether. And whoever embraces Islam and then partakes of anything forbidden in Islam is beaten, and if it is something for which a prescribed penalty is due, the penalty is carried out upon him. All of this is the saying of the Ahl al-Bayt, (عليهم السلام).

**Mention of usurpation and transgression**

Allah, the most truthful of those who speak, said: "And do not transgress; indeed, Allah does not love those who transgress." And He said: "And do not consume your wealth among yourselves through falsehood." The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said: "The sanctity of your wealth among you is like the sanctity of this sacred day, in this sacred month, in this sacred land."

So whoever comes into unlawful wealth is obligated to repent and to free himself of it. There is no repentance for him except that he rid himself of it and return it to its owners if he knows them. If he does not know them, he places it in the treasury of the Muslims until the one entitled to it comes forward. If he finds no way to that, he gives it in charity, and if its owners later make themselves known to him, he makes restitution to them. It is not permissible for him to keep it with himself nor to benefit from it.

And whoever has been transgressed against, or had something usurped, or had something stolen from him, must not himself transgress, nor usurp, nor steal from the one who did this to him, even if he is able, because usurpation, betrayal, theft, and transgression are all forbidden. It does not befit one to whom a forbidden act has been done that he do the like of it to the one who did it to him. Rather, he must keep faith with his trust and must not betray nor steal. Al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: "Render up your trusts, and do not betray the one who betrayed you."

Whoever usurps a slave woman and she bears him a child must return her to the one from whom he usurped her, and his child by her belongs as a slave to the one from whom he took her. If he usurped her and then sold her to a man, and the buyer, knowing that she was usurped, fathered a child by her, she is returned to the one from whom she was seized, and the value of the child is paid by him, and he then has recourse against the usurping seller for her price and for the value of the child he gave up. Whoever usurps a slave, a beast, or anything whatever, and it perishes in his possession, owes its value or its equivalent. If he usurped it and it increased in his hand, the increase belongs to its owner from whom it was usurped. If it diminished, the usurper is liable for what diminished from it. If he hired it out, the hire belongs to its owner. If what he seized was sheep or other animals and they bred, then they and their offspring belong to the one from whom they were usurped. And whoever assaults something belonging to the instruments of frivolity and breaks it, or pours out wine or any intoxicant, bears no liability for that. And whoever has wealth stolen or usurped from him and then comes upon the very thing itself may take it back, but it is not lawful for him to take some substitute in its place.

**On loans and deposits**

Allah, mighty and majestic, said: "And do not forget generosity among yourselves." The Messenger of Allah, (ع), said: "A loan of use is an act of affection." And Ja'far ibn Muhammad, (ع), said: "The loan, the lending of an article, and the hosting of the guest are obligations within the Sunnah." And Ali, (ع), said: "Whoever guards the property of an orphan, Allah, mighty and majestic, makes Paradise obligatory for him, just as He has made the Fire obligatory for the one who devours it." And just as sin falls upon the one who betrays what was entrusted to him, so the reward belongs to the one who is given something in trust and renders his trust faithfully.

Whoever borrows an article and stands surety for it to the one who lent it, and it perishes or is lost with him, is liable to make it good, whether or not he was at fault in it. If he did not stand surety for it, but did wrong to it, or transgressed against it, or wasted it, he is liable for it. But if it perished or was damaged with no wrongdoing on his part and no wasting of it, there is no liability upon him, and he is held trustworthy if he is a man of trust.
