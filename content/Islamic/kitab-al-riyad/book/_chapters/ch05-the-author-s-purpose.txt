# The Author's Purpose

Praise belongs to Allah, Lord of fathers and mothers, Creator of animal and plant, whom all that is in the earth and the heavens glorifies, and who in His generosity bestows His blessings upon His creation. No existing thing has existence except by His command, and nothing that comes to be comes to be except by His decree and His determination. He is firmly established, so that the intellects cannot deny Him, and He endures, so that no change can befall Him. Glory be to Him: to His omnipotence all greatness and splendor rise, by His command the favors and the bounties overflow, and by His leave the earth and the heaven stand firm. Those who liken Him to His creation are ruined, and they have strayed a far straying.

I praise Him, I thank Him, I believe in Him and I do not disbelieve in Him, and I bear witness that there is no god but Allah:

> لا إله إلا الله

the true object of worship, who originated all things in the difference of their essences, from the simple that possesses radiance and majesty to the composed that possesses order and perfection. Nothing among them resembles Him, and He is exalted above them in every respect. And I bear witness that Muhammad, His chosen and pure servant, His righteous and Godfearing Messenger, was sent by the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful, the Benevolent and Generous.

He was sent when idols stood worshipped in the sanctuary, when the landmarks of the Hanifi religion were threatened, when the fire of disbelief and unbelief blazed, when the edifices of polytheism and idol worship had been raised high, and when the people had sunk deep into the wastes of misguidance and had lost their sight in the blindness of affliction. So he rose up for Allah, striving and contending, and he stripped away from them the confusion and darkness that had overshadowed them, and he shattered the idols and images they had been worshipping. He set them upon the worship of the Most Merciful and the rites of faith and submission, and he replaced the houses of fire with the Sacred House of Allah. He abrogated the rule of the crosses by turning toward the Corner (al-Rukn) and the Station (al-Maqam). He guided the servants of Allah in His worship onto the level path, delivered them from the claws of the seducing devil, joined them by his blessing to the most trustworthy handhold, and brought them to the courtyards of right guidance and Godwariness. By him the word of Tawhid rose luminous in its risings, the word of shirk was abased and darkened in its dawnings, the tongues pronounced the clear signs, the winds drove the ships of salvation across the seas of dispute, and the ruling of Allah the Exalted came to pass in what He willed of the testing of the people of opinions and religions.

To him belongs a prayer that reaches him on behalf of his followers in general, and on behalf of the company of summoners who are from the light of his lineage in particular, that it may make easy for them, on a day when neither wealth nor sons avail:

> يوم لا ينفع مال ولا بنون، إلا من أتى الله بقلب سليم

a day when nothing avails except one who comes to Allah with a sound heart, the path of his intercession, and may discharge to them the right of his following and his obedience.

And a prayer upon the Axis of the Religion (Qutb al-Din) and the pillar of the believers, the receiver of his lights and the one who upholds the affair of his followers and his helpers, al-Kazim who restrained his rage in fulfillment of the covenant of Allah the Exalted, the patient one under the abasement of what was usurped, seeking thereby the face of Allah the Exalted: the Commander of the Believers, Ali ibn Abi Talib, whose glories cannot be numbered and whose virtues cannot be counted. And a prayer upon his progeny, the blessed Imams, by whose lights souls are made to endure, by whose following ill fortunes are lifted, by whose obedience hopes are attained, and by whose intercession perfection is reached. And a prayer upon the Imam who rules by the command of Allah, the Commander of the Believers, and upon his forefathers, the radiant and rightly guided, the family of Taha and Yasin. (عليهم السلام) (عليهم السلام).

To proceed: the worthiest matter to which the one who professes the divine Unity should turn his care and upon which he should fix his resolve is a matter that leads, in affirming the Unity of Allah the Exalted and knowing His limits, to an ordered system of belief for those who hold certainty, that calls toward lifting confusion and darkness from the souls of the believers, and that guides toward the affirmation of the Unity of the Lord of the Worlds.

When I saw that Shaykh Abu Hatim al-Razi had set out to correct in the book *al-Mahsul* what he claimed had become corrupt, and that Shaykh Abu Ya'qub al-Sijzi, who is al-Sijistani, had come to the support of the author of *al-Mahsul* and stood as a witness to the soundness of his statement, and when I saw that what they disputed over was not among the branches in which difference may be permitted while the soundness of their principles is preserved, and when I found Shaykh Abu Ya'qub al-Sijzi truthful in some of what he brought forward, in the manner he intended in his refutation, yet biased against Shaykh Abu Hatim in part of it, with some of the words of the two of them issued out of order, and when there was besides this in the book *al-Mahsul*, in the words of its author in the chapter on Tawhid and on the First Intellect in particular, apart from what pertains to the branches, that which it was more incumbent upon Shaykh Abu Hatim to correct and to address than the long passages of the branches with which he lengthened his book, yet which he left aside and neglected, and when all of this together stirred the people of the guiding summons to disagreement whenever they came upon a point of it, and to disorder in the paths of Tawhid and the knowledge of the limits, then I resolved to bring forward the sayings of each of the two: the one from the book *al-Islah* (The Reform), by way of reform, and the other from the book *al-Nusra* (The Support), by way of rebuttal, each set down in its own terms.

I resolved to speak upon what is entailed by them and what is intended by them, and upon the aspect by which it is correct to attribute their meanings to their authors, so that the truth of the one may be made clear from the other, so that the biased and the transgressing of the two may be shown, and so that the meaning of that over which they differed may be settled, that one may then believe according to it. Then I resolved to speak upon what was neglected of correction in the book *al-Mahsul*, among the principles in which difference is not permitted, and to make the truth plain, seeking reward.

So I did this and gathered all of it in this book, and I named it the book *al-Riyad* (The Gardens): on the ruling between the two, the author of *al-Islah* and the author of *al-Nusra*. For in what it gathers of their sayings and what it brings forward, it is a separation between them, and a clarification of what error persisted and what correction was neglected in the book *al-Mahsul*, and a rendering of the due therein, from what we have gained of the blessings of the Friends of Allah (عليهم السلام). I named it so because it is like gardens: a greenness that delights the eye, a fruit that comes into the hands. And in Allah we seek help:

> وبالله نستعين

that in setting forth the matter He may keep us safe from the traces of error, even though we are not wholly cleared of it.

The book gathers ten chapters, and all of them together comprise one hundred and fifty-seven sub-chapters. And Allah is the Helper, and He is our sufficiency and the best Trustee:

> وهو حسبنا ونعم الوكيل
