# The Author's Purpose

And upon Him I rely.

Praise belongs to God, Lord of fathers and mothers, Creator of animal and plant, whom all that is in the earth and the heavens glorifies, and who in His generosity bestows blessings upon His creation. Nothing that exists has any existence except by His command, and nothing that is has any being except by His decree and His determination. His being is established, so that minds cannot deny it; He endures, so that no passing of time can touch Him. Glory be to Him: to His omnipotence all greatness and splendor return; by His command the favors and bounties overflow; and by His leave the earth and the heavens stand. The misguided who liken Him to His creation are the losers, and they have strayed a far straying.

I praise Him and I thank Him; I believe in Him and I do not disbelieve; and I bear witness that there is no god but God, the true object of worship, who originated all things in the difference of their essences, from the simple that possesses splendor and majesty to the composite that possesses fine order and perfection. Nothing among them resembles Him, and He is utterly removed from them in every respect. And I bear witness that the Compassionate, the Merciful, the Gracious, the Generous, sent Muhammad, His chosen and pure servant and His righteous and God-fearing Messenger.

He sent him at a time when an idol stood worshipped in His sanctuary, when the landmarks of the Hanifi religion were laid low, when the fire of unbelief and apostasy was ablaze, when the edifices of polytheism and idolatry stood raised high, and when the people had plunged deep into the wilds of misguidance and groped blind in the blindness of affliction. So he rose up for God, striving and contending on their behalf. He lifted from them the confusion and darkness that had overshadowed them; he shattered the idols and images they had been worshipping; and he set them to the worship of the Merciful and to the rites of faith and Islam. He replaced the houses of fire with the Sacred House of God; he abrogated the rule of the crosses by turning toward the Corner (al-Rukn) and the Station (al-Maqam); he guided the servants of God upon the straight path of His worship; he delivered them from the claws of the seducing devil; and he joined them, by his blessing, to the firmest handhold. He brought them to the meadows of right guidance and God-fearing, until the word of unity rose shining in its risings and the word of association was humbled and darkened in its risings; tongues spoke the clear signs, and the winds drove the ships of salvation across the seas of dispute. So the judgment of God Most High came to pass in what He willed of the testing of the people of opinions and religions.

May God bless him with a blessing that reaches him from his followers at large, and from the company of preachers in particular who derive from the light of his soul, so that He may make easy for them, on a day when neither wealth nor sons avail:

> يوم لا ينفع مال ولا بنون إلا من أتى الله بقلب سليم

a day on which neither wealth nor sons will avail, save one who comes to God with a sound heart. May He make easy for them the path of his intercession and fulfill on their behalf the right of following him and obeying him.

And His blessing be upon the Axis of the Religion (Qutb al-Din) and the pillar of the believers, the receptacle of his lights and the keeper of the affair of his followers and his helpers; the one who restrained his anger (al-Kazim) in fulfillment of the covenant of God Most High, and who bore patiently the humiliation of usurpation, seeking the face of God Most High: the Commander of the Believers, Ali ibn Abi Talib, whose glories cannot be numbered and whose virtues cannot be counted. And upon his progeny, the blessed Imams, by whose lights souls are made to endure, by whose following misfortunes are lifted from men, by whose obedience hopes are attained, and by whose intercession perfection is reached. And upon the Imam who rules by the command of God, the Commander of the Believers, and his forefathers, the shining and rightly guided, the family of Taha and Yasin (آل طه وياسين). And (عليهم السلام).

After this: the most deserving matter to which one who professes God's unity may turn his care and devote his resolve is a matter that leads, in the profession of God's unity and the knowledge of His limits (hudud), to a settled order of belief for those who possess certainty; that summons to the lifting of confusion and darkness from the souls of the believers; and that opens the way to the profession of the unity of the Lord of the Worlds.

Now when I saw that Shaykh Abu Hatim al-Razi had undertaken to correct, in the book al-Mahsul (المحصول), what he claimed had been unsound, and that Shaykh Abu Ya'qub al-Sijzi (al-Sijistani) had come to the defense of the author of al-Mahsul and had stood as a witness to the soundness of his statement; and when I saw that what they contended over was not among the branches in which disagreement is permissible while the soundness of the principles is preserved; and when I found Shaykh Abu Ya'qub al-Sijzi, in some of what he brought forward, truthful in the aim he intended by his refutation, yet in some of it biased against Shaykh Abu Hatim, and found some of the words of both of them issued without order; and when, along with this, there was in the book al-Mahsul, in the words of its author in the chapter on the profession of unity and on the First Intellect in particular, apart from what pertains to the branches, that which it was all the more incumbent upon Shaykh Abu Hatim to correct and to address; and when I considered that none had a better right than he to address the long passages with which he himself had lengthened his book, and that he had nonetheless left them and neglected them; and when all of this together left the people of the guiding call in perplexity whenever they came upon a point of difference, and in disorder in the paths of unity and the knowledge of the limits: then I resolved to set down the sayings of each of the two of them, the one from the book al-Islah (الاصلاح) by way of correction and the other from the book al-Nusra (النصرة) by way of refutation, in their very words. I resolved to speak of what necessarily follows from them and of what is intended by them, and of the aspect by which their meanings may rightly be ascribed to their authors, so that the truth of the one may be distinguished from the other, that the one biased and transgressing among them may be made plain, and that the meaning of what they differed over may be settled, so that belief may be held accordingly. Then I speak of what was neglected of correction in the book al-Mahsul, among the principles in which disagreement is not permissible, and I make the truth clear, seeking reward.

This I have done, and I have gathered all of it in this book, and I have named it the Book of al-Riyad (كتاب الرياض), on the judgment between the two adversaries, the author of al-Islah and the author of al-Nusra. For in what it gathers of their sayings and in what it sets forth, it is a separation between them, an exposition of the error that persisted and of what was neglected of correction in the book al-Mahsul, and a rendering of what is due in this, out of what we have gained from the blessings of the Friends of God (اولياء الله), upon them be peace (عليهم السلام). For it is like gardens (riyad): a greenness that delights the eye, a fruit that comes into the hands. In God we seek help; and though in setting a thing forth we are not free of the traces of error, we seek to be guarded from them.

The book comprises ten chapters, which together contain one hundred and fifty-seven sections. God is the Helper; He is sufficient for us, and how excellent a Guardian.
