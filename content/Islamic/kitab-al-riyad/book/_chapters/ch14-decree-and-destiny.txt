# Decree and Destiny

Chapter Eight of the Book of Riyad: On the Judgment between the Two Contenders, of which he spoke in the chapter of Decree and Destiny.

**Section One of Chapter Eight**

The author of al-Islah said:

As for the saying that the decree is prior and the destiny posterior, this is an error, because destiny is before decree, and the prior is before the posterior. It is not permissible that the decree, which comes after destiny, should be identified with that which is before the posterior. Decree and destiny are two words whose meaning is known from ordinary speech: destiny is estimation, and decree is elaboration, and there is no elaboration except after estimation, so the first is before the second by the farthest reach of priority. The witnesses to this from the Book of God are His words:

قضي الامر الذي به تستفتيان
"The matter concerning which you two sought counsel has been decreed."

meaning that it has been separated out and brought to an end; and His words:

فاذا قضيت الصلاة
"When you have finished the prayer,"

meaning, when you have completed it. The decree is like a garment that the tailor first estimates: before he cuts it he estimates it, and he adds and subtracts, widens and narrows; but once he has cut it he has decreed it and made an end of it, and after that no increase in it is possible. That is the likeness of decree and destiny. The upshot of his saying is this: decree is not predicated of the prior, for it comes after destiny; and destiny is not predicated of the posterior, for it comes before decree. Destiny is estimation and decree is elaboration, and as long as the thing remains within estimation it can be increased, decreased, and widened, but once it has passed into elaboration and been decreed, no increase in it and no decrease from it is possible.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Decree is not elaboration, for the state of the very act of elaboration within the decree is no less than the state of the separated thing itself, since the separated thing is fixed upon its form by a decree that requires this, just as the separated thing is likewise established only by what has been decreed for it. As for destiny, it is estimation, as he has said, except that there is no estimation save by a judgment that requires that estimation. As for the witnesses he cited from the Book of God to the effect that decree is a bringing to completion, these are not to be dismissed, for the sages have said: when God, exalted be He, originated the Intellect, He brought the two worlds to completion, since the Intellect is the sum of the images of the two worlds, and nothing of the images of the worlds is absent from the Intellect, so it is pure completion. Yet the author of al-Islah has erred in likening destiny and decree to the garment that the tailor estimates. He made the estimation correspond to destiny and the cutting correspond to the decree, and this analogy does not stand upright. What he ought to have done is to make the image of the sewing that resides in the very soul of the tailor correspond to the decree, because it is spiritual; and the estimation by which the tailor estimates that sewing correspond to destiny, for that too is a spiritual thing that has not yet emerged to the limit of intellect, and this is the meaning of the image of the two origins. Then the cutting which he performs after estimation, upon the bodily boundaries that have emerged from the first and the second, corresponds to what appears in body. If the matter is as we have described, then decree precedes destiny, just as the sewing precedes the estimation, and by that sewing the tailor is made ready for the estimation by which he estimates. So it is established that decree is prior and destiny is posterior. This is the upshot of his saying:

Decree is ahead of destiny, and there is no estimation except by a judgment, so decree is prior and destiny posterior.

We say:

The interpretation the two contenders give of decree and destiny, each according to what he has invoked, is an impossible interpretation, even granting the meaning of the two words as we have shown it, in whatever way they assign decree and destiny to the prior and the posterior. For the author of al-Islah has committed himself, by his saying that destiny is estimation and decree is elaboration, and by the analogy he brought forward in the matter of the garment and the image of the sewing, to holding that destiny is what stands in potency, what could yet be, and that decree is what stands in act, what has already come out from the door of possibility, which is potency, into actuality. Now if this were the meaning of destiny and decree, then it is impossible to predicate them of the prior and the posterior at all, in any respect, since neither of the two stands in potency, nor was in potency and then emerged into act, according to the proofs we have already set down.

**Section Two of Chapter Eight**

The author of al-Nusrah has committed himself, by his saying that there is no estimation except by a judgment that requires that estimation, and by his representing the decree with the craft of sewing from which the estimation proceeds, to making destiny posterior, while yet conceding that the meaning of destiny is, as the author of al-Islah established it, the existence of an existent by potency. But it is not permissible to predicate this of the posterior, since it has been established from what we said above that the posterior already stands in act; and if it were permissible, then destiny would not be posterior. And if destiny is not the posterior, then decree has all the more claim to be the prior, and is even further from being predicated of it, since the posterior was never in potency such that it then went out into act to answer to what its own word conveys of the state of the prior. It is likewise impossible that decree and destiny be predicated of the prior and the posterior. And since this is impossible, we say that the statement of the two men is correct only in the manner we shall now explain, not in the manner to which they went, for decree shares its meaning with several things, and those things all belong to the world of body, not to the world of origination; and likewise destiny.

**Section Three of Chapter Eight**

As for decree, it points, first, to the Speaker who was in potency and then emerged into act by the support of God, exalted be He, and to those who take his place (عليهم السلام), who have reached in their attainment the rank parallel to the first upsurge. All of these, in their standing in act, in their command, prohibition, and decree within the summons that is already established in act, having been in potency, answer to the meaning conveyed by the word "decree" (عليهم السلام). This is what the author of al-Nusrah intends, and upon this his saying and his placing of decree first are sound.

**Section Four of Chapter Eight**

Decree points, second, to what is drawn out by interpretation from the potency of the revelation and the Law, from the sciences that are not set forth explicitly within them but reside within them in potency, into exposition and act, by which souls are brought out from the limit of potency to the limit of act. This is what the author of al-Islah intends by his interpretation of the word "decree."

**Section Five of Chapter Eight**

Decree points, third, to what emerges into act and to the latent unfolding of events and transformations appointed to arise in the world through the movement of the moving things and the stillness of the still, those that admit of no reversal. For this reason it is said, when some matter comes to pass and is realized in act, that there is no repelling the decree of God. These are the things of which decree is predicated.

**Section Six of Chapter Eight**

As for destiny, it points, first, to the promised Qa'im, foretold and heralded from the time of Adam (ع), and to his summons, whose safekeeping and upholding are borne by the Imams and the Messengers (عليهم السلام) through their summoners in the period of concealment, holding out its force in that he is appointed to come forth from estimation into act and existence. His summons, though established, stands from the side of the ranks of the cycle of the Prophet (ع) and his family in the concealment that runs in the manner of what is standing in potency, not in the manner of the manifest that runs as what is standing in act. For this reason the inner meaning of the Night of Determination, Laylat al-Qadr (ع), is he, and to him point the allusions of the shaykhs in their saying:

كوني قدر
"Be, O destiny."

**Section Seven of Chapter Eight**

Destiny points, second, to what the Prophet (ع) and his family brought from the revelation, the Law, the rulings, and the parables that hold within their potency the sciences and the knowledge the soul needs in its passage to maturity and act, being cast in a form that gathers all of that together. For this reason no complete science is found upon its outer face except through interpretation drawn out of it, and the saying of the author of al-Islah in this direction points to this meaning of destiny.

**Section Eight of Chapter Eight**

Destiny points, third, to the world with all its parts, which has within its power to come forth into being and act through the passage of days, months, years, events, and the coming-to-be whose existence proceeds from it by the perpetual movement of the movers over what is beneath them, of necessity, since these are appointed within it. For this reason it is said: what God has appointed will be, for its coming-to-be is by way of the necessity of its existence; and though the term be long, it is as one already existing in act. So God, exalted be He, said:

إِنَّهُمْ يَرَوْنَهُۥ بَعِيدًۭا وَنَرَاهُ قَرِيبًا
"They see it as far off, but We see it as near."

Whatever was in estimation, appointed to exist, is decree once it exists, since the word "decree" signals what was in potency and then came out into act. These are the meanings of decree and destiny.

If one ponders the words of the author of al-Islah, and his saying that destiny precedes decree, the soundness of its meaning holds only with respect to what the Prophet (ع) and his family brought of the revelation and the Law, which precedes the decree that is the interpretation, as he himself did and interpreted. And the saying of the author of al-Nusrah, that decree precedes destiny, if it is pondered, its meaning is sound only with respect to the Speaker (ع) who stands in act, who is the decree that precedes what he brought of the revelation and the Law, which is destiny.

**Section Nine of Chapter Eight**

The author of al-Nusrah said, reporting from the learned sages:

When God, exalted be He, originated the Intellect, He brought the two worlds to completion, because it is the gathering place of the images of the worlds.

We say:

The Intellect that is the gathering place of the images of the worlds is not the First originated Intellect, nor the second Intellect, which is the second upsurge; rather it is the Intellect that ascends from the world of nature by acquisition, in deed and in knowledge, to the parallel of the first upsurge, so that it surges a second surging and thereby becomes a compendium of the images of the worlds in all their multitude, its very essence imprinted with the forms of all existents, as is the case with the Enunciators, the Executors, and the Imams (عليهم السلام). The First Intellect is too exalted to be multiplied by this kind of multiplication, since its existence is originative, and originative existence is multiplied only according to what it intellects of its own essence, namely that it is a cause and an effect, and that its existence is only from that which is beyond itself, from which it received. The remaining existents, I mean the Intellects that surge in a first surging, are multiplied according to what I have said of their essences and of that whose existence precedes them, back to the First Intellect, their existence being, together with this, from that toward which they turned in devotion, which is beyond them. So the later in existence is not multiplied as the prior in existence is multiplied. Its need to be multiplied for the sake of intellecting all existents is a binding necessity, whereas the First in existence is entirely free of that need in the completeness of its being. If this is so, then it is clear that the one which is a compendium of the images of the two worlds is not the First Intellect, but the Intellects that surge from the abode of nature in a second surging.

Then we say:

The First Intellect does not require that it intellect what is beneath it, nor that it form any image, by reason of what its essence is from eternity: completeness, subsistence, unity, and immunity from change. Rather it is so only by reason of that from which it came to be. And since its being is eternal, complete, subsistent, one and singular, immune from transformation, only by reason of that from which it came to be, exalted be He, it is impossible that it intellect anything beneath it, for it has no need of that.

And we likewise say:

The one that is later in existence, from the second surging, is required to intellect what precedes it in existence and so to become a compendium of the images, because the completeness in which its subsistence lies it acquires by its intellecting of that, I mean of what precedes it in existence. And since it needs, for its completeness, its intellecting of that whose existence preceded it among the ranks upon whom the interpretive summons was established, which is the second worship, so that their stations might be made plain, it is required to teach them and to intellect them. And when it teaches them and intellects them, its essence is thereby multiplied from the side of the multitude of its intellected objects, and is unified from the other side, which is its own essence, so that in that unity it resembles what precedes it in existence. Our book known as راحة العقل, The Peace of the Intellect, contains of the evidences of legal proof for this, and of the religious depictions, what suffices and heals the seeker for the sake of his return. May God make us among those who follow His good pleasure in whatever brings distress or delight, and who follow His friends (عليهم السلام) therein.

**Section Ten of Chapter Eight**

The author of al-Islah said:

It is only the error of those who made decree the prior and destiny the posterior. For they found that the learned had named the posterior by the name of destiny, which is indeed its true name, because all the essences surged from the prior in an appointed measure, and that surging was destiny itself, so the sages named it destiny. Then in the speech of the people "decree and destiny" came to be uttered together, and they were found to place decree ahead of destiny in speech, so that one says "this is decree and destiny." The meaning of decree and destiny was hidden from them, so they assigned decree to the first and destiny to the second, and from this side the error occurred. This is as we said: that all the essences surged from the prior in an appointed measure, and that surging was destiny itself, and it is the compendium of the essences. Confirming this is the saying of God, exalted be He:

إِنَّا كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقْنَهُ بِقَدَرٍۢ
"Indeed, We have created everything according to a measure."

For just as all the essences surged from the First, which is the command in appointed potency, and were destiny itself, so likewise there appeared from the second, in act, out of matter and form, and that act was decree itself. And within destiny there occurs change and alteration, and it is the book of which God, exalted be He, said:

يَمْحُوا۟ ٱللَّهُ مَا يَشَآءُ وَيُثْبِتُ
"Allah erases what He wills and confirms."

Destiny is the essence of the posterior, as we have said, and decree is its act, because the posterior is what is appointed in measure from the prior. The upshot of his saying in this section is: destiny is the name of the posterior, because all the essences surged from the prior in an appointed measure, as God, exalted be He, said, "Indeed, We have created everything according to a measure," and decree is its act.

The author of al-Islah said:

As for what he invoked concerning decree and destiny from the saying of God, exalted be He, "Indeed, We have created everything according to a measure," he has erred in the interpretation of this verse. Its interpretation is as we shall expound and state, for by it He intends only the pure Ipseity.

We say:

The meaning of إِنَّا, "We," by which He intends the Ipseity, is that which no attribute and no negation of attribute taints; and "everything," كُلَّ شَىْءٍ, by which He intends the Intellect, which is all things; and "We have created it," خَلَقْنَهُ, by which He intends the image of the two worlds among them; and "according to a measure," بِقَدَرٍ, by which He intends the posterior, upon which the settling of the matter rests, so that all the spiritual and bodily images appeared from between the two in the manifest, which is the pen, the tablet, the writing, and the Writer. The Writer stands over against إِنَّا, "We," and He is the pure Ipseity; the Pen stands over against the Intellect, which is the pen of pens; "We have created it" stands over against the Tablet; the writing stands over against the letters that appear upon the Tablet; and "according to a measure" stands over against the Tablet that is receptive to all the forms of the letters. For just as all bodily and spiritual things, the simple and the dense, fall under these intelligible letters that appear from the Writer, by the Pen, upon the Tablet, so likewise all the higher and lower images God, exalted be He, brought forth by means of the Intellect and the Soul, and nothing of them was withheld from between the two, just as nothing of things is withheld from the ordered letters that appear in act from the Writer upon the Tablet. This is the saying of the author of al-Nusrah in refutation.

We say:

The two contenders have brought forward in this section what cannot stand firm, neither for this one in his emendation, nor for that one in his refutation. For the author of al-Islah, when he held that destiny is the name of the posterior and argued for it by the saying of God, exalted be He, "Indeed, We have created everything according to a measure," has already been met by what preceded in the discussion of destiny and decree, from which it is grasped that destiny may not be predicated of the posterior. And the author of al-Nusrah made his counter to the author of al-Islah's saying rest upon what he interpreted from the verse, and this is neither balanced against nor congruent with the matter. For he said, interpreting:

God, exalted be He, by إِنَّا, "We," intends only the pure Ipseity that no attribute and no negation of attribute taints. But the word إِنَّا, "We," is only at the limit from whose side the Prophet draws supply by his standing in the divine place within the world, not the pure Ipseity itself. And how could "We" fall upon the pure Ipseity, when between Him and it lies the intermediary of utter negation? Rather it falls upon the near intermediary of the Messenger of God (ع) in the outpouring upon him, and the meaning is the Messenger, since he is the one who conveys on His behalf.

**Section Eleven of Chapter Eight**

He said:

"Everything," by which he intends the Intellect, which is all things. But "everything" in this place is not the Intellect, for we have already shown that the First Intellect is not multiplied as that whose existence is "everything" is multiplied, which is what the community needs of the two comprehensive worships that gather the sciences, outwardly by deed and inwardly by the conception that leads to deliverance from the torment of the two worlds.

**Section Twelve of Chapter Eight**

He said:

"We have created it," by which he does not intend what he went to, for "We have created it" means "We have appointed it in measure." I mean: We have made the form of the revelation and the Law a form within whose potency lies everything of the sciences and the religious works the community needs to ward off pains from the soul in this world and the next. It is as though He said, speaking on behalf of God, exalted be He: "We, everything We have created according to a measure," of the matter of the two worships in knowledge and deed which the community needs in its religion and its worldly life, We have appointed in measure within what We brought of the revelation and the Law, an appointing; that is, We placed it within its potency, that it might come forth, in interpretation, to the one who merits it.

**Section Thirteen of Chapter Eight**

As for the saying of the author of al-Islah that decree is the posterior act, he has hit the mark from the side that what comes out into act from the world of nature comes out by its outpouring, and by it is completed, and from its side attains completeness. For this reason decree came to be predicated of the Speaker, since his coming out into act and his attaining perfection are by its outpouring and its support.

**Section Fourteen of Chapter Eight**

He said:

All the essences surged from the prior in an appointed measure. In this there is a contradiction, for the posterior is an essence, and it is surged from the prior, yet it is not appointed in measure. What is meant by his saying is rather that surging from which the bodily world came to be, which is appointed in measure so that things might come from it, as we explained in the chapter of destiny, not the posterior.

**Section Fifteen of Chapter Eight**

As for the saying of the author of al-Nusrah, in the passage where at its head stands "We have created it," by which he intends the image of the two worlds from between them, and "according to a measure," by which he intends the posterior upon which the settling of the matter rests, so that all the spiritual and bodily images appeared from between the two: the matter in the existence of things is not such that what is bodily was and what is non-bodily was, in the manner he mentioned, by reason of the corruption we have shown in the foundation upon which he built his speech. And were it not that discourse upon the order in which the existence of existents rests upon the First lies outside the bound of writing, we would have brought it, and its mention will not fail to appear, God willing, in our epistles and our books.

**Section Sixteen of Chapter Eight**

The author of al-Islah said:

As for the interpretation of the saying of the Messenger of God (ع):

أفر من قضاء الله إلى قدره
"I flee from the decree of God to His destiny,"

he meant only to flee from the subtle support of the prior to the exposition of the posterior, as a man flees from fire, which is a sign of support, to water, which is a sign of exposition. For he wished to flee from the thing that has already been sealed and decreed, whose repelling cannot be contrived, to destiny, which has not yet been decreed.

This is the account, and it is not well-ordered. For the Messenger of God (ع) fled from the decree of God to His destiny for a cause, and the cause was the leaning wall whose falling he feared, so he hastened his step and said, "I flee from the decree of God to His destiny," and he wished to strike for them a parable. The leaning wall is a structure that has leaned so as to fall, and it stands for one of the ranks who has resolved upon some breaking of covenant and hypocrisy and has inclined from obedience to transgression; for when he breaks his covenant he falls from his station, as God, exalted be He, said:

فوجدا فيها جدار يريد أن ينقض فأقامه
"So the two of them found in it a wall about to fall, and he set it up straight,"
