# Fleeing to God's Measure

The author of al-Islah completed his point: the setting of him upright signifies the speed of the walking, for to follow a man in his walking signifies the statement, the proof, the haste, and the quickness of the pace. So the Messenger of God (ع) used to address those bent upon hypocrisy with preaching and with warning in the outward form, without the statement in the inward. The measure is upon the outward, as we have explained, and the decree is upon the inward. His fleeing from the decree of God to His measure was for the sake of this wall, wary that it should fall, wary for himself, and not a fleeing from the dignity with which God Almighty had honored him, the degree to which God had raised him.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

He is, upon the seeking of increase, the more eager.

It is not that the words *Flee from God's decree to His measure* are to be interpreted as the author of al-Islah imagines, namely that the Messenger was fleeing from the rank of the Follower to the rank of the Preceder, for by his own claim the prophets (عليهم السلام) forever seek ascent, and the seeking of the height is not the seeking of the depth. But we would say, by the help of God Almighty and His blessing, that the meaning here turns upon three senses, and each one of them establishes that the decree belongs to the Preceder and the measure belongs to the Follower.

The first, and it is the greatest of them, the most fitting, and the most exact in meaning, is that what is intended in his fleeing from the decree of God to His measure is this: from the Preceder, God willing, comes the support; from the Follower comes the structuring; from the Speaker comes the composing of the Law; and from the Foundation comes the interpretation of the whole. The interpretation, which is the end, most resembles the support, and the composing most resembles the structuring. So the Speaker (ع), in this saying, was making plain that he is, in his composing of the Laws, like the Follower in its structuring of the world, and that he is not like the Preceder. He fled from the decree of God, which is the Preceder, and did not resemble it in the aspect that is the origination of the support, toward his measure, which is the interpretation, to which he likened himself.

The second sense is his informing his community that he draws benefit from the First by means of the Second, so that in everything he wished to stand upon from the First, it was by means of the Second that he returned to it. It is as if he said: I return, in all that I wish to grasp, to the one who is the link between me and the Intellect, and that is the Second.

The third sense, and it is the most honored, is what the author of the book (ع) mentioned: his fleeing from the intensity of the light of the support of the Preceder to the support of the Follower, as al-Jadd, with his spiritual power, could not bear it until his very identity nearly dissolved. That is his saying in the story of Moses (ع). This is their statement.

We say:

As for the author of al-Islah, he took, in interpreting the verse, a course conforming to how the matter truly stands in the meaning of the decree and the measure, and it is well done. Better still had he said that, since the Prophet (ع) was a prophet who reported the hidden things among the events of existence and the happenings, and what lay within men's breasts, by what God Almighty had singled out for him of revelation, he wished to make plain how there was opened to him what was cast to him by al-Jadd. So he said: I return, which is his saying *I flee*. I mean: by al-Jadd I cross, from the side of the ranks of God Almighty, the whole of what is on the point of coming to be among the happenings and the events of existence, which is the decree of God that inevitably goes forth into act, toward His measure, which is God's creating of the souls and the horizons and their motions that point to what He has measured within them to be, so that there is opened to me what has been made plain in the door of revelation. And for this God Almighty said:

> سنريهم آياتنا في الآفاق وفي أنفسهم حتى يتبين لهم انه الحق

We shall show them Our signs upon the horizons and within their own selves, until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth.

And He said of those who reflect upon the creation of the heavens and the earth:

> ربنا ما خلقت هذا باطلاً

Our Lord, You did not create this in vain.

This does not point to the mere precedence of a thing's existence or the lateness of its existence among the events of being; rather it points to it and gives it utterance. It is as if he said: I return, in what is cast to me by al-Jadd, wholly to the measure, so that it is glimpsed, or opened, by its taking form before me and being pictured to me. And that is his saying, *I flee from the decree of God to the measure.* This is a kind of revelation, and the book *Rahat al-Aql* contains the explanation of it.

### The Seventeenth Section of the Eighth Chapter

As for the author of al-Nusrah, his manner in the answer he pursued is proof of his surrender to the author of al-Islah, for he brought forward nothing that would overturn what the author of al-Islah built his words upon and set out, except the meanings he adduced, reckoning them his own. He held that each of them establishes that the decree belongs to the Preceder and the measure belongs to the Follower, and not because the Speaker is like the Follower in his composing of the Law, as al-Islah put it, but on account of the Speaker's returning, in all he wished to grasp, to the Follower. Even were he to establish this, it would still oblige him to establish that the decree belongs to the Preceder and the measure to the Follower, and there is no correspondence at all between the decree and the measure on the one hand and the meanings he set out on the other. Had the meaning of the decree and the measure conformed to the state of the First and the Second, and been fitted to them from some aspect, it would have been allowable to say that the decree and the measure fall upon them from that aspect. But the decree means what has already been said of it, and so too the measure, and it is not fitting to say these of the First and the Second, for they are innocent of what the meaning of the two terms would require. Whoever says these of them is as one who says of honey, whose nature is sweetness, that it is aloes, whose nature is bitterness.

### The Eighteenth Section of the Eighth Chapter

As for his likening, in the second sense, the Speaker to the Follower, it does not conform to the law of the guiding call, because the Speaker, in his standing within the world of body and the world of religion, holds the station of the Preceder, and the station of the guardian in the two of them is the station of the Follower in the world of origination. So the Speaker and the Foundation are two likenesses of the Preceder and the Follower, and two likenesses of the sun and the moon.

### The Nineteenth Section of the Eighth Chapter

As for the third sense he adduced, making it the ground of the Speaker's fleeing, namely that it was from the intensity of the light of the support of the Preceder toward the support of the Follower, it runs contrary to what is due. And the interpretation concerning the state of al-Jadd, which he cited in evidence, calls for full investigation and a further reckoning, and the discussion of it will come. In sum, the saying of the author of al-Islah in this section is the stronger.

### The Twentieth Section of the Eighth Chapter

The author of al-Islah said, drawing upon the verse in the story of Moses (ع):

> رب ارني انظر اليك قال لن تراني ولكن انظر الى الجبل فان استقر مكانه فسوف تراني فلما تجلى ربه للجبل

My Lord, show me, that I may look upon You. He said: You shall not see Me, but look toward the mountain; if it settles in its place, then you shall see Me. And when his Lord disclosed Himself to the mountain,

> جعله دكا وخر موسى صعقا

He made it crumble to dust, and Moses fell down thunderstruck.

This one sought to have unveiled to him more than had been measured for him of the rank of the Preceder, so it was said to him: You shall not see Me, but look toward the mountain. Thus He made known to him that he would not attain that, and He bade him keep to what flows to him by al-Jadd, and He made known that the one who asked him could not, for all its subtlety, attain the like of what he sought. How then could he attain what he does not see? And He says: If it settles in its place, then you shall see Me. And when his Lord disclosed Himself to the mountain, He made it crumble to dust, and Moses fell down thunderstruck. When Moses knew that he had erred in seeking what he could not receive, he humbled himself before his Lord, and when he came to himself he said:

> سبحانك تبت إليك

Glory be to You, I turn to You in repentance.

This is his saying.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

*My Lord, show me, that I may look upon You. He said: You shall not see Me* means: you have not the strength, and you cannot endure with patience upon that rank. *But look toward the mountain,* and it is al-Jadd, for all his nearness to me. If it settles upon its rank, the one I have given him, and bears what I have not disclosed to you, then you too shall bear that. But al-Jadd did not bear it when it was disclosed to him, and his identity nearly dissolved. This is his saying.

We say:

The interpretation of this verse, as each of the two of them has interpreted it, lowers Moses (ع). For when they placed him in the station of the prophets and the highest rank, among those who commune with the upper ranks, they then cast him out of the company of the knowing and counted him in the ranks of the ignorant, alleging that he sought what he could not grasp from the ranks above him. How could that be, and the whole matter passing him by in it? The interpretation of the verse is other than what they went to. For the address to Moses (ع) was not with the upper ranks; rather it was an address to him from the lower ranks, at a time when he had not yet reached the degree of Speaker. He asked of the master of the affair what its hour had not yet come, for he was in an age of interval, and the people of truth were beneath affliction, veiling, and concealment from the side of the opponents and the adversaries. Moses (ع) saw in himself a power by which he reckoned it possible, in that age of his, were he commanded, to lift that ordeal from the people of truth, to take vengeance upon the enemies of God, and to govern the community by the governance of truth, just as the Foundation (ع) had reckoned, by the power he found in himself, what he reckoned, and then repented of it when he knew that he had erred, whereupon the sun was returned to him and restored, as we have set out in our epistles by interpretation.

So Moses (ع) asked of the master of the affair, and he is Shu'ayb, when he betook himself to him, and he is his Lord, this thing, after he had made his command clear to him regarding what he found in himself of power and capacity for governance. That is his saying, *My Lord, show me, that I may look upon You,* meaning: command me, that I may order this affair and lift the distress. He said: *You shall not see Me,* meaning: you shall not see Me do any of that, nor shall I invest you with a command at this time, for to everything there is an hour and a season, and this season is not the season of taking these, meaning the community, from what they are upon of following their own whims, nor the time of returning affairs to the canons of governance, nor will what I say to you be brought to completion. Shu'ayb (ع) wished to alert him to his error in his request in the best of ways, and to teach him that what he sought is hard, and will not be brought to completion in that age of his at the hands of one possessed of power, aid, and readiness, so how then should it be at his own hands? For it came from the side of a man of great place in the call, mighty in worldly leadership, possessed of aid, wealth, men, and dominion. He said to Moses (ع): I have come to know that this affair will not be completed in this age, and that it is hard and cannot be measured out. It is not that my refraining is out of stinginess toward you, or a preference to delay you from your station; but look toward the mountain, meaning this man who possesses worldly power, readiness, and capacity. *If it settles in its place, then you shall see Me,* meaning: if he stands firm in what he has been commanded, and is enabled, with the power and aid he has, to spread out the ordinances of the governance of truth, and it is completed for him, then you shall see Me do what you asked concerning those people. *And when his Lord disclosed Himself to the mountain,* meaning, when He commanded him to raise up the beacon of truth, *He made it crumble to dust,* that is, the affair grew straitened upon the people, so they did not accept the command, and split the staff, and the affair fell into confusion and disorder upon him, and his leadership stood upon the brink of ruin as the people rose against him. So Moses (ع) knew then that what he had reckoned would be completed at his hands in that hour was a reckoning of the impossible, and that the master of the affair, who is his Lord, was more knowing of the matter than he. He awoke from what had been confused upon him by that alerting, and he said: *Glory be to You, I turn to You in repentance.* He recanted his saying, and regretted, and held to patience with what the author of al-Nusrah had commanded him, until that term in which he had been hired elapsed, and the time came, and there came from him what came, and his saying was verified, *then you shall see Me:* the affair was surrendered, and you do what you wish. This is the interpretation of the verse, not what is reckoned by the one who lowers and raises, which does not correspond.

### The Twenty-First Section of the Eighth Chapter

The author of al-Nusrah said:

As for his argument by the saying, *And He created everything and measured it out in a measuring,* this is the clearest of proofs that the measure belongs to the Follower, and the decree, which is the creating, to the Preceder, since the meaning of His saying *And He created everything* is that by it He intended the Preceder, for the Intellect is all things, and all things are the Intellect.

We say:

The discussion of the measure and the decree has already gone before, and it was made plain with it that the decree and the measure may not be said of the Preceder and the Follower. What is intended by the Almighty's saying,

> وخلق كل شيء فقدره تقديراً

And He created everything, then measured it out in a measuring,

is not what the author of al-Nusrah went to, for we have already shown its corruptness, and that it befits neither the Preceder nor the Follower that the decree and the measure be said of them. Rather, what is intended in His saying *And He created everything, then measured it out in a measuring* is that He set everything pertaining to the affair of religion and its welfare, outwardly or inwardly, within the power of the Revelation and the Law, and measured it out in a measuring according to how the Revelation and the Law stand in their comprehending of all the sciences that pertain to religion and its welfare; and these are within the two of them by potency, and the interpretation brings them forth from them.

### The Twenty-Second Section of the Eighth Chapter

As for his saying that the Intellect is all things and all things are the Intellect, this is a proposition that does not hold. The bodily world, with all it gathers, is not an intellect; it is a thing. The finest thing to which the meaning of the saying, *the Intellect is all things and all things are the Intellect,* may be attributed is this: that the Intellect, which is the very command of God Almighty, is the cause of the existence of all things, bodily and other than bodily, and the existence of all things is from the Intellect and toward its purpose. This is what he intended.

### The Twenty-Third Section of the Eighth Chapter

The author of al-Nusrah said:

God has granted the prophets license in the inner meanings of the Laws, and dispensed them from employing them, in His saying:

> ليس على الذين آمنوا وعملوا الصالحات جناح فيما طعموا اذا ما اتقوا وآمنوا وعملوا الصالحات ثم اتقوا وآمنوا واحسنوا ان الله يحب المحسنين

There is no blame upon those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they have partaken, so long as they are God-fearing and believe and do righteous deeds; then are God-fearing and believe; then are God-fearing and do good. Indeed, God loves the doers of good.

We say:

The verse contains, neither in its outward nor in its inward, any mention of a license for the prophets to abandon the employing of the Laws. I do not know how this escaped him, that he loosed such a saying without examining it fully. The exposition of this verse and its interpretation are contrary to what he went to. For God Almighty, when He knew that souls do not attain felicity, nor reach the degree of perfection, nor pass from the limit of potency to the limit of act, except by work and knowledge, laid upon the whole of mankind the establishing of the two worships, in knowledge and in deed, that by them they might attain the two felicities in the two abodes. And since souls do not attain their perfection and felicity save by the two worships, then whichever soul abandons the two of them, or one of them, is far from attaining perfection. This holds for the soul so long as it remains within the bond of life: it stands between His hands until the time of its passing, whereupon it is either in the Garden, if it kept the two, or it abandoned the two or one of them and is in the Fire, until God forgives it, as we have set out in our books. We have shown, in what has gone before of the discussion, that souls do not stand save by work and by the reforming of character, which is the greatest striving, as the Messenger of God (ع) said, and by their acquiring of the virtues through discipline and knowledge, in relation to what precedes them in existence, according to what the interpretive call requires. And if the states of souls in worship are in this likeness, how then is it allowable that license be granted to a soul, or to the Speakers, or to those beneath them among the ranks, to abandon the work and the perfecting of souls, the complete of them and the deficient by it?

No. The verse has an interpretation other than the one he interpreted, and in it is a special bearing upon those who stand in the call, the summoners to the affirmation of the oneness of God Almighty and the knowledge of His ranks by way of the Imams (عليهم السلام). For the Speaker (ع), when he knew that his community would differ after him, that each faction would set itself apart with a doctrine it invents by its own opinions and its deficient minds, and that they would choose, over the guiding community for whom God has appointed the guides, those for whom God has made no light, and knew that the summoners would suffer from the divided community every hardship in the community's setting itself against them in doing them injury and in impugning their doctrine and their masters, the pure Imams (عليهم السلام), granted to the summoners who stand in the call a license to look into the sciences that are not of their own belief, upon conditions he set forth, that these might be for them as a piece of equipment in their disputations. He said:

> ليس على الذين آمنوا وعملوا الصالحات جناح فيما طعموا اذا ما اتقوا وآمنوا وعملوا الصالحات

There is no blame upon those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they have partaken, so long as they are God-fearing and believe and do righteous deeds,

meaning: there is no blame upon those who stand in the call to the oneness of God and the knowledge of His ranks by way of the deputies (عليهم السلام), who bring the respondents to faith when they join themselves to the rope of God and hold fast, by loyalty to them, against doubts and confusions in the religion of God, and who bring them to the degree of perfection and attainment, no blame, that is, no straitness, in what they have partaken, that is, in what they read of the sciences that are not of their belief, that these might be for them as a weapon with which they fight the adversaries in establishing the truth. And that is upon three conditions:

The first, *so long as they are God-fearing and believe and do righteous deeds,* meaning: so long as their path is straight in obedience to God Almighty, in following His Messenger (ع), in belief in His ranks, and in the establishing of the two worships together according to what he brought.

The second, *then are God-fearing and believe,* meaning: and they obey the Messenger in his legatee and in the one who stands in the affair in his place, and establish the call to him, outwardly and inwardly.

The third, *then are God-fearing and do good,* meaning: and they obey the Imam in the one he sets up and invests with the affair, and do good in the call to the oneness of God Almighty under his imamate.

For if they do this and stand by these conditions, then there is no harm upon them in looking into those doctrines and sciences that belong to the peoples, and in pondering them, and there is no straitness in their religions in doing so, for these are an aid to them in what they need in withstanding the enemies, and against whoever seeks to impugn the ranks. This is the interpretation of the verse, and in it there is no license to abandon the employing of the Law; it is only a license to look into the sciences upon the conditions mentioned. And the whole of the matter is this: every interpretation that leads to making lawful what God has forbidden, or to forbidding what He has made lawful, or to altering the ranks of the ranks of God Almighty, and that becomes a slur upon the Law and the rites of the upright religion, and a summons to abandon working by it and to acquire felicity by refraining from it, is a corrupt interpretation. Our masters, the pure Imams (عليهم السلام), are quit before God Almighty of what he interpreted, and of every interpretation that opposes what the Prophet (ع) brought.

### The Twenty-Fourth Section of the Eighth Chapter

The author of al-Nusrah said:

The decree is the beginning, the measuring is the middle, and the completion is the end, for the measuring can only be by the decree.

Then he said:

And if the decree were the end, it would not be right for it to be named a decree, for the decree comes before all else, and things come to be after the decree, and the decree is for some thing, whereas the completion is not for a thing; rather the completion is for the very thing that has become complete.

We say:

In what has gone before of the discussion, the answer concerning the decree and the measure is already folded up, and there is in it sufficiency, without exhausting the discussion of it and drawing it out at length, with what might then be ascribed to us of other than what we intended.
