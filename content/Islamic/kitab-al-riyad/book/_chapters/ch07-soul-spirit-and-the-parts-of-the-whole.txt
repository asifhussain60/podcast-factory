# Soul, Spirit, and the Parts of the Whole

The judgment now to be given among the ranks concerns what has been said of the soul and of al-Hayuli (الهيولى), the dark prime matter: whether the two of them resemble the First, or do not.

**The First Section**

The author of al-Islah said:

Concerning what the author of al-Nusrah has established, we do not say that nature and the dark prime matter are born from the Second in the way that the Second is born from the First. For that world is the world of honor and virtue, while this world is the world of darkness and turbidity. The Second, though it is born from the First, resembles him; but its condition is not like the condition of al-Hayuli.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

This statement is an error, because if that world were luminous with no darkness in it, and this world dark with no light in it, then no connection would fall between this world and that world. Rather we say that the soul has two extremities: one extremity toward the intellect, and it lies upon the intellect's horizon, and this is the luminous, noble extremity; and another extremity toward nature, and nature lies upon its own horizon, and this is the one in which there is some darkness and turbidity, because nature falls upon its horizon. And likewise we say that nature has two extremities: one toward the partial soul, and it is the one in which there is some light, because it falls upon the soul's horizon; and the other extremity facing the elements, and it is the dark, turbid one in which there is no light. So by these divisions we speak of soul and of nature, of its darkness and its light; and we do not say that that world is wholly the world of light with no darkness in it, nor that this world is wholly a world of darkness with no light in it. For were it as he described it, there would be between them neither neighboring nor coupling.

He said:

So the statement is sound that the dark al-Hayuli is born from the soul, though it does not resemble it, just as the soul is born from the First though it does not resemble him. And al-Hayuli became dark only because of its distance from the First Cause, on account of its being born from the soul; and likewise the deficiency befell the soul only because it is more distant from the First Cause, I mean the Antecedent. This is his statement.

We say:

The form of what the author of al-Islah said in his book is other than what the author of al-Nusrah has established of it. For the kernel of his statement, which is a response to the statement of the author of al-Mahsul that the Second is born from the First and does not resemble him, is that he said: We do not say thus. Rather we say that the Second is born from the First, and it does not resemble him yet is his counterpart, and though the First is of loftier rank and nobler station, they are as the male and the female in all genera and substances. For the male, though he is higher and nobler than the female, each of the two is the counterpart of its fellow and its likeness.

Then we say:

The Follower is too noble and too lofty for its way with what comes after it to be as its way with the First. For the First and the Second are two counterparts, and they are the mine of honor, of perfection, and of exaltation; there is no honor above them. God has made them the mine of perfect honor, of perfect light, of perfect exaltation, and of perfect knowledge, with no ignorance there, no turbidity, and no darkness whatsoever. The two of them are the foundations of the higher world, which is the mine of purity, of al-Quds (sanctity), and of life. And al-Hayuli is a trace of the Second, which is the substance of this world; and it is a turbid, dark substance that is described by neither mercy nor power. The Second brought it forth in act from itself, and by the power derived from the First; then it brought forth from it the form, which is nature, in act.

Then the author of al-Islah said:

It is not permissible to say that al-Hayuli is born from it; rather we say that it is a trace of it, and therefore does not resemble it. And the Second is born from the First and does resemble him, even though the First is of loftier rank. This is his statement. Its gist is that the Second issues from the First and is like him, even though the First is loftier in rank than it; and that al-Hayuli is a trace of the Second, and its substance is a dark, turbid substance, because the way of al-Hayuli with the Second is not as the way of the Second with the First. For the First and the Second are counterparts, whereas al-Hayuli does not resemble the Second, since it is a trace of it.

And if this is the gist of his statement, then what the author of al-Nusrah answered is no answer. The author of al-Islah did not make it obligatory in his statement that that whole world is light and radiance; rather he said, describing the condition of the First and the Second, that God Most High made them the mine of honor and exaltation. And that statement of his is no proof that there is no existence for whatever is not in a condition like theirs. In what has already preceded of the demonstration that al-Hayuli is not born from the Second there is sufficiency; and there is no need to expose the discourse and prolong it with what might turn suspicions upon us regarding what is not our aim in this section.

We say:

al-Hayuli does not resemble the Second, nor the First, because its existence, in its issuing from the First, is by the lower proportion that we set forth in our earlier words, and in our book known as راحة العقل, the Peace of Mind. And in that is sufficiency.

**The Second Section of the Third Book**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

This statement is an error, because if that world were luminous with no darkness in it, and this world dark with no light in it, then no connection would fall between this world and that world.

We say:

This statement, in respect of its being established of the author of al-Islah as something he did not say, is an error; but in respect of its own wording it is sound. For among the world of origination and emanation is that which is shared: it suits what is above it by its being emanated, and suits what is below it by its being a body. It possesses two matters, resembling not the First from which its existence comes; it resembles the Second from one side and does not resemble it from another. The side from which it resembles is its being emanated from it; and the side from which it resembles is its being a natural body. That is the nature which, when it is joined to the world of origination and emanation, is a life flowing from that world into what is below it, granting it what it is fit to receive of persistence and of conception; and when it is joined to this world, it is a principle of motion and rest in the thing it is in essentially, not by way of accident. So nature came to be, even though in its existence it is emanated, a medium between the two emanations, the First and the Second.

**The Third Section of the Third Book**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

By the soul, meaning the Second, it has two extremities: one extremity toward the intellect and upon its horizon, and it is the luminous, noble extremity; and one extremity toward nature, and it lies upon its horizon, and it is the one in which there is some darkness.

We say:

That is not sound. The proof is that whatever has extremities is a thing possessed of dimensions measured by area. But the Second, which he named a soul on the ground of what we have explained before, is not a body; and if it is not a body, then it is void that it should have two extremities. Then he has made it obligatory, by his statement, that it has a luminous extremity upon the horizon of the intellect, and an extremity in which there is some darkness upon the horizon of nature, differing from the intellect; while the First, by what he mentioned, is upon the horizon of nature. But it is impossible that there should be in the Second anything by which it differs from the First, because its existence, in its emanation, is from the nobler proportion that is in the First, namely its being a cause.

For we have already said that the First, by its being existent, is not in its essence both cause and effect: it is a cause by relation to what is below it, and that is the nobler proportion in the First; and an effect by relation to itself, and that is the lower proportion in the First. So from its intellection of its essence as being a cause, there emanates from it its like, since it is of the nature of a cause that its effect come to be from it; and from its intellection of its essence as being an effect, there emanates from it that which is not its like, its emanation being from the lower proportion in the First, namely its being an effect. For the honor of the effect's proportion is greater than the cause's proportion; for from the cause there results elevation, and from the effect there results only what is below the effect that exists from the cause.

Witnessing to what we have mentioned is the condition upon which stands al-Asas, the Foundation, which is the likeness that stands for the Second in the knowledge of the body, and its being not different from al-Natiq, the Speaker. For all that was existent in the Foundation is existent in the Speaker from whom the existence of the Foundation came; and thus it is not different from him. Yet not all that belongs to the Speaker is existent in the Foundation, for the Speaker has that by which the Foundation differs from him, namely the vision in which the Foundation does not share. So it has become clear, by the Foundation's being, through what exists in it, a sharer with the Speaker, and by there being not found in it what is not found in the Speaker, that it is not different from him. And when it is not different from him, and it is the likeness by which, and by whose rank, the existence of the Second and its rank are inferred, then the Second is not different from the First; and when it is not different, then its existence in what he mentioned, that it is upon the horizon of nature, is impossible.

**The Fourth Section of the Third Book**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

al-Hayuli became dark only because of its remoteness from the First Cause, on account of its being born from the soul.

We say:

al-Hayuli's being unlike the First, and the Second's being so too, is not because of its remoteness from the First by the proportion upon which the First's condition stands, namely its being an effect; for the First is a confluence of two proportions, a proportion by its being a cause and a proportion by its being an effect. And the existence of what exists from the proportion that is an effect is not like the existence of what exists from the proportion that is a cause, for the cause is forever nobler than the effect, and prior to it in rank and in nature. For of the First it is said that it is a cause and that it is an effect; yet it is a single essence, which acquires these proportions by the relations.

**The Fifth Section of the Third Book**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

The deficiency befell the soul only because it is more remote from the First Cause, I mean the Antecedent.

We say:

There is no deficiency in the Second, which he named a soul, as we have already stated, nor is it remote from the First; rather it is its like. The deficiency is a necessary consequence, not the very self of nature; and nature became deficient not from the side of its own essence, which is its perfection, but it became deficient from the side of its second perfection, which it acquires by way of benefit.

**The Sixth Section of the Third Book**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

The proportion of nature's being born from the soul is no greater than the soul's being born from the intellect, since the soul in respect of the intellect is less than nature in respect of the soul. And if it is admissible that the soul be born from the intellect, with its perfection, its nobility, and its nearness to the First Cause, then it is admissible that there be born from the soul the like of nature; for nature is more similar to the soul in all its conditions, in its acts and its powers, than the soul is similar to the intellect in its acts and its powers. For nature, among the ancients, is marked as the principle of motion, and some of them said that it is the active power; and if you look upon nature from these two definitions, you find in it the ampler portion and the more abundant share. For the soul, among the first ones, is the moving, abiding substance, and it is withal marked with intellect; yet there is nothing in the intellect of the mark of the soul's definition, nor is the soul defined by the definition of the intellect. For the definition of the intellect is that it is the first of things, that it is the Originated, that it is the first effect, and the first intermediary; and it is not admissible to say that the soul is the first of things, nor that it is originated, nor that it is the first effect. So if it is admissible that there be born from the intellect, with its perfection, its nobility, and its nearness to the Word, the like of the soul, which is not defined by the definition of the intellect, and this is not to be denied, then it is admissible that there be born from the soul the like of nature, which resembles in its definitions the definitions of the soul, and this is not to be denied. And were one to look upon the thing from the aspect of consideration, this measure of knowledge would not be hidden. Had the author of al-Islah stood upon the wonders and the wisdom that are in nature, and upon the splendor, nobility, beauty, and comeliness in its acts, by reason of which the partial soul desires to incline toward it and to follow it, he would have known and been certain that it is born from the soul, even though it does not resemble it; for causes forever find beautiful the order of their effects. From this the partial soul came to desire to follow nature, and to know that every beauty, splendor, nobility, and adornment is from the higher, noble world. For had nature not been born from the soul, it would never have had it in itself to tell of any beauty, comeliness, or adornment; but because of its being born from the soul, it told what it saw in the world of its cause of beauty and nobility, a deficient telling, short of the telling of the soul. So know this. This is his statement.

We say:

In what has already preceded of the discourse upon the intellect, the soul, and nature, and upon the existence of that whose existence precedes another's, and upon what is besides that, there is folded the sound exposition regarding what he has adduced; and we have no need to prolong it with certainties upon what has persisted of error in conception. And in that is sufficiency.

**The Fourth Book of the Book of Riyadh, on the judgment among the two contenders concerning what has been said of the soul's being parts and traces**

**The First Section of the Fourth Book**

The author of al-Islah said:

Concerning what the author of al-Nusrah has established: as for the parts that are in us, they are traces of the First Substance, not parts of it. For our union is by the traces that are from the First Substance, not by its parts; and the union of the First is not by its parts, because the union of the First is by the very essence of the Word. The parts that are in us, though they are traces of the First, are not parts of it, nor are they united with it; rather they are united with its traces, as we have shown of the union of its products with our traces within them. And the union of the First Substance with the Word is not thus, for the First Substance was united with the very essence of the Word until it and the Word became one thing. This is his statement.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

This statement contains confusion and manifest contradictions.

This statement is confused, and its contradictions are plain. For he claimed that the parts within us are traces of the First Substance. Yet how can the parts be traces, when they are substances subsisting in their own right, while traces have no subsistence of their own? If he calls the parts within us traces of the First Substance, then, having once applied to them the name of "parts," he is bound to show of what substance they are parts. Had they not been parts of the First Substance, he would not have said of them that they are parts of any substance at all; and once it is established that they are parts of some substance, it becomes void to call them traces, for the parts of a thing are not traces of something other than it. This is his statement.

We say:

The author of al-Nusrah is right in what he said, that the souls are substances subsisting in themselves after acquisition, and that it is impossible for traces to subsist in themselves. But the author of al-Islah, in what he said, and in his refusal to give the soul the name of "part," acted as he did because he saw that the things which admit division are bodies, which are never free of the nine accidents and are grasped by the five senses. The soul is not a body, nor is what flows upon it a body; so he would not give it the name of "part," lest he run it in the course of bodies, so that it would no longer match what its own essence is and what its essence has taught him of it. When he was unwilling to do that, he said "traces," and named them as that by which the souls become substantial and endure. What he means by that is that these souls have their substantiality and their endurance through the traces of those separate substances that reach them by way of the hudud, who are the intermediaries (عليهم السلام). And if that is so, then it is clear that the intention of the author of al-Islah, in saying "traces," is what runs in the soul the course of the form, which is that by which the substantiality of the souls is completed, and by which they endure when they receive it. That is what he was obliged to affirm where he says: we said that honor and merit belong over these worlds only because we received the trace of that noble substance in full, and were prepared to receive it. So it is established that by "trace" he means that which receives; and that the intention of the author of al-Nusrah, in his word "part," is what runs in the soul the course of the hayula, which is that which receives; and that this is a likening and an approximation to the understanding.

The Second Section

As for the author of al-Nusrah's saying that the souls are parts of the Universal Soul, this rests upon no proof that stands. For whatever has been divided, its whole is the totality of those parts; and if its whole is the totality of those parts, then he has made it necessary that the souls be parts of the Universal Soul. But as for the souls in the abode of this world, what has been established by proof, in what came before and after, makes it plain that the Second, which he named the Soul, has its existence not in the abode of the body, which is the abode of this world. Since it is not in this world, it is void that the Second be a whole of these souls; and once it is void that it be a whole of these souls, these are not parts of it.

The Third Section

Then his assertion that they are parts of the Universal Soul does not hold together with his own intention that the Universal Soul is the Second. For if the soul has parts belonging to the Second, the matter cannot escape one of these: either the whole of them, which is the Second, is in the abode of this world; or some of them are in this world and some in the higher world; or it is not, in the sense of partition, in this world at all. It is void that the whole which is the Second should be, in its entirety, in the abode of this world, since what is in this world subsists by potentiality, and has no passage into actuality except through what already subsists in actuality; and the First Intellect is precluded from being that by which the souls subsisting in potentiality pass into actuality, because of what that would require of the precedence of something whose existence is impossible for God, glory be to Him, according to what has been discussed in a book other than this. And it is void, too, that some be in this world and some in the higher world, since the part in the higher world, were it like this one in the world, subsisting by potentiality just as this one's condition is, would be voided by the proof already established that the existence of the Second is an existence subsisting in actuality, not like the existents of the abode of this world, which subsist by potentiality. So, once the two divisions are void, it is established that the Second is nothing of partition in this world, and it is void that the souls be parts of it.

The Fourth Section

Then, concerning parts: whatever is given to a whole is given to all its parts, in all their states, according to their measure. If these souls were parts of the Second, and we have found them neither knowing nor wise, then the whole whose parts are of this description would be of this same measure; and to hold such a belief concerning that mighty hadd is unbelief. We seek refuge in God, when it is known that the materials of the Messengers (عليهم السلام) are from it, and that it is the proximate cause of the overflowing upon the lower world. The souls, then, are not parts of the Following. Since it is void that these souls be parts, we say that the souls are of the nature of those separate things whose existence is an originated existence; their manifestation and their existence are only from between the two worlds, the natural and the emanated. It is a natural power that attains, by what it acquires from the direction of the hudud through obedience and the two worships, so that there is emanated from its essence an abiding, abstracted form, which resembles the abstracted things in the abode of the Hereafter on one side, and differs from them on another.

Hence we say:

It is the second emanation, and our epistles on the Return contain, God willing, the mention of that.

The Fifth Section

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Likewise, our traces united with our products do not receive our traces after we have finished the work; nor do the products prepared to receive them from us accept anything further, except by the entry of corruption upon them and their change from their states, because they are not parts of us, nor are they substances subsisting in themselves; rather they are vanishing accidents. This is his statement.

We say:

What is meant by that is that bodies reach a limit in receiving what they can receive, a limit beyond which they receive nothing further. It is like iron held in the fire: once it reddens, that is its utmost, and it grows no hotter; and like a black body, which grows no blacker, for it may reach a limit of blackness beyond which it is not further blackened. That, in meaning, is more fitting to be held than what his own expression requires. It is not the traces that are the receiver; the receiver is other than them, namely the body set down for the succession of accidents and traces upon it.

The Sixth Section

The author of al-Nusrah said:

As for the rational souls within us, they cease not to acquire from the light of the higher, noble world, and to increase thereby in nobility in their essence, without any change occurring in their essence, or any corruption entering upon them from the Soul when it wishes to benefit them. So it is established that they are parts and substances of the Soul, not traces of it.

We say:

The matter of the soul's being unlimited in receiving is as he said, and it is one of the proofs that the soul is not a body. But the matter of the soul's being parts of the Second is not as he held and set forth by what it entails and encloses, of what has already been discussed. The best to which the matter can be ascribed, in his saying that, for one who intends by "parts" a sound meaning, is that these souls, which are the receivers, when they intellect their own essences by that which is such, then they and what they intellect are one thing: it is that, and that is it, distinguished from it only by rank. So the soul is like the part of a thing in which is found what is found in the whole of it, their way in their essence being one and the same from this side.

The Seventh Section

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Again, to say that the rational souls existing in this world are not parts of the Universal Soul nullifies prophethood, the imamate, the executorship, revelation, and the sacred laws. For it would not then be permitted to the soul to grasp that which influences it, or to encompass it, or to benefit from it. Yet the prophets (عليهم السلام) claimed that they beheld the noble upper world, and stood upon the spiritual hudud, and benefited from what they delivered to those after them of light and clear exposition, from those above them, the two roots and the three branches; and that they beheld the angels. If their pure souls were traces of the Universal Soul, they would not have been able to comprehend what was revealed to them, nor to stand upon the spiritual entities which are parts of the Universal Soul.

We say:

It has already been said that the souls are neither parts nor traces, but a second emanation; that the meaning of the one who called them "traces" is that by which the substantiality of the souls is completed and which earns them endurance; and that the meaning of the one who called them "parts" is that, by their intellecting their own essence in that manner, they are like those things which are their causes. Neither prophethood is nullified by this, nor the imamate, nor anything else, for it is established that these ranks are not from this side. The prophets (عليهم السلام) were able to grasp those things absent from them, from the upper world, only after they reached the rank of perfection, so that by it they came to be at the utmost of completeness; and when they return to their own essences, it is as though they had grasped those transcendent essences, being, from the side of completeness, one thing with them, with no difference except in rank. We have set out, in our book known as *Rahat al-Aql*, enough of that to satisfy and to heal, for whoever would know it.

The Eighth Section

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Rather, the necessary thing to say is that the Intellect is originated through the Word, that the Soul is the image of the Intellect, that nature is born of the Soul, and that the rational souls are parts of the Universal Soul, so that by this order tawhid, reward, punishment, prophethood, the imamate, the sacred laws, and revelation may become sound.

We say:

The soundness of tawhid, of reward and punishment, of prophethood and the imamate, of the mission and of the laws drawn from revelation, and their establishment, is not from the side that the Intellect is originated through the Word, and that the Soul is the image of the Intellect. Rather, their establishment is from another side, which we have set out in our epistles and our writings concerning them. As for the Intellect, it is not permitted to say that it is originated through the Word, for that would require the precedence of the Word over the Intellect, on account of the disorder it brings into the paths of tawhid, according to what we have expounded in our discourse.

Then we say:

The Intellect is nothing but the essence of the Word, and the Word is nothing but the essence of the Intellect. This is confirmed by the saying of our master the Imam al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah (ع), in the book *Ta'wil al-Sharia*, where he speaks of the intelligibles: that the Word is an expression of the Intellect, and that these names are merited by it by relation. And there has already preceded, in our exposition and our discourse upon the Originator, the Intellect, the Word, and what is said of the world of origination and of the first emanation, in *al-Risala al-Mudi'a* and in the rest of the epistles, what suffices the inquirer. As for the Soul and nature, they are two emanations from the First Intellect, each of them according to its own relation; and for that reason one of them became more excellent than the other, which is the first emanation, while the rational souls are the second emanation. What ran in the first of them is emanated in the last of them. Our singular epistles on the Return contain, of the mention of that, what makes length unnecessary, for expansiveness of speech upon the like of that, in every place, is forbidden; a gesture suffices the seeking inquirer, while another discards it for the difficulty of its reach and the hardness of its knowledge. God grants the knowing believer success to know that according to his strength in sincerity to God and to His friends, and aids him when he is sincere to His face, exalted be He, and gives his allegiance to Him and to His friends. May God make us among the followers who receive, who keep faith with His covenant, and grant us success to praise Him, by His grace and His majesty.
