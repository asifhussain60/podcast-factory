# Decree and Destiny

Chapter Eight of the Book of Riyad, on the judgment between the two sages, concerns decree and destiny.

**Section One**

The author of al-Islah said:

As for the claim that the decree is prior and the destiny posterior, this is wrong, because destiny is before decree, and the prior is before the posterior. It is not permissible that the decree, which comes after destiny, should be the very thing that stands before the posterior. Decree and destiny are two words whose meaning is known from ordinary speech: destiny is estimation, and decree is elaboration, and there is no elaboration except after estimation. The first is before the second by the farthest measure of priority. The evidence for this from the Book of God is His saying:

> قضي الامر الذي به تستفتيان
>
> "The matter concerning which you two sought a verdict has been decreed,"

that is, it has been separated out and finished; and His saying:

> فاذا قضيت الصلاة
>
> "When you have finished the prayer,"

that is, when you have completed it. The likeness of the decree is that of a garment which the tailor first estimates. He estimates it before he cuts it, and while it is still in estimation it can be increased, decreased, widened, or narrowed; but once it has been cut, it is finished and completed, and no further increase in it is possible.

The upshot of his saying is this: decree is not said of the prior, for it comes after destiny; and destiny is not said of the posterior, for it comes before decree. Destiny is estimation, and decree is elaboration; so long as the thing remains within estimation it can be increased, decreased, and widened, but once it has been elaborated and decreed, no increase in it and no decrease from it is possible.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Decree is not elaboration, for the state of the very elaboration within the decree is no less than the state of the thing separated out; the separated thing is fixed in its form only by a decree that requires it, just as the separated is itself established only by what has been decreed for it. As for destiny, it is estimation, as he said; yet there is no estimation except through a decree that requires that estimation. As for the evidence he cited from the Book of God the Exalted, that decree is a completing, this is not to be rejected, for the sages have said that when God the Exalted originated the Intellect He completed the two worlds, since the Intellect is the sum of the images of the two worlds, and nothing of the images of the worlds is absent from it; it is pure completion. Yet the author of al-Islah erred in likening destiny and decree to the garment that the tailor estimates. He made the tailor's estimation correspond to destiny and his elaboration correspond to decree, and this likeness is not sound. He ought rather to have made the image of the sewing that lies in the tailor's own soul correspond to the decree, because it is spiritual; and the estimation by which the tailor estimates that sewing correspond to destiny, for it too is a spiritual thing that has not yet emerged to the limit of intellect, and this is the meaning of the image of the two origins. Then the elaboration that he carries out after the estimation, upon the corporeal boundaries that appeared from the first and the second, corresponds to what is made manifest. If the matter is as we have described, then decree precedes destiny, just as the sewing precedes the estimation; and by that sewing the tailor is made ready for the estimation by which he estimates. So it is established that decree is prior and destiny posterior. The upshot of his saying is that decree comes before destiny, that there is no estimation except by decree, and that decree is the prior and destiny the posterior.

We say:

The interpretation that the two sages have given of decree and destiny, each according to what he has invoked, even though the meaning of the two words, on the basis of what we have shown, makes such an interpretation impossible, comes in every case to this: that decree and destiny are the prior and the posterior. The author of al-Islah has bound himself, by his saying that destiny is estimation and decree is elaboration, and by the likeness he drew from the garment and the image of the sewing, to holding that destiny is what stands in potency, what could yet be, and that decree is what has been brought into act, having emerged from the door of possibility, which is potency, into actuality. But if the meaning of destiny and decree is this, then it is impossible to speak of them as the prior and the posterior at all, in any respect; for neither of them stands in potency, nor was it in potency and then emerged into act, according to the proofs we have already set down.

**Section Two**

And the author of al-Nusrah bound himself, by his saying that there is no estimation except through a decree that requires that estimation, and by his representing decree with the craft of sewing from which the estimation comes, to placing destiny, which he made posterior, while granting that the meaning of destiny is, as the author of al-Islah established it, an existence standing in potency. Yet this cannot be said of the posterior, since it has already been established from what we said above that the posterior stands in act; and if it could be said, then destiny would not be posterior. And if destiny is not posterior, then decree is the more fitting to be the prior, and it is more proper still that there never be a state in which it was in potency and then emerged into act, so that what its word conveys would match what actually holds of the prior. It was likewise impossible that decree and destiny be spoken of as the prior and the posterior. And since it is impossible, we say that their saying is correct in the way we shall explain, not according to what they intended; for the word "decree" shares its meaning among several things, and all those things belong to the world of body, not to the world of origination; and likewise destiny.

**Section Three**

As for decree, it points, first, to the Speaker who stood in potency and then emerged into act by the support of God the Exalted, and to those who stand in his place (عليهم السلام), who have reached in their attainment the rank parallel to the First Emanation. All of these (عليهم السلام), in their standing in act, in command, prohibition, and decree within the summons that stands in act, after they had been in potency, correspond to the meaning that the word "decree" indicates. This is what the author of al-Nusrah intends, and upon this his saying, and his placing of decree first, is correct.

**Section Four**

Decree points, second, to what is brought out by interpretation from the potency of the Revelation and the Law: the sciences that are not set forth plainly in them but are present in them in potency, brought out into exposition and act, by which souls are taken out from the limit of potency to the limit of act. This is what the author of al-Islah intends by his interpretation of the word "decree."

**Section Five**

Decree points, third, to what emerges into act, and the latency of events and the transformations destined to arise in the world through the motion of the things that move and the stillness of the things that are still, which admit no reversal. It is for this reason that, when some matter comes to pass and is realized in act, it is said, "There is no reversing the decree of God." These are the things of which decree is spoken.

**Section Six**

As for the Qa'im, he is, first, the promised Qa'im, foretold from the time of Adam (ع) and by his summons, whose guarding and upholding is carried out by the Imams and the Messengers (عليهم السلام) through their summoners in the period of concealment, extending the force by his being destined to emerge from estimation into act and existence. His standing summons, from the side of the boundaries of the cycle of the Prophet (ع) and his household in the concealment, runs the course of the qa'im-in-potency, not the manifest course that runs as the qa'im-in-act. For this reason the interpretation of Laylat al-Qadr (ع) refers to him, and to him point the allusions of the shaykhs in their saying:

> كوني (قدر)
>
> "Be al-Qadr."

**Section Seven**

Destiny points, second, to what the Prophet (ع) and his household brought of the Revelation, the Law, the rulings, and the parables, which hold within their potency the sciences and the knowledge that the soul needs in its passage to maturity and act, by being cast in a form that gathers all of that together. For this reason not every science is found in its outer face except through the interpretation of it. The saying of the author of al-Islah tends toward this meaning of destiny.

**Section Eight**

Destiny points, third, to the world with its parts, which has within its power to emerge into being and act across the passage of days, months, years, events, and existents, whose existence comes from it through the perpetual motion of the movers over what is below them, this being necessary because they are destined within it. For this reason it is said, "What God has destined will be," for the destined comes to be by the necessity of its existence, and though its term be long, it is as the thing already in act. Hence God the Exalted said:

> إِنَّهُمْ يَرَوْنَهُۥ بَعِيدًۭا ونراه قريباً
>
> "They see it as far, and We see it as near."

So whatever was within estimation, that it should come to be, is decree when it comes to be, since the word "decree" indicates what was in potency and then emerged into act. These are the meanings of decree and destiny.

If one considers the words of the author of al-Islah, and his saying that destiny precedes decree, its meaning has no sound sense except in what the Prophet (ع) and his household brought of the Revelation and the Law, which precedes the decree that is the interpretation, as he did and interpreted, God's mercy be upon him. And the saying of the author of al-Nusrah, that decree precedes destiny, when it is pondered, has no sound sense except in the Speaker (ع), the one standing in act, who is the decree that precedes what he brought of the Revelation and the Law, which is destiny.

**Section Nine**

The author of al-Nusrah said, reporting from the wise scholars: when God the Exalted originated the Intellect, He completed the two worlds, for it is the assembly of the images of the worlds.

We say:

The Intellect that is the assembly of the images of the worlds is not the First originated Intellect, nor the Second Intellect, which is the Second Emanated; rather it is the Intellect that ascends from the world of nature, by acquisition in deed and in knowledge, to a parallel with the First Emanation, so that it is emanated a second emanation and thereby becomes an assembly of the images of the worlds despite their multitude. Upon its essence are imprinted the forms of all the existents, as is the case with the Speakers, the Executors, and the Imams (عليهم السلام). The First Intellect is too exalted to have this kind of multiplicity, since its existence is by origination, and the multiplicity of originated existence is only according to what it intellects of its own essence, that it is a cause and an effect, and that its existence is from what it received therein from what is outside it alone. As for the rest of the existents, I mean the intellects emanated in a first emanation, their multiplicity is according to what I have said of their essences and of what precedes them in existence, back to the First Intellect, while their existence is nonetheless from that in which they found their godward reach, from what is outside themselves. So the later in existence is multiple, but not with the multiplicity of the prior in existence; and its need to be multiple, for the intellection of all the existents, is a necessary need, from which the first in existence is free in its completeness. If this is so, then it has become clear that the one that is the assembly of the images of the two worlds is not the First Intellect, but the intellects emanated from the abode of nature in a second emanation.

Then we say:

The First Intellect is not bound to intellect what is below it, nor to represent it, not on account of its standing in eternity, completeness, subsistence, unity, and immunity, but on account of that from which it came to exist. And since its being is eternal, complete, subsisting, one, single, immune from transformation, on account of that from which it came to exist, exalted be He, it is impossible for it to intellect what is below it, for it has no need of that.

And likewise we say:

The one that is later in existence, from the second emanation, comes to be bound to intellect what precedes it in existence, so that it becomes an assembly of the images, because the completeness in which lies its subsistence he acquires by his intellecting of that whose existence precedes his own. And since he is in need, in his completeness, of intellecting that which precedes his existence, from among the boundaries upon whom the interpretive summons was established, which is the second worship, for the making clear of their ranks, he is bound to teach them and to intellect them. And when he teaches them and intellects them, his essence becomes multiple thereby from the side of the multitude of its intellected objects, and becomes one from the other side, which is its essence, so that it resembles thereby what precedes it in existence. Our book known as Rahat al-Aql contains, of the proofs of the Law's evidence for this and of the religious depictions, what suffices and heals the seeker for his return. May God make us among those who follow His good pleasure in what grieves and what gladdens, and who follow His friends (عليهم السلام) in that regard.

**Section Ten**

The author of al-Islah said:

The error of those who made decree the prior and destiny the posterior arose only from this: that they found the scholars had called the posterior by the name of destiny, which is indeed its true name, because all the entities were emanated from the prior as things destined, and that emanation was destiny itself, so the sages named it destiny. And "decree and destiny" ran together in the speech of the people, and they found them placing decree before destiny in speech, saying "this is decree and destiny." The meaning of decree and destiny was hidden from them, so they assigned decree to the first and destiny to the second, and an error arose from this side. This is as we have said: that all the entities were emanated from the prior as things destined, and that emanation was destiny itself, which is the assembly of the entities. The confirmation of this is the saying of God the Exalted:

> إِنَّا كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقْنَهُ بِقَدَرٍۢ
>
> "Indeed, We have created everything according to a measure."

For just as all the entities were emanated from the First, which is the command in the destined potency, and were destiny itself, so there appeared from the Second, in act, out of matter and form, and that act was decree itself. Destiny is that in which change and alteration occur, and it is the book of which God the Exalted said:

> يَمْحُوا۟ ٱللَّهُ مَا يَشَآءُ وَيُثْبِتُ
>
> "God erases what He wills and confirms."

Destiny is the essence of the posterior, as we have said, and decree is its act, because the posterior is what is destined by the prior. The upshot of his saying in this section is that destiny is the name of the posterior, because all the entities were emanated from the prior as things destined, as God the Exalted said:

> إِنَّا كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقْنَهُ بِقَدَرٍۢ
>
> "Indeed, We have created everything according to a measure,"

and that decree is its act.

The author of al-Islah said:

As for what he advanced concerning decree and destiny, namely the saying of God, the Exalted:

> إِنَّا كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقْنَهُ بِقَدَرٍۢ
>
> "Indeed, We have created everything according to a measure,"

he has erred in his interpretation of this verse. Upon the interpretation we set out and hold, the verse is wholly innocent of his reading, for by it He intends only pure ipseity.

We say:

The "We" by which He intends a subject untouched by attribute and by no-attribute, and "every thing" by which He intends the intellect, which is the totality of things, and "We have created it" by which an image is intended, are such that the two worlds are among them; and "a measure," by which the posterior is intended, is that upon which the matter is made firm. All the spiritual and physical images appeared among these in the world of witness, which is the pen, the tablet, the writing, and the writer. The writer stands in the place of "We," which is pure ipseity. The pen stands in the place of the intellect, and it is the pen of pens. "We have created it" stands in the place of the tablet. The pen answers to the letters that appear upon the tablet, and "a measure" answers to the tablet itself, which is receptive to all the forms of the letters. Just as all things physical and spiritual, the simple and the dense, fall beneath these intelligible letters that appear from the writer, by the pen, upon the tablet, so likewise all the images above and below were brought forth by God the Exalted by means of the intellect and the soul, and nothing among them was withheld, just as nothing of the things is withheld from the ordered letters that appear in act from the writer upon the tablet. This is the position of the author of al-Nusra in his refutation.

We say:

The two authors have set down in this chapter what cannot stand, neither for the one in al-Islah nor for the other in the refutation. For the author of al-Islah held it necessary that destiny be the name of the posterior, and argued for this from the saying of God the Exalted, "Indeed, We have created everything according to a measure." Yet the discussion already given upon destiny and decree shows that destiny may not be predicated of the posterior. The author of al-Nusra took the rebuttal of al-Islah to be its interpretation of the verse, but that is neither balanced nor congruent with the matter. For al-Islah said, by way of interpretation:

God the Exalted, by "We," intends only pure ipseity, which no attribute taints and no no-attribute taints; and the word أنا is only at the limit from which the Prophet draws by taking his station in the world, not pure ipseity. How could "We" fall upon pure ipseity, when between Him and it lies the intermediary of negation? Rather it lies upon the near intermediary of the Messenger of God (ع) in the emanation upon him, and the meaning is the Prophet, since he is the one who conveys from Him.

Section Eleven of Chapter Eight

His saying, "every thing is what He intends by the intellect, which is the totality of things," fails, for "every thing" in this place is not the intellect. We have already shown that the first intellect is not multiple in the way that thing is multiple whose existence is "every thing." That is rather what the community needs from the two worships that gather the sciences, outwardly by deed and inwardly by the perception that leads to deliverance from the torment of the worlds.

Section Twelve of Chapter Eight

His saying, "We have created it," does not intend what he supposed, for "We have created it" means "We have measured it." That is, We made the form of the revelation and the Law a form having within its power every thing of the sciences and the religious works that the community needs to ward pain from the soul in this world and the next. It is as though He said, glorified and exalted be He: "We, everything We have created according to a measure," of the matter of the two worships in knowledge and deed that the community needs in its religion and its worldly life. "We have measured it" means: We brought it in measured form in what We sent down of the revelation and the Law; that is, We placed it within her power, that it might come forth in interpretation to the one who merits it.

Section Thirteen of Chapter Eight

The author of al-Islah said:

Decree is the posterior act. Here he hit the mark from one side, for what comes forth into act from the world of nature comes forth by its outpouring, and by it is completed, and from its side attains its completion. For this reason decree came to be predicated of the speaker, since his coming forth into act and his attainment of perfection are by its outpouring and its support.

Section Fourteen of Chapter Eight

His saying, "all the essences were emanated from the prior as things measured," contains a contradiction, because the posterior is an essence emanated from the prior and is not measured. What is meant by that saying is only the emanation from which the physical world arose, which was measured so that things might come forth from it, in the way we explained in the chapter of destiny; it is not the posterior.

Section Fifteen of Chapter Eight

The author of al-Nusra said:

In the passage whose opening is, "We have created it, by which He intends the image of the two worlds among them, and a measure, by which He intends the posterior upon which the matter is made firm, and all the spiritual and physical images appeared among them," the case is not that the existence of things was bodily and not bodily, as he set it down, by the corruption of what we showed in the foundation upon which he built his words. Were it not that the discussion of the order in which existent things follow upon the first lies outside the bound of this writing, we would bring it forth, and it will not fail to be mentioned, God the Exalted willing.

Section Sixteen of Chapter Eight

The author of al-Islah said:

As for the interpretation of the saying of the Messenger of God (ع), "I flee from the decree of God to His destiny":

> أفر من قضاء الله إلى قدره
>
> "I flee from the decree of God to His destiny,"

he intended only to flee from the subtle support of the prior to the plain declaration of the posterior, as a man flees from fire, which is the sign of support, to water, which is the sign of declaration. He wished to flee from the thing already sealed and decreed, whose repelling cannot be arranged, toward the destiny that has not yet been decreed. This is the saying, and it is not well ordered.

The Messenger of God (ع) fled from the decree of God to His destiny for a cause, and the cause was the leaning wall whose fall he feared, so he quickened his walking and said this, wishing to strike for them a parable. The leaning wall is a building that has inclined to fall, and it stands for one, at a certain limit, who has resolved upon something of treachery and hypocrisy and inclined from obedience toward corruption; for if he turns treacherous he falls from his station. As God the Exalted said:

> فَوَجَدَا فِيهَا جِدَارًۭا يُرِيدُ أَن يَنقَضَّ فَأَقَامَهُۥ
>
> "And they found in it a wall about to collapse, so he set it upright."

I mean, one of the limits had resolved upon hypocrisy and rupture, so he set it upright until it held firm and neither broke nor fell. Such was the leaning wall past which the Messenger of God (ع) quickened his walking: he had recognized in one of the limits that it had sensed hypocrisy and inclined to rupture, so he addressed it in the outward, by the speed of walking, for the posterior, in walking, signifies declaration, proof, haste, and swiftness of pace in the outward. So he (ع) would address the one who had resolved upon hypocrisy with admonition and warning in the outward, without the declaration of the inward. The destiny is upon the outward, as we have explained, and the decree is upon the inward. His flight from the decree of God and His destiny was for the sake of this wall, in caution against its fall, in caution over himself, and not in flight from the honor with which God the Exalted had honored him, from the degree to which God had raised him.

The author of al-Nusra said:

And he is the more eager for increase.

The interpretation of "I flee from the decree of God to His destiny" is not as the author of al-Islah imagined, namely that he was fleeing from the rank of the posterior to the rank of the prior, on the ground that the prophets (عليهم السلام), by his account, forever seek elevation, and the seeking of loftiness is not the seeking of lowliness. But we say concerning it, by the help of God the Exalted and His blessing, that the meaning here turns upon three senses, and each of them establishes that decree is upon the prior and destiny upon the posterior.

The first, and the greatest, most fitting, and most exact in meaning, is that what he (ع) intended in his flight from the decree of God to His destiny is this: that from the prior comes support, from the posterior composition, from the speaker the authorship of the Law, and from the foundation the interpretation of the whole. The interpretation, which is the end, is more like support, and authorship is more like composition. So the speaker (ع), in this saying, made plain that he resembles, in his authorship of the Laws, the posterior in its composition of the world, and does not resemble the prior. He fled, then, from the decree of God, which is the prior, and did not resemble it, from the aspect that is the origination of support, toward his destiny, which is the interpretation, and to this he likened himself.

The second meaning is his making known to his community that he draws benefit from the first by means of the second, so that in all he wished to apprehend of the first he returned, by means of the second, to it. It is as though he said: I return, in all I wish to grasp, to the one who is the link between me and the intellect, which is the second. This is the second meaning.

The third meaning, and the most noble, is what the author of the book (ع) mentioned: his flight from the intensity of the light of the prior's support to the support of the posterior, as the Grandfather (الجد), for all his spiritual power, could not bear it until his identity nearly dissolved. This is his saying in the story of Moses (ع). This is what the two of them said.

We say:

As for the author of al-Islah, he moved in the interpretation of the verse in a way conformable to the truth of the matter in the meaning of decree and destiny, and it is good. It would have been better still had he said this: since the Prophet (ع) was a prophet who is informed of the hidden things among beings and events, and of what lies in men's hearts, by the revelation with which God the Exalted had singled him out, he wished to make clear how what was cast to him was opened by the Jadd. So he said, "Return," which is his saying, "I flee." That is, I cross by the Jadd, from the side of the limits of God the Exalted, the whole of what is about to occur of events and beings, which is the decree of God that goes forth inevitably into act, toward His destiny, which is God's creation, the souls and the horizons and their motions that point to what He has measured within them to be, so that there is opened to me what we made plain in the chapter of revelation. For this God the Exalted said:

> سنريهم آياتنا في الآفاق وفي أنفسهم حتى يتبين لهم أنه الحق
>
> "We shall show them Our signs upon the horizons and within themselves, until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth."

And those who reflect upon the creation of the heavens and the earth say:

> ربنا ما خلقت هذا باطلاً
>
> "Our Lord, You did not create this in vain."

This does not merely indicate what has come before and what has come after in the existence of beings; rather it signifies it and speaks it. It is as though he said: I return, and the whole of what is cast to me by the Jadd is carried to the destiny, so that it is shown, or opened, by its representation to me and its conception. That is his saying, "I flee from the decree of God to His destiny," and this is a kind of revelation. The book راحة العقل contains an explanation of that.

Section Seventeen of Chapter Eight

As for the author of al-Nusra, his manner in the answer he gave is proof of his submission to the author of al-Islah, since he brought forward nothing that overturns what al-Islah built his words upon and made plain, only the meanings he set down, supposing them to be its interpretation, saying that each of them establishes decree upon the prior and destiny upon the posterior. This does not follow from the speaker's resembling the posterior in his authorship of the Law, as he set it down, nor from his returning, in all he wished to apprehend, to the posterior, as he affirmed. It obliges him to establish decree upon the prior and destiny upon the posterior only if there is a correspondence between decree and destiny and the meanings he expounded, and there is none at all. Had the meaning of decree and destiny corresponded to the state of the first and the second, and been fitting to them from some aspect, it would have been permissible to say that decree and destiny fall upon them from that aspect. But decree means what has already been discussed, and so too destiny, and it does not deserve to be predicated of the first and the second, for they are free of what their meanings require. Whoever predicates them of these has spoken as one who says of honey, whose nature is sweetness, that it is aloes, whose nature is bitterness.

Section Eighteen of Chapter Eight

As for his likening the speaker to the posterior in the second meaning, it does not conform to the law of the guiding call, because the speaker, in his standing in the world of the body and the world of religion, holds the station of the prior, while the station of the guardian in these two holds the station of the posterior in the world of origination. So the speaker and the foundation are two likenesses of the prior and the posterior, and two likenesses of the sun and the moon.

Section Nineteen of Chapter Eight

As for the third meaning, which he set down as compelling the speaker's flight, namely from the intensity of the light of the prior's support to the support of the posterior, it runs contrary to what is due. The interpretation touching the case of the Grandfather that he cited as witness needs full inquiry and thorough treatment, and the discussion of it will come. In sum, the saying of the author of al-Islah in this section is the stronger.

Section Twenty of Chapter Eight

The author of al-Islah said, interpreting the verse in the story of Moses (ع):

> رب أرني أنظر إليك قال لن تراني ولكن انظر إلى الجبل فإن استقر مكانه فسوف تراني فلما تجلى ربه للجبل
>
> "My Lord, show me, that I may look upon You. He said: You shall not see Me, but look at the mountain; if it holds firm in its place, then you shall see Me. But when his Lord manifested Himself to the mountain..."

This one sought to have disclosed to him more than what had been measured out for him of the limit of the prior. So he was told, "You shall not see Me, but look at the mountain," making known to him that he would not attain that, and commanding him to keep within what runs to him by the Jadd; and He made known that the very thing he asked, the Jadd, for all its subtlety, has no power over its like, so how should he attain what he cannot see?

And He says, "if it holds firm in its place, then you shall see Me":

> فلما تجلى ربه للجبل جعله دكا وخر موسى صعقاً
>
> "But when his Lord manifested Himself to the mountain, He made it crumble to dust, and Moses fell down senseless."

When he knew that he had erred in seeking what he could not attain, he turned in humble supplication.

When he recovered, he said:

> سبحانك تبت إليك

"Glory be to You, I repent to You." This is the saying of the author of the Islah.

The author of al-Nusra said:

> رب ارني انظر اليك قال لن تراني ولكن انظر الى الجبل

"My Lord, show me, that I may look upon You. He said, You will not see Me, but look at the mountain." By "You will not see Me" is meant: you have not the strength for that rank, nor can you bear patiently to stand upon it. The mountain is the Grandfather (الجد), with his nearness to me. If he settles upon the rank I have given him and bears what I did not disclose to him, then you too shall bear it. But the Grandfather did not bear it when his Lord was manifested to him, and his very identity all but dissolved away. This is his saying.

We say:

The interpretation to which each of these two men turned demeans Moses (ع). For when they set him at the station of the prophets and the highest rank, one who converses with the upper limits, they then cast him out of the company of the learned and counted him among the ignorant, alleging that he asked for what lies beyond his reach among the limits above him. How could this be, and the matter escape him so? The interpretation of the verse is other than what they supposed. Moses (ع) was not addressing the upper limits at all; his address was to the lower limits, at a time when he had not yet attained the rank of the Speaker. He asked of the Master of the Command what its hour had not yet come, for he was living in a time of interruption, and the people of truth were under affliction, veiling, and concealment from their adversaries and opponents.

Moses (ع) saw in himself a power by which he reckoned that, in that time of his, he might command the lifting of that ordeal from the people of truth, take vengeance upon the enemies of God, and govern the community by the governance of truth, just as the Foundation (ع) reckoned, by the power he found in himself, what he reckoned, and then repented of it when he knew he had erred, whereupon the sun was returned to him, as we have set forth by interpretation in our epistles. So Moses (ع) asked this of the Master of the Command, who is Shu'ayb (ع), when he came to him, and he was his Lord, after his charge had been made plain to him regarding the strength and capacity for governance he found within himself. That is his saying:

> رب ارني انظر اليك

"My Lord, show me, that I may look upon You," meaning: give me the command, that I may order this affair and lift the grief. He said:

> لن تراني

"You will not see Me," meaning: you will not see Me do any of that, nor will I invest you with any command at this time, for everything has its hour and its season, and this season is not the season for taking these people, that is, the community, away from their following of their own desires, nor the time for returning affairs to the laws of governance; what we say to you shall not be brought to completion.

Shu'ayb (ع) wished to alert him to his error in his request in the best of ways, and to teach him that what he sought was hard, and would not be brought to completion in that time of his even at the hands of one possessing power, might, and equipment. How much less could it be so at the hands of a man of great station in the summons, of great worldly presidency, possessed of might, wealth, men, and dominion? He said to Moses (ع): I know that this affair will not be brought to completion in this time, and that it is hard and cannot be measured out; and my withholding is not from any grudging toward you, nor from preferring to delay you from your station. But look at the mountain, meaning this man who has worldly power, equipment, and capacity.

> فان استقر مكانه فسوف تراني

"If it settles in its place, then you shall see Me," meaning: if he holds firm in what he is commanded, and is able, with the power and might he has, to establish the laying-out of the ordinances of the governance of truth, and it is brought to completion for him, then you shall see Me do what you asked of these people.

> فلما تجلى ربه للجبل جعله دكا

"And when his Lord was manifested to the mountain, He made it crumble to dust," meaning: when he commanded him to raise the beacon of truth, the affair pressed hard upon the people, and they did not accept the command; they broke the staff, the affair was confounded and thrown into disorder, and his presidency came to the brink of ruin through the people rising against him. Then Moses (ع) knew that what he had reckoned would be completed at his hand in that hour was an impossible reckoning, and that the Master of the Command, who is his Lord, knew the matter better than he. So he awoke from the confusion that warning had cast over him, and said:

> سبحانك تبت إليك

"Glory be to You, I repent to You." He retracted his word, felt remorse, and held to patience under what the Master of the Support had commanded him, until that term for which he had hired himself was spent, and the hour came, and there came about from him what came about, and his word was verified: "then you shall see Me," the affair being surrendered to you, and you doing as you will. This is the interpretation of the verse, not what is measured out by the one who lowers and raises, and it does not correspond.

The twenty-first section of the eighth chapter.

The author of al-Nusra said:

As for his citing the words:

> وخلق كل شيء فقدره تقديراً

"And He created everything and measured it out with due measure," this is the clearest proof that the measuring-out belongs to the Follower, and that the decree, which is the creating, belongs to the Preceder, since the meaning of "and He created everything" is the Preceder, because the Intellect is all things and all things are the Intellect.

We say:

The discourse on the decree and the measuring-out has already gone before, and it was made plain there that neither decree nor measuring-out may rightly be predicated of the Preceder and the Follower, and that what is meant by His words, "and He created everything and measured it out with due measure," is not what the author of al-Nusra turned to; we have already shown the corruption of that, and that it does not befit either the Preceder or the Follower that decree and measuring-out be predicated of them. Rather, what is meant by "and He created everything and measured it out with due measure" is that He set everything pertaining to the affair of religion and to the interests, whether outward or inward, within the power of the revelation and the sacred law, and measured it out according to what the revelation and the sacred law comprise of all the sciences bearing upon religion and the interests, which lie within them in potency, and which interpretation brings forth from them.

The twenty-second section of the eighth chapter.

As for his saying that the Intellect is all things and all things are the Intellect, this is a proposition that is not sound as it stands, for the corporeal world, with all it gathers, is not intellect; it is a thing. The best sense that may be attributed to his words, "the Intellect is all things and all things are the Intellect," is that the Intellect, which is the very Command of God, is the cause of the existence of all things, corporeal and non-corporeal alike, and that the existence of all things is from the Intellect; this is his intended meaning.

The twenty-third section of the eighth chapter.

The author of al-Nusra said:

God has granted the prophets license in the inner meaning of the sacred laws and in leaving off their outward use, in His words:

> ليس على الذين آمنوا وعملوا الصالحات جناح فيما طعموا اذا ما اتقوا وآمنوا وعملوا الصالحات ثم اتقوا وآمنوا واحسنوا ان الله يحب المحسنين

"There is no blame upon those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they have eaten, so long as they fear God and believe and do righteous deeds; then fear Him and believe; then fear Him and do good, and God loves the doers of good."

We say:

The verse contains, neither outwardly nor inwardly, any mention of a license to the prophets to leave off the use of the sacred laws. I do not know how this escaped him, that he should let fall such a saying without pursuing it to the end. The exposition and the interpretation of this verse are contrary to what he supposed. For God, knowing that souls do not attain happiness, nor reach the degree of perfection, nor pass from the bound of potency to the bound of act save through work and knowledge, charged the whole of mankind with the upholding of the two worships, in knowledge and in deed, that by them they might attain the two felicities in the two abodes. And since souls do not attain their perfection and their happiness save through the two worships, then whichever soul abandons both of them, or one of them, is far from attaining perfection. So it stands with the soul so long as it remains in the bond of this life: it is held between His hands until the hour of its passing, either to be in the Garden, or, should it abandon them or one of them, to be in the Fire, until God forgives it. And we have made plain in our books, and in what has preceded of this discourse, that souls are not upheld save by work and by the reforming of character, which is the greatest jihad (الجهاد الأكبر), as the Messenger of God (ع) said, and by their acquisition of the virtues through spiritual discipline and knowledge, in relation to what precedes them in existence, according to what the interpretive summons requires.

If the states of the souls in worship stand in this likeness, how can it be permitted that license be granted to the souls, or to the Speakers, or to those beneath them among the limits, to leave off work and the full reforming of souls, and what is deficient in them? Nay, the verse has an interpretation other than the one he gave it, bearing in particular upon those who uphold the summons among the preachers who call to the Tawhid of God and to the knowledge of His limits by way of the Imams (عليهم السلام). For the Speaker (ع), knowing that his community would differ after him, and that each faction of it would set apart for itself a doctrine it fashioned by its own opinions and its own deficient minds, and would prefer over the community of guidance, the guides, upon whom be the blessings of God, one for whom God has made no light; and knowing that the preachers would suffer from the divided community every hardship in their standing forth against them, in their being injured and in the assailing of their doctrine and of their masters, the pure Imams, upon whom be the peace of God, granted to the preachers who uphold the summons a license to look into the sciences that are not of their own belief, upon conditions he laid down, that these might be for them as equipment in their disputations. He said:

> ليس على الذين آمنوا وعملوا الصالحات جناح فيما طعموا اذا ما اتقوا وآمنوا وعملوا الصالحات

"There is no blame upon those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they have eaten, so long as they fear God and believe and do righteous deeds," meaning: there is no blame, that is, no constraint, upon those who uphold the summons to the Tawhid of God and to the knowledge of His limits by way of the deputies (عليهم السلام), those who make the respondents believe when they take hold of the rope of God and cleave to their guardianship, delivering them from doubts and confusions in the religion of God and bringing them to the degree of perfection and attainment; there is no blame upon them in what they have eaten, that is, in what they read of the sciences that are not of their belief, so that these become for them as a weapon with which they do battle against the adversaries in the establishing of the truth, so long as they fear and believe and do righteous deeds. This is a condition from Him.

The first condition is that their path be kept straight in obedience to God Almighty, in following His Messenger (ع) and believing in His limits and upholding the two worships together according to what He brought. The second condition, in "then fear Him and believe," is that they obey the Messenger in his legatee and in the one who upholds the affair in his stead, and that they raise the summons to him, outwardly and inwardly. The third condition, in "then fear Him and do good," is that they obey the Imam in the one he sets up and invests with the affair, and that they do well in the summons to the Tawhid of God under his imamate. When they do this and stand by these conditions, there is no harm upon them in looking into those doctrines and sciences that belong to other peoples, and in pondering them, and there is no constraint upon their religion in that; for it is a help to them in what they need for withstanding the enemies and whoever seeks to assail the limits.

This is the interpretation of the verse, and there is in it no license to leave off the use of the sacred law; it is only a license to look into the sciences upon the conditions mentioned. And the sum of the matter is that every interpretation leading to the permitting of what God has forbidden, or the forbidding of what He has permitted, or the altering of the ranks of the limits of God Almighty, and which is a slighting of the sacred law and of the rites of the upright religion, and a summons to leave off working by it and to acquire happiness by refraining from it, is a corrupt interpretation. Our masters, the pure Imams (عليهم السلام), are innocent before God Almighty of what he interpreted, and of every interpretation that contradicts what the Prophet (ع) brought.

The twenty-fourth section of the eighth chapter.

The author of al-Nusra said:

The decree (القضاء) is the beginning, the measuring-out (التقدير) is the middle, and the completion (التمام) is the end, because the measuring-out comes about only through the decree.

Then he said:

If the decree were the end, it would not be right that it be named a decree, for the decree is only before everything, and things come after the decree, and the decree is for some thing; but the completion is not for some thing, rather the completion is for the very thing that has become complete.

We say:

In what has gone before of the discourse, the answer concerning the decree and the measuring-out is already folded up, and there is in it sufficiency, without our taking in the whole of the discourse upon it and drawing it out at length in a way that might be ascribed to us as other than what we intended.
