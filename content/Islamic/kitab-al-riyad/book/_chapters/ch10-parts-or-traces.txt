**The First Section**

The author of the Reform said:

According to what the author of al-Nusrah has established: as for the parts that are within us, they are traces of the first essence, not parts of it. For our union is by the traces that come from the first essence, not by its parts; and the union of the First is not by its parts, because the First is united by the very Word. The parts that are within us, though they are traces of the First, are not parts of it, nor is it united by them; rather they are united by its traces, as we have shown in the union of its products with our traces within them. Nor is the union of the first essence with the Word of this kind, for the first essence is united with the very Word, until it and the Word became one thing. This is his statement.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

This statement holds confusion and manifest contradictions. For he said that the parts within us are traces of the first essence, yet how can the parts be traces, when they are essences subsisting in themselves? And traces have no subsistence in themselves. If he says that the parts within us are traces of the first essence, then, once the name "parts" has fallen upon them, he is obliged to show that they are parts of some essence. Were they not parts of the first essence, he would not have called them parts of some essence at all; and once it is established that they are parts of some essence, it is void that they should be traces, because the parts of a thing are not traces of another. This is his statement.

We say:

The statement of the author of al-Nusrah, that the souls are essences subsisting in themselves after acquisition, and that traces cannot subsist in themselves, is true. Yet the statement of the author of the Reform, and his refusal to give the souls the name of parthood, arose because he saw that the things which admit of division are the bodies, which are never free of the nine accidents and are grasped by the five senses. The soul is not a body, nor is that which overflows upon it a body; so he did not see fit to give it the name "part," lest he set it in the course of bodies and it fail to correspond to what its own essence is and to what it has told him of itself. Since he did not intend that, he said "traces," and named them as things that come to have essence and to endure. What he means by this is that these souls have their coming-to-essence and their endurance by the traces of those separate essences that reach them from the side of the limits, who are the intermediaries (عليهم السلام). If this is so, then it appears that the intention of the author of the Reform, in saying "traces," is that which runs in the soul in the course of the form: that by which the essentiality of the souls is completed, and by which the soul endures when it receives it. This is what he made necessary where he says: and we said only that honor and merit belong to these worlds because we received the trace of that noble essence in full, and were prepared to receive it. So it is established that by "trace" he intends that which is received; and that what the author of al-Nusrah intends by "part" is that which runs in the soul in the course of the hayula, which is that which receives. And this is by way of likeness and approximation to the understandings.

**The Second Section**

As for the statement of the author of al-Nusrah, that the souls are parts of the Universal Soul, it rests upon no proof. For that which has been divided, its whole is the sum of all those parts; and if its whole is the sum of all those parts, then he has made it necessary that the souls are parts of the Universal Soul. But concerning the souls in the abode of this world, the matter is drawn out by the proof already established, earlier and later: that the Second, which he named the Soul, has its existence not in the abode of the body, which is the abode of this world. And since it is not in this world, it is void that the Second should be a whole for these souls; and once it is void that it be a whole for these souls, then these are not parts of it.

**The Third Section**

Then his assertion that they are parts of the Universal Soul does not hold together with his intention that the Universal Soul is the Second. For the matter concerning the soul, if the souls are parts of the Second, is not free of one of these cases: either it is the whole of them, which is the Second, in the abode of this world; or some of them are in it and some in the higher world; or it is not of the Second by way of parthood in this world at all. It is void that the whole which is the Second should be in the entire abode of this world, since what is in this world subsists by potentiality and has no passage into act except by that which subsists in act; and the first Intellect is prevented from being that by which the souls subsisting in potentiality pass into act, because of what this would require of the precedence of that whose existence is impossible for God, glorified be He, upon which the discourse has already passed in a book other than this. And it is likewise void that some of them be in this world and some in the higher world, since the part in the higher world, were it like this one in the world, subsisting by potentiality just as this one is, would stand in the very condition of this one; and that is void by the proof established that the existence of the Second is an existence subsisting in act, not like the existents of this world's abode, which subsist by potentiality. So, after the voidness of the two divisions, it is established that the Second is nothing of parthood in this world, and it is void that the souls should be parts of it.

**The Fourth Section**

After the matter of the parts, we note that a whole gives to all that is under it what it gives, in all its states, according to it. So if these souls were parts of the Second, and we have found them neither knowing nor wise, then the whole whose parts they are would be of this same condition; and to hold such a thing of that mighty limit is unbelief, we seek refuge in God, when it is known that the substances of the Messengers (عليهم السلام) are from it, and that it is the proximate cause of the overflow upon the lower world. So the souls, then, are not parts of the Second. And when it was void that these souls be parts, we said: the souls are of the nature of those separate things whose existence is a creative existence; and their appearance and their existence are only from between the natural world and the world of emanation. The soul is a natural power that attains what it acquires from the side of the limits through obedience and the two worships; and there is emitted from its own self an abiding, abstracted form, which resembles the abstracted things in the abode of the Hereafter from one aspect, and differs from them from another.

Hence we say: it is the second emanation; and our epistles on the Return (al-Ma'ad) contain the mention of that, God willing.

**The Fifth Section**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Likewise, our traces united with our products do not receive our traces after we have finished the work, nor do the products prepared to receive from us receive any further, except by the entry of corruption into them and their change from their states; for they are not parts of us, nor are they essences subsisting in themselves, but rather vanishing accidents. This is his statement.

We say:

What is meant by this is that bodies reach a limit in receiving what is theirs to receive, a limit beyond which they receive nothing further, like iron heated in the fire: once it has reddened, that is its utmost, and it grows no hotter; and like a black body, which grows no blacker, for it may reach in blackness a limit beyond which it is not further blackened. That meaning is more fitting to be held than what his own phrase requires. And it is not the traces that are the receiver; rather the receiver is other than they, namely the body, which is the substrate for the succession of the accidents and the traces upon it.

**The Sixth Section**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

But the speaking souls within us do not cease to acquire from the light of the higher, noble world, and to increase thereby in nobility within themselves, without any change occurring in themselves, nor by the entry of corruption into them from the Soul when it wishes to benefit them. So it is established that they are parts and essences of the Soul, not traces of it.

We say:

The matter, as to the soul being unbounded in receiving, is as he said, and it is one of the proofs that the soul is not a body. But the matter, as to the soul being parts of the Second, is not as he held and set forth by what it entails and folds within it, of which the discourse has already passed. The best that can be ascribed to his statement, for one who intends by "parts" this, is that these souls, which are the receiver, when they intellect their own essences as such, are then one thing with what they have intellected: it is they, and they are it, distinguished from it only by rank. So it is like the part of a thing in which is found what is found in the whole of it, their way in their own essence being one and the same from this aspect.

**The Seventh Section**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Also, to say that the speaking souls existing in this world are not parts of the Universal Soul nullifies prophethood, the imamate, the executorship, revelation, and the sacred laws. For it would then not be permitted for the soul to perceive that which influences it, nor to encompass it, nor to benefit from it. Yet the prophets (عليهم السلام) claimed that they beheld the noble upper world, and stood upon the spiritual limits, and benefited from what they conveyed to those after them of light and exposition, from those above them, from the two roots and the three branches, and that they beheld the angels. Were their pure souls only traces of the Universal Soul, they would not have been able to comprehend what was revealed to them, nor to stand upon those spiritual realities that are parts of the Universal Soul.

We say:

The discourse has already established that the souls are not parts, nor traces, and that they are a second emanation; and that what is meant by the one who called them "traces" is that by which the essentiality of the souls is completed and which earns them endurance; and, in the statement of the one who called them "parts," that they, by intellecting their own essence as such, are like those things that are their cause. Prophethood is not nullified by this, nor the imamate, nor anything else; for it is established that these ranks are not from this aspect. The prophets (عليهم السلام) were able to perceive those things absent from them of the upper world only after they attained the rank of perfection, and became thereby in completeness at its utmost, so that when they return to their own essences it is as though they have perceived those transcendent essences by their very being, they and those essences being, from the aspect of completeness, one thing, with no difference save in rank. We have set out, in our book known as Rahat al-'Aql (راحة العقل), enough of that to suffice and to heal, that it may be known.

**The Eighth Section**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Rather, it is necessary to say that the Intellect is creative through the Word, the Soul is the image of the Intellect, nature is generated from the Soul, and the speaking souls are parts of the Universal Soul, so that by this order tawhid, reward, punishment, prophethood, the imamate, the sacred laws, and revelation are made sound.

We say:

The soundness of tawhid, of reward and punishment, of prophethood and the imamate, of the mission and the laws drawn from revelation, together with their establishment, is not from the aspect that the Intellect is creative through the Word and that the Soul is the image of the Intellect; rather their establishment is from another aspect, which we have set out in our epistles and our writings concerning them. As for the Intellect, it may not be said that it is creative through the Word, for that would require the precedence of the Word over the Intellect, with the disorder this brings into the paths of tawhid, according to what we have expounded in our discourse.

Then we say:

The Intellect is nothing but the very Word, and the Word is nothing but the very Intellect. This is confirmed by the statement of our master the Imam al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah (ع) in the book Ta'wil al-Shari'a (تأويل الشريعة), where he speaks of the intelligibles: that the Word is an expression of the Intellect, and that it merits these names by relation. And there has already passed, in our exposition and our discourse upon the Creator, the Intellect, the Word, and what is said of the world of creation and the first emanation, in the Illuminating Epistle (الرسالة المضيئة) and the other epistles, what suffices the seeker. As for the Soul and nature, they are two emanations from the first Intellect, each according to its own relation; and for that reason one of them became more excellent than the other, the more excellent being the first emanation, the Soul, and the speaking souls being the second emanation. So what occurred in the first of them is emanated in the last of them. Our epistles alone on the Return (al-Ma'ad) contain of the mention of that what makes lengthening unnecessary, for the expansion of speech upon the like of this in every place is forbidden, and a gesture suffices the seeking student, while another discards it for the difficulty of its reach and the hardship of knowing it. God grants success to the believing knower for the knowledge of that according to his strength in sincerity to God and to His friends, and aids him when he is sincere to His face, exalted be He, and gives his allegiance to Him and to His friends. May God make us among the followers, the receivers, who fulfill His covenant; and may He grant us success in His praise, by His grace and His glory.
