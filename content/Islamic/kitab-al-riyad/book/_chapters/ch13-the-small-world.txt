# The Small World

The Small World

Here I set myself to judge between the two, and I speak now, in the sections that follow, of the divisions of the world.

**The First Section**

The author of al-Islah said:

The whole world is composed of four jewels, four structures, and four classes of beings: three of them without humankind, the fourth being humankind. The knotted and the dissolved are all one essence. We do not say that the pair of humankind is the form of the horse; rather, the pair of the horse and its form is what belongs to the horse's own essence, just as gold is paired with silver, and ruby with emerald, so too the horse with its like. And the horse's likeness lies among the animals, in the elephant, the falcon, the lion, and the rest. Since all of these are animals, it is not permissible to say that the horse is the pair and form of humankind, for humankind is alone in possessing the rational soul and has no peer among the parts of the world and its generated beings. Rather, humankind is the counterpart of the world, because it gathers within itself all the jewels of the world. For this reason it is named the small world, since it stands as a counterpart that collects all the world's parts.

We say:

His claim that humankind has no peer among the parts of the world, and that humankind is the counterpart of the whole world, follows from his saying that the world is divided into four parts, that the horse is not in the form of humans, and that humans have no peer among the world's parts but are the equivalent of the whole world.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

The state of pairing is not only the state of union; the state of pairing is also the state of acceptance.

We say:

This dense lower world is the pair of the subtle upper world, and if you look between the two you will find the distance between them greater than the distance between the horse and the human. The horse is not of the human race except insofar as it shares in the genus of life. He made this dense lower world a pair of the spiritual world by virtue of the connection between them in the matter of benefit and beneficence: the forces of the upper world are joined to this world, and this world receives those forces from the upper world. For this he made this world the pair of that world. Likewise the human and the horse: he made the horse the human's pair because it accepts teaching from him more than the other animals accept it. This is the substance of his saying, that the horse was made a pair for humankind because it accepts teaching from him as no other animal does.

We say:

The two who investigated these matters did not rely upon sound research. The author of al-Islah divided the world into four jewels, four structures, and four classes including humankind, and that is impossible, for the divisions of the world are more numerous than what he set down. The word "world" names many things, from the center to the encompassing ocean: the sphere, the planet, fire, air, water, earth, and the generated beings. From the plain sense of his words it is clear that he means the generated beings of the world, not the world and its generated beings together. These are three genera, with no fourth, and under each of them fall many species. The first is the nearest of the four elements, and the first of it is the minerals, which are everything whose existence is confined to one place in the earth rather than another without its parts increasing; and this gathers together what melts and what does not, what can be beaten out and what cannot. The second is the plant, which is all that grows from the earth, and this gathers together the herb and the tree. The third is the animal, which is all that moves among those that have species, and this gathers together what flies and what does not. The generated beings of the world do not exceed this, and the human, whom he named the fourth, is of the genus of animal, a species among its species, set apart by consciousness.

**The Second Section**

He said:

That humankind has no peer and that the horse has no pair. This is impossible from two angles. The first is that the human is a species within the genus of animal, and insofar as it is a species it is a pair to the other species of its genus, since all species are forms of one another in respect of their being species. If humankind is a species, then it is a pair to the other species. The second is that humankind, being similar in its structure, morals, actions, conditions, motions, and natures to all the beings that preceded it in existence, is for that very reason called the small world, and so must be a pair to all things according to the correspondence and likeness within it. If this is so, then humankind, when it is good, virtuous, wise, religious, knowing, and pure, is married to the angels by the correspondence between them; when it is courageous, brave, bold, and ready to help, its pair is the lions; when it is unjust, cruel, and murderous, its pair is the wolves; when it is deceitful, its pair is the foxes; when it is a thief and a traitor, its pair is the crows, the magpies, and the cats; when it possesses guile, cunning, and force, its pair is the leopard; and when it is given to fornication, immorality, amusement, and play, its pair is the monkeys. Upon this pattern, whatever a person trains himself to resemble he comes to resemble, whether by his actions, his morals, his customs, or in any manner of his ways, and it becomes a pair to him. If this likeness is established, then it is plain that his saying is not exhausted where he claimed that humankind has no peer, for humankind has forms and pairs from many sides.

**The Third Section**

As for the author of al-Nusrah, he spoke rightly regarding the meaning of the genus, yet he erred in his saying that the horse was made a pair for humankind because it accepts the teaching of humankind. For if it becomes a pair by way of acceptance and teaching, then the horse has no greater claim than any other to be its pair, since among the animals there are those besides the horse that accept teaching and discipline; and if it is by way of species, then again the horse has no greater claim than the rest of the animal species to be its pair. In that case it has many pairs, not one alone.

In sum we say:

The pair of humankind, in truth, can only be the angels. And what we mean by "humankind" here are those possessed of all the virtues, such as the prophets, the guardians, and the imams (عليهم السلام), and those who follow in their footsteps, who uphold their obedience and worship Allah with both acts of worship. Not those beneath them among the people who are in the form of humankind while their souls are the souls of beasts and predators, as we have said and shown, like the monkeys, the donkeys, the foxes, the beasts of prey, and the wolves. Those who follow the traces of the Imams (عليهم السلام), and above them the prophets and the guardians (عليهم السلام), are distinguished from the beasts by their striving in obedience through the two acts of worship and their holding fast to command and prohibition, though they resemble the beasts in form. In this way they come to resemble the guardians (عليهم السلام), and so they are saved in their company and among them and none other. This accords with the verse:

> وَمَنْ يَتَوَلَّهُمْ مِنْكُمْ فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ

"And whoever takes them as guardian from among you, he is one of them." May Allah place us among those whose gathering and raising is with the prophets, the guardians, and the pure Imams (عليهم السلام).

**The Fourth Section**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

He mentioned Bandhaqlis, who said:

The bestial soul returns to the rational soul through love; and the rational soul, when it deems good what returns to it from her, inclines toward her with benefit and mastery and finds her image beautiful. There is no image among the bestial souls more beautiful than the image of the horse, nor any more disposed to receive the traces of the rational soul. For this he made it a pair for it. This is plain.

We say:

Had the author of al-Nusrah drawn his evidence from the Book of Allah, Lord of the Worlds, and from the words of our masters the pure Imams (عليهم السلام), for what he intends concerning the matter of the soul by way of interpretation, what is found there would have freed him from clinging to the words of Bandhaqlis. For had he and his like among the ancients lived in our time, they would have needed to draw from the seas of the knowledge of the House of Revelation, overflowing from the treasuries of Allah in His earth, and from our pure masters (عليهم السلام); and they would have turned back from much of what they believed concerning what is hidden from the senses, of which they spoke, the intellects and their ranks, the soul and its principles, and believed otherwise. For what Bandhaqlis intended in his saying is other than what the author of al-Nusrah took it to be, namely that the bestial soul is the horse and the rational soul is humankind, once it is returned to the balance of consideration and taken upon the face that is sound. Rather, since the sensory soul, which is the bestial one, is such that it accepts the ruling of the rational soul when no obstacle hinders it, and the rational soul, when it sees the other accepting its ruling, inclines toward it with benefit and sets it right, we say: by "the sensory soul" he means whoever is in the form of humankind, whose nature it is to accept teaching, discipline, and reason, and not the horse; and by "the rational soul" he means the teachers, who are the prophets, the guardians, the imams (عليهم السلام), and their followers, whose nature it is to incline toward whoever is in the form of humankind, to teach them their interests in their religion and their world, to benefit them in their right guidance, and to raise them in degrees according to their acceptance, they being in truth humankind, and not every one of the people. This agrees with what is indicated by the interpretation of the saying of Allah Almighty:

> يَا أَيَّتُهَا النَّفْسُ الْمُطْمَئِنَّةُ ارْجِعِي إِلَى رَبِّكِ رَاضِيَةً مَرْضِيَّةً

"O reassured soul, return to your Lord, pleased and pleasing" (the verse). And to carry his statement upon the face that is sound is preferable to carrying it upon the face that is not sound. The horse is no more accepting than one in the form of humankind, nor is its image better than his image; nor are the guardians, the imams, and their followers less compassionate toward those below them among some of the people than men are in the sport and gentling of the horse; rather, they are far more so. And since this is so, and there is nothing in the plain words of Bandhaqlis to show that there is no image among the bestial souls more beautiful than the image of the horse, nor is the case, among the animal images that exist, such as to surpass the beauty of the horse's image, nor is the matter as he said, then to single out the horse to be the pair of humankind on the side of teaching and beauty of image, and to argue by it, is impossible.

**The Fifth Section**

The author of al-Islah said:

Humankind is the counterpart of the whole world and is preferred to it by a noble essence that is not of this world. His body stands opposite all bodies in their genera, the solid and the dissolved, because in his body there is something corresponding to each genus of them, such as bone, flesh, nerve, veins, and cartilage, and all the flowing substances that issue from his body, such as blood, semen, and sweat, and what flows from the eye, the ear, and the mouth, and the rest of his interior. All of these are of the essence of bodies and of their kinds, and they correspond to the kinds of the essence that is one of the three essences of the world, namely the essence of bodies, which is not united with any of the three souls. His growth stands opposite the growing kinds among the species of trees in their varieties, and this is of the kinds of the second essence, united with the growing soul. His sense stands opposite the animal in its differing kinds, and this is of the kinds of the third essence, united with the sensory soul, the bestial soul. This is his correspondence to the three jewels of the world, and he stands alone in the fourth rank, so that he became the fourth essence, which is the essence of the rational soul, alone in this station. The substance of his saying is that humankind is the counterpart of the whole world, that his body stands opposite all the bodies of the world in the difference of their forms, his growth opposite the plants, his sense opposite the animal, and that he possesses that by which he surpasses all and stands alone, which is the rational soul that is his.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

As for his saying that the dissolved and the contracted are one essence, that is not to be rejected. Rather, the dissolved, the contracted, the growing, the sensory, and the rational are all one essence in respect of being essence; but in respect of their divisions and properties, the contracted essence is not the dissolved essence, and the dissolved is not the contracted. They are distinguished only in the locus where they are distinguished, not in the locus where they are not distinguished. He needed to distinguish between them only because he made the human a mediator between the dense bodily world and the subtle spiritual world. For in him is all that is in the two worlds, and so everything in the lower world is likened to him, until nothing of it is left out. He therefore set the contracted bodies opposite his body, his body likewise being contracted, as the bodies are contracted; then he set the dissolved bodies opposite his growth, on account of the dissolution within them, for dissolution occurs only in the motion of the jewels that dissolve, and likewise growth occurs only by motion, so that when the two resemble each other in respect of motion, each of them is likened to its companion. He set the growing bodies opposite his sense, and set the animal opposite his speech. So the remainder of the world's bodies, contracted, dissolved, growing, and sensory, is gathered in him, and he surpasses the rest of the jewels by speech. The substance of his saying is that humankind resembles the world until nothing of it is left out, for his body stands opposite the contracted bodies, his growth opposite the dissolved bodies, his sense opposite the growing bodies, and his speech opposite the animal, and that he surpasses by speech all the rest of the jewels.

We say:

The disagreement between the two in this section is that the author of al-Islah set, opposite the human's growth, the plant of the greater world that grows and develops, while the author of al-Nusrah set opposite it the dissolved bodies; and the author of al-Islah set, opposite the human's sense, the animal of the greater world, while the author of al-Nusrah set opposite his sense the growing bodies; and the author of al-Islah made the rational soul the very thing by which the human is set apart, preferred above the world, and made its master, while the author of al-Nusrah set that opposite the animal. The saying of the author of al-Islah is the closer, since what he likens is of the genus of that to which it is likened, and it has a conformity that the saying of the author of al-Nusrah does not have. In sum, the human is not the counterpart of this world only, but the counterpart of both worlds together, being the collector of all the things that preceded him in existence and, by what is in him, resembling both of them. The matter concerning him is upon this, and not upon what he said.

**The Sixth Section**

We said that the human, who is humankind, is the counterpart of the two worlds because he gathered the multiplicity existing between origination and the second emanation, then became one, so that in himself he became parallel to the world of origination and emanation, and by his body, the collector of its parts, parallel to the bodily world, and so parallel to both worlds. As for his resemblance to this world: the world is entirely body, of it the subtle, which is that which is high, and of it the dense, which is that which is low; and the human body is likewise subtle and dense, the subtle being that which is high and the dense that which is low. In the world there are planets and encompassing spheres, and in the human body likewise there is the skin encompassing the whole body and the rest that clothes the bone; and what runs in the course of the planets, such as the two ears, the two eyes, the nose, and the mouth. In the human there is likewise a mover that moves, which is the soul. In the world there are the four elements, and in the human likewise the four natures. In the world there are rivers, valleys, and waters of differing tastes, and mountains and minerals, and in the human likewise, as the author of al-Islah set forth, the bones and cartilage, the flesh and the blood, and what flows from him and what issues out. In the world there is what grows and develops without sensing, and in the human likewise there is what grows and develops without sensing, such as the hair and the nails, and his body grows and increases from its smallness to its greatness. In the world there is the sensing animal, and likewise in the human the sensory faculties. The whole world is structures and compositions, and by what exists in the human the resemblance to the world is complete, though all that we have set down here in summary is detailed in our book known as The Peace of Mind. As for his resemblance to the world of origination and the first emanation: that world is the world of intellect, the world of glorification, praise, and purification, the world of unity and deification, the world of virtues, the world of generosity, and the world of bounties. And the human, who is humankind, is likewise the collector of these virtues, in intellect and glorification, in unity and deification, and in bounty and generosity toward others and in himself. That world has marked him, and by his body he has resembled this world; and being thus, he came to be distinguished from those that preceded him in existence by rank alone. May Allah make us among those who follow the traces of His friends, and let us not be excluded from the intercession of His chosen ones, by His grace and His bounties.

**The Seventh Section**

And the saying of the author of al-Islah, that humankind is preferred over the world by the noble essence that is not of this world, is not to say that the essence which he denied to be of this world is of that world, I mean the high and noble upper world, according to what was disclosed to him. Rather, since it is neither of that world only nor of this world only, we said that it is of both worlds together. For his state in existence between the two worlds is like the state of the child in his existence between his two parents: it is not permissible to attribute him to one of them apart from the other, since it is by them both together, and from them, that he came to be. What may be attributed of him to this world is that which runs in him in the course of matter, which is the receptive; and what may be attributed of him to that world is that which runs in him in the course of form, which is the received, as with the child, who has from the side of his two parents, the higher and the nobler, his form, and from the other side, which is below that, the receptive matter. The proofs of this are many, found in our book known as The Peace of Mind. And Allah Almighty grants us success toward the abode of His religion, and provides us the intercession of Muhammad His prophet, and the Imams of his pure family, and his household (عليهم السلام).
