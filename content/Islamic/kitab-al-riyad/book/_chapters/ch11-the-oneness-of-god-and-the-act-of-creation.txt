# The Oneness of God and the Act of Creation

We say:

When the author of *al-Mahsul*, may Allah have mercy on him, exerted himself, strove to the utmost, discharged the right of the daʿwa and its sons, and opened the doors of the landmarks by what he composed of his books that keep watch over its regions, and when Shaykh Abu Hatim, may Allah have mercy on him, corrected of it what he corrected, and was able to do so, most people supposed that this correction was a stab against the author of *al-Mahsul*, made on account of the apology the author of the correction offered for it in his book. So they faulted him, and they overturned the matter. That is a corrupt estimation, an act inclined away from the truth, and the affair is other than what they imagined.

For the Natiq (ع), when he knew, at the ordering of things, that the sciences of religion are too many for a single soul below the Imam to encompass, or to be memorized all in a single utterance, appointed for them many persons below the Imamate, so that they might cooperate among themselves over all the sciences, their awareness, and their preservation, like the twelve hujjas (proofs). Thus each of them benefits, from the one to whom Allah has entrusted the reins of guidance among the righteous imams (عليهم السلام), the abstruse of matters, their inner aspect and their outer, according to their capacities and their strength in reception and deduction, so that they may be to others in guidance as the imams (عليهم السلام) are in exposition and benefit. Religion through them is like a single person who gathers his parts, and they are to it like the senses that perceive things: if one of them misses a thing, another perceives it, without that second perception being a defect upon the sense that missed the perception, nor upon the others.

That being so, Abu Hatim's correction, may Allah raise his degree, of what he corrected from the book of *al-Mahsul*, is no fault upon him, nor a deficiency upon the author of *al-Mahsul*, nor a challenge against either of them, except what we have said: that the persons appointed to correct the sciences of religion are like the senses. If one of the senses fails to perceive something of the world of nature, the other does not fail it, but rather cooperates over it, discharging the right of religion and confirming the obligation owed to the Lord of the Worlds.

Since that is so, and since I have seen in the book of *al-Mahsul* what was more binding upon Abu Hatim, and more incumbent upon him to explain and correct, than what he did correct, yet which he did not address and passed over, the right of cooperation obliged me to discharge the right of religion by clarifying what is folded into what he set down, of what it is not permissible to believe in the chapter of monotheism and the First Creator (al-mubdiʿ al-awwal), apart from the other branches which, if Allah aids by deferring the term, we shall discuss elsewhere than in this book. So I have done it, and it is not permissible for me to apologize for it, since the souls are, in respect of religion, all one, even were I to apologize to myself. Allah accepts from us what we offer of nearness to Him, of the walayah that leads to the good pleasure of Allah, Lord of the Worlds, and gathers us, by His abiding grace, the former of us and the renewed, the preceding and the later, with our pure masters (mawali) in the Abode of Holiness; truly He is Generous, Noble.

The author of *al-Mahsul* said:

In the chapter of monotheism: He is the creator of the thing (al-shayʾ) and of the no-thing (al-lā-shayʾ), the rational, the illusory, the intellectual, the logical, I mean what falls beneath these categories and what does not fall beneath them.

We say:

By this saying he has made it necessary that among the existents from Allah, the Almighty, which fall under His creation, there is that which is not perceived by reason, nor by illusion, nor by thought, and is not reported by logic, and yet whose existence is impossible while belief in it is treated as licit. For whatever is not perceived by reason, illusion, or thought, and is not reported by logic, cannot, if it has existence, fail to be one of three:

either preceding reason in its existence, or existing together with reason, or following reason in its existence.

It is impossible that it precede reason, for reason is the very act behind which is none but Allah, the Almighty, who issued it and from whom it came to be. It is pure creation (ibdaʿ), and the true existence (al-wujud al-haqq) which is the first existence from Allah, glorified be He.

It is likewise impossible that its existence be together with the very intellect (al-ʿaql), for the intellect is the very intellect that is the creation, and the intellect, at its origination, is only one essence, not two. Then, were its existence to be together with the intellect, they being distinct yet sharing in existence, it would follow that Allah, the Almighty, from whom it came to be, is a manifold creative cause, or that there is together with Him another, exalted be God far above that with a great exaltation. For it is the nature of the effect that its existence is according to its cause, and their being two distinct effects requires two causes, or a cause multiplied by relations, so that the existence of each of them comes from a relation of it other than the others. It is impossible for two distinct effects to exist from a single cause whose essence is kept safe from difference by relations and additions. And since its existence together with the intellect requires this, its existence is impossible, for Allah, the Almighty, is sanctified above all kinds of multiplicity, additions, and othernesses, and above whatever would make Him a cause in that manner.

It is impossible, too, that its existence follow reason, for were its existence subsequent to the intellect and from it, knowledge of it would not escape the intellects, nor would the defining of it be hard for them; rather they would perceive and define it just as they perceived and defined all the other existents, sensible and intelligible, in the field of affirmation and negation. And since, in its existence, it cannot escape these three divisions, and each of the divisions requires that its existence be impossible, it is established that its existence is impossible, and to assert it is misguidance.

Then, had the author of *al-Mahsul* not said, after his affirming of the thing and the no-thing, that Allah created them both, I mean what falls under these categories and what does not, we would have said that by his saying "the thing" he intended what is described among bodies, and by "the no-thing" what is not described by the attributes of bodies, from the world of creation, faulting the error of whoever said that while believing in it. But since he brought in what is folded into it of affirming the impossible, it results only in one's believing, among the existents, in something that shares with Allah in His transcendence in some manner, so that the corruption becomes general; and we have shown it to be impossible.

Then we say:

"The thing" is known to be a common term under which fall all the existents, of substances and accidents, sensible and intelligible. His meaning by his saying "the no-thing" is not free of one of two: either what some of the philosophers held, that it is an affirmation of what is other than a thing, an affirmation stripped of that to which the thing is confined in all its divisions of substances and accidents, and they named it the privative proposition (al-qadiyya al-maʿdula) and adduced it. They said it is like our saying "not-man," which is an affirmation of everything that is not a man, negating existence of the one spoken of, without affirming what is not, for anything. They said it is the nature of the particle lā (لا), when a specific entity is intended by it in order to remove it from existence, negating it of what it is, without affirming of it what is other than it. It is like his saying "the thing," which is a universal entity from which every existent proceeds: when we intend it by the particle lā we say "no-thing" (lā shayʾ), and the entity of the thing becomes removed from what it was a thing in, without affirmation, being other than it.

And if a particular entity within that entity is intended by it, and something other is affirmed, then, if his meaning is the affirmation of what is a not-thing, his saying that Allah is its creator is an error. For the no-thing, being that which no attribute befalls, and which is not, in its condition, a substance or an accident, and the negation of the attributes of the thing from Allah, the Almighty, being a transcendence and a sanctification, He, the Almighty, is neither substance nor accident; so the attributes of the thing are not an attribute of Him, nor from Him, nor is He, the Almighty, like them. It is as though, by his saying that He is the creator of the thing and the no-thing, he said that He is the creator of His own essence, which is other than the thing, and the creator of the thing, which is other than His essence; and that is an error. And if his meaning is the negation of the very thing, his saying that Allah, the Almighty, is its creator is an error, since the thing is the creation, and it is the very existence, and the no-thing is the negation of the creation and the nullification of existence. And if the no-thing is the negation of the creation, how can it be sound that He is its creator, when he has negated it, placing it among the impossible?

The author of *al-Mahsul* said:

The Creator of things, not from anything, is only He, and no thing with Him, from eternity. So when we say "He, and no thing with Him," we have negated the thing and the no-thing, and made them both created, and negated every form, simple and composite, from His identity, and made everything, whether the statement applies to it or does not apply to it, created, caused in truth, finite. And the no-thing is after the thing, for the origination of its name only occurred at the appearance of the thing.

We say:

What has preceded of the discussion on the no-thing is sufficient. His saying, "the Creator of things, not from anything, He and no thing with Him, from eternity," folds into itself what it is not permissible to believe. For the no-thing, taken as the affirmation of what is not a thing among the philosophers in the way we have mentioned, means that when he says "He, and no thing with Him," it is as though he said "He, and what is not a thing with Him, from eternity," and that is shirk. In sum, there is not among the languages existing between human beings any that could serve to express Allah, the Almighty, as befits Him, since all that words announce, and the letters, composite and simple, is originated, and words, letters, and languages are instruments for humans in making one another understand what is within their souls. They are originated, and they indicate only what was, in its condition, originated, as we have explained in our treatise known as *al-Rawda* (الروضة).

He, the Almighty, being beyond the intellects and the perceptions, and behind a veil from the understandings and thought, the paths of inquiry are cut off before Him. When one is driven to affirm Him, after knowing that all that tongues utter and lips move with is applied to what He has created and brought into being, glorified be He, there is no expression that may be borrowed for affirming Him, without establishing an attribute or a multiplicity, except the word "He" (هو), which Allah, the Almighty, established after the exception in His saying:

> شهد الله ان لا إله

Allah bears witness that there is no god,

for then He said إلا هو, "except He," and did not say "except Allah," nor "other than Allah," but rather said "He" (هو). Likewise Imam al-Muʿizz (ع) indicated it in his book known as *Taʾwil al-Shariʿa* (تأويل الشريعة), where he said that it is "He" only. For this expression, even if borrowed, is, on the part of the intellects, an affirmation of what is outside them, without attribute and without multiplicity. Moreover, in traversing the path of affirming Him, the Almighty, the use of the particle lā (لا) in negating every sort of the existents is more expressive of the intent than mere safety from likening (tashbih) and from divesting (taʿtil), and it is more fitting and more deserving, in the view of the scholars and the jurists among the people of religion.

And his saying:

If we say "He and no thing with Him," we have negated the thing and the no-thing, and made them both created. From where, then, is it sound that there exist what is said to be a no-thing falling under creation, or the existence of what is said to be a no-thing while it is prior to creation and is other than Allah, such that its negation is sound? Nay, it is nothing but the thing, the thing which is the "thinged" (al-mashiʾ); and the "thinged" is that whose existence we negated in the languages, saying there is nothing to express Him, on account of the greatness of His majesty, the Almighty, and the incapacity of the composite, originated letters to signify as befits His grandeur, the Almighty. The thing which is the "thinged" is other than Allah, who brought it into being, not from anything, having an entity and a right in existence upon all its divisions. Then the no-thing is the negation of the entity of the thing, which is pure creation, as the discussion has already preceded. And if the no-thing is the negation of the entity of the creation, which is the entity of existence and the truth of existence, how can it become, in existence, a created thing? Those statements are not sound.

And his saying:

We have negated every form, simple and composite, from His identity, and made everything, whether the statement applies to it or does not apply to it, created, caused in truth.

We say:

How can that upon which the statement does not apply, that from which all the existents came to be, become itself created and caused? That is an error.

And his saying:

The no-thing is after the thing, for the origination of its name only occurred through it at the appearance of a thing.

The no-thing is not a name that applies to an entity which has a share in existence, for "the thing" is a name, and lā, which is the particle of negation, has negated it; and the negation of a name does not become a name for other than that of which the name was a name, nor is anything reported by it other than what the philosophers mentioned, that it folds into itself the affirmation of what has no name. And there has already preceded, of the discussion on that, what contains the answer to it, and in it is sufficiency.

The author of *al-Mahsul* said:

In the chapter of the First Creator: the Maker (al-Bari), the Almighty, created the world all at once. That is, He created the intellect as a single whole, and in it, by His determination, emerged the forms of the two worlds and what is in them, without those forms being pointed to by their identities at the intellect. Rather the intellect's knowledge falls upon them, and they are known to it, except that the intellect has preceded them by act. So, whether by act or by potency, it and the forms are all together.

We say:

This knowledge, the intellect that he mentioned was created, in which emerged the forms of the worlds and what is in them, cannot be the First Intellect, the one that exists from Allah, the Almighty, first. For that which precedes in existence is independent of conceiving that by which it exists, since its existence is bound to its Originator who brought it into being, and it is not incumbent upon it to know more than its knowledge of its own essence: that it is created, that its existence is not by itself but by another, who is its Creator, and that it is a cause for the existence of what is below it. It is like the Imam, upon whom knowing others below him is not incumbent, who knows no more than that he is the cause of salvation for those below him, the intermediary between the one above him and the one below him. Rather, what is incumbent upon him is to know the one above him, and it is incumbent upon others to know him, and to know those whose existence and rank preceded them, so that they may attain salvation thereby. For this reason it is incumbent upon the later one in existence to conceive all that preceded it in existence among the limits (hudud), so that this may earn it permanence and complete existence. And since that is so, what he has mentioned cannot be that first existent which is from Allah, the Almighty, at the beginning.

The intellect that emanates from the world of nature, not the First Intellect, is what supports what we have said. He set forth in it the forms of the worlds, as he says:

> إنه أبرز فيه صور العالمين من غير أن تكون تلك الصور مشاراً إليها بهوياتها عند العقل، بل عالم العقل واقع عليها

He set forth in it the forms of the worlds, without those forms being pointed to by their identities in the presence of the intellect; rather the world of the intellect falls upon them. And up to his saying:

> فأما في القوة فهو والصور جميعاً معاً

As for what is in potency, it and the forms are all together. For this saying of his affirms what subsists by potency, and reports of something whose existence is an existence not in act. But the First Intellect does not subsist in act, and it is not permissible to say that within it is anything that subsists by potency, because were it so, there would have to precede it, from the type of its own existence, something subsisting in act, so as to bring it out into act. Yet it has already been shown invalid that anything exist together with the First Intellect, or that anything precede it. And when that is invalid, the statement of the author of al-Maḥṣūl is correct only in regard to that which is last in existence, behind which there is no other existence, and which is substantiated by knowing what preceded it in existence among the two worlds. This is the second emanation from the world of nature, like the Speaker who possesses the forms of the worlds; and the sum of these forms has come to subsist in act and to be emanated in act.

The Ninth Section of the Tenth Title

The author of al-Maḥṣūl said:

> إن العقل لما كانت علته وحدة الباري جل وعز، والوحدة أزلية، صار العقل أزلياً

When the cause of the intellect was the unity of the Maker, glorified and mighty is He, and unity is eternal, the intellect became eternal.

Upon this we say: by his statement he has affirmed something that precedes the First Intellect in its existence, namely that cause which he named unity. But we have said that the intellect is preceded in its existence by nothing at all, other than that which is from Allah, the Almighty, at the beginning; so it is the worthier of eternity, because of the impossibility of any thing existing. Unity, then, is not the cause of the intellect, nor is it other than it such that it would precede the intellect. Rather it is the very essence of the intellect, and the intellect is the very essence of unity, and it is the One.

It is the cause, and it is the effect; it is origination, and it is the Originator; and it is the Perfect. The Perfect is the Eternal, and it is the everlasting, and it is Existence, and it is the Existent by one essence. The meanings that these names require are merited only by relating it to that from which its existence comes, which is Allah, the Almighty; or by relating it to what is below it; or by relating it to its own essence, because the essence in its existence is manifold. Sufficient indication of this is his saying in this title concerning origination:

> والمبدع عن المبدع واحد، وصورته صورة المعقول

The Originator and that which is originated from the Originator are one, and its form is the form of the intelligible. If that is so, and unity is the very essence of the intellect, then his saying that the intellect became eternal, and not that its cause is eternal, is impossible, since the intellect is not a thing other than the cause such that it becomes eternal through it. Rather it is it, and it is it. An attribute of this sort, that its eternity is not the eternity of its cause, holds true only of the emanated intellects that come from the world of nature, such as the Speakers (al-nuṭaqāʾ), the Foundations (al-asās), and their followers (عليهم السلام), whose intellects are one thing and that by which they become eternal is another thing. For the cause of their being sanctified and eternal is those separate eternal intellects that are called the proximate angels, and the extension of their lights to them, and their union with them. The words of the author of al-Maḥṣūl, then, are about the intellects that come to be in the world of nature, not about the measure in the world of origination. And since his words and the meanings of his statement are directed toward the intellects in the abode of nature, to conceive that great and majestic limit and that rank by this description is impossible.

The Tenth Section of the Tenth Title

Then we say: the meaning of his statement that the cause of the intellect is one does not escape one of these: either that it proceeds from it in the manner of its Originator, exalted be He, which he named unity; or that it is that which proceeds from it in the manner of prime matter (al-hayūlā); or that it proceeds from it in the manner of the form among the existents in the abode of body; or that it is that which proceeds from it in the manner of the purpose for the sake of which it was brought into existence, it being its very self and essence.

It is invalid that it be that which proceeds in the manner of its Originator. For if unity is a cause for the intellect, and the cause is a thing, and a thing whose existence is not by its own essence, then it is as though he made Allah, the Almighty, whom he named unity, an originator and a thing brought into being (muḥdath) after having been the Originator, by making Him dependent upon another in the sense his statement conveys; and that is impossible. Then, because unity is other than Allah, and that which is other than Allah is an originated thing brought into being, he has necessitated that Allah, whom he named unity, be an originator brought into being; and this is impossible.

It is invalid that it be that which proceeds in the manner of prime matter among the existents, because of the impossibility of a thing existing so as to run in existence alongside Allah, the Almighty, whereby something else would exist with Him, as the discussion has already established.

It is invalid that it be that which proceeds in the manner of the form among the existents by which it comes to be, because of the impossibility of the form existing except through that which runs in relation to it in the manner of matter; and the existence of matter alongside Allah, the Almighty, by which the form would exist, has already been invalidated. Moreover, Allah, the Almighty, is far transcendent above multiplicity through forms, which is the mark of origination in time, such that He would become an originator after being the Originator. Exalted and magnified is Allah above that.

It is invalid that it be that which proceeds in the manner of that for the sake of which it was brought into existence, because the intellect was not brought into existence for the sake of unity; for had it been brought into existence for the sake of unity, it would not have produced multiplicity. But multiplicity has come to be; so it was not brought into existence for the sake of unity.

And when these aspects of the meaning of his statement, that unity is the cause of the intellect, are invalidated and prevented from holding, it is established that the essence of unity is the very cause, and its truth. And if the intellect is the very essence of unity and the cause, then his saying that the intellect became eternal on account of the eternity of its cause, while what he intends by that statement is the First, which is the First Existent, is not correct.

The Eleventh Section of the Tenth Title

The author of al-Maḥṣūl said:

> فلما كانت الصورة مبروزة فيه من الوحدة كذلك صارت الصورة المجردة أزلية عنده، لأن العقل صار بكليته أزلياً لا ببعضه

Since the form was made manifest in it from unity, likewise the abstracted form became eternal in his view, because the intellect became eternal in its entirety, not in part of it.

We say: this is an attribute of a thing within whose potency lies that form made manifest in it; and it is impossible for that to be said of that majestic limit which subsists in act, not by potency. And if it is so, then his statement is correct only regarding the emanated intellects, such as the intellects of the Speakers, the Foundations, the imams, and their followers (عليهم السلام), who came forth into the intellect through their whole community, which is the separate intellect called the proximate angel, surnamed the Pen, in its entirety and the eternity of their intellects. The intellects of those who follow them in truth realize this and substantiate it, as the author of al-Maḥṣūl confirms in what follows what he mentioned:

> ولو لم تكن الصور مبدعة بالأزلية لم يكن لها بقاء، ولا عودة إلى مهد معدنها الذي بدت منه مع الصورة، ولكانت تفنى وتندثر، ولم يكن رجاء ولا خوف ولا ثواب ولا عقاب، فإن كل شيء تكون علته أزلية فيه صائر بها أيضاً أزلياً

Had the forms not been originated in eternity, they would have no permanence, nor any return to the cradle of their source from which they arose together with the form; they would perish and be effaced, and there would be no hope, no fear, no reward, and no punishment. For everything whose cause is eternal within it becomes, through it, likewise eternal. For this saying of his implies that the said forms, which have no permanence and no connection with their sources except by the eternal power, are those in the world of nature, derived from the souls that have no subsistence in act, and that gain permanence and continuance only through those who have attained eternity and perfection. Were it not for these, reward and punishment would be invalidated, according to what he said. For it is through the Speaker and those who stand in his place that this is made correct. So the meaning of his statement is valid only in the intellects that emerge from the world of nature, below the world of origination.

The Twelfth Section of the Tenth Title

The author of al-Maḥṣūl said:

> ولما كان العقل معلولاً بالأزلية التي تفسيرها عندنا بالقول المركب كلمة الله، ولم يكن بينهما واسطة ثالثة صار العقل مثلها

Since the intellect was caused by eternity, whose meaning for us is the composite expression "the Word of God," and there was no third intermediary between the two, the intellect became like it.

We say: his saying leads to meanings such as those already mentioned, and it is not fitting, nor does it befit the First Intellect, that it be an essence of which that is said, since it is the very essence of the Word, neither something other than it, nor something that was and then became like it. All this attaches to the intellects that proceed from potency into act in the abode of nature, which become so by receiving the effects of the Word, which is the First Intellect, and by uniting with it, as he confirms when he says:

> فلما صارت صورة الفعل بالكلمة صورة العقل، صار العقل بذلك علة لما دونه من الأشياء، وصار بالكلمة محيطاً بها بما يرى الباري فيه

When the form of act became, through the Word, the form of the intellect, the intellect thereby became a cause for what is below it among things, and it became, through the Word, encompassing them by what the Maker sees within it. For that whose form became, through the Word, a form for it, and which became a cause for what is below it, requires that it was not previously a cause, and that its form was not the form of the Word. And that which is in this position is only the intellects of the Speakers, not that intellect originated without intermediary, which was never anything other than the Word, nor anything other than the cause of the existent, whose existence is a first existence, beyond which the descriptions are cut off. Magnified and exalted is it. In what we have already said concerning this great limit there is sufficiency without prolixity. And since that is so, to run that great limit in the course of these lower limits is impossible.

The Thirteenth Section of the Tenth Title

The author of al-Maḥṣūl said:

> فلما كان هكذا سمي تاماً بالمبدعية، وذلك أن الإبداع من المبدع، إذ بالإبداع التام لا يكون إلا تاماً

Since it is thus, it is named perfect by originatorship, for origination is from the Originator, since through perfect origination there arises none but the perfect.

We say: we have already spoken of that, and his saying is mistaken in two respects. The first is that he applied the name of the intellect to the Originator and made it like its Originator, of whom he said that it is perfect; and this is shirk, and it is not permissible to say, because Allah is set apart by His own self from the perfection and the perfect that exist by His originating the two of them. Nor is it said that the Originator, by perfect origination, is none but the perfect, such that origination itself would be the perfect. The second is that he applied the name of originatorship to what belongs to the world of nature, and that is impossible, because the one who deserves the name of originatorship is the one whose existence precedes all things, not the thing that subsists in act, nor by potency. And since that is so, it is not permissible to hold what he said except in the manner that is correct.

The Fourteenth Section of the Tenth Title

The author of al-Maḥṣūl said:

> إن العقل إنما يفيد الصور من علته التي هي الكلمة، كما أن الشمس تفيد القابسين من ضوئها، لا من جرمها، وإذا كان إخراجه لما فيه من قوة علته التي هي الكلمة، وإفادته إياهم كذلك منها استبان أن الكلمة هي التي صارت علة الأشياء البارزة من العقل، لا هوية العقل، إلا أن العقل صار واسطة بين الكلمة وما دونه

The intellect bestows the forms only from its cause, which is the Word, just as the sun bestows upon those who kindle from it from its light, not from its body. And since its bringing-forth of what is in it is from the power of its cause, which is the Word, and its bestowing them upon them is likewise from it, it becomes clear that the Word is what became the cause of the things that emerge from the intellect, not the very identity of the intellect, except that the intellect became an intermediary between the Word and what is below it.

We say: the intellect he mentions is the intellects of the Speakers and their followers, which derive from their cause, that being other than themselves, from which they kindle; not the First Intellect, which is the First Originator. For the First Intellect does not need anything else for the existence of what comes to be from it, since it is itself the cause, and the very Word from which, and from whose essence, is the existence of what has come to be. For this reason we said that it is the Perfect, from which the Perfect comes to exist, so that it needs nothing outside its own essence, since it is the cause of the existence of things. Were it preceded by something other than itself, that which precedes would necessarily be the First Originator, not it, and the matter would necessarily lead to a disorder in the ranks of the existents; and that is impossible. As for his saying that the intellect became an intermediary between the Word and what is below it, we have already shown the impossibility of the matter in what preceded, at the opening of our book.

There is no harm in adding, so we say: how can there be a thing that precedes the First Intellect, so as to be an intermediary between it and what is below it? The intellect is the very essence of the Word, which is the cause existing from Allah, the Almighty. And if it were permissible for something to precede it, that which precedes would not escape being either of the same type as its existence, or not of its type.

If it is of the same type as its existence, then its precedence over it is invalid, for their ranks and limits would be equal, since they are of one type.

And if it is not of the type of its existence, then it does not escape being either a thing or not a thing. If it is a thing, then it is a target for the perception of intellects; and whatever the intellect perceives, the intellect is what precedes it, not it. And if it is not a thing, then that is not the Word, but rather that which is transcendent above the attributes that a thing deserves in its divisions. Glorified is He, exalted with a great exaltation.

So in whichever of the two states it is taken, the impossibility stands that anything precede the First Intellect. There is nothing above the thing except the Thing, exalted be He. And since that is so, it is the Word, it is the cause, it is unity, and it is the intellect, according to what we have explained above. وحسبنا الله ونعم الوكيل. Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best Trustee.

The Fifteenth Section of the Tenth Title

The author of al-Maḥṣūl said:

> والإبداع واسطة بين المبدع والمبدع هو أثر من المؤثر في المؤثر، وقال: وصورة الإبداع متوسطة بين الفاعل والمفعول، ووجودها من جهة الفاعل المبدع، فأثر الصورة يوجد من جهة المبدع في المفعول، فهذه الصورة أعني الإبداع صارت في المبدع سبحانه

Origination is an intermediary between the Originator and the originated; it is an effect from the influencer within the influenced. And he said: the form of origination is intermediate between the agent and the object; its existence is from the side of the originating agent, and so the effect of the form is found from the side of the Originator within the object. This form, I mean origination, has come to be within the Originator, glorified be He.

We say: this speech contains the affirmation of that which Allah has not originated, nor brought into being, nor produced; and this is contrary to what the affirmers of divine oneness (al-muwaḥḥidīn) hold. For by his statement he made origination other than the Originator, and so necessitated that origination be a form in the Originator. Then by his statement he necessitated that that of which the form he named origination is a form, and which he named an influencer within it, be fixed, not invented, and not brought into being.

It is not permissible to hold this. For origination is not a thing other than the Originator, since, were what he conceives as originator other than origination, it would not escape being either in existence together with Allah, the Almighty, or existing by its own essence. That its existence be by its own essence is invalid, for it would be partitioned within itself, while it is yet with Allah, the Almighty. As for its existence together with Allah, the Almighty, its existence with Allah does not escape being either similar to Him, exalted be Allah above that, and a likeness of Him, so that the two would be two likenesses. This would make each of them singled out by that through which the duality occurred; and being singled out in two things occurs only through a necessitating cause that makes them so, prior to them, and from which is their existence. Or it is not similar and not a likeness, so it would be an opposite; and the existence of two opposites occurs only through another that preserves their existence for them, since in the existence of one of the two opposites is the annulment of the existence of the other, because its existence is necessary. So he has necessitated for their existence that which precedes them and preserves their existence for them, and this requires that the Originator, exalted be He, be originated, and that there precede Him that which is prior, and by divinity, exalted and magnified is He. And that is more strongly impossible and more absurd. And since the existence of that whose existence he necessitated does not escape two aspects, when both aspects together are invalidated, it is invalidated that its existence be by its own essence; and when it is invalidated that its existence be by its own essence, it is established that its existence is from

God, exalted is He. And origination too has its existence from Him, so the One is not one of the things that come to be by origination. As for the Originator, to say that He is an originator, or a primary originator set apart from another, cannot stand; for if that were so, existence itself would be invalidated, since that which exists produces nothing but one. Thus it is established that origination is the Originator; and if origination is the Originator, then what the author of *al-Maḥṣūl* said is in error, so let him avoid it, God willing. And with this we have come to what we promised at the beginning of the book.

We say:

Tawḥīd and the knowledge of the limits are difficult, and yet upon them depend the life of souls and their survival, and their deliverance from the world of nature and its instabilities. Because this is difficult, and because God, exalted is He, knows that not all His servants attain the degree by which they reach perfection in knowing the limits of God and His oneness, He has, from age to age, singled out one man endowed with perfection and election, such as the Imam (ع). By perfection I mean that he is complete in receiving the overflow that courses through the world of nature by way of the angels who are brought near; and by this he becomes the one set over them, raising them up into his own totality. So God, exalted is He, has made obedience to him and love of him obligatory, that by this they may become part of his totality, out of His compassion toward them.

There is no way to this except by upholding the two acts of worship, and discharging the due of each in knowledge and in deed, outwardly and inwardly, and being diligent in guarding them. And this comes about only through the remembrance of death and what follows upon it, the parting from parents, children, kin, and homeland, and through the certainty that there is no arrival at the promised return except by provision, and no provision that is stored up except through an obedience whose obligations are performed, a duty whose right is fulfilled, a good that is done, a righteous work that is enacted, and a knowledge of the realities of things that is acquired.

May God place us and the company of the believers, in the easts of the earth and its wests, among those who are sealed with what is good; and may He grant to us and to them the intercession of Muḥammad, of ʿAlī, and of the pure family, and of our Imam, the Ruler by the Command of God, and his guiding forefathers (عليهم السلام), in the holy land, by His grace and His mercy.

With this we seal the book, with praise to God, Lord of the Worlds, and with blessings upon the best of the first and the last, Muḥammad and his radiant, blessed family, and the rightly guiding Imams. And we say:

> لا حول ولا قوة الا بالله العلي العظيم ، ما شاء الله ، واستغفر الله ، وافوض أمري الى الله ، حسبي الله نعم الوكيل ، نعم المولى ونعم النصير

There is no power and no strength save in God, the Most High, the Almighty. What God wills. I ask God for forgiveness, and I entrust my affair to God. God is sufficient for me; how excellent a guardian, how excellent a Master, and how excellent a Helper.

> والحمد لله حتى حمده ، والصلاة على محمد وآله الطيبين الطاهرين اجمعين

Praise be to God until His praise is fulfilled, and blessings upon Muḥammad and his family, the good and the pure, all of them.
