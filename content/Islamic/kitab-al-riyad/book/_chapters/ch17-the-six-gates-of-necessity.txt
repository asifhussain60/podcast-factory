# The Six Gates of Necessity

The author of al-Nusrah said that we must also come to know the reason the Law exists, so that it may then be sound for us to claim that Adam (عليه السلام) had no law of his own. He argued that the ground of the Law's obligation is the neglect of the limits, their disruption, and creation's pursuit of their opposites, together with their claiming for Him what Allah did not place in him. This meaning, he said, neither existed nor appeared in Adam's day, for the learned who were before him and his people alike kept steadily to the limits, and not one of them transgressed a single limit, as they themselves testified when they said:

> ( أَتَجْعَلُ فِيهَا مَنْ يُفْسِدُ فِيهَا وَيَسْفِكُ الدِّمَاءَ وَنَحْنُ نُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِكَ وَنُقَدِّسُ لَكَ )

"Will You place in it one who corrupts it and sheds blood, while we glorify You with praise and sanctify You?" And so, he concluded, no obligation fell upon Adam to establish the Law, given all this. Such is his statement.

We say that the reason for the necessity of the Law's existence is other than what the author of al-Nusrah reached when he named the neglect of the limits, their disruption, and creation's following of the opposite. That does not oblige a law, nor does it establish one. Rather, we answer on our own behalf. As for the reason the Law must exist, the Law being the ordinances of deeds, we have set it out in our book known as al-Masabih fi al-Imamah. The nearest of these reasons is the need of the people, first of all and according to their ranks, in their very survival, for that by which they are guarded against destruction: the ordinances by which blood is preserved, and by which their hearths, their women, and their wealth are made secure, and by which they are ransomed in the necessary matters that arise among them. Were these ordinances not to exist, their affairs would come to a standstill, and some of them would destroy others: the strong would set upon the one beneath him in strength out of rage at the wealth and the rest that such a one possesses, and the fires of discord would be kindled among them. Then, since it is barred in the wisdom of the Maker, the Most High, that He deprive His creation of what guards them from destruction and keeps it constant, the ordinances that are the Law are of necessary existence.

Then we say that their existence, by which we mean the ordinances of the laws, became a necessary existence because the souls belonging to human beings do not, each of them, hold back from one another in the acts proper to each, so that from those acts there arises what cannot do without ordinances by which the community is stilled, taking hold of their firm bonds, and by which one of them is made safe from another. And once the ordinances are of necessary existence, we say that some of them are necessary at every time and season, while some are not, existing at one time and not at another, increasing and decreasing.

As for what is of necessary existence, it is drawing near to Allah, the Most High; the ordinance of restraint by force and of retribution upon souls and persons; the ordinance of marriage; the division of wealth, such as inheritance and the like; the ordinance for concealing the dead; and the ordinance for extracting rights and returning them to their owners. This is because human beings have three souls. The first is the rational soul, which possesses discrimination and thought; it knows that it has a Creator, submits to Him by nature, and by that submission draws near to Him, and in its drawing near it is driven to ordinances by which it fulfils its aim, whether those ordinances are many or few. The second is the sensitive soul that belongs to him; through its irascible part he is angered when he is angered, and it loves to overcome, to subdue, to take revenge, to wrong, to kill, and to deal unjustly, so that he is driven to ordinances by which the violence of one is turned back from another, and by which the right of the wronged is taken from the wrongdoing aggressor, whether in the soul by force, or in the body by retribution, or by what runs in the course of blood-money. Through its appetitive part he seeks pleasures and seeks marriage of necessity, so that he is driven to ordinances by which he takes hold, and which remove from among them the ruin of jealousy that would drive them to killing.

This soul demands that all wealth be its own, by whatever way it may, of necessity, so that ordinances are needed by which one is made safe from another against the cheating, cunning, and deceit that would lead them to overpowering and fighting. And these souls, in their greed, stir in the seeking of livelihood, and dealings become necessary, so that, treachery and cunning being in the natures, ordinances are needed upon which the claimant and the denier proceed in extracting the right for its owner. Death, too, is necessary among them, and they cannot do without concealing the person of the dead from among them, whether by burial, by burning, by casting into the valleys, or otherwise, for else harm would return upon them, so that one is driven to adopt one of these acts.

These are six gates that run in the manner of genera, and beneath them fall the kinds whose existence is necessary among human beings without fail. As for what is not necessary, and is found at one time greater and at another lesser, these are the ordinances in the worship of Allah, the Most High, and in drawing near to Him through the knowledge of His limits, by which completeness is attained, and this falls under the genus of drawing near to Allah. All of these ordinances belong to the Law, and there must first be one who lays them down and inscribes them among human beings at the beginning of their existence. And since these ordinances exist with the existence of human beings, and Adam (عليه السلام) was of the human beings, together with his angels, the Law is established for him.

As for whoever was before Adam, his ruling is the ruling of one who is at the end of the seventh cycle, like for like. He was not free of the necessary ordinances we have mentioned; he was free only of the ordinances that fall under the genus of worship in drawing near to Allah, the Most High, which we have said may increase at one time and decrease at another, and by which the second completeness is gained. So Allah, the Most High, sent Adam (عليه السلام) to teach them and others what they had not known, and to bring them to completeness where they were deficient, for though they were distinguished above others by merit, they fell short of the degree of completeness by their leaving off the work of worship. He therefore laid down the Law, affirmed it, and called to the two acts of worship, in knowledge and in deed. Some of them followed and obeyed, and some refused and turned aside upon the six that went before, at the beginning of the cycles. Such was the affair of those people.

The author of al-Nusrah said that Noah (عليه السلام) was obliged to establish it, meaning the Law, because before his cycle there had been cycles given to violating the limits and following the opposites, such as al-Wadd (الود), al-Suwa' (السواع), Yaghuth (يغوث), and others, so that it became obligatory upon Noah (عليه السلام) to establish it, that whoever perished might perish upon clear proof, and whoever lived might live upon clear proof.

We say that this saying of his does not require that Adam had no law; rather it requires that he had ordinances and a law. For he has affirmed as necessary that a cycle turned upon the violation of the limits and the following of opposites, and violation befalls the one followed only from the follower, by his abandoning his command and his ordinance. So the establishment of violation by his own saying makes the ordinances that are the Law, and by which the violation occurred, established for Adam (عليه السلام); and therefore the Law is established for him.

The author of al-Islah said that it has been related that Allah, mighty and majestic, commanded Adam (عليه السلام) to pitch a tent which He sent down to him from the Garden, and to make it his qiblah and the qiblah of his children; then Seth built after him the place of the Law, made its rulings firm, gathered into it revelation and interpretation, and established its branches and its roots and the firm reckoning of its obligations. We say that the saying of the author of al-Islah is established, since Adam has a law and ordinances, as have the other speaker-prophets.

The author of al-Nusrah said that in the composition of the world and its ordering, in the matter of pointing to the divine unity, there is that which makes one independent of the laying down of deeds and stations, and that the laying down of deeds and signs has removed many of creation from the divine unity and cast them into likening Him to creatures, and this is exceedingly impossible. He argued that the composition of the world, the ordering and wonders of His wisdom, and what lies in the composition of the world of bodies, the joining together of bodies, the pairing of contrary natures within differing bodies, and the measured persons, all point to the divine unity in a way that makes one independent of the laying down of deeds, of like actions and differing stations that signify agreeing meanings, of likenesses drawn upon a standing thing likened, and of proofs standing upon a clear thing indicated. What is in these proofs and in the deeds that are contradictory by the way of the outward, and what is in these likenesses struck to establish known limits, is itself a proof of the One, since the firm, wrought, and wondrous acts of the world and of the souls make one independent of deeds and signs. Indeed, he said, many of these signs, deeds, and outward words have removed most of creation from the divine unity and cast them into a likening that does not befit the least of His creatures. The sum of his saying is that in the composition of the world and its ordering, in the matter of pointing to the divine unity, there is that which makes one independent of the laying down of deeds and stations, and that the laying down of deeds and signs has removed many of creation from the divine unity and cast them into likening.

We say that this saying of his makes the sending of the messengers a superfluity of which there is no need. For if the world is itself firm and wrought and points to the divine unity, and the sending of the messengers (عليهم السلام) is for the call and for pointing to the divine unity, then to send them while a proof already stands, doing that for which they were sent, is a superfluity of which there is no need. I do not know how this escaped him, when it is plain that the world's being firm and wrought is not sufficient in the matter of pointing to the divine unity if there is none present who knows it and makes it plain. One benefits from the knowledge of the world's composition, its ordering, and the arrangement of its existents only when there is one whose rank has risen in knowledge through the limits' having taught him the manner of the deeds and the signs that revelation and the Law gather together, so that he reflects, draws out, and infers from it what he seeks of the knowledge of the limits of Allah, the Most High, and of His signs whose knowledge is made obligatory upon human beings. Allah, the Most High, says:

> [ حَتَّى إِذَا جَاءُوا قَالَ أَكَذَّبْتُمْ بِآيَاتِي وَلَمْ تُحِيطُوا بِهَا عِلْمًا ]

"Until, when they come, He says: Did you deny My signs when you did not encompass them in knowledge?" But if you did know, then the foremost in existence are the noble substances that are the angels brought near. So it is with the affair when there is need of the deeds, the signs, the alerting, and the teaching, since not everyone who has knowledge and works and is alerted is able to infer from the existents the true inference; and how much less when one does not know at all. In sum, the saying that leads to the nullifying of the ranks of the limits is a saying neither of our belief nor of the way of our masters, may (عليهم السلام). Perhaps this saying was only planted by the adversaries in the book, that it might be ascribed to the author of the book, and Allah knows best.

Furthermore, the deeds are not laid down for the divine unity alone, but for the setting straight of the soul in worship, for likeness to the highest assembly, and for the acquiring, through discipline, of the noble morals and generous habits by which the soul is made ready for union with what is above it, and for its being drawn out of the baseness of potency, that the honor of the intellect may be rescued by devotion to its obligations and attention to its marks. Were it not for the deeds and rites within the community, and for the completeness of souls through persevering in them and taking hold of them, souls would perish. It is for this that Allah, the Most High, did not grant the angels their claim when they supposed that the worship they were upon was sufficient in the matter of nearness to Allah, the Most High, and of completeness for them, and that they had no need, in what they were upon, of a vicegerent to teach them anything or to command them to work. He said:

> [ إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ ]

"Indeed, I know what you do not know." Allah, the Most High, said this because the states of being, by His decree and His wisdom, are held wavering between potency and act: at one time standing in potency, at another standing in act. And a thing, therefore, when it reaches the end of what it may reach of potency and act, returns backward: if it was in potency, toward act, and if it was in act, toward potency. It is like the hot thing that, when it reaches the limit of heat, the heat sinks away and coldness appears, as with what dissolves among bodies and the like; and like the cold thing that, when it reaches the limit of coldness, the coldness sinks away and heat appears, as with snow; and like the congealed thing whose parts are gathered from the mountains, which, when it reaches the end of that for which it congealed, namely dryness, dissolution appears in its parts, so they scatter and return to sand; and the like of these among the existents in the abode of nature. Knowledge and ignorance in the world of coming-to-be and passing-away are likewise: at one time ignorance stands in act and knowledge in potency, in the sense that ignorance is general through the abundance of its people, and knowledge lost through the fewness of its people.

The time was the time of ignorance's dominion and its reaching its end in act, and of knowledge's sinking within and reaching the limit of its end in potency, through the effacing of the traces of the teachers and the disruption of the marks of the laws and the deeds by the length of the age and the passing of the times. By the laws here I mean those ordinances and rites whose existence is not necessary. Those named after the angels, the best of them in themselves and their excellent ones who bore knowledge in that age, and whoever worships Allah upon an edge, related that what they were upon was sufficient in the matter of worship and completeness. But the affair was other than they estimated, since they were in need, for their completeness, of the knowledge of the limits foremost in existence, who are in the abode of origination and the first sending-forth, and of the knowledge of those later in existence, like the second sending-forth, and of working by that which gains them completeness. When they knew that their time was the time in which the cycle of ignorance reached its completeness, and that they were not in the cycle of knowledge nor in the cycle of works, He, the Most High, knew that to leave them as they were, while ignorance had reached its utmost and the time had become the time of the beginning of knowledge's appearance, would run counter to wisdom. So He, mighty and majestic, said in effect: I know of ignorance's reaching its end in the world in act, and the completion of its cycle and its appearance; and of knowledge's reaching its end in the world in potency, and the return of repentance to it in appearing, and the standing need, in its appearing in act, for the raising up of teachers and guides, what you do not know.

So Allah sent Adam (عليه السلام), making him a vicegerent for Himself, that knowledge might be shown forth, and that he might bring them to completeness in act, guide them to the knowledge of the limits, and teach them what they had not known. This was the beginning of the affair in the coming forth of knowledge from potency into act, through the existence of its people, from his days (عليه السلام); and its reaching-out extends to the one who arises, may the peace of Allah be upon him. Likewise it is said that when the one who arises (عليه السلام) appears, knowledge becomes in act, meaning that it becomes general and its people multiply, while ignorance sinks within, meaning that its people grow few and its root is cut off. And what is said by way of narration, that the treasures of the earth appear in his days and it casts them out to its surface, is a pointing to the one who arises and to the standing of knowledge in act, so that all that has been deposited in the depths of the revealed books and in the knotted symbols is shown forth. And since the angels, together with the existence of the heavens, the goodness of their ordering, and the firmness of the composition of the world's existents, did not have knowledge, nor had they completeness, nor any inference from these, except by what Adam (عليه السلام) taught them and called them to of the work, what the author of al-Nusrah mentioned was not fully examined, and the deeds, the signs, and the likenesses were not things one could do without in the matter of inference.

These deeds, these signs, and these likenesses are indispensable in the matter of inference from them concerning monotheism, politics, the correction of the soul, and its discipline in knowing the limits and in bringing them out into act. If that is so, then Adam (ع) is the author of a law and of customs to which he called, and which pointed to the limits of Allah and to His unity.

Chapter Twenty-One of Title IX

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Does the author of this discourse say that the creation in the great cycle did not know, since there was no apparent law resembling one another among them? Or does he say that the Qa'im (ع), in raising the represented laws, nullifies the knowledge of monotheism and brings the people out to the assertion of divesting? For if the creation was not ignorant of monotheism, and if in the cycle of the Qa'im likewise they do not remain ignorant of it while the laws are raised, then it would have been permissible for Adam (ع) to have called the people to monotheism without signs and without the striking of likenesses such as these, by his mere word.

And we say:

There has already gone forth from our discourse a treatment of knowledge and ignorance, and of how each of them is at one time in potentiality and at another in act, and in that is folded the explanation of the state in which the creation stood in the great cycle. Their being at the utmost limit of ignorance, according to the very saying of the author of al-Nusrah, makes Adam's call (ع) and his being a teacher of the landmarks of monotheism to his followers a mere superfluity that was not needed. For he has made it obligatory that the people already knew monotheism, and if they already knew monotheism, then Adam's calling them to the thing they already knew would be nothing but a superfluity of which there was no need. And that is impossible.

For the people were not in need of that by which the completeness of their souls is attained, and so Allah would not have sent Adam to them, nor made him His caliph among them, nor chosen him out from among them, nor made obligatory upon them obedience to him and a returning to him in what they did not know. The saying of the assembly named the angels, when they said,

ونحن نسبح بحمدك ونقدس لك

"And we glorify You with Your praise and sanctify You," was only their estimation that they were sufficient in glorification, sanctification, and monotheism, and that what they were already in was enough in the matter of worship and in the knowledge of the limits. But Allah knew of their affair what His mercy made obligatory, that Adam be chosen out from among them and sent to them, so that He might make him a caliph in teaching them, calling them to that by which their completeness is reached, of work and of knowledge together, which leads to the knowledge of the limits in which monotheism is made sound. For this He said,

اني اعلم ما لا تعلمون

"I know what you do not know," because He, exalted be He, knew that they were deficient, that ignorance had reached its end in act, that the work had come to begin in manifestation, and that they did not know that. So it became obligatory in wisdom to establish the office of teaching, which is the cause of the spreading of knowledge and the multiplying of its people. So Allah chose Adam, and he laid down a law, and he taught, as Allah, exalted be He, said:

وعلم آدم الأسماء كلها ثم عرضهم على الملائكة فقال انبثوني بأسماء هؤلاء ان كنتم صادقين قالوا سبحانك لا علم لنا إلا ما علمتنا انك انت العليم الحكيم قال يا آدم انبئهم باسمائهم فلمًا انبأم باسمائهم قال : الم اقل لكم اني اعلم غيب السماوات والارض

"And He taught Adam the names, all of them, then He presented them to the angels and said: Inform Me of the names of these, if you are truthful. They said: Glory be to You, we have no knowledge except what You have taught us; You are the Knowing, the Wise. He said: O Adam, inform them of their names. And when he had informed them of their names, He said: Did I not say to you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth," the verse. So the people were not knowing, and because they were so, Adam came to them as a teacher for them.

Chapter Twenty-Two of Title IX

And we say:

As for his saying and his affirmation that the creation was not, at the first, in the cycle of the Qa'im (ع), ignorant of monotheism: had they not been ignorant, they could not have deduced it except from the side of those who give aid, in which lies the completeness of their essences and their forms; and there would have been no cause for Adam to be chosen out from among them to be the caliph of Allah, exalted be He, in teaching them what holds their good sooner and later. And it is impossible that this conception be pictured in those people together with the granting to them of a rank that would be their own.

And as for his saying, "nor in the cycle of the Qa'im are they ignorant": if what he means is the state of the summons in the cycle of the Qa'im (ع) before his manifestation and before his existence from the world of nature, when it is preserved by his caliphs and his summoners after the completion of the cycle of Muhammad al-Mustafa (ع) and his House, up to his time and the appearance of his signs in the world, then it is true. For knowledge at that time has raised high the beacon of its manifestation by the multitude of the learned. It is the time of which Allah, exalted be He, said,

يوم يجعل الولدان شيباً

"A day that will make the children grey-haired." But if what he means by his saying, "in the cycle of the Qa'im they are not ignorant," is the state after the Qa'im (ع), who is the possessor of the seventh cycle, and of the decisive utterance, and of the distinguishing of the people of the Garden from the people of the Fire, and of the closing of the door of teaching and of repentance, then it is not sound. For they do not remain knowing, nor are they of the people of knowledge, since that time is the time for the waters to overflow and be swallowed inward, and the time for dryness and barrenness to prevail and appear. And the words of the author of al-Nusrah in that are not those of one who has examined the matter to its end.

Chapter Twenty-Three of Title IX

In sum, the author of al-Nusrah only brought forward these sayings and these citations in support of the author of al-Mahsul in his refutation of the law of Adam. And Adam, in his summoning of them and in his being a prophet who teaches and guides, is not free of one of these: either he called them by encouragement toward the Garden and the neighborhood of the Lord of the Worlds, or he called them by warning of the Fire and of remoteness from the mercy of the best of creators, or he called them by both together.

If he called them by encouragement, then encouragement, when the thing encouraged is not sensible, cannot be except by the striking of likenesses and by reports about it in terms of what is known to the senses, of the kinds of pleasures and blessings and the good places, of dwellings, gardens, rivers, and trees, that its picture may fall in their minds. And that is a form of speech that requires interpretation. If he called them by warning, then likewise his speech cannot be except by the striking of likenesses for them, of that which is feared, drawn from the world of sense, that they may picture it, and in its interpretation it runs the course of the others that have gone before. And if he called them by both together, then his speech is not free of an apparent sense to be acted upon, and so it requires interpretation. If that is so, then the three divisions make obligatory a fixed apparent sense established for Adam, followed by an interpretation. So Adam, then, is the possessor of a law and of manifest customs.

Chapter Twenty-Four of Title IX

Then we say:

Adam (ع) is not free of one of these: either he called the people of his time to the obedience of Allah, exalted be He, and to obedience of him, by wisdom, or not by wisdom. And he does not do so devoid of wisdom, for that is impossible, and to hold it of him is a going out from religion. So if he called them by wisdom, and wisdom requires knowledge and work together, then Adam, then, is the possessor of a law that gathers knowledge and work, which are the worship of Allah, and by which perfection is attained and the ascent to the stations of honor.

Chapter Twenty-Five of Title IX

As for the Qa'im (ع) and the saying that he raises the laws, we say:

To hold that belief is unsound. For the estimation of his existence from the world of nature does not arise by the way of the second emanation, because he does not nullify anything that pertains to the worship of Allah, exalted be He. Rather he is a seal for cycles that have passed, and a completion of a matter that was completed in his place. He removes the limits from their ranks by the falling of the referral in teaching about them, and the fullness of the matter in the new creation, of which Allah, exalted be He, said,

بل هم ليس من خلق جديد

after He has established a decree in the souls of any from whom there would run the course of a foundation among the possessors of the empty cycles. For this Allah, exalted be He, said, making plain the possessor of the Resurrection,

واذا السماء انفطرت وإذا الكواكب انتشرت

"And when the heaven is cleft asunder, and when the planets are scattered." And He said,

إذا الشمس كورت واذا النجوم انكدرت واذا الجبال سيرت واذا العشار عطلت

"When the sun is folded up, and when the stars fall, and when the mountains are set moving, and when the pregnant camels are left untended." All these likenesses are struck upon the limits and the summonses in their passing away from their ranks, not upon the laws and the customs of the works, whose landmarks, with the passing of time, are effaced by the absence of teachers, except what was necessary in its existence, according to what we have already mentioned. And that time in which this act appears, of the closing of the door of teaching and the ceasing of the summonses on the part of the possessor of the greatest Resurrection, is the beginning of the return of knowledge to potentiality, so that it is swallowed inward, and the beginning of the increase of ignorance and its standing in act. So the appearance of the signs in that manner suffices the one who is sufficient and has come of age. May Allah make us and the community of the believers of the people of the divine graces, by the following of His friends, and may He gather us with Muhammad and Ali and the pure Imams (عليهم السلام), the lights of His purity, by His bounty and His mercy.

Chapter Twenty-Six of Title IX

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Everyone who says that monotheism is not grasped except by the signs and the works has thereby ruled that the First Intellect, the Soul, the angels, the Jadd, the Fath, and the Khayal did not know monotheism, since there were no signs and no works among them or between them.

And we say:

The one who says that does not mean the First Intellect, nor the high limits, but rather the intellects that are in the world of the body, which is the second emanation, since their attaining of completeness is by teaching and by obligation, by knowledge, by signs and works, and by a standing in acquisition. As for the First Intellect and the transcendent limits that are separate from bodies, they did not attain completeness by any of that; rather they gained their perfections from the very beginning of their existence, by origination and by emanation. And it is not permissible that the ruling of this saying be run upon those. And as for what the author of al-Nusrah brought forward concerning the meaning of the seven stars, their fixed positions, their return, and their rectitude, and concerning the six days in which Allah created the heavens and the earth and what is between them, and as for what he mentioned of the resolve, the author of al-Islah has left nothing of the interpretation of that and its meanings that he did not bring forward, and in it is sufficiency, dispensing with an Arabization in the interpretation that scarcely balances and does not match.

Chapter Twenty-Seven of Title IX

The author of al-Islah said:

The single line does not necessitate the opening except by the appearance of the second line, for the line indicates the Speaker, and the opening indicates the law. And the form of a thing does not of necessity appear except after its completion. Just as the opening appeared after the second line, likewise the law of the first was not completed except by the appearance of the second Speaker. So when the second Speaker appeared, the law of the first was completed, and its form appeared in perfection. And likewise the six openings are completed by the seven lines, for there is no law before the first of the Speakers, and no law at the appearance of the seventh; rather the six laws are from the Speakers whose seventh is the Qa'im. And if you draw seven lines, six openings appear from among them, according to these likenesses: one, two, three, four, five, six. And were the analogy to be drawn upon that.
