# Cycles, Prophets, and the Ark

Were the analogy then drawn to say that the first of the Speakers has no law, five openings would have to appear between the seven lines. But six openings appeared from among the seven lines, and by this the seven lines became the masters of the seven cycles, the six openings became the six laws, and the law was made obligatory upon Adam (ع).

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Concerning the chapter of the six openings between the seven lines, and his inference from the lines upon the Prophets and from the openings upon the six laws, the matter is not as he stated. Rather, the seven lines stand for the higher letters, since every Prophet inscribed a single letter of them; and the six openings stand for the cycles of the Imams: between Adam and Noah, who are the seven Imams; between Noah and Abraham in the second cycle; between Abraham and Moses in the third cycle; and so on up to Muhammad (ع). No further cycle remains for the two Qa'ims save the cycle of their successors and substitutes in the world. Thus six openings fell across five lines, inasmuch as between the cycles of the Imams there fell five Speakers who were the masters of the laws, in this likeness:

Adam - Noah - Abraham - Moses - Jesus - Muhammad - al-Qa'im
the foundation - the foundation - the foundation - the foundation - the imams - the imams - the imams

Just as no Imams occur after the Qa'im (ع), so he is not charged with establishing the law; and likewise, since no Imams occurred before Adam (ع), he too was not charged with establishing the law. For if the law, in its establishment, must be preceded by Imams, it is likewise followed by them. The foundation of Noah (ع) preceded the cycle of the foundation of Adam (ع), so Noah was followed by Imams, and therefore the establishment of the law fell to him. Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad (عليهم السلام) were the same. But the Qa'im (ع) was preceded by the cycle of Muhammad (ع) and is followed by no Imams, and therefore the establishment of the law does not fall to him. Likewise Adam was followed by the Imams of his cycle and was preceded by no other Imams, and therefore the establishment of the law did not fall to him. This is what they say.

We say:

The statement of the author of al-Islah agrees with what he has indicated, save for his saying, within the body of his words, that "just as the opening appeared after the second line, so the law of the first was not fulfilled except by the appearance of the second Speaker." This is a sick saying and not upright. For the law of Adam (ع) is itself its own foundation, and he it was who raised up its foundation for it. Were it permissible that the law of Adam was not fulfilled until the coming of the Speaker who holds the second cycle, it would likewise be permissible to say that the law of Muhammad (ع) is not complete and is fulfilled only by the coming of the Qa'im, who holds the seventh cycle (ع); and that is among the impossibilities of speech, for the law is a completed stature that requires no increase and no decrease. Had the author of al-Islah instead said that the first cycle, which belongs to Adam (ع), was not fulfilled except by the appearance of the holder of the second cycle, who is Noah (ع), and that likewise no one of the cycles is fulfilled except by the appearance of the one that follows it, so that he set "the first cycle" in place of his phrase "the law," his words would have been consistent and balanced. And Allah forgives us and him through His mercy.

Chapter Twenty-Eight of Title IX

As for the saying of the author of al-Nusrah concerning the line and the opening, and his interpretation of the six openings upon the Imams, it is sound, provided that the lines stand for the Speakers and not for the higher letters, and that the openings stand for the duration of their command in the cycle and not for the foundations. Even so, this does not require that Adam (ع) have no law; rather it makes the law obligatory precisely by the presence of the Imams after him. For the presence of the Imams is on account of the duty to preserve the station of the Speaker, his law, and his designs within his community; and were it not for that, no obedience to an Imam after a Speaker would be obligatory. As for the five lines and the six openings, the author of al-Islah may take them and set the lines upon the five Possessors of Resolve and the openings upon the cycles of the masters of the six laws, since the foremost thing in interpretation is that the thing be identical and balanced when it is stated, so that its place in the souls is the more exalted.

In sum, we say:

The cycle is of two kinds, a major cycle and a minor cycle. The major cycle belongs to the Speakers, whose place the Imams after them preserve within their community; and the minor cycle belongs to the completing foundations, who seal the weeks. If the lines are interpreted upon the masters of the major cycles, who are the Speakers, then the openings can only be the duration of their governance of their community through the Imams who come after them in the cycle that is sealed by the coming of the last of their like, and its measure is known. And if they are interpreted upon the masters of the seven minor cycles, who are the completing Imams in every cycle, then the openings can only be the duration of the command of the Imams in each week, together with their completers whose cycle is sealed by the seventh of them; and their number is known, as is the number of Imams in each cycle, and the number of the appendages according to the existing higher bodies already described. This will be set down in a book other than this one, if Allah the Exalted permits.

Chapter Twenty-Nine of Title IX

The author of al-Nusrah said:

By way of example, the words of Allah the Exalted:

((كما بدأكم تعودون))
"As He originated you, so shall you return."

That is, as the beginning of the matter was, which was not from the side of the realities, so shall the end of the matter be.

We say:

Even if this is an interpretation, the proper face of it is to say what corresponds. The words of Allah the Exalted are a reference to what comes to pass after the Qa'im (ع): the return of knowledge, after its going forth into act and its abundance, to potency and to fewness; and the coming of all people after him to be as they were in the time of Adam; and the return of ignorance, after its having been in potency and fewness, to act and to abundance, by reason of the measured wisdom therein. Blessed be Allah, the best of creators.

Chapter Thirty of Title IX

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Again, had the author of al-Islah looked at what lies in his name, I mean Adam, of the indication that he had no law, that would have become plain to him. For his name is of three letters, which are Alif, Dal, and Mim (الف، دال، ميم), and they are neither compounded nor composed; rather each letter of them stands apart from the other, a clear indication that he was not the author of the composition of the law. But the five Prophets who came after him have names that are composed and compounded, such as Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad (نوح، إبراهيم، موسى، عيسى، محمد), which is evidence that each of them was the master of a law. This is what he said.

We say:

If the separateness of the letters of Adam's name requires that Adam (ع) have no law, then the fact that these letters are in themselves not separate requires that he have a law. For the Alif of the name is composed of alif, lam, and fa' (الف، لام، فاء); the Mim of it is composed of mim, ba', and mim (ميم، باء، ميم); and likewise the Dal. If separateness does not oblige a law for him, then non-separateness does oblige a law. Yet inference of this kind is an impossibility and does not hold. For were it sound, it would follow that the Qa'im (ع) is the author of composition, since his name, as the Prophet (ع) reported it, is Muhammad, and Muhammad is composed of four letters. And since it is impossible that the Qa'im be the master of a law while his name is composite, it is void that the separateness of the letters of Adam's name should indicate that he is not the author of composition and of a law, just as it is void that the joining of the letters of the name of the Qa'im (ع), who is Muhammad, and its being composite, should indicate that he is the master of a law. When that is void, no weight attaches to inference of this sort. So the law of Adam (ع) is established, and praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds.

Chapter Thirty-One of Title IX

The author of al-Islah said:

As for the saying that Noah (ع) was the first to raise up his foundation, and the likening of him to the ship, enough has already been said of it in the chapter on the foundations.

We say:

Noah (ع) was singled out by the ark because he was the first of the Possessors of Resolve, and the first to undertake the abrogation of the laws and to compose a new law. His law was in the station of the ship, and that with which he abrogated the former law was in the station of the flood, for by it he destroyed the people of the earth save those who answered his law and boarded his ship, that is, who established obedience to his foundation. The meaning is that his law endured through his lifetime; and when he had perfected it, he committed the matter to his foundation and commanded him to raise up the summons upon his law, and commanded the appendages to hearken to him, so that they attained the flowing grace only through him. Whoever obeyed his foundation was in the ship, and whoever disobeyed him and held to the first law was drowned. Do you not see that those who worshipped Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq, and Nasr (وداً، وسواعاً، ويغوث، ويعوق، ونسراً) were described as drowned by the flood, for He said:

((من خطيئاتهم اغرقوا فادخلوا النار))
"Because of their sins they were drowned, then made to enter the Fire."

The author of al-Nusrah said, and we say:

If the station of the ship is like the station of the outward law, then whoever held to it from the people of the outward was saved, and the veiled, true summons is annulled. Moreover, what is outward in the Book of Allah the Exalted is other than what the author of al-Islah went to. For Allah the Exalted described the beginning of the command of Noah (ع), and his being sent to his people, and his calling them to his law, and then commanded him to fashion the Ark. This account is set down in the Sura of Hud; and had the ship been the outward law, Allah would have commanded him first to fashion the Ark. But the story turns upon the fact that he first composed the outward law and called creation to make use of it, and only then commanded him to fashion the Ark, meaning to raise up his legatee. The evidence of this is the words of Allah the Exalted:

((واصنع الفلك))
"And fashion the Ark,"

up to His words:

((وكلما مرّ عليه ملأ من قومه سخروا منه))
"And whenever a company of his people passed him, they mocked him,"

meaning that whenever Noah (ع) raised up his legatee, the nobles of his people mocked him and marveled at him, because he was the first to inaugurate the raising up of the legatee; and for that they mocked him. This is what they said.

We say:

As for the saying of the author of al-Islah and his interpretation of the ship upon the outward of the new law, it is because whoever entered beneath its rulings had it become for him in the station of a ship, so that he was delivered from abasement and humiliation and every kind of affliction, just as the one within a ship is delivered from drowning; and this meaning is admissible. As for the author of al-Nusrah and his interpretation of the ship upon the summons and the legatee, it is because whoever entered the guiding summons and bound himself to obedience to the foundation was made safe from drowning in error, away from the unity of Allah the Exalted and the knowledge of His limits, that summons being for him in the station of guidance. Both interpretations are sound in this respect, the one in the matter of this world and the other in the matter of religion, and neither of them diminishes the other, for each of them stands upon its own head.

Chapter Thirty-Two of Title IX

As for the saying of the author of al-Nusrah that Noah was the first to raise up the legatee, by the interpretation that came to him, it does not hold. For the prophethood of Adam (ع) and his law are established, and it is confirmed that Seth (ع) was his legatee, the foremost in the law and its establisher under him. Reason itself indicates that each of the Speakers must of necessity have a legatee who takes his benefit from him, especially since every mover of necessity has a moved, the mover being one even if many are moved from him. And since the Speaker in his age is a mover of all souls by his summons to seek subsistence through the raising up of the two worships, it must of necessity be that he have a moved who runs in the course of a learner from him, even if many learners learn from him; and the true learner from him is the one whom we name the legatee. Adam, being a Speaker, must have a legatee, and so must every Speaker. And since Adam did raise up a legatee for himself, it is not permissible to hold any belief that contradicts that.

Chapter Thirty-Three of Title IX

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Following his interpretation of what Allah the Exalted related concerning the son of Noah:

((سآوي الى جبل يعصمني من الماء))
"I shall take refuge on a mountain that will shield me from the water,"

and Noah's reply:

((لا عاصم اليوم من امر الله))
"There is no shield today from the command of Allah,"

he held that the matter was, before Noah, in the hands of the appendages, since the son did not know the rank of the legatee; and that had Adam possessed a legatee, his son would have known of it. This is what he said.

We say:

This saying of his contradicts what he himself said in the chapter of the cycles: that the Imams after Adam (ع) took charge of his cycle. I do not know how this escaped him. I suspect that the copy of the book has been altered, added to, and diminished through the fewness of those who attend to the matter of religion and their neglect of correcting what they write by collation and by reading it back to the people of knowledge and religion; and Allah is the one whose help is sought.

The interpretation of "the son of Noah" is that the son ascribed to Noah was not of his loins. Rather he was a man of great worldly standing, whom Noah had advanced, like the adversary in the cycle of Muhammad our Prophet (ع). He took his benefit from Noah, learned what he had, and by his own strength became a son to him in rank, so that he reckoned it within himself, and the people too imagined that he would be his legatee. But when the command of Allah the Exalted came, that he fashion the Ark, that is, that Noah confer the legateeship upon another below him in accordance with what Allah had commanded, Noah called the people who followed him to enter beneath his command and obedience, and that is His saying:

((اركبوا فيها))
"Embark in it."

So this man held back from what others had entered into, namely obedience to his foundation, by his refusal to enter the ship. Noah (ع) called him with his words:

((يا بني اركب معنا ولا تكن مع الكافرين))
"O my son, embark with us, and be not with the disbelievers,"

calling him to obedience and to binding himself to the command, especially with the desire that he return to his beginning, reckoning that he would act as Noah had seen of his former goodly compliance with his commands. But upon Noah's calling him, and his despairing utterly of that rank he had hoped for, he grew hypocritical and arrogant and refused to obey his foundation in what had come to pass. Noah said that the command of Allah lay upon him: were he not to relinquish his headship, it would be nullified; and the like of this adversary inclined against our guardian (ع). Noah admonished him at one time and warned him at another, saying that the command of Allah lay in it, and that if he did not heed the admonition his headship would depart and abasement would encompass him, and he would see none to take his part nor to help him in his affair; and that is His saying:

((لا عاصم اليوم من امر الله))
"There is no shield today from the command of Allah."

When the son heard this from him, he showed rebellion and said, in his ignorance, that this matter would not be brought to completion for Noah, that he should set the whole of the headship within his own house; and that even if constraint and injury and harm appeared, he would be supported by chiefs who would strengthen his hand and help him to ward off abasement from himself; and that is his saying:

((سآوي الى جبل يعصمني من الماء))
"I shall take refuge on a mountain that will shield me from the water."

The water here is that which is poured out upon the force that slays, and the mountain is that which he clings to of men and partisans. So he set his purpose, and he and his like took up his cause, and they perished in his cycle, and there remained only those who bound themselves to obedience to his foundation.

Allah has set down such tidings in His noble Book as an admonition to the community concerning what befalls the command of the foundation through him who is intransigent toward it and does not bind himself to its obedience. Now He indicates it through our Prophet's word, "O children of Adam"; now He points to it by the she-camel of Salih; and now by the son of Noah; that they may know that the states of the cycle of Muhammad are like the states of the bygone cycles, and that He did not single out Muhammad and his foundation (عليهم السلام) with the intransigence of their community toward them and its hostility toward them. This is a custom that has run through all the Prophets and legatees, reaching to the time of the seventh. The refuge sought by the son of Noah was not to the appendage, but to the people of the outward who were in a state of hypocrisy like his own; he sought their aid, and the sword encompassed them by the power of Allah, and Allah purified the earth of them. That is the flood that overspread outwardly and inwardly. So likewise shall it be in the cycle of Muhammad (ع): some of it has already come true and passed, and some of it shall come true and come to pass by the leave of Allah, and none knows that save the discerning one among the people of the Alawi summons, whose lights Allah has spread abroad.
