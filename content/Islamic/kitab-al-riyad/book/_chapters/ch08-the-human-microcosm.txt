# The Human Microcosm

On the judgment between the two claimants, concerning what has been said about the human being's being the fruit of the physical world.

**The First Section**

The author of al-Islah said:

Man is the fruit of this whole world, and it is for his sake that this world has been brought into being in all its parts, from its very origins and foundations. The first of these is prime matter (al-hayula), which is the principle of the individuals born of it through motion and rest; then the individuals, which are the foundation of the vehicles; then the compounds, which are the foundation of all that this world generates; then the natures out of which the form of the human being is composed, that form which is made ready to receive the three souls: the vegetative, the sensitive, and the speaking.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

To say that human beings are the fruit of this world is likewise the saying of the materialists, those who did not affirm the survival of the soul after its separation from bodies. For if human beings were the fruit of this world, this world would hold him fast and would not release him to pass on to another world.

But we would say:

Human beings are the fruit of the highest world, the noble and luminous world. It is for this reason that the prophets (عليهم السلام) came with their warning, so that men might provision themselves from the house of mortality for the house of permanence, and from this world for the abode of the Return. For the prophets knew that this world is not a lasting home, and that man is not the fruit of this world. So he said.

We say:

Each of the two claimants is truthful in what he said from one side, and untruthful from another. The side on which both are truthful is that each has affirmed of the human being what genuinely belongs to him. The side on which both are untruthful is that each denies him the other portion that is also his, namely his being the fruit of both worlds together. For the human being issues from between the two worlds. From this world he receives his first perfection, and from that world he receives his second. The end of the natural world is to grant its existents their first perfection, which is the bringing forth of the bodily form by way of the revolving bodies, the luminous planets, and the proximate causes, into the disposition by which he is made ready to accept what belongs to him. And how should he not accept it, when he is its fruit, beyond which there is scarcely anything further? The end of that other world is to grant what has already received the first perfection from the existents of the natural world its second perfection, which is the overflowing upon the spiritual form, the thing naturally made ready, that by which it becomes substantial and endures: the knowledge of the Oneness of Allah the Exalted by means of all the hudud (عليهم السلام), and obedience to them in the two modes of worship, in knowledge and in deed. That is his fruit. What corrects what we have said in this is the saying of our master, the Commander of the Faithful, al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah Ma'ad ibn Isma'il (ع), in the book *The Interpretation of the Sharia* (Ta'wil al-Shari'a):

> ان الانسان هو غرض الثاني ونهاية فعل الطبيعة

that man is the purpose of the second world and the end of the action of nature.

**The Second Section**

As for the statement of the author of al-Nusrah and his objection, that if human beings were the fruit of this world then this world would hold him fast and not release him to pass from it to another world, he has guided his opponent to the reversal of the matter against him.

So his opponent says:

If, as I have said, man is the fruit of the higher world, then that world would hold him fast and not release him to send these persons back, so that he should be forgotten and pass away, as I have written and laid down as necessary. Yet this is not a statement that proves in the abstract what he intends to establish, nor is what he adduced concerning the sending of the prophets and their command to men to provision themselves any proof that human beings are the fruit of this world. For the human being who is in truth human is such by the gathering of the overflow of the two worlds: from the natural world, that which runs in the channel of prime matter, and from the world of the Intellect, that which runs in the channel of the form. The one comes by intermediaries, and the other by intermediaries.

**The Third Section**

The author of al-Islah said:

The Soul emanates from the First Intellect in perfection, and prime matter emanates from the Soul in perfection, and the form emanates from prime matter in perfection. So the whole was perfect.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

If the matter stands as he has said, that the Soul is perfect like the Intellect, then his statement concerning what is spread out over prime matter, the form, and the compounds is void and self-contradictory. For it is not permissible to imagine that these fashioned things come to be except from the pairing of one that is perfect and rich with another that is deficient and in need, so that a marriage may take place between them, as we see among things witnessed, such as the heavens and the earth. The heavens stand in the rank of the male, of perfection and self-sufficiency, in all their motions and their manifest orbits, and the earth is deficient, in the rank of the female, and her need is established in her. For this reason a marriage took place between them, and from between them appeared the various kinds of metals with their differing forms, and the plants, and the animals, not one of which is actually present in the heavens, nor in the earth, nor in the mothers, but they were present in them in potency. When the two were married, all of them appeared in actuality. Had their state not been as we have described, the one deficient and the other perfect, nothing would have appeared from them. So he said.

We say:

As for the statement of the author of al-Islah that the Soul is the second, emanating from the First Intellect in perfection, that is correct, according to what was said above. But as for his statement that prime matter emanates from the Soul, we have already shown that its existence is not from the Soul, which is the second; rather its existence is from the First Intellect and the second together, neither of the two being prior to the other, and neither having existence in isolation from the other except within the soul by way of imagination. Outside the soul the two are found only together. And as for the statement of the author of al-Nusrah, that it is not permissible to imagine the fashioned things coming to be except from the pairing of one perfect and rich with another deficient and in need, so that they may be coupled, as he set it out and likened it to the heavens and the earth, he has already refuted himself by the words that follow, when he said that from between them appeared the various kinds of metals with their forms, and the plants, and the animals, not one of which is actually present in the heavens, nor in the earth, nor in the mothers, because they were in potency. For this is to say that some of these things, I mean the generated beings, even if they are not actually present in the heavens and the earth because they are in potency, then the heavens and the earth are equal in respect of their being in potency, and no superiority of the one over the other has appeared. It is not the case that when existents come to be from between two things, one of the two things must be perfect and the other deficient. We have shown that deficiency is disclosed in things not in their selves, but in their ranks when some are added to others, and in their acts when they are brought about only through something else by which their existence is completed. In their selves each is perfect. For if their selves were not perfect in the existence that befits them, no act could proceed from them, neither by themselves nor by another. The state in which existents are found bears witness to this. For the heaven, which he singled out for perfection and mastery, is deficient in its act, since its act is completed only by that upon which it revolves, and the earth, which he singled out for deficiency and need, has its deficiency not in its self but in its act, and its act is completed only by that which revolves upon it. Such too is the state of the animal and of the newborns. If this is so, then the saying of the author of al-Nusrah, that the statement of the author of al-Islah is a contradictory and biased statement, is itself the contradictory and biased statement.

**The Fourth Section**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

He did not answer what was raised at the end of this section concerning the name of perfection, al-Tamamiyyah, falling upon the Originator, glorified be He, on account of its falling upon the complete origination.

We say:

The author of al-Islah mentioned nothing of the sort. What he quoted from the sages in the book of al-Islah, that the Originator whose origination is complete is complete, and that the one who is the perfection could only be perfect, is a saying than which none is more sound. By that saying he affirmed that the perfect and the perfected are the origination and the originated, not the Originator, glorified be He, and that the name of perfection, by what he mentioned, does not fall upon the Originator, glorified be He, and that nothing of the sort is folded into his words. The claim of the author of al-Nusrah here is a bias against the author of al-Islah.

**The Fifth Section**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

All of us say that origination is the cause of the originated, and that it, together with the originated, from the side of the origination, is one single thing, and it is perfect, and the originated by the complete origination can only be perfect. Nor are we obliged to say that the Originator, glorified be He, is perfect, or that He is a cause, or that He, glorified and exalted, and the origination are one single thing.

We say:

That is a statement sound in meaning. Yet we do not say that the originated by the complete origination can only be perfect because origination is perfection; rather we say that the originated by the origination which is the perfection can only be perfect, and that the originated is the origination, and it is the cause, and it is the effect (al-ma'lul), and the perfection, and the perfect. The names follow upon it in succession according to the relations, because there is there a multiplicity in the essences, so that the meanings of these names fall upon them all, even though the thing there is one. In what we have adduced upon this in this book and in others there is sufficiency, without prolonging the matter.

**The Sixth Section**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

When it is established that this world is the act of the Soul, and that it is withal in the utmost order and arrangement, with perpetual circular motions, we know and are certain that by her act she sought to attain the rank that was withheld from her before she began to act. Since this is so, and since there is nothing in the world nobler than the human being, nor anything more fit to receive the effects of the Soul from her and in which her benefits take better hold, we know and are certain that the human being is the fruit of the Soul's attainment of the end appointed for her by her cause. So understand that.

We say:

We have already said, in treating of the perfection of the consequent, which in his view is the Universal Soul, what shows that in her act she did not seek to attain a rank she had not gained. The Universal Soul, which is the second, overflows upon this world not in order to draw to herself what is not hers, for Allah enriched her, when He brought her into being, with perfection, so that she has no need to seek anything not given her; rather she acts to establish wisdom in bringing forth what is in potency into actuality, and to make what is base excellent, and what is later in existence a follower of what precedes. When one reflects, the benefit returns upon that which is brought forth into act, and upon the one who is under the burden of obligation, for training in perfection, not upon her who is neither obligated nor in need. To hold such a thing of that mighty limit is error and sin. Since this is so, and since the human being has two sides, one side natural and one side divine, he is neither the fruit of this world purely, nor the fruit of that world purely, but the fruit of both worlds together. He is the last in the created things, united with eternity by what he has acquired in time. It is for this that it has been said:

> ما جرى في أولهم، جرى في آخرهم

what came to pass in the first of them came to pass in the last of them.

And whoever has known from that the stations of the Prophets, the guardians, the imams, and the righteous (عليهم السلام), he is made ready for deliverance. May Allah gather us with our pure masters in the Abode of Holiness, Dar al-Quds.

**The Seventh Section**

As for the statement of the author of al-Nusrah, that every agent acts only in seeking what he wishes to attain, and especially when the act proceeds from the agent with motion, order, and arrangement, and his holding that this is not necessary in the First Originator and in origination, because the act there is metaphorical, not by motion, nor order, nor arrangement, but its first is its last, and its beginning and its middle are the utmost of its end, and its end is its middle, there being in it no order and no arrangement; rather order and arrangement are in that which the consequent gains from the lights of that in which there is no arrangement and no order, which is the First Intellect; and the Soul has become very like the Intellect, so that when you look through her toward it you apprehend the powers of things that are not ordered and not arranged, apprehending things all at once, which belongs to her only when she is united with the Intellect and has pushed back the material things, the dark things of nature.

We say:

How can what he said be sound, when order and arrangement are found only from the Intellect? And how can it be sound, when things taken in the whole are made excellent only by the Intellect, while the Soul, so long as the effects of the Intellect do not take hold in her, she and the beasts are alike? It is for this that Allah has lifted knowledge from the Soul until the effects of the Intellect take hold in her. We have already said that the proposition concerning agents is not universal, so that not every agent seeks by his act the attainment of a want or a purpose. What I judge he intended by that saying is that the essence of the First Intellect is not composite, that it should be possessed of arrangement and order. As for apprehension, the Intellect apprehends only a true apprehension.

The author of al-Nusrah said: this is not the case of the soul and the body, where the measure between them is only the gentler and the denser. For if the case were as he stated it, then the tangible essences would have to be bodies for the accidents, since they are denser than the accidents, and the accidents would have to be souls for them, since they are gentler than they. Rather, we must look to the limit of the body and the limit of the soul, and then liken what resembles the limit of the soul to the soul, and what resembles the limit of the body to the body. We say: we found the body to be that which accepts the three dimensions, which are length, width, and depth. We found the soul to have parts that resemble one another in like manner. And we found motion to be divided into three sections, just as the body is divided, namely motion from the center, motion toward the center, and motion around the middle. So motion must resemble the body in its division, splitting as the body splits into length, width, and depth. It is not so for the hyle, since the parts of the hyle resemble one another. And because motion resembles the body in the matter of division, while the hyle resembles the soul in the similarity of its parts, it is more fitting to liken motion to the body, which is divided, and the hyle to the soul, whose parts are undivided and alike, than to liken motion to the soul. This is what they said.

We say: in this chapter the two of them have strayed far in representation and simile, and both have built upon a corrupt foundation on which no proof can stand, so that neither the likeness nor the thing likened to it holds true. The author of al-Islah made it obligatory that motion and stillness be like the soul for the hyle and the form, and that is impossible. For motion is the act of the mover, and the mover is what we call the soul, the soul itself; and likewise stillness is the mover's letting go of what it moves. If the mover is the soul, then to represent by it the motion which is its own act is to represent the impossible, and the same holds for stillness. And if the matter is invalidated for motion and stillness, then it is also invalidated that motion and stillness are the hyle and the form. Moreover, the path of the hyle and the form is not the path of motion and stillness, for motion and stillness, even when they are acts, are two opposites that do not meet in one place, in one state, and at one time; whereas the hyle and the form exist together, neither of them separable from the other, since one of them is an agent, which is the form, and the other is acted upon within it, which is the hyle. Our book known as The Rest of the Mind gathers the explanation of that.

Then, in what follows, he was obliged to say that the soul is gentler than the body, because the soul is a body, and that is impossible. The soul cannot be a body such that density or gentleness attaches to it, for density and gentleness belong only to that which is a body, just as air, which is a body, is characterized by gentleness, and earth, which is a body, is characterized by density. The soul is the soul, and the proofs have established that it is not a body. And if the soul is not a body, then it is impossible to argue from it that it is gentler or denser, or to represent anything by it.

The author of al-Nusrah then came and promised to define the body and the soul. He defined the body by its accepting the three dimensions, and defined the soul by the resemblance of its parts, and he set it in the course of water, fire, and air, each of which is alike in its parts. That is impossible, in view of what has already been said, that the soul is not a body such that this would attach to it. Al-nafs (النفس) and al-ruh (الروح) are two synonyms for one and the same thing, and that thing is not a body. For if it were a body, it would necessarily require of the qualities and accidents the nine that a body requires, and it could be perceived by sense. But since it is not perceived by sense, it is not perceivable, and the nine accidents are not conceived in it, nor found in it; and since the bodily accidents are not found in it, it is invalidated that it be a body. Our well-known book With Lamps contains what heals this.

Then he made motion, in its division into three sections, resemble the body in the way he mentioned, that the body is divided into length, width, and depth, and that is impossible. For the three sections into which he claimed the body divides, which are the three dimensions, none of these three dimensions is a body, nor is it said to be a body; whereas each section of motion is itself a motion, and is said to be a motion. So how can the body, which divides in form into nine sections, as we have shown in our well-known book Milestones of Religion, be likened to motion, which divides into three sections, or six sections? That is far removed. The discourse of the author of al-Mahsul, the author of al-Islah, and the author of al-Nusrah in this chapter is a disorder without order.

We say: motion is that by which the very being of a thing is altered, whether in place, or in coming to be, or in corruption, or in increase, or in decrease, or in transformation; and its occurrence is from the mover, which is the principle in the thing in which it resides, by nature and not by accident. The hyle and the form are what is sent forth from the First according to the lower relation within it, just as the second is sent forth according to the nobler relation within it. There is no existence of the hyle apart from the soul at all, except together with the form; and from it are the heavens and the earth and what lies between them. God Almighty brought all of this into being from the First Intellect, which He originated not out of any thing. Exalted is God with a great exaltation.

The author of al-Islah said: the first cause, which is origination, and which the learned call the word of the Maker, glorified and exalted, has for its form كن ("Be!"), which is two letters, a moving كاف (kaf) and a still نون (nun). The letters, the كاف and the نون, stand for the word in the position of the body, while motion and stillness stand in the position of the soul. On this footing the example is set in the motion and stillness that are caused by the word, and the first cause, which is the word, is the hyle of the whole world. On this same example it must be said of motion and stillness in all the substances, from the hyle and the form down to what is below them, throughout all the worlds. The whole of the account concerning the three hyles is this: the first hyle is the imaginary one, and it is motion and stillness, and it is the very substance of the hyle and its form; the second hyle is the individuals and the things generated from the first hyle; and the third hyle is the four composite mother elements, composed from the second hyle.

Then we say: the hyle and its form are the act of the command, which is origination. As we have said that the hyle is imaginary, so too origination is imaginary, its essence appearing only in the First Originated.

The author of al-Nusrah said: as for what the author of al-Islah advanced concerning كن, saying that the كاف is moving and the نون is still, and that this stands in the position of the body while motion and stillness stand for it in the position of the soul, that is not granted to him. These two letters were used to express the matter only here; there is no كاف there, nor نون, nor motion, nor stillness, for it is not said that above the First Effect there is motion or stillness or anything of this kind. There is only pure origination and the complete cause. This is what they say.

We say: the saying of the author of al-Islah, that the first cause, which is origination, is the word of God Almighty and its form, and that these are two letters, is a remote likeness in the way he intended it, and it scarcely holds. For our saying "form" requires that there be something of which it is the form, and the word has nothing of which the form would be its form. When we say "form" and mean by it what is above nature, we mean the abstracted form; and when we say "form" and mean by it what is in the natural world, we mean the form that is in matter. Yet if he had said that origination, which is the word of God Almighty and His command, as expressed by كن, is two letters, one of them moving and the other still, indicating an essence gathering two attributes, then he would have inferred from that the noble Essence and its attributes.

He said: it gathers two states, just as the word كن gathered two states, motion and stillness. So he attributed the two states to what the Essence bears of the two noble relations, one of which is its being a cause, and the other its being an effect. He set the motion whose end is stillness over against the relation which is the cause whose end is the effect, and the stillness whose end is motion over against the relation which is the effect whose end is the cause, not because there is motion or stillness there, but only by way of instruction in the likeness. Had he said this, it would have been more fitting, and what he said would have met the very nature of the thing spoken of, which is the thing represented by the likeness.

Then he said that the first hyle is imaginary, and he made it motion and stillness; then he made motion and stillness the hyle, while the form is not the hyle, but rather it is motion and stillness that are the hyle and the form. Yet the hyle is a substance sent forth from the First, as the discourse upon it has already stated, and from it is the lower world; and motion and stillness have their existence in bodies according to the principles from which their appearance comes; and the hyle and the form together are the body, the body being hyle and form.

Then he said, when he set the first hyle upon the matter which is origination, that the first hyle is imaginary, so that origination is imaginary, its essence appearing only in the First Originated. His saying requires that there be, from eternity together with the Maker, exalted be His majesty above that, something in which origination appeared, so that by origination He became an originator. That is a grave error, for the doctrine and belief in the affirmation of unity is that whatever is other than God Almighty is originated and brought into being, and had no reality in existence at all; and if it be so, then origination is nothing other than the Originator. We have established the proof of this, in what has gone before of our epistles, with what suffices.

The saying of the author of al-Nusrah, in what he said and in the objection he raised against the author of al-Islah, is sound, except that his saying that it should not be said that above the First Effect there is motion and rest is an affirming of motion and rest for the First Effect and for what is below it; and we have already shown that the First Effect is not moving, that motion does not befit it, and that there is no stillness in it either, since it is other than a body.

The author of al-Nusrah said: opposition occurs in bodies, and there is no opposition in spirits. We say: bodies, in so far as they are bodies, have no difference between them and no opposition; the difference and the divergence fall only in the forms that are within them, which move them, not in so far as they are bodies. And his saying, that it is more fitting to liken that in which opposition is found to the like of it, holds as he said, if the very nature of the thing spoken of is such.

The author of al-Islah said: the whole world rests upon four jewels, four compositions, and four classes of humankind; and that it is upon three jewels apart from humankind, the fourth being humankind; and that the congealed and the molten are all one jewel. We do not say that the pair of humankind and its likeness is the horse; rather, the pair of the horse and its likeness is what belongs to its own jewel, just as the pair of gold is silver, and the pair of the ruby is the emerald, and likewise the pair of the horse and its likeness among the animals is the elephant, and among birds the falcon, and among beasts of prey the lion, and so on. Because they are all animals, it is not permissible to say that the horse is the pair of the human and its likeness, for the human is alone in the rational soul and has no counterpart among the parts of the world and its generated beings; rather, he is the counterpart of the world, because he has gathered all the jewels of the world. For this reason he is called the small world, the microcosm, because he is a counterpart of it, gathering all its parts.

We say: the counterpart of the whole of humankind is the sum of his saying, that the world is upon four divisions, that the horse is not a likeness for humankind, and that humankind has no counterpart among the parts of the world, but rather its counterpart is the whole world.

The author of al-Nusrah said: the state of pairing is not the state of genus only; rather, the state of pairing is the state of acceptance. We say: this dense lower world is the pair of the subtle higher world, and when you look at what lies between them you find the gap wider than the gap between the horse and the human. The horse is not of the genus of the human, except that its genus is life, in which the horse shares. He made this dense lower world a pair of the spiritual world only because of the bond between them in the matter of giving and receiving benefit, for the powers of the higher world are joined to this world, and this world receives those powers from the higher world; and for this he made this world the pair of that world. Likewise the human and the horse: he made the horse a pair for it only because it accepts teaching from the human more than the other animals accept it. The sum of his saying is that the horse was made a pair for humankind only because of its acceptance of teaching from him, and that there is no animal whose acceptance is like its acceptance.

We say: the two of them, in what they discussed, did not rest upon sound inquiry. The author of al-Islah made the world upon four jewels, four compositions, and four classes together with humankind, and that is impossible, because the divisions of the world are more numerous than what he brought forward. For our word "world" is a name pointing to many things, from the center to the encompassing ocean, such as sphere, star, fire, air, water, earth, and the generated beings, according to what he reckoned. It is clear from the evidence of his speech that he means the generated beings of the world, not the world and the generated beings; and these are three genera with no fourth, each genus having many species beneath it. The first is the nearest of the four elements, and it is the minerals, which is everything whose existence is confined to one place in the earth rather than another without its parts increasing, and this gathers what dissolves and what does not dissolve, what is malleable and what is not malleable. The second is the plant, which is everything that grows from the earth, and this gathers the shrub and the tree. The third is the animal, which is everything that moves among the things that have species, and this gathers what flies and what does not fly. The generated beings of the world do not exceed this, and the human, whom he mentioned as the fourth, is of the sum of the animal and a species among its species.

The Second Section of the Seventh Chapter

He said:

That humankind has no peer, and that the horse has no pair, is impossible from two angles. The first is that humankind is a type within the genus of animal, and inasmuch as it is a type it is a pair to the other types within its genus, since all the types are forms of one another in respect of quality; so if humankind is a type, it is a pair to the other types. The second is that humankind, being similar in its structure, its morals, its actions, all its conditions, its motions, and its natures to everything that preceded it in the universe of existence, as he himself mentioned, and as is the very reason it is called the small world, must be a pair to all existing things according to the correspondence and likeness within it. And if this is so, then humankind, when it is good, virtuous, wise, religious, knowing, and pure, is paired with the angels by the correspondence between them; when it is courageous, strong, bold, and valiant, its pair is the beasts of prey; when it is unjust, oppressive, and murderous, its pair is the wolves; when deceitful, its pair is the foxes; when it is a treacherous thief, its pair is the crows, the magpies, and the cats; and when it is cunning, wily, and overpowering, its pair is the tiger.

When it is adulterous, immoral, and given to amusement and play, its pair is the monkeys. And upon this pattern, according to whatever a person trains himself to resemble, whether by his actions, his morals, his customs, or in any of his ways, that becomes his pair. If this likeness is established, then it is clear that what he said is not carried through in the chapter where he claimed that humankind has no peer, for humankind has many forms and many pairs from many sides.

The Third Section of the Seventh Chapter

As for the author of al-Nusrah, he spoke correctly regarding the meaning of pairing, but he fell into absurdity in his claim that the horse was made a pair for humankind because it accepts humankind's teaching. For if it is on the basis of acceptance and instruction that the horse becomes its pair, then the horse has no greater claim than any other to be its pair, since among the animals there are those besides the horse that accept teaching and discipline; and if it is on the basis of type, again the horse has no greater claim than the remaining types of animal. So humankind has pairs, not one pair.

In sum we say:

In truth, the pair of humankind can only be the angels. What we mean by humankind here are those possessed of all the virtues: the prophets, the guardians, and the imams (عليهم السلام), and those who follow in their footsteps, uphold obedience to them, and worship Allah with both worships; not those beneath them, people who are merely in the form of humankind while their souls are the souls of beasts and predators, as we have said and shown, like the monkeys, the donkeys, the foxes, the beasts of prey, and the wolves. Those who follow the traces of the imams (عليهم السلام), and above them the prophets and the guardians (عليهم السلام), are distinguished from the beasts only by their striving in obedience and in both worships and their holding fast to the command and the prohibition; and so they come to resemble them (عليهم السلام). Thus they are saved within their company and among them, and no other, for:

> ومن يتولهم منكم فانه منهم

"And whoever takes them as guardians among you is one of them." May Allah make us among those whose gathering and raising is with the prophets, the guardians, and the pure imams (عليهم السلام), all together.

The Fourth Section of the Seventh Chapter

The author of al-Nusrah said:

He cited Bandhaqlis, who said:

The bestial soul returns to the speaking soul by love; and the speaking soul, when it finds good what returns to it from her, inclines toward her with benefit and mastery, and finds her image beautiful. There is no image among the bestial souls more beautiful than the image of the horse, and none more disposed to receive the effects of the speaking soul; and for that reason he made it its pair. This is clear.

And we say:

Had the author of al-Nusrah drawn his evidence from the Book of Allah, Lord of the Worlds, and from the speech of our masters the pure imams (عليهم السلام), for what he wants concerning the matter of the soul by way of interpretation, what he found there would have spared him any dependence on the speech of Bandhaqlis. For if he and his like among the ancients had lived in our time, they would have needed to draw from the seas of the knowledge of the House of Revelation, overflowing from the treasuries of Allah in His earth, and from our pure masters (عليهم السلام); and they would have turned back from much of what they believed concerning what is hidden from the senses, of which they spoke, the intellects and their ranks, the soul and its principles, and would have believed otherwise. Moreover, what Bandhaqlis intended in what he said is not what the author of al-Nusrah supposed, namely that the bestial soul is the horse and the speaking soul is humankind, when it is returned to the balance of consideration and taken in the sense that is sound. Rather, since the sensory soul, which is the bestial one, is by its nature disposed to accept the ruling of the speaking soul when no impediment hinders it, and since the speaking soul, when it sees the other receptive to its ruling, inclines toward it with benefit and sets it aright, we say: what he intended by "the sensory soul" is the one in the form of humankind whose nature is to accept teaching, discipline, and reasoning, not the horse; and what he intended by "the speaking soul" is the teachers, who are the prophets, the guardians, and the imams (عليهم السلام) and their followers, whose nature is to incline toward the one in the form of humankind, to teach them their interests in their religion and their world, to benefit what lies in their right guidance, and to raise their degrees according to their acceptance, being in truth the mothers of humankind, though not every one. And this agrees with what is indicated by the interpretation of the saying of Allah the Exalted:

> يا ايتها النفس المطمئنة ارجعي إلى ربك راضية مرضية

"O reassured soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and well-pleasing" (the verse). To carry his statement upon the sound sense is preferable to carrying it upon the unsound sense; for the horse is not more accepting than the one in the form of humankind, nor is its image more beautiful than the human image, nor are the guardians, the imams, and their followers less inclined toward those beneath them among people than a man is inclined to the training and gentling of the horse; rather, far more so. And if this is so, and there is nothing in the plain sense of Bandhaqlis's words to show that there is no image among the bestial souls more beautiful than the image of the horse, and the matter with the existing animal images is not such that they surpass the beauty of the horse's image, and the case is not as he said, then singling out the horse to be the pair of humankind on the basis of teaching and beauty of image, and arguing from it, is impossible.

The Fifth Section of the Seventh Chapter

The author of al-Islah said:

Humankind is the counterpart of the whole world, and is preferred over it by a noble essence that is not of this world. Its body stands opposite all bodies in their genera, the hard and the solid and the soluble, because in his body there is that which corresponds to each of those genera: bone, flesh, nerve, veins, and cartilage, and all the flowing substances that issue from his body, such as blood, semen, and sweat, and what flows from the eye, the ear, and the mouth, and the rest of what is within him. All of these belong to the essence of bodies and to their genera, and they correspond to the genera of the essence that is one of the three essences of the world: the essence of bodies, which is not united with any of the three souls. His growth, set opposite the growing kinds among the genera of trees in their varieties, corresponds to the genera of the second essence, which is united with the growing soul. His sense, set opposite the animal in its differing genera, is of the genera of the third essence, which is united with the sensory soul, the bestial soul. This is his correspondence with the three jewels of the world; and he is set apart in the fourth rank, becoming the fourth essence, which is the essence of the speaking soul, alone in this station. The upshot of his saying is that humankind is the counterpart of the whole world; that his body stands opposite all the bodies of the world in their differing forms, his growth opposite the plants, his sense opposite the animal; and that he has that by which he is preferred over all and set apart, the speaking soul that is his.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

As for his saying that the soluble and the congealed are one essence, that cannot be rejected; rather the soluble, the congealed, the growing, the sensory, and the speaking are all one essence in respect of essentiality. But in respect of divisions and properties, the congealed essence is not the soluble, and the soluble is not the congealed; they are distinguished only in the place where they were divided, not in the place where they were not divided. He needed to distinguish between them only because he made the human being an intermediary between the dense corporeal world and the subtle spiritual world.

For in him is all that is in the two worlds, so he likened everything in the lower world to him, leaving nothing of it out. He set the congealed bodies opposite his body, his body too being congealed, like the congealing of bodies; then he set the soluble bodies opposite his growth, for the dissolution within them, and dissolution occurs only by the motion of the jewels that dissolve, just as growth occurs only by motion, so when the two resembled one another in respect of motion he likened each to its fellow. He set the growing bodies opposite his sense, and set the animal's semen before it. Thus the remainder of the world is gathered in him, of congealed and soluble and growing and sensory bodies, and he surpasses, by speech, all the rest of the jewels. The upshot of his saying is that humankind has resembled the world until nothing of it is left out; that his body stands opposite the congealed bodies, his growth opposite the soluble bodies, his sense opposite the growing bodies, and his speech opposite the animal; and that he surpasses, by speech, all the rest of the jewels.

And we say:

The dispute between the two contenders in this section is this: the author of al-Islah set, opposite the human being's growth, the plant of the great world that grows and increases, while the author of al-Nusrah set opposite that the soluble bodies; and the author of al-Islah set, opposite the human being's sense, the animal of the great world, while the author of al-Nusrah set opposite his sense the growing bodies; and the author of al-Islah made the speaking soul the very thing by which the human being is set apart, preferred over the world, and made its master, while the author of al-Nusrah set that opposite the animal. The saying of the author of al-Islah is the closer, since what he likens is of the same genus as that to which it is likened, and it has a correspondence that the saying of the author of al-Nusrah does not. In sum, the human being is not the counterpart of this world only, but the counterpart of both worlds together, in that he gathers all the matters that preceded him in existence and is like, in what is within him, to them both. The matter concerning him is upon this, not upon what he said.

The Sixth Section of the Seventh Chapter

We said that the human being, who is humankind, is the counterpart of the two worlds because he gathered the multiplicity existing between the origination and the second emanation, then became one, and so became in himself parallel to the world of origination and emanation, and by his body, the gatherer of its parts, parallel to the corporeal world; and by that he became the two worlds. As for what he has of resemblance to this world: the world is entirely body, of it the subtle, which is what is high, and of it the dense, which is what is low; and the human body is likewise subtle and dense, the subtle being what is high and the dense being what is low. In the world are stars and encompassing spheres, and in the human body likewise the skin encompassing the whole body, and the other coverings of the bone; and what runs the course of the stars, such as the two ears, the two eyes, the nose, and the mouth. In the human being likewise there is a mover that moves, which is the soul. In the world are the four elements, and in the human being likewise the four natures. In the world are rivers, valleys, and waters of differing taste, and mountains and minerals; and in the human being likewise, as the author of al-Islah set forth, the bones and cartilage, the flesh and blood, and what flows and issues from him. In the world is that which grows and increases without sensing, and in the human being likewise that which grows and increases without sensing, such as hair and nails; and his body grows and increases from its smallness to its greatness. In the world is the sensing animal, and likewise in the human being the sensing faculties. The whole world is compositions and combinations, and by what exists of that within him the resemblance to the world is completed, though all that we have adduced here in summary is set out in detail in our book known as Rahat al-Aql. As for what he has of resemblance to the world of origination and the first emanation: that world is the world of intellect, the world of glorification, praise, and sanctification, the world of oneness and devotion, the world of virtues, the world of generosity, and the world of bounty; and humankind, who is the human being, is likewise the gatherer of these virtues, in intellect, glorification, oneness, devotion, bounty, and generosity toward others and in himself. That world has stamped him, and by his body he has resembled this world; and being so, he came to be undistinguished from what preceded him in existence except by rank alone. May Allah make us among those who follow the traces of His friends, and may the intercession of His chosen ones not pass us by, by His grace and His bounties.

The Seventh Section of the Seventh Chapter

The author of al-Islah said:

That humankind is preferred over the world by the noble essence that is not of this world, not meaning that the essence which he denied to be of this world is of that world, namely the noble upper world, according to what was revealed to him. Rather, since it is neither of that world alone nor of this world alone, we say that it is of both worlds together; for its condition, in existing within the two worlds, is the condition of the child in his existing between his two parents, so that it is not permitted to ascribe him to one of them apart from the other, since by them both, and from them both, he came to be. What may be ascribed of him to this world is that which runs the course of matter within him, which is the recipient; and what may be ascribed to that world is that which runs the course of form within him, which is the received, just as with the child, who has from his two parents the higher and nobler form on the one side, and on the other side, lower than that, the receptive matter. The proofs of this are many, found in our book known as Rahat al-Aql. And Allah the Exalted grants us success to the abode of His religion, and provides us the intercession of Muhammad His prophet, and the imams of his pure household, and his progeny (عليهم السلام).

By this the human being comes to resemble that higher world in his soul, and his body comes to resemble this lower world, and being so constituted he is no longer set apart from that whose existence preceded him except in rank alone. May Allah make us among those who follow the traces of His friends and do not overstep them, and grant us the intercession of His chosen ones, by His grace and His bounties.

The seventh section of the seventh chapter.

And the statement of the author of al-Islah:

> ان البشر يفضل على العالم بالجوهر الشريف الذي ليس هو من هذا العالم

That the human being surpasses the world by the noble substance which is not of this world.

Now I do not hold that the substance which he denied could belong to this world is therefore from that world, I mean the higher and noble world above, according to what was disclosed to him. Rather, because the human being is neither from that world alone nor from this world alone, I said that he is from both worlds together. For his condition in existing within the two worlds is the condition of the child in his coming to be between the two parents, so that it is not permitted to trace him to one of them apart from the other, since it is by both together, and from both, that he was brought into being.

What may rightly be traced from him to this world is that in him which runs the course of matter, and this is the receptive. And what is traced to that world is that in him which runs the course of form, and this is the received. This is as it stands with the child, who has from his two parents the higher and nobler form on the one side, and on the other side, lower than that, the receptive matter.

The proofs of this are many, and they are found in our book known as Rahat al-Aql.

> والله تعالى يوفقنا الى موطن دينه ، ويرزقنا شفاعة محمد نبيه ، والأئمة من آله الطاهرين ، وعترته صلوات الله عليهم

And Allah the Exalted grants us success to the abode of His religion, and provides us the intercession of Muhammad His prophet, and the imams of his pure household, and his progeny (عليهم السلام).
