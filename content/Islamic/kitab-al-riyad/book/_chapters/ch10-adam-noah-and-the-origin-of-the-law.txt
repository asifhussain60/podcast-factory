# Adam, Noah, and the Origin of the Law

On Ruling Between the Two

He spoke about it in the chapter of the Law of Adam and the wilayah of Noah (ع).

The First Section

The author of *al-Islah* said:

Whoever claims that the first of the speaker-prophets (عليهم السلام) had no Shari'ah is mistaken. He is mistaken again when he counts him among the possessors of determination, according to what we have explained, because determination occurs only by abrogating a Shari'ah or by renewing another. The first Speaker had no Shari'ah before him to abrogate, so that the name of determination should fall upon him, as we have already argued. Rather, he was the first to draw the lines of religion in command and prohibition. Whoever works to profit from what the rest of the speaker-prophets drew in their apparent laws, from which are inferred the subtleties recommended within them, the meanings of the parables that are called interpretation, which is the manifest wording of the wise, knows that every drawing set down by the speaker-prophets (عليهم السلام), and every command, prohibition, and act of servitude, is a Shari'ah, whether small or great, whether little or much. Each Speaker composes only the measure of what he is commanded, and he does not exceed it. Some of them composed much, and some were restricted to a little and confined to the drawing of statutes and to their wording, as we see that fasting in the Shari'ah of Moses (ع) is less than what it is in the Shari'ah of Jesus (ع), or in the Shari'ah of Jesus there is more than what is in the Shari'ah of Muhammad (ع). Likewise the rest of the rulings in religion increase and diminish according to the difference of their cycles and according to the measure of what they were commanded.

No one may hold that this great cycle, the longest of these cycles, passed without work, that its people went on without profit, and without apparent statutes whose rulings they observed. For if there were no work, no profit, and no apparent statutes, then there is no interpretation, no inner meaning, and no true summons, because the summons is raised upon the Shari'ah, and interpretation requires the revelation, and the inner requires the outer, and the meaning requires the parable. If there is no outer, then there is no inner, no summons, and no Shari'ah. Then creation is left neglected and the people are ignorant, for knowledge goes together with work, and this is the origin of wisdom among the wise, as they have said:

"الحكمة هي عمل مع علم"

"Wisdom is action with knowledge."

It is impossible to say that the people of that cycle were upon such a path.

Rather, we say:

The first Speaker deserved the name of speaking-prophecy by his composing of a Shari'ah, and were it not for that, he would not have been counted among the six speaker-prophets, even if it were incumbent upon him to establish a foundation that calls to the Tawhid of Allah Almighty. If he had no Shari'ah and no summons, then there were no statutes by which those who belonged to him in his cycle could stand, that they might raise up those who came after upon the drawing of the rest of the speaker-prophets. This is a diseased belief. It is not permissible to say that there was no revelation in that cycle and no Shari'ah, because command and prohibition come only by an inspiration from Allah, and the manifesting of the command of Allah is revelation. When command, prohibition, and revelation are established, the Shari'ah is established as the manifestations of the statutes, and this indicates that the first Speaker had a Shari'ah, over and above what the necessary proof indicates, according to what the wise have said. Allah Almighty said:

"واتل عليهم نبأ ابن آدم بالحق اذ قرّبا قرباناً فتقبل من احدهما ولم يتقبل من الآخر"

"And recite to them the tidings of the son of Adam in truth, when the two offered an offering, and it was accepted from one of them and not accepted from the other" (the verse).

The offering is one of the laws, and it is the most firmly established of the causes of the Shari'ah. Allah has commanded it in every Shari'ah, and He has made it the root of devotion and purity. For this reason the offering is named a rite. Allah Almighty said:

"ولكل امة جعلنا منسكاً ليذكروا اسم الله على ما رزقهم من بهيمة الأنعام"

"And for every nation We have appointed a rite, that they may mention the name of Allah over what He has provided them of the beasts of cattle."

And He said:

"ولكل امة جعلنا منسكا هم ناسكوه فلا ينازعنك"

"And for every nation We have appointed a rite which they perform, so let them not dispute with you" (the verse).

The rite is the place in which the offering is brought near. And in the hadith:

"العرجاء تجر رجلها الى المنسك"

"The lame ewe drags her leg to the rite."

The rite itself is the offering. A man is called a rite-keeper when he is known for religion, scrupulousness, and purity. The offering is called devotion because it is the utmost of the statutes of religion, and the utmost of drawing near to Allah Almighty in every Shari'ah and among all nations, and this is the most firmly established of the causes of the Shari'ah. It is called an offering because by it they draw near to Allah Almighty, and by their nearness to Allah Almighty there arise the first causes of obedience and disobedience, of disbelief and faith, of command and prohibition, and it is the first of the statutes of the Shari'ah.

The author of *al-Nusrah* said:

The author of *al-Islah* argued for establishing the Shari'ah for Adam (ع) by the saying of Allah Almighty, "واتل عليهم نبأ ابن آدم بالحق اذ قرّبا قرباناً," and he claimed that the offering is only one of the statutes of the laws, after he had drawn out at length the distinction between determination and Shari'ah and multiplied the witnesses to it from the poetry of the Arabs, with no benefit lying beneath them and no proof that verifies what he wished to establish. The matter in the interpretation of this verse is not as he supposed. For if the offering in this place were that which occurs only in the apparent laws, the slaughter of sheep and camels, then it would be as he mentioned. But the affair of the offering in this place is that which does not occur in the apparent laws, namely the establishing of the proofs from beneath their hands, and of those who stand in their place when they depart from the world. The interpretation of this story is apparent to the wise. Had he read this story to its end, it would have been plain to him that Adam (ع) had no Shari'ah, since one of his two sons did not bury the corpse of his brother, and did not know how to do it well, until Allah sent a raven to scratch in the earth to show him how to bury the corpse of his brother. For the burying and interring of the dead, in whatever manner it is, is one of the laws. Had Adam a Shari'ah, its place, and the practice of it, would not have been hidden from him. This is his statement.

And we say:

The Shari'ah of Adam (ع) is established both by the fact that what the author of *al-Islah* argued establishes it, and by the fact that what the author of *al-Nusrah* brought forward does not overturn it. For the author of *al-Islah* argued for establishing the Shari'ah, which is one of the statutes the speaker-prophets set for the worship of Allah Almighty and the road to the knowledge of the causes, from the apparent sense of the Book: by the verse in which the offering is mentioned, and by the universally affirmative verse in the saying of Allah Almighty, "ولكل امة جعلنا منسكاً ليذكروا اسم الله" (the verse), and by the other verse, "ولكل امة جعلنا منسكاً هم ناسكوه." Thus it is established, and the words of the author of *al-Nusrah*, "the matter in the interpretation of this verse is not as he supposed," do not contradict the words of the author of *al-Islah*, since the author of *al-Islah* did not argue for what he established by the interpretation of this verse, but by its apparent sense. And when the words of the author of *al-Islah* establish the Shari'ah of Adam (ع), and the words of the author of *al-Nusrah* in reply to him do not overturn it, then the Shari'ah, in the state of its being proven, is proven.

The Second Section of the Ninth Discourse

And the words of the author of *al-Islah*:

That if the offering were that which occurs only in the apparent laws, the slaughter of sheep and camels, then it would be as he mentioned, but the affair of the offering in this place is that which does not occur in the laws, according to his interpretation, this too does not contradict the words of the author of *al-Islah*. Rather it necessitates what he made necessary, since his saying, "only what is in the apparent laws," is itself an affirmation of the offering, for it is one of the statutes of religion and of the apparent Shari'ah, even though he pushed away the claim that what is meant by it is that which occurs in the apparent. And his saying, that the offering in this place does not occur in the apparent sense of the Shari'ah, scarcely overturns the words of the author of *al-Islah*, since what the author of *al-Nusrah* said runs the course of a summons that is established only by proof. And it is permissible that its apparent sense stand present and existing, just as its inner sense is present and existing, all the more so since the verse is not among those that run the course of the parable whose interpretation is sought while its apparent sense is abandoned. When that is so, and the apparent sense of the verse establishes the work as fixed, then the statutes of the Shari'ah of Adam (ع) are fixed.

The Third Section of the Ninth Discourse

Then the words of the author of *al-Islah*:

That the offering is the establishing of the proofs from beneath their hands, and of those who stand in their place, requires that Adam have something to which those call. For the proofs, and those who are set up from among the ranks, are only set up to call to what the Speaker proclaims and draws down, in which lies the welfare of those summoned, in this world and in religion, in the Hereafter and the first life. And Adam (ع) cannot be other than a father, since he laid down the Shari'ah and enacted the statutes which are the firmly binding matters. Had he laid down nothing, and manifested no statute, small or great, of the laws of worship, then he would have no need to establish the proofs and to send forth the callers who stand in his place, since he would have left behind nothing that needs to be preserved and by which the community is governed. So by the affirmation of the author of *al-Nusrah*, through his interpretation, that he did establish the proofs, the Shari'ah and the statutes are made necessary. Then the Shari'ah belongs to Adam (ع).

The Fourth Section of the Ninth Discourse

And after this we say:

Since the need for the prophets (عليهم السلام) was for the teaching of the community what makes it happy in the Hereafter and in this world, and by which it is guided to its interests in this world and in religion, and since Adam (ع) was a prophet, it necessarily follows that he was a teacher and a guide. And if he must be a teacher and a guide, then that which he teaches his community, by which it is made happy and guided to its interests, must exist. And if that must exist, then it can be nothing other than the laws of worship, which are the Shari'ah. So the Shari'ah of Adam (ع) is fixed. This is verified by the saying of Allah Almighty:

"كان الناس امة واحدة"

"Mankind was one nation," meaning in ignorance,

then Allah sent the prophets as bearers of glad tidings and as warners,

"وانزل معهم الكتاب بالحق ليحكم بين الناس فيما اختلفوا فيه"

"and He sent down with them the Book in truth, that it might judge between the people in that wherein they differed" (the verse).

Adam (ع) is among the prophets, and if he was a prophet, Allah did not send him except that he might bear glad tidings, warn, judge, and decree. And glad tidings, warning, judgment, and decree occur only by the statutes and the enactments. So the statutes, which are the Shari'ah, are then fixed.

The Fifth Section of the Ninth Discourse

And the author of *al-Nusrah* said:

Had he read this story to its end, it would have been plain to him that Adam had no Shari'ah, since one of his two sons did not bury the corpse of his brother, and did not know how to do it well, until Allah sent a raven to scratch in the earth to show him how to bury the corpse of his brother. For the burying and interring of the dead, in whatever manner it is, is one of the laws. Had Adam (ع) a Shari'ah, the taking of it would not have been hidden from his two sons. So this is a statement issued without inquiry and without examination.

And we say:

Had the author of *al-Islah* leaned upon sophistry, as the author of *al-Nusrah* leaned upon it against him in the matter of the verse he cited concerning the meaning of the offering, when he said, "the matter in the interpretation of the verse is not as he supposed," then the author of *al-Nusrah*, in his support, would not escape one of two courses. Either he assigns to the verse he cited, in pushing away the Shari'ah of Adam (ع), an interpretation in which he stands alone and by which he argues, and then his argument in pushing away the Shari'ah of Adam (ع) that he wishes to push away is voided, since his citing of it was first by the apparent sense of the verse. Or he assigns to it no interpretation at all, and lets it run upon what its apparent sense conveys of its meaning, and then it does not indicate anything of what he intended. For the verse does not indicate that the son of Adam, when the raven scratched in the earth, saw it and buried his brother and interred him, so that the argument by it should be established for him. The verse is:

"فبعث الله غراباً ليبحث في الارض ليريه كيف يواري سوأة اخيه قال يا ويلتا أعجزت ان اكون مثل الغراب فأواري سوأة أخي فأصبح من النادمين"

"So Allah sent a raven to scratch in the earth, to show him how to bury the corpse of his brother. He said: Woe to me, am I unable to be like this raven, that I might bury the corpse of my brother? And he became one of the regretful."

It does not mention that he buried it, nor does it indicate that he interred him.

The Sixth Section of the Ninth Discourse

Moreover, the interpretation of the verse and its inner sense do not indicate any of that either. For each of the opponent and the friend believed, in the worship of Allah Almighty, in something that was his offering, by which he draws near to Allah Almighty. The opponent believed that worship is nothing but the apparent works alone, and that deliverance lies in nothing but that, and that the one who stands in the place of the Messenger (ع) will be established by this belief and by sincerity in it. The friend believed that the worship of Allah Almighty is nothing but the apparent, which is the work, together with the inner, which is knowledge, and that deliverance lies in nothing but the two of them together, and that the one who stands in the place of the Messenger (ع) is he whom the Messenger (ع) put forward and approved. So they contended over that one day, and disputed over it before the Prophet, and each of them made plain his belief concerning that by which he worshiped Allah. This is His saying, "اذ قربا قرباناً," that is, each of them believed in something by which he draws near to Allah Almighty and to His Messenger, "and it was accepted from one of them," and he is the legatee, meaning that the Speaker who stands in the place of Allah approved his belief concerning Allah and concerning His worship, "and it was not accepted from the other," and he is the opponent, meaning that the Speaker did not approve his belief, its being upon other than its right face. And the Speaker (ع) had approved of the friend's belief concerning himself what he approved, without declaring it openly, out of tenderness lest the opponent ascribe to him inclination toward the friend beneath him. So he said:

"ان أكرمكم عند الله اتقاكم"

"Indeed the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most God-fearing of you."

And the opponent had heard him (ع) say, "اتقاكم ابو بكر," "the most God-fearing of you is Abu Bakr." So together with his hearing that from the Messenger, he reckoned that the verse he recited was recited only because of his approval of that man's belief and way, and that he is the one who stands after him. So he rose, and in the heart of the opponent was that the friend had prevailed. The opponent said to him:

"لأقتلنك"

"I will surely kill you,"

that is, I will surely push you away from this belief that he did not approve from you, and away from the matter that you reckon is yours. The friend said to him:

"انما يتقبل الله من المتقين"

"Allah accepts only from the God-fearing,"

meaning that the one who stands in the place of Allah, who is the Messenger, does not set up anyone in his place, and does not approve a belief from anyone save from the God-fearing, those who join knowledge together with work, and who draw near to Allah Almighty by raising up the landmarks of both together. So the opponent turned to reviling the friend and disparaging him, and to claiming that he knew nothing. And when the two of them brought their belief before the Prophet (ع), he approved the one belief over the other. The friend said:

"لئن بسطت اليّ يدك لتقتلني ما انا بباسط يدي اليك لأقتلك اني اخاف الله رب العالمين"

"If you stretch out your hand against me to kill me, I will not stretch out my hand against you to kill you. Indeed I fear Allah, the Lord of the worlds,"

that is, if you make war upon me and loose your tongue against me, and lower my standing, and speak in what is not due, and are not content with what I believe concerning the worship of Allah, the Lord of the worlds, then I will not do the like of that to you, nor will I revile you. For I am under the command of the Speaker who stands in the place of Allah, who is the master of the cycle and the lord of the imams, and under his obedience. I fear him, and I do not put myself forward.

Adam, Noah, and the Origin of the Law

This word of the guardian worked upon the opponent, so that he slackened from what he had been set upon, and his own soul made the killing of his brother easy to him. His soul rose against the second opponent, and he ran on with the first opponent, the one with whom alone he would resolve nothing, bind nothing, and take no counsel. That is, when the first opponent brought back to mind what had befallen the second, he said to him, "There is no belief in Allah and in His worship except what I have said," meaning that the guardian is astray, and he made the affair seem good to him in the lowering of the guardian, and barred him from his rank, and charged him with knowing nothing. So he thrust him from his station, overcame him, and said, "Worship is nothing but this outward observance, nor is it what this man says to you." And so he became one of the losers, in that he worshipped Allah by the obedience of the Speaker without the obedience of the Foundation, and by the outward without the inward.

Then Allah sent a raven scratching in the earth:

> فبعث الله غراباً يبحث في الارض

That is, when the term had lengthened and the passing of the opponent drew near, the guardian wished to complete the proof against him, so that his departure from the world might come after a reminder. So he sent to him, which is His saying, "And Allah sent," that is, the one who stands in the place of the Speaker, who himself stands in the place of Allah, sent a man from among his companions to make plain to him his affair and his station, and what Allah Almighty holds in the secret of His silence over the seeking of his right, and to make for him a way out from what he had done, and to move him to return the right to its people. This is the meaning of the raven's scratching in the earth, to show him how to free himself from what he had brought upon his own soul, and to guide him to his duty. The man whom the guardian of Allah sent for this was Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, for he is among those bound in a special way to the guardian from the side of religion, and to the opponent from the side of lineage. So Muhammad did what he was sent for, and the opponent was roused to his crime, his soul softened, and he inclined toward binding himself to the guardian's mandate. But the second opponent prevailed over his judgment and barred him from what he had resolved upon, namely to acknowledge the guardian's right. So he grieved, and said within himself when he was prevented:

> يا ويلتا أعجزت أن أكون مثل هذا الغراب

"O woe to me! Was I unable to be like this raven?" That is, "Would that I were in the standing of this man," meaning Muhammad his son, who is a stranger to the guardian and yet his like in guarding the covenant of Allah, His religion, and the concealment of the secrets of the guardian of Allah. So he became one who regretted what he had missed in the following of the guardian at the first, and regretted his deed, which would remove him at the last to the abode of the Hereafter. We seek refuge in Allah from the breaking of the covenant while arguing by it.

If this is so, then the author of al-Nusrah has nothing in his argument by way of this verse, except the saying of the author of al-Islah concerning what he held obligatory, of the ordinances of the Shari'ah, for Adam (ع).

**The Seventh Section of the Ninth Chapter**

It has been mentioned that the laws, the dispensations, the ordinances, and the observances may increase and decrease according to the time in which the Speaker arises, and according to what is needed for the setting-right of the community. Narrations have come down that the washing of the dead, the embalming, the shrouding, and the burial are from the law of Adam (ع). If the reports of this have come in unbroken succession, as has been mentioned, then this verse, in what it relates of the raven and the scratching in the earth at the killing of the son of Adam, runs only in the course of the parables whose interpretation is to be sought, and whose outward sense is not to be taken according to what we have done and interpreted. So the Shari'ah of Adam (ع) is established beyond dispute.

**The Eighth Section of the Ninth Chapter**

Then we say: the argument by this verse for the rejection of the ordinances of the Shari'ah of Adam (ع) is itself made void. For the burial of the dead is not among the matters whose ordinances would be lifted by its lifting, which are purity, prayer, zakat, and the rest of the acts, such that by the lifting of its ordinance there would come the lifting of all the other ordinances of the laws that are the foundations of worship. Rather it is one kind among the kinds of what is done with the dead: burning with fire, or casting into water, or carrying to a cave or a wilderness, or burying in the earth, or feeding to the beasts. And when it is one kind among the kinds of an ordinance of the Shari'ah, it cannot be that by the lifting of the ordinance of burial, for which some other kind may be put in its place, the ordinances of the whole law are made void. And when that is impossible, the Shari'ah is established for Adam (ع).

**The Ninth Section of the Ninth Chapter**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Adam (ع) was not the possessor of a law, for had he been the possessor of a law, the command of Allah Almighty to the angels to prostrate to him would have been by his mediation.

We say:

By this saying the author of al-Nusrah has himself made it binding that Adam (ع) is the possessor of a law. For the command of Allah Almighty to the angels to prostrate, though outwardly it is not from the side of Adam (ع), was only by his mediation. The proof of this is that the command to prostrate to him would be absurd were he not one chosen; and the command to prostrate to him is not absurd, and he is one chosen. And it has been established that he is chosen, by the saying of Allah Almighty:

> ان الله اصطفى آدم ونوحا

"Indeed Allah chose Adam and Noah," the verse. And when he is one chosen, it is absurd that Allah should command one who is below the chosen except by the mediation of the chosen. So the command of Allah to the angels to prostrate to Adam was only by his mediation; and when it was by his mediation, the law is established for him by his own saying.

**The Tenth Section of the Ninth Chapter**

Further, the command of Allah to prostrate to Adam (ع), in His saying,

> اسجدوا لآدم

"Prostrate to Adam," is like His command to the Children of Israel in His saying:

> يا بني اسرائيل اذكروا نعمتي التي انعمت عليكم وأوفوا بعهدي اوف بعهدكم وإياي فارهبون وآمنوا بما انزلت مصدقاً لما معكم ولا تكونوا اول كافر ولا تشتروا بآياتي ثمناً قليلاً وإياي فاتقون ولا تلبسوا الحق بالباطل وتكتموا الحق وانتم تعلمون وأقيموا الصلاة وآتوا الزكاة واركعوا مع الراكعين

"O Children of Israel, remember My favor which I bestowed upon you, and fulfill My covenant, and I shall fulfill your covenant, and Me alone hold in awe. And believe in what I have sent down, confirming what is with you, and be not the first to disbelieve in it, and sell not My signs for a small price, and Me alone be mindful of. And confound not the truth with falsehood, nor conceal the truth while you know. And establish the prayer, and give the zakat, and bow with those who bow."

This command, though outwardly in a form that does not point to his being a mediator, reached them by the mediation of Muhammad. And were it allowable to say that Muhammad has no law on the ground that the verse is bare of any mention of Allah's command to the Children of Israel that would show the command to be by his mediation, and if the two verses were set together in the class of not pointing to a single, well-rooted mediator, this would be absurd. For the command of Allah Almighty to the Children of Israel, though outwardly bare of the mention of a mediator, was only by the mediation of Muhammad. And when that is absurd, then Adam is the possessor of the law, and the law is established for him.

**The Eleventh Section of the Ninth Chapter**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

The angels are not charged with obligation, nor is the practice of the law incumbent upon them, for they disobey Allah not for the blink of an eye; rather they glorify the praise of their Lord, and do what they are commanded.

We say:

The angels who were charged with prostration, had they not been of those upon whom obligation is laid, Allah Almighty would not have charged them with prostrating to Adam, nor commanded them by His saying, "Prostrate to Adam." As for the angels who bear no obligation, they are those who were never in the abode of body, nor ever standing in potency, but came forth into act; these are the primal ones in the abode of eternity. What is meant here, rather, are the charged angels, the assembly who were in the abode of nature and possessed the excellences by which they resemble those in the abode of eternity, the angels of origination and of emanation, as we have explained before, since none advances save one who is in the abode of origination and emanation. As for prostration, and the like of prostration, from among the ordinances of worship and works, these are found only in the world of nature. Those who are charged are charged only with the establishing of the two worships, that by them they may attain of increase and of the blessings of felicity what raises their standing before Allah, and attain the completeness that gains them enduring and abiding. For this reason there was the charging of the angels and the calling of them to the work.

**The Twelfth Section of the Ninth Chapter**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Had the prostration of the angels to Adam been a law for them, Adam would have said, "Allah commands you to prostrate and to obey me," and he would have striven against whoever opposed him in the command of Allah, as was the way of all the Speakers.

We say:

It is open to the author of al-Islah to say: it does not follow, from Adam's not having said, "Allah commands you to prostrate to me and to obey me," that prostration must therefore not be among the ordinances of the law. For the Prophet (ع) did not say to the Children of Israel, "Allah commands you to establish the prayer and to give the zakat to me," and this was no proof that the prayer, the zakat, and all that he commanded them are not among the ordinances of the law. Moreover, were the laws established by their bringer being one who strives, then Jesus (ع) would have no law appointed for him, since he did not strive; and this is not sound.

**The Thirteenth Section of the Ninth Chapter**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Further, Allah Almighty did not mention in the Qur'an that Adam (ع) had a people and followers, nor did He mention his being sent as He mentioned others, when He said,

> انا ارسلنا نوحاً

"Indeed We sent Noah"; and He did not mention him in the sum of the law, in His saying,

> شرع لكم من الدين ما اوصى به نوحاً والذي اوحينا اليك وما وصينا به إبراهيم وموسى وعيسى ان اقيموا الدين ولا تتفرقوا

"He has laid down for you of the religion that which He enjoined upon Noah, and that which We have revealed to you, and that which We enjoined upon Abraham, Moses, and Jesus: that you establish the religion and be not divided therein," the verse.

We say: the author of al-Nusrah has not been fair in his argument, nor has he pondered the saying of the author of al-Islah in his book. As for Adam, he is mentioned in the Qur'an, and his followers and his people are the angels commanded to prostrate to him, who learned from him the names according to what Allah Almighty commanded in His saying,

> يا آدم أنبئهم بأسمائهم

"O Adam, inform them of their names." As for the mention of his being sent, there is no sending greater than his sending. For the sending of messengers was made obligatory only that they might be the vicegerents of Allah in His earth, since Allah Almighty is withheld from apprehension; and Allah Almighty has stated in His Book that He made him a vicegerent in the earth, to whom its people return in what they need of the affair of their religions. As for his not being mentioned among those named as bringers of the law in the verse, the author of al-Islah has said: because he was not among the resolute, he was not mentioned with them, for he was not one of firm resolve; and were it allowable that Adam be excluded from the company of the possessors of the laws because his name is not mentioned with the five (عليهم السلام), it would likewise be allowable, because he is not mentioned among those to whom revelation was sent, in the saying of Allah Almighty,

> انا أوحينا اليك كما اوحينا إلى نوح والنبين من بعده واوحينا الى ابراهيم واسماعيل واسحاق ويعقوب والأسباط وعيسى وأيوب ويونس وهرون وسليمان

"Indeed We have revealed to you as We revealed to Noah and the prophets after him; and We revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, the tribes, Jesus, Job, Jonah, Aaron, and Solomon," the verse, that he should fall outside their company. And that is absurd. So the saying is the saying of the author of al-Islah, and not another's.

**The Fourteenth Section of the Ninth Chapter**

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Further, it behooves us to know the cause of the being of a law, so that it may be sound for us to say that Adam (ع) had no law.

We say:

The cause of the obligation of a law is, in his view, the neglect of the limits and their suspension, the following of the opposite by the creatures, and their claiming for it what Allah did not place in it. And this sense was not present, nor did it appear, in the days of Adam, for the world that was before him, and his people, were keeping to the limits, and not one of them opposed a single limit, as they told of themselves when they said,

> اتجعل فيها من يفسد فيها ويسفك الدماء ونحن نسبح بحمدك ونقدس لك

"Will You place therein one who works corruption in it and sheds blood, while we glorify You with praise and sanctify You?" the verse. So there was no obligation upon Adam to establish a law. This is his saying.

We say: the cause of the obligation of the being of a law is other than what the author of al-Nusrah went to in his saying, "the neglect of the limits, their suspension, and the following of the opposite by the creatures." For that does not make a law obligatory, nor does it establish it. Yet we take his part and say: as for the cause of the obligation of the being of the law, which is the ordinances of the works, we have explained it in our book known as al-Masabih fi al-Imamah. The nearest of it is the need of people, first of all in their ranks, in their subsisting, for that by which they guard themselves against destruction, of the ordinances by which blood is preserved, and by which the honors, the persons, and the properties are safeguarded, and by which redemption is made in the necessary matters that arise among them. Were these ordinances not present, their affairs would come to a standstill, and some of them would destroy others; the strong would set upon the one below him in strength, out of anger at what he owns of property and the like, and the fires of discord would be kindled among them. Then, since it is impossible, in the wisdom of the Maker Almighty, that He should deny His creatures what guards them against destruction, the ordinances that are the law are of necessity present.

We say: the reason for the necessity of the law's existence is not what the author of al-Nusrah supposed when he said that it lay in the neglect of the limits, in their disruption, and in creation's following of opposites. That is not what obligates a law, nor what establishes one. Rather, we answer in our own words. As for the true reason for the necessity of the law's existence, which is the sum of the ordinances of deeds, we have set it out in our book known as al-Masabih fi al-Imamah, the Lamps in the Imamate. The nearest of these grounds is the people's need, first of all and according to their several ranks, in their very survival, for what guards them against destruction: the ordinances by which blood is preserved, and by which their households, their offspring, and their wealth are fortified, and by which they are ransomed in the necessary matters that arise among them. Were these ordinances not present, their affairs would halt and they would destroy one another; and because it is impossible, in the wisdom of the Maker Almighty, that He should deny His creatures what keeps them safe from ruin, the ordinances that are the law are of necessity present.

Then we say: their existence, that is, the drawing up of the laws, has become a necessary existence, because the souls that belong to human beings do not restrain themselves one from another in the acts particular to each. From those acts there arises what makes them unable to do without ordinances that steady the community, so that by holding fast to their bonds each is made secure from the other. And once the ordinances are of necessary existence, we say further: some of them are necessary in every age and season, and some are not; what is not necessary may be present at one time and absent at another, and may increase and decrease.

As for what is of necessary existence, it is such matters as drawing near to Allah, and the ordinance of restraint and retribution in the case of souls and persons; the ordinance of marriage; the division of wealth, such as inheritance and the like; the ordinance for concealing the dead; and the ordinance for the recovery of rights and their return to their owners. For human beings have three souls. The first is the rational soul, which possesses discrimination and thought. It knows that it has a Creator, submits to Him by nature, and through that submission draws near to Him; and in its drawing near it is driven to ordinances by which it fulfills what it seeks, whether those ordinances are many or few. The second is the irascible soul that a man possesses, by which he is angered when anger comes upon him, and which loves mastery, domination, revenge, oppression, killing, and injustice. This soul makes ordinances necessary, ordinances by which the violence of one is turned back from another, and by which the right of the wronged is taken from the wrongdoer who has transgressed: in the case of souls by force, in the case of the body by retribution, and otherwise by what stands in the place of blood-money. The third is the appetitive soul, which seeks pleasures and seeks marriage of necessity, so that it too makes ordinances necessary, ordinances by which holding fast removes the calamity of jealousy among them that would otherwise drive them to killing.

This appetitive soul seeks that all wealth should belong to it by whatever way it can, of necessity, so that men are driven to ordinances by which they are made secure one from another against cheating, cunning, and deceit that would lead them to contention and combat. And these souls, in their eagerness, move in the pursuit of livelihood, so that dealings between men become a necessity; and since treachery and cunning are set in the natures, they are driven to ordinances by which the claimant and the denier are brought to their course in extracting the truth for its rightful owner. Death, too, is necessary among them, and they cannot do without some means of removing the body of the dead from among them, whether by burial, by burning, by casting it into the valleys, or by some other way; otherwise harm would return upon them, and so a man is driven to adopt one of these acts. These are six gates that run in the manner of genera, and beneath them fall the several kinds whose existence is necessary among human beings without fail.

As for what is not necessary, and is at one time greater and at another less, these are the ordinances in the worship of Allah and in drawing near to Him through the knowledge of His limits, by which completeness is attained; and this falls under the genus of drawing near to Allah. All of these ordinances belong to the law, and there must first be one who lays them down and inscribes them among human beings at the beginning of their existence. And since the existence of these ordinances comes with the existence of human beings, and since Adam (ع) was of the human beings, he and those with him, then the law is established for him.

As for whoever was before Adam, his case is the case of one who would come at the end of the seventh cycle, like for like. He was not free of the ordinances whose existence is necessary, which we have named; he was free only of the ordinances that fall under the genus of worship in drawing near to Allah, those which, we have said, at times increase and at times diminish, and by which the second completeness is acquired. So Allah sent Adam (ع) to teach them and others what they had not known, and to bring them to completeness when they were deficient. For though they were distinguished above others by excellence, they fell short of the degree of completeness by their leaving off the work of worship. So he laid down the law, and believed, and called to the two acts of worship in knowledge and in deed. Some of them followed and obeyed, and some of them refused and turned away, along the six that came before at the beginning of the cycles. Such was the case of those people.

The author of al-Nusrah said: It became obligatory upon Noah (ع) to establish it, that is, the law, because before his cycle there had passed cycles that violated the limits and followed the opposites, such as Wadd (الود), Suwa' (السواع), Yaghuth (يغوث), and others. So it was obligatory upon Noah (ع) to establish it, that whoever perished might perish upon clear evidence, and whoever lived might live upon clear evidence.

We say: this saying of his does not entail that Adam had no law; rather it requires that he too had ordinances and a law. For he has granted that a cycle turned upon the violation of the limits and the following of the opposites, and violation falls only to the follower with respect to the one followed, by his abandoning that one's command and ordinance. So, by the establishing of violation in his own words, the ordinances that are the law, by which the violation occurred, are established for Adam (ع). Then the law is established for him.

The author of al-Islah said: It has been narrated that Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, commanded Adam (ع) to pitch a tent which He sent down to him from the Garden, and to make it his qiblah and the qiblah of his children; then Seth built after him the place of the law, and made it firm, and gathered within it the revelation and the interpretation, and set up its branches and its roots, and the sound ordering of its obligations.

We say: the saying of the author of al-Islah is established, since Adam has a law and ordinances, as do the other Speakers.

The author of al-Nusrah said: In the composition of the world and its ordering, as a proof of the divine oneness, there is that which makes one independent of the setting down of works and appointed rites; and the setting down of works and signs has turned many of the people away from the divine oneness and cast them into likening Him to creatures, which is a thing utterly impossible. For the composition of the world, the ordering and wonders of His wisdom, what lies in the composition of the world of bodies, the joining of frames, and the pairing of opposite natures in distinct bodies and measured persons, as a proof of the divine oneness, makes one independent of the setting down of works, of like actions and differing arrangements that point to agreed meanings, of parables set up for a subsisting original, and of proofs set up for a plain object of proof. What is in these proofs and in these deeds that are contradictory in their outward way, and what is in these parables struck to establish known limits, is itself a proof of the One, since the firm, precise, wondrous acts of the world and of the souls make one independent of works and signs. Indeed, many of these signs, works, and outward expressions have turned most of the people away from the divine oneness and cast them into a likening unfitting for the least of His creatures. The sum of his saying is that in the composition of the world and its ordering, as a proof of the divine oneness, there is that which makes one independent of the setting down of works and rites, and that the setting down of works and signs has turned many of creation away from the divine oneness and cast them into likening Him to creatures.

We say: this saying of his makes the sending of the messengers an excess of which there is no need. For if the world, being firm and precise, points to the divine oneness, and if the messengers (عليهم السلام) were sent for the call and for the pointing to the divine oneness, then to send them while a proof already stands, and while another already accomplishes that for which they were sent, is an excess of which there is no need. I do not know how this escaped him, when it is plain that the world's being firm and precise is not sufficient as a proof of the divine oneness if there is none present who knows it and makes it plain. One profits from the knowledge of the world's composition, its ordering, and the arrangement of its existents only through one whose rank in knowledge has risen by the limits teaching him the manner of the works and the signs which the revelation and the law gather together, so that he reflects, deduces, and infers from it what he seeks of the knowledge of the limits of Allah and of His signs whose knowledge is made obligatory upon human beings. Allah says:

[ حتى اذا جاءوا قال اكذبتم بآياتي ولم تحيطوا بها علماً ]

Until, when they come, He will say: Did you deny My signs when you did not encompass them in knowledge? And if you do know, then the foremost in existence are the noble substances that are the angels brought near. So too is the matter when there is need of works, signs, arousing, and teaching, since not everyone who has known, worked, and been aroused is able to draw from the existents a true inference. How much less, then, one who does not know at all? In sum, the saying that leads to the nullifying of the ranks of the limits is a saying that is not of our belief, nor of the doctrine of our masters (عليهم السلام). Perhaps this saying was only planted in the book by the adversaries, so that it might be ascribed to the author of the book. And Allah knows best.

Furthermore, the works are not set down for the divine oneness alone, but for the straightening of the soul in worship, for the likening of it to the highest assembly, and for its acquiring, through discipline, the noble traits and the generous habits by which it is made ready for union with what is above it, and for its being brought out from the baseness of potentiality, so that the honor of the intellect is saved by its applying itself to the obligations and keeping to their marks. Were it not for the works and rites within the community, and the completeness of souls that comes through perseverance in them and taking them up, the souls would perish. For this reason Allah did not concede to the angels when they reckoned that the worship they were upon was sufficient in the matter of nearness to Him and of their completeness, and that they had no need, in what they were upon, of a vicegerent to teach them anything or to command them to work. He said:

[ اني أعلم ما لا تعلمون ]

I know what you do not know. And the Almighty said this only because the states of being, by His decree and His wisdom, are set to waver between potency and act: at one time subsisting in potency, at another subsisting in act. A thing therefore, when it reaches the utmost of what it may reach of potency and act, returns backward; if it was in potency, toward act, and if it was in act, toward potency. It is like the hot thing which, when it has reached the end of its heat, the heat is spent and coldness appears, as in what melts among bodies and the like; and like the cold thing which, when it has reached the end of its coldness, the coldness is spent and heat appears, as with snow; and like the compacted mass whose parts are gathered from the mountains, which, when it has reached the utmost of that for which it was compacted, which is dryness, dissolution appears in its parts, so that it scatters and returns to sand; and other such things among the existents in the abode of nature.

So too were knowledge and ignorance in the world of coming-to-be and passing-away: at one time ignorance subsisting in act, and knowledge in potency, in the sense that ignorance was general through the abundance of its people, and knowledge lost through the fewness of its people. The time was the time of ignorance's dominion and its reaching its utmost in act, and of knowledge's being hidden and reaching the utmost of its end in potency, through the effacing of the traces of the teachers and the disruption of the marks of the laws and works with the drawing out of the age and the passing of the times. By the laws here I mean what is not of necessary existence among the ordinances and rites. And those distinguished by the name of angels were in themselves the best, and their most excellent the bearers of knowledge in that age, and those who worshiped Allah upon an edge, and it was narrated that what they were upon was sufficient in the matter of worship and of completeness.

Yet the matter was other than what they reckoned, for they were in need, for completeness, of the knowledge of the limits foremost in existence, those in the abode of origination and the first arising, and of the knowledge of those later in existence, like the second arising, and of the work by which they might gain completeness. Since they did not know that their time was the time of the completion of the cycle of ignorance, and that they were not in the cycle of knowledge nor in the cycle of works, the Almighty knew that to leave them upon what they were upon, while ignorance had reached its utmost and the time was the time of the beginning of knowledge's appearing, is a thing at odds with wisdom. So the Mighty and Majestic said, in effect: I know of the arrival of ignorance in the world at its utmost in act, and the completion of its cycle and its manifestation; and of the arrival of knowledge in the world at its utmost in potency, and of the return of turning back to it in manifestation, and of the rising of the need, in its appearing in act, for the setting up of teachers and guides, what you do not know.

So Allah sent Adam (ع), making him His vicegerent, that he might bring forth knowledge, gain them completeness in act, guide them to the knowledge of the limits, and teach them what they had not known. This was the beginning of the matter, the coming forth of knowledge from potency into act, by the presence of its people from his days (ع); and its end extends to the Qa'im (ع). Likewise it is said that when the Qa'im (ع) appears, knowledge becomes in act, meaning that it becomes general and its people multiply, and ignorance is hidden, meaning that its people grow few and its root is cut off. And what is said in the narration, that the treasures of the earth appear in his days and it casts them up to its surface, is a sign of the Qa'im pointing to the standing of knowledge in act, so that all that was laid up in the bellies of the revealed books and the knotted symbols appears.

And since the angels, with the presence of the heavens, the beauty of their ordering, and the precision of the composition of the world's existents, still did not know, and had no completeness, and drew no inference from them, except by what Adam (ع) made general among them and called them to of work, then what the author of al-Nusrah mentioned is not thoroughly examined, and the works, the signs, and the parables are not to be dispensed with in the matter of inference.

If that is so, then Adam (ع) is the author of a Sharia and of customs to which he called, and by which he pointed to the limits of Allah and to His unity. For what was indispensable to them concerning monotheism, politics, the correction of the soul, and its discipline lay in coming to know the limits and in bringing that knowledge out into act.

The twenty-first section of the ninth chapter.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Does the author of this article claim that the creation, in the great cycle, did not know, since there was no manifest, similar law among them? Or does he claim that the Qa'im (ع), in raising the represented laws, nullifies the knowledge of monotheism and drives people out into the assertion that all obligation is suspended? For if the creation was not ignorant of monotheism, and is likewise not ignorant of it in the cycle of the Qa'im even when the laws are raised, then it would have been possible for Adam (ع) to have called people to monotheism without any references or striking of parables, and the like of this he says.

We say:

Our earlier discussion of knowledge and ignorance, and of how each of them is at one time in potency and at another in act, already unfolds the explanation of the state in which the creation stood in the great cycle. If the creation was at the very end of ignorance, as the author of al-Nusrah's own saying requires, then Adam's call (ع) and his being a teacher to his followers in the landmarks of monotheism would be a mere superfluity that was not needed, since he has made it necessary that the people already knew monotheism. And if they already knew monotheism, then Adam's calling them to a thing they already knew would be nothing but a superfluity there was no need for, and that is impossible. For the people were in fact in need of that by which their own selves are perfected. It was for this that Allah sent Adam (ع) to them, made him His caliph among them, chose him out from among them, and made obedience to him and return to him in what they did not know obligatory upon them. As for the saying of the assembly named the angels,

> ((ونحن نسبح بحمدك ونقدس لك))

"And we glorify You with Your praise and sanctify You," this was their own estimation that they had reached the utmost in glorification, sanctification, and monotheism, and that what they were already in was sufficient in the matter of worship and in the knowledge of the limits. But Allah, exalted, knew of their affair that which His mercy made obligatory: the choosing of Adam from among them and the sending of him to them, so as to make him a caliph in their instruction, that he might call them to that by which their perfection comes, of work and knowledge together, which leads to the knowledge of the limits within which monotheism is sound. For this He said:

> ((اني اعلم ما لا تعلمون))

"I know what you do not know," because He, exalted, knew that they were deficient, that ignorance had reached its end in act, that work had come to the point of beginning to appear, and that they did not know this. So wisdom made it obligatory to establish the office of instruction, which is the cause of the spreading of knowledge and the increase of its people. Allah chose Adam, and he legislated and taught, as Allah, exalted, said:

> ((وعلم آدم الأسماء كلها ثم عرضهم على الملائكة فقال انبئوني بأسماء هؤلاء ان كنتم صادقين قالوا سبحانك لا علم لنا إلا ما علمتنا انك انت العليم الحكيم قال يا آدم انبئهم باسمائهم فلما انبأهم باسمائهم قال الم اقل لكم اني اعلم غيب السماوات والارض))

"And He taught Adam the names, all of them, then He set them before the angels and said: Inform Me of the names of these, if you are truthful. They said: Glory be to You, we have no knowledge except what You have taught us; You are the Knowing, the Wise. He said: O Adam, inform them of their names. And when he had informed them of their names, He said: Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth" — the verse. The people, then, were not knowing, and because they were so, Adam came to them as a teacher for them.

The twenty-second section of the ninth chapter.

We say:

As for his saying and his affirmation that the creation was not, at first, ignorant of monotheism in the cycle of the Qa'im (ع): had they not been ignorant, they could only have derived it from the side of the confirming aids, in which lies the perfecting of their essences and their forms, and Adam would not have been chosen from among them to be the caliph of Allah, exalted, in teaching them what holds their welfare, sooner and later. It is impossible to conceive of them both this conception and, at the same time, the granting of a rank that would belong to them. And as for his saying, "nor in the cycle of the Qa'im are they ignorant": if what he means is the aspect the da'wah takes, that the cycle of the Qa'im (ع), before his appearance and before his coming to be out of the world of nature, is preserved by his caliphs and his summoners after the completion of the cycle of Muhammad al-Mustafa (ع) and his household, up to his own time and the appearance of his signs in the world, then that is true, for at that time knowledge has already raised high the beacon of its appearance through the multitude of the learned. This is the time of which Allah, exalted, said:

> ((يوم يجعل الولدان شيباً))

"A day that will turn the children gray." But if what he means by his saying, "in the cycle of the Qa'im they are not ignorant," is that they are not ignorant after the Qa'im (ع), who is the master of the seventh cycle and of the decisive word, at which the people of the Garden are set apart from the people of the Fire, the door of instruction is closed, and repentance is no longer valid, then it is not sound, for they do not remain knowing, nor are they of the people of knowledge; for that age is the age when the waters overflow and sink inward, and the age when dryness and barrenness prevail and come forth. The words of the author of al-Nusrah in this are not those of one who has examined the matter thoroughly.

The twenty-third section of the ninth chapter.

In sum, the author of al-Nusrah brought forward these sayings and citations only in support of the author of al-Mahsul in his rejection of the Sharia of Adam. Yet Adam, in his summoning of them and in his being a prophet, a teacher, a guide, cannot but have done one of these: either he called them by holding out to them the enticement of the Garden and the nearness of the Lord of the Worlds, or he called them by warning them of the Fire and of being kept far from the mercy of the Best of Creators, or by both together. If he called them by enticement, then enticement, when the thing held out is not perceptible to the senses, can be conveyed only by the striking of parables and by reporting of it through what is known to the senses of the kinds of delights, blessings, and goodly places, such as dwellings, gardens, rivers, and trees, so that its image may form in them; and that is a form of speech that calls for interpretation. If he called them by warning, then likewise his speech can only be by the striking of parables of what is to be feared, drawn for them from the world of the senses so that they may picture it, and its interpretation runs in the same channel as the rest that has gone before. If he called them by both together, then his speech was not devoid of a manifest sense that is acted upon, and so calls for interpretation. If that is so, then all three divisions make necessary a fixed, manifest sense belonging to Adam, upon which an interpretation follows. Adam, then, is the possessor of a Sharia and of manifest customs.

The twenty-fourth section of the ninth chapter.

Then we say:

Adam (ع) cannot but have called the people of his time to the obedience of Allah, exalted, and to his own obedience, either with wisdom or without wisdom. He did not act without wisdom, for that is impossible, and to hold it is a going forth from religion. Since, then, he called them with wisdom, and wisdom requires both knowledge and work together, Adam is the possessor of a Sharia that gathers together knowledge and work, which two are the worship of Allah, and by them perfection is attained and the ascent to the stations of honor.

The twenty-fifth section of the ninth chapter.

As for the Qa'im (ع) and the saying that he raises the laws, we say: to hold this is unsound. The estimation of his coming to be out of the world of nature does not proceed by way of the second emanation, for he abolishes nothing of what pertains to the worship of Allah, exalted. Rather he is the sealing of cycles that have passed and the perfecting of a matter completed in its place. He removes the limits from their ranks by the occurrence of the cessation of instruction concerning them, and the completing of the matter in the new creation, of which Allah, exalted, said:

> ((بل هم ليس من خلق جديد))

after He has established a judgment concerning the souls from which the course of the foundations of the masters of the past cycles is derived, those whose cycles are emptied out. And Allah, exalted, said, making plain the master of the Resurrection:

> ((واذا السماء انفطرت واذا الكواكب انتشرت))

"And when the heaven is cleft asunder, and when the planets are scattered." And He said:

> ((إذا الشمس كورت واذا النجوم انكدرت واذا الجبال سيرت واذا العشار عطلت))

"When the sun is folded up, and when the stars fall, and when the mountains are set moving, and when the pregnant camels are abandoned." All these parables are struck upon the limits and the summonses in their passing away from their ranks, not upon the laws and the ordinances of the works, whose landmarks wear away with the passing of time through the absence of teachers, except what is of necessary existence, as we have mentioned before. That age, in which this act appears through the closing of the door of instruction and the ceasing of the summonses on the part of the master of the Greater Resurrection, is the beginning of the return of knowledge to potency, so that it sinks inward, and the beginning of the increase of ignorance and its rising into act. The appearance of the signs in this manner suffices the one who is sufficient and has come of age. May Allah make us, and the company of the believers, of the people of the divine goods, by following His friends, and gather us with Muhammad and Ali and the pure Imams (عليهم السلام), the lights of His purity, by His grace and His mercy.

The twenty-sixth section of the ninth chapter.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Everyone who says that monotheism can be grasped only through references and works has thereby ruled that the First Intellect, the Soul, the angels, the Jadd, the Fath, and the Khayal did not know monotheism, since there were no references or works among them or between them.

We say:

The one who says this means neither the First Intellect nor the high limits, but rather the intellects that are in the world of the body, which are the second emanation, since their attaining of perfection was through instruction, obligation, cognition, references, works, and a standing to acquisition. The First Intellect and the transcendent limits that are separate from bodies did not attain their perfection through any of that; rather they gained their perfections from the very beginning of their existence, by way of origination and emanation. It is not allowable that the judgment of this saying be carried over onto those. As for what the author of al-Nusrah brought forward concerning the meaning of the seven stars, their fixity, their return, and their setting straight, and the six days in which Allah created the heavens and the earth and what is between them, and what he mentioned of the resolve, the author of al-Islah left nothing of the interpretation of that and its meanings unbrought, and in it is sufficiency, so as to dispense with a rendering into Arabic in an interpretation that scarcely balances and does not match.

The twenty-seventh section of the ninth chapter.

The author of al-Islah said:

The single line does not make the crosswise mark obligatory except by the appearance of the second line, because the line points to the Speaker, and the crosswise mark points to the Sharia, and the form of a thing ought not to appear except after its completion. So just as the crosswise mark appeared after the second line, likewise the Sharia of the first was not completed except by the appearance of the second Speaker; and when the second Speaker appeared, the Sharia of the first was completed and its form appeared in perfection. Likewise the six crosswise marks are completed by the seven lines, for there is no Sharia before the first of the Speakers, and no Sharia at the appearance of the seventh; the six laws belong rather to the Speakers, the seventh of whom is the Qa'im. If you draw seven lines, there appear from among them six crosswise marks upon these likenesses: one, two, three, four, five, six. Were the reckoning upon the saying that the first of the Speakers has no Sharia, then five crosswise marks would have to appear between seven lines. Since six crosswise marks appeared from among seven lines, the seven lines came to stand for the masters of the seven cycles, and the six crosswise marks for the six laws, and the Sharia was made obligatory upon Adam.

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Concerning the matter of the six crosswise marks between the seven lines, and his inferring from the lines to the Prophets and from the crosswise marks to the six laws, the matter is not as he said. Rather, the seven lines stand for the higher letters, of which each Prophet wrote one single letter; and the six crosswise marks stand for the cycles of the Imams: between Adam and Noah, who are the seven Imams; and between Noah and Abraham in the second cycle; and between Abraham and Moses in the third cycle; and so on up to Muhammad (ع). No further cycle remains for the two after the Qa'im save the cycle of his followers and his caliphs who succeed him in the world. So six crosswise marks of five lines fell, in that there occurred, between the cycles of the Imams, five Speakers who are the masters of the laws, and the likeness of that is this:

Adam — Noah — Abraham — Moses — Jesus — Muhammad — al-Qa'im.
the Imams — the Imams — the Imams — the Imams — the Imams — the Imams — the Imams.

Just as, after the Qa'im (ع), no Imams occur, so the establishing of the Sharia was not made obligatory upon him; and likewise, since before Adam (ع) no Imams occurred, the establishing of the Sharia was not made obligatory upon him. For if the Sharia, in its establishment, must have Imams before it, it must also have them after it. The foundation-Imams of Noah (ع) preceded the cycle of the foundation-Imams of Adam (ع) and so came after the Imams of his own cycle, and for this reason the establishing of the Sharia was made obligatory upon him; and so too Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad (ع). But the Qa'im was preceded by the Imams of the cycle of Muhammad (ع), and no Imams came after him, and for this reason the establishing of the Sharia was not made obligatory upon him. Likewise Adam had his foundation-Imams come after him, and no other Imams preceded him, and for this reason the establishing of the Sharia was not obligatory upon him. This is what the two of them say.

We say:

The saying of the author of al-Islah agrees with what he has pointed to, except for what he said in the course of his statement: "Just as the crosswise mark appeared after the second line, likewise the Sharia of the first was not completed except by the appearance of the second Speaker." This is an unsound and crooked saying, for the Sharia of Adam is itself its own foundation, and he established its foundation for it. Were it allowable that the Sharia of Adam was completed only by the coming of the Speaker, the master of the second cycle, then it would be allowable to say that the Sharia of Muhammad (ع) is not complete and is completed only by the coming of the Qa'im, the master of the seventh cycle (ع), and that is among the impossibilities of speech, for the Sharia is a stature that needs no increase upon it nor any decrease from it. Had the author of al-Islah said that the first cycle, which is Adam's (ع), was not completed except by the appearance of the master of the second cycle, who is Noah (ع), and that likewise no one of the cycles is completed except by the appearance of what follows it, so that he set in the place of his saying about the Sharia the first cycle, it would have been consistent and balanced. And Allah forgives us and him, by His mercy.

As for the author of al-Nusrah, in the matter of the line and the crosswise mark and his interpretation of the six crosswise marks upon the Imams, it is a good interpretation, provided the lines stand for the Speakers and not for the higher letters, and the crosswise marks for the span of their affair in the cycle and not for the Imams. Even so, this does not require that Adam have no Sharia; rather it makes the Sharia obligatory through the existence of the Imams after him, and the existence of the Imams is for the sake of preserving the station of the Speaker, his Sharia, and his ordinances among his community. Were it not for that, obedience to an Imam after a Speaker would not be obligatory. As for the five lines and the six crosswise marks, it is open to the author of al-Islah to interpret them by setting the lines upon the five possessors of firm resolve and the crosswise marks upon the cycles of the masters of the six laws.

The ground for this is that, in interpretation, the soundest reading is the one that comes out identical and balanced when it is stated, for a reading of that kind holds a nobler place in the souls.

In sum, we say: the cycle is of two kinds, a major cycle and a minor cycle. The major cycle belongs to the Speakers, whose place their Imams preserve within the community after them; and the minor cycle belongs to the faithful foundations, who seal the weeks. If the lines are interpreted as the masters of the major cycles, who are the Speakers, then the ʿarḍas can only concern the span of their governance of the community through the Imams who come after them in a cycle sealed by the coming of another like the first, and the measure of that is known. And if they are interpreted as the masters of the seven minor cycles, who are the Imams that bring each cycle to completion, then the ʿarḍas can only concern the span of the Imams' command in each week, together with those who complete them, whose cycle is sealed by their seventh; and their number is known, as is the number of Imams in each cycle, and the number of the appendices according to the existing higher bodies already described. We shall set this out in a book other than this one, if Allah wills.

Chapter Twenty-Nine of Title IX

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Take, for example, the words of Allah, may He be exalted:

«كما بدأكم تعودون»

"As He began you, so you shall return."

That is, as the matter was at its beginning, which was not by way of the realities, so shall the matter be at its end.

We say:

Even granting that this is an interpretation, the fitting way to state it, so that it corresponds properly, is this: that these words of Allah point to what comes about after the Qā'im (ع), namely the return of knowledge to potentiality after it has gone out into actuality, and of its abundance to scarcity, so that after him all people become as they were in the time of Adam; and the return of ignorance, after it had been in potentiality and in fewness, to actuality and abundance, by reason of the wisdom decreed within it. And blessed be Allah, the best of creators.

Chapter Thirty of Title IX

The author of al-Nusrah said:

Furthermore, had the author of al-Islah looked at the significance contained in his name, I mean Adam (آدم), it would have become plain to him that Adam had no law of his own. For his name is of three letters, alif (ألف), dāl (دال), and mīm (ميم), and they are not compounded or joined together; rather each letter of them stands apart from the other, a clear indication that he was not the author of a composed Sharīʿa. As for the five Prophets who came after him, their names are joined and compounded, such as

نوح وإبراهيم وموسى وعيسى ومحمد

Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad (ع), which is proof that each of them possessed a Sharīʿa, and the revelation of it is his.

We say:

If the separateness of the letters of Adam's name (ع) entails that Adam has no law, then the fact that these very letters are, in themselves, not separate entails that he does have a law. For the alif of the name is itself composed of alif (ألف), lām (لام), and fā' (فاء); the mīm of it is composed of mīm (ميم), yā' (ياء), and mīm (ميم); and likewise the dāl (دال). If separateness does not oblige a law for him, then non-separateness would oblige a law. In truth, drawing an inference of this kind is impossible and cannot stand. For were it sound, it would follow necessarily that the one who authors the Sharīʿa must be the author of composition, and that his name, according to what the Prophet (ع) reported, is Muhammad (محمد), and Muhammad is composed of four letters. And since it is impossible for the Qā'im to be the master of a Sharīʿa while his name is compounded, it is void that the separateness of the letters of Adam's name should indicate that he is not an author of composition and Sharīʿa, just as it is void that the joining of the letters of the name of the Qā'im (ع), which is Muhammad, and its being compounded, should indicate that he is the author of a Sharīʿa. Once that is void, no weight attaches to inference of this kind. The Sharīʿa of Adam is established. And praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds.

Chapter Thirty-One of Title IX

The author of al-Islah said:

As for the statement that Noah (ع) was the first to establish his foundation, and the likening of him to the ship, enough has already been said on that in the chapter on the foundations.

We say:

Rather, Noah (ع) was singled out by the ark because he was the first of the possessors of firm resolve, and the first to abrogate the earlier laws and to compose a new law. His law stood in the place of the ship, and that with which he abrogated the old law stood in the place of the flood; for by it he destroyed the people of the earth, save those who answered his call to his law and boarded his ship, that is, established obedience to his foundation. The meaning is that he laid down a law during his lifetime; and when he had perfected it, he committed the matter to his foundation, and commanded him to raise the call upon his law, and commanded the appendices to give ear to him, so that they attained the flowing grace only through him. Whoever obeyed his foundation was in the ship, and whoever opposed him and held to the first law was drowned. Do you not see that he described as drowned by the flood those who worshipped

وداً، وسواعاً، ويغوث، ويعوق، ونسراً

Wadd, Suwāʿ, Yaghūth, Yaʿūq, and Nasr? He said:

«من خطيئاتهم اغرقوا فادخلوا النار»

"Because of their sins they were drowned and made to enter the Fire."

The author of al-Nusrah said:

If the rank of the ship is like the rank of the apparent law, then whoever holds fast to it among the people of the apparent has been saved, and the true hidden call is nullified. Moreover, what appears from the Book of Allah, may He be exalted, is other than what the author of al-Islah went to. For Allah described the beginning of Noah's affair (ع), and His sending him to his people, and his calling them to his law, and then He commanded him to make the ark. This description is set forth in Sūrat Hūd. Had the ship been the apparent law, Allah would have commanded him to make the ark first. But this account rests upon his having first composed the apparent law and called creation to make use of it, and only then having been commanded to make the ark, that is, to appoint his guardian. The proof of that is the saying of Allah, may He be exalted:

«واصنع الفلك»

"And make the ark,"

down to His saying:

«وكلما مرّ عليه ملأ من قومه سخروا منه»

"And whenever a company of the chiefs of his people passed by him, they mocked him,"

meaning that whenever Noah (ع) set up his guardian, the notables of his people mocked him and marveled at him, because he was the first to institute a guardian; and so they mocked him. This is what they said.

We say:

As for the statement of the author of al-Islah, with his interpreting the ark as the face of the new Sharīʿa: this is because whoever came under its rulings became like one inside a ship, delivered from humiliation, abasement, and every kind of affliction, just as one inside a ship is delivered from drowning. This meaning is admissible. And as for the author of al-Nusrah, with his interpreting the ark as the call and the guardian: this is because whoever entered the guiding call and bound himself to obedience to its foundation was kept safe from drowning in error, in the oneness of Allah, may He be exalted, and in the knowledge of His limits, the guardian standing for him in the rank of a guide. Both interpretations are sound in that respect: the one concerns the affair of this world and the other the affair of religion, and neither of them dispenses with the other, for each of them stands by itself.

Chapter Thirty-Two of Title IX

As for the statement of the author of al-Nusrah, that Noah was the first to set up a guardian by the interpretation that came to him, it is not sound. For the prophethood of Adam (ع) and his Sharīʿa are established, and it is proven that Seth (ع) was his guardian; the precedence is in the Sharīʿa and in laying it down, and the guardian and his appointment come from him. And of what reason indicates is that every one of the Speakers must, of necessity, have a guardian who takes up the deriving of benefit from him, especially since every mover necessarily has something moved: the mover is one, even if many moved things proceed from it. And since the Speaker, in his age, is the mover of all souls by his call to seek subsistence through the establishment of the two acts of worship, it necessarily follows that he must have something moved, which stands to him in the course of the learner from him, even if many learners learn from him; and the one truly learned from is the one whom we call the guardian. Adam, being a Speaker, must therefore have a guardian, and likewise every Speaker. And since Adam did indeed appoint a guardian for himself, it is not permitted to hold any belief contrary to that.

Chapter Thirty-Three of Title IX

The author of al-Nusrah said:

After his interpretation of what Allah, may He be exalted, related concerning the son of Noah:

«سآوي الى جبل يعصمني من الماء»

"I will take refuge on a mountain that will protect me from the water,"

and Noah's reply:

«لا عاصم اليوم من امر الله»

"There is no protector today from the command of Allah,"

it becomes plain here, by his saying that he will take refuge with an appendix and by Noah saying that there is no protector today from the command of Allah, that the matter, before Noah, was on the part of the appendices, since the son did not know the rank of the guardian. Had Adam possessed a guardian, his son would have known that. This is what he said.

We say:

This statement of his contradicts what he himself said in the chapter on the cycles: that the Imams took charge of the cycle of Adam (ع) after him. I do not know how this escaped him. I think that the copy of the book has been altered and added to, and diminished, through the fewness of those who attend to the affair of religion and their neglect in correcting what they write by collation and by reading it back to the people of knowledge and religion. And Allah is the one whose help is sought.

The interpretation of the son of Noah is that the son ascribed to Noah was not of his loins, but was rather a man of great worldly rank whom Noah had advanced, like the adversary in the cycle of Muhammad, our Prophet (ع). He benefited from Noah and learned what he had, and by his own influence became a son to him. He reckoned it within himself, and the people too imagined that he was his guardian. Then the command of Allah, may He be exalted, came, to make the ark, that is, the affair of Noah, for the guardianship was conferred upon another, not upon him, in accordance with what Allah had commanded him. He called the people who followed him to enter under his command and to obey him, which is His saying:

«اركبوا فيها»

"Embark in it."

So this man held back from what others had entered into, namely obedience to his foundation, by his refusal to enter the ark. Noah (ع) called him, saying:

«يا بني اركب معنا ولا تكن مع الكافرين»

"O my son, embark with us, and be not with the disbelievers,"

calling him to obedience and to holding fast to the command, all the more because of the longing to return to the beginning, reckoning that he would act as before, by analogy with the good compliance to his commands that had been seen from him.

When Noah called him, and he despaired of him entirely, that is, of the rank he had been hoping for, he became a hypocrite, grew arrogant, and refused to obey his foundation over what had happened. Noah said that the command of Allah concerning him was that, if he did not yield up his presidency to the guardian, it would be nullified. The adversary inclined toward our guardian (ع). Noah admonished him at one time and warned him at another, saying: the command of Allah is upon it, and if you do not yield to it, your presidency will go and humiliation will overtake you; you will see none who feigns to be your equal, nor any helper in your affair. This is the meaning of his saying:

«لا عاصم اليوم من امر الله»

"There is no protector today from the command of Allah."

When the son heard this from him, he showed his disobedience and said, out of his ignorance: he has no knowledge of this; this will not be done to you, that you should keep the whole presidency in your house, and though intent, reviling, and abuse should appear, I am supported by chiefs who strengthen my hand and help me to ward off humiliation from me. This is the meaning of his saying:

«سآوي الى جبل يعصمني من الماء»

"I will take refuge on a mountain that will protect me from the water."

The water here refers to that which he pours out against the force that destroys, and the mountain stands for what he takes as his protection of men and partisans. So he set his mind on this, and he and those like him took up his cause, and they perished in his cycle, and there remained only the one who bound himself to obedience to his foundation.

Allah set down news of this kind in His Noble Book as a warning to the community concerning what befalls the affair of the foundation from those who are intransigent toward him and do not bind themselves to his obedience. At one time He points to it through our Prophet, "O children of Adam"; at another He signals it by the she-camel of Ṣāliḥ; and at another by the son of Noah, that they may know that the states of the cycle of Muhammad are like the states of the cycles that went before. He did not single out Muhammad and his foundation (عليهم السلام) with the intransigence of their community toward the two of them and their reviling of the two of them; rather this is a pattern that has run through all the prophets and the guardians, extending down to the time of the seventh. The refuge sought by the son of Noah was not to the appendix, but rather to the people of the apparent who were in a state of hypocrisy like his own. He sought their aid, but the sword encompassed them by the power of Allah, and Allah purified the earth of them. That is the flood, which spread outwardly and inwardly. Likewise it will be in the cycle of Muhammad (ع): some of it has already proven true and come to pass, and some of it will prove true and come to pass, by the leave of Allah. And none knows that save the discerning one among the people of the ʿAlawī call, may Allah spread wide its lights.
