# The Creator of the Thing and the No-Thing

The Creator of the Thing and the No-Thing

The First Section of the Tenth Chapter

We say:

The author of al-Mahsul (المحصول), may Allah have mercy on him, when he exerted himself, and strove, and discharged the right of the daʿwa and its sons, and opened the doors of the landmarks by what he composed of his books that stand over their expanses; and when Sheikh Abu Hatim, may Allah have mercy on him, corrected of it what he corrected: most people supposed that this correction was a fault in the author of al-Mahsul, and so they attacked him on account of the apology which the author of the correction made for that in his book. They faulted it and overturned it. That is a corrupt judgment, an act inclining away from the truth, and the matter is other than what they imagined.

For the speaker (ع), when he knew, at the time of laying down the order of things, that the sciences of religion are too many for a single soul beneath the imam to encompass, or to be able to preserve them all in a single utterance, arranged for them many persons beneath the imamate, that they might cooperate among themselves over all the sciences, their awareness, and their retention, like the twelve hujjas. So each of them benefits, from those to whom Allah the Exalted has entrusted the reins of guidance among the righteous imams (عليهم السلام), in the abstruse matters, their inner and their outer, according to their capacities and their strength in reception and deduction, that they might be to others in guidance as the imams (عليهم السلام) are in exposition and instruction. Religion, through them, is like a single person who gathers his parts together, and they are to it like the senses that perceive things: if one of them misses something, another perceives it, without this second perception being a defect against the one that missed the perception, or against the others.

If that is so, then Abu Hatim's correction, may Allah raise his degree, of what he corrected from the book of al-Mahsul, is no fault against him, nor a deficiency against the author of al-Mahsul, nor a challenge against either of them. It is only what we have said: that the persons ranked for the correction of the sciences of religion are like the senses. If one of the senses misses to perceive some thing of the world of nature, the other does not miss it, but cooperates over it, discharging the right of religion and confirming the obligation owed to the Lord of the Worlds.

Since that is so, and since I have seen in the book of al-Mahsul what was most binding upon Abu Hatim, most obligatory for him to expound and to correct, which he did not correct, nor speak upon, but passed over, the truth of cooperation obliged me to discharge the right of religion by clarifying what is folded within what he brought forward, of what it is not permissible to hold as belief in the chapter of monotheism and the first Creator (المبدع الأول), apart from the other branches which, if Allah aids by deferring the term, we shall discuss in other than this book. So I did it. It is not permissible for me to apologize for that, since the souls are, for religion, all one, even if I apologize to my own soul. Allah accepts from us what we offer to draw near to Him of the guardianship (walaya) that leads to the good pleasure of Allah, Lord of the Worlds, and gathers us, by His grace, both the past and the renewed, the foremost of us and the latecomer, with our pure loyalists (mawali) in the Abode of Holiness; He is Munificent, Generous.

The Second Section of the Tenth Chapter

The author of al-Mahsul said:

In the chapter of monotheism: He is the Creator of the thing and of the no-thing, the rational, the illusory, the intellectual, the logical. I mean what falls beneath these elements, and what does not fall beneath them.

We say:

By this saying he has necessitated that among the existents which are from Allah the Exalted, falling beneath His origination (ibdaʿ), there is that which is not perceived by reason, nor by illusion, nor by thought, and of which no report is given by logic. Yet its existence is impossible, while he would make belief in it licit. For that which is not perceived by reason, illusion, or thought, and of which logic gives no report, if it had existence, could not but be either prior to the intellect in its existence, or with the intellect in its existence, or posterior to the intellect in its existence.

It is impossible that it be prior to the intellect, for the intellect is the very act behind which is none but Allah the Exalted, from whom it issued and from whom it came to be. It is pure origination, and the true existence (الوجود الحق) which is the first existent from Allah, glory be to Him.

It is impossible, too, that its existence be together with the very intellect; for the intellect is the very intellect that is the origination, and the intellect, at its coming-to-be, is but a single essence, not two. Further, were its existence to be with the intellect, they being distinct yet sharing in existence, it would follow that Allah the Exalted, from whom it came to be, is a manifold originating cause, or that there is with Him another; exalted is He above that with a great exaltation. For it belongs to the effect that its existence be according to its cause, and their being two distinct effects requires two causes, or a cause made manifold by relations, such that the existence of each of them is from a relation within it other than the other's, primarily. So it is impossible that two distinct effects exist from a single cause whose essence is preserved from difference by relations and additions. And if its existence with the intellect requires this, then its existence is impossible, for Allah the Exalted is sanctified above every mode of multiplicity, and additions, and othernesses, and above whatever would make Him a cause.

It is impossible, too, that its existence be posterior to the intellect; for were its existence posterior to the intellect and from it, then knowledge of it would not escape the minds, nor would its definition be hard for them. Rather they would perceive it and define it, just as they defined all the other existents, sensible and intelligible, in the arena of affirmation and negation. Since, then, in its existence it is not free of the three divisions, and each division of them necessitates that its existence be impossible, it is established that its existence is impossible, and to hold it is misguidance.

The Third Section of the Tenth Chapter

Then, had the author of al-Mahsul not said, following his affirmation of the thing and the no-thing, that Allah originated them both, I mean what falls beneath these elements and what does not fall beneath them, we would have said that by "the thing" he intended what is described among bodies, and by "the no-thing" what is not described with the attributes of bodies, from the world of origination, upon the error of him who says that, believing it. But since he brought forward what folds within it the affirmation of the impossible, it resulted only in one's believing, among the existents, in something that shares with Allah in His transcendence in some manner, so that corruption becomes general. And we have shown that this is impossible.

Then we say:

"The thing" is known to be a common term under which fall all the existents, of substances and accidents, sensible and intelligible. His intention in his saying "the no-thing" cannot but be either what some of the sages held, that it is an affirmation of that which is other than a thing, an affirmation stripped of what confines the thing to all its two divisions, of substances and accidents, and raised to the corrected form of the privative proposition, and they alluded to it, saying: that is like our saying "not-the-man," which is an affirmation of everything that is not a man, whereby the one reported of is stripped from us by existence, without affirming what belongs to no thing. They said: it belongs to the particle لا, when by it is intended a specific entity so as to remove it from existence, negating it of that which it is, without affirming of it something other than itself, as in his saying "the thing," which is a universal entity under which every existent falls: when we intend it by the particle لا, we say لا شيء, and the very entity of the thing becomes made to vanish from that in which it is a thing, without affirmation, it being other than it. Or, if by it is intended a particular entity within that entity, and something other is affirmed:

then if his intention is the affirmation of that which is a not-thing, his saying that Allah is its originator is an error. For the no-thing, being that which no attribute befalls, and which is not in a state like that of being a substance or an accident, and the negation of the attributes of the thing from Allah the Exalted being a transcendence and a sanctification, He, the Exalted, being neither substance nor accident, then the attributes of the thing are no attribute of His, nor from Him, nor is He, the Exalted, like them. So it is as though by his saying "the originator of the thing and the no-thing" he said: the originator of His own essence, which is other than the thing, and the originator of the thing, which is other than His essence. And that is an error.

And if his intention is the negation of the very entity of the thing, his saying that Allah the Exalted is its originator is an error. For the thing is the origination, and it is the very entity of existence, and the no-thing is the negation of origination and the nullification of existence. And if the no-thing is the negation of origination, how can it be sound that He is its originator, when He has negated it from that impossible thing?

The Fourth Section of the Tenth Chapter

The author of al-Mahsul said:

The Originator of things, not from a thing; He alone, and no thing with Him, everlastingly. So when we say "He, and no thing with Him," we have negated the thing and the no-thing, and made them both originated; and we have negated every form, simple and composite, from His identity (huwiyya); and we have made everything upon which speech falls, or upon which speech does not fall, an originated effect in truth, finite; and that the no-thing is after the thing, since the coming-to-be of its name came to be only at the appearing of the thing.

We say:

Enough has already passed of the discourse upon the no-thing. His saying, "the Originator of things, not from a thing only, He and no thing with Him, everlastingly," folds within it what is not permissible to hold as belief. For "no-thing" is, with the sages, the affirmation of that which is not a thing, according to what we have mentioned. So when he says "He, and no thing with Him," it is as though he said "He, and that which is not a thing, with Him, everlastingly," and that is associationism (shirk).

In sum: there is not, among the tongues existing between mankind, that by which expression can be made of Allah the Exalted in a manner befitting Him. For all that the composed and simple words and letters announce is originated, and words, letters, and languages are instruments for mankind in making one another understand what is within themselves. They are originated, and they indicate only what is, like their own state, originated, as we have explained in our treatise known as al-Rawda (الروضة).

He, the Exalted, being beyond the minds and the perceptions, and behind a veil from the understandings and thought, the ways of inquiry are cut off short of Him. When one is driven to affirm Him, after the knowledge that all that the tongues pronounce and the lips move with falls upon what He has originated and brought into being, glory be to Him, there is no expression borrowed for affirming Him without affirming an attribute or a plurality, save the expression هو, which Allah the Exalted affirmed after the exception in His saying:

شهد الله انه لا إله إلا هو

Allah bears witness that there is no god but He.

He said "but He," and did not say "but Allah," nor "but other than Allah"; rather He said "He." Likewise Imam al-Muʿizz (ع) pointed to it in his book known as Taʾwil al-Shariʿa (تأويل الشريعة), saying that it is "He" only. For this expression, though borrowed, is, from the minds, an affirmation of that which is outside them, without attribute or plurality.

This, whereas the treading of the path of affirming Him, the Exalted, by the use of the particle لا in the negation of every kind of the existents, is more attaining to the intent than the bare safety from likening (tashbih) and stripping (taʿtil), and is more fitting and worthier, in the view of the scholars and the jurists among the people of religion.

The Fifth Section of the Tenth Chapter

And his saying:

"If we say He, and no thing with Him, we have negated the thing and the no-thing, and made them both originated." From where, then, is the existence sound of what is said to be a no-thing among what falls beneath origination? And the existence of what is said to be a no-thing, it being prior to origination, and it being other than Allah, such that its negation would be sound? Nay: there is nothing but the thing, and the thing that is the willed-thing (al-mashiʾ); and the willed-thing is that which we have negated to be existent, in that there is, in the languages, that which expresses it, because of the greatness of His affair, the Exalted, and the shortfall of the composed, originated letters from signifying in a manner befitting His majesty, the Exalted.

The thing that is the willed-thing is other than Allah, who brought it into being, not from a thing, it having an entity and a right in existence upon all its divisions. Then the no-thing is a negation of the very entity of the thing, which is pure origination, according to what discourse has already passed upon. And if the no-thing is the negation of the very entity of origination, which is the very entity of existence and the true existence, how does it come to be, in existence, an originator? Those sayings are not sound.

The Sixth Section of the Tenth Chapter

And his saying:

"We have negated every form, simple and composite, from His identity, and we have made everything upon which speech falls, or upon which speech does not fall, an originated effect in truth."

We say:

How does that upon which speech does not fall, the speech from which all the existents came to be, become, all of it, an originated effect? That is an error.

The Seventh Section of the Tenth Chapter

And his saying:

"The no-thing is after the thing, since the coming-to-be of its name came to be from it at the appearing of a thing."

The no-thing is not a name that falls upon an entity having a share in existence. For "the thing" is a name, and the لا, which is the particle of negation, has negated it; and the negation of the name does not become a name for other than that of which the name was a name, nor is report made by it of other than what the sages mentioned: that folded within it is the affirmation of that which has no name. Discourse has already passed upon that which contains the answer to it, and in it is sufficiency.

The Eighth Section of the Tenth Chapter

The author of al-Mahsul said:

In the chapter of the first Creator: that the Maker, the Exalted, originated the world at a single stroke; that is, He originated the intellect as a single whole, and there emerged within it, by His determining, the forms of the two worlds and what is in them, without those forms being pointed to by their identities before the intellect. Rather the intellect's knowledge falls upon them, and they are, with it, known. Yet the intellect has preceded them in act; so, whether in act or in potency, it and the forms are all together.

We say:

This knowledge, which is the intellect that he mentioned as originated, within which emerged the forms of the two worlds and what is in them, cannot be the first intellect existent from Allah the Exalted, first of all. For that which is prior in existence is independent of conceiving that by which it exists, since its existence is bound to its Originator who brought it into being. It is not incumbent upon it to know more than its knowledge of its own essence, and that it is originated, and that its existence is not by its own essence but by another, which is its Originator, and that it is a cause for the existence of what is below it. It is like the imam, who is not obliged to know others below him, and knows no more than his knowledge that he is the cause of deliverance for those below him, and a mediator between the one above him and the one below him. Rather it is incumbent upon him to know the one above him, and incumbent upon others to know him, and to know others whose existence and rank preceded him, that they may be delivered thereby. For this reason posteriority in existence obliges the posterior to conceive all that preceded it in existence among the limits, that this may earn it subsistence and complete existence.
