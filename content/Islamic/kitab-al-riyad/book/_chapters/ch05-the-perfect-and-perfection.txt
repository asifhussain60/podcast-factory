# The Perfect and Perfection

This is the first chapter, from the book *al-Riyad*, concerning the ruling between the two disputants, in the chapter on what was discussed regarding the soul, which is the first emanation.

Section One

The author of *al-Islah* said: the soul is complete in itself, because it was emitted from the First Intellect complete. It is a complete emanation from the complete, because the Intellect is perfection.

The author of *al-Nusra* said, in refutation: he does not recognize that perfection is better and more complete than the perfect. For perfection is an attribute while the perfect is what is described; perfection is what is borne while the perfect is the bearer; and what is described, the bearer, is better and more complete than the attribute and the borne. For the perfect is described by perfection and bears it, while perfection is the attribute of the perfect, predicated upon it. This is what they say.

And we say: what the author of *al-Islah* means, in saying that the soul is complete because it is emitted from perfection, is not that perfection is an attribute or no attribute, or that the perfect is described or not described. Rather, what he means is that perfection is like the cause from which the existence of the effect comes to be. For he saw that perfection is the cause of the perfect, and precedes it in rank; for were it not for perfection, the perfect would not exist. The proof of this is that if perfection is lifted away in the imagination, the perfect is lifted away with its lifting, like the source-forms from which the past act, the future, the agent, and the object are derived: when these are lifted away, whatever is derived from them upon conjugation is lifted away with their lifting. So he treated the First Intellect, since it was first in existence and from it the existents came to be, as the cause, and said: it is perfection; and he treated what he called the emanating soul as the effect, and said: it is the perfect. And since this was the intention of the author of *al-Islah*, his statement that the First Intellect is perfection is true. And since it is true, what the author of *al-Nusra* said in this manner is an overreach against the author of *al-Islah*.

Section Two

Then the author of *al-Nusra*'s saying that the perfect is better than perfection, while the discourse here about perfection and the perfect is about the First Principle, which is the First Effect and the First Emanation, and not about any other, necessitates that the nobility belong to the effect and not to its cause. The proof of this is that the perfect is perfect only by perfection, without which it would not be perfect; and whatever a thing's being is by, that thing is its cause. And if that is so, and the perfect's existence is by perfection, then perfection is the cause of the perfect, and the perfect is the effect of perfection. And if the perfect is the effect of perfection, and he assigned nobility and merit to the perfect over perfection, then by that assignment he has necessitated that the effect be nobler than its cause, and that is impossible. For whatever exists by the existence of something such that, when that something is lifted away, its existence is lifted away with its lifting, that existence by which it exists is below it in nobility and precedence. And since that is impossible, the author of *al-Nusra* in what he brought forward was overreaching against the author of *al-Islah*.

Section Three

The author of *al-Nusra* said, secondly: perfection is an attribute and the perfect is what is described; perfection is what is borne and the perfect is the bearer. This as an indication of his claim that the perfect is nobler than perfection, and that the bearer is nobler than the borne.

We say: this is not true without qualification. For we find, from the side of the beings and the second emanation, what is borne yet nobler than its bearer. Such as the nutritive power, which is the proximate cause of the existence of the growth power and is its bearer, while the growth power is a form borne within it and its completion, and growth is nobler than it. And such as the growth power, which is the proximate cause of the existence of the sensitive power and is its bearer, while the sensitive is a form borne within it and its completion, and the sensitive is nobler than it. And such as the sensitive power, which is the proximate cause of the existence of the speaking power and is its bearer, while the speaking power is a form borne within it and its completion. And the nobility of this is not like the nobility of that.

Then, because it is impossible, from the side of origination and the first emanation, that the First Intellect be borne and the Second Intellect emanating from it be the bearer, or that the perfect, which is the First Intellect, be an attribute of the perfect, which is the Second Intellect. And since that is so, the author of *al-Nusra*'s inference in his refutation, by what he brought forward, was an overreach against the author of *al-Islah*; and the meanings of his statement in it would be true only if carried upon the natural things, which are far from what the author of *al-Islah* speaks of, namely the originative and emanative principles.

The statement of the author of *al-Islah* is correct with regard to the world of origination and emanation; and the statement of the author of *al-Nusra*, even though the author of *al-Islah* is mistaken about it, is correct with regard to the world of bodies and their accidents.

Section Four

And we say, in consideration of this: perfection is a name applying to the First Thing, which is the First Cause; and it is the First Perfect, which is the First Effect. Its perfection is by the addition of its existence to that from which its existence comes, and its perfection is by its addition to that upon which its essence rests, and that is as with origination and the Originator upon this order. Origination is not something other than the Originator; rather it is that by which the Originator is itself. And what is it? It is a single essence: when it is added to that from which its existence came, it is origination; and when it is added to that upon which its essence rests, of the mark of temporal occurrence, it is originated. So the First Intellect is perfection, and it is the perfect in truth. And it deserves to be perfect only because existence of its own kind is precluded. For the perfect thing is that outside which there is nothing like it in its species, as with the existents in natural things, let alone in origination. The body, too, is a name applying to a thing perfect in its three dimensions, so that outside it there is nothing like it; rather whatever is like it is from it, and counted as of it. And likewise the sun is perfect: there exists nothing outside it that is like it in its species.

In sum, the existence of what is below the First Intellect, in relation to it, is a deficient existence; except that that deficiency is not in its essence but in its rank, in relation to what is above it. For all things are perfect in their essences, since their existence comes from the perfection that is the First Intellect, and there is no deficiency in them. For were they deficient in their essences, their existence from perfection would be impossible, and it would likewise be impossible that from the deficient there should come to be something other than it, since the deficient is precluded from producing something that itself needs the completion of its own essence, let alone the existence of something other than it from it. So deficiency attaches to them only when they are added, by what is above them in rank, in relation to another, and not in their essence; because the essence, inasmuch as essences are of a single thing, is like the numbers, which are perfect in their essences and whose deficiency is only according to their addition to others by which their existence is; and like all the existents of the world, which are perfect, with no deficiency in them, inasmuch as they are bodies, and deficient inasmuch as their ranks in the forms, and differing when some forms are nobler than others, like the human being, who is the noblest of all the forms, and is in his essence perfect like the others, and deficient in relation to what is above him. And if the matter is thus in bodily things, then the way of what is not a body, upon this order in respect of ranking, is likewise, like for like.

And therefore Allah, exalted, said:

> سنريهم آياتنا في الآفاق وفي أنفسهم حتى يتبين لهم أنه الحق

"We shall show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth." (the verse.) That is: We shall establish for them the Imams and the limits, in the manifest and the hidden, that they may be guided to what the state of the observed bodies is, and of the souls whose actions exist, upon what had been hidden from them of knowing the limits of Allah, which are the truth, so that the truth of the form may become clear to them.

Section Five

The author of *al-Islah* said: what is in need of perfection is the act of the soul, not the essence of the soul; because the soul's act is completed only in time, and the emanation of the soul is with time, just as the First Intellect and time are one, because the First Originator, and origination, and time, and perfection are one existent. The Creator originated all the originated things at once. And the soul, though it needs to derive benefit from the First, this is not a deficiency in its essence, just as the First was not deficient though it was in need of the command. Completion and deficiency appear in the act of the soul, not in its essence.

The author of *al-Nusra* said: he did not recognize that the Intellect does not stand in need of anything in the way the soul needs to derive benefit from the Intellect. The state of the Intellect and the matter is not like the state of the soul and the Intellect; for the matter, from the side of the Intellect, is nothing other than the Intellect, rather their identity is one identity. And it is impossible to say that a thing needs what it itself is; rather the need of a thing for a thing is only when it is other than it. This is what they say.

We say: the statement of the author of *al-Nusra* is a reply to what the author of *al-Islah* maintained, of the Intellect's need for the command only. And since the discourse was about the principles that come out of the natural world, the statement of the author of *al-Nusra* in that is closer to the truth than the statement of the author of *al-Islah*. For what is fit to be said to be in need of perfection, and to be completed only in time, because it is an emanation from the natural world and not from outside it, is the souls that attain the degree of perfection, that is, the ascent to the degree of the second perfection, only in time, and whose existence is an emanative existence. So if the statement of the author of *al-Islah* is carried upon this aspect, it is correct.

Section Six

And the statement of the author of *al-Islah*, that the Intellect and time are one, because the Originator, and origination, and time, and perfection are one existent, is not correct. For time is the number of the motions of the degrees of the celestial sphere; it is the imagined duration between the motion of the first degree of it and the motion of what follows it of the other degree, to where the first had been. And origination, which is the Intellect, and perfection, its existence precedes the sphere by the motion of whose degrees the existence of time comes to be. And to attach the existence of time to the existence of the First Intellect, and to hold that belief, contradicts what the law of the guiding Da'wa rests upon. And we have explained, concerning the coming-to-be of time, and what precedes it in existence, and the indication of it from the ordinances of the prophetic Law, in our book known as راحة العقل (Peace of Mind), what suffices the seekers.

Section Seven

The author of *al-Nusra* said: were it not for the Intellect's denial of divinity of itself, the Following one would not have been able to carry its knowledge beyond the rank of the Intellect to the Originator. What deficiency is more evident than this, if it knows the Originator only by way of the First?

We say: that may not be believed of the Following one. For the power flowing in all the noble existents in the world of unity gives them the knowledge that they have an Originator; and were it not for that power, nothing would have existence or subsistence, and it would vanish away. For this reason every thing deserves the name of oneness; and though it be multiple in its essence, its share of that power is unseen, and the justice in it is evident. And it is impossible to conceive of a single one of the human kind that it does not know it has a Creator, or that it imagines it has no Creator, let alone of that great and majestic Limit. Rather we say: it apprehends that from which its existence comes, that it is the True Object of worship, even if there be in it a god. Then, if we apply to the First Intellect what he mentioned, and to the Following one what he said of it, it would be obligatory that the Asas, whose standing is in the world of body, stand in the place of the Following one in the world of Intellect, since, were it not for the Speaker, one would not know one has a god. But the matter is not so; for each of the two has a portion from Allah, and the Speaker precedes it in rank only by his nearness to the First and his coming after it. And therefore Allah said:

> لا الشمس ينبغي لها أن تدرك القمر ولا الليل سابق النهار وكل في فلك يسبحون

"It is not for the sun to overtake the moon, nor does the night outstrip the day; and each is swimming in an orbit." And all, from the side of movement, are one, and by ranks they differ.

Section Eight

The author of *al-Nusra* said: and enough for you, of the deficiency, is that all the ancients placed the soul in the station of the form, and the Intellect in the station of prime matter and material, and they said: forms ever yearn and long for materials, and materials have no subsistence without forms.

We say: his statement that the soul is a form is true; but his statement that the Intellect is in the station of prime matter and material, since the discourse was about the First Intellect, was an overreach against the author of *al-Islah*, addressing him with that. For "Intellect" is a shared name, and what he said is not correct concerning what was separate of the originative and first emanative intellects; rather it is correct concerning what is in the world of nature of the other intellects, and the intellect in potency, and the intellect in act. And since the discourse was about what is separate, then to carry what is said upon what is natural, and to contradict by it, was an overreach. And his statement that forms ever long and yearn for materials, and materials have no subsistence without forms, is an incorrect statement. For forms do not long for materials, since their existence is not for the sake of materials, nor are they what acquire materials; rather it is materials that yearn for forms, since their existence is for the sake of forms, and it is they that acquire forms; and matter is added in relation to the base form and the nobler form.

And since that is so, and he has run the soul in the course of the form, and the Intellect in the course of matter, and has ruled that materials have no subsistence without forms, it is as though he has necessitated that the existence of the Intellect be by the soul. And how can that belief hold concerning what its existence was an originative existence? Or how can it be conceived that the Intellect be the cause of the existence of the Intellect and the soul, while the discourse is about the separate that comes out of the world of nature? Never. For it is known that the Following one's existence is by the Preceding one, not the Preceding one's existence by the Following one; and that is a plain fallacy, arising only from his carrying it upon what is said about the natural things. As for what is in the world of origination and the first emanation, the matter of the existents from the abode of nature, of the Intellect and the soul, which are of the category of the second emanation, is only as he mentioned, and it is correct from this aspect.
