# Why I Wrote This Book

وupon Him is my reliance: وعليه توكلي.

بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم

Praise be to God, the Lord of fathers and mothers, the Creator of animals and plants, whom all that is in the earth and the heavens glorifies, and who in His generosity bestows His blessings upon His creation. Existing things have no existence except by His command, and created beings have no coming-to-be except by His decree and His measure. His being is established, so that minds cannot deny it, and it endures, so that no change can overtake it. Glory be to Him: an object of worship to whose might all greatness and splendor return, by whose command the favors and bounties overflow, and by whose permission the earth and the heavens stand firm. Those who went astray and likened Him to His creatures lost everything, and strayed far indeed.

I praise Him, I thank Him, I believe in Him and do not disbelieve, and I bear witness that there is no god but God, the true object of worship, who originated all things in the difference of their essences, whether the simple that possesses splendor and majesty or the composite that possesses order and perfection. Nothing among them resembles Him, and He is exalted above them in every respect. And I bear witness that the Compassionate, the Merciful, the gracious, the generous One sent Muhammad, His chosen and pure servant, His righteous and God-fearing Messenger.

He sent him when idols were worshipped in the sanctuary, when the landmarks of the upright religion had been leveled, when the fire of unbelief and apostasy was kindled and the structures of polytheism and idolatry were raised high, and when the people had plunged deep into the darkness of misguidance and grown blind in the blindness of affliction. So he rose for God as one who strove and struggled. He cleared away from them what had shadowed them of confusion and darkness, and he broke the idols and images they had been worshipping among themselves. He inaugurated the worship of the Merciful and the rites of faith and submission. He replaced the houses of fire with the Sacred House of God, and abrogated the rule of the crosses by turning toward the Corner, al-Rukn, and the Station, al-Maqam. He guided the servants of God in His worship onto the level path, delivered them from the claws of the seducing devil, joined them by his blessing to the most trustworthy handhold, and brought them to the pools of right guidance and God-fearing. So the word of Tawhid rose with its dawning bright, and the word of shirk was humbled with its rising darkened. Tongues spoke the clear signs, the winds drove the ships of salvation across the seas of dispute, and the judgment of God, exalted be He, was carried out in what He willed of testing the people of opinions and religions.

So may God bless him with a blessing that reaches, through him, his followers in general, and the company of the callers, the du'at, who are of the substance of his light, in particular, so that on a Day whose worth is in the verse:

> يوم لا ينفع مال ولا بنون إلا من أتى الله بقلب سليم

On a Day when neither wealth nor sons will be of any use, save to him who comes to God with a sound heart, the way of his intercession may be made easy for them, and the right of following him and obeying him may be discharged on their behalf.

And may that blessing be upon the Axis of the religion, Qutb al-Din, and the support of the believers, the receiver of his lights, the one who upholds the affair of his followers and his helpers, who restrained his anger in fulfillment of the covenant of God, exalted be He, and bore with patience the humiliation of what was usurped, seeking the face of God, exalted be He: the Commander of the Believers, Ali bin Abi Talib, whose merits cannot be numbered and whose virtues cannot be counted. And upon his progeny, the blessed Imams, by whose lights souls are made to endure, by whose following calamities are lifted, by whose obedience hopes are attained, and by whose intercession perfection is reached. And upon the Imam who rules by the command of God, the Commander of the Believers, al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah, and upon his noble and rightly guided forefathers, the family of Taha and Yasin (عليهم السلام).

Now to the purpose. The most deserving matter to which a believer in the divine Unity should turn his care, and upon which he should fix his resolve, is a matter that serves the affirmation of God's Unity, exalted be He, and the knowledge of His hudud, His limits, so as to order the belief of those who have attained certainty, and that summons to the lifting of confusion and darkness from the souls of the believers, and that opens the way to the affirmation of the Unity of the Lord of the worlds.

For when I saw that Shaykh Abu Hatim al-Razi, may God have mercy upon him, had undertaken to correct in the book al-Mahsul, The Harvest, what he claimed had been corrupted in it, and that Shaykh Abu Ya'qub al-Sijzi, may God have mercy upon him, had come to the defense of the author of al-Mahsul and stood as a witness to the soundness of his statement; and when I saw that what the two of them disputed was not among the branches in which disagreement is permissible while the soundness of the principles is preserved; and when I found Shaykh Abu Ya'qub al-Sijzi, may God have mercy upon him, to be, in some of what he advanced, truthful in the manner he intended in his refutation, yet in some of it prejudiced against Shaykh Abu Hatim, may God have mercy upon him, and found some of the words of both to have issued without proper order; and when, together with all this, there was in the book al-Mahsul, in the words of its author in the chapter on Tawhid and concerning the First Intellect in particular, apart from what pertains to the branches, that which it was more incumbent upon Shaykh Abu Hatim, may God have mercy upon him, to correct and to address, and who was worthier to address than the long passages of his own book, yet he left it and neglected it; and when I saw that all of this together left the people of the Guiding Da'wa, whenever they came upon it, exposed to disagreement in the paths of Tawhid and to disorder in the knowledge of the hudud:

I resolved to bring together the sayings of each of these two men, this one from the book al-Islah, The Reform, as a correction, and that one from the book al-Nusra, The Defense, as a refutation, each set beside its counterpart, and to speak about what necessarily follows from them, what is intended by them, and the sense in which their meanings may rightly be ascribed to their authors, so that the truth of the one may be distinguished from the other, so that whichever of the two was prejudiced and overbearing may be made plain, and so that the meaning of what they differed over may be made clear, that a man may hold his belief accordingly. Then I resolved to speak about what was neglected of correction in the book al-Mahsul, among the principles in which disagreement is not permissible, and to make the truth plain in pursuit of reward.

This I have done, and I have gathered all of it in this book, and I have titled it the book al-Riyad, The Gardens, On the Judgment Between the Two: the author of al-Islah and the author of al-Nusra. I named it so because, in what it gathers of their sayings and in what it advances, it stands as a separation between them, a clarification of the error in which they persisted and of what they neglected to correct in the book al-Mahsul, and a fulfillment of the duty owed in the matter, drawn from what we have gained of the blessings of the Awliya of God (عليهم السلام). For it is like gardens, riyad: a greenness that delights the eye, and a fruit that comes into the hands. Upon God we call for help, and it is He who guides in His earth in the setting forth of a thing, so that we may be kept safe from the marks of error, even if we cannot be wholly cleansed of it.

The book gathers ten chapters, and these together comprise one hundred and fifty-seven sub-chapters. God is the Helper; He is our Sufficiency, and what an excellent Guardian.
