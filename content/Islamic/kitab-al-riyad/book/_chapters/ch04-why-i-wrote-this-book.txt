# Why I Wrote This Book

Upon Him is my reliance.

بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم

Praise be to Allah, Lord of fathers and mothers, Creator of animal and plant, to whom all that is in the earth and the heavens gives praise, and who in His bounty bestows His blessings upon His creation. Nothing that exists has existence except by His command, and nothing that is has being except by His decree and His measure. His decree is firm, so that minds cannot deny it; it endures, so that no change comes upon it. Glory be to Him: to His might all greatness and splendor return, by His command the favors and the bounties overflow, and by His leave the earth and the heavens stand. The misguided who liken Him to His creation are lost, and they have gone far astray.

I praise Him, I thank Him, I believe in Him and do not disbelieve in Him. And I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, the true Object of worship, who originated all things in the difference of their essences: the simple that possesses splendor and majesty, and the composed that possesses order and perfection. Nothing among them resembles Him, and He is exalted above them in every respect. And I bear witness that the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful, the Gentle, the Generous, sent Muhammad, His chosen and pure servant, His righteous and God-fearing Messenger.

He sent him while idols were worshipped in Allah's sanctuary, while the landmarks of the upright hanifi religion were laid low, while the fire of disbelief and atheism blazed and the edifices of polytheism and idolatry stood high, and while the people had sunk deep into the wastes of misguidance and plunged blind into the dust of affliction. So he rose up for Allah, striving and contending on their behalf. He stripped away the confusion and darkness that had overshadowed them, shattered the idols and images they had been worshipping, and began to call them to the worship of the Most Merciful and to the rites of faith and Islam. He replaced the houses of fire with the Sacred House of Allah, abrogated the rule of the crosses by turning toward the Corner (al-Rukn) and the Station (al-Maqam), guided the servants of Allah in His worship onto the straight path, saved them from the claws of the seducing devil, and brought them by his blessing to the most trustworthy handhold (العروة الوثقى). He led them to the courtyards of right guidance and God-fearing, until the word of tawhid rose shining and the word of shirk was abased and darkened, until tongues spoke the clear verses, and the winds drove the ships of salvation across the seas of dispute. So the decree of Allah the Exalted came to pass in what He willed for the trial of the people of opinions and religions.

May Allah bless him with a blessing that reaches, through him, his followers in general, and the company of the callers in particular, so that He may make easy for them the way of his intercession, and fulfill for them the right of following him and obeying him, upon

> يوم لا ينفع مال ولا بنون ، إلا من أتى الله بقلب سليم

a day on which neither wealth nor children will be of any use, except for him who comes to Allah with a sound heart.

And may He bless the Axis of the Religion and the pillar of the believers, the receiver of his lights and the one who upholds the cause of his followers and his helpers, the restrainer of anger in fulfillment of the covenant of Allah the Exalted, the one patient under the humiliation of usurpation in seeking the face of Allah the Exalted: the Commander of the Believers, Ali bin Abi Talib (ع), whose deeds cannot be numbered and whose virtues cannot be counted. And may He bless his progeny, the blessed Imams, by whose lights souls are made to endure, by whose following calamities are lifted, by whose obedience hopes are attained, and by whose intercession perfection is reached. And may He bless the Imam who rules by the command of Allah, the Commander of the Believers, and his forefathers, the radiant and rightly guided, the family of Taha and Yasin (عليهم السلام).

To proceed: the most deserving matter to which the one who affirms the divine unity should turn his care and devote his resolve is a matter that leads, in the affirmation of the unity of Allah the Exalted and the knowledge of His limits, to a settled order of belief for those of certainty; that calls toward the removal of confusion and darkness from the souls of the believers; and that opens the way to the affirming of the unity of the Lord of the Worlds.

When I saw that Shaykh Abu Hatim al-Razi, may Allah have mercy on him, had undertaken to correct in the book al-Mahsul (المحصول) what he held to have been corrupted, and that Shaykh Abu Ya'qub al-Sijzi, may Allah have mercy on him, had come to the defense of the author of al-Mahsul and had stood as a witness to the soundness of his statement; and when I recognized that what the two of them disputed was not among the branches in which disagreement is permissible while the soundness of the principles remains intact; and when I found Shaykh Abu Ya'qub al-Sijzi, in some of what he brought forward, truthful in the aim he intended by his refutation, yet in some of it prejudiced against Shaykh Abu Hatim, and found some of the words of both of them issued without proper order; and when, alongside all this, there was in the book al-Mahsul, in the words of its author in the chapter on tawhid, concerning the First Intellect in particular and apart from the branches, that which it was incumbent upon Shaykh Abu Hatim to correct and to address, and which he was more entitled to treat than the long passages of branches with which he lengthened his book, and yet he left it and neglected it; and when all of this together weighed upon the people of the Guiding Da'wa, so that whenever they stood upon a point of difference they were drawn into disagreement in the paths of tawhid and into disorder in the knowledge of the limits:

I resolved to set down the sayings of each of the two. This I would set down in the book al-Islah (الإصلاح), that is, "The Reform," and that in the book al-Nusra (النصرة), "The Support," each by its own text. Then I would speak upon what necessarily follows from them, what is intended by them, and the aspect by which their meanings may rightly be attributed to their authors, so that the truth of the one may be distinguished from the other, the prejudiced and the transgressor between them may be made plain, and the meaning of what they differed over may be made clear, so that a man may hold his belief accordingly. Then I would speak upon what was neglected of correction in the book al-Mahsul, among the foundations in which disagreement is not permissible, and I would make the truth plain, seeking reward.

So I did this, and gathered all of it in this book, and named it the book al-Riyad (الرياض), "The Gardens," on the ruling between the two: the author of al-Islah and the author of al-Nusra. For in what it gathers of their sayings, and in what it sets forth, it is a division between them, a clarification of the error they persisted in and of what they neglected to correct in the book al-Mahsul, and a discharge of what is due therein, out of what we have gained of the blessings of the Awliya of Allah (عليهم السلام). I named it so because it is like gardens: a greenery pleasing to the eye, and a fruit that comes into the hands. In Allah we seek help. In bringing forth this matter I have sought to be kept from the marks of error, though we are not free of it.

The book comprises ten chapters, and together they contain one hundred and fifty-seven sub-chapters. And Allah is the Helper.

> وهو حسبنا ونعم الوكيل

He is sufficient for us, and the best Trustee.
