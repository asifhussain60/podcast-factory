# Creativity Is the Creator

If this is so, then what he has described is the intellect that emanates from the world of nature, not the First Intellect, and this confirms what we have said. For he holds that it brought forth within it the images of the worlds without those images being pointed to by their own identities before the intellect, but rather that the knowledge of the intellect falls upon them, down to his words: as for in potency, it and the images are all together. That saying of his affirms what subsists in potency, and reports of that whose existence is not in act. But the First Intellect does not subsist in act by way of potency, and it cannot be said that within it there is anything subsisting in potency; for were it so, there would have to precede it, of its own kind of existence, something subsisting in act, so as to bring it out into act. Yet it is void that anything should exist together with the First Intellect, or precede it. And once that is void, the saying of the author of al-Maḥṣūl holds true only of that which is posterior in existence, behind which there is no further existence, and whose substance is realized by knowing what preceded it in existence among the worlds. That is the second thing emanated from the world of nature, like the Speaker who possesses the images of the worlds; and the sum of these images has come to subsist in act and to be emanated in act.

In the ninth chapter of the tenth title, the author of al-Maḥṣūl said: Since the cause of the intellect was the unity of the Almighty, and unity is eternal, the intellect became eternal.

Upon this we say: By his saying he has made necessary something that precedes the First Intellect in its existence, namely the cause he named unity. But we have said that nothing at all precedes the intellect in its existence beyond God; so it is worthier of eternity, since it is impossible that anything else exist. Unity, then, is not the cause of the intellect, other than it such that it precedes the intellect; rather it is the very essence of the intellect, and the intellect is the very essence of unity, and it is the One. It is the cause and it is the effect; it is creativity and it is the Creator; it is the Perfect and the Complete; it is the Eternal and the everlasting; it is Existence and the Existent, by a single essence. The meanings these names require are merited only by relating it to that from which its existence comes, which is God the Almighty, or by relating it to what is below it, or by relating it to itself, because the essence in its existence is manifold. Sufficient proof of this is his own word in this chapter concerning creativity: the Creator, from the standpoint of the Creator, is one, and its form is the form of the intelligible. If that is so, and unity is the very essence of the intellect, then his saying that the intellect became eternal by the eternity of its cause is impossible; for the intellect is not something other than the cause such that it became eternal through it, but rather it is the cause and the cause is it. An attribute like this, that its eternity is from the eternity of its cause, holds true only of the emanated intellects that come from the world of nature, such as the Speakers (النطقاء) and the Foundations (الأسس) and their followers (عليهم السلام), whose intellects are one thing and that by which they become eternal another; for the cause of their being sanctified and eternal is those separate eternal intellects called the angels brought near, and the extension of their lights to them, and their union with them. The words of the author of al-Maḥṣūl, then, bear upon the intellects that arise in the world of nature, not upon the measure in the world of creation. And since his words and their meanings are directed toward the intellects in the abode of nature, to conceive that great and majestic limit, and the rank, under this description, is impossible.

In the tenth chapter of the tenth title, we then say: The meaning of his words, that unity is the cause of the intellect, cannot escape one of these senses: either that it stands to it in the place of its Originator, the Almighty, which he named unity; or that it stands to it in the place of prime matter; or that it stands to it in the place of form among the existents in the abode of body; or that it stands to it in the place of the end for whose sake it was brought into being, being its very self and essence. It is void that it stand to it in the place of its Originator; for if unity is the cause of the intellect, and a cause is a thing, and a thing whose existence is not by itself, then he would in effect have made God the Almighty, whom he named unity, a creator originated after having been a Creator, by making Him dependent on another in the very sense his own words convey; and that is impossible. Then, since unity is other than God, and that which is other than God is a created, originated thing, he would have made it necessary that God, whom he named unity, be a created, originated thing; and this is impossible. It is void that it stand to it in the place of prime matter among the existents, because of the impossibility that anything exist and run in existence together with God the Almighty, so that something else be found with Him, as the argument has preceded. It is void that it stand to it in the place of form among the existents by which it was found, because form cannot exist except through that which stands to it in the place of matter, and the existence of matter together with God the Almighty has been shown void, by which form would exist; and further, God the Almighty is far above being multiplied by forms, which are the mark of origination, so that He should become a creator after having been the Creator. Exalted and magnified is God above that. And it is void that it stand to it in the place of that for whose sake it was brought into being, because the intellect was not brought into being for the sake of unity; for had it been brought into being for unity, no multiplicity would have been brought into being. Yet multiplicity has been found; so it was not for the sake of unity that it was brought into being. And when these senses of his words, that unity is the cause of the intellect, are shown void and prevented from being, it is established that the essence of unity and the cause are one and the same, and its right. And since the intellect is the very essence of unity and the cause, his saying that the intellect became eternal by the eternity of its cause, while intending by that saying the First, which is the First Existent, is not sound.

In the eleventh chapter of the tenth title, the author of al-Maḥṣūl said: Since the form was made manifest within it from unity, likewise the abstracted form became eternal before it, because the intellect became eternal in its entirety, not in part.

We say: This is an attribute of something in whose potency lies that form made manifest within it, and it is impossible to say this of that majestic limit which subsists in act, not in potency. And if it is so, his saying is sound only of the emanated intellects, such as the intellects of the Speakers (النطقاء), the Foundations (الأسس), the Imams, and their followers (عليهم السلام), which came forth into intellect by their whole, which is the separate intellect called the angel brought near, surnamed the Pen, in its entirety and the eternity of their intellects. The intellects of those who follow them in truth realize this and confirm it. The author of al-Maḥṣūl says, following what he mentioned: Had the forms not been originated with eternity, they would have no subsistence and no return to the cradle of their source from which they came forth with the form; they would perish and vanish, and there would be no hope, no fear, no reward, and no punishment; for everything whose cause is eternal within it thereby becomes eternal also. That saying of his requires that the forms he mentioned, which have no subsistence and no connection with their sources except by the eternal power, are those in the world of nature, from among the souls that have no subsistence in act and gain no subsistence and permanence except through those who attained eternity and perfection. Were it not for these, reward and punishment would be void, as he mentioned; for it is by the Speaker and those who stand in his place that this is made sound. So the meaning of his words is sound only of the intellects emanated from the world of nature, below the world of creation.

In the twelfth chapter of the tenth title, the author of al-Maḥṣūl said: Since the intellect was caused by the eternity whose interpretation with us is the composite saying, the Word of God, and there was no third mediator between the two, the intellect became like it.

We say: That saying of his leads to meanings like those already mentioned, and it does not befit the First Intellect. It is not an essence of which this may be said, since it is the very essence of the Word, nor is it something other than the Word, nor something that was and then became like it. All that pertains to the intellects that go forth from potency into act in the abode of nature, which become so by accepting the effects of the Word, that is, the First Intellect, and by union with it become like it. This is confirmed by his saying: Since the form of the act became, by the Word, the form of the intellect, the intellect thereby became a cause of what is below it among things, and became, by the Word, encompassing them by what the Creator sees within it. For that whose form became, by the Word, a form for it, and which became a cause of what is below it, implies that it once was without being a cause and without its form being the form of the Word; and that which is of this rank is only the intellects of the Speakers, not that Creator-intellect originated without mediator, which was never other than the Word, nor other than the cause of the existent whose existence is first, beyond which the descriptions are cut off. Magnified and exalted is He. In what we have already said concerning this great limit there is sufficiency, without prolonging. And that being so, to run that great limit in the course of these lower limits is impossible.

In the thirteenth chapter of the tenth title, the author of al-Maḥṣūl said: Since this is so, it was named Perfect by creativity; for creativity is from the Creator, and by perfect creativity there arises only the perfect.

We say: We have already spoken of this. His saying is mistaken in two respects. The first is that he applied the name intellect to the Creator and made it like its Originator, of whom he said it is perfect, and this is association, which may not be said; for God is set apart by His own selfhood from the perfection and the perfect that exist by His originating them both. Nor is it said that the creator, by perfect creativity, is only the perfect, such that creativity itself be the perfect. The second is that he laid the name of creativity upon what belongs to the world of nature, and that is impossible; for the one who deserves the name of creativity is that whose existence precedes all things, not the thing that subsists in act or in potency. That being so, what he said may not be believed except in the sense in which it is sound.

In the fourteenth chapter of the tenth title, the author of al-Maḥṣūl said: The intellect bestows the forms only from its cause, which is the Word, just as the sun bestows upon those who kindle from its light, not from its body. And since its bringing forth of what is in it is from the power of its cause, which is the Word, and its bestowing them thus is from it, it became clear that the Word is what became the cause of the things that come forth from the intellect, not the identity of the intellect, save that the intellect became a mediator between the Word and what is below it.

We say: The intellect he mentions is the intellects of the Speakers and their followers, which kindle from their cause, which is other than they, not the First Intellect, which is the First Creator. For the First Intellect has no need of another in the existence of what comes to be from it, being itself the cause and the very Word, from which and from whose essence is the existence of what exists. For this reason we said that it is the Perfect from which the perfect came to exist, needing nothing outside its essence, being the cause of the existence of things. Were something other than it to precede it, that which precedes would have to be the First Creator, not it; and it would follow that the matter led to a disorder in the ranks of the existents, which is impossible. As for his saying that the intellect became a mediator between the Word and what is below it, we have shown the impossibility of the matter earlier, at the opening of our book.

There is no harm in adding, so we say: How can anything precede the First Intellect, so as to be a mediator between it and what is below it, when the intellect is the very essence of the Word, which is the cause existing from God the Almighty? Were it permissible for something to precede it, that precedent could not but be either of the kind of its existence, and then it is void that it precede it, since their ranks and limits are equal, being of one kind; or not of the kind of its existence, and then it is either a thing or not a thing. If it is a thing, it is an object for the apprehension of intellects, and what the intellect apprehends, the intellect is prior to, not it. And if it is not a thing, then it is not the Word, but the One transcendent above the qualities that a thing deserves in its divisions. Glory be to Him, exalted with great exaltation. In whatever way it is taken, the impossibility stands that anything precede the First Intellect; for there is nothing above the Thing except the Thing, exalted. And that being so, it is the Word, and it is the cause, and it is unity, and it is the intellect, as we have explained above.

> وحسبنا الله ونعم الوكيل

And God is sufficient for us, and He is the best Trustee.

In the fifteenth chapter of the tenth title, the author of al-Maḥṣūl said: Creativity is a mediator between the Creator and the created, the created being an effect of the influencer upon the influenced. And he said: The form of creativity is intermediate between the doer and the deed; its existence is from the side of the creative doer, and the effect of the form is found from the side of the Creator in the deed. This form, I mean creativity, came to be in the Creator, glory be to Him.

We say: This speech encloses within it the affirmation of what God did not originate, nor bring about, nor create anew, which is contrary to what the affirmers of unity hold. For by his saying he made creativity other than the Creator, and thereby made it necessary that creativity be a form in the Creator; then by his saying he made it necessary that that for which the form he named creativity is a form, and which he named an influencer within it, be fixed, uninvented, unoriginated. It is not permissible to believe this. For creativity is not something other than the Creator; for if what he conceives as Creator were other than creativity, it could not but be either that its existence is with God the Almighty or that its existence is by itself. It is void that its existence be by itself, for then it would be divided within itself while yet not ceasing to be with God the Almighty. Its existence with God the Almighty could not but be either resembling Him, exalted is God above that, and a likeness to Him, so that the two would be two likenesses, each of them specified by that through which the duality and the specification in two things occurred, which occurs only by a necessitating cause of their being so, prior to them and from which is their existence; or not resembling and not a likeness, and then it would be an opposite, and the existence of two opposites occurs only by another that preserves their existence for them, since in the existence of one of the two opposites is the annulment of the existence of the other. So their existence necessitated something prior to them that preserves their existence for them, which implies that the Creator the Almighty be created, and that there precede Him what is prior and worthier of divinity, exalted and magnified is He; and that is more strongly prevented and more impossible. And since the existence of what he made necessary cannot be free of two aspects, and both aspects being void, it is void that its existence be by itself; and once it is void that its existence be by itself, it is established that its existence is from God the Almighty. And creativity has its existence from God the Almighty; so neither of the two, creativity and the Creator, is such that one may be called creativity or Creator prior to the other. And that being so, and it being void that existence proceed from that which the existent issues from as anything but one, it is established that creativity is the Creator. And since creativity is the Creator, what the author of al-Maḥṣūl said is mistaken; so let it be avoided, if God the Almighty wills. And now we have come to what we promised at the beginning of the book.

We say: Unity and the knowledge of the limits are a hard matter, yet in them lie the life of souls, their subsistence, and their deliverance from the world of nature and its transmutations. Since it is hard, and God the Almighty knew that not all His servants attain the degree by which they reach perfection through knowing the limits of God and His unity, He singled out from among them, in every age, one man by perfection and election, such as the Imam (ع). By perfection I mean that he is complete in receiving the overflow coursing through the world of nature from the side of the angels brought near, so that he is thereby a guardian over them and one who raises them to His whole. So God the Almighty made obligatory obedience to him and love of him, that they might thereby become part of His whole, out of His mercy toward them. There is no way to this except by holding fast to the two acts of worship, and rendering the right of both in knowledge and in deed, outwardly and inwardly, and being eager to preserve them. And that is only through the remembrance of death and the parting it brings from family, child, kin, and homeland, and through certainty that there is no arrival at the return except by provision, and no provision except what is stored up, gained only by an obedience whose obligations are performed, a duty whose right is rendered, a good that is done, a righteous deed that is worked, and a knowledge of the realities of things that is acquired. May God place us and the company of the believers in the easts of the earth and its wests among those sealed with the good, and grant us and them the intercession of Muḥammad and Ali and the pure Imams, and our Imam, the Ruler by the Command of God, and his rightly guiding fathers. (عليهم السلام) in the holy land, by His grace and mercy.

With that we seal the book with praise to God, Lord of the Worlds, and prayer upon the best of the first and the last, Muḥammad and his family, the radiant and blessed, and the rightly guiding Imams. And we say:
