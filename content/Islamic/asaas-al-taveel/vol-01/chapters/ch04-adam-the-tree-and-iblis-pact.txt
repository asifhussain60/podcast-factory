We are now in the chapter on Adam, the first of the seven speaker-prophets. Al-Nu'man opens by gathering all seven together as a frame: faith and submission, which he has already explained, formed the gateway, and now he turns to the inner meaning of guardianship, because God has linked the obedience owed to the Messengers and to those in authority with obedience to Himself. He cites the Quran: "Obey God and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you" (verse fifty-nine of the chapter on women). The seven are gathered in this single duty of obedience even though their degrees and ranks differ. The pronouncement is a single matter of knowledge. They are: Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, the Prophet of Islam, and the seventh of them, the Master of the Age, the Riser at the close of the cycles. Then there follow, in their virtue, the foundations God established from among them in their lifetime, to carry out the inner meaning. The foundation rises after the Speaker-Prophet and succeeds him, becoming an Imam, and establishing for himself a proof to take his place, likewise to circulate his cycle until that Speaker-Prophet passes away. Then another Speaker-Prophet comes after him with another sacred law, and so it proceeds. The earth is never devoid of a guardian of God with His proof, even for the twinkling of an eye. It is only ever one after one, a proof of the unity of the Almighty. He cites: "If there were gods in them other than God, they would both be corrupted" (verse twenty-two of the chapter on the prophets). The companion of the age, whether prophet, messenger, or Imam, is called the Speaker because he pronounces what is apparent and carries it out. His proof is called the silent one, because he is silent from the outward. This is the reason for the twelve proofs that God mentions when He says: "And We sent forth from among them twelve chiefs" (verse twelve of the chapter on the table). The Imam and the proof are successive like day and night. The day is like the Imam; the night is like the proof and the concealment of the inner. The Quran confirms: "And We have made the night and the day two signs" (verse twelve of the chapter on the children of Israel). The verses of God, in the inner meaning, are His proofs against His creation.

The Imam is called Speaker because he speaks what the prophet used to speak, in his turn, from the apparent sacred law with which he was sent. He is called Imam because the servants follow his example. He is called the one prayed behind in congregation, because he is in front of them and they pray behind him, so he is called the leader. The Quran speaks also of leaders of misguidance, an imam who is not to be followed: "And We made them leaders inviting to the Fire" (verse forty-one of the chapter on the stories). The name of prophecy was given only to those who were connected to the upper substance without an intermediary, no lower limit between him and it. So the Imamate is one limit below the limit of prophecy, even though their obedience is equal in obligation, and is likewise coupled with obedience to God. We must first mention the origin of the Imamate in the lower limit, which is Adam. Then we will coordinate what follows and recite it little by little, as much as one can bear of this limit of speech that we say, and by God we seek help.

"And when your Lord said to the angels: 'I am placing upon the earth a successor,' they said: 'Will You place therein one who will corrupt it and shed blood, while we glorify You with praise and sanctify You?' He said: 'Indeed, I know what you do not know'" (verse thirty of the chapter on the cow). And: "When your Lord said to the angels: 'I am creating a human being from clay. So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, fall down to him in prostration'" (verses seventy-one and seventy-two of the chapter Sad). The first thing that must be mentioned in this statement is the origin of the knowledge of the saints of God. The words and speech attributed to God refer to what happens to those who hear, not as you see and hear from the speech of those who speak — sounds that arise from bodies and cavities, broken by the playthings of tongues and lips, nor like the voices that come from the friction between created bodies. God is exalted above being likened to anything He has created, infinitely high. It may be permissible in the language to call something speech or saying without it being speech with audible sound or known pronunciation. The Arabs say "the money is gone," "the wall has fallen," "the world has preached to its people and addressed them with her sermons," "time has reproached so-and-so." So He addressed them, and God knows what they know of it. A man may see in his dream what he did not see with his eyes, and he hears speech without a speaker, and the voice is heard without a voicer. It is within the power of God to do what He says without it being done in this way. He has said, in verse forty of the chapter on the Bee: "Our only word for a thing when We will it is to say to it 'Be,' and it is."

This is the first interpretive key to the entire angelic scene: the question God "asked" and the answer the angels "gave" did not pass through mouths and ears. What the verse describes is a communication between God and a select group of His guardians — not all the angels, but those twelve whom God designated when He spoke to them concerning the command of Adam. The scene is a parable of God's relation with those guardians, expressed in a language human beings can receive. Nothing less than a parable would serve, because the reality it points to — divine knowledge flowing down through ranks of beings to a chosen representative on earth — has no simpler description. One must understand, before proceeding, that every attribution of "speech" or "saying" to God in this narrative belongs to this same category.

When God wanted to create Adam, the second creation — which is the creation of knowledge, as the Almighty said, "Then We brought him forth as another creation" (verse fourteen of the chapter on the believers), and "He forms you in the wombs of your mothers, creation after creation" (verse six of the chapter on the throngs) — and to appoint him as an Imam, He delegated from among the angels twelve kings to establish him for them as leader. They take from him and report him to the people of the earth. When He knew that He would create him from clay, and clay, in the inner sense, is the outward, the dense bodily knowledge, He created the angels in the creation of the gentle and subtle spiritual knowledge. They had already been taught that gentle knowledge is not connected to matter, and that dense knowledge comes only by teaching. They were not tested by deeds. They already had the knowledge that the one who is charged must be humbled and held short.

Here is what the angels knew, and why their objection carries its own weight. They had been formed entirely within subtle, spiritual knowledge — knowledge that does not bind itself to the material world, knowledge that carries no obligation of outward action. Their praise and sanctification of God came naturally to them, without the burden of law or the risk of error that comes with embodied life. When they heard that Adam would be fashioned from clay — from that dense, argumentative, outward form of knowledge — they understood immediately what that meant: the one created within that kind of knowledge would fall short, would make mistakes, would at times corrupt and shed blood. They were not wrong in their assessment of clay-knowledge. What they did not know, and what God's response pointed to, was that He would provide Adam with the same divine support He had granted them, and that if Adam fell short or erred, God would rescue him from destruction. The angels' objection was not malicious; it was a failure of imagination, a failure to see how the dense and the subtle might be joined in one being, and what a being so joined might become.

They said: "Will You place therein one who will corrupt it and shed blood, while we glorify You with praise and sanctify You?" And He said: "I know what you do not know" (verse thirty of the chapter on the cow).

The Father of Imams confirms the same in his opening sermon of The Peak of Eloquence (sermon one, translator Sayed Ali Reza): God gathered clay from the earth, formed it into a shape with limbs and joints, held it firm until it dried, and then breathed into it of His spirit.

What the Almighty meant is that Adam was permitted whatever the angels had been permitted, and was warned against the same things, lest he fall short, err, or be ruined. Satan was among them, then still a high angel. He saw himself as better than Adam. He claimed for himself Adam's rank, refused to be his follower, and grew arrogant before the command of his Lord. He wanted God to see the angels in their incapacity, so He created Adam, as He said, from clay. That is, He began with apparent knowledge, the outward, as is the established custom He set down for His servants. Adam remained in that state for a long time, knowing nothing other than what he had learned. What the tradition mentions is that when God formed his image from clay, it remained a lifeless body for a long stretch before the spirit was breathed into it, meaning the inner, spiritual knowledge. Satan, in that interval, would come to him and speak to him of knowledge that Adam did not yet possess. He found in him only the apparent knowledge, in which Adam took delight, and Satan reported back that Adam was hollow, that is, empty of any inner or esoteric content. This was before the angels, and Satan among them, were commanded to obey him. The Almighty had said to them: "When I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, then fall down to him in prostration" (verse twenty-nine of the chapter on the rocky tract). That is, submit to him in obedience once I have endowed him with spiritual knowledge. At that, the adversary among them turned against him, out of the enmity he harbored, the envy that had taken hold of him, and the arrogance he felt toward him. He saw himself as better than Adam and had taken pleasure in what he had observed of Adam's lagging behind in inner knowledge, until now he was filled with regret and pride.

When God willed to bring Satan low, He extended Adam's reach and taught him with that inner knowledge. As the Almighty said: "And He taught Adam the names, all of them" (verse thirty-one of the chapter on the cow). That is, the names of every thing that human beings need to know, since each thing has a name. He taught him this and then summoned the angels and began questioning them about what He had taught Adam. They had nothing to answer, and said: "Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Indeed, it is You who is the All-Knowing, the All-Wise" (verse thirty-two of the chapter on the cow). He said: "O Adam, inform them of their names" (verse thirty-three of the chapter on the cow). And when he had informed them, He said: "Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and I know what you reveal and what you have been concealing?" (verse thirty-three of the chapter on the cow). The fifth Imam, Ja'far al-Sadiq, explains what the angels were concealing. In a tradition from the Father of Imams, what they revealed was their objection about corruption and bloodshed; what they concealed was a thought within themselves, that God would not create any creation more honorable than they were. When they recognized their disobedience, they took refuge at the Throne and circled it seeking their Lord's pleasure, and He commanded them to descend and build a house on earth as a place of pilgrimage for the children of Adam.

Then God commanded the angels to prostrate to Adam, that is, He commanded them to obey him, to call upon him, and to accept him. "Do you not see that to God prostrates whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on the earth, and the sun, the moon, the stars, the mountains, the trees, the animals, and many of mankind, and many are deserving of punishment?" (verse eighteen of the chapter on the pilgrimage). And they prostrated, as the Almighty said: "Except him: he was arrogant and became one of the disbelievers" (verse thirty-four of the chapter on the cow). The people of the language hold that the word for Satan follows the pattern of words meaning ruined or perished: this angel was called Satan once he was destroyed by the limit at which he had become the enemy. It is also said that Satan is the one who is cut off and has no proof. All these conditions were gathered in him. God spoke: "And the Hour is their appointed time, and the Hour shall be more calamitous and more bitter" (verse forty-six of the chapter on the moon).

"What prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you?" He said: "I am better than him. You created me from fire, and You created him from clay" (verse twelve of the chapter on the heights). The argument he was making was this: You raised me up in spiritual cultivation and granted me brilliant supportive knowledge — the kind that carries no dense body with it, knowledge that flows like fire, luminous and without weight — and You created Adam with a thick, heavy, corporeal knowledge, knowledge that must be acquired through teaching and argument, knowledge that is bound to matter. How should I prostrate to one whom I am better than? This is what pride built from knowledge always produces. The one who possesses the more subtle or elevated kind of knowledge — the kind that feels untethered from the heavy world — can come to believe that his elevation is his own achievement, his nature's verdict, rather than a gift given to serve a purpose. Satan's fire-knowledge was no less a gift than Adam's clay-knowledge. But he had forgotten that, and so his objection was not a judgment about kinds of knowledge at all; it was a declaration that he had placed himself above the command. In this way he exposed what he had been concealing. God cursed him, that is, expelled him, saying: "Get out of it: you are accursed, and indeed upon you is My curse until the Day of Recompense" (verses seventy-seven and seventy-eight of the chapter Sad). So He brought him out of the bounds of supportive knowledge, cut him off from it, expelled him from it, and cursed him. Curse, in the language, is expulsion. The limit that the adversary had occupied, the limit of being the proof of Adam, is now empty.

God created Eve from Adam, because He said: "And from it He created its mate" (verse one of the chapter on women). But the creation here is the creation of support, not a physical creation. God took her up, supported her, taught her, joined her to Adam in marriage, and made her his proof, compensating Adam with her in place of the expelled one. It is not, as the common folk claim, that God cast Adam into sleep and made Eve from one of his ribs. They heard a fragment of the interpretation which they did not grasp. Rather, God made Eve one of the twelve chiefs of inner knowledge. The "rib" they heard about in a fragment of the tradition points to this: what was removed was the rank that had been held by Satan, one position in the twelve, and Eve was placed into that vacancy. Her counterparts in the apparent creation of the human being are the twelve ribs on each side of the body — the right resembles the inner knowledge, the left the apparent. The human body itself is a parable: its bilateral symmetry of twelve and twelve mirrors the two registers of knowledge, inner and outer, each with its twelve holders. Eve did not come from Adam's body; she came from his rank, filling the place that had been vacated by the one who fell.

Her role as proof was not ornamental. The proof, in the structure of each prophetic cycle, is the one who holds the inner meaning in trust, who nurses and raises those who enter the call from within. The Speaker-Prophet is like a father; the proof is like a mother, because the proof carries the initiates of the inner knowledge, feeds them with it, and raises them into it. This is why the Messenger of God said to the Father of Imams: "I and you, O Ali, are the two fathers of this nation." What was once a single position held by the adversary, used by him in his pride as a vantage from which to judge Adam wanting, was now held by Eve, in service rather than in judgment, in support rather than in envy.

Then God settled Adam and his wife in the Garden, which inwardly is the ranks of the messengers of support, and beneath them the chiefs. He made Paradise a glad tiding for the believers. The Almighty said: "Their reward is with their Lord, gardens of eternity beneath which rivers flow" (verse eight of the chapter on the clear evidence). Let no one be misled into thinking that when we mention the inner meaning of Paradise we deny that there is an eternal Paradise, or that a subtle inner interpretation annuls outer reality, we seek refuge in God from that. He does nothing except that it has an inner aspect, and we do not see a soul rising in a human being except within a body, nor an inner reality except within an outer one, if it were not for the outer, the name inner would not even apply. Many among the people of this knowledge have perished at this very point. It was for this same reason that Adam fell into sin, as we shall mention. The Almighty said: "And forsake the outwardness of sin and its inwardness" (verse one hundred and twenty of the chapter on the cattle), and: "He has lavished upon you His bounties, outward and inward" (verse twenty of the chapter Luqman). Everything has an outer and an inner.

"And these parables We strike for the people, but none understand them except those who know" (verse forty-three of the chapter on the spider). The way of these parables is not the outer-and-inner symbolism in which both sides are real, established things; rather, the obligatory thing is what is represented, as in "Guide us to the straight path" (verse six of the opening chapter). The path, in the language, is the road; the Imam stands in the place of the road. What is meant by road here is the Imam, not a road taken on the ground.

Returning to Adam and the inner meaning of the Garden, which is supportive knowledge with its true, pure, spiritual content, for the bliss in it surpasses every other bliss, and the pleasure in it exceeds every other pleasure. For this reason, whoever attains it renounces all worldly attachments; they shrivel in his eye, every thing in this world is diminished in his sight, and he comes to make light of his attachment to anything but that knowledge. His union with it he finds to be the perfect bliss and the glory that knows no humiliation thereafter, as in the tradition of the Father of Imams to his companion Kumayl, preserved in The Peak of Eloquence (saying one hundred forty-seven): "Knowledge is the ruler, and wealth is the ruled. Wealth runs out as it is spent, while knowledge purifies as it is spent. Wealth's storage decays, but the scholars endure as long as time itself endures. Their persons are absent, yet their traces remain in the hearts."

The Almighty said: "O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in the Garden, and eat freely from it wherever you wish, but do not approach this tree, or you will be among the wrongdoers" (verse thirty-five of the chapter on the cow). He warned him against the adversary: "Indeed, this is an enemy to you and to your wife, so let him not drive you both out of the Garden so that you become miserable" (verse one hundred and seventeen of the chapter Ta-Ha). And He entrusted this to him: "We had already taken a covenant with Adam before, but he forgot, and We found no firm resolve in him" (verse one hundred and fifteen of the chapter Ta-Ha). So He permitted to them the ranks of the messengers in accordance with their conditions and the duties He had laid upon them, which include declaring lawful what is lawful, declaring forbidden what is forbidden, warding against what is forbidden, and warning of it. And the tree which He forbade Adam to approach, prevented him from, and made unlawful for him, He made a likeness for the Master of the Resurrection — the Awaited Riser, the Master of the Age — within whose limits the obligations of charge lie, and before whose appearance they do not yet apply. The Almighty said: "No soul will be benefited by its faith if it had not believed before, or earned good through its faith. Say: Wait, indeed we too are waiting" (verse one hundred and fifty-eight of the chapter on the cattle). From such a soul no recompense will be accepted, no punishment exacted, and no intercession received.

The forbidden tree is the disclosure that has been entrusted to the Master of the Resurrection alone. The inner will be stripped away at his coming, the outer will fall, and the inner will become apparent. The Master of the Resurrection stands beyond the bounds of the messengers who preceded him, because the inner knowledge entrusted within their ranks is paid out to their proofs, concealed from them, carried from one to the next, a deposit and a knowledge by which he is illuminated until it reaches its rightful holder, namely the Master of the Resurrection, who will then make it openly manifest and strip away its outer covering. That manifest disclosure was forbidden and unlawful before him. So God forbade Adam at the outset of the matter, so that the unalterable custom of God might be carried out upon him. And the adversary asked for respite until the appointed Day, meaning the Day of Resurrection. He said: "Grant me respite until the Day they are resurrected" (verse thirty-six of the chapter on the rocky tract), so that I may have a little reach into his offspring.

Why is the tree sealed? Not because the knowledge it represents is harmful, but because disclosure before the appointed moment breaks the structure of the cycle. Every prophetic era has its own register of knowledge, guarded by its proofs, passed in trust from one holder to the next. The inner meaning does not belong to any one era to make openly manifest; it belongs to the Riser, the last in the sequence, the one who completes the cycle. To disclose it early — to reach for it before the time — is not merely a breach of protocol; it is a structural rupture in the unfolding of the sacred covenant. The tree stands precisely for that premature reaching. It is the shape that forbidden timing takes.

In Kulayni's The Sufficient, Book of Divine Unity, chapter on the secrets and divine wisdom (a hadith transmitted through Muhammad ibn Yahya from Ahmad ibn Muhammad), the fifth Imam, Ja'far al-Sadiq, is asked about the tree that Adam was forbidden to approach, and he answers that it was the tree of the knowledge of the family of the Prophet, knowledge that God preserved for them and did not authorize for any other.

He said: "Go, and whomever of them you can rouse with your voice, meaning by stirring them to remember you, and assault them with your cavalry and your foot, and become a partner with them in wealth and offspring" (verse sixty-four of the chapter on the children of Israel). That is, a partner with them in the knowledge of wealth, if your doubt and falsehood enter them. He means the children born through faith, because they are like the people of the call in the inner sense. Concerning His children, the Almighty said: "It is the religion of your father Abraham. It is He who named you submitters" (verse seventy-eight of the chapter on the pilgrimage). The one who calls the believers is like a father, and the proof is like a mother, because she carries them, nurses them, and raises them with knowledge. Therefore the Messenger of God said to the Father of Imams: "I and you are the two fathers of this nation." So the adversary wished to share with them in those sons whom he could transfer from the birth of faith into the birth of disbelief, having promised them his arrogance and his falsehood. And God made it a trial for them.

Then He told them about His servants who are loyal to Him, that the adversary does not prevail over them, that He returns them to Himself in full, and that they are of His party and His sons. The Almighty said: "Indeed, over My servants you will have no authority" (verse forty-two of the chapter on the rocky tract). He made for Himself a party from among them.

The tree is not wheat. The tree is not fig. The tree is not grape. The tree is the disclosure that belongs to the Master of the Resurrection, the manifest unveiling of the inner that has not yet been authorized, and Adam transgressed it not because he was greedy for a fruit but because, prompted by the adversary, he reached for a rank that was not his to occupy. Adam's transgression was not a failure of character in the ordinary sense — it was not greed, nor disobedience born of contempt. It was a misstep in timing, a reaching toward something real and eventually authorized, but not yet. The adversary swore to them that the limit forbidden them was forbidden only so they should not become kings or among the immortals, and he swore: "Indeed I am to you among the sincere advisors" (verse twenty-one of the chapter on the heights). He made clear, in his lying advice, that if they were to leave the obligation of restraint they would end up among the gentle spiritual angels and be stripped of the dense material world, two kings eternal in what they desired. The adversary's lie had a particular shape: he told them that the obligations of their station were the source of their heaviness and limitation, and that crossing the boundary into the Riser's domain would liberate them into a purely spiritual existence. He dressed premature disclosure as transcendence. They transgressed the limits of speech permitted to them, into the limit belonging to the Master of the Resurrection, and extended their hands to what was not theirs. So they ate from the tree, and that which supported them was cut off. That support is the garment of piety which God mentioned: "And that is from the signs of God" (verse twenty-six of the chapter on the heights).

Adam was stripped bare of it, and there appeared to them what was reprehensible, the intensity of the natural image, because of the nakedness of the garment of piety, which is the word, by which they saw, by its light, all the darkness in which they were now compounded. For support does not accompany sins, and extending one's hand to what does not belong to one is, by ruling, obligatory to cut off. When the support for it ceased, as the Almighty said: "They began to stitch upon themselves leaves from Paradise" (verse twenty-two of the chapter on the heights), they joined together what remained to them of the light of wisdom in order to cover some of the shame that had appeared in them. What was revealed about their condition was the disappearance of support, and through ignorance many of those attached to this matter perished, and it became permitted.

"Did I not forbid you that tree and tell you that the adversary is to you a clear enemy? They said: 'Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves; if You do not forgive us and have mercy on us, we will surely be among the losers'" (verses twenty-two and twenty-three of the chapter on the heights). Adam did not turn aside as the adversary turned aside; rather he repented to God and prayed to Him, asking for His mercy and forgiveness. "Then Adam received from his Lord words, so He turned to him in forgiveness; indeed He is the Accepter of Repentance, the Merciful" (verse thirty-seven of the chapter on the cow). This means that he drew close to Him and found Him repenting from the side of the higher limits, so He turned to him in mercy. The adversary had no authority over him to drag him to himself and take possession of him, so that he might become of his party, rather, he was of the Party of God. The Almighty turned to him in mercy, forgave him, and received his words. Likewise those of his descendants who did evil and then repented from it, He turned to them in forgiveness, and they were of His party. But whoever persisted in his sin and did not repent of it, and to whom no words came from his Lord, was of the party of the adversary and his soldiers. The Almighty said: "If, when they wronged themselves, they had come to you and asked forgiveness of God, and the Messenger had asked forgiveness for them, they would have found God Accepting of Repentance and Merciful" (verse sixty-four of the chapter on women). The repentance of the repentant is not accepted except by His receiving His words for them, and they are not forgiven until after the seeking of forgiveness for them and the seeking by the words on their behalf.

The tree was the limit of the Master of the Resurrection. The transgression was to seize a disclosure that was not yet authorized. The fall stripped the garment of piety, that is, the word, the support of the supportive knowledge. And the words that returned were the words of repentance, by which Adam re-entered the line of those whom the Imamate could pass through. He had stumbled, but he had not turned aside. He had stretched out his hand to the limit of the Master of the Resurrection, but he had then asked forgiveness through the guardian of his own time, which, after the expulsion of the adversary, was the rank that had been filled by Eve. Adam was forgiven, and the line of the Imamate that begins with him was preserved. The first proof became the first enemy; a replacement proof was supplied; a sealed disclosure was placed under guard until the appointed Day; a pact was extracted by the expelled one; and a repentance, addressed through the guardian of the time, returned the first caliph to the rank from which he had fallen.
