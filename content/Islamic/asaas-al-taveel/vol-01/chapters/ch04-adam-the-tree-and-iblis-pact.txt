The author now turns to his interpretation of the prophetic cycles themselves. Having already laid out, in his treatment of the four limits of the testimony, that the testimony of faith has four words and four limits, two above and two below, and that the architecture extends outward into the seven Speaker-Prophets, the seven Imams per cycle, the twelve successors, and the nineteen entrusted ones, he now begins to walk that architecture through history, starting where the lower limit of the Imamate itself begins, with Adam, peace be upon him. The author moves from the Quranic scene of the angels' objection, through the formation of Adam from clay and the long pause before the spirit is breathed into him, through Satan's refusal to prostrate and his transformation from a high angel into the adversary, through the creation of Eve as Adam's proof in place of Satan, through the inner meaning of the Garden as the ranks of supportive knowledge, and finally through the forbidden tree, which is not a tree of wheat or fig but a likeness for the Master of the Resurrection and the inner disclosure entrusted to him alone. The author closes with the famous pact that Satan extracts after his expulsion, the request for respite, the boast that he will share with Adam's children in their wealth and offspring, and with Adam's repentance, which restores him to the rank of caliph and Imam even after the fall. Throughout, the author is doing one thing: he is showing that the Quranic story of Adam, read on its inner side, is the story of how the Imamate enters the world, of how a foundation is supplied for it when its first proof becomes its first enemy, and of how the testimony to that hidden order survives even the breach that opens within it. He will go on to show why, for the author, the first lesson of prophecy is not creation in the abstract but the way support is taught, withdrawn, and restored.

He begins by gathering the seven. We have already mentioned, he says, the interpretation of faith and submission, which is the opening of the chapters of the book *The Pillars of Islam*, the book in which we have undertaken to simplify the interpretation of what it contains. The door of guardianship is recited for the Messengers and for the first of those in authority whose obedience God has joined to obedience to Himself. Quranic verses in this chapter are given in the author's working rendering of the Arabic unless a specific translator is named. He cites the Quran: "Obey God and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you" (Quran 4:59). We have gathered, he says, the interpretation of what is said about them, for God has gathered them together in the duty of obedience, even if their degrees and ranks differ. The pronouncement is a single matter of knowledge. They are: Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, the Prophet of Islam, and the seventh of them, the Master of the Age, the Riser at the close of the cycles. Then there follow, in their virtue, the foundations God established from among them in their lifetime, to carry out the inner meaning. The foundation rises after the Speaker-Prophet and succeeds him, becoming an Imam, and establishing for himself a proof to take his place, likewise to circulate his cycle until that Speaker-Prophet passes away. Then another Speaker-Prophet comes after him with another sacred law, and so it proceeds. The earth, he insists, is never devoid of a guardian of God with His proof, even for the twinkling of an eye. It is only ever one after one, a proof of the unity of the Almighty. He cites: "If there were gods in them other than God, they would both be corrupted" (Quran 21:22). The companion of the age, whether prophet, messenger, or Imam, is called the Speaker because he pronounces what is apparent and carries it out. His proof is called the silent one, because he is silent from the outward. This, he reminds us, is the reason for the twelve proofs that God mentions when He says: "And We sent forth from among them twelve chiefs" (Quran 5:12). The Imam and the proof, he adds, are successive like day and night. The day is like the Imam; the night is like the proof and the concealment of the inner. The Quran confirms: "And We have made the night and the day two signs" (Quran 17:12). The verses of God, in the inner meaning, are His proofs against His creation.

The author's framing here sits on an older doctrine in the early call literature: that the proof and the Speaker together are one structural piece, never one without the other, and that the seven Speakers form a single architecture stretched across time.

He pauses on the way the name of the Imam and the name of the Speaker pass into each other. The Imam is called Speaker because he speaks what the prophet used to speak, in his turn, from the apparent sacred law with which he was sent. He is called Imam because the servants follow his example. He is called the one prayed behind in congregation, because he is in front of them and they pray behind him, so he is called the leader. The Quran speaks also of leaders of misguidance, an imam who is not to be followed: "And We made them leaders inviting to the Fire" (Quran 28:41). The name of prophecy was given only to those who were connected to the upper substance without an intermediary, no lower limit between him and it. So the Imamate is one limit below the limit of prophecy, even though their obedience is equal in obligation, and is likewise coupled with obedience to God. We must first, he says, mention the origin of the Imamate in the lower limit, which is Adam. Then we will coordinate what follows and recite it little by little, as much as one can bear of this limit of speech that we say, and by God we seek help.

He begins from the Quranic scene. "And when your Lord said to the angels: 'I am placing upon the earth a successor,' they said: 'Will You place therein one who will corrupt it and shed blood, while we glorify You with praise and sanctify You?' He said: 'Indeed, I know what you do not know'" (Quran 2:30). And: "When your Lord said to the angels: 'I am creating a human being from clay. So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, fall down to him in prostration'" (Quran 38:71-72). The first thing that must be mentioned in this statement, the author says, is the origin of the knowledge of the saints of God. The words and speech attributed to God refer to what happens to those who hear, not as you see and hear from the speech of those who speak, sounds that arise about the bodies and cavities, broken by the playthings of tongues and lips, nor like the voices that come from the friction between created bodies. God is exalted above being likened to anything He has created, infinitely high. It may be permissible in the language to call something speech or saying without it being speech with audible sound or known pronunciation. The Arabs say "the money is gone," "the wall has fallen," "the world has preached to its people and addressed them with her sermons," "time has reproached so-and-so." So He addressed them, and God knows what they know of it. A man may see in his dream what he did not see with his eyes, and he hears speech without a speaker, and the voice is heard without a voicer. It is within the power of God to do what He says without it being done in this way. He has said, in a verse Yusuf Ali renders as "Verily, when We intend a thing, Our only word is, Be, and it is," from the chapter on the Bee: "Our only word for a thing when We will it is to say to it 'Be,' and it is" (Quran 16:40).

The angelic scene, the author explains, is one of these parables of God's relation with His angelic guardians, not all the angels, but those whom God mentioned when He spoke to them concerning the command of Adam. When God wanted to create Adam, the second creation, which is the creation of knowledge, as the Almighty said,"Then We brought him forth as another creation" (Quran 23:14), and "He forms you in the wombs of your mothers, creation after creation" (Quran 39:6), and to appoint him as an Imam, He delegated from among the angels twelve kings to establish him for them as leader. They take from him and report him to the people of the earth. When He knew that He would create him from clay, and clay, in the inner sense, is the outward, the dense bodily knowledge, He created the angels in the creation of the gentle and subtle spiritual knowledge. They had already been taught that gentle knowledge is not connected to matter, and that dense knowledge comes only by teaching. They were not tested by deeds. They already had the knowledge that the one who is charged must be humbled and held short. They said: "Will You place therein one who will corrupt it and shed blood, while we glorify You with praise and sanctify You?" And He said: "I know what you do not know" (Quran 2:30).

The Father of Imams enters the same scene in his opening sermon in *The Peak of Eloquence* (sermon one, translator Sayed Ali Reza), telling the assembly that God gathered clay from the earth, formed it into a shape with limbs and joints, held it firm until it dried, and then breathed into it of His spirit, the same long pause the author leans on when he speaks of the body lying as clay before the spirit is breathed in.

What the Almighty meant, the author says, is that Adam was permitted whatever the angels had been permitted, and was warned against the same things, lest he fall short, err, or be ruined. Satan was among them, then still a high angel. He saw himself as better than Adam. He claimed for himself Adam's rank, refused to be his follower, and grew arrogant before the command of his Lord. He wanted God to see the angels in their incapacity, so He created Adam, as He said, from clay. That is, He began with apparent knowledge, the outward, as is the established custom He set down for His servants. Adam remained in that state for a long time, knowing nothing other than what he had learned. This, the author reminds the listener, is what the tradition mentions: that when God formed his image from clay, it remained a lifeless body for a long stretch before the spirit was breathed into it, meaning the inner, spiritual knowledge. Satan, in that interval, would come to him and speak to him of knowledge that Adam did not yet possess. He found in him only the apparent knowledge, in which Adam took delight, and Satan reported back that Adam was hollow, that is, empty of any inner or esoteric content. This was before the angels, and Satan among them, were commanded to obey him. The Almighty had said to them: "When I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, then fall down to him in prostration" (Quran 15:29). That is, submit to him in obedience once I have endowed him with spiritual knowledge. At that, the adversary among them turned against him, out of the enmity he harbored, the envy that had taken hold of him, and the arrogance he felt toward him. He saw himself as better than Adam and had taken pleasure in what he had observed of Adam's lagging behind in inner knowledge, until now he was filled with regret and pride.

Then comes the reversal. When God willed to bring Satan low, He extended Adam's reach and taught him with that inner knowledge. As the Almighty said: "And He taught Adam the names, all of them" (Quran 2:31). That is, the names of every thing that human beings need to know, since each thing has a name. He taught him this and then summoned the angels and began questioning them about what He had taught Adam. They had nothing to answer, and said: "Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Indeed, it is You who is the All-Knowing, the All-Wise" (Quran 2:32). He said: "O Adam, inform them of their names" (Quran 2:33). And when he had informed them, He said: "Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and I know what you reveal and what you have been concealing?" (Quran 2:33). The author pauses on the closing phrase and cites the fifth Imam on what exactly the angels were concealing. In a tradition from the Father of Imams, what they revealed was their objection about corruption and bloodshed; what they concealed was a thought within themselves, that God would not create any creation more honorable than they were. When they recognized their disobedience, they took refuge at the Throne and circled it seeking their Lord's pleasure, and He commanded them to descend and build a house on earth as a place of pilgrimage for the children of Adam. The story is long, the author notes, and he has taken only what is needed.

Then God commanded the angels to prostrate to Adam, that is, He commanded them to obey him, to call upon him, and to accept him. "Do you not see that to God prostrates whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on the earth, and the sun, the moon, the stars, the mountains, the trees, the animals, and many of mankind, and many are deserving of punishment?" (Quran 22:18). And they prostrated, as the Almighty said: "Except him: he was arrogant and became one of the disbelievers" (Quran 2:34). The author pauses to explain why the adversary is called Satan only after this point. The people of the language hold that the word for Satan follows the pattern of words meaning ruined or perished: this angel was called Satan once he was destroyed by the limit at which he had become the enemy. It is also said that Satan is the one who is cut off and has no proof. All these conditions were gathered in him. God spoke: "And the Hour is their appointed time, and the Hour shall be more calamitous and more bitter" (Quran 54:46).

The Almighty said: "What prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you?" He said: "I am better than him. You created me from fire, and You created him from clay" (Quran 7:12). That is, and here the author translates the adversary's claim into the doctrine of the chapter, You raised me up in spiritual cultivation and granted me brilliant supportive knowledge carrying no dense body with it, and You created Adam with a thick, heavy, corporeal knowledge, so how should I prostrate to one whom I am better than? In this way he exposed what he had been concealing. God cursed him, that is, expelled him, saying: "Get out of it: you are accursed, and indeed upon you is My curse until the Day of Recompense" (Quran 38:77-78). So He brought him out of the bounds of supportive knowledge, cut him off from it, expelled him from it, and cursed him. Curse, in the language, is expulsion. The limit that the adversary had occupied, the limit of being the proof of Adam, is now empty.

What the author does next is the structural turn that gives the chapter its inner shape. God created Eve from Adam, because He said: "And from it He created its mate" (Quran 4:1). But the creation here, the author insists, is the creation of support, not a physical creation. God took her up, supported her, taught her, joined her to Adam in marriage, and made her his proof, compensating Adam with her in place of the expelled one. It is not, as the common folk claim, that God cast Adam into sleep and made Eve from one of his ribs. They heard a fragment of the interpretation which they did not grasp. Rather, God made Eve one of the twelve chiefs of inner knowledge. Their likeness, in the apparent creation of the human being, is the twelve ribs on each side of the body: the right resembles the inner knowledge, the left the apparent.

This is one of the chapter's central doctrinal moves, and the author states it plainly. Eve is not a wife in only the household sense. She is the replacement proof. She is, on the inner reading, the first of the twelve chiefs of the inner knowledge, supplied to Adam to fill the limit that the adversary vacated when he was expelled from the rank of support. The garden into which God settles them is, accordingly, the garden of that support itself. He says it directly. Then God settled Adam and his wife in the Garden, which inwardly is the ranks of the messengers of support, and beneath them the chiefs. Our saying this, the author hastens to clarify, is not a denial of the outer reality, namely that the Garden is itself a created entity, because the Almighty has said: "And of every thing We created pairs, that perhaps you may remember" (Quran 51:49). Paradise is among the greatest of the things God has created. The Almighty has said: "On the Day God will not disgrace the Prophet and those who believed with him: their light will proceed before them and on their right hands" (Quran 66:8). And: "Give glad tidings to those who believe and do righteous deeds that for them are gardens beneath which rivers flow" (Quran 2:25). And: "And for him who fears the standing before his Lord are two gardens" (Quran 55:46).

So He made Paradise a glad tiding for the believers, the author says, and He said: "Their reward is with their Lord, gardens of eternity beneath which rivers flow" (Quran 98:8). Let the listener not be misled, he warns, into thinking that when we mention the inner meaning of Paradise we deny that there is an eternal Paradise, or that a subtle inner interpretation annuls outer reality, we seek refuge in God from that. He does nothing except that it has an inner aspect, and we do not see a soul rising in a human being except within a body, nor an inner reality except within an outer one, if it were not for the outer, the name inner would not even apply. Many among the people of this knowledge have perished at this very point. It was for this same reason that Adam fell into sin, as we shall mention. The Almighty said: "And forsake the outwardness of sin and its inwardness" (Quran 6:120), and: "He has lavished upon you His bounties, outward and inward" (Quran 31:20). Everything has an outer and an inner.

He pauses briefly on how Quranic parables work, because the same logic governs the tree we are about to meet. "And these parables We strike for the people, but none understand them except those who know" (Quran 29:43). The way of these parables is not the outer-and-inner symbolism in which both sides are real, established things; rather, the obligatory thing is what is represented, as in "Guide us to the straight path" (Quran 1:6). The path, in the language, is the road; the Imam stands in the place of the road. What is meant by road here is the Imam, not a road taken on the ground.

Let us return, he says, to the mention of Adam and to the inner meaning of the Garden, which is supportive knowledge with its true, pure, spiritual content, for the bliss in it surpasses every other bliss, and the pleasure in it exceeds every other pleasure. For this reason, whoever attains it renounces all worldly attachments; they shrivel in his eye, every thing in this world is diminished in his sight, and he comes to make light of his attachment to anything but that knowledge. His union with it he finds to be the perfect bliss and the glory that knows no humiliation thereafter, as in the tradition of the Father of Imams to his companion Kumayl, preserved in the canonical collection *The Peak of Eloquence* (saying one hundred forty-seven): "Knowledge is the ruler, and wealth is the ruled. Wealth runs out as it is spent, while knowledge purifies as it is spent. Wealth's storage decays, but the scholars endure as long as time itself endures. Their persons are absent, yet their traces remain in the hearts." It is a long tradition, of which the author has mentioned only what is needed. The canonical attribution is firm: the saying is the Father of Imams' to his trusted companion, not a free-floating proverb.

Now the tree. The Almighty said: "O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in the Garden, and eat freely from it wherever you wish, but do not approach this tree, or you will be among the wrongdoers" (Quran 2:35). He warned him against the adversary: "Indeed, this is an enemy to you and to your wife, so let him not drive you both out of the Garden so that you become miserable" (Quran 20:117). And He entrusted this to him: "We had already taken a covenant with Adam before, but he forgot, and We found no firm resolve in him" (Quran 20:115). So He permitted to them, the author says, the ranks of the messengers in accordance with their conditions and the duties He had laid upon them, which include declaring lawful what is lawful, declaring forbidden what is forbidden, warding against what is forbidden, and warning of it. And the tree which He forbade Adam to approach, prevented him from, and made unlawful for him, He made a likeness for the Master of the Time, the Possessor of the Resurrection, within whose limits the obligations of charge lie, and before whose appearance they do not yet apply. The Almighty said: "No soul will be benefited by its faith if it had not believed before, or earned good through its faith. Say: Wait, indeed we too are waiting" (Quran 6:158). From such a soul no recompense will be accepted, no punishment exacted, and no intercession received.

This is the doctrinal core of the chapter, and the author wants the listener to feel its weight. The forbidden tree is the disclosure that has been entrusted to the Master of the Resurrection alone. The inner will be stripped away at his coming, the outer will fall, and the inner will become apparent. The Master of the Resurrection stands beyond the bounds of the messengers who preceded him, because the inner knowledge entrusted within their ranks is paid out to their proofs, concealed from them, carried from one to the next, a deposit and a knowledge by which he is illuminated until it reaches its rightful holder, namely the Master of the Day of Resurrection, who will then make it openly manifest and strip away its outer covering. That manifest disclosure was forbidden and unlawful before him. So God forbade Adam at the outset of the matter, so that the unalterable custom of God might be carried out upon him. And the adversary asked for respite until the appointed Day, meaning the Day of Resurrection. He said: "Grant me respite until the Day they are resurrected" (Quran 15:36), so that I may have a little reach into his offspring.

The same reading of the tree is preserved in the wider canonical tradition literature. In Kulayni's *The Sufficient*, Book of Divine Unity, chapter on the secrets and divine wisdom (hadith from the fifth Imam transmitted through Muhammad ibn Yahya from Ahmad ibn Muhammad), the fifth Imam is asked about the tree that Adam was forbidden to approach, and he answers that it was the tree of the knowledge of the family of the Prophet, knowledge that God preserved for them and did not authorize for any other. The author's framing of the tree as the likeness of the Master of the Resurrection is the early call tradition's distinctive shaping of that same hadith, a reading taken further, but not invented, by the foundational teachers of the call.

Then comes the pact itself, the one piece of the chapter that the listener will likely know in some form already, and the author reads it through the doctrine of the two parties. He said: "Go, and whomever of them you can rouse with your voice, meaning by stirring them to remember you, and assault them with your cavalry and your foot, and become a partner with them in wealth and offspring" (Quran 17:64). That is, the author says, a partner with them in the knowledge of wealth, if your doubt and falsehood enter them. He means the children born through faith, because they are like the people of the call in the inner sense. Concerning His children, the Almighty said: "It is the religion of your father Abraham. It is He who named you submitters" (Quran 22:78). The one who calls the believers is like a father, and the proof is like a mother, because she carries them, nurses them, and raises them with knowledge. Therefore the Messenger of God said to the leader of the believers: "I and you are the two fathers of this nation." So the adversary wished to share with them in those sons whom he could transfer from the birth of faith into the birth of disbelief, having promised them his arrogance and his falsehood. And God made it a trial for them.

Then He told them about His servants who are loyal to Him, that the adversary does not prevail over them, that He returns them to Himself in full, and that they are of His party and His sons. The Almighty said: "Indeed, over My servants you will have no authority" (Quran 15:42). He made for Himself a party from among them. The doctrine of the two parties, the Party of God against the party of the adversary, which will dominate the next half of the author's exposition, is established here on the floor of the chapter on Adam. It is established, as the author wants the listener to see, by the pact that the adversary extracted after his expulsion: the pact to share with Adam's descendants in their wealth, which is knowledge, and in their offspring, which is the line of those who answer the call.

For now, the lesson of the tree is what the listener should carry. The tree is not wheat. The tree is not fig. The tree is not grape. The tree is the disclosure that belongs to the Master of the Resurrection, the manifest unveiling of the inner that has not yet been authorized, and Adam transgressed it not because he was greedy for a fruit but because, prompted by the adversary, he reached for a rank that was not his to occupy. The adversary swore to them that the limit forbidden them was forbidden only so they should not become kings or among the immortals, and he swore: "Indeed I am to you among the sincere advisors" (Quran 7:21). He made clear, in his lying advice, that if they were to leave the obligation of restraint they would end up among the gentle spiritual angels and be stripped of the dense material world, two kings eternal in what they desired. They transgressed the limits of speech permitted to them, into the limit belonging to the Lord of the Resurrection, and extended their hands to what was not theirs. So they ate from the tree, and that which supported them was cut off. That support is the garment of piety which God mentioned: "And that is from the signs of God" (Quran 7:26).

Adam was stripped bare of it, the author says, and there appeared to them what was reprehensible, the intensity of the natural image, because of the nakedness of the garment of piety, which is the word, by which they saw, by its light, all the darkness in which they were now compounded. For support does not accompany sins, and extending one's hand to what does not belong to one is, by ruling, obligatory to cut off. When the support for it ceased, as the Almighty said: "They began to stitch upon themselves leaves from Paradise" (Quran 7:22), they joined together what remained to them of the light of wisdom in order to cover some of the shame that had appeared in them. What was revealed about their condition was the disappearance of support, and through ignorance many of those attached to this matter perished, and it became permitted.

Then comes the cry, and the words. "Did I not forbid you that tree and tell you that the adversary is to you a clear enemy? They said: 'Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves; if You do not forgive us and have mercy on us, we will surely be among the losers'" (Quran 7:22-23). Adam did not turn aside as the adversary turned aside, the author emphasizes; rather he repented to God and prayed to Him, asking for His mercy and forgiveness. "Then Adam received from his Lord words, so He turned to him in forgiveness; indeed He is the Accepter of Repentance, the Merciful" (Quran 2:37). This means, the author explains, that he drew close to Him and found Him repenting from the side of the higher limits, so He turned to him in mercy. The adversary had no authority over him to drag him to himself and take possession of him, so that he might become of his party, rather, he was of the Party of God. The Almighty turned to him in mercy, forgave him, and received his words. Likewise those of his descendants who did evil and then repented from it, He turned to them in forgiveness, and they were of His party. But whoever persisted in his sin and did not repent of it, and to whom no words came from his Lord, was of the party of the adversary and his soldiers. The Almighty said: "If, when they wronged themselves, they had come to you and asked forgiveness of God, and the Messenger had asked forgiveness for them, they would have found God Accepting of Repentance and Merciful" (Quran 4:64). The repentance of the repentant, the author comments, is not accepted except by His receiving His words for them, and they are not forgiven until after the seeking of forgiveness for them and the seeking by the words on their behalf.

This is the closing note that the listener should hold as the chapter ends. The tree was the limit of the Master of the Resurrection. The transgression was to seize a disclosure that was not yet authorized. The fall stripped the garment of piety, that is, the word, the support of the supportive knowledge. And the words that returned were the words of repentance, by which Adam re-entered the line of those whom the Imamate could pass through. He had stumbled, but he had not turned aside. He had stretched out his hand to the limit of the Master of the Resurrection, but he had then asked forgiveness through the guardian of his own time, which, after the expulsion of the adversary, was the rank that had been filled by Eve. Adam was forgiven, and the line of the Imamate that begins with him was preserved. The architecture has been laid down. The first proof became the first enemy; a replacement proof was supplied; a sealed disclosure was placed under guard until the appointed Day; a pact was extracted by the expelled one; and a repentance, addressed through the guardian of the time, returned the first caliph to the rank from which he had fallen. That is the lesson the chapter on Adam means to leave standing.
