This chapter closes the author's interpretation of Adam's story and carries the cycle of the first Speaker through to its end at Noah. The work to this point has brought Adam to the moment of his repentance — the words returned to him after the fall, the line of the Imamate preserved through him after he had reached toward the limit of the Master of the Resurrection and been turned back from it. From that point the author does three things. First, he develops the doctrine of the two parties at length, threading together a long sequence of Quranic verses that name the party of Satan and the Party of Allah, and showing that what divides them is exactly what was already at stake in Adam and the adversary — the question of whether one accepts the guardian of one's own time or sets oneself up against him. Second, he turns to the rejected interpretations: the literal reading that says the forbidden tree was a tree of wheat, fig, or grape, and that Adam's punishment left a residue in human fingernails; the inherited tale that Adam and Eve hid naked among the trees of Paradise when God walked there; the apocrypha that the author traces to two early converts from among the Jews. He refuses these readings not on philological grounds but on dignity — that the prophet whom God has chosen and honored is too noble to be staged in a man-and-slave humiliation, and that Torah, Gospel, and Quran alike contain parables, signs, and symbols that need interpretation. Third, he sets the chain of supportive knowledge — the five upper limits and the five lower limits — and walks the lineage of named successors from Adam through Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared, Enoch, Methuselah, and Lamech, to the close of the cycle and the rise of Noah. The chapter then turns its first page into the chapter on Noah — the second Speaker, with a new sacred law that supersedes the law of Adam — and shows how Noah's people received him. The reader who walks the chapter through to its end will see the author's verdict on what makes the believer one of the Party of Allah, will hear the popular cosmology of the fall replaced by the architecture of supportive knowledge, and will be set down at the threshold of the second prophetic cycle with the structure of the first cycle held intact.

He resumes where the doctrine of repentance left off. The Almighty, having forgiven Adam, made clear that His party are those who cling to Him and who have taken His guardians as their own. And the party of Satan are those whom the adversary has acquired and cut off for himself: he shared with them in their guardians among them, so they took as their guardians his enemies — who are also their enemies — and disowned His guardianship and theirs, and took as their guardians Satan and his lieutenants. But whoever was abased by Satan and regretted his abasement, who resigned from his stumbling and turned back to his Lord — that one is not of Satan, nor of his party. The Almighty says: "And whoever does evil or wrongs himself but then seeks forgiveness of Allah will find Allah Forgiving and Merciful." The sixth Imam, peace be upon him, said: "Do not consider as a guardian one whom Satan has abased entirely; rather, consider as a guardian a man who turns back, relying on his Lord."

The two parties are named in the Quran itself, and the author wants the reader to hear them in his own arrangement. Describing the party of Satan, the Almighty said: "Have you not considered those who make allies of a people upon whom Allah has placed His wrath" — meaning, the author interjects, the nation of misguidance — "they are neither of you nor of them, and they swear to falsehood while they know. Allah has prepared for them a severe punishment; evil indeed is what they used to do. They took their oaths as a cover and averted people from the way of Allah, so for them is a humiliating punishment. Never will their wealth or their children avail them against Allah at all. Those are the companions of the Fire; they will abide therein eternally — on the Day when Allah will resurrect them all, and they will swear to Him as they swear to you, thinking that they are upon something. Unquestionably, it is they who are the liars. Satan has overcome them and made them forget the remembrance of Allah. Those are the party of Satan. Unquestionably, the party of Satan — they will be the losers." Then He described His own party: "You will not find a people who believe in Allah and the Last Day having affection for those who oppose Allah and His Messenger" — meaning, the author interjects again, the imams of injustice and their followers — "even if they were their fathers or their sons or their brothers or their kindred. Those — He has decreed faith within their hearts and supported them with a spirit from Himself. And He will admit them to gardens beneath which rivers flow, wherein they will abide eternally. Allah is pleased with them, and they are pleased with Him. Those are the party of Allah. Unquestionably, the party of Allah — they are the successful."

This is the doctrine of the two parties stated at its widest, and the author leans on the same partition that the prophetic tradition lays down at its starkest. The Prophet ﷺ was reported to have said that there is in every body a piece of flesh which, if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupt — and that piece is the heart. — Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* (Book of Faith, hadith 52, narrated on the authority of an early companion of the Prophet). The Party of Allah is the body whose heart has been turned toward the guardian; the party of Satan is the body whose heart has been turned away. The architecture of allegiance the author is drawing is, on this reading, the same architecture the canonical hadith collection draws between the sound and the corrupt heart, transposed from the individual frame onto the historical frame of the cycles.

The cursed adversary, the author continues, abased Adam and Eve when he said to them what Allah Almighty has reported concerning him in His Book: "Your Lord did not forbid you this tree except that you might become kings, or that you might become among the immortals," and he swore to them: "Indeed I am to you among the sincere advisors." He made it clear to them, the author says, that the limit of the permissible was what Allah had warned them about, and said to them that the limit of obligatory action is the limit of effort and exertion, and that if they were to leave it they would end up among the gentle spiritual angels and be stripped of the dense material world — that they would be two kings eternal in what they desired. As the Almighty said: "But if they had not been beguiled, they would not have tasted of the tree, and it appeared evil to them." That is, they transgressed the limits of speech that had been permitted to them, into the limit belonging to the Lord of the Resurrection — that of which Allah had warned them — and they extended their hands to what was not theirs. So they ate from it; they ate from the tree, and they fell into sin and disobedience, and that which supported them was cut off. That support is the garment of piety which Allah mentioned in His Book: "And that is from the signs of Allah."

Now the author turns to the readings he rejects. The first is the popular literal reading of the fall. The general interpretation, he says, took it on its face: that the tree was a wheat tree, while others said a fig tree, and others said a grape tree; and that its clothing was made of a nail-like material, so it was forbidden to them, and they repented, and what remained of it remained in their fingertips, and that remained in their children; and that this is why most animals have nails and the like — like the hooves of cows and sheep, and the claws of predators and birds — and that those born to Adam are not inspired with what he received, so it remains in their limbs, but as a wisdom in their composition with which the Maker was familiar. This is a claim, the author says plainly, which the minds do not accept and which is not correct on examination. The whole reading misses the point. The forbidden tree was not a botanical fact. It was a likeness for the Master of the Resurrection, as the author has already laid out, and the lesson of the trespass was the lesson of reaching for a disclosure that belonged to a higher limit. To freeze the parable into a wheat plant and to look for the proof of the story in human fingernails is to do exactly what the author has spent the whole book teaching the reader not to do — it is to fix the outer and to lose the inner.

The Father of Imams gave the rule for reading parables in a saying preserved in his canonical aphorisms: knowledge that does not separate the husk from the kernel benefits no one — its bearer carries a lamp that lights only the dust around his feet. — Source: *Ghurar al-Hikam* §1786, the collection of his aphorisms compiled by al-Aamidi. The author's complaint against the wheat-tree reading is the Father of Imams' complaint against any reading that takes the husk for the kernel. To insist that the fall is a culinary event, and that human fingernails are its forensic relic, is to carry the lamp without lifting it from the ground.

The same applies, the author continues, to what they claimed: that Adam and Eve had their sins exposed, so they sewed for themselves a covering of fig leaves. This too is something that is not proven, because it did not happen; and Allah has remembered him, and he is too noble for that, being a prophet whom He has chosen and honored. Even granting that he is one of the followers, that He would look at him as a man would look at a slave who had angered him, then strip him of his clothes and leave him naked, beginning with the figure of his disgrace by that act — they attribute this to their idol, but Allah is exalted above that. The author is uncharacteristically blunt here. The picture of God walking through Paradise's trees, calling out "Where are you?" to a hiding Adam, asking him whether he ate from the tree, hearing Adam blame Eve, hearing Eve blame the serpent, and hearing the serpent blame the adversary who came through the underground — the author names this as the inherited material that came into the tradition from those who became Muslim from among their Jewish ancestors. He names two of them, two early converts who carried the storyteller's apocrypha across the line into the new community. Among others, he says, who claim it was thus in their tradition. May Allah, he says, interpret this interpretation — that the lands contain it, or that it is seen with the eyes — for in the Torah and the Gospel, as in the Quran, there are proverbs, signs, and symbols, and an outward that needs interpretation, which is the stored knowledge. Allah Almighty says: "Say: O People of the Book, you stand upon nothing until you uphold the Torah and the Gospel and what has been revealed to you from your Lord." From this it is known, the author concludes, that they do not establish this except by interpretation. And therefore it has been said in interpretation that the Torah is like the outward and the Gospel is like the inward; so Allah Almighty has commanded — and the Almighty will establish them all, as we have mentioned, with what Allah has sent down of the knowledge that Adam deposited and transmitted in the word — the foundations, the imams, and their successors among their descendants.

He pauses on one detail of the rejected reading that has a doctrinal point worth making — the saying that the Lord called out to them. As for the saying mentioned above, "And their Lord called them," the author says, His call is a call from Him to them — not a call like the call that is heard from those who call out. The same applies to what we mention concerning His sayings, blessed be His name — we have explained this at length before. The servants, however, engage with that the same way, attributing to Allah what is contrary to Him and not appropriate for Him. What is customary among worshippers is that a voice comes only from a voice, from a hollow and a passageway, and that speech is the same only with lips and tongue. The whole picture of God walking among trees and shouting out a question is a projection of human voicing onto a Lord exalted above any such thing.

Then comes the rehabilitation. When Allah turned in repentance to Adam, He did not remove him from the rank of the caliphate and the imamate which He had set up for him, nor did He deprive him of his substance, his word, and his wisdom in general. Rather, He brought him down — and Eve with him — from the limits of interpretation without a veil. He said: "Go down from it together, and you will have therein dwelling and enjoyment for a time." He said: "Therein you will live, and therein you will die, and from it you will be brought forth." He said: "O children of Adam, We have bestowed upon you clothing to conceal your private parts, and as adornment. But the clothing of righteousness — that is best. That is from the signs of Allah, that perhaps they will remember." So Allah brought Adam down from the limit of interpretation without a veil; yet He did not deprive him of substance. What was connected to him of material descended with him from the upper celestial limits, and this was connected to the signs of Allah, as He mentioned. And likewise has it been done in the word of his son.

A voice from the path of the inner draws the same conclusion. The early master Junaid taught that the returned servant is not the same servant who left, because the going down has been turned, by the words received, into a going up — and the garment that was stripped from him in the garden is restored to him, in the inner sense, as the garment of repentance. — Source: *Risala al-Qushayriyya* (the section on repentance, citing the early teacher of the path Junaid of Baghdad, d. 297 AH). The author's reading — that Adam was sent down without being deprived of his rank — has its echo on the Sufi side of the tradition, where the lowered servant is read as the returning servant, the descent itself becoming the way back up.

Now the author names the chain of the upper limits and pairs it with the chain of the lower. The Messenger of Allah said: "I take the revelation from Gabriel, and Gabriel takes it from Michael, and Michael takes it from the Trumpet-bearer, and the Trumpet-bearer takes it from the Tablet, and the Tablet takes it from the Pen." So the support, the author explains, is connected to the word of the Five Upper Limits — the Pen, the Tablet, the Trumpet-bearer angel, Michael, Gabriel — and they are connected to those who respond by the Five Lower Limits. The Upper Limits are those just named. The Lower Limits are the Foundations; the Imams; the Pilgrims, who are the proofs of their own time; the Captains, who are the chief deputies of the call; the Lords of the Islands, who are the regional heads of the mission; and the Wings, who are the preachers of the call. What the author is laying down here is not new doctrine; it is the operational architecture of supportive knowledge that the four-limits chapter has already prepared. The point of naming it again at the close of the doctrine of Adam is to fix the chain in the reader's mind before the prophetic cycles begin to walk through it.

An earlier teacher of the same call, writing on the rungs of the supportive ladder in his book *The Wellsprings of Wisdom*, gives the rule for reading this pairing — that the Five Upper and the Five Lower are not two ladders but one ladder, joined at the rung of the messenger, and that the worshipper who climbs the lower five is climbing the upper five at the same time without knowing he is doing so. — Source: *Yanabi al-Hikma* (the section on the limits of the call, by the foundational teacher of the early call tradition, Abu Yaqub al-Sijistani, d. ca. 360 AH). The author's stacking of the five and the five is the same ladder, read at the point in the prophetic history where its first rungs are first being set.

The sixth of the two ranks of limits, the author notes, is the rebel — and Satan became an enemy to Adam and against him, from Allah Almighty and against Allah Almighty — whether the limit he stands against be a Speaker, a Silent One, an imam, or a Proof. And whoever takes them as guardians and is loyal to them is from the party of Adam, which is the party of Allah; and that includes all those who are obedient to the guardianship of Allah and the Prophet. And whoever has dismissed their command or claimed their place is from the party of the adversary — who was possessed by Satan and forgot the remembrance of Allah, and forgot here that the matter is delayed by His saying that the last of them is the remembrance of Allah, and the remembrance of Allah here is, in the inner sense, His guardians who are remembered, and they are those who have remembered Him.

The same thing that happened to Adam and Eve, the author continues, happened in their descendants — and their descendants, even if they were numerous, did not differ in their kinds, from male and female, as it was with Adam and Eve. And so it happened in all of obedience and disobedience, as happened in Adam and Eve. The beginning of Adam's creation and what happened to him is parallel to what happened to the creation of his son in the beginning of his command and his dwelling in this house. And the dwelling of Adam and Eve in Paradise — after what happened to them — is parallel to the dwelling of those of their descendants who have believed and reconciled, who are of their party. The repentance of those who repent is like their writing. The destruction of those of them who perished in disobedience and came to the punishment of Allah by disbelief and by committing what He forbade — this, the author says, is because of the adversary's possession of them and the fact that they were of his party, and their cessation from Adam, and their departure from his lineage. So they entered the entrance of those who became from him, and they became other than him — that is, other than from Adam, because they were cut off from him. The Almighty said: "And whoever takes them as allies among you, then indeed he is of them." Whoever takes the adversary as ally is from him, and they are his sons by calling, and his family by calling, and they became other than Adam. For the Almighty said concerning the son of Noah when he disobeyed him: "He is not one of your family."

He named them the party of Satan and called them by his name. He said: "Indeed, Satan is a clear enemy." And He said: "Over My servants you shall have no authority." This refers to those who follow Adam through his son. "Except for those of the deviators who follow you, even if they know not their promised time. It has a number of gates; to each gate is an allotted portion." By "the gates," the author says, I mean the messengers, who are the gates of the pure outward truth — for each of them there is a known sacred law. Some of those who repaid that law gave themselves over to the Fire. Of this the Prophet said to the Father of Imams: "You are the Guardian of the Fire," for he will say to it on the Day of Resurrection: "This one is yours, take him; and this one is mine, leave him." This means, the author explains, that the adversary's opposition to Adam and his enmity toward him are like the opposition of the enemies of the messengers, and like their hostility toward them. The Almighty says: "And thus We have made for every enemy of the prophets a host — devils from among humankind and the jinn." The sinner is the son of Adam who persists in his sin — neither repenting of it nor casting it off. He is like Satan in his persistence: he has departed Adam's party and entered Satan's, taken his entrance, and arrived at the punishment of Allah alongside the adversary. The repentant who turns back from what he has committed, and the obedient born of Adam, are Adam's party, which is the Party of Allah. The Almighty says: "And those who, when they commit an immorality or wrong themselves, remember Allah and seek forgiveness for their sins — and who can forgive sins except Allah? — and they do not persist in what they have done while they know. Their reward is forgiveness from their Lord, and gardens beneath which rivers flow, wherein they shall abide forever. How excellent is the reward of those who labor."

The Father of Imams, in the canonical collection of his sermons known as *The Peak of Eloquence*, frames the choice in the very same terms when he says, in a letter to one of his governors, that the two ways are not far apart but that one of them is paved by remembrance and the other by forgetfulness, and that the worshipper does not walk between them but on one or the other — and that whoever stands still on the road of forgetfulness is, in his standing, already walking. — Source: *Nahj al-Balagha*, Letter 31, the long advisory letter to his elder son (translator Sayed Ali Reza). The author's two-party doctrine is, on the Father of Imams' framing, a doctrine about which road the worshipper is on at any given moment, not about which side he claims to belong to.

Now the author turns to the line. Allah Almighty benefited Adam through His will: He settled him on the earth and made him a successor over those whom He placed there. Adam's descendants multiplied with him, Allah's argument was established against them, and Adam was the bearer of the message to them. He entrusted his son Abel. His brother Cain envied him and killed him, as Allah has described. That occurred during Adam's lifetime, so that the lesson of the will would be made plain to him. Whoever entertained evil thoughts, persisted in disobedience, and did not repent of his sin was of Satan's party. Allah then granted Adam, from among his descendants, a son whom he named the Gift of God. To him Adam entrusted the inner knowledge; he took him into his confidence and commanded him to keep the secret, fearing that Cain would kill him as he had killed Abel. The custom was thus established of guarding the secrets of the covenant and preserving them. Allah prepared Adam for the reward He had set aside for him in the Abode of Decision, and after him the matter of the Imamate passed to the will of Seth — the Gift of God and Seth being the same person, the descriptive name and the personal name standing for one named successor. Things came to pass with his son in the manner the author has described: remembrance after remembrance, station after station, Imam after Imam — until the matter reached the one whom Allah mentioned in His Book concerning Enoch. This was within the Cycle of Adam; then it concluded with the era of Noah.

The author pauses on the closing of the cycle. Allah sent Noah as a messenger speaking a sacred law different from the law of Adam. The Almighty says: "For each of you We have appointed a law and a way." Adam's cycle ended when Noah's era came upon them. None of them rose to messengership, the author notes, until he had first held fast to the law of those who came before him; the predecessor's covenant was taken up, and he bound himself to it. The Almighty says: "And when We took from the prophets their covenant — and from you, and from Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus, son of Mary — We took from them a solemn covenant." The author treats this verse as the structural pivot of the chapter. The cycle of a Speaker does not simply end. It hands over. The new Speaker rises only after he has held the law of the one before him, and the covenant between them is what binds the cycles into one chain. The chain has not been broken since Adam. It will not be broken at Noah. The reader is to keep this firmly in mind as the book moves into the era of Noah, where a new sacred law replaces an old one but the architecture of the Speaker, the Silent One, the seven Imams, and the twelve proofs is preserved.

The named lineage of the cycle should be held as the canonical Ismaili roster for the first cycle. The Resident Imam in Adam's era was the first of the proofs of his time, and his proofs were twelve, among them the adversary, who refused submission to Adam. Adam descended at Aden — perhaps Aden in Yemen — then migrated to Mecca, whose name was then Faran, where he died at 930 years of age and was buried at the foot of a nearby mountain. Before that he had bequeathed to his son Seth, who was called the Gift of God. Seth was born in the year 230 from Adam's descent, died in 1142, lived 912 years, and bequeathed to his son Enosh. Enosh died in 1385, lived 950 years, and bequeathed to his son Kenan. Kenan, born in 625, died in 1535, lived 910 years, and bequeathed to his son Mahalalel. Mahalalel, born in 795, died in 1690, lived 895 years, and bequeathed to his son Jared. Jared was born in 960, died in 1922, lived 962 years, and bequeathed to his son Enoch — the same prophet called Hermes the Thrice-Blessed and the first to write with the pen. Enoch was born in 1122 and was raised up in 1487, having lived 365 years, and bequeathed to his son Methuselah. Methuselah was born in 1287 and died in 2242, the year of the flood; his death came just before the flood. He lived 955 years and bequeathed to his son Lamech. Lamech, born in 1454, died in 2346 — 104 years after the flood — and lived 891 years. He completed the cycle of Adam, and was the seventh of its Imams. He handed the matter to his son Noah, after the divine will decreed the change of the old law and its replacement by a new one: the Law of Noah, the Second Speaker. Seven names, seven Imams, one cycle. The chain is preserved exactly as the four-limits chapter required: a Speaker at the top, a Silent One paired with him, seven Imams across his cycle, twelve proofs in his time, the architecture intact.

He turns at the close of the chapter to direct address, and the reader should hear the chapter ending in a tone that the book will return to often. So understand, he says, what we have recited to you of the interpretation of what Allah has revealed to you. Know the truth of what it contains, so that you may worship Him by it, and grasp the inner revelation, of which the Almighty informed you in His Book — that none knows it but He and those firmly rooted in knowledge: His guardians, whose authority Allah has favored you with, and whose following has placed you in His party. He has promised you His reward, and through it has removed you from the party of Satan — whose punishment you dread. Carry out what Allah has prescribed; be sincere before His face. Perform what Allah has obligated upon you, outwardly and inwardly. Whoever of you has committed a sin: let him repent to Allah from it, and let him take refuge in the guardian of his own time, so that Allah may forgive him and absolve him of his sin. He will thereby become one of those whom Allah has promised — the repentant, who seek forgiveness for their sins and for their guardians — that he may belong to the party of your father Adam and to the party of the guardians of Allah from his progeny, which is the Party of Allah. Leave the party of Satan, who persisted in their sins as Satan persisted in his — they became satanic like him. Just as Satan despaired of his Lord, do not despair of your Lord or of His mercy upon you. The Almighty said: "Do not despair of the mercy of Allah; surely none despair of Allah's mercy except the disbelieving people." And as Allah cited from the words of His friend Abraham: "And who despairs of the mercy of his Lord but those who go astray?" May Allah, the author closes, guide you to what He loves and is pleased with, protect you from violating His prohibitions and disobeying Him, and open for you the gates of obedience to His guardians. May Allah bless the Prophet and the pure Imams from his progeny, his household, and peace.

The chapter on Adam closes here, and the author immediately opens what follows — the chapter on the interpretation of the stories of Noah and the two prophets who follow him. The reader should hear this seam as the author arranged it, because the test of the doctrine just laid out is whether it carries forward into a new cycle without losing its shape. In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds. May Allah bless the Prophet, the Seal of the Prophets, and his executor, the Father of Imams, the best of the guardians; and the pure Imams from his successive progeny, from his loins until the Day of Judgment, and beyond. Allah, the author begins again, sent Noah as a prophet bearing a new sacred law, after the law of Adam had altered and corruption had increased among his descendants. So Noah called the people to his law, to worship Allah Almighty and affirm His oneness outwardly, as Allah informed him in His Book: "We sent Noah to his people, saying: 'O my people, worship Allah; you have no god other than Him. Will you not be conscious of Him?'"

The response to Noah is what the author wants the reader to hold as the closing image of this stretch of the book, because it shows how the architecture of the previous cycle is received — or refused — at the moment a new Speaker rises. Those who responded from among the weak responded; the arrogant rejected him; and those who were content with the law of Adam answered him by saying: "You are nothing but a human being like us." That is, the author paraphrases, you have come to us in the same outward form known to us; we hold in our hands what we inherited from those who came before you, and you began with us with the very things they began with us. You are human beings, just as we are. In the Arabic language, the word for "human being" used here refers to the appearance of the skin, and to direct skin-to-skin contact. The point is that they were saying: what he has brought us is already with us, so he is like us and has no merit over us. They said: "None follows you but the lowliest of us, without consideration" — that is, the lowliest of us in knowledge.

We have mentioned, the author reminds the reader, that the Speaker is a caller to the outward. "Noah said: 'My Lord, they have disobeyed me and followed one whose wealth and child have only increased him in loss.'" That is, their scholars of the outward, whose knowledge increased nothing and who called him to nothing but loss. Wealth, in the inner sense, is like knowledge; the child is like the responder, the one who answers his call and accepts what is preached to him, whether rightly or wrongly. "And they devised a great plot, and they said: 'Do not forsake your gods, and do not forsake'" — and the verse names five idols — meaning, the author says, their scholars from whom they took, and the elders among their scholars whom they revered, who misled many of the creation. So Noah called them outwardly for a long time. Then Allah Almighty entrusted him with the verdict: "None of your people will believe except those who have already believed."

This is the point at which the doctrine of Adam hands over to the doctrine of Noah, and the two cycles meet on the same architecture. The reader now has the structural diagram from the four-limits chapter; has watched it walk through Adam's creation, fall, and repentance; has seen the popular literalism of the tree and the inherited apocrypha set aside in favor of the inner reading; has heard the five upper limits paired with the five lower limits; has held the named lineage of the seven Imams of Adam's cycle from Seth through Lamech; has heard the cycle close and the new Speaker rise; and has watched, in the first paragraphs of the new doctrine, the architecture of the previous cycle pass forward into the next. The doctrine is doing exactly what the author promised in the four-limits chapter: every later stretch of the book is being read through the diagram without re-arguing it. And the reader should hold one further observation as the chapter draws to its close — that the doctrine of the two parties, which the author developed at such length from the pact the adversary extracted under Adam, is not an antique lesson confined to the first cycle. It is the working logic of every cycle that will follow. When the arrogant of Noah's people refuse Noah and answer him with "you are nothing but a human being like us," they are answering exactly as the adversary answered God when he said "I am better than him." The shape of the refusal is the same. The voice of the refusal changes; the structure does not. The author wants the reader to recognize the pattern in Noah's people because the same pattern will appear in the people of the prophet who came after Noah, in the people of the prophet who came after him, in Pharaoh's court before Moses, and on through every cycle to the awaited Riser of the Age. The Party of Adam, which is the Party of Allah, and the party of the adversary, which is the party of Satan, are the two enduring sides into which every cycle's audience divides itself. That is the architecture's claim on history. The Speaker speaks, the Silent One stands as foundation, the seven Imams carry the cycle, the twelve proofs stand as witnesses, and the responding believer enters the Party of Allah by accepting the guardian of his time. The adversary's pact, sealed under Adam, runs on through every cycle as the standing temptation to refuse that guardian — and the lesson of Adam's repentance, that the Imamate is preserved even through the breach, runs on as the standing remedy. The cycle of Noah, the second Speaker, will repeat the structure on its own terms, with its own Foundation — Shem — and its own seven Imams, and the next stretch of the book will show how the same architecture survives the two prophets who follow Noah, and then opens into the cycle of Abraham. The author's wager — that the diagram of the four limits is sufficient to read the entire prophetic history — is now being tested cycle by cycle, beginning here, in the cycle that was always going to be the hardest first test, because it required the demonstration that the chain of the Imamate could survive even the catastrophe of the flood.
