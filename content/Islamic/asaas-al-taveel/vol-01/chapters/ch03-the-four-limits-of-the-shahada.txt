The testimony — "There is no god but Allah" — has four words. Those four words stand for four limits that organize the whole created order. Two of the limits are above, in the spiritual world. Two of the limits are below, in the world of bodies. The two above are the Pen and the Preserved Tablet, named in the prophetic report that says the first thing God created was the Pen. The two below are the speaker-prophet, who articulates the sacred law in his time, and the Silent One, who succeeds him as the master of its interpretation. To recognize all four, and to accept them, is to complete the testimony. To acknowledge only some — as the philosophers do when they affirm intellect and soul but deny the messengers, or as those of the outward Islam do when they affirm the prophets but do not know their inner office — is to leave the testimony broken. From the four, the argument turns to the seven and the twelve. There are seven speaker-prophets across history, and each carries seven Imams in his cycle and twelve successors in his time. The numbers are not ornamental. They are the architecture by which heaven, earth, the human body, the months of the year, and the letters of the testimony itself are read as one continuous sign of the same structure.

The testimony is divided into two clauses — one negation, one affirmation. "There is no god" is the negation. "But Allah" is the affirmation. Each clause is made of two words. The first word in each pair is joined to the second; the second is elevated above the first. The separation between the two clauses marks the affirmation as higher than the negation that precedes it. The negation is like the lower limit, and the affirmation is like the upper limit. Within the upper, the first word of the affirmation stands as the highest boundary, and the word preceding it stands as the next. Within the lower, the second word of the negation stands as the lower of the lower boundaries, and the word preceding it, joined to it and yet beneath it, stands as the boundary beneath. Four words, four positions, four limits. The whole architecture is already in the syntax.

The two words that meet in the limit of affirmation and in the limit of negation function as two words and no more. They do not collapse into one. If they did, the joined pair would be loftier and more freighted with meaning than they ought to be, and the result would be God Himself. The words point to the limits; they are not the limits. The limits point to the One; they are not the One. The architecture of pointing is preserved through the architecture of separation.

By Allah, this points to the noblest of the upper and lower boundaries. Each of the four words is joined to the others and dependent upon them. Each has fewer letters than the cluster as a whole. And the final word — the name Allah itself — carries three letters that together gather every letter that appears anywhere in the testimony. This is the sentence of the saying within the limits of the testimony.

What has been transmitted from the Messenger of Allah, both by the elect and by the general public, is that he said: "The first thing Allah Almighty created was the Pen. Then He set running a river sweeter than honey, whiter than milk, and softer than butter. Then He created the Preserved Tablet, which Allah Almighty mentioned in His mighty Book." The general public carried this report toward what they already knew — that pens are made of reed and tablets of wood, as we observe — and did not turn to the fact that these are names, titles, and proverbs that need to be uncovered, unveiled, and explained. This is from the interpretation of the report mentioned by Allah, the Almighty.

The Pen is the highest limit that Allah has set for His servants. They may not transgress it. As He said: "Whoever transgresses the limits of Allah has wronged his own soul" (verse one of the chapter on divorce). The Almighty stands above the boundaries and has erected them for His creation beneath Himself, so that they may reach knowledge of Him through them, and that the creation might be led to command and prohibition. The Pen is the bearer of knowledge — derived from Allah and fixed in the Tablet. The Preserved Tablet then limited the limits of God beneath itself, meaning that, beneath the Pen, the Tablet took its knowledge from the Pen, and the knowledge became, for the Tablet, from the Pen. These are the two upper boundaries: the Pen above, the Tablet beneath the Pen, both above the world of bodies.

As for the river that Allah Almighty made to flow for the Pen — the river sweeter than honey, whiter than milk, softer than butter — the Almighty has spoken of it: "And if they had remained on the right way, We would have given them water in abundance" (verse sixteen of the chapter on the jinn). The way is the prophet who speaks in his time, and the Imam in his time after him. The water is abundant knowledge. And again: "And We have created above you seven ways, and We are not heedless of creation" (verse seventeen of the chapter on the believers).

The general public have confirmed this much in their interpretation of the esoteric, because they have found no evidence against it. By saying that the way provides water with knowledge, they understand it as the apparent water that Allah causes to descend upon the righteous land and upon the immoral alike. And Allah has said that if creatures were to stand upright before their guardian, they would have learned the most abundant knowledge — "that We may try them therein," meaning, He says, We test them, see what they say, and teach them about it. Then He said: "And whoever turns away from the remembrance of his Lord, He will lead him into an ever-increasing torment." So whoever turned away from his guardian, the punishment of God became his — and He called the guardian a "remembrance," that is, a person of remembrance, so the thing was named for him. This is well known in the language of the Arabs. They call a thing by what is suited to it and to its owner. The Almighty Himself said: "Indeed, Allah has sent down to you a Reminder — a Messenger" — He called the Messenger a "Reminder" because he is the bearer of the remembrance. The way is the prophet. The water is the knowledge. The remembrance is the messenger. The river above the Pen is the abundant knowledge that Allah caused to flow for the Pen to draw from.

Now the two upper limits have been placed: Pen above, Tablet below the Pen, and the river of knowledge flowing for the Pen. Then Allah Almighty, in correspondence with these two upper spiritual limits, established two lower physical limits — namely the speaker-prophet and the Silent One. The speaker-prophet is the Ismaili term given to every messenger who came with a message and articulated a law. The foundational speaker-prophets, the foundational seven, are Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, the Prophet, and the Awaited Riser. The Silent One is given on the basis of the speaker-prophet. He is the speaker-prophet's successor after him, the one from whom the Imams come in succession, the one entrusted with the affairs of the esoteric mission during the speaker-prophet's own lifetime. The foundational Silent Ones among the Ismailis are likewise seven, paired with the seven speaker-prophets: Seth, Shem, Isaac, Aaron, Simeon, the Father of Imams, and the awaited successor of the seventh cycle. Each speaker-prophet has his Silent One. Each Silent One stands as the foundation of his speaker-prophet's sacred law.

The speaker-prophet is the master of the sacred law. The Silent One is the foundation of the sacred law in the era of the speaker-prophet, and the master of its interpretation. The Messenger utters the manifest. The Foundation is silent about that manifest — that is, from the manifest, he conducts toward the subtle, toward the inner meaning that the Prophet himself has affirmed. The standing of the two below echoes the standing of the two above. The Pen has knowledge. The Tablet has the knowledge of the Pen. The speaker-prophet has the sacred law. The Silent One has the inner sense of that law, which the speaker-prophet authorized. The structure of derivation is the same upstairs and downstairs. The Father of Imams gave the relation its starkest sentence in a sermon collected in the book *The Peak of Eloquence*: "I am the speaking Quran, and this is the silent Quran" (sermon one hundred and fifty-eight, in the standard Beirut edition of 1980, page 222). The geometry is older than this book; the author is naming the architecture his community already saw in that sermon.

The world cannot be left without a witness. When the house that God Almighty has established holds the two lower boundaries as a place of transformation, and the bodies in it are mortal, then by the wisdom of God the house must not be left empty of an argument. So Allah Almighty established a foundation after the speaker-prophet to take his place in the apparent matter and in the other duties the speaker-prophet had been performing, and He instructed him to appoint an argument for himself, to entrust him with the knowledge of interpretation, and to install it with him just as it had been with the speaker-prophet. Then He established an argument after him before him, and ordered him to take an argument for himself in turn — according to the pattern that the way of Allah Almighty has established, as He said in His Mighty Book: "And you will not find any change in the way of Allah" (verse forty-three of the chapter on the creator of the heavens). This continues until the role of that speaker-prophet turns over seven Imams. Then the cycle completes.

The speaker-prophets are seven: Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, the Prophet, and the Awaited Riser. Six of them are designated by a covenant — six, not seven — and the exception is Adam. He cites the Quran: "And We had already taken a promise from Adam before, but he forgot, and We found in him no firm determination" (verse one hundred and fifteen of the chapter Ta-Ha). This indicates that Adam has no firm determination. Every speaker-prophet — whether messenger or Imam — has twelve successors in his time.

The four words of the testimony stand as a likeness for these four limits. Whoever recognizes them, accepts them, and bears witness to them completes the limits of the testimony. The testimony gathers them, and gathers its boundaries within them. The two words of the affirmation correspond to the two upper limits — the Pen and the Tablet. The two words of the negation correspond to the two lower limits — the speaker-prophet and the Silent One. Four words, four limits, one testimony.

There are those who affirmed the Creator Almighty — from among the philosophers, the logicians, and others — and yet denied the messengers. These people affirmed the two upper boundaries and named them by other than these two names. Instead of "Pen" and "Tablet," they called them "the intellect" and "the soul." But they did not know the two lower boundaries, so the testimony was not established for them, because they acknowledged some and denied others. The philosophers came close. They saw the upper architecture. They renamed it in their idiom — intellect and soul — and read it well enough on its own terms. But they could not see, or would not see, that the upper architecture has a worldly correspondent below it, in the person of the speaker-prophet and his Silent successor. Without that, the testimony is not whole.

As for those from among the "people of refuge" who hold fast to the testimony and the outward marks of Islam, who acknowledge the Tablet, the Pen, and the speaker-prophet among the prophets — these people did not have proper knowledge of these matters, and they professed something other and so did not complete the testimony. From them, this incomplete testimony is accepted only insofar as their blood is spared and their wealth made inviolable. They are also prevented from being among the protection-tax payers, as the Prophet established for them by the command of Allah Almighty when they became Muslim. The form of the testimony was on their tongue. The substance of it — its four limits — was not in their knowledge.

Islam is a word taken from a root meaning reconciliation and surrender — the casting of one's hands into obedience. Faith is a limit that follows Islam. It is the knowledge of the limits of the testimony, the belief in them, and the rendering of their due. And this is sincerity, as the Messenger of Allah said in the saying cited here. The Messenger promised the one who is sincere to the testimony Paradise, and told us that the testimony is returned to whoever is not sincere to it. So the testimony is the root of the religion. Indeed the whole religion is in it. And so the call to it is as has been said: a call to the root that already contained the trunk and the branches.

If we look at the count of the letters of the testimony, "There is no god but Allah" comprises seven clauses. This is a proof and a likeness for the seven speaker-prophets and for the seven Imams in the role of every speaker-prophet. He cites the Quran: "And We made the son of Mary and his mother a sign" (verse fifty of the chapter on the believers). And: "Indeed, the Messiah, son of Mary, is the Messenger of Allah and His Word, which He bestowed upon Mary" (verse one hundred and seventy-one of the chapter on women). And: "When the angels said, O Mary, indeed Allah gives you good tidings of a Word from Him whose name will be the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary" (verse forty-five of the chapter on the family of Imran). And: "We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth" (verse fifty-three of the chapter on the explained). And: "And in the earth are signs for the certain in faith, and in your own selves — do you not then see?" (verses twenty and twenty-one of the chapter on the scatterers).

These signs are like those of the testimony, but their likenesses come from heaven and earth. From the side of religion, the heaven is the likeness of the master of the age — whether he is a speaker-prophet or an Imam — and the earth is the likeness of his argument. Water descends from heaven onto the earth, and from it trees and plants sprout, grow, and flourish. So too does knowledge descend from the speaker-prophet to the Silent One, and from it the believers sprout and live. And like them too is the sun. In the two heavens — the spiritual and the physical — the sun is like the speaker-prophet who illuminates the world. The moon is like the argument, because it draws its light from the sun. Likewise the knowledge of the argument is derived from the knowledge of the Imam. The speaker-prophet in the body is like the heart, which is the prince of the bodily limbs. The argument is like the brain, which is the seat of the mind.

From the earth, the likeness is the west and the east. The east is like the speaker-prophet, for from it the light comes. The west is like the argument that receives that light and is where it comes to rest. The seven heavens are like the seven speaker-prophets. The seven earths are like the seven arguments. The seven planetary stars — the arrangers — that Allah Almighty mentioned in His Book when He said: "And those who arrange the affair" (verse five of the chapter on the snatchers). So too are the seven climes of the earth. And so too in the human being: "And certainly We created the human being from an extract of clay; then We placed him as a sperm-drop in a firm lodging; then We made the sperm-drop into a clinging clot; then We made the clot into a lump of flesh; then We made out of the lump bones; then We clothed the bones with flesh; then We brought him forth as another creation. So blessed is Allah, the best of creators" (verses twelve through fourteen of the chapter on the believers). These are some of the witnesses to the signs of Allah, His limits, and their likenesses, and their inward meanings in the greater and the smaller worlds.

These are the limits that Allah Almighty has commanded be safeguarded, and by which the believers are marked with faith. He cites: "Indeed Allah has purchased from the believers their souls and their wealth, in exchange for Paradise being theirs. They fight in the way of Allah, so they slay and are slain — a true promise upon Him in the Torah, the Gospel, and the Quran. And who is more faithful to his covenant than Allah? So rejoice in your transaction which you have contracted, and that is indeed the great success" (verse one hundred and eleven of the chapter on repentance). And the attributes of those believers: "Those who repent, who worship, who praise, who fast, who bow down, who prostrate themselves, who enjoin the good and forbid the wrong, and who keep the limits of Allah — and give glad tidings to the believers" (verse one hundred and twelve of the same chapter).

As for the twelve successors, they are instruments — causes — for every speaker-prophet in every age. Four of them are above and closest to the speaker-prophet, and each of these four has two — one to the right and one to the left — making twelve in all. The four are like the four birds of Abraham in his story, as the Almighty said: "He said, Then take four birds and incline them toward yourself" (verse two hundred and sixty of the chapter on the cow). They are also like the four sacred months, according to His saying: "The number of months with Allah is twelve months in the Book of Allah from the day He created the heavens and the earth, of which four are sacred. So do not wrong yourselves with them" (verse thirty-six of the chapter on repentance). Their likeness is found in the testimony itself, whose letters number twelve. The twelve letters that together form the negation-and-affirmation correspond to the twelve missionaries who are charged with the propagation and organization of the mission. Their likeness is in the twelve constellations of the heavens, and in the twelve islands of the earth. He cites: "Praise be to Allah, who made the angels messengers having wings — two, three, and four. He increases in creation what He wills. Indeed, Allah is able to do all things" (verse one of the chapter on the originator).

Understand, then, that the twelve limits in the lower world correspond to the angels in the upper world. The Almighty has said: "And of all things We created pairs" (verse forty-nine of the chapter on the scatterers). The seven and the twelve together total nineteen — and these are the ones whom Allah has entrusted. The Almighty said: "Over it are nineteen" (verse thirty of the chapter on the cloaked one). The testimony has gathered them all together. Whoever knows them, believes in their allegiance, and acts upon what he has taken from them, is a believer among the people of Paradise — accepted in his testimony. Whoever is unable to do any of that, or falls short of it, but bears witness with his tongue alone, is a Muslim whose testimony is not accepted in the rank of faith, and his judgment rests with Allah. For this reason, it is mentioned in the book *Pillars of Islam*, from the saying of the fifth Imam: "Faith encompasses Islam, but Islam does not encompass faith. A man may be a Muslim without being a believer, but he is not a believer until he is also a Muslim." The two clauses, faith and Islam, fit inside each other in only one direction. The outward Islam is the gate. The inward faith is the room. To enter the room, one must pass through the gate. But to stand in the gate is not yet to have entered the room.

Connected to the first testimony is the second testimony, which pertains to the branches: the testimony that the Prophet is the Messenger of Allah. This second testimony is three words, and these three words represent three subordinate spiritual limits — the upper spiritual ones — namely Raphael, Michael, and Gabriel. They correspond to three earthly corporeal limits, in extension of the speaker-prophet and the Foundation — namely the Imam, the Proof, and the Successor. Together they also form six divisions corresponding to the six pronunciations of the first determination, and together twelve letters, like the twelve proofs already mentioned. Thus the testimony to Allah Almighty is the highest degree — the foundations. The testimony to the Messenger is the lowest degree — the branches. The two testimonies share the architecture, and they share it by twelve letters apiece.

The upper and lower limits are contained within the letters of the testimony. The outward of the testimony is a statement with the tongue. Its inner meaning is statement and knowledge together. Its substance is action. Its outward form is Islam. And at its core is faith. On this, the fifth Imam said, in the book *Pillars of Islam*, when asked whether faith is speech or action: "Faith is all action, and speech is only a part of it." The testimony lives on the tongue. But the tongue is the smallest part of it. Knowledge is what gives the tongue what to say. Action is what makes the testimony real in the world.

So preserve, O believers, the testimony to which you have been called. Be sincere in it. Whatever you have been called to, be faithful to it in word and deed. Fulfill His promise to you, in the words of the Almighty: "And fulfill My covenant and I will fulfill your covenant" (verse forty of the chapter on the children of Israel). Allah has promised you, through the tongue of His Messenger, Paradise if you are sincere in your testimony. Act and do not stray. And you will receive what Allah has promised you of Paradise, outward and inward, as He said: "And for him who fears the standing before his Lord, there are two gardens" (verse forty-six of the chapter on the Most Merciful).

The doubling of the garden is not incidental. The testimony is two clauses, outward and inward. The believer who keeps both receives both. What has been mentioned in this chapter — the chapter on faith — should suffice as an explanation of the testimony and what it indicates of the limits. When mentioning the obligatory prayers, you will know that this testimony has descended upon it and has encompassed the totality of that by which you worship Allah. That is why the call of the Prophet was directed to it, and why he took upon you the covenant that you would testify with sincerity after him. You have borne witness to it with your tongue. You bear witness to it as a testimony of belief in your hearts. And you act upon it as a ruling next to a deed.

May Allah aid you in that, and guide you to it. May Allah's blessings be upon the Prophet and upon the righteous of his descendants who succeed one another until the Day of Judgment. And praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds.
