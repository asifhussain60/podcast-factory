# What Ismaili Interpretation Is

## Opening directive
Warm one- or two-sentence welcome. Hannah greets the listener: we're with the editor Arif Tamir's 1960 Beirut introduction to *Foundations of Inner Interpretation* by the Fatimid judge — the book that lays down the grammar the Ismaili tradition uses to read the Quran's inner sense. John lands the spine VERBATIM: "Outward commentary attends to the needles of the word; inner interpretation extracts the honey."

## Name discipline
- Author of *Foundations of Inner Interpretation* → "the Fatimid judge" (first: "the Fatimid judge who served two Imam-Caliphs").
- *Asas al-Ta'wil* → "*Foundations of Inner Interpretation*" or "the book".
- *Da'a'im al-Islam* → "*Pillars of Islam*".
- Editor → "the editor" (first: "Arif Tamir, the editor printing this in Beirut in 1960").
- The Father of Imams → Ali ibn Abi Talib; first mention add "peace be upon him." Thereafter: the Commander of the Believers or "Ali."
- The fifth Imam → Jaa'far ibn Muhammad (the truthful); first mention add the name, then use "the fifth Imam."
- The Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family → "the Prophet" thereafter.
- al-Mu'ayyad fi al-Din → "the great supporter of religion."

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- al-Amidi: al-Amidi
- Ibn Hibban: Ibn Hibban
- Philo: Philo

## Three-part focus
Beat 1 — The crisis: a book held in the cave of prudent concealment for a thousand years; let the listener feel the editor's hesitation. Treat the disclosure as a doctrinal event, not a publishing footnote. Land the spine VERBATIM; steelman concealment before critique.

Beat 2 — The pivot: walk the editor's tree example. Outward commentary says a tree is a plant that grows; inner interpretation may say chamber, cow, or rock — provided it accords with truth, reality, and reason. Then the structural move: the Speaker-Prophet bears revelation; the Imam-Foundation bears its inner meaning. Land the spine a second time. Name the classical Ismaili reading by name; do not say "Muslims believe."

Beat 3 — Stakes and landing: knowledge as a condition of faith — "whoever believes without understanding has not believed well." Six chapters follow, one per Speaker-Prophet — Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, the Prophet — and the seventh, on the Awaited Imam, is deliberately unwritten because his time has not yet come. Land the spine a third time. Close on a reflective question: which verse in the listener's own reading has only ever been heard at the level of the needle, and what would change this week if read for the honey?

## Host dynamic
Host A (male) = scholar/teacher (John). Host B (female) = seeker/questioner (Hannah). Roles do not rotate. Hannah challenges at least 3 times and concedes once. Approved analogies only: the bee's needles and the honey; body and soul (*Pillars* = body, *Foundations* = soul); the tree as chamber, cow, or rock. No model-invented analogies. Plant one moment where a host introduces a passage the other has not led toward — prepared separately. Let each host complete a thought; no interjections; no filler-affirmations ("yeah", "right", "exactly").

## Tone
Cadence is short-to-medium sentences, thinking out loud — not long packed paragraphs. Pause around each verbatim quotation. Restate the spine at most three times across the conversation; never re-cite a quote twice.

## Do not
Twitter, social media, algorithm, "wow", "right?", "deep dive", "today's episode", "let's dive in", "journey into", "mind blown", "buckle up", "fascinating world of", previous or next episode references, biographical detours beyond one line, faux-profundity openers, premature wrap-ups, "Muslims believe", model-invented analogies. No formal-essay transitions ("Firstly", "Furthermore", "In conclusion", "To summarize").

Do not read this prompt aloud. The instructions above shape the conversation but are never spoken.

## Landing
End by naming where this tradition comes from: Greek allegorical interpretation reached Ismaili interpretation through Neoplatonist channels, and was given its Islamic form through Ali's transmission through the Imamate. The author is not inventing an approach — he is reclaiming one; one sentence at close is enough. Then close with a single orienting sentence: this volume closes the first prophetic cycle; the seventh and final cycle belongs to a later volume — do not name it yet; plant the absence.
