# Show notes — EP03

**Title:** The Four Limits of the Shahada

**Blurb:** Al-Nu'man opens the testimony itself and shows what is inside it. The shahada has four words; those four words stand for four limits, two above and two below. Above are the Pen and the Preserved Tablet — named in the prophetic hadith that says the first thing God created was the Pen. Below are the Speaker, the prophet who articulates the sacred law in his time, and the Silent One, the Imam-Foundation who is master of its interpretation. To recognize all four is to complete the shahada. The philosophers affirmed the upper two and called them intellect and soul, but denied the lower two and so left the testimony broken. The chapter then maps the seven Speaker-Prophets across history — Adam, Noah, Ibrahim, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad, and the awaited al-Qa'im — and the seven Imams in each cycle, the twelve successors arranged as proofs in every age, and the seven-plus-twelve nineteen that the Qur'an names. It closes on the second testimony to the Messenger as the branches of the first, and on al-Sadiq's verdict that faith encompasses Islam but Islam does not encompass faith.
