# Supplication and the Many Forms of Prayer

On the tashahhud in prayer, on supplication, and on what is longed for when calling upon Allah, the teaching begins with the word of Allah Himself.

Allah the Almighty said: ﴿وَقَالَ رَبُّكُمُ ادْعُونِي أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِي سَيَدْخُلُونَ جَهَنَّمَ دَاخِرِينَ﴾, "And your Lord said: Call upon Me; I will answer you. Indeed, those who disdain My worship will enter Hell, humbled" [Ghafir: 60]. And He, blessed and exalted, said: ﴿وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي عَنِّي فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌ أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ الدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا لِي وَلْيُؤْمِنُوا بِي لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْشُدُونَ﴾, "And when My servants ask you concerning Me, indeed I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls upon Me. So let them respond to Me and believe in Me, that they may be rightly guided" [al-Baqarah: 186].

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that the Messenger of Allah (ص) said: "Whoever sits after the second rak'ah, remembering Allah the Almighty and calling upon Him, Allah honors him. Good deeds are written for him, evil deeds are wiped away from him, and he is raised in degrees, and an angel is charged over him who says to him, 'Increase in honor,' until he departs." And he (ص) said: "By Him in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, a man's supplication after the Fajr prayer until sunrise is more effective in securing his needs than the one who travels the earth trading with his wealth."

He (ص) said: "Shall I not direct you to a weapon by which Allah will fortify you against your enemy and by which He will make your provision easy?" They said, "Yes, O Messenger of Allah." He said: "You call upon your Lord by night and by day, for the weapon of the believer is supplication."

And he (ص) said: "The most beloved glorification to Allah is the tasbih of talk, سُبْحَة الحديث." It was said, "O Messenger of Allah, and what is the tasbih of talk?" He said: "People are plunged in the world, its vanities and its amusements, and a man seizes that very moment and calls upon Allah the Almighty, and remembers Him, and glorifies Him."

He (ص) said: "Allah the Almighty has not opened for a servant a door of supplication except that He has opened for him the door of response. He has not opened for him a door of seeking forgiveness except that He has opened for him the door of pardon. He has not opened for him a door of gratitude except that He has opened for him the door of increase. And He has not opened for him a door of deed except that He has opened for him the door of acceptance." This is borne witness to by the Book of Allah, whose mention is exalted. Allah, blessed be His name, says: ﴿ادْعُونِي أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ﴾, "Call upon Me; I will answer you" [Ghafir: 60]. And He said: ﴿ثُمَّ يَسْتَغْفِرِ اللَّهَ يَجِدِ اللَّهَ غَفُورًا رَّحِيمًا﴾, "Then seeks the forgiveness of Allah, he will find Allah Forgiving, Merciful" [al-Nisa': 110]. And He said: ﴿لَئِن شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ﴾, "If you are grateful, I will surely increase you" [Ibrahim: 7]. And He said: ﴿فَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ﴾, "So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it" [al-Zalzalah: 7], and He said, "Who is it that will lend Allah a goodly loan?"

The Messenger of Allah (ص) said: "Supplication turns back the decree though it has been firmly ordained," and he clasped his fingers together, palm against palm, joining them all, thumb against thumb and little finger against little finger, as though showing you a thing. He said that an affliction descends toward the servant and comes down to him from the heaven, yet he calls upon Allah and asks Him for well-being, so the supplication ascends into the heaven. The supplication meets the affliction, and the two contend with one another until the Day of Resurrection. And he (ص) said: "Whoever looks upon one stricken with affliction and says, 'Praise be to Allah who has kept me sound from what He has tried you with, and has favored me over you and over much of His creation with great favor,' it becomes a right upon Allah that He not strike him with that affliction."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: "Whoever supplicates, let him send prayers upon Muhammad in his supplication, for when the servant supplicates, his supplication is not raised to the heaven until he sends prayers upon the Prophet and upon the people of his house." The Prophet (ص) said: "Place me at the beginning of the supplication, and in its middle, and at its end." And he (ص) said: "Four are made intercessors in the heavens and the earth: Paradise, the Fire, the pure-eyed maidens, and an angel at my head in my grave when I have died. When the servant says, 'O Allah, I ask You for Paradise,' Paradise says, 'O Allah, make him among my dwellers.' When he says, 'O Allah, deliver me from the Fire,' the Fire says, 'O Allah, deliver him from me.' When he says, 'O Allah, wed me to the pure-eyed maidens,' the pure-eyed maidens say, 'O Allah, wed him to us.' And when he says, 'O Allah, send prayers upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad,' the angel at my head says to me, 'O Muhammad, so-and-so has sent prayers upon you,' and Allah says, 'O Allah, send prayers upon him as he has sent prayers upon Me,' so I say, 'O Allah, send prayers upon him as he has sent prayers upon me.'"

He (ص) said that the supplication of the believer is answered so long as he is not hasty. It was said, "And how is haste, O Messenger of Allah?" He said: "He supplicates but sees no trace of his supplication, and so he says, 'I called upon Allah, and Allah has not answered me.'"

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that Allah the Almighty grants the believing servant whatever He wills of what he asks, and defers of it whatever He wills, according to what is better for him, and Allah the Almighty is truer than any who promises. ﴿وَقَالَ رَبُّكُمُ ادْعُونِي أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ﴾, "And your Lord said: Call upon Me; I will answer you" [Ghafir: 60]. He, exalted be His praise, undertakes the reckoning of His believing servant on the Day of Resurrection, and there appear to the servant good deeds he never performed. It is said to him, "Do you recognize these good deeds?" He says, "No." It is said to him, "You called upon Allah on such a day in such a matter, and He answered you and deferred that until this day, for it was better for you." Then, when the believer sees on that Day the greatness of the reward of his supplication, he says, "O Lord, would that You had hastened nothing of my supplication for me, but had deferred it all to this day."

Then Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that Allah raises up, by supplication, the matter which He knows the servant will supplicate for, and He answers it. Were it not for Allah's granting the servant that supplication, an affliction would have struck him that would have uprooted him from the face of the earth. There is no Muslim who calls upon Allah but that He answers him, or turns away from him by his supplication an affliction greater than what he asked, or stores it for him until a day and a time in which it is more beneficial for him, so long as he does not supplicate for wrongdoing or the severing of kinship. He (ع) said: "Supplication turns back the decree though it has been firmly ordained, so multiply supplication, for it is the key to every good and the success of every need. What is with Allah is not attained except by supplication, and there is no door that is knocked upon much but that it is soon opened to the one who knocks."

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) was asked by a man about the saying of the Messenger of Allah (ص) concerning the black seed, which is al-shuniz. He said, "Yes, the Messenger of Allah (ص) said of it, 'In it is a cure for every disease save death.' But shall I direct you to that for which the Messenger of Allah (ص) gave you no cure?" The man said, "And what is it?" He said: "Supplication, for it turns back the decree though it has been firmly ordained," and Abu Ja'far ibn 'Ali (ع) clasped his fingers and joined them before him at his words "though it has been firmly ordained." Then he (ع) said: "There is nothing more beloved to Allah the Almighty than that He be asked, and nothing turns back the decree except supplication."

He was asked about the word of Allah the Almighty, ﴿فَإِذَا فَرَغْتَ فَانصَبْ وَإِلَى رَبِّكَ فَارْغَب﴾, "So when you have finished, then labor, and to your Lord direct your longing" [al-Sharh: 7-8]. He said: "That is supplication after the obligatory prayer. Beware of forsaking it, for its excellence over the rest of supplication is like the excellence of the obligatory prayer over the voluntary." And he (ع) said: "Supplication is before the prayer, and within the prayer, and after the prayer." And he (ع) said: "Supplication after the obligatory prayer is more excellent than the voluntary prayer." Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: "Supplication is worship," and he recited the word of Allah the Almighty: ﴿ادْعُونِي أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِي سَيَدْخُلُونَ جَهَنَّمَ دَاخِرِينَ﴾, "Call upon Me; I will answer you. Indeed, those who disdain My worship will enter Hell, humbled" [Ghafir: 60]. Then he said: "It, by Allah, is worship."

When he (ع) prayed the two rak'ahs of Fajr, he would call with this supplication, saying: "I have taken hold of the firmest handhold of Allah, which has no breaking, and I have clung to the strong rope of Allah. I seek refuge in Allah from the evil of the devils of mankind and jinn all together. I seek refuge in Allah from the evil of the corrupt among the Arabs and the non-Arabs. Allah is sufficient for me. I rely upon Allah. I lean my back upon Allah. I seek my need from Allah. There is no might nor power except in Allah. O Allah, make for me a light in my heart, a light in my hearing, a light in my sight, a light in my tongue, a light in my hair, a light in my skin, a light in my flesh, a light in my blood, a light in my bone, a light in my sinews, a light before me, a light behind me, a light on my right, a light on my left, a light above me, a light beneath me, and a light in my grave. O Allah, make my light great, and abundant, and full of joy, and a blessing, and a delight."

Then he recites five verses from the end of Surah Al 'Imran, from "Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth" to His word, "You do not break the appointment." Then he says, three times: "Glory be to the Lord of the morning, the Cleaver of the dawn, who made the night and the day a repose, and the sun and the moon a reckoning." Then he says: "O Allah, make the first of this day of mine righteousness, and its middle prosperity, and its last success. O Allah, whoever wakes with his need and his request directed to a creature among Your creation, my need and my request are to You alone, who has no partner." Then he recites the Verse of the Throne and the two suras of refuge, and then he says, one hundred times: "Glory be to my Lord the Magnificent, and by His praise. I seek the forgiveness of Allah and turn to Him in repentance." If this is missed, he makes it up.

He (ع) said that the worshipper says in the first tashahhud: "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. In the name of Allah, and by Allah, and all the beautiful names belong to Allah. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, alone, having no partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and His messenger (ص), and the Imams from the people of his house, the righteous." Then he rises. And when he sits in the last tashahhud, in which he gives the salutation and from which he departs, he says: "In the name of Allah, and by Allah, and all the beautiful names belong to Allah. The greetings belong to Allah, and the pure prayers, the good, the untainted, the tender, the ample, the morning-going, the flowing, belong to Allah. Whatever is good and pure and increasing and honored and exalted and refined belongs to Allah. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, alone, having no partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and His messenger, the excellent Messenger and the excellent Guardian, and that Paradise is true, and the Fire is true, and the Resurrection is true, and that the Hour is coming, no doubt in it, and that Allah will raise up those who are in the graves. O Allah, send prayers upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad, and bless and have mercy upon Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You sent prayers and mercy and blessing upon Ibrahim and upon the family of Ibrahim in all the worlds; indeed You are Praiseworthy, Glorious. O Allah, forgive me and my parents and have mercy upon us as they raised me when I was small." And he said that one whose parents are Muslim says this, and adds: "And forgive us and our brothers who preceded us in faith, and place not in our hearts any rancor toward those who believe; indeed You are Kind, Merciful." Then he supplicates with whatever he loves of supplication for this world and the hereafter, then he sends prayers upon the Prophet and upon his family and says: "Peace be upon you, O Prophet, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings. Peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of Allah."

The whole of the tashahhud he says under his breath, uttering it with his tongue in a slight utterance. Then he gives the salutation, and he announces the salutation aloud if he is the imam, and lowers his voice with it if he is behind an imam. If he raises it a little, there is no harm in that. And if he is alone, he may lower his voice if he wishes and announce it if he wishes. When he rises from the first tashahhud, he leans upon his hands and says quietly, "O Allah, by Your power I rise and I sit," and he pronounces the takbir for standing. When the worshipper has given the salutation from the obligatory prayer, he turns the backs of his hands upon his thighs and raises them if he wishes, and that is more excellent, placing the inner surfaces of his palms toward his face, and he recites and says: "O Allah, I draw near to You through You, and through Muhammad Your Prophet, and 'Ali Your friend, and Fatima al-Zahra the daughter of Your Prophet, and al-Hasan and al-Husayn, and 'Ali ibn al-Husayn, and Muhammad ibn 'Ali, and Ja'far ibn Muhammad," and he names the Imams one by one down to the Imam of the Age. Then he says: "O Allah, I draw near to You through them and turn toward You by them, so make me through them honored in Your sight in this world and the hereafter, and among those brought near, and make certainty firm in my heart, and increase me in guidance and light." Then he supplicates with whatever he wishes.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) said: "When you rise to the prayer, or enter the mosque intending the prayer, say: 'In the name of Allah, and by Allah, and from Allah, and to Allah, and as Allah wills, and there is no might nor power except in Allah. O Allah, make me among Your visitors and among those who frequent Your mosques. Open for me, O Lord, the door of Your forgiveness, and close against me the doors of Your disobedience. Praise be to Allah who has made me among those who commune with Him. O Allah, turn toward me with Your Face; exalted is Your praise and blessed is Your name.' Then begin the prayer."

'Ali (ع) said: "Whoever would be pleased to be measured with a full measure that falls not short, let him say, when he has turned away from his prayer: 'Glory be to your Lord, the Lord of Might, above what they describe; and peace be upon the messengers; and praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds.'" And he (ع) said: "I heard the Messenger of Allah (ص) say: 'Whoever recites after every prescribed prayer, "Say: He is Allah, One," one hundred times, will cross the Sirat on the Day of Resurrection, and on his right will be eight cubits and on his left eight cubits, and Gabriel will take hold of his waistband while he gazes into the Fire to his right and to his left; and whoever he sees within it, among those he knows who entered by a sin other than shirk, he takes him by the hand and enters him into Paradise by his intercession.'" This, and Allah knows best, is a report given in summary, and it is particular, not general, and the intercession is not for all people, so that whoever performs this recitation would have earned what he (ص) said. Rather, intercession belongs only to the one whom Allah the Almighty has appointed for it, "and they possess no power of intercession except one who has taken with the Most Merciful a covenant."

'Ali (ع) said: "Whoever sits in the place in which he prayed the Fajr prayer, after he has prayed it, and recites within it, 'Say: He is Allah, One,' eleven times before the sun rises, no sin will follow him that day, though Satan strive hard against him." And he said: "The Messenger of Allah (ص) said to me, 'O 'Ali, recite after every prayer the Verse of the Throne, for none guards it faithfully but a prophet, or a truthful one, or a martyr.'"

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: "Whoever glorifies with the tasbih of Fatima (ع) before he folds his legs from the obligatory prayer, after he has given the salutation, is forgiven; and he begins with the takbir." He said, and the tasbih of Fatima (ع) is: "Allah is the Greatest, and Glory be to Allah, and Praise be to Allah," thirty-three times of each, which he seals with "There is no god but Allah," so that it comes to one hundred. He said that the Messenger of Allah (ص) taught this tasbih to Fatima (ع) when she came asking him for a servant. He said: "O Fatima, this is better for you than a servant, and a servant, and a servant, and than all that is in the world. You say it after every prescribed prayer, and one hundred good deeds are written for you, and the good deed is tenfold its like, so that becomes a thousand." And whoever says one hundred times, "Glory be to Allah, and praise be to Allah, and I seek the forgiveness of Allah, and there is no god but Allah, and Allah is the Greatest, and as Allah wills, and there is no might nor power except in Allah; Allah is sufficient for me, I have taken refuge in Allah, I have relied upon Allah."

None knows what reward such a person has with God, whose name be exalted, save God alone; and the least of it is ten thousand good deeds, for God multiplies a single good deed tenfold, as He has said. It is recommended that whoever rises from the prayer, on leaving it, utter the takbir three times and then say: "There is no god but God, alone, with no partner; to Him belongs the kingdom and to Him belongs praise; in His hand is all good, and He is able to do all things. There is no god but God alone; He fulfilled His promise, gave victory to His servant, and defeated the confederates alone; to Him belongs the kingdom and to Him belongs praise. Praise be to God, Lord of the Worlds. There is no god but God, and God is greatest; glory be to God, and praise be to God." This is said ten times.

'Ali (ع) used to say after every prescribed prayer: "Then by Your light You guided, so to You belongs praise; Your forbearance is great, so You pardoned, and to You belongs praise; You stretched out Your hands and gave, so to You belongs praise. Our Lord, Your face is the most generous of faces, Your station the best of stations, and Your gift the most beneficial and the most pleasant of gifts. You are obeyed, our Lord, and You give thanks; You are disobeyed, our Lord, and You forgive. You answer the supplication of the distressed, heal the sick, and deliver from affliction. Our Lord, You accept repentance and forgive sins for whom You will; none can requite Your favor, and no speaker can number Your due." Abu 'Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: "Because this is a remembrance of God as He deserves to be remembered a thousandfold, whoever says it will have a thousand good deeds."

It is right and recommended, too, to say after the prayer: "O God, for You I have prayed, to You I have called, and in You I have hoped; so I ask You to place in my prayer and my supplication a blessing by which You expiate my sins, whiten my face, honor my standing, and lift my burden from me. O God, put down my burden, and make what is with You better for me. Praise be to God, who has discharged from me a prayer that was ordained upon me and upon the believers as a timed obligation." And Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) said: "The least that will suffice you in supplication after the obligatory prayer is to say: O God, I ask You for every good that Your knowledge encompasses, and I seek refuge in You from every evil that Your knowledge encompasses. O God, I ask You for well-being in all my affairs, and I seek refuge in You from the disgrace of this world and the punishment of the Hereafter."

### The desirable forms of supplication

There is no appointed time for supplication. Whoever wishes to pray may do so, asking God for his needs and calling upon Him for whatever he loves, so long as he does not call for a wrong or for the severing of kinship. It is fitting for one who would supplicate that he set a righteous deed before his supplication, that he repent to God of his sins, that he clear himself of the claims standing between him and others of God's creation, and that he make his deed and his intention sincerely for God. For it is related of the Messenger of God (ص) that he said: "One who supplicates without action is like one who draws a bow without a string." And Abu Dharr (رض) said: "Righteous conduct is sufficient accompaniment for supplication, as salt is sufficient seasoning for food."

Whoever would supplicate should begin by praising God his Lord and extolling Him as He deserves, and by blessing Muhammad His prophet and his family; then let him make his petition, and let him bless the prophet and his family in the middle of his supplication and at its end. For the Messenger of God (ص) heard a man supplicating before he had praised God, and said: "This man has been hasty with his Lord." And when he wished to supplicate he would begin by praising God, then say: "O God, there is none who can withhold what You give, and none who can give what You withhold; none who can constrain what You expand, and none who can expand what You constrain; none who can bring forward what You defer, and none who can defer what You bring forward. O God, You are the Forbearing, so You are never rash; You are the Bountiful, so You are never niggardly; You are the Mighty, so You are never abased; You are the Impregnable, so You are never assailed; You are the Possessor of Majesty and Honor; and there is no power and no strength save in God, the Most High, the Almighty."

He also said: "Do not make me like the rider's cup." For the rider fills his cup and sets it aside, and when he has finished with his gear and his need he looks: if he has need to drink he drinks, or if he has need to make ablution he makes ablution, and otherwise he pours it out. "Place me at the beginning of the supplication, and in the middle of the supplication, and at the end of the supplication." He meant by this that the blessing upon him and his family should not stand at the end of the supplication only, but should stand at its beginning, its middle, and its end.

When God sent down the verse commanding the believers to bless the prophet, the people said: "O Messenger of God, you have taught us how to greet you; how, then, are we to bless you?" He said: "Say: O God, bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You blessed Ibrahim and the family of Ibrahim; You are Praiseworthy, Glorious."

'Ali (ع) was asked about the saying of God, ﴾فَمَا اسْتَكَانُوا لِرَبِّهِمْ وَمَا يَتَضَرَّعُونَ﴿, "They did not humble themselves to their Lord, nor did they supplicate" (al-Mu'minun: 76). He said: "They were not lowly in their supplication to God, nor were they sincere in it."

Supplications are more than can be numbered or contained in a book, yet we mention some of that whose answering may be hoped for, as it has come to us from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام), from the reports and the encouragement to it. Among this is what was related of the Messenger of God (ص), that he said: "There are ninety-nine names; whoever numbers them enters Paradise." This tradition has been narrated often, and men have differed over these names and been at sea concerning them. Sufyan al-Thawri set them down in his compendium, and named names other than those handed down from the Imams (عليهم السلام), naming them at variance with what they gave. 'Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) was asked about them, and he described their excellence and the answering of the supplication of whoever prays by them, and magnified their standing in words too long to relate. He took a covenant of whoever learned them of him that he would teach them only to one he trusted, and would not supplicate God by them for any wrong or for the severing of His kinship. He said that they are in the Book of God, whose mention is exalted.

In the Opening of the Book are five names: By God, O Lord, O Most Merciful, O Ever Compassionate, O King. In Surah al-Baqarah are twenty-six: O Encompassing, O All-Powerful, O All-Knowing, O Wise, O Ever-Relenting, O Patient, O Vast, O Originator, O Hearing, O Sufficient, O Knowing, O Kind, O Grateful, O God, O One, O Forgiving, O Forbearing, O Withholder, O Expander, O Living, O Self-Subsisting, O Most High, O Great, O Rich, O Guardian, O Praiseworthy. In Surah Al 'Imran are four: O Ever-Standing, O Bestower, O Swift in Reckoning, O Aware. In Surah al-Nisa': O Watchful, O Witness, O Pardoning, O Nourisher, O Trustee. In Surah al-An'am are five: O Fashioner, O Subduer, O Able, O Subtle, O Ruler. In Surah al-A'raf are two: O Giver of Life, O Giver of Death. In Surah al-Anfal are two: O Best of Protectors, O Best of Helpers. In Surah Hud are seven: O Preserver, O Near, O Answerer, O Strong, O Glorious, O Loving, O Doer of what He wills. In Surah al-Ra'd are two: O Great, O Most Exalted. In Surah Ibrahim is one: O Ever-Giving. In Surah al-Hujurat is one: O Ever-Creating. In Surah al-Hajj is one: O Raiser of the dead. In Surah al-Anbiya' are two: O Truthful, O Inheritor. In Surah al-Mu'minun is one: O Generous. In Surah al-Nur: O Guide. In Surah Saba' is one: O Opener. In Surah al-Mu'min are four: O Forgiver, O Accepter of repentance, O Severe in punishment, O Possessor of Bounty. In Surah al-Dhariyat are three: O Provider, O Possessor of Strength, O Firm. In Surah al-Tur is one: O Ever-Kind. In Surah Iqtarabat is one: O Omnipotent. In Surah al-Rahman are three: O Everlasting, O Possessor of Majesty and Honor. In Surah al-Hadid are four: O First, O Last, O Manifest, O Hidden. In Surah al-Hashr are ten: O Holy, O Peace, O Granter of Security, O Overseer, O Mighty, O Compeller, O Supreme, O Creator, O Maker, O Fashioner. In Surah al-Buruj are two: O Beginner, O Restorer. In Surah al-Ikhlas are two: O One, O Eternal Refuge.

These are the names of God, exalted is His mention, in His Book: ninety-nine names, and with "By God" a hundred. By them, when God is called upon He answers, and when He is asked He gives. A company of the believers who prayed by them with sincerity and certainty have known the blessing of their being answered; their mention is attested and their excellence handed down.

The Messenger of God (ص) said to 'Ali (ع): "O 'Ali, shall I not teach you words which, were your sins upon you like the foam of the sea, God would put away from you? Say: O God, fix for me affection in the breasts of the believers, and make for me with You a covenant," or, "make for me with You affection." Then God sent down: ﴾إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ سَيَجْعَلُ لَهُمُ الرَّحْمَنُ وُدًّا﴿, "Verily those who believe and do righteous deeds, the All-Merciful will appoint for them affection" (Maryam: 96). And this was among the supplications recited concerning him (ع).

There was a supplication of 'Ali (ع), the supreme supplication of the Imams, by which he would pray in weighty matters. He would say: O God, I seek from You the grace to thank You well; I ask of You the gift of a tongue unloosed in proclaiming Your praise; and I desire from You to be set right in what earns Your increase. I seek refuge in You from that which calls down the visitation of other than You and brings on the sudden strokes that befall the like of me. O God, You began with me by the most abundant of gifts and singled me out for them above much of Your creation, so that, without my asking, there fell to me from Your apportioning the most fortunate share and the fullest lot, when You made me among the people of Your knowledge and those who hold fast to the rope of Your oneness, before the passing of ages, the elapsing of times, and the running of the eras, before the creation of male and female, the multiplying of generations, and the succession of descendants. Then You created me in the finest form and the most complete image, and made me hearing, seeing, speaking, understanding, discerning. Then You set my frame straight and completed my faculties, and did not deprive me of any of my limbs, but fortified them against afflictions by Your power and kept them from the stillness of incapacity by Your governance. As You have taken it upon Yourself, O my God, to repel maladies from my body by my health and to guard my weakness, so bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You are worthy and as they are worthy of You. Remove from my soul the hindrance of sloth and the discouragement of delay; release it from the grip of weariness and loose from it the fetters of listlessness; rouse it to bear the burdens with which You have charged it, and set it upon the bounds of Your sacred limits.

O God, loose my tongue with the abundance of Your remembrance, the goodness of thanking You, and the repeating of Your favors; make it flourish with the recitation of Your Book at every hour of night and day; make it prefer truth in hope of its benefit and forsake falsehood in fear of its harm; restrain it from sharing in the backbiting of the backbiters and from chewing over the honor of the believers; and silence it from the stabbing of slanderers and the perjury of every contemptible swearer. O God, spread out my hand in giving, freely and without stint, from spending in Your way; a hand that seeks Your mercy for those who hope in You, not inclining, in Your displeasure, toward hoarding, nor preferring, when Your good pleasure is within reach, riches over open-handedness. O God, when I have drawn down the treasures of Your abundant storehouses and prayed for the rain of Your gifts in mercy, do not turn my two hands away disappointed of the grant of Your bestowal; rather bind their rising to You to the descent of Your aid and the swiftness of Your answer, and hold them back from touching what the hands of those who tamper with Your sanctities and transgress Your command have long reached for. Let my eyes pour down, O God, with the flowing of tears and the streaming of weeping out of fear of You, and lower them from the moments You have forbidden them; make them humble before what may adorn them of the trinkets of this world and its flowers; and guard them, O God, from the drowsiness of slumber and the sweetness of sleep when the heedless lie down upon their beds. Bar my hearing, O God, from giving ear to what does not please You of the speech of the erring, and make it convey to my heart that which draws me near to You and earns Your good pleasure over me: the holding-lawful of Your lawful, the holding-forbidden of Your forbidden, good conduct at Your doors, and longing for the voluntary acts by which one hastens to Your good pleasure. Empty my heart, O God, of every breach in which I was heedless of Your command; take it back from what earns Your displeasure; and pour into it a full measure of Your love, that it may drive it to the nearness of intimate discourse with You and impel it to long for what is with You, of that which You have prepared for Your friends in the abode of Your honor. Question it about what has slipped away of the wreckage of this world, so weighty in the eyes of its people. Set my feet firm for You, O God, in the standing of devotion for Your intimate discourse, and in knocking at the door of Your answer, a door opened only to one who turns his face toward Your face; and aid me by them to recite Your Book at every hour of night and day, and grant them the strength of Your increase when I journey to You upon them through the pitch-dark of the far-off nights, that You may fulfill Your promise. Leave in me no limb but that I have made it ready for its task, no aim but that I have accomplished it, no rising resolve but that I have made it firm, no thread I mean to twist upon right guidance but that I have made it fast, and no craving of greed but that I have crushed it. O God, whatever failing has held me back from attaining a good benefit hoped for from none but You, and whatever visiting misfortune is lifted only by humble entreaty drawing near to You, bring me to it by Your bounty and Your generosity; for You do whatever You will and decree whatever You wish, and You are the most merciful of the merciful. So deal with me in all my states as befits You, by Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, the truthful, upon whom be greetings and peace all together, in well-being, O Lord of the Worlds.

He would say in his supplication, (ع): O Allah, You are more worthy of what befits You of Your pardon than I am of what befits me of Your punishment. O Allah, honor Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You are worthy of them and they are worthy of You. Do not discipline me with Your punishment, do not scheme against me with Your cunning, and do not lead me astray from where I do not know. O Allah, whoever is bettered by Your grace prospers, and whoever errs by his own sin perishes. No benefactor can dispense with Your support and Your aid, and no wrongdoer overcomes You, nor does any affair pass beyond Your power. So do with me what is worthy of You, for You are worthy of every good. Then he would pray for whatever he wished.

And it was among his supplications, (ع): O Lord, how many an enemy has drawn against me the sword of his aggression and honed for me the edge of his blade, sharpened the point of his resolve against me, and mixed for me his deadly poisons. His watchful eye never slept over me, and he resolved to visit me with harm and to make me drink its bitterness. Seeing my weakness before such heavy blows, and my helplessness to take revenge upon the one who set out to war against me, and seeing that I stood alone among the many who bore me enmity, You reinforced me with Your victory over him and strengthened me. You scattered his gathering after it had assembled, and raised my standing above his, and turned back upon him the arrows he had aimed at me. You choked him with his own rage and struck him with his own dart, so that he was thrown back upon himself after his arrogance, and humbled and brought low after his overbearing. Servile and captive he lay in the snare of his own rope, that very snare in which he had felt sure he would see me on the day of his triumph. To You be praise, as You are worthy of it, powerful and never overcome, forbearing and never hasty.

This is a supplication that 'Ali (ع) taught to al-Husayn (ع). He described to him such of its excellence that Allah, mighty and majestic, has made the reward binding upon whoever supplicates with it, a merit too long to recount. He took from him a covenant not to teach it to anyone save his family, his Shi'a, and his loyal ones, and al-Husayn (ع) pledged him upon that. He said to him: When you wish to ask Allah, mighty and majestic, for a need, or to call upon Him for any affair of this world or the hereafter, then say:

Glory be to Allah in the depths of the night and at the ends of the day. Glory be to Allah in the morning and in the evening. Glory be to Allah when you enter upon evening and when you enter upon morning. To Him is praise in the heavens and the earth, and in the late afternoon, and when you reach midday. Glory be to the Everlasting, the Abiding. Glory be to the Living, the Self-Sustaining. Glory be to the High, the Most High. Exalted and sanctified is He, all-glorious and all-holy, our Lord and the Lord of the angels and the Spirit. Glory be to our Lord the Most High, exalted and sanctified is He.

O Allah, by Your light I was guided, by Your grace I seek help, and within Your favor I have entered evening and morning. O Allah, I call You to witness, and You suffice as a witness, and I call to witness Your angels, the bearers of Your throne, and all Your creation, that You are Allah, there is no god but You alone, having no partner, and that Muhammad is Your servant and Your messenger, may Allah bless him and his family. O Allah, to You be praise, a praise before which the sky lowers its shelter and by which the earth and all upon it glorify You. O Allah, to You be praise, a praise whose beginning ascends and whose end is never exhausted. O Allah, to You be praise, everlasting, abiding, without end. And to You be praise upon me, within me, and with me, and with my child, whether I sleep or wake, and when I have died and passed away and You alone remain, O my Master. O Allah, to You be praise with all the praises that are Yours, for all the blessings that are Yours. O Allah, to You be praise upon every vein at rest and every vein that throbs. To You be praise upon every morsel and every drink, every grasp of the hand, and upon every place where a hair grows. O Allah, to You be praise for Your forbearance after Your knowledge, and to You be praise for Your pardon after Your power. To You be praise, the Sender of praise. To You be praise, the Inheritor of praise. To You be praise, the Originator of praise. To You be praise, the Bestower of praise. To You be praise, the Ancient of praise, Raiser of ranks, Sender down of blessings from above the seven heavens, who brings out of the darknesses into the light, who changes evil deeds into good deeds and makes good deeds into ranks. O Allah, to You be praise, Forgiver of sin, Accepter of repentance, Severe in punishment, Lord of bounty, there is no god but He, to Him is the return. O Allah, to You be praise in the night when it covers over, and to You be praise in the day when it shines forth, and to You be praise in the hereafter and the first. O Allah, to You be praise to the number of the dust, the pebbles, the seeds, of mankind and jinn, of birds, beasts, and wild things. To You be praise to the number of what Your book has counted. To You be praise for all that Your knowledge has encompassed, a praise abundant, wholesome, and blessed.

Then he says: There is no god but Allah alone, having no partner. His is the dominion and His is the praise. He gives life and gives death, and He is Living and does not die. In His hand is all good, and He is powerful over all things, ten times.

Then he says: I seek forgiveness of Allah, other than whom there is no god, the Living, the Self-Sustaining, for all my sins, and I repent to Him, ten times.

'Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) would supplicate in the heart of the night, after every eye had grown still, saying in his supplication: My God, the stars of Your heavens have set, the eyes of Your creation have slept, and the voices of Your servants have fallen silent. The kings of Banu Umayyah have shut their doors upon themselves, set their guards to circle them, and veiled themselves from any who would ask them for a need or seek from them a benefit. At these words he (ع) would weep, and would sob until his family and his loyal ones took fright at his weeping. An account of what is permissible and what is not permissible of speech and action within the prayer

It has been related to us from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that speech, and the breaking of ablution, and turning away wholly from the Qiblah, and whatever is gross in action, all cut off the prayer. But supplication, glorification, the takbir, the recitation of the Qur'an, and whatever a servant addresses to his Lord within the prayer are not so. Though these be speech, they are not the speech that cuts off the prayer. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) said, concerning that with which a man addresses Allah in prayer, that it is speech which does not cut off the prayer. They said: whoever is greeted with peace while he is in prayer, if he returns the greeting within himself or by a slight gesture, does not thereby cut off his prayer. They did not hold that a gesture, or a word said within oneself, is among the things that cut off the prayer. Yet to leave that aside and to turn wholly to the prayer is better. So if the one greeted leaves it until he has finished his prayer and returns the greeting after he has given the closing salaam, if he wishes, that is better.

They gave leave to one who sees a matter he must forbid, or fears something, or sees that he is about to miss something, to glorify Allah by saying "Glory be to Allah" while he prays, intending it as part of the Qur'an, since it is from it, or intending it as glorification, since that is permitted within the prayer. If the matter is one that carries no great harm, then to turn to his prayer and leave it aside is better. A gesture indicating what the worshipper intends is likewise in this same sense: if he points to one he wishes to address without turning his face from the Qiblah, there is no harm in it, though if he leaves it and turns to his prayer, that is better, if he finds in that some good.

They related that laughter in the prayer cuts it off, but that a smile does not cut it off, though it is disliked for the worshipper. They forbade blowing within the prayer, as when the worshipper blows upon the place of his prostration should he see upon it something he dislikes to prostrate over. If it is of what may not be prostrated upon, there is no harm in his removing it with his hand. If there is something in his place of prayer, let him move it aside, and if it is on the ground, let him move it beneath his feet, and if he takes it away with a garment, that is better. The worshipper should not spit toward his face nor to his right, but let him spit to his left, and let him not turn his face from the Qiblah. They forbade the cracking of the fingers in the prayer, which is that the worshipper bends them so that they crackle. This is of idle play and heedlessness in the prayer, and it cuts it off, since he who does it has no need of it. But if they crack from an act he did not intend, or from something he could not avoid, there is no harm in that.

They gave leave to scratch within the prayer for one to whom an itch comes, if he cannot bear it patiently and it distracts him from his prayer. They gave leave to the worshipper to kill a snake or scorpion he sees in the prayer, and every fearsome, harmful creature, and to kill a louse, a flea, or a gnat he finds upon his body or his garment while he is in prayer. They forbade deliberately toying with the hand upon the beard or elsewhere in the prayer, but gave leave for that if it happen without intent. They gave leave to recite from a written Qur'an in the prayer, and forbade stretching and yawning in the prayer. Whoever is overtaken by that without intending it does not thereby cut off his prayer. Likewise a sneeze does not cut off the prayer, though let the worshipper make it light if he is able, cover his mouth with his hand, praise Allah, and bless Muhammad and his family.

They forbade the worshipper to wipe his forehead of dust or of anything else he prostrates upon while he is in the prayer, but he may wipe that away when he has turned from the prayer, and let him not leave it. They forbade the tawarruk in the prayer, which is that the worshipper sets his hand upon his hip. And they forbade a man to pray with his face veiled, save from a necessity or an ailment.

As for the garments a person wears in prayer, the Messenger of God (ص) said: "Let every one of you fear God in his garment during his prayers." For this reason one should pray in the finest clothing he can find, for prayer is the best occasion on which a person adorns himself. We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they permitted praying in a single garment, provided it covers the worshipper so that his 'awrah is not visible. If it is so light that it reveals him, they did not hold it sufficient for him to wear alone; he must have with it something that conceals the 'awrah. For a man, the 'awrah is what lies between the navel and the knees. For a woman, her whole body is 'awrah that she must cover in prayer, except her face. A free woman, if she wishes to pray, veils herself; a slave woman may pray without a veil, but she covers her head and does not leave her hair exposed. It is permissible for a man to pray in a wrapper that covers his 'awrah even if the rest of his body remains uncovered, when he finds nothing else. He should not do this deliberately while he has something else at hand; rather he wears what he finds. The worshipper drapes himself in a robe, a gown, or a cloak, laying its middle length across his shoulders, casting the end on his left over to his right and the end on his right over it to his left.

They forbade the wrapping known as ishtimal al-samma' in prayer, which is that a man gathers the edge of the garment while it lies on his shoulder, draws it with his left hand to his hands, throws what was on his right over to his left, and winds himself in it as the people of the wilderness do, keeping his hands inside it. He should not pray wrapped in such a way. They permitted praying in the covering burnus, and they forbade sadl in prayer, saying it is the practice of the Jews. Sadl is to lay the robe from its middle length upon the shoulders and let its two sides hang loose beside the hands, without folding any of it back over the shoulders. They permitted a worshipper to pray girded with a sword, and they commended this for the Friday prayer, the two Eids, for Jihad and Ribat, and wherever a worshipper fears for himself in travel or otherwise. They saw no harm in praying in leather socks, and they permitted praying in sandals if they do not distract the worshipper and he can manage in them. They forbade praying in the garments of the polytheists that they had worn until such garments are purified. They commended that a woman pray wearing her ornaments if she has them, and that she not strip herself of this, even to the extent of tying about her neck a cord or some small thing by which she is distinguished from men; and they disliked that women divest themselves of ornament and of henna, or alter their palms, or resemble men in appearance. Everything permissible to wear is permissible to prostrate upon, whether of the growth of the earth or of other materials such as wool, hair, fur, and the like. The worshipper should stand firm in his prayer and not prostrate upon any foodstuff. They disliked that he prostrate upon any part of his own clothing, neither upon his sleeve nor upon the coil of his turban, and held that he must uncover his forehead so that it, or such part of it as he is able, reaches what he prostrates upon, together with his nose.

Concerning the Friday prayer, the Messenger of God (ص) said that four have what preceded them forgiven: the sick man when he recovers, the polytheist when he embraces Islam, the pilgrim when he completes his Hajj, and the one who returns from Jumu'ah in faith and in hope of reward. He said: "Pray abundantly upon me on Friday, for it is a day on which deeds are multiplied." And he said: "When Friday night comes, God Almighty sends down angels bearing pens of gold and silver and scrolls, and when the dawn breaks they record nothing but the prayers upon me and upon my Ahl al-Bayt until the sun sets. And angels stand at the doors of the mosques recording those who enter, each in his rank, first and then next, from the breaking of dawn until the Imam ascends the pulpit; when the Imam ascends the pulpit they fold their scrolls and enter to listen to the remembrance." He said: "There are three things which, if my nation knew the merit they hold, they would cast lots for them: the call to prayer, going early to Jumu'ah, and the first row in prayer." And he said: "Whoever goes early to the mosque on Friday and recites Surah Al Imran to its completion, seventy angels ascend with it and seventy angels receive it, and he is forgiven what lies between the two Fridays. And if he recites Surah al-Kahf, seventy angels ascend with it and seventy angels receive it, and he is gathered on the Day of Resurrection with a light spanning the distance between Mecca and Medina."

Ali (ع) said, concerning the words of God Almighty, "حافظوا على الصلوات والصلوات الوسطى" ("Guard strictly the prayers and the middle prayer"), that the middle prayer is the Friday prayer, which on the remaining days is the noon prayer; and he used to command that the time of the sun's decline be carefully observed. He (ع) said: "Beware of forsaking attendance at the mosques, for one of you is soon to begin so that he comes to the mosque only on Friday, then falls back and comes one Friday and misses another, then falls back until he no longer comes at all, and so God sets a seal upon his heart." Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Hussein (عليهم السلام) said: "Deeds are multiplied on Friday, so I love to increase my prayers and charity on that day." And Ali (ع) said: "Friday night is a resplendent night and its day is radiant. Whoever dies on Friday night, God writes for him a release from the torment of the grave; and whoever dies on Friday, God frees him from the Fire. Hell is kindled every day except Friday, for on it it is not kindled." And there is no harm in praying throughout the whole of Friday.

Ali (ع) said that the Friday prayer is an obligation, and gathering to it with the just Imam is an obligation; if a man forsakes it without excuse three times, he has forsaken three obligatory duties, and none forsakes three without excuse but a hypocrite. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that when Friday night comes, God commands an angel who calls out from the first of the night to its last, as He calls out every other night in the final third: "Is there one who asks, that I may give him? Is there one who repents, that I may turn to him? Is there one who seeks forgiveness, that I may forgive him? O seeker of good, draw near; O seeker of evil, hold back." And when the morning of Friday comes, God apportions the provisions among His servants until the sun rises. We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they commended ghusl on Friday, and disliked taking medicine on Friday and Thursday lest it weaken the one who takes it from attending Jumu'ah; and they enjoined perfume and the finest clothing a person can find when he intends to go out to witness the Friday prayer. And the Messenger of God (ص) said that on Friday there is an hour in which no believing servant asks God for any good but that He grants it. Ali (ع), Abu Ja'far, and Abu Abdullah (عليهم السلام) said that it is from the sun's decline until an hour has passed.

They (عليهم السلام) said that Jumu'ah is obligatory except upon the traveler, the woman, the child, the sick, and the owned slave, and whoever has an excuse; and whoever of these attends it, it suffices him in place of the noon prayer. They said that whoever is within two farsakhs of the congregational mosque, or less, is obliged to come to Jumu'ah; the farsakh is three miles, and the mile is three thousand cubits. If there are three miles between two congregational mosques, then Jumu'ah is obligatory in each of them, even within a single town. They said that the least by which Jumu'ah is established is five men, one of them the Imam; if they are fewer than five they do not gather for it but pray the noon prayer, for the Friday prayer with the Imam is equal to seventy prayers. This applies when they are in a settled town.

Regarding the words of God Almighty, "يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا نُودِيَ لِلصَّلَاةِ مِن يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ فَاسْعَوْا إِلَىٰ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ" ("O you who believe, when the call to prayer is made on Friday, hasten to the remembrance of God") [al-Jumu'ah: 9], they said this is a command of God, and the hastening is neither a trot nor a run; rather the merit lies in going early before the call. When the call to prayer is given, one may not stay behind, and the one walking to it walks, catching of it what he catches, without running or trotting. And regarding His saying, "فَإِذَا قُضِيَتِ الصَّلَاةُ فَانتَشِرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ وَابْتَغُوا مِن فَضْلِ اللَّهِ" ("And when the prayer is concluded, disperse through the land and seek the bounty of God") [al-Jumu'ah: 10], this is a command of permission: as He commanded hastening at the call, He permitted departure after the prayer for whoever wishes. Whoever remains in the mosque, praying, listening to the remembrance, and sitting, has the merit and reward of that; and whoever leaves after the prayer is concluded, there is no blame upon him, for God has permitted it to him.

If one enters the mosque on Friday before the prayer, he prays voluntary prayers as he wishes before the Friday prayer, and he may pray voluntary prayers after it as he wishes. They disliked that one who comes when the people have already filled the mosque should climb over their necks. Ali (ع) said: "That I should sit out Jumu'ah is dearer to me than that I should hold back until, when the Imam has sat, I come stepping over the necks of the people." Whoever comes to the mosque when it is full should sit wherever he finds room, without stepping over the necks of the people; but if he finds them spread out so that he can walk between them to a space he sees at the front of the mosque, there is no harm in walking to it. They forbade speech once the Imam has ascended the pulpit, and enjoined the people to face him and to listen attentively to the sermon; when the Imam has finished, there is no harm in speech between that and the raising of the prayer.

When the Imam enters the mosque on Friday, he begins by ascending the pulpit, settles, turns his face to the people, greets them, and sits. The muezzins rise and give the call before him, and when they finish the adhan they sit. He rises, praises God and extols Him, prays upon the Prophet and his family, and exhorts the people as much as he is able and as is prepared for him; then he sits a brief sitting, rises, praises God again and extols Him, prays upon the Prophet and his family, and supplicates for the Imam and the believers as much as he is able. When he has finished, the muezzins rise and call the iqama; the Imam descends and leads the Friday prayer of two rak'ahs. In the first rak'ah, after the opening chapter of the Book, he recites Surah al-Jumu'ah, and in the second, after the opening chapter, Surah al-Munafiqun; he recites aloud, makes qunut after finishing the recitation in the second rak'ah, performs the tashahhud, gives the salam, and departs. He should deliver the sermon only while turbaned and girded with a sword, when that is possible and available to him. Whoever catches the last rak'ah of the Friday prayer with the Imam before the Imam raises his head from bowing has caught that rak'ah, and he adds a second rak'ah to it; but if he does not reach it until the Imam has raised his head, he has not caught Jumu'ah and prays four. If the people miss Jumu'ah they do not pray it; they pray the noon prayer, and if the Imam is absent and none is found to lead them in the congregational mosque, one of them leads them in the noon prayer of four rak'ahs.

As for the prayers of the two Eids, God Almighty said: "شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ الَّذِي أُنزِلَ فِيهِ الْقُرْآنُ" ("The month of Ramadan in which the Quran was sent down"), through to His saying: "وَلِتُكْمِلُوا الْعِدَّةَ وَلِتُكَبِّرُوا اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَاكُمْ وَلَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ" ("that you may complete the number and magnify God for having guided you, and that you may give thanks") [al-Baqarah: 185]. And He said: "وَأَذَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ إِلَى النَّاسِ يَوْمَ الْحَجِّ الْأَكْبَرِ" ("And a proclamation from God and His Messenger to the people on the day of the Greater Pilgrimage") [al-Tawbah: 3]. We have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of God (عليهم السلام) that the day of the Greater Pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice, and that within it fall the Visitation, the Tawaf al-Ifadah, the slaughter, the shaving, the casting of the pebbles. They (عليهم السلام) said that the takbeer is after the completion of the number of the fast, on the night of Fitr and the day of Fitr, and likewise the takbeer is made on Adha, and we shall mention that in its place, God willing.

Ali (ع) said: "It pleases me that a person free himself four nights in the year for prayer and the remembrance of God: the night of Fitr, the night of Adha, the night of the middle of Sha'ban, and the first night of Rajab." And Ali (ع) said: "I heard the Messenger of God (ص) address the people on the day of Adha, saying: 'This is the day of thajj and 'ajj. As for thajj, it is the blood you shed; whoever's intention is sincere, the first drop of it is an expiation for him of every sin. And as for 'ajj, it is supplication, so raise your voices to God. By Him in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, none departs from this standing place but is forgiven, save the one who persists in one of the major sins and does not resolve within himself to abandon it.'"

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they commended ghusl on the day of Fitr and the day of Adha before going out to the prayer ground; that one break his fast on the day of Fitr, with whatever is available, before going out to the prayer ground, and not eat on the day of sacrifice until he returns from the prayer; that the one going out to the two Eids wear the finest clothing he has and perfume himself with what perfume is at hand; and that the Imam be turbaned, whether in winter or summer. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع), when he wished to go out to the two Eids or to Jumu'ah, would say: "O God, whoever has prepared and made ready and equipped and provided himself to arrive before a creature, hoping for its bounty and its gift, then to You, O God, is my preparation, my making ready, my equipping, and my provisioning, hoping for Your bounty, Your gift, and Your favors. I have gone out to You, presenting no righteous deed I have advanced and no intercession of any creature in whom I have placed hope, save the intercession of Muhammad and his family. Rather I have gone out to You confessing my sins and my wrongdoing against myself. O Magnificent, O Magnificent, O Magnificent, forgive the great sin, for none forgives the great sin but You. O Exalted, O Magnificent, there is no god but You." And they (عليهم السلام) related the showing of weapons, equipment, and mounts on the day of Eid, that one may take pride in it and awe the enemy by it. The pulpit is brought out and set up on open ground; and if that is near the burial-ground, the cemetery, that is better. And the Imam goes out.

The Commander of the Believers (ع) used to go out to the two Eids in Kufa proclaiming the takbeer, and around him and before him a thousand of the city's elders and men of mature years proclaimed it with him. So too the people go out in their finest garments, with dignity, magnifying Allah, declaring His oneness, and praising Him as they go and as they return. When they reach the prayer ground, they sit without offering any voluntary prayer before the Eid prayer or after it. They sit in awe and reverence, remembering Allah, not pressing upon one another; each man sits where he has arrived. The rows are to be spaced so that every worshipper has room to prostrate and to bow, for whoever cannot manage that has no valid prayer.

When the imam arrives he begins with the prayer before the sermon, for such is the Sunnah of the Eid, and upon this was the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام) and those who came after him, until 'Uthman changed it. He had prayed with the people at an Eid, and when he finished the prayer most of them dispersed and did not stay to hear the sermon; so thereafter he set the prayer after the sermon, that they might not leave him as they leave on Friday. But it is not fitting that the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام) be altered, or exchanged, or turned from that upon which he established it. Allah, mighty and exalted, has said: ﴿وَمَا آتَاكُمُ الرَّسُولُ فَخُذُوهُ وَمَا نَهَاكُمْ عَنْهُ فَانتَهُوا﴾ [al-Hashr: 7]. And He said: ﴿فَلْيَحْذَرِ الَّذِينَ يُخَالِفُونَ عَنْ أَمْرِهِ أَن تُصِيبَهُمْ فِتْنَةٌ أَوْ يُصِيبَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ﴾ [al-Nur: 63].

The imam opens the prayer without adhan and without iqama, for there is neither adhan nor iqama for the two Eids. He pronounces the takbir al-ihram, then recites the Opening of the Book and Surah al-Shams wa duhaha, reciting aloud. Then he pronounces five takbeers, and between each two of them he offers a light qunut, which is to say: "O Allah, forgive me, have mercy upon me, grant me well-being, and pardon me in this world and the hereafter; You are powerful over all things." Then he pronounces the sixth takbir, rising with it, and prostrates two prostrations. He stands for the second rak'ah and recites the Opening of the Book and Surah Hal ataka hadith al-Ghashiyah. Then he pronounces four takbeers, and between each two of them a light qunut, then the fifth takbir, rising with it, and prostrates two prostrations. Then he gives the tashahhud and the salam.

He departs and ascends the pulpit, and the people fall silent, facing him, seated in their places, and he delivers two sermons as we described for the Friday prayer, save that in the sermon of Eid al-Fitr he mentions the Sunnah of the Fitr charity and enjoins it, and in the sermon of Eid al-Adha he mentions the Sunnah of the sacrifice and urges it upon whoever is able and possesses the means. Then he descends and departs, and the people depart. It is recommended that they return by a route other than the one on which they went out, if they find this possible and are able. Whoever misses the Eid prayer with the imam prays it alone in his house as four rak'ahs. Women are permitted to go out to the two Eids. Upon the people of the open country there is no obligation of gathering or of going out for the two Eids; the Eid is held only in the settled towns, wherever the Friday prayer is gathered. The Eid prayer is established by the same number that establishes the Friday prayer, five men one of whom is the imam; if they are fewer than that, no Eid is held for them. There is no Eid upon the traveler, though if the people of the wilderness attend, and any of the travelers choose to attend the Eid, that is good and there is merit in it for them. It is fitting that one of the people of the wilderness who is within two leagues of the town or nearer should witness the Eid.

If the day of Eid falls upon a Friday, they pray the Eid at its time and the Friday at its time, and the people of the wilderness return to their dwellings if these are distant, once the Eid prayer is done, which they prefer. If the people rise on the day of Eid not knowing of it, and then before midday it becomes clear to them that it is the Eid, they go out and pray the Eid and break their fast if it is Eid al-Fitr. But if they do not know of it until after midday, they break their fast and then go out the following day, at the hour of going out to the Eid prayer, and make up the Eid prayer just as they would have prayed it on the day itself. The time of going out to the two Eids is when the sun has risen somewhat, and they delay going out on the day of Fitr a little later than on the day of Adha.

The takbeer of the days of Tashreeq runs from the Fajr prayer on the day of Arafah to the Asr prayer on the last of the days of Tashreeq; after that Asr the takbeer is pronounced and then cut off. The takbeer of the days of Tashreeq is obligatory upon men and women, in travel and in residence, after every prescribed prayer, and there is no takbeer following a voluntary prayer. The takbeer of the days of Tashreeq is: "Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, la ilaha illa Allah, Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, wa lillahi al-hamd. Allahu akbar 'ala ma hadana, wa Allahu akbar 'ala ma razaqana min baha'im al-an'am." So too is the takbeer while going out to the prayer ground on the day of sacrifice, and on the day of Fitr likewise, except that on it one does not say "for what He has provided us of the grazing beasts." The days of Tashreeq are the three days after the day of sacrifice, named so because in them the people spread the strips of meat, that is, they lay them out for the sun. The day of Arafah is the day before the sacrifice. The day of sacrifice is the day of Eid, following the day of Arafah. The day of settlement follows the day of sacrifice, named so because the people settle upon it at Mina. Then follows the first day of departure, then the last day of departure, which is the last of the days of Tashreeq; on it the takbeer is pronounced after the Asr prayer and then cut off. When the people pray in congregation during the days of Tashreeq, the imam gives the salam and pronounces the takbeer; when he has finished it, those behind him pronounce it, and he raises his voice and they raise theirs with the takbeer. Whoever the imam has preceded in part of the prayer, once the imam has given the salam, rises and makes up what he missed, then pronounces the takbeer after he has given the salam.

As for forgetfulness in the prayer: it is related from the Imams of the House of the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام) that they said: whoever forgets the takbir al-ihram, the opening takbeer, and prays, then learns that he did not pronounce it at the beginning of his prayer, repeats the prayer. Whoever doubts whether he has bowed a rak'ah of his prayer bows, then prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. Whoever doubts and does not know whether he has prayed one rak'ah or two: if it is established that he prayed one, he builds upon it and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness; and if that is not established, he begins the prayer afresh, making the rak'ah he is in the first of his prayer, then prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. If he doubts and does not know whether he has prayed two or three, he builds upon certainty, which is two, and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. If he doubts and does not know whether he has prayed three or four, he gives the salam and prays two rak'ahs seated and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness; for if he had already prayed four, the two are a voluntary prayer for him, and if he had prayed only three, the two rak'ahs from sitting stand in the place of one rak'ah. Thus it has come from the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام), that he said: the prayer of the seated, without excuse, is half the prayer of the standing. If he doubts and does not know whether he has prayed two or four, he gives the tashahhud and the salam, then rises and prays two rak'ahs, reciting in them the Opening of the Book, gives the tashahhud and the salam, and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness; for if he had prayed only two, his prayer is complete, and if he had prayed four, his last two rak'ahs are a voluntary prayer for him, though he should fix his heart upon them as being of the obligation.

Whoever forgets the bowing repeats the prayer. Whoever forgets a prostration, when he remembers, gives the tashahhud and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. Whoever forgets the tashahhud prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness and gives the tashahhud after them and the salam. If he departs and forgets to give the salam, having given the tashahhud, then the salam of the tashahhud suffices him, meaning his words "peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of Allah." Whoever forgets the recitation but has completed the bowing and the prostration owes no repetition; he prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. But if he leaves the recitation deliberately, he repeats the prayer. If he forgets to sit for the first tashahhud and rises for the third rak'ah without sitting, then remembers before he bows, he sits, and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness after he has finished the prayer. In all that we have mentioned, the two prostrations of forgetfulness come after the salam. If he remembers before rising and before speaking, he prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness, then gives a light tashahhud, gives the salam, and departs. If he has forgotten and given the salam after two rak'ahs, then realizes this before he has left his prayer, he completes the prayer, gives the tashahhud, and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. Likewise, if he has left the prayer and spoken, he need only bring what remained upon him and prostrate the two prostrations of forgetfulness.

So too did the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام) do. He forgot and gave the salam after two rak'ahs of a prayer he had shortened, and departed. Dhu al-Yadayn said to him: "Has the prayer been shortened, or have you forgotten, O Messenger of Allah?" He said, "How is that?" He said, "You departed after two rak'ahs." So he rose to the people and asked, "Is what Dhu al-Yadayn said true?" They said, "Yes, O Messenger of Allah." Then he prayed two rak'ahs and gave the salam, then prostrated the two prostrations of forgetfulness, then gave the salam and departed.

Whoever forgets and adds to his prayer: if he had given the tashahhud at the end of his prayer and then rose and added, his prayer is complete and what he added is a voluntary prayer, and he prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. If he had not sat for the tashahhud, and the addition was a bowing, he repeats the prayer; but if the addition was only a prostration, he completes the prayer and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. Whoever forgets and does not know whether he added to his prayer or fell short, his mind inclining to nothing in particular, gives the tashahhud and the salam, then prays two rak'ahs reciting in them the Opening of the Book, gives the tashahhud and the salam, and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. Whoever doubts about the bowing after he has prostrated proceeds with his prayer; likewise if he doubts about the prostration after he has risen, he proceeds and owes nothing until he is certain; and likewise if he doubts about anything of his prayer after he has left it, he owes nothing until he is certain. Whoever neglects his prayer and abandons it deliberately prays it when he remembers. Whoever is often afflicted with forgetfulness proceeds upon his certainty and builds upon it and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. Whoever imagines within his prayer that he has broken his ablution proceeds with the prayer unless he is certain of it. Whoever forgets behind the imam while following him owes nothing.

As for what cuts off the prayer: whoever is met by something he fears for himself, his property, or another, or by some sudden matter that does not permit him to complete his prayer while he is within it, there is no harm in his cutting it off, then beginning and resuming the prayer. The breaking of ablution within the prayer cuts off the prayer; so when the worshipper breaks his ablution he leaves, performs the ablution, and resumes the prayer. Whoever vomits or has a nosebleed in the prayer, and is able to rinse away the vomit and wash off the blood without turning his face from the qibla, does so and builds upon his prayer; but if he turns his face from the qibla, he begins the prayer afresh. Whoever speaks in the prayer has cut it off and owes its repetition. Whoever, while in the prayer, remembers that he is in a state of major impurity or otherwise without purity, cuts it off, purifies himself, and begins the prayer afresh; and if he remembers this after he has prayed, he purifies himself and repeats the prayer, and if he was an imam, all who prayed by his prayer repeat theirs. The worshipper does not cut off the prayer on account of something passing before him, but wards it off as much as he can. Ali (ع) said: the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام) prayed one day, and there passed before him a woman, then a donkey passed, then a dog passed, while we were watching. When he had finished he said, "I have seen what you have seen; nothing cuts off the prayer of the believer. But ward off what you are able to ward off."

As for one who has been preceded in part of the prayer: when a man arrives while the imam is leading the people, and enters into their prayer, he pronounces the takbeer of consecration. If he reaches the imam before he bows, or before he raises his head from the bowing, he bows with him and counts that rak'ah. If he does not reach him until he has raised his head from the bowing, he enters with him in his prayer and prostrates with his prostration but does not count that rak'ah; he counts what comes after it and takes that as the beginning of his prayer. If the imam has preceded him by a rak'ah, he prays the second with him; and when the imam sits for the first tashahhud, the latecomer does not sit fully settled but sits poised to rise, and does not give the tashahhud. When the imam rises for the third rak'ah, it is the latecomer's second. When the imam prostrates, he prostrates with him; and when the imam rises for the third, the latecomer returns briefly to the ground, gives a light tashahhud, then rises. When the imam sits, the latecomer sits as he sat at first, poised, and does not give the tashahhud; and when the imam gives the salam, the latecomer does not give it but rises and makes up a rak'ah, then sits settled, gives the tashahhud and the salam, and departs.

If the imam has preceded him by two rak'ahs, he takes the one he caught with him as the first of his prayer; and when the imam gives the salam, the latecomer does not give it but rises and brings two rak'ahs to complete his prayer, then gives the tashahhud and the salam and departs. The tashahhud he performs at the imam's final one is, for him, the first tashahhud. So too, if he catches only a single rak'ah of the prayer, he builds upon it and makes it for himself the first of his prayer, observing at the imam's sitting and rising the like of what we have described for one who has missed a rak'ah. If it is the Maghrib prayer and the imam has preceded him by a rak'ah, he enters with him in the second, likewise taking it as the first of his prayer, and sits when the imam sits for the first tashahhud, poised as we described, without giving the tashahhud. When the imam rises for the second rak'ah, the latecomer takes it as his own second; and when the imam sits for the last tashahhud, the latecomer sits with him and gives, for himself, the first tashahhud. When the imam gives the salam, the latecomer rises and prays one rak'ah, sits, gives the tashahhud and the salam, and departs. If he does not catch the rak'ah with him, he takes what he has as the first of his prayer, and when the imam sits to give the tashahhud he sits as we described, poised, without giving the tashahhud; and when the imam gives the salam, he rises and prays a rak'ah reciting in it the Opening of the Book and a surah, then sits for the first tashahhud, then rises and completes his prayer. And if the imam has preceded him by a rak'ah in the Fajr prayer, and the imam sits for the tashahhud, the latecomer sits with him as we described, poised, without giving the tashahhud; and when the imam gives the salam, the latecomer rises and prays a rak'ah, then sits, gives the tashahhud and the salam, and departs. If the latecomer forgets and gives the salam with the imam's salam, then remembers, he makes up what he missed and prostrates the two prostrations of forgetfulness. This is the teaching of the Imams of the House of the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام).

If the imam has preceded a latecomer by one rak'ah in the Fajr prayer, and the imam sits for the testimony, the latecomer sits as well in the manner already described, poised and ready, but does not recite the testimony himself. When the imam gives the salutation, the latecomer rises and prays a single rak'ah, then sits, recites the testimony, gives the salutation, and departs. Should the latecomer forget that the imam had already given the salutation and give it with him, then remember, he makes up what he missed and performs the two prostrations of omission. This is the teaching of the imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام).

As for the time at which boys are commanded to pray, the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said: "The pen is lifted from three: from the child until he reaches puberty, from the sleeper until he wakes, and from the insane until he recovers." In this there is proof that prayer does not fall upon boys as a binding obligation until they reach puberty. Yet it has come down from Ali and the imams of his lineage (عليهم السلام) that they directed boys to be taken to prayer once they can understand it, and to fasting once they can bear it, before puberty, not as a strict duty but so that they may be trained in it, grow accustomed to it, and settle into it. Then, when they arrive at the age of puberty, they will already know it, will have been keeping it, and will not be plunged into it all at once in a way that breeds weariness, restlessness, and indifference. For when habits are laid down early, they become like a second nature.

It has likewise come from them (عليهم السلام) that whoever takes up a good deed, whether prayer or fasting or any of the other gates of righteousness, and persists in it, should hold to it without cutting it off before a full year has passed over it. By this they intended to lead people to good by degrees and to make it light upon them. Had they said, "persist in it forever," the weight of it would have fallen heavy on those who heard. But when a man is told, "keep to this for a year," it is easier for him, and once he has kept to it a year it becomes for him a nature and a habit he cannot cut off, for he has grown familiar with it, found ease in it, and taken comfort in it, so that it is hardly possible for him to abandon it. In the same spirit they commanded that boys be taken to prayer and fasting before puberty. They said: children are to be commanded to pray at seven years of age, and disciplined for neglecting it at nine. They are to be separated in their beds at ten, and this separation at that age is a precaution, since their nearness to one another in sleep coincides with their nearness to puberty and the first stirring of desire, lest their closeness in bed lead to anything to be feared. This is the discipline of the friends of Allah toward His servants, whom Allah has entrusted to their care and whose affairs of religion and of the world He has committed to their keeping.

As for the traveler's prayer, the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said: "Allah, blessed and exalted, has bestowed on my nation a gift He has bestowed on no other nation before it," an act of honor from Allah, mighty and majestic. It was said: "O Messenger of Allah, what is this gift?" He said: "The breaking of the fast and the shortening of the prayers in travel. Whoever does not do so has returned to Allah His gift." Ali (عليه السلام) said: "Whoever shortens the prayer in travel has accepted the relief of Allah, mighty and majestic, and his prayer is complete." And it was said that the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) forbade that the prayer be prayed in full during travel. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (عليه السلام) said: "I am innocent of anyone who prays four rak'ahs in travel." Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (عليه السلام) said that whoever prays the prayer in full while traveling, knowing that the sunnah is to shorten it, must repeat the prayer, but whoever does not know this need not repeat it.

They (عليهم السلام) said that shortening in travel means to pray every prayer as two rak'ahs, except Maghrib, which is not shortened in travel or at home. They said that the traveler prays the sunnah and the supererogatory prayers as well; if he is journeying by day and the road presses him on, he prays the obligatory prayer as two rak'ahs and delays the last of the sunnah until he alights at night, when he makes it up together with the night prayer. This they held praiseworthy and did not approve its neglect. The traveler does not pray in full, nor does he pray fewer than the full number in the case of any obligatory prayer, whether in travel or at home.

They (عليهم السلام) said that the distance of travel at which shortening and breaking the fast become obligatory is two barids, twenty-four thousand paces. The barid is four farsakhs, the farsakh is three miles, and the mile is three thousand cubits, which comes to one thousand moderate paces measured by a single leg, from where the right foot is lifted to where it is set down. Even if the place a traveler intends is but a single barid away, and he has there neither family nor dwelling, and intends to return from it within less than ten days, he shortens and breaks his fast until he departs. The traveler breaks his fast and shortens his prayers from the moment he leaves his house and sets out on his journey, until he returns and enters his house, provided he is pressing on in his travel.

If a traveler enters a congregational prayer led by residents, he gives the salutation after two rak'ahs and departs. If a traveler leads residents in prayer, he ends at two rak'ahs and then bids them complete the prayer. Both of these are disliked, as has been mentioned, because the ruling of the traveler in prayer is not the ruling of the resident. Whoever has an unpaid prayer from his time of residence and remembers it while traveling makes it up in full as it was owed. Whoever has an unpaid prayer from his travel and remembers it while resident makes it up according to how it was, shortened.

The traveler may pray voluntary prayers while mounted, wherever he turns, toward the Qibla or away from it. This rests upon the word of Allah, mighty and majestic: ﴿فَأَيْنَمَا تُوَلُّوا فَثَمَّ وَجْهُ اللَّهِ﴾ "Wherever you turn, there is the face of Allah" [al-Baqarah: 115]. The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) used to pray voluntary prayers upon his mount on his return from the Farewell Pilgrimage, with the House behind his back. But this is permitted only in the supererogatory prayer; the obligatory prayer is not prayed except upon the ground, turned toward the Qibla. They forbade praying upon the beaten track, that is, the highway where people pass, or in the space between two roads.

At sea the traveler shortens the prayer and prays aboard the ship. If the ship turns, he turns his face with it toward the Qibla; if he cannot pray standing, he prays seated. One who is drowning, one who is naked, and one wading through water pray by gesture, making the prostration lower than the bowing, and the naked person likewise prays seated when he finds nothing to cover his nakedness.

Shortening is not for one who goes out merely to take his ease, nor for a governor making his rounds among his people, nor for a tax-gatherer moving about in his collection, nor for the hired driver, nor for those seafarers whose very calling is perpetual travel, nor for one who alights in his travel upon a household of his own intending to stay with them. Whoever alights at a place in his travel intending to remain ten days prays in full; if he intends less, he shortens. If his stay is drawn out without such intention, while he intends each day to depart, he shortens for as long as a month, and after the month he prays in full until he leaves.

As for the prayer of the sick, we have it from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that if the sick person cannot pray standing, he prays lying on his back with the Qibla before his face in the direction of his feet, or lying on his right side with his face toward the Qibla, gesturing so that his prostration is lower than his bowing. Likewise, one who prays seated and cannot bow and prostrate does the same. One afflicted with a nosebleed that will not stop, or a wound that keeps discharging and cannot be bound and cannot be held back, performs ablution and prays in that state, for Allah is most ready to excuse His servant when He has tried him.

If the sick person cannot pray anything beyond the obligatory, then when he recovers he may, if he wishes, make up the sunnah and supererogatory prayers he left, and in that there is merit and reward, though their making-up is not binding as the making-up of the obligatory is binding. If he can pray seated with his legs folded, he folds them as he would fold them in sitting during the standing; if he cannot, he prays however he is able and it suffices him. It suffices him to recite the Opening of the Book alone, and if he can do no other, to say a single glorification in his bowing and his prostration when he can manage no more. If he is able to prostrate, he prostrates; if not, he nods with his head, making the prostration lower than the bowing; and if he cannot nod his head, he signals with what he can, his head or his eye, as he is able, for Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity. If he is able to pray standing, he prays only so, unless standing strengthens his illness and increases it, in which case he may pray in the manner described according to what he can do. Whoever falls into a swoon makes up the prayers he missed, be they few or many, when he comes to.

As for the prayer of fear, Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿فَلَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ أَن تَقْصُرُوا مِنَ الصَّلَاةِ إِنْ خِفْتُمْ أَن يَفْتِنَكُمُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا إِنَّ الْكَافِرِينَ كَانُوا لَكُمْ عَدُوًّا مُّبِينًا وَإِذَا كُنتَ فِيهِمْ فَأَقَمْتَ لَهُمُ الصَّلَاةَ فَلْتَقُمْ طَائِفَةٌ مِّنْهُم مَّعَكَ﴾ "There is no blame upon you if you shorten the prayer, if you fear that those who disbelieve may harm you. Indeed, the disbelievers are to you a clear enemy. And when you are among them and lead them in prayer, let a party of them stand with you" [al-Nisa': 101 to 102], down to His words: "a humiliating punishment." Thus Allah, mighty and majestic, granted the relief of shortening the prayer in the state of fear, making no exception in it of travel from residence. Abu Abd-Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn Ali (عليه السلام) was asked about the prayer of fear and the prayer of travel, whether both are shortened, and he said: "Yes, and the prayer of fear is more deserving of shortening than the prayer of travel, for in the prayer of travel there is no fear."

We have it from the imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) prayed the prayer of fear in the campaign of Dhat al-Riqa'. He divided his companions into two parties, stationing one facing the enemy and placing the other behind him. He said the opening takbir and they said it, he recited and they listened, he bowed and they bowed, he prostrated and they prostrated. Then the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) stood, and this party prayed a further rak'ah on their own, gave the salutation one to another, and withdrew to their companions, standing in their place facing the enemy. Then the other party came and stood behind the Messenger of Allah ((ع)). He said the takbir and they said it, he recited and they listened, he bowed and they bowed, he prostrated and they prostrated, and he sat and they sat and bore witness. When the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) gave the salutation, they rose and made up a further rak'ah for themselves, bore witness, gave the salutation one to another, and withdrew.

They (عليهم السلام) said: thus is the prayer of fear prayed, whenever it is possible, in all the prayers save Maghrib, which is not shortened, as was mentioned before. The first party prays one rak'ah with the imam and prays two rak'ahs for itself, according to what we have described of the prayer of the Messenger of Allah ((ع)), and the other party prays two rak'ahs with him, so that each party may hear the recitation in the first two rak'ahs, in which he recites aloud.

They (عليهم السلام) said: if they cannot pray in this manner and the fear grows severe, they pray mounted upon their beasts or standing on their feet, nodding with their heads and making the prostration lower than the bowing, neither dismounting nor prostrating upon the ground when they are unable to do so. If they cannot face the Qibla, they face it at the first takbir and then turn to meet the enemy. This is the word of Allah, mighty and majestic: ﴿فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ فَرِجَالًا أَوْ رُكْبَانًا﴾ "If you are in fear, then on foot or riding" [al-Baqarah: 239]. If the fighting closes in and there is the clash of swords and the thrust of spears so that they cannot even gesture, they pray with the takbir alone, saying a takbir for each rak'ah with the intention of prayer. In just this way the Commander of the Believers, Ali (عليه السلام), and his companions prayed on the night and the day of al-Harir.

As for the eclipse prayer, Ali (عليه السلام) said that the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said: "The moon was eclipsed while Jibril (عليه السلام) was with me, and I said to him: 'What is this, O Jibril?' He said: 'Truly it is more obedient to Allah than you, and it has not disobeyed Allah since He created it; but this is a sign and a lesson.' I said: 'What is to be done at such a time?' He said: 'Prayer and the recitation of the Qur'an.'" When the sun or the moon was eclipsed, he ((ع)) would say to the people: "Hasten to your mosques."

We have it from the imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that the prayer for the eclipse of the sun and of the moon is one and the same, save that the prayer for the eclipse of the sun is a little longer. When an eclipse occurs, the people are to be summoned to prayer without the adhan, and they gather at the Grand Mosque if they are in a city. The imam comes forward and they draw up behind him. He says the takbir aloud and they say it, then he begins the recitation with the Opening of the Book and the longest surah he is able to recite, reciting aloud. Then he says the takbir and bows, and they bow, and he remains long in the bowing and they remain with him for as long as he recited, glorifying and saying what is said in the bowing. Then he raises his head with a takbir and they raise theirs, and he recites the Opening of the Book and a long surah like the first, and after the recitation and before the bowing he makes the qunut, then bows in the same way and rises with a takbir. So he does through five bowings, making the qunut after each pair. When he raises his head from the fifth, he says: "Allah hears the one who praises Him," saying this only there and in the tenth, at which he prostrates. In the remaining bowings he says "Allahu akbar" when he raises his head, and then prostrates.

He draws out the prostration as he drew out the bowing, glorifying and saying what is said in prostration. Then he raises his head and sits a while between the two prostrations, saying what is said between them in its fullest form, then makes the second prostration in the same way, rises, and prays a further five bowings like the first. Then he prostrates and sits for a long testimony in which he calls upon Allah, turns to Him in longing, and asks Him for what he can of this world and the Hereafter in supplication, and then gives the salutation. If the eclipse has cleared, he departs; if not, he remains seated in his place facing the Qibla and bids the people sit likewise, reciting the Qur'an, supplicating, and remembering Allah until it clears or passes away.

The eclipse prayer is not prayed until the eclipse is seen and made plain. No one relies in this on the word of an astrologer that the sun has been eclipsed when its eclipse has not been seen, or that says it was eclipsed by night, or the moon by day, when it is not seen. The prayer for the eclipse of the sun is prayed only by day, and the prayer for the eclipse of the moon only by night, after the eclipse has been seen. The qunut in the eclipse prayer is in every pair of bowings before the bowing, in the second, the fourth, the sixth, the eighth, and the tenth. There is no harm in reciting in each rak'ah, at each standing, a surah or more, apportioning the recitation across the bowings. If he does this, he recites the Opening of the Book only at the outset with the surah, making the beginning of each standing the opening of a surah, and likewise when he rises from the prostration into the second.

The eclipse prayer is not to be performed until the eclipse has actually been seen and confirmed. One should not rely on someone merely saying that the sun has eclipsed when he has not seen it himself, nor on a claim made at night that the moon has eclipsed by day, nor by day that the sun has eclipsed by night. The solar eclipse prayer is prayed only during the day, and the lunar eclipse prayer only at night, and in both cases only after the eclipse has been seen.

The qunut in this prayer is performed in every second rak'ah, before the second, fourth, sixth, eighth, and tenth bowings. It is also permitted to recite in every rak'ah, and more than that, before bowing. When one does so, the Opening of the Book is recited only at the outset of each surah, so that he makes it the first opening of the surah, and likewise when he rises from the prostration in the second rak'ah of the eclipse prayer.

These two rak'ahs, with their four prostrations and their five bowings in each rak'ah, were the form in which the Commander of the Faithful, 'Ali (ع), led the people in the eclipse prayer. He prayed it at any time, by night or by day, whether travelling or resident in a city. If the eclipse fell at the time of an obligatory prayer, he would begin with the obligatory prayer. But if he had already entered the eclipse prayer and feared that the time of the obligatory prayer would lapse, he would interrupt the eclipse prayer, perform the obligatory prayer, and then, having finished it, resume and build upon what he had already completed of the eclipse prayer. In the same manner he prayed at every sign that occurred, whether wind, darkness, tremors, earthquakes, or any other portent, just as the eclipse prayer is prayed. Whoever misses the eclipse prayer, or the prayer of the signs, does not make it up. It is recommended that the eclipse prayer be performed in an open, cleared space so that the clearing of the eclipse may be seen. If the imam departs, those who can see the eclipse should be informed of it; and if he does not depart, the people are instructed to remain seated in their places, as we have mentioned.

As for the prayer of al-Istisqa, the seeking of rain, Allah Almighty said: "And when Moses sought water for his people, We said: Strike the rock with your staff" (Al-Baqarah: 60). It is narrated that when the people of Moses (ع) were in need of water, and rain later fell upon Madinah, the Messenger of Allah went out and prayed for it, and they were so drenched that they grew concerned for themselves from the intensity of the rain. We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that the prayer of al-Istisqa is like the two Eid prayers. The imam goes out, and the people go out to the prayer ground in humility before their Lord, in fear and self-abasement, and they do not carry weapons as they do for the Eid. The imam prays two rak'ahs without the call to prayer and without the iqamah, giving the takbeer as he gives it for the two festival prayers, and reciting in them as he recites for the two Eids. Then he ascends the pulpit, and when he reaches the top he sits briefly, then stands and turns his cloak, placing what was on his right side onto his left and what was on his left onto his right, as the Messenger of Allah did.

He begins his sermon with the takbeer, the tahleel, and the seeking of forgiveness, offering as much of this as he is able. Then he praises Allah and sends blessings upon the Prophet and his family, and he urges the people to seek forgiveness, to repent, and to do good deeds, making them aware that rain is withheld on account of their sins. He repents to Allah Almighty on their behalf, asks Him, and implores Him for water and mercy to the extent that he is able. It is recommended that this prayer be performed on a Monday.

Concerning the prayers of the Shaf', the Witr, and the two rak'ahs of Fajr, Allah Almighty said: "By the even and the odd" (Al-Fajr: 3), swearing by all of that. The shaf', in the speech of the Arabs, is the paired or even, and the witr is the odd. In prayer, the witr consists of three rak'ahs, a sunnah of the night prayer performed after the final Isha, and no prayer is prayed after it until the dawn breaks. The name witr is applied to all three rak'ahs; it may also refer to the single final rak'ah, with the two rak'ahs before it being the shaf', and this is what is preferred in what is established from the words of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام). The worshipper gives the salam after the two rak'ahs, then stands and prays a single rak'ah as witr.

There has come strong encouragement toward the witr, which the Messenger of Allah commanded, saying that Allah is witr and loves the witr, so pray the witr, O people of the Qur'an. 'Ali (ع) was severe regarding it and did not permit its abandonment. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that the witr ought not to be abandoned, yet it is not among the prescribed obligatory prayers but is of the sunnah; it is recommended that whoever misses it make it up, though this is not obligatory upon him as a prescribed duty, since it is not among the obligations. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) granted a dispensation that the witr may be prayed while travelling upon a mount, as the supererogatory prayers are prayed. They preferred that the witr be prayed at the end of the night, and whoever prays it at the beginning of the night has fulfilled it.

They preferred that in its two rak'ahs one recite the Opening of the Book, and in the first of them "Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High, who created" (Al-A'la), and in the second the Opening of the Book and "Say: O disbelievers" (Al-Kafirun), and in the third "Say: He is Allah, the One" (Al-Ikhlas) after the Opening of the Book. In the witr he performs the qunut after bowing, raising his hands in it and supplicating with whatever comes readily to him. In the Fajr prayer he performs the qunut before bowing and after the recitation. The two rak'ahs of Fajr are prayed before the Fajr prayer: the call to Fajr is given, and then he prays two rak'ahs after the dawn has broken. It is preferred to keep them light, reciting in the first the Opening of the Book and "Say: O disbelievers," and in the second the Opening of the Book and "Say: He is Allah, the One." He then makes the tashahhud, remains seated in his place of prayer, and continues to supplicate until he prays the Fajr. They said (عليهم السلام) that there is nothing in supplication that is fixed and appointed such that no other will suffice in its place, neither in the prayer nor outside it; whoever wishes to supplicate may call upon Allah Almighty with whatever he wishes.

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) many forms of supplication and qunut, all of which show that there is nothing fixed and appointed in it, as we have said. The least and lightest of the qunut is that one glorify Allah within it with three glorifications. There have come from them (عليهم السلام) long, short, and medium supplications for the qunut of the witr and the qunut of the Fajr. Among what has come from them of the medium kind, upon which the narrations agree, is that they instructed that one say in the qunut of the witr:

"O Allah, You see and are not seen, and You are in the highest of vantages. To You are lifted the eyes and moved the feet; toward You necks are stretched and hands spread open; by tongues You are called upon, and to You deeds are submitted. O You to whom hands are stretched out, O You to whom hearts have turned, O You before whom eyes are cast down, O You to whom necks are bowed: we complain to You of the hardship of the age, the massing of enemies, the fewness of our number, and the division of our hearts. We give thanks to You for Your blessings through our guardian and our imam, the son of the daughter of our Prophet, named the imam of his age, our guide to You and the one who points us to You. We ask You to send blessings upon him and upon his forefathers, and to support him with a victory by which You give might to Your religion and by which You aid Your friends. O Allah, gather hearts upon Your obedience and his obedience and upon holding to his imamate, and support him against his renegade enemies, O God of creation, Lord of the worlds. O Allah, make certainty firm in my heart, and increase me in light, guidance, and forgiveness, and guide me to Your straight path. Ameen, Ameen. I ask You, my Lord, for good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and I ask You to guard me from the torment of the Fire."

Whoever wishes to lengthen the supplication may add to it as he wishes. And in the qunut of the Fajr:

"O Allah, we praise You, and seek Your help, and seek Your forgiveness, and extol You with good, and give thanks to You and do not deny You; we humble ourselves to You and submit to You, and we disown whoever is wicked toward You and denies You. O Allah, You alone we worship, and to You we pray and prostrate, and to You we strive and hasten; we hope for Your mercy and fear Your severe punishment, for Your punishment surely overtakes the disbelievers. O Allah, punish the disbelievers, the hypocrites, the liars, and those who deny Your friends, the imams from the pure family of Your Prophet, and send down upon them Your scourge, Your might, Your anger, and Your torment. O Allah, punish the disbelievers among the People of the Book, and the idolaters, men and women. Forgive me, and the believing men and women and the Muslim men and women, and set right what is between them, and unite their word upon Your obedience and the obedience of Your friends, and make firm in their hearts faith and wisdom, and steady them upon the religion of Your Prophet, and support them against Your enemy and their enemy. O Allah, guide me among those You have guided, take me into Your care among those You have taken, heal me among those You have healed, bless me in what You have given, and protect me from the evil of what You have decreed, for You decree and none decrees against You. He whom You befriend is not humiliated, and he whom You oppose is not honoured. Blessed are You and exalted. There is no god but You. I seek Your forgiveness and repent to You, and I ask You, my Lord, for good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and I ask You to guard me from the torment of the Fire."

And in the qunut of the Friday prayer and in the qunut of the eclipse:

"There is no god but Allah, the Wise, the Generous. There is no god but Allah, the High, the Great. Glory be to Allah, Lord of the seven heavens and Lord of the seven earths and all that is within them and between them, and Lord of the Mighty Throne. Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds. O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad, and make my heart firm upon Your religion and the religion of Your Prophet, and let not my heart swerve after You have guided me, and grant me mercy from Yourself, for You are the Bestower. O Allah, make me one of those You have created for Your Garden and chosen for Your religion, and turn to me in acceptance, for You are capable of all things, and You are the Accepter of repentance, the Merciful. And send blessings upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad, as You are worthy of it and as they are worthy of it from You, may (عليهم السلام)."

As for the obligatory prayer, it is seventeen rak'ahs in each day and night. The Dhuhr prayer is the first of them, called the first because it was the first prayer that the Messenger of Allah prayed, at the hour in which the obligation of prayer was sent down upon him. It is four rak'ahs. In the first two he recites the Opening of the Book and a surah and makes the tashahhud after them, and in the last two he recites the Opening of the Book alone, then makes the tashahhud and the tasleem. The Asr is likewise four rak'ahs, as Dhuhr, with the same recitation. The Maghrib is three rak'ahs: he makes the tashahhud after two rak'ahs, reciting aloud in them, then rises and prays a third rak'ah in which he recites the Opening of the Book silently, then sits, makes the tashahhud, and departs. The final Isha is four rak'ahs, like Dhuhr, except that he recites aloud in its first two rak'ahs. Then the Fajr is two rak'ahs in which he recites aloud. That is seventeen rak'ahs.

The sunnah prayer is the like of the obligatory prayer in number, its recitation all silent, though if one wishes he may recite a little aloud in the night prayer. He gives the salam from all of it after every two rak'ahs, and in each rak'ah he recites the Opening of the Book and a surah. These are six rak'ahs before Dhuhr, after the sun has passed its zenith, and four after it; four rak'ahs before Asr, after which there is no prayer until the sun sets; six rak'ahs after Maghrib before the final Isha, and four before Isha and four after it; and the witr, three rak'ahs, from which he gives the salam after two, then rises and prays the third, then prays two rak'ahs seated; and two rak'ahs before the Fajr prayer. That is, in number, thirty-five rak'ahs; but since the two rak'ahs seated are counted as one rak'ah of standing, the total is thirty-four, which is the like of the obligatory prayer. It is called the sunnah prayer because the Messenger of Allah used to pray it and bound himself to it, and likewise the imams from his progeny (عليهم السلام) bound themselves to it, commanded their friends and their followers to it, encouraged them toward it, and mentioned to them its merit and its reward.

As for the supererogatory prayer, it has no limit. It is a voluntary act; whoever wishes prays of it what he wishes at the times in which prayer is permitted, by night or by day. It is narrated of 'Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) that he used to pray a thousand rak'ahs in each day and night when he was able, and the most excellent of this is what is prayed at night and kept vigil with after sleep. Allah Almighty said to His Prophet Muhammad: "And in a part of the night keep vigil with it, as an additional prayer for you; perhaps your Lord will raise you to a praised station" (Al-Isra: 79). Of a man it is said that he "hajada" when he sleeps, and "tahajjada" when he rises from sleep for some matter he intends; so he awakes from his sleep for it and keeps vigil with it as an additional prayer, meaning with the Qur'an. And He said: "O you who are wrapped in garments, rise the night except a little, half of it, or lessen from it a little" (Al-Muzzammil: 1-4), to the end of it.

The Messenger of Allah used to stand at night until his feet would swell from the length of his standing, and he forbade a man to sleep the whole night through, so that he would be like a carcass thrown down. It is recommended to prolong the standing in the night prayer, and one ought not to abandon it, but rather to set for himself a fixed portion of it. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) said: I hold in contempt the servant who has read the Qur'an and then wakes in the night yet does not rise, until, when the morning draws near, he rises and hurries through his prayer. Whoever has not read the whole of the Qur'an may recite of it what comes readily to him. Allah Almighty said: "So recite what is easy for you of the Qur'an" (Al-Muzzammil: 20). Whoever wishes may stand the whole night with a single surah that he repeats, or with part of it, or with two surahs, or with more than that. There is no harm in reciting, in the supererogatory prayer, part of a surah in the rak'ah together with the Opening of the Book, or in joining two surahs within it, or many surahs. Some of them would stand the whole night, or most of it, with a single verse that he repeated whenever he came to it, a verse of promise and warning, pondering it and repeating it so that he might understand it and grow humble before it. They preferred to open the night prayer with two light rak'ahs and then to pray after them whatever one wishes.

The most excellent form of the supererogatory prayer, of charity, and of all the works of righteousness is what is done in concealment and in secret; and the most excellent way to render the whole of the obligatory acts, the prayer, the zakat, and others, is openly. All the supererogatory prayers are not to be prayed in congregation, neither in the month of Ramadan nor in any other, and the people are to pray them only singly. The most excellent place for them is the houses, for in their concealment and secrecy lies their merit. The Messenger of Allah once prayed a voluntary prayer at night in the mosque during the month of Ramadan, and the people ranged themselves behind him, praying with his prayer, so he forbade them from that, entered his chamber, and prayed within it. The first to make the standing in congregation during Ramadan a practice was 'Umar ibn al-Khattab; yet had there been merit in it, the Messenger of Allah would not have forbidden it, and he would have been the foremost to reach it before 'Umar and others. The common people have narrated this very account from the Messenger of Allah, and then followed 'Umar in it, in opposition to the Prophet and in defiance of his prohibition. And Allah Almighty says: "Let those who go against his command beware, lest a trial befall them or a painful punishment strike them" (Al-Nur: 63), and He says: "Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it, and whatever he forbids you, abstain from it, and fear Allah" (Al-Hashr: 7). The most they have said in this matter is that the Messenger of Allah disliked it and forbade it only for those who did it with him, lest it become an obligation upon the people.

You do not claim that he granted a license to others in this matter, for had there been a license in it he would have spoken of it as a prophet, may Allah bless him and his family. When he forbade a thing, it was because Allah Almighty had made the ruling plain, and Allah had made the clear declaration an obligation upon him. So you are bound to refrain from what he forbade you, just as Allah Almighty commanded, unless a clear allowance reaches you for what you claim is permitted. They claim it was permitted, yet the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, neither did it in the days of his life, nor in the days of Abu Bakr, nor in the early days of 'Umar. It appeared only in 'Umar's later period. By their own reckoning this makes it an innovation, and some of them acknowledge this while calling it a good innovation. But this contradicts the word of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family: "Every innovation is misguidance, and every misguidance leads to the Fire. Follow, and do not innovate." How can an innovation be good when Allah Almighty forbade it upon the tongue of His Messenger, may Allah bless him and his family?

In the same way they invented the Duha prayer, which the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, had forbidden. A man of the Ansar asked Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) about it, and he answered him: "Were your own people, the Ansar, not the first to invent it?" The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, had forbidden it. They used to come from their homes and enter the mosque in the forenoon and pray, and the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, forbade them from that. Then Yazid ibn Mu'awiyah revived it at the severed head of al-Husayn (ع) in the forenoon: he rose and prayed two rak'ahs and made it a fixed practice for himself, not out of anything his people held against him for what he had committed, but taking it, by his own claim, as a thanksgiving for what he had done, though it will only sink him deeper in the blaze of Hell.

'Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) held that a person should make up whatever voluntary prayers he had missed when he was kept occupied from them or overtaken by sleep. This is not an obligation, but he loved, may Allah's blessings be upon him, not to cut off or let go to waste anything that Allah Almighty had made a part of his devotion. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, said: "Whoever intends to rise in the night, and takes to his bed, let him say: 'O Allah, do not leave me safe from Your cunning, and do not make me forget Your remembrance, and do not place me among the heedless. I will rise, Allah willing, at such and such an hour.' Then Allah Almighty appoints for him an angel, an angel of that hour, who rouses him." And he said: "Whoever intends something of the night vigil, and sleep overcomes his eyes until morning, his sleep is a charity to him from Allah, and Allah owes him the reward of his intention and completes for him the vigil of his night."

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, said: "Allah has an angel who, when half the night has passed, declares: 'Glorified and Holy is our Lord, the Lord of the angels and the Spirit, our Lord the Merciful, there is no god but He. Let those who strive arise.' So whoever Allah wills arises. Then he falls silent as long as Allah wills, then he says: 'Glorified and Holy is our Lord, the Lord of the angels and the Spirit, our Lord the Merciful, there is no god but He. Let those who remember arise.' So whoever Allah wills arises. Then when dawn breaks he says: 'Glorified and Holy is our Lord, the Lord of the angels and the Spirit, our Lord the Merciful, there is no god but He. Let the heedless arise.'"

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) said that when a third of the night has passed a caller cries out: "O seeker of good, draw near. O seeker of evil, hold back. Is there one who repents, that he may be turned to in mercy? Is there one who seeks forgiveness, that he may be forgiven? Is there one who asks, that his request may be granted? O Allah, grant every one who spends a replacement, and grant every one who withholds ruin." This call goes out every night from the passing of a third of the night until morning, and on the night of Friday from the first of the night to its last. He said: when a third of the night remains, the gates of heaven are opened, and the angel calls out in this way until morning.

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and his family, said: "The people of the highest ranks are set level in the Garden upon dappled horses whose bridles are of gold and whose saddles are of pearl and ruby. They pass by those who are below them, who say: 'Our Lord, by what have You brought Your servants to this honour?' And it is said to them: 'They used to rise in the night while you slept, and they used to fast in the day while you ate, and they used to fight while you shrank in cowardice, and they used to spend in the way of Allah while you were miserly.'"

As for the mention of prostration in the Qur'an, Allah Almighty said: ﴿ وَإِذَا قُرِئَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْقُرْآنُ لَا يَسْجُدُونَ ﴾ [al-Inshiqaq: 21], meaning at the places of prostration within it. We have narrated from the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that there are fifteen prostrations in the Qur'an. Whoever recites them, or hears them recited, should prostrate. Some of them are obligatory, such that one is not permitted to omit the prostration at that point, and we will note these as they come.

The first prostration of the Qur'an is at the end of Surah al-A'raf. Then in Surah al-Ra'd, at "and their shadows in the mornings and evenings." In Surah al-Nahl, at "and they do what they are commanded." In Surah al-Isra', at "and it increases them in reverence." In Surah Maryam, at "they fell down prostrate and weeping." In Surah al-Hajj, at "Allah does what He wills," and in it also at "and do good, that you may succeed." In Surah al-Furqan, at "and it increases their aversion." In Surah al-Naml, at "the Lord of the mighty Throne." In Surah al-Sajdah, at "and they are not arrogant," and this is one of the obligatory ones. In Surah Sad, at "and he bowed low and turned in repentance." In Surah Fussilat, at "if it is He whom you worship," and this is one of the obligatory ones. And at the end of Surah al-Najm, which is likewise obligatory. In Surah al-Inshiqaq, at "and when the Qur'an is recited to them, they do not prostrate." And at the end of Surah al-'Alaq, at "Read in the name of your Lord who created," and this too is one of the obligatory ones.

Whoever is in prayer and hears a reciter reciting a verse of prostration, or has it recited before him, and does not prostrate for it, should incline his head in a nod, or he may form the intention of prostration if it is one of the obligatory ones. They disliked reciting in an obligatory prayer a surah containing one of the obligatory prostrations, for if he recites it he must prostrate, even should the verse fall at the end of the surah. In that case he prostrates, then rises and recites the Opening of the Book, then bows and prostrates. In what lies outside the obligatory prostrations he has the choice: if he wishes he prostrates, and if he wishes he does not. Whoever recites a verse of prostration while riding prostrates in whatever direction he faces, nodding with his head. There is no takbir in the prostration of the Qur'an, and no tashahhud, and no taslim. It is to be performed by whoever hears it, whether he was attentively listening to the Qur'an or reciting it himself, even if he is not in a state of ablution. He glorifies Allah while prostrate, and asks of Allah Almighty and calls upon Him with whatever he wishes.
