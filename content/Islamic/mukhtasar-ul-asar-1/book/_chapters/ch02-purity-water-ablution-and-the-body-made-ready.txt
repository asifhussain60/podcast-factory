# Purity: Water, Ablution, and the Body Made Ready

The comprehensive chapters on purity begin here.

**On the merits of purity**

God Almighty said: *"Indeed, God loves those who constantly repent and loves those who purify themselves"* [Surah al-Baqarah: 222]. And He said: *"Within it are men who love to purify themselves, and God loves those who purify themselves"* [Surah al-Tawbah: 108]. And He said: *"And He sent down upon you rain from the sky to purify you thereby, and to remove the harm of Satan from you, and to fortify your hearts and make your feet firm thereby"* [Surah al-Anfal: 11].

The Messenger of God (ص) said: "God does not accept prayer except with purity." And he said: "God will gather my community on the Day of Resurrection among the nations, radiant and distinguished by the traces of ablution." And he said: "When I was taken up to the heavens, I was asked concerning what the denizens of the highest assembly were disputing. I said, 'I do not know,' and then it was made known to me: the thorough completion of ablution in times of cold, the walking of feet to the congregations, and the awaiting of prayer after prayer."

From Ali (ع) it is reported that he said: "Ablution is half of faith." And he said: "Whoever purifies himself and performs the purification well, then walks to the mosque, is in a state of prayer so long as nothing occurs to break it." He (ع) would perform ablution for every prayer even when nothing had occurred to break it, seeking thereby the merit of purity and its reward, for the excellence that has been reported concerning it.

**On the purity and impurity of water**

God Almighty said: *"And We sent down from the sky pure water"* [Surah al-Furqan: 48]. And He said: *"And He sent down upon you from the sky water to purify you thereby"* [Surah al-Anfal: 11]. And He also said: *"Have you not seen that God sent down water from the sky, then caused it to flow as springs in the earth?"* [Surah al-Zumar: 21]. By this He, exalted be His praise, indicated that the water of the earth originates from the sky, and the reports have confirmed its purity.

The Messenger of God (ص) said: "Water is a purifier; nothing renders it impure." And he said concerning the sea: "Its water is a purifier and its dead are lawful." By this he meant what is extracted from it of that which is permissible to eat, which then dies after being brought out, not what dies while still within it. For God Almighty said: *"Lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food, as provision for you and for travelers"* [Surah al-Ma'idah: 96], that is, what is drawn from it and eaten outside, not what is eaten beneath the water or at its floor. By "its dead" he meant what dies after having been brought out alive. As for what dies within the water before it is caught, it is not lawful to eat, according to the position of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام), and that is the fish which floats. We will address this in the chapter on foods, God willing.

As for water that occurs naturally, in whose making man has no hand, it is pure. God Almighty made clear in His Book, and His Messenger (ص) made clear in his speech, that if something is cast into water and alters it, transforming it from the attribute of water, the ruling of water is removed from it, and it is ascribed instead to whatever has been cast into it and prevailed over it. This is like water into which dates, raisins, or honey are thrown: if they do not change it and remain undiscernible within it, its ruling remains the ruling of water, until what was cast into it becomes evident and manifest, altering its color or its taste. At that point it becomes a fermented drink, the name of water ceases to apply to it, and purification with it is no longer permitted.

I know of no disagreement among the Muslims on this matter, except for the position held alone by Abu Hanifah and those of his followers who agreed with him. He said that it is permissible to perform ablution with date wine in particular, though not with the other cooked beverages. His companion Abu Yusuf disagreed with him in this and said that ablution is valid with nothing but water. Muhammad ibn al-Hasan said: "If you do not find water, perform ablution with date wine, then perform tayammum." This is a position without meaning. For God Almighty said: *"And if you find no water, then betake yourselves to clean earth"* [Surah al-Nisa': 43]. If date wine is not water, there is no sense in using it for purification; and if it is water, there is no sense in the tayammum. What led Abu Hanifah into this was the report that reached him, that the Messenger of God (ص) performed ablution with date wine on the Night of the Jinn. This hadith is not established; and even were it established, that drink would not be what people know today as date wine. Rather, the Prophet (ص) used to steep dates in the waterskin from which he drank, in order to sweeten the water for him when it was brackish. He would drink from it and perform ablution with it so long as it had not changed. We have already mentioned that there is no harm in this, and that it does not strip the ruling of water from the water unless it alters it and transforms it from its attribute.

The same holds for milk that mixes with water: if the milk is not discernible within it, it remains water in its state until the milk prevails, at which point its ruling becomes the ruling of milk and the name of water is removed from it. Likewise for whatever drinks and other things mix with water. Upon this same principle stands whatever impurity mixes with water and enters into it: if it becomes apparent within it and alters something of its color, its taste, or its smell, the water is impure and takes on the ruling of that impurity. But if it does not appear within it and cannot be discerned, the water remains in its state, lawful to drink and to use for purification. This is the agreed position of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام), established from them, and those who claim mastery of reasoned inquiry among the companions of al-Shafi'i, the people of hadith, and many attributed to jurisprudence among the general body have concurred with them upon it.

If the color, smell, or taste of water changes through something other than impurity that has mixed into it, as with stagnant water that sits long in its place and is thereby altered, it remains upon its purity, for it has not been changed by impurity. Ali (ع) said: "Nothing renders water impure," meaning so long as the ruling of water applies to it, as we have described. He (ع) was asked about running water that passes by a carcass, excrement, and blood, whether one may perform ablution from it and drink from it, and he said: "Yes," meaning so long as none of those things is discernible within it. He was asked about a pool in which beasts had urinated and dropped dung and in which the one in janabah had bathed, and he said: "There is no harm in it. The Messenger of God (ص) once halted with his companions on a journey at a pool, and their mounts would urinate in it and drop dung in it, and they would bathe in it, perform ablution from it, and drink from it."

He (ع) was asked about a small ablution basin near the mosque, into which a menstruating woman puts her hand and a boy puts his hand, and he said: "Perform ablution from it, for the water is defiled by nothing." He was asked about a pool beside a village, into which a child and the beasts urinate and drop dung, and he said: "If something troubles your heart concerning it, do this," and he gestured with his hand, meaning stir the water, "then drink and perform ablution, for the religion is not a narrow thing." And God Almighty said: *"And He has laid upon you no hardship in religion"* [Surah al-Hajj: 78]. He was asked about a pool in which there was a carcass, and he said: "If the water is dominant and no smell is found in it, then perform ablution from it." And he said: "If the water is two cubits by two cubits at a depth of two cubits, nothing renders it impure except what prevails over it and changes its color, its smell, or its taste," meaning from impurities. The reports of this kind narrated from them (عليهم السلام) are many. Such likewise is the ruling of a well in which an animal dies: if its water has been altered, it is drawn out until the alteration is gone and the water prevails again; and if nothing of it has been altered, there is no harm. Whatever died in it is taken out and cast away lest it alter the water. There is no sound ruling regarding water except this.

If those who oppose this were held to their own analogy in the matter, no purification would ever be valid for them upon their own principle. For when one who cleanses himself touches his private part with the water, the water mixes with the impurity there by the very act of touching, and by their words and analogy that water would become impure. Likewise the further water that flows toward it would, by contact with that moisture, become defiled, so that upon their analogy it could never purify. Such too is the garment washed in a wash-tub that holds impurity: everything poured over it, mingling with the moisture of that water, could never be purified upon their analogy. And such too is when a thing possessing blood dies in a well: they say a set number of buckets must be drawn out, yet they also hold that the water of the well, before that number is drawn out, is impure, and that if any of it fell into another well, that well too would be defiled. When they have drawn out the stated number, one bucket short, the well is by their reckoning still not pure; and when that last bucket is drawn out, it becomes pure by their reckoning, while the water within that bucket is impure by their reckoning. Yet as it rises, much of it spatters back into the well and into the bucket, and that bucket had been plunged into the well water they claimed became pure at the final draw, and it carries the moisture of impure water. So by their own analogy it has spoiled it, and upon their analogy it could never be purified. The proofs in this are many, and our condition of brevity prevents us from exhausting them.

**On what is obligatory in ablution and what is obligatory in the greater washing**

God Almighty said: *"O you who believe, when you rise to pray, wash your faces and your hands to the elbows, and wipe your heads and your feet to the ankles. And if you are in a state of janabah, then purify yourselves. But if you are ill, or on a journey, or one of you has come from the place of relieving himself, or you have touched women and find no water, then betake yourselves to clean earth and wipe your faces and hands with it"* [Surah al-Ma'idah: 6].

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: His words "when you rise to pray" mean from sleep; and His words "or one of you has come from the place of relieving himself" are an indirect expression for the occurrence of ritual impurity, the ghā'iṭ being the low-lying ground, the place to which people would withdraw for the need of a person; and His words "or you have touched women" mean intercourse, expressed indirectly by touching, for touching is contact. When a person has purified himself, his purity is not broken except by sleep that overcomes him and comes between him and his awareness of what may have issued from him. As for one who dozes lightly while still perceiving what happens to him and does not sink into heavy sleep, his ablution is not broken. Ablution is broken by urine, by excrement, and by wind issuing from the posterior, and by everything that issues from the posterior or the front, whether blood, discharge, pus, worms, moisture, or the like, all of which break ablution. What issues from other than these two outlets, whether blood, vomit, discharge, or anything else, does not break it. Ablution is not broken by touching with the hand, by contact short of intercourse, by a kiss, by direct touching, by touching the male organ or the private part, nor by food or drink. Yet one washes from the body whatever impurity has reached it, and it is recommended to cleanse oneself of grease and other things that carry an odor, without this being obligatory in a binding sense such that prayer is not valid without it. Rather cleanliness and purity are recommended. Whoever is certain that he is in purity and then doubts whether he has broken it, without being certain of the breaking, owes no fresh purification. But whoever has broken his purity and then doubts whether he has purified himself must purify himself. All of this is the position of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام). With them, nothing breaks ablution except what issues from the two outlets, the front and the posterior.

As for al-madhī, the thin fluid that issues from the male organ through the desire for intercourse, it requires only ablution, not the greater washing. And al-wadhī, the thin fluid that follows urine, likewise requires only ablution and not the greater washing. If a man has intercourse, or otherwise enters janabah, and performs the greater washing before urinating, and then after the washing water issues from his urethra, it is a remnant of the janabah, and he must repeat the washing. But if he had urinated before washing, the urine has expelled the remnant of the janabah; and if anything issues after the urination or the washing, only ablution is required, and no washing is upon him. For this reason it is fitting that whoever has had intercourse or a nocturnal emission should not perform the greater washing until he urinates. Whoever has a nocturnal emission and finds the gushing fluid must perform the greater washing. If he dreams that he is having relations but finds no wetness, nothing is required of him; and if he supposes that he emitted in sleep, the greater washing becomes obligatory upon him only by the actual emergence of semen. Whoever has relations short of penetration while awake and does not emit owes no washing until he emits. But whoever penetrates the private part owes the washing, whether or not he emits, once the two circumcised parts have met, that is, once the glans, which is the head of the male organ, has disappeared within the private part. When that has occurred, the washing is obligatory, whether or not he emits. All of this is the position of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام).

**On the manner of ablution and the greater washing and their proper etiquette**

It has been transmitted to us from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they commanded the covering of the private parts when going to relieve oneself, the seeking of a place for urine and excrement, and the taking of whatever concealment can be found for it, and withdrawing as far from people as one is able. They forbade urinating and defecating in stagnant water, on the brinks of rivers and wells, on the roads, beneath fruit-bearing trees, among the graves, and wherever it would bring harm to people. One who intends this should not face the qiblah nor turn his back to it, and should guard against anything of it reaching himself or his garment. They commanded whoever enters the place of relieving himself, or intends to relieve himself, to invoke the name of God and to seek refuge from the accursed Satan; and when he has finished, to praise God and give thanks for what He has made easy for him of the wholesomeness of his food and drink and the removal of their harm from him. They permitted urinating and defecating into a basin and into vessels, and they commanded the drawing out of the male organ from urine, so that what remains in the shaft may come forth. If he finds water, he washes it; and if he finds no water, he wipes his urethra upon clean ground, a stone, a cloth, or the like. If he has defecated, he wipes with stones, clods of earth, rags, or the like, wiping repeatedly until he is clean, and looking at what he wiped with last to see whether anything still clings to it. They forbade wiping with any food, with bone, with dried dung, and with fruit stones, by which they meant the pits of fruits, that is, their kernels, and they forbade cleansing with charcoal.

They commanded whoever intends ablution or the greater washing to make the intention of it. If he does not intend it, and washes only to cool himself, or performs ablution in that manner, or does so to instruct another and does not intend it for himself, it does not suffice him, and he must repeat whatever was made obligatory upon him with an intention he forms within himself. They recommended that whoever intends this invoke the name of God at its beginning. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) said: "Whoever names God over his ablution, his whole body is purified," meaning of sins, God willing. As for its purifying him of janabah before he performs the greater washing, no; but he is encouraged toward it. And whoever does not name God has not thereby spoiled his ablution. They commanded the washing of the two hands before entering them into the vessel; but if he enters them into the vessel while they are clean, nothing is upon him, though washing is better. Then he cleanses himself from excrement and urine and from everything that issues from their two outlets. There is no obligatory cleansing for wind that issues from the posterior, though it is recommended, and some of them used to do it, seeking its merit. He begins the cleansing with the front, then lowers his hand to the posterior and clears away whatever moisture or smear is there until it is gone, pouring the water with his right hand and washing with his left, then rubbing with water after the cleansing until the smell is gone. He does not cleanse with his right hand except from some excuse. Then he rinses his mouth and draws water into his nose three times, and this is of the Sunnah that ought not to be left off; yet if one leaves it off without intending to abandon it, nothing is upon him. Then he washes his face from its top to its bottom. The man gathers his beard in his hand and wipes over it, and if he runs water through it, that is good. He washes it three times, and if he washes it twice, or once completely, that suffices him, though three is better; there is no merit beyond three. Such too is the washing of the hands. Then he washes his arm to the elbows, doing this three times.

In the washing of the hands, as in the washing of the face, one time is sufficient and three is better; yet beyond three there is no further merit of obligation. If a man has a ring upon his hand, he must move it so that the water reaches what lies beneath it, and he should leave no place upon his face or his arms that the water has not touched. The face extends from the hairline of the forehead down to the bottom of the chin, and across from the tip of one set of sideburns to the other, taking in the lobes of the ears. He then wipes his head, facing forward and then turning back: he begins from the top and wipes toward the front of his head, then returns from where he began, wiping with the moisture remaining on his hands as far as the hairline at the nape. This suffices done once, and if he wipes three times that is better, though not obligatory. Together with this he wipes his ears, their outer and inner surfaces alike, and if he takes fresh water for a second wiping of them, that is better. Then he wipes the tops of his feet in a wiping motion. This is the meaning of the word of Allah Almighty:

"فَاغْسِلُوا وُجُوهَكُمْ وَأَيْدِيَكُمْ إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ وَامْسَحُوا بِرُءُوسِكُمْ وَأَرْجُلَكُمْ إِلَى الْكَعْبَيْنِ" — "Wash your faces and your hands to the elbows, and wipe your heads and your feet to the heels." [al-Ma'idah 6:6]

For the Almighty has made obligatory the washing of the face and the hands and the wiping of the head and the feet. If a man washes his feet as well as wiping over them, that is good; and if he passes his fingers between his toes, that too is good and carries reward. He must not put one limb before another in the ablution, but begins with what Allah began: the face, then the hands, then the head, then the feet. If he begins out of this order and washes an earlier limb after a later one, he returns and washes the later limb again so that the sequence is restored, first things first. If in the midst of his ablution he interrupts it for some matter that did not lead him astray, and then afterward completes what remained of it without beginning the ablution afresh, that suffices, and he need not treat the interruption as spoiling it. This is permitted only if he broke it off for a genuine excuse, not if he did so deliberately. As for the quantity of water used in ablution, there is no fixed measure. People differ in this: some are gentle and content with a little, while others are wasteful and are not satisfied with what suffices another. And in the full and generous performance of ablution there is merit.

The limbs of ablution must be thoroughly wetted with the water as commanded; otherwise the water merely passes over them without effect. Where a hand or a foot has been amputated, the water is passed over the place of amputation. It is recommended to perform ablution before the time of the prayer enters, so that the time may arrive while a person is already in a state of purity and he may pray at its outset. The scholars disapproved of wastefulness with water in ablution and called it a whispering of the devils, yet they permitted a person to dry himself with a garment from the wetness of ablution or of ghusl once he has finished. They commended supplication and the remembrance of Allah while washing each of the limbs of ablution, and after finishing it. They forbade wiping over socks and were severe in this, holding no prayer valid for one who wipes over his socks after a state of ritual impurity, nor for one who follows him in prayer, because that contradicts the Book of Allah, whose mention is exalted. Allah has commanded only the wiping of the feet. Whoever has in his feet an ailment that would be harmed by washing may wipe water over them; and if they are within socks, or bound with dressings, or if some other limb of ablution is so bound, or a splint is set over a wound or a break, the wiping suffices over all of that. Wiping over sandals suffices when the water reaches the tops of the feet, but it does not suffice over anything else, such as a head-cloth or a turban or the like, where there is no excuse of ailment.

Whoever intends the greater washing from janabah or from menstruation begins by performing ablution as we have described, then pours the water over his head and over the whole of his body until the hair is wetted and whatever soiling was on the body is cleansed. He passes his hands over the rest of his body so that no place remains that the water has not reached, passing his hands over all that he can reach. When he has done this he is purified. If any place on his body remains that the water has not touched, he must bring water to it. There is no fixed measure for the water of ghusl, as we said concerning ablution. Ghusl is valid with rainwater at the time of rain, and by the bather's immersing himself in water in a single plunge, provided he passes his hands over his body as described and the water reaches all of it. Ghusl becomes obligatory from janabah, from menstruation, when a disbeliever accepts Islam, and when one who has been unconscious for days, or for a stretch of time he cannot account for, comes to himself, since he cannot be sure whether a state of janabah befell him; and this holds when the unconsciousness was of that severity. Where the unconsciousness was slighter and its circumstances could be perceived and known, so that a person would sense whether a discharge had occurred, then no ghusl is required for it.

The dead must be washed when they die. When the bather performs ghusl from janabah and passes his hands over the limbs of ablution, intending by it both the ghusl and the ablution, that suffices him for both. If a woman experiences intercourse in her sleep and a discharge occurs, ghusl becomes obligatory upon her just as it is upon a man who experiences the like and finds that a discharge has occurred. One upon whom ghusl is due should perform it whenever he is able and not delay it, unless he intends to return to intercourse soon, in which case there is no harm in his delaying the ghusl until he has done so and then washing; and if he washes between the acts, that is better. If he has intercourse again, he washes again. But if the time of a prayer arrives, he should not delay the ghusl, whether or not he intends to return to intercourse. It is disliked for the one in janabah to eat or drink before he has washed his body, and disliked that he trim his nails or shave his hair or be cupped while in that state, and he should not sit in the mosque while in janabah. There is no harm in his passing through it for some need that arises, without sitting, as in the word of Allah Almighty: "وَلَا جُنُبًا إِلَّا عَابِرِي سَبِيلٍ" — "nor while in janabah, except those passing through." [al-Nisa 4:43] If a woman has had intercourse, or become junub, and then menstruates before she has washed, one ghusl suffices her for both once she sees her purity. When a woman washes, she need not undo the braids of her hair, provided she wets it with a wetting that she knows has reached all of it and penetrated to the skin of her head; there is no harm in her washing while it is braided, so long as the braid is not so tight that the wetness cannot enter, and where there is some slackness in it that admits the moisture. If there is a binding upon it, she moves it so that the water reaches beneath. The bather inserts a finger into his navel and passes water within it, and after finishing the ghusl he supplicates with whatever has been apportioned to him and asks Allah to purify him of sins.

**The purity of garments and of the ground**

It is related from the People of the House (عليهم السلام) that every animal whose flesh is lawful to eat has pure skin, feathers, hair, wool, fur, bone, and sinew, provided it was slaughtered in the prescribed manner; but if it died without proper slaughter, all of that from it is impure. The scholars permitted wearing and making use of such a hide as one wears and makes use of a garment that has been touched by impurity, only he does not pray in it. As for an animal whose flesh is not lawful to eat, all of that from it is impure; it too may be put to use, but it is not lawful to pray in it. We shall mention in the chapter on foods what of this is lawful and what is forbidden to eat. Whatever is bought from the markets of the Muslims, when one does not know it to be from a carcass, is treated as pure until that is known, just as it is purchased; and meat sold in the markets of the Muslims is eaten even if the buyer has not ascertained how it was slaughtered, for in such matters the Muslims are to be trusted concerning it.

If a pure garment is struck by urine, semen, prostatic fluid, wine, an intoxicant, blood, pus, the blood of a wound, ordure, or the dung of an animal whose flesh or urine is not lawful, or if that reaches the body while it is wet, one does not pray in it until it is washed. There is no harm in wearing the garment and making use of it before that is washed out of it, only he does not pray in it until it is washed. As for what is slight, such as the blood of fleas and ticks, and what splashes up of water at the time of ablution or ghusl from ground that impurity has reached, or a fly that alights upon impurity and then alights upon the garments, and the like of these small and unnoticed matters, there is no harm in them so long as they do not become manifest to a gross degree; when it becomes gross, it is washed. Whoever has a wound or sores that flow, and cannot hold back their flowing so as to wash it from his body and garment, should guard against it as much as he is able; and what overcomes him of it at the time of prayer does not harm him nor spoil his prayer, and he prays with it, for Allah is most excusing of the servant when He afflicts him. As for the garments of the idolaters, one should not pray in them until they are washed, and one prays in the garments of Muslims unless he knows that impurity is upon them.

If he does not know that there is impurity in it, there is no harm in praying in it, and it is treated as pure until impurity becomes evident in it. Whoever prays in a garment supposing it pure and then, after praying, learns that there had been impurity in it, his prayer stands and nothing is required of him. If he learns of it while he is in the prayer, he removes the garment; and if beneath it is a pure garment that covers him, he builds upon his prayer, but if it was worn alone, he removes it, puts on a pure garment, and prays in it. There is no harm in the sweat of one in janabah or of a menstruating woman, nor in their leavings, when they are Muslims, for the Muslim is pure. Likewise, if a wet garment clings to such a body, that does not render it impure, unless there is upon the body a smear of impurity that clings to the garment, in which case that place of it is washed. And likewise, if there is impurity in a garment and the one wearing it sweats in it, or his body touches, while wet, the place of that impurity, so that its moisture clings to the body, then that is washed. But what reaches the garment or the body of a carcass or of impurity while dry and not moist, from which nothing has clung to it, does not harm it. The urine of every animal whose flesh is lawful to eat is pure with respect to ablution, except one that has been eating impurity or is in the habit of eating it, such as chickens and their like, for then what its droppings touch is washed if it feeds on filth; but if it is confined to pure fodder, there is no harm in its droppings. The mud of rain, so long as it has not been altered by impurity, is pure; and if it is altered by something other than impurity mingling with it, it is likewise pure; but if impurity mingles with it and alters it, then it is impure. If one walking treads upon damp impurity with his shoe and then wipes it upon the ground so that the moisture is gone, it is pure; and if it does not go, it is washed. Likewise, if he walks upon it with his foot and then walks upon pure ground so that the moisture is gone, no washing is required of him; but if it remains, he washes his feet. If impurity reaches the ground and then the sun takes it so that its moisture and its odor are gone from the ground, it has become pure and one may pray upon it. One does not pray upon impure ground, nor in a graveyard, but one may pray in the folds of sheep and the resting-places of camels when they do not know of any impurity present.

**The tooth-stick**

The Messenger of Allah (ص) said: "The tooth-stick is a purifier for the mouth and a means of the Lord's pleasure. Jibril did not come to me but that he charged me with the tooth-stick, until I feared it would be made obligatory upon the front of my mouth." And Ali (ع) said: "Three things were given to the Prophets: the tooth-stick, perfume, and wives." And Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) said: "The tooth-stick is half of ablution, and ablution is half of faith, and the tooth-stick drives away phlegm." The Messenger of Allah (ص) said: "Were it not that I would burden my community, I would have made the tooth-stick obligatory with every ablution." Whoever is able for it should not neglect it. And he said: "Use the tooth-stick across, and do not use it lengthwise." He said that rubbing with the thumb and the forefinger at the time of ablution, that is, using them in place of the tooth-stick, suffices in its stead. And he forbade using a tooth-stick of reed, or of basil, or of pomegranate, and said that this stirs up the vein of leprosy.

**The dry ablution**

Allah Almighty said: "يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا قُمْتُمْ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ فَاغْسِلُوا وُجُوهَكُمْ" — "O you who believe, when you rise to pray, wash your faces," [al-Ma'idah 6:6] down to His word: "فَإِنْ لَمْ تَجِدُوا مَاءً فَتَيَمَّمُوا صَعِيدًا طَيِّبًا فَامْسَحُوا بِوُجُوهِكُمْ وَأَيْدِيكُمْ مِنْهُ" — "and if you do not find water, then betake yourselves to clean earth and wipe your faces and your hands with it." [al-Ma'idah 6:6] It is related from the People of the House (عليهم السلام) that whoever does not find water, whether a traveler, or one who is unable for it through sickness and fears that washing would destroy him or strengthen his ailment against him, or is simply unable for it, should perform tayammum with the clean earth, which is pure dust. He places the inner surfaces of his palms upon it, then shakes them off with a light shaking, and wipes his face and the backs of his hands with them: he wipes the top of the back of his right hand with the inner surface of his left palm, and the back of his left hand with the inner surface of his right palm. The limit of this is the place from which the hand of the thief is cut off, because Allah Almighty said: "فَامْسَحُوا بِوُجُوهِكُمْ وَأَيْدِيكُمْ مِنْهُ" — "wipe your faces and your hands with it," [al-Ma'idah 6:6] and He, the Exalted, said: "وَالسَّارِقُ وَالسَّارِقَةُ فَاقْطَعُوا أَيْدِيَهُمَا جَزَاءً بِمَا كَسَبَا" — "As for the male thief and the female thief, cut off their hands as a recompense for what they have earned." [al-Ma'idah 5:38] So the limit of tayammum matches the limit of cutting, according to the plain sense of the Book of Allah; and the limit of cutting we shall mention in its place, if Allah wills. One begins the wiping from the fingertips.

They said (عليهم السلام) that the one performing tayammum should not do so except at the end of the time of the prayer. When the end of the time has come, he performs tayammum and prays with that tayammum, so long as he does not break his state and does not find water, in which case he performs ablution if he is able for it. If he finds water and is one who is able but does not purify himself, his tayammum is annulled; and when another prayer comes upon him and he still does not find water or is unable for it, he performs tayammum again. If he performs tayammum and then finds water before he prays, he purifies himself and does not pray with his tayammum. If he has already opened the prayer and then finds water, he does not break off the prayer, and it suffices him when this occurs at the end of the time. If he prays having performed tayammum at the beginning of the time, and then reaches water while enough of the prayer's time remains for him to purify himself and pray, he purifies himself and prays. Tayammum is only with dry clean earth, and it is not valid with clay, unless one smears it upon something until it dries and then performs tayammum with it. If he finds no clean earth, he shakes his garment or his felt or his saddle-pad, if there is dust in it, and performs tayammum with that; and one striking suffices him, striking the clean earth with both hands for his face and his hands, once each. Tayammum is not valid while at home, except that a funeral is present and water is not found within the time, for then he performs tayammum and prays over it, if he fears it will be missed. Whoever is on a journey and has with him a little water, and fears that if he purifies himself with it he will perish of thirst, may perform tayammum and keep his water, and not destroy himself. And if he cannot find water except in a place where he fears that going to it would destroy him, he is not obliged to go to it and expose himself to destruction, but performs tayammum and prays. And if he finds water at a dear price, and has the means for its price, he buys it, unless that would cause him ruinous hardship.

When water must be bought, one is not required to pay beyond a fair price when the seller's demand is extortionate, for there is no obligation to meet a ruinous cost. And whoever has no water with him on a journey, and wishes to return to his household, may do so; he performs tayammum and prays.

**Menstruation**

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, said:

﴿وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْمَحِيضِ قُلْ هُوَ أَذًى فَاعْتَزِلُوا النِّسَاءَ فِي الْمَحِيضِ وَلَا تَقْرَبُوهُنَّ حَتَّى يَطْهُرْنَ فَإِذَا تَطَهَّرْنَ فَأْتُوهُنَّ مِنْ حَيْثُ أَمَرَكُمُ اللَّهُ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ التَّوَّابِينَ وَيُحِبُّ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ﴾

"And they ask you about menstruation; say, 'It is harm, so keep away from women during menstruation, and do not approach them until they are purified. And when they have purified themselves, then come to them as Allah has ordained, for Allah loves those who turn in repentance and loves those who purify themselves.'" [Al-Baqarah: 222]

This shows that during menstruation one is not in a state of purity. It has been related from the Messenger of Allah (ص) that he said: the menstruating woman is not made pure by water until after the menstruation has ceased.

From the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) it is related that the sign of purity for the menstruating woman is that she inserts a clean piece of cotton or cloth, and if it comes out white, she has seen the sign of purity. She then washes with water as she washes from janabah, and she performs the prayers that fall due at that time. She does not make up the prayers she missed during the days of her menstruation. As for the days of her menstruation, she makes up the fast but does not make up the prayer.

Her husband may approach her while she is menstruating if he wishes, though it is better that he keep himself from that. The root of mubasharah (approach) is the placing of one skin (bashara) upon another, and from this the word is derived; it is said that a man "approaches" his wife when their skin touches. But if he has intercourse with her while she is menstruating, he has sinned and transgressed what Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has commanded. He must seek Allah's forgiveness, repent to Him, and give charity for that act; and it is better that he not return to it.

If the bleeding continues beyond what is known of ordinary menstruation, this is istihada. The blood of istihada is distinguished from menstrual blood: menstrual blood is turbid, thick, and foul in odor, while the blood of istihada is thin, clear, and without odor. When the menstrual bleeding stops, the mustahada purifies herself with water, prays, seeks forgiveness, and resumes her ordinary way of life, and her husband may approach her.

It has been related from the Ahl al-Bayt of the Messenger of Allah (ص) that the mustahada should perform ghusl for every prayer, seeking thereby the reward of Allah, Mighty and Majestic; and Allah will record its merit for her if she is sincere in it. If a menstruating woman becomes pure and then sees a faint blood that soon ceases, no ghusl is required of her, for that is like the blood of istihada. But if it is thick, like menstrual blood, she must wash from it. Likewise, if she sees blood while pregnant and it resembles menstruation, then she and her husband must observe in her case what is obligatory during menstruation. She washes when her postpartum bleeding has ended, just as she washes from janabah.

The menstruating woman and the one in a state of janabah may not recite the Quran, nor may they sit in the mosque. And if a menstruating woman becomes pure at the time of a prayer but delays the ghusl until that prayer's time has passed, she is obliged to make up that prayer.
