# The Due of Wealth: Zakat

The chapters of zakat open here, and with them the command to give it, the encouragement toward it, and the severity that attends its withholding. Allah the Almighty said: "Successful indeed is he who purifies himself, and remembers the name of his Lord, and prays" [al-A'la]. And He said: "Blessed be the believers who are humble in their prayers, and who turn away from ill speech, and who are active in giving zakat" [al-Mu'minun]. And He said: "Establish the prayer and pay the zakat." The Almighty said: "When the sacred months have passed, kill the polytheists wherever you find them, and take them, and confine them, and wait for them at every lookout. But if they repent, establish prayer, and pay zakat, then let them go their way" [al-Tawbah: 5]. So Allah does not accept the Islam of a polytheist who withholds zakat, and He said: "Woe to the polytheists who do not pay zakat" [al-Fussilat: 6-7].

The Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) was asked about this verse, "Woe to the polytheists who do not pay zakat," and he said: Allah does not reproach the polytheists for it. Have you not heard His words, "Woe to those who pray, who are heedless of their prayers, who make a show of themselves and withhold the ma'un"? For the ma'un is zakat. Then he said: By the One in Whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, none betrays Allah in anything of the zakat of his wealth except a polytheist.

And he (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) said: When Allah wills good for a servant, He sends an angel from among the keepers of Paradise, who wipes his breast so that his soul grows generous with zakat. And he said: Whoever gives the zakat of his wealth, let him give it with a willing soul, for he gives only a portion of what Allah has given him, and Allah stores away the rest for him; and whoever gives the zakat of his wealth, its evil has departed from him. And he said: No wealth is ever destroyed on land or sea except through the withholding of zakat from it; so fortify your wealth with zakat, treat your sick with charity, and turn away affliction with supplication. And he said: No one gives the zakat of his wealth and finds his wealth thereby diminished, nor withholds it and finds it thereby increased, nor does any wealth decrease through charity; so give, and do not shrink from it. And he said: Allah the Almighty has tracts of ground that are called the parched wastes, to which is driven whoever withholds Allah's due from his wealth, so that he is left in want among them.

And he (ع) said: Whoever has camels or cattle or sheep and does not pay their zakat will be laid out flat on the Day of Resurrection upon a barren, level plain, and they will trample him with their hooves and gore him with their horns and bite him with their mouths; whenever the last of them has passed over him, the first of them returns to him, until the reckoning of all creation is finished.

From the Commander of the Believers, Ali (ع), it is related that he said: The worshipper has three signs: prayer, zakat, and fasting. And the Prophet (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) said in his bequest to al-Hasan (ع): I counsel you, O Hasan, and all my children and the people of my house, and whoever among the believers this writing of mine reaches, to fear Allah your Lord. And Allah, Allah, regarding zakat, for it extinguishes the anger of Allah from you.

And he (ع) said: Allah the Almighty has made obligatory upon the wealthy, from their wealth, the measure that suffices their poor. If the poor go hungry or naked, it is because of what the wealthy withhold, and Allah the Almighty will hold them to account for that on the Day of Resurrection and punish them severely. And he said: Whoever amasses wealth and does not pay its due, his wealth becomes a serpent that will devour him on the Day of Resurrection. And he said: The withholder of zakat is like the devourer of riba, and no one who withholds zakat is a Muslim.

And he (ع) said concerning the words of Allah the Almighty, "They will be encircled by what they were stingy with on the Day of Resurrection" [Al 'Imran: 180]: The wealth on which zakat was withheld is fashioned into the likeness of a bald serpent that winds about his neck and bites at him until the people are finished with the reckoning, and it says: I am your wealth, whose zakat you withheld. And he said concerning the words of Allah the Almighty, "And those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend it in the way of Allah, give them tidings of a painful punishment, on the day when it will be heated in the fire of Hell, and with it will be branded their foreheads, their sides, and their backs. This is what you hoarded for yourselves, so taste what you used to hoard" [al-Tawbah: 34-35]: The hoard is the wealth on which zakat is not paid; but wealth whose zakat has been paid is no hoard. As the Prophet (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) said: On the Day of Resurrection there will be brought the wealth of one who withheld its zakat, and it will be beaten into plates, then heated in the fire of Hell, and with them his forehead, his sides, and his back will be branded; whenever they cool, they are heated again and the branding resumed, until the reckoning of all creation is finished. And he said: Whoever pays the zakat of his wealth has discharged what was owed upon him in it.

A man asked Abu 'Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) about a man who complained to him that he was seized with terror in his sleep, as though a woman were pressing a demand upon him and frightening him. He said: This is a man who does not pay the zakat of his wealth. The questioner reported this to the man, who said: By Allah, I do pay it. The questioner conveyed that back to Abu 'Abd Allah (ع), and he said: If that is so, then he does not place it in its proper place. The man said: As for that, yes.

**The payment of zakat to its rightful recipients, and the prohibition of placing it elsewhere.**

Allah the Almighty said to Muhammad, His Prophet (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله): "Take from their wealth a charity that will purify them and cleanse them by it" [al-Tawbah: 103]. Then He informed him of the places in which it is to be set, and said: "Charity is only for the poor and the destitute, and those who administer it, and those whose hearts are to be reconciled, and for freeing captives, and for those in debt, and in the cause of Allah, and for the wayfarer, an obligation from Allah, and Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise" [al-Tawbah: 60].

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: The poor is the needy man who does not beg from people; the destitute is one in even greater hardship, the one who begs from people. Those who administer it are the collectors of the charity who take charge of receiving it from the people. Those whose hearts are to be reconciled are men of enmity who nonetheless hold power and standing and obedience among their people; such a one is given from the zakat so that he may be won over and may incline toward obedience those who follow him, as the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه) used to give to the chiefs of the tribes in order to reconcile them, men such as 'Uyayna ibn Husn and al-Aqra' ibn Habis. Their like exists in every age, and whenever they exist the imams give to them from the zakat. "In the freeing of captives" is manumission; "those in debt" are the people burdened with debts; "in the cause of Allah" is in the struggle; and "the wayfarer" is the stranger cut off from his means.

These, then, are eight categories upon whom Allah the Almighty has made the charities obligatory. It is not proper that one category be given the whole of it to the exclusion of another, for there is no dispute among the Muslims on this. Consider: if a man were to name several men by name and set a sum for them, and one of them rose up to claim the whole of it for himself without handing his companions their share, this would not be lawful for him. How much less lawful, then, for one who has heard the words of Allah the Almighty, "Charity is only for the poor and the destitute, and those who administer it," until He has named all eight categories, and who then gives it to a single category among them, when he is bound with this to hand it over to the one whom Allah has commanded to receive it. Allah has made it an obligation upon that one to set it in its proper places, has entrusted to him the oversight of it and the knowledge of the state of each class of its people, and of the measure that should be given at every time and season according to what befalls the Muslims and Islam. This is what none can know or fulfill except the Imams, whom Allah the Almighty has charged with the affairs of His servants and set over His earth and His lands.

The people do not differ that the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) himself took charge of receiving the charities from those present with him, and sent his agents to receive them from those distant, who then brought them to him so that he might distribute them where Allah commanded them distributed. Allah made this clear and plain in mentioning those who administer the charity in His Book and assigning them a share of it for their labor upon it, just as He said to His Messenger (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله), "Take from their wealth a charity" [al-Tawbah: 103]. When He then took him to Himself, the people did not differ that this obligation remained, undiminished by the passing of the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله); rather they agreed that whoever stands in his place takes charge of receiving it as he received it (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) and directs it to the mentioned channels. They agreed with Abu Bakr upon fighting those who withheld the zakat, named them the people of apostasy, and held their blood lawful. Abu Bakr, 'Umar, 'Uthman, and the Commander of the Believers Ali (ع) each took charge of receiving it in their days, none differing with them or disputing it.

When the affair passed to the Banu Umayya and the people saw that they had cut it off for themselves, some held back from paying the zakat to them and turned in alarm to those who remained of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) and questioned them about it. Every one of them commanded that it be paid to the rulers, and said: upon them is what they have taken on, and upon you is what you have taken on; you are to give it to them, and it is upon them to set it in its proper places. Thus they related from 'A'isha, Abu Hurayra, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, and 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar, and they related no contrary word from any of the Companions, nor do they relate any disagreement among them. When Ibn 'Umar was pressed with much questioning, he said: Woe to you. Tell me: if I were to seize thieves and cut off the hands of some of them and leave the rest, would you be in the right? They said no. He said: And if you handed them to the ruler and he did that, would it be upon you? They said no. He said: So too do these matters run. Pay it to them, even if with it they devour the flesh of serpents.

This is the word of the generality and what they relate from their predecessors, though today the whole of them has stripped it away and departed from it altogether, without a proof they have advanced or a cause they have brought. Rather they have entered into the company of those whom they themselves reckoned people of apostasy for withholding from their rulers the zakat of their wealth, just as those others withheld their zakat from Abu Bakr. And for that withholding there is a ground which this is not the place to mention, whose exposition would grow long were it set down.

As for the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام), they do not hold that zakat is to be paid to any but the Imams of guidance from the household of the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله), whom Allah the Almighty has commanded His servants to obey in His Book and whom He has entrusted with the affairs of His servants. Whoever pays the zakat of his wealth willingly to other than them, it does not suffice for him, and he must pay it to the Imam of his age from among them. But if the imams of injustice overpower him and take it from him by force, that suffices for him. The Commander of the Believers Ali (ع) said to one of his agents over al-Jazira: Whoever pays the zakat of his wealth to Mu'awiya and his agents, of those whom our cavalry and our men have kept at bay, do not credit that to him, but take it from him, for it did not reach him and did not protect him. But where they overpowered a man and took it from him by force, accept his word and do not press him.

And he (ع) said: The Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) forbade a man to conceal his zakat from his imam, and said that this is hypocrisy. Allah the Almighty has made clear to His servants, upon the tongue of His Messenger Muhammad (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله), to whom they are to hand their charities, in that He appointed them trustees over it, since He forbade it to them and to none besides. The community is agreed that it is not lawful for the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) nor for the people of his house. They related that al-Hasan ibn Ali (ع), while a small boy, took a date from the dates of the charity and put it into his mouth, and the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) looked at him and hastened to him and drew it out of his mouth with its moisture upon it and threw it back among the dates of the charity, and said: We, the people of the house, charity is not lawful for us. So He informed them that they are the trusted ones over it, charged with receiving it and setting it in its proper places, and that this belongs to none besides them, to no one else at all among the people.

In like manner He assigned to them the fifth (khums) which He assigned to the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله), as a token of their trustworthiness and their standing in the place of the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) after him in that for his community. This belongs to the Imams (عليهم السلام) among them, and not to the rest, for the rest of them are bound in what is due from them, like the rest of the community, to pay their charities to the Imam, just as the rest of the people are bound, as was the state of the household of the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) with him in his lifetime. It is for one after another, an honor passed on and a virtue kept unbroken. Whoever has denied their right and been ignorant of their trustworthiness, the least that binds him by the word of the Imams and his own predecessors, when Allah makes their affair manifest and gives them dominion over the one who denied them, is that he pay them the zakat of his wealth in every one of his states. We have already set down what suffices of the proof in this, though we have made it brief, for whoever is granted its understanding, and Allah guides to his right course.

**The zakat of gold, silver, and precious substances.**

We have narrated from the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that zakat is not due on gold until it reaches twenty mithqals, with a full year having turned over it in the possession of the one who came by it; nor on silver until it reaches two hundred dirhams, with a full year having turned over it in the possession of the one who came by it. When the year has turned over it in the possession of its owner, then upon the twenty mithqals is half a mithqal, and upon the two hundred dirhams is five dirhams; and this has become a nisab in his hand. Whatever exceeds it bears a quarter of a tenth, if he came by it before the head of the year, due at the head of the year, and he pays it out likewise. If at the head of the year it falls short of this measure, there is no zakat upon it; and whatever exceeds bears zakat by that reckoning. The one is not joined to the other, and no zakat is due on gold or on silver until each of the two reaches the measure we have mentioned.

If a man has one hundred dirhams and ten dinars whose value is one hundred dirhams or more, there is no zakat upon him in that, until the dinars reach twenty dinars and the dirhams reach two hundred dirhams and the year has turned over it, unless he spends some of it before the year in flight from the zakat. If he does that, zakat is upon him. This is like a man who had two hundred dirhams and, when he saw that the zakat was becoming due upon him, spent some of it away.

Whoever converts some of his silver into dinars in order to escape the zakat owed on it, or spends some of his dinars for the same purpose, has done what is not permitted. As for mineral wealth such as pearls and amber, when they are drawn out from their source and their place, the fifth is due upon the one who extracts them, and no zakat falls upon what he keeps back. Once he sells such a thing and the buyer takes possession of it, it becomes like the rest of the buyer's wealth: he assesses its value and pays zakat upon it at the completion of the year, if it is held for trade. There is no zakat upon jewelry or upon mineral goods that are taken for personal use, unless a person takes them as a stratagem to flee from zakat. If that is his intent, the intent invalidates the stratagem and zakat is due. The same rule governs what is drawn from the mines, whether gold, silver, brass, iron, lead, mineral stone, or the like: the fifth is due upon it, and once it passes into circulation it is treated like the rest of one's wealth. Whoever holds debased coinage pays its zakat from it in proportion to the pure metal it contains, giving debased for debased; and if it is copper, iron, lead, or some other base metal with a coating over it, its value is referred back to what it truly is in its origin, joined to the rest of his holdings, and zakat is paid upon the whole.

There is no zakat upon clothing, riding animals, household vessels, or furnishings, so long as they are not held for trade and were not acquired as a means of fleeing from zakat. If they are held for trade, or if they were bought with wealth on which zakat was due and then set aside precisely so that no zakat would be paid, then zakat is due upon them. Whoever buys trade goods assesses their value at the head of the year and pays their zakat, provided the goods are present with him or in a place from which he is not barred. So too with a debt owed to him, if he is not prevented from it: when the year turns upon it, its zakat is due, and it is treated as though it were already in his hands. But if he can reach it only through litigation, and the one who owes it refuses him, he does not pay its zakat until he receives it and it comes into his hand. There is no zakat upon the wealth of a child until he reaches maturity, takes possession of it, and a full year passes upon it in his keeping. Yet if that wealth passes into the hand of an adult who trades with it, zakat is due upon it, and the zakat falls upon the one who trades with it; any loss belongs to him, while the profit belongs to the child. The wealth of a woman's dowry follows the rule of a debt, as we have already explained. Whoever pays out the zakat of his wealth to deliver it, and it is lost before it reaches its due place, must make it good again, for he remains its guarantor until he sets it where it belongs. And whoever dies still owing zakat he did not discharge, it is taken from the principal of his estate, whether or not he left a bequest to that effect, once it is known and established.

As for the zakat of camels, cattle, and sheep, we have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام) that there is no zakat upon working camels, those upon which loads are carried or by which irrigation is drawn, whatever their number and however great it grows. But if they are grazing camels, kept at pasture for their increase and their milk, then no zakat is due upon fewer than five. When they reach five and a year has passed upon them, one sheep is due; there is nothing more upon what exceeds five until they reach ten, when two sheep are due; at fifteen, three sheep; at twenty, four sheep. At twenty-five a bint makhad is due, which is a she-camel that has completed a year since her birth and entered the second, her mother having conceived again after her and being now in labor, not yet delivered. If no bint makhad is found, then an ibn labun suffices, which is a male that has completed two years and entered the third, whose mother conceived after him and gave birth, so that she is now giving milk; and this holds up to thirty-five. When one more is added, a bint labun is due, up to forty-five. If one more is added, a hiqqah is due, which is a she-camel that has completed three years, entered the fourth, and become fit to bear and to be covered; and this holds up to sixty. If one more is added, a jadha'a is due, which is a she-camel that has completed four years and entered the fifth; and this holds up to seventy-five. If one more is added, two daughters of labun are due, up to ninety. If one more is added, two hiqqas are due, up to a hundred and twenty. Beyond that, for every forty a bint labun is due, and for every fifty a hiqqah. If the assessor does not find among the camels the exact age that is owed, he takes the age below it together with two sheep or twenty dirhams, or the age above it and returns two sheep or twenty dirhams.

There is no zakat upon working cattle, those by which one plows and draws water, nor upon the grazing cattle kept for increase and milk until they reach thirty. When they reach thirty and a year has passed upon them in the ownership of their owner, a tabi' or tabi'a is due, which is the yearling whose horns have grown even. There is nothing upon what exceeds thirty until forty, when a musinnah is due. From forty to sixty it is one musinnah; at sixty, two tabi's; at seventy, one musinnah and one tabi'; at eighty, two musinnahs; at ninety, three tabi's; at a hundred, one musinnah and two tabi's; and so onward, for every thirty a tabi' and for every forty a musinnah, with nothing due for what lies between.

There is no zakat upon the domestic sheep, which are those raised within the houses and not sent to pasture, but upon the grazing flock zakat is due once it reaches forty and a year has passed upon it in its owner's keeping. When it reaches forty, one sheep is taken from its owner, and there is nothing upon what exceeds forty until it reaches a hundred and twenty. If it passes that by one or more, two sheep are due, until it reaches two hundred. If it passes two hundred by one or more, three sheep are due; and when the flock grows large, for every hundred a sheep is due.

Concerning what the workers of charity are to do in the districts they oversee and what they are to take, and concerning the wealth of those whose holdings are mingled, we have narrated from the Commander of the Believers, 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), that he appointed Mikhnaf ibn Sulaym al-Azdi over the zakat of Bakr ibn Wa'il and wrote him a covenant. In it he said: This is what Ibn Abi Talib charged upon Mikhnaf ibn Sulaym when he sent him to gather the charity. He commanded him to fear Allah his Lord in all his affairs and in the hidden acts of his conduct, where there is no witness over him but Allah and no watchful guardian besides Him. He commanded him not to let his obedience to Allah in what appears outwardly differ from what he keeps concealed, for whoever does that has fallen into treachery, has made light of the right of Allah, and has exposed himself to His displeasure. He commanded him to conduct himself with justice among those over whom he is set, to deal fairly with them in what is apportioned, to meet them with an open face and a gentle side, to secure for the wronged among them redress from the one who wronged him, and to hold the balance even among them in his seat, his countenance, his bearing, and his leave to approach, so that no man among them might see any favoring of another over himself. He commanded him not to revile them, nor to scowl upon them, nor to turn from them out of a sense of superiority through the authority he held over them, for they are brothers in religion and helpers against the enemy and aids in the drawing out of what is owed. And he said (ع): You have in this charity, O Mikhnaf ibn Sulaym, a portion and an appointed right, and you have partners in it: the poor, the needy, and the weak of household. If they render you your right, then render them theirs; and if you do not, you will be among the people most beset by adversaries and misery on the Day of Judgment. Woe to the man whose adversary is the poor, the destitute, the ruined, and the wayfarer.

It is narrated from him (ع) that the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) forbade that people be made to swear over their charity, and said of them that they are trusted, meaning trusted as to what is hidden and concealed of their livestock; but what is manifest is taken from them. He said: the one who transgresses in charity is like the one who withholds it, and he forbade that people be dealt with harshly in the matter of charity. The Commander of the Believers, 'Ali (ع), said: the charity of the people of the open country is taken in their own places, and they are not driven from place to place; and in the year of drought they are granted delay until the land grows green again. It is narrated from him (ع) that the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) forbade the joining of what is separate and the separating of what is joined for the sake of charity. The separating of what is joined is that a man has a hundred and twenty sheep, upon which only a single sheep is due until the number increases; the assessor is forbidden to break them into groups of forty and take three sheep from them. Likewise, if a man owns forty, he is not to be required to divide them into two twenties so as to give no zakat upon them. As for the joining of what is separate, it is that three men, each owning forty sheep and not partners in them, should not be combined so that the assessor takes a single sheep, when, held apart, three sheep would be due. So too the assessor is not to combine the flocks of two men upon each of whom, separately, no zakat is due, so that by joining them zakat becomes due. Nor are partners in livestock charged zakat until each of them holds what makes zakat due upon him; when that is so and their shares are equal, it is taken from them accordingly, and if one holds more than another, they settle the difference between themselves by mutual adjustment. This is like two men who share a flock upon which nothing is due until it reaches eighty; when it reaches eighty, two sheep are due, one upon each of them, if the flock is divided between them in halves. But if one owns a third and the other two-thirds, only a single sheep is due, falling upon the owner of the two-thirds, until the flock reaches a hundred and twenty. If one more is added, two sheep are due, and the two partners settle the difference between them, for the assessor took from the whole flock two sheep, while one owned forty and the other eighty. Thus he took from the owner of two-thirds one sheep and a third, though only one sheep was truly owed by him, and from the owner of forty a third of a sheep, though a full sheep was owed by him; so it comes to rest at one sheep upon each, the shortfall in each one's share being made good against what the assessor took from him.

Upon this same footing runs the rule of all wealth, whether coin, camels, cattle, sheep, grain, or anything upon which zakat is due: when it is held among partners, each partner owes upon his share only what he would have owed were he no partner at all, because partnership does not dissolve ownership nor transfer any part of it from one to another. Each partner has only what he owns; whatever was due upon him before the partnership is what remains due upon him after it. And when men of wealth gather their properties and mingle them without a true partnership between them, from each is taken what is due upon him, and no regard is given to the mingling of their holdings.

Whoever holds a quorum of wealth on which zakat has become due, and a year passes upon it, and he pays what is owed, and then some addition comes to it, joins that addition to it at the head of the second year and pays zakat upon the whole together. Of coin he gives out a quarter of a tenth; of livestock, the increase is reckoned within the count of the herd. If a mother gives birth while the assessor is present over the flock, he does not reckon the newborn, nor take it, nor take its like in charity. He takes from camels and cattle only the ages that have been described, and from sheep only the full-grown; he does not take the aged, nor the defective, nor the male of the flock, nor its choicest, nor its poorest, but takes from the middling. If the assessor and the owners of the wealth fall into dispute, the owners choose a third of the flock and set it apart, and the assessor chooses from the remaining two-thirds what is due and takes it.

As for the zakat of grains and fruits, Allah, mighty and majestic, has said: ﴿وَهُوَ الَّذِى أَنشَأَ جَنَّاتٍ مَّعْرُوشَاتٍ وَغَيْرَ مَعْرُوشَاتٍ وَالنَّخْلَ وَالزَّرْعَ مُخْتَلِفًا أُكُلُهُ وَالزَّيْتُونَ وَالرُّمَّانَ مُتَشَابِهًا وَغَيْرَ مُتَشَابِهٍ كُلُوا مِن ثَمَرِهِ إِذَا أَثْمَرَ وَآتُوا حَقَّهُ يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ﴾ [Al-An'am: 141]. It is narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) concerning the words of Allah, mighty and majestic, ﴿وَآتُوا حَقَّهُ يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ﴾ [Al-An'am: 141], that he said: this is over and above the obligatory charity; it is given at the time of harvest to the one who asks, who is given the handful, that is, the fistful, and the thing given in charity alongside the imposed obligation. It is narrated from him, from his forefathers, that the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) made it clear beyond all clarity that the tenth is due upon all that the sky and the rivers water, and half the tenth upon what is watered by wells. It has come from the household (عليهم السلام) that the tenth is due upon all grains once they have set and ripened; at that point they are appraised, when they first appear, and their owners are given a just estimate. They are then left free to their crop and are held to a tenth of what was estimated. The one who makes the estimate is charged not to press it to the utmost, but to leave something aside. They said: the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) sent 'Abdullah ibn Rawaha al-Ansari to the people of Khaybar, having been his agent among them upon the half, when the fruit began to ripen. He estimated it upon them, and they objected. So 'Abdullah ibn Rawaha al-Ansari said to them: I have made my estimate and have not laid more upon you than is right. If you wish, render to us half of what we have estimated; and if you wish, hand it over to us and take half of it. The Jews said: This is the truth by which the heavens and the earth stand. As for honey, they said (عليهم السلام): if it is held for trade, zakat is due upon it; but what is bought of it, and what is drawn from the combs and cut from the hives, carries no zakat.

As for the zakat of the breaking of the fast, Allah, mighty and majestic, has said: ﴿قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن تَزَكَّى وَذَكَرَ اسْمَ رَبِّهِ فَصَلَّى﴾ [Al-A'la]. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said of His words, "He has prospered who purifies himself," that it means he who gives the zakat of the breaking of the fast; and of "and mentions the name of his Lord and prays," that it means the prayer of the festival. He said (ع) that the zakat of the breaking of the fast is a duty upon the rich and the poor alike. The Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) said that the charity of the breaking of the fast is due upon every soul, for himself and for all his dependents, young and old, free and enslaved, a sa' of food from each one of them. It is narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that he said: a man pays the zakat of the breaking of the fast on behalf of his Jewish and Christian slave. It is narrated from him (ع) that he said: if the slaves of a man's wife are among his dependents, he pays on their behalf; and if they are not among his dependents, then the wife must pay on their behalf. And he (ع) used to pay the zakat of the breaking of the fast on behalf of his father after his father's death, and this is a voluntary good, not an obligation. He said (ع) that one who cannot find food gives in its place dirhams to the value current at the time, and that it is the way of the sunnah to pay the zakat of the breaking of the fast before the festival prayer.

The zakat upon honey follows the same principle that governs merchandise. If the honey is held for trade, then zakat is due upon it. But what a man buys for himself, or draws from his own hives, or cuts from the combs, carries no zakat.

There remains the zakat of the breaking of the fast, the zakat al-fitr. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: ﴿قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ وَذَكَرَ اسْمَ رَبِّهِ فَصَلَّىٰ﴾. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) explained this verse. His words "He has indeed succeeded who purifies himself" mean the one who pays the zakat al-fitr, and "and remembers the name of his Lord and prays" means the prayer of the Eid.

He (ع) said that the zakat al-fitr is obligatory upon the rich and the poor alike. And the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) said that the sadaqat al-fitr is due upon every person, on behalf of himself and on behalf of all his dependents, the young among them and the old, the free and the slave. For each one of them the measure is a sa' of food.

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) it is related that he said a man pays the zakat al-fitr on behalf of his slave, whether that slave be a Jew or a Christian. And from him (ع) it is related that he said, when a man's wife has slaves who are counted among his dependents, he pays on their behalf; but if they are not among his dependents, then it falls upon the wife to pay on their behalf.

He (ع) used to pay the zakat al-fitr on behalf of his father after his father's death. This is a voluntary act of goodness, not an obligation.

He (ع) said that whoever does not find food should give in its place dirhams according to the value at the time. And the sunnah is that the zakat al-fitr be paid before the fast is broken, before the Eid.
