# The Pillar of Religion: Standing in Prayer

Allah Almighty has made prayer a decree of specified times, saying: "Prayer was for the believers a decree of specified times." [Al-Nisa: 103] And He said: "Guard strictly the prayers and the middle prayer, and stand up devoutly before Allah." [Al-Baqarah: 238] And He said: "Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing, and the remembrance of Allah is greater." [Al-Ankabut: 45] And He said: "And seek help through patience and prayer, and indeed it is difficult except for the humbly submissive." [Al-Baqarah: 45]

The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said: "Guard yourselves, and know that the best of your deeds is prayer." He said: "Prayer is the offering of every pious person." He said: "Prayer is half of faith." He said: "Everything has a face, and the face of your religion is prayer." And he said: "Prayer is the pillar of religion and the mainstay of Islam."

He also said: Whoever perfects his ablution, prays well, pays the zakat on his wealth, restrains his anger, imprisons his tongue, extends his kindness to others, seeks forgiveness from his Lord, and gives sincere counsel to the people of my household has fulfilled the realities of faith, and the gates of Paradise stand open for him. He said: The devil remains in awe of the believer so long as he safeguards the five prayers; but when the believer neglects them, the devil grows bold against him and casts him into grave sins.

It is narrated from Ali (ع) that he said: Prayer is the pillar of religion and the mainstay of Islam, and it is the first thing Allah Almighty examines of the deeds of the son of Adam. If it is sound, the rest of his deeds are examined; and if it is not sound, none of his deeds are looked at, and there is no portion in Islam for the one who abandons prayer. He said: The five prayers are an expiation for what falls between them, so long as the major sins are avoided. This is what Allah Almighty meant when He said: "Indeed, good deeds remove evil deeds; that is a reminder for those who remember." [Hud: 114]

He said: The likeness of one who does not complete his prayer is the likeness of a pregnant woman who, when her time of delivery draws near, miscarries, so she is neither one who carries a child nor one who has a child. He said: Two rak'ahs performed with composure in the latter part of the night are more precious than standing the whole night without composure. And he said: Prayer is a balance; whoever gives it in full receives in full.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: I know of nothing after knowledge, meaning knowledge of Allah and His friends, that is superior to prayer, zakat, fasting, Hajj, and jihad. He said: The nearest a servant comes to Allah is when he is in prayer. And he used to say: O seeker of knowledge, pray before you are no longer able, for there will come a time when you can neither pray by night nor by day. The likeness of prayer for the one who offers it is the likeness of a man who is admitted before a sultan and remains silent and attentive until his need is fulfilled. So it is with the Muslim: when he enters into prayer, Allah continues to look upon him until he finishes his prayer.

He was asked about the words of Allah Almighty: "And those who carefully maintain their prayers." [Al-Ma'arij: 34] He said: This is the obligation. Whoever prays it in its time, knowing its due, preferring nothing over it, Allah Almighty writes for him by it an immunity from the Fire and does not punish him. And whoever prays it outside its time, not knowing its due, preferring other things over it, his affair rests with Allah Almighty: if He wills, He forgives him, and if He wills, He punishes him.

He said: The worshipper has three signs. Mercy showers down upon him from the reaches of heaven to the crown of his head; the angels enfold him from the place of his feet to the reaches of heaven; and a caller calls out to him: "O worshipper, if you knew Whom you address and Who watches you in this prayer, you would not turn away." It is narrated of Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) that when he rose to pray his color would change and he would tremble, and when he was asked about this he said: "I am about to stand before a great King."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: The five prayers, whoever safeguards their times and observes their limits will meet Allah on the Day of Resurrection with a covenant with Him that will admit him into Paradise; and whoever squanders their times and does not observe their limits will meet Allah with no covenant with Him: if He wills, He forgives him, and if He wills, He punishes him. It was said to him: What of one who is in his prayer and a need presses upon him in haste? He said: First let him know that the fulfillment of the need lies in the hand of the One before Whom he prays.

He said: The most beloved of deeds to Allah is prayer, and it is the last of the counsels of the prophets. Nothing is more excellent than that a man should wash or perform ablution and complete it, then withdraw to a place where no companion sees him, and Allah looks upon him as he bows and prostrates. When the servant prostrates, Iblis cries: "Woe to me! This one obeyed while I disobeyed; this one prostrated while I refused." He said: The nearest a servant comes to Allah is when he prostrates. And he said: When the Muslim servant devotes himself in his prayer, Allah Almighty turns His face toward him and appoints an angel to gather up the Quran from his mouth. He remains thus so long as he is turned toward his prayer; but if he turns away from it, Allah Almighty turns away from him and consigns him to the angel.

As for the times of the prayers, Allah Almighty said: "So exalt Allah when you reach the evening and when you reach the morning, and to Him is all praise in the heavens and the earth, and in the late afternoon and when you reach the time of noon." [Al-Rum: 17-18] And He said: "and after the 'Isha prayer." [Al-Nur: 58] And the Almighty said: "Establish prayer at the decline of the sun until the darkness of the night, and the Quran of Fajr; indeed, the Quran of Fajr is ever witnessed." [Al-Isra: 78]

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: The decline of the sun is its departure from the zenith, and that is the first time of Zuhr. The darkness of the night is its midpoint, and these encompass four prayers. And the Quran of Fajr is the Fajr prayer. Concerning the words of Allah Almighty, "So exalt Allah when you reach the evening" [Al-Rum: 17], he said: that is the Maghrib and 'Isha prayers. "And when you reach the morning" [Al-Rum: 17] is the Fajr prayer. "And in the late afternoon" [Al-Rum: 18] is the 'Asr prayer. "And when you reach the time of noon" [Al-Rum: 18] is the Zuhr prayer. These are the times of the prayers in the Book of Allah Almighty, stated in summary, and the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) interpreted and clarified them, as Allah Almighty had commanded him to make plain what was revealed to him.

He said: Jibril came to me when the light of dawn broke across the eastern horizon and stretched to the right and to the left. Dawn is of two kinds. The true dawn is this spreading light, and it is the first time of the Fajr prayer, by which food and all else forbidden to the fasting person become forbidden to him. Before it is the first dawn, called the wolf's tail, a thin light that rises from the place of sunrise and climbs upward without spreading across the horizon as the second dawn does. The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said: Jibril said to me, "Rise and pray Fajr," so I did. He came to me again on the second day after the light had spread, when the people could see one another, and said to me, "Rise and pray Fajr." He came to me for Zuhr when the sun had passed its zenith and the shadow of everything had become equal to it, meaning after the decline. For 'Asr, when the shadow of everything had become twice its length. For Maghrib, when the disc had set, meaning when the sun had gone down, and he came at that same time the next day. For the later 'Isha, when the twilight had vanished. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: this is the redness that lingers on the western horizon after the setting of the sun. On the second night he came when a third of the night had passed. The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said that Jibril then said to him: "The time is what lies between your prayer yesterday and your prayer today."

This is a well-attested report from the Messenger of Allah ((ع)), which we have set down in brief. It occurred at Makkah, for the report states that Jibril (ع) came to him there; then the Messenger of Allah ((ع)) later established at Madinah a Sunnah concerning the times. Many reports on this have been narrated from the Imams of his household (عليهم السلام), and from many others among the general body of Muslims as well, and I mention here only what suffices within the scope I have set for this book.

What we have narrated from the Imams of the household of the Messenger of Allah (عليهم السلام) is that every prayer has two times, except the Maghrib prayer, whose time is the setting of the sun and is not to be delayed beyond it. The most excellent times are their beginnings, save the later 'Isha, for its merit lies in delaying it, on account of the hardship in this for one who wishes to sleep, since the one who awaits the prayer is himself in prayer, as has come from the Messenger of Allah ((ع)). The Messenger of Allah ((ع)) said: One of you prays the prayer, and what has passed him of its time is a graver loss to him than to be deprived of his family and his wealth.

Ali (ع) said: Render the rights of your Lord at their due moments; pray the prayer at the first of its times. He said: The first of the time is the most excellent, so hasten to good as much as you are able. He said: The first of the time is the pleasure of Allah, and the end of the time is the pardon of Allah, and pardon is only for a sin or a shortcoming. And Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) said: The first of the time is ever the more excellent, so hasten to the merit as much as you are able. He said: The first of the time is the time of Allah, and it is the better of the two. He said: The first of the time is the most excellent, and what follows it remains open until the end of the time.

It is narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that he said: It is not for anyone to take the later of the two times as his time except for an infirmity or an excuse; the later time is only for one who is occupied, or forgets, or sleeps, for it was made for the sick and the one driven to it by necessity. He said: When the sun declines, the gates of heaven are opened, and I do not wish anyone to outstrip me in works, for I love that my scroll be the first scroll in which a righteous deed is written. And he said: How can one of you feel secure from the sudden calamities that may befall him if he neglects the prayer when its time has already entered?

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they enjoined watching for the zawal, which is the sun's departure from the middle of the sky toward the west. People have devised many instruments to determine this, the soundest and most reliable being that a circle is drawn with a compass upon a board or tile fixed in a place that catches the sun, laid perfectly level so that no edge rises above another. This is tested by pouring water upon it: if one side sinks, the water runs to it, and it is adjusted until level, then fastened, and the circle is drawn upon it. In its center, at the point where the compass foot stood, a small even nail is fixed, of a height such that the tip of its shadow reaches the line of the circle at the time of the sun. Then one observes at the start of day: when the tip of the nail's shadow reaches the line of the circle on the western side, that place is marked on the line with a point. In the late afternoon one likewise observes where the shadow reaches the line of the circle and marks it. Then with the compass one measures the arc between the two points on the southern side and marks its middle, and does the same on the northern side and marks it. A line is drawn along a straight edge from the southern mark to the northern mark, passing through the center of the nail, and that line lies opposite the middle of the sky. When the shadow lies straight along it, the sun stands at the middle of the sky; and when the shadow inclines from it a little, the sun has declined, and that is the first time of Zuhr, when one prays the Sunnah and then the obligatory prayer.

Ali (ع) said: The prayer of the decline is the prayer of the oft-returning. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: At that hour the gates of heaven are opened, supplication is answered, the winds stir, and Allah looks upon His creation.

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that when the sun declines, the time of the two prayers, Zuhr and 'Asr, enters. Rabi'a agreed with this saying, holding that the time of Zuhr and 'Asr, whether traveling or at home, is when the sun declines. Ibn al-Mubarak expressed a view close to it, saying: If a man were to rise when the shadow of everything equals its length after the decline and pray Zuhr, and another were to pray 'Asr at that same time, each would have prayed his prayer in its time. It was said to him: How can there be one time for two prayers without travel or excuse? Ibn al-Mubarak said to the questioner: Does this trouble you? It is Jibril who brought it; had he brought a single time for three prayers, we would have made it so for them.

The time of the 'Asr prayer, according to the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام), is after the worshipper has finished the Zuhr prayer, its obligatory and its Sunnah. The most they mentioned for this is about two hours of the day, so that the 'Asr prayer falls at the beginning of the ninth hour, and upon this is the practice; it ought not to be delayed. If it is delayed for an excuse or infirmity, the end of its time is the yellowing of the sun. The Maghrib prayer, its time is the setting of the sun below the western horizon, and it has no other time, as already mentioned. The times of the later 'Isha and of Fajr are as set out before. The rising of the sun is known by its rising itself; and if a mountain or some obstacle stands before its place of rising, the sign of its rising is the reddening of the western horizon, and the sign of its setting is the darkening of the eastern horizon. The night prayer follows the later 'Isha, and Witr follows the night prayer; there is no prayer after Witr until dawn rises, when one prays the two rak'ahs of Fajr and then Fajr after them.

They forbade the supererogatory prayer after sunrise until the sun has climbed, unless one enters the mosque and prays two rak'ahs before sitting; and likewise no supererogatory prayer is offered after 'Asr until the sun sets, though there is no harm in making up an obligatory prayer at these two times. They forbade prayer at the moment of the sun's rising and at its setting. When it is feared that an obligatory prayer will be lost, one begins with it before the supererogatory. The Zuhr prayer is delayed a little in intense heat, past the decline, to seek some coolness, for those who pray in congregation in a mosque. The Friday prayer is prayed when the sun declines and should not be delayed.

Zuhr and 'Asr, and Maghrib and 'Isha, may be joined at the end of the time of the first of them, while traveling, if there is need; and in residence, in the mosques, when there is an excuse such as rain, wind, or darkness. They are joined with one adhan and two iqamahs: when the imam finishes the first prayer he remains in his place, the muezzin rises and gives the iqamah, then the imam rises and prays the second.

Whoever forgets a prayer and does not recall it until the end of the time of a prayer now due, and fears that if he begins with the one he has missed he will lose the one whose time he is in, let him begin with the present one. But if he knows he can make up the missed prayer and still catch the present one, he begins with the missed one. If many prayers have escaped him, he begins with those he knows he can make up while still reaching the prayer whose time he is in, and leaves the rest to make up afterward. Whoever forgets a prayer and does not recall it until he has already stood in another prayer: if the one he forgot was Zuhr and he did not recall it until he had entered 'Asr, he forms the intention of Zuhr and then makes up 'Asr; and if it is otherwise, he completes the prayer he is in and then makes up what escaped him. Abu Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about the difference between these two cases, and he said: because the 'Asr prayer has no prayer after it. Whoever misses a supererogatory prayer may make it up if he wishes, and in that there is merit, though it is not obligatory. And whoever prays a prayer before its time has not discharged it, and must repeat it after its time has entered.

Once the worshipper has attained the purity that prayer requires, what summons him to stand before his Lord is the call to prayer, and the place appointed for that standing is the mosque. Of both the tradition has much to say.

### The Call to Prayer

Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) was asked about the origin of the call to prayer. He said that the Messenger of Allah (ص) was taught it on the night of al-Isra'. He recounted the hadith of al-Isra' at length: that the angel called the adhan to the Messenger of Allah (ص), and that he led the Prophets (عليهم السلام) in prayer.

He was then told that some people claimed the call to prayer had arisen from a vision seen by a man of the Ansar. At this he grew very angry and said, "إنا لله وإنا إليه راجعون. To Allah we belong and to Him we shall return. They have set their hand to the very face of their religion and fixed it upon a vision that a man among the people saw, as though the Messenger of Allah (ص) had followed him in it." Yet Allah, Mighty and Majestic, had said to His Messenger: "Say: I follow only what is revealed to me."

The Messenger of Allah (ص) said, "There are three things which, if my ummah knew what reward lay in them, they would draw lots to attain them: the call to prayer, going early to the Friday prayer, and the first row." It was said to him, "O Messenger of Allah, do you not fear that your nation will contend over this after you, even with swords, when they hear it?" He said, "Only the weak among them will stoop to contend over it."

He said, "The muezzins will be raised on the Day of Resurrection as the longest-necked of people, calling out the testimony that there is no god but Allah." By the length of their necks that day he (ص) meant their yearning toward the mercy of their Lord and their security from His punishment, in contrast to the state in which Allah, Mighty and Majestic, described the guilty when He said: "And if you could see the criminals bowing their heads before their Lord" [al-Sajdah: 12]. This is a known usage in the speech of the Arabs, who say of a man visited by something he detests that his head has fallen low.

Ali (ع) said, "I grieve over nothing except that I wish I had asked the Messenger of Allah (ص) for the adhan for al-Hasan and al-Husayn."

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they commanded the adhan be given with measured deliberation and the iqamah briskly; that both be given in paired phrases, with the final testimony of the iqamah pronounced singly; and that the adhan be given with "حي على خير العمل, Come to the best of deeds," and the iqamah likewise.

"Come to the best of deeds" was part of the call to prayer in the days of the Messenger of Allah (ص), of Abu Bakr, and in the first part of Umar's days. Then Umar saw fit to strike it from the call, saying, "If the ignorant hear that prayer is the best of deeds, they will abandon jihad." This has been related by many of the general body of scholars. But Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has set out the merit of prayer in more than one place in His Book, and set out the merit of jihad in it as well; and what may befall jihad is no reason to cut away what Allah has commanded and what the Sunnah of His Messenger (ص) established. Rather, the ignorant ought to be taught the truth of which they were ignorant and brought to see it clearly, so that their ignorance of a thing is not made a cause for dropping it. Were such a thing warranted, most of the obligations would fall away, for people are ignorant of most of them and confused about them in general.

They said that the muezzin, when he gives the adhan, should stand facing the qiblah. When he reaches "حي على الصلاة, Come to prayer," he turns his face to the right; and when he reaches "حي على الفلاح, Come to success," he turns his face to the left, keeping his feet in their place. There is no harm in a traveler giving the adhan while mounted, but he should give the iqamah only while standing on the ground. He should not give the adhan unless he is in a state of purity; if he gives it without purity, there is no harm, but he must not lead the prayer except in purity.

When the Messenger of Allah (ص) heard the muezzin, he would repeat what the muezzin said, until, reaching "Come to prayer," "Come to success," and "Come to the best of deeds," he would say, "لا حول ولا قوة إلا بالله, There is no power and no strength except with Allah." He said that whoever says this seeking the pleasure of Allah has from Allah a covenant that the Fire shall never touch him.

When the muezzin says that the prayer is now ready, it becomes obligatory upon those present in the mosque to fall silent and rise for prayer; there is no harm in speaking before that. The muezzin should not break off the adhan with speech or anything else; but if ignorance or necessity compels him to speak, let him resume the adhan from where he left off, and nothing further is required of him. The same holds for the iqamah. The adhan and the iqamah are to be given in every mosque in which prayer is performed.

It is not fitting to omit them, or either of them. Whoever prays alone should still give the adhan and the iqamah. Abu Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said, "Whoever gives the adhan and the iqamah and then prays has two rows of angels praying behind him; and whoever gives the iqamah without the adhan and prays has one row of angels behind him." To give both the adhan and the iqamah for every prayer is better. Whoever gives the iqamah without the adhan, that suffices him; and whoever prays with neither adhan nor iqamah need not repeat the prayer, for the call of the town suffices him. Neither adhan nor iqamah is required of women; if they give them, it is good and meritorious, and if they content themselves with the call of the town, that too is permitted them.

Whoever forgets the adhan and the iqamah, or one of them, until he has entered the prayer, owes nothing; he continues his prayer and does not break it off, for the prayer is an obligation and the adhan is a Sunnah, and an obligatory prayer is not to be broken for a Sunnah whose place is fulfilled without it, as we have said. And if, before entering the prayer, he is uncertain whether he gave the adhan or the iqamah, not knowing whether he did so or not, then if he gives them it is good, and if he prays without them nothing is required of him.

The adhan should not be given before the time of the prayer has come in. Whoever does so must repeat it once the time enters; if he does not repeat it, nothing is required of him, and his case is that of one who prays without the adhan. They permitted the adhan for the Fajr prayer alone to be given before dawn, though the adhan at dawn itself is better; the earlier call was permitted in order to rouse the sleeper. Confusion may arise on this account for one ignorant of the dawn, who prays when he hears the adhan before the time of prayer, so that his prayer does not count for him. The adhan at its proper time is better, and it is the practice that the Messenger of Allah (ص) established; upon it the imams call to prayer today (عليهم السلام), the muezzin greeting the imam and announcing the prayer to him after the adhan.

They permitted the adhan and iqamah to be given by a slave or by a boy who has not yet reached the age of dreams, and that the prayer be led in that manner. When the muezzin gives the adhan on behalf of the mosque and remains, the adhan and the iqamah stand for every prayer performed in that mosque; and those who were in the mosque when the adhan was given should not leave until they have prayed, unless one goes out for a need intending to return, knowing that he will still reach the prayer, or unless one is without ablution and goes out to perform it, striving to return in time. Even if he does not reach it, he may pray alone or join another congregation in another mosque, and no blame falls upon him.

There is no harm in giving the adhan in the mosque before the imam arrives, even at the very beginning of the time. If the appointed imam of the mosque comes, none other should be put forward to lead them, for he is an imam installed by the Imam; he waits with the prayer until the end of the time, and if he comes, well and good, and if not, they pray for themselves. Among the people of a locality, when they dispute over who should give the adhan, the worthiest, if they are equal in merit, is the one with the loudest voice. There is no harm in the adhan of a blind man if he follows the adhan of another who knows the time. There is no adhan or iqamah for a supererogatory prayer. A muezzin should not climb a minaret so high that he looks down from it upon the private quarters of the people and sees into their homes.

The Messenger of Allah (ص) said, "Whoever has a child born to him, let him give the adhan in the child's right ear and the iqamah in the left, and let him recite the Opening of the Book, the Verse of the Throne, the end of Surat al-Hashr, Surat al-Ikhlas, and the two surahs of refuge." He said, "Whoever does this for his child, the child is guarded from the accursed devil and his licking." The licking is when the devil licks the child's tongue: he touches the newborn with his tongue to defile it with his filth, and whoever does these things for the child guards him from it. The Messenger of Allah (ص) also said, "If the desert demons beset you, give the call to prayer." The ghilan are the sa'ali, and they are called the enchanters of the jinn; and his words, that the desert demons beset you, mean that the road has led you astray. So the Messenger of Allah (ص) commanded that whoever strays from the road and does not know it should give the call to prayer, for it will guide him, if Allah wills.

### On the Mosques

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, said: "The mosques of Allah are maintained only by those who believe in Allah and the Last Day, and establish prayer, and give the zakat, and fear none but Allah; it may be that those will be among the rightly guided" [al-Tawbah: 18]. And He, Exalted, said: "And the places of worship are for Allah alone, so call not upon anyone alongside Allah" [al-Jinn: 18]. And He, Exalted, said: "The polytheists are but impurity, so let them not approach the Sacred Mosque after this year of theirs" [al-Tawbah: 28].

The Messenger of Allah (ص) said, "Do not set out on a journey to any mosque save three: the Sacred Mosque, my mosque, and the mosque of Jerusalem." He said that prayer in the Sacred Mosque is equal to a hundred thousand prayers; prayer in the mosque of Medina to ten thousand; prayer in the mosque of Jerusalem to a thousand; prayer in the great mosque, meaning the mosque in which the Friday prayer is gathered in each region, to a hundred; prayer in the mosque of one's tribe to twenty-five; prayer in the market mosque to twelve; and a man's prayer alone in his house to a single prayer. He said that sitting in the mosque to await the prayer is itself an act of worship. He said, "Whoever takes the Quran as his companion and the mosque as his home, Allah builds for him a house in Paradise and raises him a degree below the middle station." And he said that the believer's place is his mosque, and his home is his hermitage.

He forbade the uncovering of the private parts in the mosque, that being what lies between the navel and the knee. He forbade that the prescribed punishments be carried out in the mosques, meaning retaliation for killing, and stoning, and the penalties for adultery, for false accusation, and for drinking; but as for matters of correction and discretionary discipline, there is no harm in their being addressed in the mosque. He forbade the raising of voices in the mosque, the drawing of weapons in it, buying and selling in it, the crying of lost property in it, and the sitting in it of one in a state of janabah.

It was narrated from Ali (ع) that he said, "There is no prayer for a neighbor of the mosque except in the mosque." He was asked, "And who is a neighbor of the mosque?" He said, "Whoever hears the call," meaning the adhan. This, and Allah knows best, is by way of stern admonition and discipline, for the prayer of one who lives near the mosque yet prays in his house is defective and he is bound to repeat it; yet the merit lies in praying in the mosque. It may be that his saying (ع) "there is no prayer" means that it has no prayer worthy of the name, that is, no prayer possessing the merit of one prayed in the mosque; and we have already spoken of the multiplication of reward for prayer in the mosque.

It was narrated from the Messenger of Allah (ص) that a man asked him, "O Messenger of Allah, I go out to my camels with my family, my children, and my servant-boys, and I give the adhan and the iqamah and lead them in prayer; are we a congregation, with the merit of one?" He said, "Yes." The man said, "Sometimes the servant-boys go off to graze the camels, so I give the adhan and the iqamah and lead my family and my children in prayer; are we a congregation?" He said, "Yes." The man said, "Sometimes the children go off tracking the traces of rain, so I give the adhan and the iqamah and pray with my family; are we a congregation?" He said, "Yes." The man said, "Sometimes the household is occupied with its labor, so I give the adhan and the iqamah and pray alone; am I a congregation?" The Messenger of Allah (ص) said, "The believer alone is a congregation."

We have mentioned before that whoever gives the adhan and the iqamah and prays has two rows of angels praying behind him. What is commended for the worshipper who intends to pray is that he pray in the great mosque, for the multiplied reward that has been transmitted concerning prayer in it, as we have mentioned. If he cannot, let him pray in the mosque of his tribe; if that is not possible, let him pray in congregation in a market or wherever a congregation may be formed; and if he cannot do even that, let him pray in his house and gather his family and his children with him, for in that lies the merit of congregation and the reward of keeping his household constant in prayer. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, said: "And enjoin the prayer upon your household, and be steadfast in it" [Taha: 132]. And if he cannot do that, let him give the adhan and the iqamah and pray alone; and we have mentioned the merit of that and how the reward is lessened, degree by degree.

Ali (ع) said, "Waiting for the prayer after the prayer is a kind of frontier-guarding." And it is of the Sunnah, when you sit in the mosque, to face the qiblah, that is, when one is able; and if he turns aside from it, there is no harm. He said, "When the afflictions descend, the people of the mosques are kept safe." And he said, "Reclining at ease in the mosques is the monasticism of the Arabs."

The Messenger of Allah (ص) said, "Keep your mosques clear of your buying and selling, your disputes, your quarrels, your weapons, and your madmen"; and he forbade the raising of voices in them and the carrying out of the prescribed punishment. He said that they were to be perfumed with incense every seven days, and that the means of purification were to be placed in them.

Whoever keeps the mosque clear of his spittle will meet Allah on the Day of Resurrection with a smiling face, his record given into his right hand; for the mosque recoils from spittle as a morsel of flesh recoils in the fire, and writhes at it as one of you writhes from the stroke of a cane. Spitting in the mosque is a sin, and its expiation is to bury it; and whoever spits in the mosque, let him pray two rak'ahs.

He said (ع), concerning the mosque and what is forbidden within it, that the prohibition falls upon whoever casts something onto the wall of the mosque, its floor, or its carpet. Should something impure come upon a man, let him cover it with his garment, wipe it away with the underside of his sandal, or bury it so that it cannot be seen and will harm no one, and in that there is nothing wrong.

From 'Ali (ع) it is related that he said: "Do not make the mosques a place for idle talk." And he told of a man from the people of Yemen who entered the mosque of the Messenger of Allah having eaten garlic, so that its odor filled the air. The Messenger of Allah was troubled by it and said: "Whoever eats this plant, let him not draw near the mosque." He taught that upon entering the mosque one should say: بسم الله وبالله، السلام عليك أيها النبي ورحمة الله وبركاته، السلام علينا وعلى عباد الله الصالحين, and then greet the people of the mosque. He said: "It is among the rights of the mosque that when you enter it, you pray two rak'ahs therein," meaning during those times in which voluntary prayers are permitted, which we have already set forth.

'Ali (ع) said that for one who does not know the precise direction, the qibla lies between the east and the west, and that the qibla faces the Sacred House. Allah, mighty and majestic, said: ﴿فَوَلِّ وَجْهَكَ شَطْرَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ وَحَيْثُ مَا كُنْتُمْ فَوَلُّوا وُجُوهَكُمْ شَطْرَهُ﴾ [البقرة: ١٤٤].

The Messenger of Allah said that one should not pray except in clean, dry clothing, and that to pray without a barrier before oneself is a mark of coarseness. He said: "If one of you prays in an open field, let him set before him something like the back of a saddle, if he can find it." From 'Ali (ع) it is related that he disliked a man's praying toward a sleeping person placed before him, or toward a woman's shoes, unless he first sets a barrier before him. He said: "If you pray toward a barrier, draw near to it, and let the space between you and it be the measure of your prostration when you prostrate." For the Messenger of Allah said: "When one of you prays, let him draw near to the barrier and not leave a gap between himself and the qibla, lest the devil take it as a passage." He forbade the display of images in the place of prayer. And he said: as for the mosque, or the room in a man's house where he prays, if he does not turn to face the people but wishes only to make room and put the space to use, there is nothing wrong in that.

As for the imamate, the Messenger of Allah said: "Set forward the best among you in your prayer, for the imam stands as the delegate of the people." From 'Ali (ع) it is related that he said the sick man should not lead the sound in prayer, this being particular to the Messenger of Allah alone, and that one who is bound should not lead those who are free. We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they disliked the imamate of the leper, the one wasted by leprosy, the child of fornication, the insane, the enslaved, and the blind, unless such a one leads rightly when there is need of him, and he is not to be taken as a settled imam. Likewise they did not hold that the bedouin of full standing, nor the one who has undergone a prescribed punishment, nor the madman, should lead, though they permitted that such men lead one another, each leading those in a condition like his own. They did not hold prayer permissible behind those who bear enmity toward the family of Muhammad and openly declare their hatred of them, except in a state of dissimulation, and we seek refuge in Allah from such affliction. Whoever is tried with this prays behind one whose condition is as described only if he fears for himself. He forms the intention for himself alone and does not follow the man as imam. He bows when the man bows, prostrates when he prostrates, and departs when he departs, intending all of it for himself as though he prayed alone with no imam.

If one who has performed tayammum is more deserving of the imamate, he may lead those who have performed ablution. And whoever leads a people in prayer, then afterward learns that he was not in a state of purity, must repeat the prayer, and so must they. 'Umar once led the people, then, when he had finished, said: "O people, 'Umar has led you while he was in a state of major impurity." They asked, "What then do you hold?" He said: "In my view, I must repeat, but there is no repetition upon you." Yet 'Ali (ع) said: "Rather, the repetition is upon you and upon them, for the people bow and prostrate by their imam."

The most deserving of the imamate is the most excellent, the best in recitation, the most learned. If they are equal in that, then the eldest in years takes precedence. When a man leads two others, he stands before them and they form a row behind him. When he leads one, he sets him at his right; and if another comes and joins the prayer, the newcomer advances and stands behind the imam. There is no harm in the imam's leading with a light barrier between himself and the man behind him. They disliked the imam's lengthening the prayer when leading a congregation, for among them may be the weak and the one with a pressing need, while one who prays alone does better to lengthen it. They said a woman leads other women but does not stand ahead of them; rather she stands in the midst of them, in a single row with them.

If the imam errs or slips, passing from one surah to another, or leaving out something of the Quran, or moving to something else of it, there is no harm in that, and those behind him are not obliged to correct him so long as he continues to recite. But if he falters, halting and falling silent, they prompt him and open for him from behind what he stumbled over, and once he continues they fall silent again. Even if two men stand in prayer, each intending to lead the other, and their prayers are completed and they learn of this only afterward, their prayers are valid, for the imam's prayer is not spoiled if no one stands behind him or if all turn away. But if each had intended to follow the other, their prayers are void, for each held himself to be a follower praying behind an imam when there was no imam over him.

They disliked the imam's offering voluntary prayer, after the obligatory one, in the very spot where he led, and preferred that he move to another place. They recommended that, when he turns to depart, he turn to his right, and that he not break through the rows or walk between them, and that he sit a while in his place after the salutation, calling upon Allah so that whoever missed part of the prayer may complete what he missed.

Concerning the congregation and the rows, the Messenger of Allah said: "My community will not cease to be shielded from affliction until they deal in usury, bribery appears among them, they sever the ties of kinship, they abandon prayer in congregation, and they no longer make for this House. When they do that, they will be left without regard." And he said that whoever prays the five prayers in congregation is to be thought well of in every good and his testimony accepted, so long as no ill is known of him. Abu Ja'far ibn 'Ali (ع) was asked whether prayer in congregation is obligatory. He said: "The prayer is obligatory, but the congregation is not obligatory in every prayer; it is Sunnah. Yet whoever forsakes it out of aversion, turning from it and from the community of the believers with neither excuse nor cause, in disdain of it, has no prayer."

It is related of 'Ali (ع) that he once stood the whole night in prayer, and when dawn came he slept and missed the Fajr in the mosque. The Messenger of Allah, not seeing him, came to the house of Fatimah after finishing the prayer and asked: "Daughter, what has kept your cousin from witnessing the prayer with us?" She told him what had happened, and he said: "What he missed of the Fajr in congregation is better than a whole night's standing." Then he roused him and said as much to him. And he said: "O 'Ali, the earth cries out to Allah from the sleeper upon it at the rising of the sun." 'Ali (ع) said: "That I should pray the 'Isha' and the Fajr in congregation is dearer to me than that I should spend all that lies between them in worship." And he said: "Beneath the shade of the Throne, on a Day when there is no shade but its shade, is a man who goes out from his house, having perfected his purification, walking to one of the houses of Allah to fulfill therein one of the obligations of Allah, and who perishes upon that path."

The Messenger of Allah said: "The best of the rows in prayer is the first, and the best of the rows in funeral prayer is the last, and the best of the rows for women is the rearmost." 'Ali (ع) said the best place in the first row is at the imam's right, and Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said the best of it is what lies nearest the imam. We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they commanded the completing of the rows and the closing of the gaps. Whoever stands in a row and sees a gap in the row before him may walk forward to fill it, and whoever feels crowded in the row may step back to a gap in the row behind him and stand in it. There is no harm in one man's praying alone behind the row when he finds no place within it, and they permitted one who finds no place in the first row to advance and stand level with the imam when he can do no other. The least space between two rows is the measure by which the worshipper may fall into prostration, prostrate, and rise from it, for when the people of a row cannot reach the place of prostration there is no prayer for them. They permitted forming rows between the pillars when nothing else can be found. And the Messenger of Allah said: "Straighten your rows, align your shoulders, and do not differ, lest your hearts differ and the devil slip in among you as the little ones of the ḥadhaf slip among the flock." The ḥadhaf are small black sheep, and he likened their entering among the large sheep to the devil's entering between the rows when he finds a breach in them, to corrupt the prayer of its people and to occupy their hearts against it. We seek refuge in Allah from the devil and his works.

As for the rows of prayer and their foundation, the Messenger of Allah said: "Deeds are only by intentions, and to each man belongs only what he intended." 'Ali (ع) said: "Whoever prays with his intention set upon the prayer alone, nothing else entering into it, has it accepted from him when it is sound in what is outward and what is inward." Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said it does not befit the worshipper to enter into prayer until he has formed the intention, settled his heart upon it, and determined which prayer it is, whether an obligation, a Sunnah, or a voluntary act; and if it is an obligation, that he intend which of the five it is, holding this firmly as he enters. He opens the prayer with the takbir, raising his hands with palms open and fingers together and extended, his fingertips level with his ears, then letting his hands fall along his sides and thighs. He does not lay one hand upon the other, nor hold them beneath his breast as some of the common people do, claiming this to be from the words of Allah, mighty and majestic: ﴿فَصَلِّ لِرَبِّكَ وَانْحَرْ﴾ [الكوثر: ٢]. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) was asked about this and said: "The sacrificing is after the prayer of the Eid, and the 'sacrifice' within the prayer is uprightness in standing, that the worshipper hold his spine and his chest erect." He said the Messenger of Allah forbade laying the hands one upon the other in prayer, saying that this is what the Jews do, and he commanded that the hands be let free. And Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) said that a woman, when she stands in prayer, places her hands upon her breast to cover it.

The worshipper then turns himself and says: وجهت وجهي لله ناظر السموات والأرض حنيفًا مسلمًا وما أنا من المشركين، إن صلاتي ونسكي ومحياي ومماتي لله رب العالمين لا شريك له، وبذلك أمرت وأنا من المسلمين، على ملة إبراهيم ودين محمد وولاية علي بن أبي طالب أمير المؤمنين، وأبرأ إلى الله من أعدائه الظالمين، أعوذ بالله السميع العليم من النار ومن الشيطان الرجيم. He utters this with his tongue, quietly and with reverence, not aloud where the recitation is silent nor where it is aloud.

Then he opens the recitation with بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم, saying it quietly where the recitation is quiet and aloud where it is aloud, and he must not do otherwise, for it is a verse of the Book of Allah, mighty and majestic. Some have claimed it is not of the Book of Allah, yet it stands established at the head of every surah, revealed thus by Jibril (ع) to the Messenger of Allah; and the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام), together with many of the generality, agreed upon this and narrated that it was so revealed, and that the completion of one surah and the beginning of the next were known when بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم came down. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: "Dissimulation is my religion and the religion of my fathers, save in three: the drinking of intoxicants, the wiping over the leather socks, and the abandoning of the recitation of بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم." Meaning that he himself neither drinks an intoxicant, nor wipes over the socks, nor omits the recitation of بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم at the head of every surah, in prayer or otherwise, and does not enjoin its omission nor permit it to anyone, whether in a state of dissimulation or not.

The worshipper then recites, after بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم, the Opening of the Book and a surah, opening the surah too with بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم, in the two rak'ahs of Fajr and the first two of Dhuhr, 'Asr, Maghrib, and the last 'Isha'. For the remaining rak'ahs nothing particular of the surahs is fixed, and no recitation of the Quran is required in them. Yet it is of merit that one who prays alone lengthen his recitation as far as he is able, though he may shorten it if he wishes; and it is of merit that one who leads a people lighten the prayer, to the measure by which he knows those behind him are able and equal to it, for among them may be the weak and the one in need. The Messenger of Allah rebuked Mu'adh ibn Jabal over this very thing, when word reached him that Mu'adh had prayed with a people and lengthened it upon them, and he said to him: "Do not be one who drives people away; pray a prayer suited to the weakest of your companions." The Messenger of Allah would lighten the prayer, while keeping it complete, when he led the people, and would lengthen it when he prayed alone, until his feet would swell from the length of his standing upon them.

"O Messenger of Allah, you are bringing hardship upon yourself, though Allah has forgiven you your past and future sins." He answered, "Should I not then be a thankful servant?" And concerning the measure of the standing in the prayer, what has come from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) is that the longest standing belongs to the Fajr prayer, and after it the Isha, which is less lengthy than Fajr, then the Dhuhr, then the 'Asr, and then the Maghrib, whose standing is the shortest. No fixed measure is laid down for this.

In an obligatory prayer one should not join two surahs after the Opening of the Book within a single rak'ah, though there is no harm in doing so in the voluntary prayers. If a worshipper begins one surah and then wishes to recite another, he may turn to the second only before he has passed the midpoint of the one he began, except for Surah al-Jumu'ah on Friday, Surah al-Ikhlas, and Surah al-Kafirun; once he has begun any of these, he must not break off from it.

The worshipper recites aloud in the Fajr prayer and in the first two rak'ahs of the Maghrib and the Isha, whether he prays alone or leads others, and he recites quietly in all the rest. In the remaining rak'ahs, the last two of Dhuhr, 'Asr, and Isha, and the final rak'ah of Maghrib, he recites the Opening of the Book alone in a low voice.

After completing the Opening of the Book he should not say "Amin," as the common people do, nor should the one who prays behind the imam say it, for the Messenger of Allah (ع) forbade that. He and the Imams of his descendants (عليهم السلام) said, "My nation will remain upon the right path of its religion so long as they do not raise a clamor with 'Amin' in their prayer," and the rest of the hadith was mentioned. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that only the Jews used to do this, and it was the ignorant among the people who imitated them in it.

The worshipper should turn to his prayer with his heart and empty his mind for it as far as he is able, for the Messenger of Allah (ع) said, "When the servant turns toward his prayer, Allah, mighty and majestic, turns toward him with His face and His mercy; and when he turns away from it, Allah turns away from him and entrusts him to His angels." Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said, "Of the worshipper's prayer only that is raised up which he offered with presence of heart, and this may be a half of it, or a quarter, or less, or more." Yet Allah, mighty and majestic, completes this through the voluntary prayers. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام).

They command the worshipper to be humble in his prayer, to magnify the majesty of Allah within it, and to make his own soul feel this. When Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) wished to enter into prayer, his color would change and he would tremble, so that those present would look at him and ask what had befallen him, and he would say, "I am about to stand before a great King."

They forbade turning about in prayer and letting the gaze wander, and they commanded the worshipper to keep his gaze upon the place of his prostration and to still his limbs as much as he can. There is no harm, if he grows weary, in his shifting his weight from one foot to the other, nor in his attending to some need of his body or his clothing, or reaching for it with his hands; yet if he is able to leave this undone and remain still, that is better. It was narrated of Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) that while he prayed his robe slipped from him and he did not reach out to restore it. When he was asked about this he said, "Do you not know before Whom I was standing, and with Whom I was in intimate speech? By Allah, what I was engaged in occupied me from what you saw."

If a garment falls from a worshipper during prayer and lays bare some part of his nakedness, he must hasten to restore it as far as he can and to cover what has been exposed. The nakedness of a man is what lies between the navel and the knee, while the whole body of a woman is nakedness, save her face and her hands. As for Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع), whose robe fell and who did not replace it, at that time the robe was an outer covering worn over the garment, so that when it fell he was content with the garment beneath it.

The worshipper should not spread his feet apart when he stands in prayer, unless there is some infirmity in him; rather he stands in a balanced posture, leaving between them only a moderate space by which he can stand at ease.

There is no harm in the worshipper's repeating a verse or a word of the Qur'an by way of admonition and the instilling of awe. If he weeps out of the fear of Allah, this does not sever his prayer; rather there is in it merit and reward. But he should not force weeping upon himself, for such tears are only proper when they come unbidden and he cannot hold them back; if he contrives them, he severs his prayer, and there is in it ostentation, which is detestable.

They said that the worshipper should not break off his recitation and fall silent to attend to something he hears, such as a groan or a cry or the like from someone near him; he should do this for no cause. As for other than this, we have been told that Ibn al-Kawwa' entered the mosque in Kufa while the Commander of the Faithful, Ali (ع), was leading the people in prayer and reciting. Ibn al-Kawwa', from behind him, raised his voice with the verse, "And it has already been revealed to you and to those before you: if you associate others with Allah, your work will surely come to nothing, and you will surely be among the losers." So the Commander of the Faithful (ع) fell silent, then resumed his recitation; Ibn al-Kawwa' repeated the verse, and he fell silent again. This happened three times. Then the Commander of the Faithful (ع) recited, "So be patient; indeed the promise of Allah is true, and let not those who have no certainty of faith unsettle you."

They commanded that the Qur'an be pondered when the reciter recites it, whether within his prayer or outside of it.

They said that the least by which a prayer is completed, and the smallest measure of it, is the opening of the prayer with the takbir of consecration, the standing for whoever is able, the bowing, the prostration, and the sitting for the tashahhud. Whoever performs these five bounds among the bounds of the prayer, and forgets or does not know what lies beyond them, his prayer is nonetheless complete. Whoever is ignorant of the remaining bounds of the prayer is obliged to learn them and to observe them without delaying or putting it off, and to exert his utmost in learning and memorizing them. Whoever knows the bounds of the prayer is not permitted to leave any of them deliberately, nor to leave off the recitation within it, nor the rest of the takbirs, nor the tashahhud, nor the tasbih, nor to depart from the prayer without the salutation, nor to omit any of its obligatory and its established bounds deliberately. If he does any of that deliberately, he has no prayer, and he is obliged to repeat whatever of the prayer he left in this way.

Whoever prays behind an imam and follows him should make the takbir when the imam makes the takbir, and be silent when the imam recites aloud. He should say, "Our Lord, to You be praise," when the imam says, "Allah hears the one who praises Him," and make the takbir in the same manner throughout the whole prayer, doing this quietly without raising his voice. He performs the tashahhud when the imam performs it and gives the salutation when the imam gives it. And if the imam is one whose state he does not approve of, and behind whom he does not truly take his lead, but prays behind him out of taqiyyah, he opens the prayer for himself, intending that he prays alone and does not take that imam as his leader; he recites, and bows and prostrates along with him without intending thereby to follow him, and he departs when the imam departs, doing in all of this as he would were he praying alone.

Whether he is an imam or not, the worshipper raises his hands when he means to bow, and raises them again when he lifts his head from the bowing, just as he raises them at the takbir of consecration; he does this with the takbir of the bowing and with his saying, "Allah hears the one who praises Him." When he bows, he grasps his knees with his palms and spreads his fingers over them, and he stretches his back evenly until his shoulders and his back are level, for the Messenger of Allah (ع) used to do this, so that were water poured upon his back it would settle and not run off. He neither raises his head nor lowers it, but keeps it level with his back. In the bowing he says, "Glory be to my Lord, the Magnificent, and praise be to Him," three times, unhurriedly and with composure, whether he is an imam, prays alone, or follows an imam. In the prostration he says, "Glory be to my Lord, the Most High, and exalted is He," likewise three times, without haste, letting his heart feel in all of this the magnifying of Allah, mighty and majestic.

If he prays alone, with no congregation whom he leads, and he adds to the magnifying of Allah in this what he wishes, there is merit in it. It has been narrated of a company of the Imams (عليهم السلام) that before the three glorifications in the bowing they would say, "O Allah, to You have I bowed, to You have I humbled myself, in You have I believed, and to You have I submitted; and You are my Lord. My hearing and my sight, my hair and my skin, my flesh and my blood, my sinews and my bones, and all that my two feet bear up, have humbled themselves to You, not disdaining, nor arrogant, nor weary of Your worship, but in reverence toward You and lowliness before Your obedience. Blessed is Allah, Lord of the worlds." And in the prostration they would say, "To You have I prostrated, to You have I humbled myself, in You have I believed, and to You have I submitted; and You are my Lord. My face has prostrated to the One who created it and fashioned it, who brought forth its hearing and its sight and shaped it in due proportion; blessed is Allah, the best of creators. O Allah, Your forgiveness is wider than my sins and Your mercy is greater than my wrongdoings, so forgive me and have mercy upon me, O Most Merciful of the merciful. There is no god but You, truly and in truth. I have prostrated to You, O Lord, in worship and in servitude; I prostrate to none but You and I worship none, O Lord, but You alone." They would also increase the glorifications of the bowing and the prostration, making them seven, and in this there is merit for the one who does it when he prays alone. As for the one who leads a congregation, we have already mentioned that the sunnah in his case is to make it light while yet completing it.

They said that when the worshipper raises his head from the bowing and says, "Allah hears the one who praises Him," he should say quietly, upon his tongue, "Our Lord, to You be all praise," whether he is an imam or not, and stand upright and erect. If he is an imam he then goes down into prostration, and he does the same if he prays alone. If he prays alone and adds to this, that is good, for it has come down from them (عليهم السلام) that here too there is an addition, as in the bowing and the prostration, and that they would say thereafter, "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds, Possessor of might and majesty, of greatness, of grandeur, and of power. O Allah, forgive me, have mercy upon me, restore me, raise me, and provide for me, for I stand in need of whatever good You send down to me." Then he makes the takbir of the prostration and goes down to prostrate.

When the worshipper goes down to prostrate, he rests upon the inner surface of his palms and spreads them upon the ground, setting them down before his knees; then he kneels upon his knees and prostrates. He holds his elbows somewhat away from his sides, extends his neck, and prostrates upon his forehead and his nose. He does not lay his forearms upon the ground but holds them up, raised from the earth. He draws the fingers of his hands together, with their tips level with his ears yet at a distance from them, and he presses against that upon which he prostrates with the inner surface of his palms, not drawing them into his sleeves.

When he raises his head from the first prostration, he sits until every joint of his returns to its place. He sets his hands upon his thighs, spreads his left leg beneath his right thigh without sitting upon it, and sets his right leg upright upon its toe if he is able, and if not he lays it upon its side. Then he performs the second prostration, and he sits in this same manner for the tashahhud, both the first and the last. Between the two prostrations he says, "O Allah, forgive me and have mercy upon me." If it is prolonged and he prays alone he says, "O Allah, forgive me, have mercy upon me, restore me, raise me, guide me, pardon me, grant me well-being, and provide for me, for I stand in need of whatever good You send down to me."

When he rises from the first rak'ah, he rises from the prostration without lowering himself to sit upon the ground. He should not recite anything of the Qur'an in his bowing or in his prostration. When he departs from the prayer he does so with two salutations, turning his face to the right and to the left, saying with each salutation, "Peace be upon you, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings," intending thereby those around him if he is in a congregation, and the recording angels if he prays alone. When he has finished the prayer and means to rise and go, he turns away to his right. If he is the imam, then when the people have turned to their voluntary prayers he does not walk between the rows, but passes straight through them by an opening, and he does not walk before one who is praying.
