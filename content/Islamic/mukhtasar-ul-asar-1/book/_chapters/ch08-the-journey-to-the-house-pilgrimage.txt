# The Journey to the House: Pilgrimage

There remains the greatest of the journeys undertaken for the sake of Allah, the Hajj, and what is stated concerning it. Allah Almighty said: "And proclaim the Hajj among the people; they will come to you on foot and on every lean camel; they will come from every distant mountain pass" [al-Hajj: 27]. It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع), from his father, that the Messenger of Allah (ع) said: When Allah Almighty commanded Ibrahim (ع) to call the people to Hajj, he ascended Mount Safa, placed his finger in his ear, and called out as Allah Almighty had commanded him. His call was heard by those in the east and in the west, even those yet unborn in the loins of their fathers, and they answered him. Whoever fulfills the Hajj once has answered once, whoever fulfills it twice has answered twice, and whoever fulfills it more times has answered accordingly. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: "From this the Talbiyah was made."

The Messenger of Allah (ع) said: "Hajj is the jihad of every weak person." He also said: "Whoever circumambulates this House seven times and prays two rak'ahs, the best of prayers, will be forgiven." When he stood on the eve of Arafah, he faced the people and said three times: "Welcome to the delegation of Allah!" Then he said: "Those who ask shall be given, and those who fall short in this world shall be compensated; for every thousand dirhams spent, good deeds are written for them. O people, shall I not give you glad tidings? This evening Allah Almighty commanded His angels, and they descended to the earth, and He said to them: 'O My angels, look upon My servants, dusty and disheveled, who have come to Me from the ends of the earth. Do you know what they ask?' They will say: 'Our Lord, You know best.' And He will say: 'They ask Me for forgiveness, and I bear witness to you that I have forgiven them.'"

Hajj carries the reward of the Garden, and 'Umrah is expiation for every sin. He (ع) said: "Alternate between Hajj and 'Umrah, for they negate poverty and sins and bring sustenance." He said: "There are sins that cannot be forgiven except at 'Arafat." Concerning the Hajj caravan he said: "Do not turn them away," meaning that their reward is written for them whether or not you conceal it; no veil is lifted from them unless their evil deeds are removed; and when they have fulfilled their rites it is said to them: "You have built a foundation, so do not demolish it. What has passed is enough for you, so do well in what is to come." The Prophet (ع) said: "Whoever desires this world and the next, let him come to this House, for whatever a servant has asked of his Lord, Allah will grant him some of it or store it for him." Then he said: "O people, you must perform Hajj and 'Umrah and alternate between them, for they wash away sins as water washes away grime, and they negate poverty as fire removes the dross of iron."

'Ali (ع) used to say that the righteous man, once he has completed the Hajj, takes up his works anew, meaning that he has already been forgiven for what preceded. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) traced back to the Prophet (ع) that he said: "Pilgrims are of three kinds, and the best of them in portion is the man who has been forgiven for his past and future sins. Next is the man who has been forgiven for his past sins and begins his works afresh. Next, and the least of them, is the man who has merely safeguarded his family and his wealth." The pilgrim must inevitably sustain some loss in his wealth, whether through the exertion itself or through a shortfall in his provisioning. Whoever suffers loss in either his wealth or his family while on Hajj has forfeited one of the two rewards, yet he is forgiven for what he has done and what he has deferred, his former sins are forgiven, and he is summoned to fear Allah in what lies ahead.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: When Allah Almighty sent Adam (ع) down to the earth, he wandered to the House just as he had seen the angels circling the Throne, and he called out to his Lord asking for forgiveness. The call came: "Allah has forgiven you." He said: "O Lord, and my descendants?" And the call came: "O Adam, whoever among your descendants comes to this place bearing his sin as you have borne yours, Allah will forgive him." Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: "The guarantee of the believing pilgrim rests with Allah Almighty, that even if he sins after returning to his family, he remains under Allah's protection for seventy nights." He (ع) said concerning the words of Allah Almighty, "Purify My House for those who circumambulate it, those who remain in devotion, and those who bow and prostrate" [al-Baqarah: 125]: "When Allah Almighty revealed that, He sent down upon the Ka'bah one hundred and seventy mercies: sixty for those who circumambulate it, fifty for those who remain in devotion, forty for those who worship within it, and twenty for those who behold it."

A man asked Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع): "Which is better, voluntary Hajj or voluntary charity?" The questioner kept recalling charity and its virtue, and Abu 'Abdullah (ع) kept answering that Hajj is better, repeating it several times, until he looked toward the mountain of Abu Qubays and said: "Even if you were to give in charity its weight in gold and silver, you would not attain the reward of Hajj." Then he said: "Is there in charity a circumambulation of the House? Is there in charity a standing at 'Arafah? Is there in charity anything of the rites?" The questioner said no. He said: "Then Hajj is better."

He (ع) said concerning the words of Allah Almighty, "That they may witness benefits for them" [al-Hajj: 28], that these are benefits for them in their religion. He said: "When the believing servant sets out from his home as a pilgrim, he takes no step, and his mount carries him no stage, except that a good deed is written for him by it, an evil deed is erased from him by it, and he is raised a degree by it. When he stands at 'Arafah, though his sins were as the number of the dust of the earth, they are erased from him. When he casts the pebbles, his rank is raised, to be fulfilled for him on the Day of Resurrection. When he shaves his head, not a hair falls from him but Allah makes its place a light for him on the Day of Resurrection. Whatever he spends is written for him. And when he circumambulates the House, he returns as the day his mother bore him," meaning sound and free of sins. Then he recited the words of Allah Almighty: "Whoever hastens his departure in two days, there is no sin on him, and whoever delays, there is no sin on him, for the one who fears Allah" [al-Baqarah: 203]. And he (ع) said: "No one stands upon those mountains, whether righteous or wicked, and calls upon Allah except that He answers him. As for the righteous, he is answered in his Hereafter, and as for the other, he is answered in this world."

On the obligation of Hajj, Allah Almighty said: "And Hajj to the House is a duty owed to Allah by the people, for whoever is able to find a way to it; and whoever disbelieves, then indeed Allah is free of need of the worlds" [Al 'Imran: 97]. The Prophet (ع) said: When this verse was revealed, the people asked: "O Messenger of Allah, is it every year?" He remained silent. They asked him twice more, and he said: "No; and had I said yes, it would have become obligatory." Then Allah Almighty revealed: "O you who believe, do not ask about things that, if disclosed to you, would distress you" [al-Ma'idah: 101]. 'Ali (ع) said concerning the words of Allah Almighty, "And whoever disbelieves, then indeed Allah is free of need of the worlds" [Al 'Imran: 97]: "This is said of the one who abandons Hajj while he is able to perform it."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about the words of Allah Almighty, "for whoever is able to find a way to it" [Al 'Imran: 97]: what is the ability to find a way? He asked the questioner what people say about it, and the man replied that they say it is provisions and a mount. Abu 'Abdullah (ع) said: "Abu Ja'far (ع) was asked about this, and he said: 'The people would be ruined then. If a man who has nothing but provisions and a mount, with no sustenance for his family besides that, were compelled to set out with it and not leave it for them, they would indeed be ruined.'" When asked, then, what the ability is, he said: "The ability to make the journey, sufficient provision for it, the presence of what sustains one's dependents, and the security of the road. Has not Allah Almighty made zakat obligatory only upon the one who possesses two hundred dirhams?"

He (ع) was asked about the man who postpones the Hajj, prevented from it only by a trade that occupies him or a debt he owes, and he said: "He has no excuse; it is not fitting for him to postpone the Hajj, and if he dies, he has abandoned one of the laws of Islam." He (ع) said: "Whoever dies without having performed the Hajj of Islam, not prevented by a need that pressed hard upon him, nor by an illness he could not bear the journey with, nor by a ruler who barred him, let him die a Jew or a Christian." He said: "Hajj is obligatory upon a person once in his lifetime." It was said to him: "And whoever abandons it while able, until he dies?" He said: "He is among those of whom Allah Almighty said, 'And We will gather him on the Day of Resurrection blind.'" It was said: "Blind?" He said: "Yes, blind to the path of truth and good." He said that the Messenger of Allah (ع) said: "If my ummah were to abandon this House and cease to make for it, they would not be granted respite."

He was asked about the child taken on Hajj, and he said: "That does not suffice for him; the Hajj is upon him when he reaches maturity." He was asked about one who performs Hajj while not knowing this matter, then Allah favors him with its knowledge, and he said: "His Hajj suffices him, though it would be more beloved to me that he perform Hajj again. And if he was a Nasib holding to enmity and performed Hajj upon that, then Allah favored him with knowledge, the Hajj is upon him when he is able." He (ع) said of the owned servant who performs Hajj: "It suffices for him so long as he is owned; and if he is freed, the Hajj is upon him when he is able, for there is no obligation of Hajj upon him while he is owned." Likewise the mother of a child whose master takes her on Hajj: it does not suffice for her. A man may not prevent his wife from the obligatory Hajj if she finds a mahram of hers to travel with her, and provision to spend, and the road is secure. He (ع) said: "The divorced woman may perform Hajj during her waiting period if she wishes." If a man is insolvent and another performs Hajj on his behalf, then he becomes able, the Hajj is still upon him.

He (ع) was asked about 'Umrah and said: "It is obligatory as the Hajj is obligatory," and he recited the words of Allah Almighty: "And complete the Hajj and 'Umrah for Allah" [al-Baqarah: 196]. He said: "Their completion is their performance." He said that a man may bring forward whichever of the two he wishes, the Hajj or the 'Umrah, and whoever wishes may join them together. Whoever joins them and drives the offering does not leave the state of ihram until he has sacrificed, and that is the qarin, because Allah Almighty says: "Do not shave your heads until the offering reaches its place." And whoever has no offering with him and joins them is performing tamattu': when he comes to Mecca he performs tawaf and sa'y and leaves ihram, and that is the 'Umrah; then he enters ihram for the Hajj from Mecca. Allah Almighty said: "Then whoever enjoys 'Umrah until the Hajj, then whatever offering is feasible." Abu 'Abdullah (ع) said: "A sheep, or more than that."

As for what one who intends the Hajj should do, Allah Almighty said: "Hajj is during well-known months, so whoever has made Hajj obligatory upon himself therein, let there be no lewdness nor wickedness nor quarreling in Hajj" [al-Baqarah: 197]. Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) said: The well-known months are Shawwal, Dhu al-Qi'dah, and Dhu al-Hijjah; Hajj is made obligatory in none but these. The making of Hajj obligatory upon oneself is the Talbiyah, the ish'ar, and the taqlid; whichever of these the one intending Hajj performs, he has made the Hajj obligatory upon himself. Lewdness is intercourse; wickedness is lying, cursing, and quarreling; and quarreling is a man's saying "No, by Allah" and "Yes, by Allah," and boasting.

'Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) said to Abu Khalid al-Wasiti, counseling him when he wished to perform Hajj: "O Abu Khalid, when you set out for Mecca, God willing, then hold to the fear of Allah, remember Him abundantly, and speak little except in good, for the completion of Hajj and 'Umrah lies in a man's guarding his tongue, as Allah Almighty said: 'No lewdness, no wickedness, no quarreling in Hajj.' Lewdness is intercourse; wickedness is lying, cursing, and quarreling; and quarreling is a man's saying 'No, by Allah' and 'Yes, by Allah,' and boasting. Hold to scrupulousness, which restrains you from disobedience to Allah, to forbearance, by which you master your anger, and to good companionship with those who travel with you. There is no power except by Allah."

Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said to a man he was counseling: "When you go out as a pilgrim, hold to the fear of Allah, to forbearance, to scrupulousness, and to good companionship with those who accompany you, for Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) said: 'No account is taken of one who makes for this House if he does not possess three qualities: scrupulousness that restrains him from disobedience to Allah, forbearance by which he masters his anger, and good companionship for those who travel with him.'"

And Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said to a man who wished to perform Hajj, counseling him: "When you depart from your home intending Hajj or 'Umrah, make the supplication of relief and say: There is no god but Allah, the Forbearing, the Generous; there is no god but Allah, the Most High, the Great; glory be to Allah, Lord of the seven heavens and Lord of the seven earths and Lord of the Throne; and praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds. O Allah, open before me in this journey of mine with good and seal it for me with good, and preserve me from every creature whose forelock You hold, for You, my Lord, are upon a straight path. In the name of Allah I have set out, in the name of Allah I enter, and for the sake of Allah I journey. O Allah, I place before me my forgetfulness and my haste. In the name of Allah and by the will of Allah, whatever of this journey of mine I remember or forget, O Allah, You are the One whose help is sought over all affairs, the Companion in travel and the Successor over family, wealth, and children. O Allah, make our journey easy for us, fold up the earth for us, and let us travel through it in obedience to You and obedience to Your Messenger. O Allah, set right our affairs for us, bless us in what You have provided us, and guard us from the torment of the Fire."

When you set out upon the road, there is a way to place yourself in Allah's keeping from the very first step. When you set your leg among the travelers, say: "In the name of Allah, and by Allah, and Allah is the Greatest." When you are settled in your journey, praise Allah seven times, glorify Him seven times, magnify Him seven times, and say "La ilaha ill Allah" seven times, and say: "Praise be to Allah who guided us to Islam and honored us with Muhammad, (ع) and his family. Glory be to the One who has subjected this to us, for we could not have subdued it ourselves, and indeed to our Lord we shall return. Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds. O Allah, You are the Carrier upon the back and the Helper in affairs. O Allah, grant us the best of news, and lead us to Your forgiveness and Your pleasure. O Allah, no bird flies but by Your leave, and no good comes but from You, and none else protects. O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the hardships of travel, from the distress of turning back, and from an ill return to family, wealth, and children. O Allah, I ask You in this journey of mine for piety, for contentment, and for deeds that please You. O Allah, cut short the distance for me and make it easy, and set aright my affairs and leave me well among my family." And when you return, say in your returning: "We are those who repent, who worship, who praise, returning to our Lord, giving thanks, devoted, and to Him we return."

As for entering the city of the Prophet (ص), the Commander of the Believers (ع) said in his sermon that the Messenger of Allah (ص) declared Madinah sacred between the mountains of 'Ayr and Thawr, and that whoever causes an unlawful act therein or shelters one who commits it has upon him the curse of Allah, the angels, and all people, and Allah will not accept from him any obligatory or supererogatory act. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that the two sides of Madinah are sanctified. When he was asked what its two sides were, he answered that it is whatever the free woman surrounded with her garment, which is to say what the Messenger of Allah (ص) prohibited: disturbing its inhabitants, hunting its game, and cutting its trees.

It is recommended to perform a ritual bath before entering the city, and to begin the visit at the Mosque of the Prophet (ص). One should stand at his grave, offer prayers upon him and his family, and testify to his prophethood and his message. One should pray whatever one is able, and then pray two rak'ahs before sitting at the pillar that stands forward on the side of the grave, from the corner of the grave, for that is the place nearest the head of the Prophet (ص). Then one should rise, approach the grave, and say: "I bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner; and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and messenger; and I testify that you are the Messenger of Allah, truly; and I testify that you are Muhammad ibn 'Abd Allah; and I testify that you conveyed the message of your Lord and gave sincere counsel to your nation, and that you strove in the way of Allah, and that you worshipped Allah until certainty came to you, and you fulfilled what was due upon you. This is the clear truth. May Allah reward you with the best of rewards. O Allah, bless Your servant and messenger Muhammad, as You have described him, as befits his faithfulness and the best of Your creation, and bless the family of Muhammad with the best, most complete, and most beautiful blessings, as You prayed upon one of Your prophets, Your purified ones, and the people of dignity, for You are Glorious and Generous. O Allah, bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad as You blessed Noah in the worlds; and bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad as You blessed Moses and Aaron; and bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad as You blessed Ibrahim and the family of Ibrahim, for You are the Praised, the Glorious. O Allah, whatever need I have of You, whether I have set about seeking it or have not, whether I have pronounced it or have not, whether I have known it in myself or have not, I ask You by the truth of Your messenger Muhammad and his blessed and noble grave, to graciously attend to all my needs, small and great."

He (ع) said that if one is able to remain in the city for three days, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday, then let him pray on Wednesday between the grave and the pulpit, at the pillar that adjoins the grave, and let him strive there and ask Allah his needs for this world and the hereafter. Let him pray likewise and supplicate on Thursday at the pillar of repentance, and pray likewise and supplicate on Friday at the shrine of the Prophet (ص), opposite the large pillar, and let him fast those three days, for in that there is merit.

The Messenger of Allah (ص) said, as narrated from Ali (ع): "Whoever visits my grave after my death is as one who migrated to me during my life. And whoever cannot visit my grave, let him convey peace upon me, for it will be reported to me." Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that it is desirable for whoever witnesses the city to also visit the Mosque of Quba', which is the mosque founded upon piety, and al-Fadikh Mosque, and al-Fath Mosque, and the Mosque of Umm Ibrahim, and to pray there, and to visit the grave of Hamza (ع) and the graves of the martyrs, may Allah have mercy upon them. The last act of the pilgrim in Madinah should be to visit the grave of the Prophet (ص) and bid him farewell, saying at it what he said at the first.

The Messenger of Allah (ص) fixed the sites of ihram at five stations. For the people of Madinah and those allied with them it is al-Shajarah Mosque; for the people of Syria and those beyond them, al-Juhfah; for the people of Yemen, Yalamlam; for the people of al-Ta'if, Qarn; and for the people of Najd, al-'Aqiq. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that these are the miqats of ihram for whoever wishes to perform Hajj or Umrah, or both together. To complete Hajj and Umrah, ihram must be assumed from the miqats the Messenger of Allah (ص) fixed. One must not pass them without entering ihram, nor enter ihram before reaching them. Whoever enters ihram before the miqat has departed from the Sunnah, and if he then spoils something of his ihram, nothing is required of him, because he was not truly in ihram, for one does not become a muhrim until he becomes one from the miqat. He said that whoever's home is closer to Mecca than the miqats should enter ihram from his home and is not obliged to travel to the miqat.

He (ع) also said, as part of the completion of Hajj and Umrah, that one should enter ihram in the vicinity of one's family. And he said that whoever fears the passing of the month for his Umrah may enter ihram before the miqat. So if a man departs in Rajab intending Umrah but knows he will not reach the miqat until Sha'ban, he must not leave off ihram until he reaches the miqat, and so his Umrah becomes a Sha'bani Umrah; but if he enters ihram in the remaining days of Rajab, before the miqat, his Umrah becomes a Rajabi Umrah, for the Rajabi is better, and that was what he intended.

He also said, concerning one who passed beyond the tree and did not enter ihram, that he should enter ihram from wherever lies between him and al-Juhfah. And he said that whoever comes to the miqat and forgets to enter ihram, or is ignorant of it, until he has passed it, or proceeds knowingly toward Mecca, is in a state that must be set right. If he is able to return to the miqat, let him return and enter ihram from it. If he fears missing the Hajj and cannot return, let him enter ihram from the place he has reached. And if he is already in Mecca, let him go out to the edge of the Haram and enter ihram from the unrestricted ground, then enter the Haram in a state of ihram; if he cannot, let him enter ihram from his place.

The Messenger of Allah (ص) commanded those who were with him during the Farewell Pilgrimage, when they reached al-Shajarah, to pluck the underarm hair, shave the pubic hair, and put off sewn garments, taking instead a waist-wrapper and a cloak. It is narrated from Abu 'Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that he said the muhrim should, along with that, use the tooth-stick, trim his nails, and take from his moustache, and that it does him no harm with which of these he begins. He should enter ihram directly after an obligatory or supererogatory prayer, and it is best for this to be after the sun has passed its zenith, though he is not confined to any hour, for ihram may be assumed by day or night, at any time when prayer is permitted. He said that the menstruating woman, upon reaching the miqat, should perform a ritual bath and enter ihram, and likewise the woman in post-natal bleeding. He said that the muhrim should not perfume himself before ihram with a scent whose fragrance will remain upon him after ihram. And if a man enters ihram without having prayed, nothing is upon him, though entering it after a prayer is better.

He (ع) said that when the muhrim wishes to enter ihram, he fixes his intention upon it and, following the prayer, utters what he binds himself to, whether Hajj, or Umrah, or Hajj alone, or Umrah alone. So he says: "O Allah, I intend to enjoy Umrah unto Hajj." Or he says: "O Allah, I intend to join Hajj to Umrah," if he has a sacrificial animal with him. Or he says: "O Allah, I intend Hajj," or "Umrah," whichever he wishes, and he mentions it upon Your Book and the Sunnah of Your Prophet: "O Allah, my place of release is wherever You detain me, by Your decree which You have decreed upon me. O Allah, help me in that and make it easy for me and accept it from me." Then he supplicates with whatever he wishes. And if he intends in his heart what he means to do, without pronouncing it, that suffices him and is valid.

He (ع) said that the best Hajj is tamattu', Umrah unto Hajj, which is the form upon which the Qur'an was revealed. When the Messenger of Allah (ص) performed the Farewell Hajj, he joined Hajj and Umrah because he had driven the sacrificial animal. When he reached Mecca, he circumambulated the House and performed sa'y between Safa and Marwah, and Allah, Mighty and Majestic, revealed to him what He revealed, and he said: "Had I foreseen from my affair what I have now seen, I would not have driven the sacrificial animal, and I would have made it a tamattu'." So whoever had no sacrificial animal with him was to release himself from ihram, and the people released themselves and made it an Umrah, except for those who had a sacrificial animal with them; then they entered ihram for Hajj from the Sacred Mosque on the Day of Tarwiyah. Thus enjoying Umrah unto Hajj is what the Messenger of Allah (ص) commanded, and this is as Allah, Mighty and Majestic, said: it is for whoever's family is not present at the Sacred Mosque. As for those who are of the people of the Haram, it is not for them to enjoy Umrah unto Hajj, because they are able to perform Umrah whenever they wish. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, made this concession only for those who come from other lands, granting them that they perform Umrah and Hajj in a single journey, as a widening from Him and a kindness toward them.

Whoever enjoys Umrah unto Hajj, or performs Umrah on its own, enters ihram, then comes to Mecca and circumambulates the House seven circuits and prays the two rak'ahs of the tawaf, then walks between al-Safa and al-Marwah seven circuits, beginning with al-Safa and ending with al-Marwah, has thereby completed his Umrah. Let him then release himself from his ihram. If he is performing tamattu' and intends the Hajj, he takes from the ends of his hair and his nails and remains disheveled, resembling the muhrim, until the Day of Tarwiyah, when he enters ihram for the Hajj from the Sacred Mosque, just as he entered ihram from the miqat. But if he is joining Hajj and Umrah and has driven the sacrificial animal, he does not release himself and remains in his ihram until he completes the Hajj, for Allah, Mighty and Majestic, says: ﴿وَلَا تَحْلِقُوا رُءُوسَكُمْ حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ الْهَدْيُ مَحِلَّهُ﴾, "And do not shave your heads until the sacrificial animal reaches its place of slaughter" [al-Baqarah: 196]. As for whoever performs Hajj alone and comes to Mecca, there is no obligatory tawaf upon him until he returns from Arafah; and if he performs a voluntary tawaf, that is good and has merit in it. And if he comes to Mina without entering Mecca, there is no harm upon him.

As for the marking of the offering and the raising of the voice, Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) said that in former times people used to garland camels, cattle, and sheep, then they left off garlanding cattle and sheep. He said that to garland is to tie a strap or a cord upon what is driven for Hajj and Umrah, and this is done after entering ihram at the miqat, and one thus dedicates it. The gashing is that a cut is made in the right side of its hump with a blade until its blood flows, so that it is known to be an offering. And it is recommended to hang upon its garland a sandal in which its owner has prayed, so that if it strays it is known to be an offering. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that the offering is gashed on the right side of its hump, and it is covered and led along.

When one who has entered ihram from al-Shajarah reaches al-Bayda', he raises his voice with the talbiyah and the announcement, saying: "لبيك اللهم لبيك، لبيك لا شريك لك لبيك، إن الحمد والنعمة لك والملك، لا شريك لك," Labbayk Allahumma labbayk, labbayk la sharika laka labbayk, inna al-hamda wa al-ni'mata laka wa al-mulk, la sharika lak. He repeats the talbiyah likewise whenever he ascends a height or descends into a valley, whenever he meets a rider, at the end of every obligatory prayer, when he wakes from sleep, and at the hours before dawn, whether in a state of purity or not, though purity is better; and the more he says the talbiyah the better. It is recommended for a man to raise his voice with the talbiyah, while women lower their voices, saying it only so far as they hear themselves. Whoever needs to ride his offering out of necessity may ride it, and to drink of its milk when in need may drink, so long as this does not harm the animal. If it is an offering under a vow or an expiation, and something befalls it, whether through the owner's fault or otherwise, it is a guaranteed obligation, and he must replace it; but if it is a voluntary offering, nothing is upon him. And whatever is a guaranteed offering, he does not eat from it when he slaughters it, but gives it all in charity; whereas what is voluntary, he may eat from it, and feed others, and give in charity.

As for what is forbidden to the muhrim and what falls upon him if he does it, Allah, Mighty and Majestic, said: ﴿الْحَجُّ أَشْهُرٌ مَعْلُومَاتٌ فَمَنْ فَرَضَ فِيهِنَّ الْحَجَّ فَلَا رَفَثَ وَلَا فُسُوقَ وَلَا جِدَالَ فِي الْحَجِّ﴾, "Hajj is in well-known months, and whoever has made the Hajj obligatory upon himself therein, there is no rafath and no transgression and no quarreling during the Hajj" [al-Baqarah: 197]. And it has already been said that the rafath is intercourse. He, Blessed and Exalted, said: ﴿وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا الصَّيْدَ وَأَنتُمْ حُرُمٌ﴾, "And do not kill game while you are in ihram" [al-Ma'idah: 95], and He whose remembrance is Majestic said: ﴿وَحُرِّمَ عَلَيْكُمْ صَيْدُ الْبَرِّ مَا دُمْتُمْ حُرُمًا﴾, "And forbidden to you is the game of the land as long as you are in ihram" [al-Ma'idah: 96]. And it has come from the People of the House (عليهم السلام) that the muhrim is barred from hunting, from intercourse, from perfume, and from wearing sewn garments.

The one who offers a sacrificial animal wounds it upon the right side of its hump with his iron blade until its blood flows, so that it may be known to be a sacrificial offering (hadi). It is recommended that he hang upon its collar a sandal in which its offerer has prayed, so that if it is found straying it is known to be a hadi. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: the sacrificial camels are marked on the right side of the hump, draped, and driven forward.

When the pilgrim reaches al-Bayda, having entered ihram from the tree, he raises the talbiyah and the ihlal, lifting his voice and saying: لبيك اللهم لبيك، لبيك لا شريك لك لبيك، إن الحمد والنعمة لك والملك، لا شريك لك. He renews the talbiyah in this way whenever he ascends a height, descends into a valley, or meets a rider, and at the close of every obligatory prayer, and when he wakes from sleep, and in the hours before dawn, whether he is in a state of purity or not. The more one calls the talbiyah, the better. It is recommended that a man raise his voice with it, and that women lower their voices, only so much that they may hear themselves.

Whoever needs to ride his sacrificial animal may ride it, and to drink of its milk, so long as this brings no harm to the animal. If the animal is harmed, whether through his use of it or otherwise, and it was pledged as a vow or as a compensation, it is a guaranteed offering, and he must furnish its replacement. If it was a voluntary offering, nothing is due from him. What is a guaranteed offering he does not eat from when he slaughters it, but gives it in charity; what was voluntary he may eat from, feed others with, and give in charity.

As to what is forbidden to the one in ihram and what he owes should he do it: Allah, Mighty and Majestic, said, ﴿الْحَجُّ أَشْهُرٌ مَعْلُومَاتٌ فَمَنْ فَرَضَ فِيهِنَّ الْحَجَّ فَلَا رَفَثَ وَلَا فُسُوقَ وَلَا جِدَالَ فِي الْحَجِّ﴾ [البقرة: ١٩٧], and it has already been said that the rafath is intercourse. He, Blessed and Exalted, said, ﴿وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا الصَّيْدَ وَأَنتُمْ حُرُمٌ﴾ [المائدة: ٩٥], and He, exalted be His mention, said, ﴿وَحُرِّمَ عَلَيْكُمْ صَيْدُ الْبَرِّ مَا دُمْتُمْ حُرُمًا﴾ [البقرة: ٩٦]. From the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) it has come that the one in ihram is forbidden from hunting, from intercourse, from perfume, from wearing sewn clothing, from removing hair, and from trimming the nails, along with other matters we shall mention in this chapter and in the one that follows it, and what is owed for each, if Allah the Exalted wills.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: whoever deliberately has intercourse with his wife while in ihram, knowing that this is not lawful for him, and reaches her, owes a sacrificial camel; and if he had not yet circled the House on the Day of Sacrifice, he must perform the Hajj again the following year. If the woman consented to him, knowing that this was not lawful for her, she too owes a sacrificial camel. If he compelled her, or came to her while she slept, nothing is due from her, and two camels are due from him. If both were ignorant that this was unlawful for them, nothing is due from either; but whichever of them knew must expiate and repeat the Hajj the following year. If he came to her other than through intercourse, he owes a camel and need not repeat the Hajj. If he did this in forgetfulness, nothing is due from him, and he is in the position of one who ate and drank in the daytime of Ramadan while forgetting.

He said: it is not lawful for the one in ihram to contract a marriage during his ihram, nor to conduct the marriage of another. If he does so, his marriage is void; and if he knew that this was unlawful, or the woman knew it, she is never afterward lawful for him. If both were ignorant, they are separated, and he may marry her once he leaves ihram, if he and she wish.

He said: if the one in ihram is intimate with his wife, or kisses her with desire, or deliberately looks at her with desire, or touches her with desire, and emits, he owes the blood of a sheep or more than that. But if this was without intent and without desire, and he emits, nothing is due from him.

He said: if the one in ihram disputes, saying "No, by Allah" and "Yes, by Allah," and is truthful in that, nothing is due from him, until he says it three times or more, whereupon he owes blood. But if he was lying, he owes blood for a single instance, and it increases as he adds to it.

He said: if the one in ihram shaves his head, he owes a sheep. If he removes some of the hair of his head or beard, he gives charity in proportion to that. If he merely wipes his head or beard and something slight falls from it, nothing is due from him. He said: if the one in ihram needs cupping, he may be cupped if he wishes; and if he shaves the place of the cupping-glasses, he gives a charity. If he clips all his nails, he owes blood; if he clips some of them, he gives charity in proportion to what he clipped.

The one in ihram was permitted, if he finds no sandals, to wear leather footwear cut below the ankles. He said: if the one in ihram fears for himself that he would be left uncovered, he may enter ihram in his clothing, and the recompense for that is the blood of a sheep, or charity, or fasting in proportion. The one in ihram may apply kohl if he needs it, but not with antimony, nor with kohl containing perfume; he may use the tooth-stick if he wishes, and may treat himself with anything lawful for him to eat, so long as it contains no perfume. It is disliked for the one in ihram to shade himself in the litter except from an ailment, and he is permitted this when he alights. If the one in ihram wears clothing, or does something forbidden to him, in ignorance or forgetfulness, nothing is due from him.

He said: there is no harm in entering ihram in a dyed garment, so long as it is not dyed with saffron, wars, or perfume; and white is better, and it is what is commanded, unless it cannot be found. He said: there is no harm in a woman in ihram wearing ornaments, so long as she does not display them to men. The one in ihram whom something harmful afflicts was permitted to remove it. He said: the man in ihram does not cover his head, nor the woman in ihram her face, though she may let a garment fall over her face without it clinging; and the man in ihram does not cover his ears. There is no harm, if he has a headache, in his binding his head, nor in his placing the waterskin upon it when he draws water. If he covers his head, or the woman in ihram covers her face, each of them gives a charity. There is no harm in the one in ihram washing, though it is disliked for him to plunge his head into the water.

As to the recompense for the game that the one in ihram takes: Allah, Mighty and Majestic, said, ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَقْتُلُوا الصَّيْدَ وَأَنتُمْ حُرُمٌ وَمَن قَتَلَهُ مِنكُم مُتَعَمِّدًا فَجَزَاءٌ مِّثْلُ مَا قَتَلَ مِنَ النَّعَمِ﴾ [المائدة: ٩٥]. Thus did the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) recite it, "one possessing justice among you," in the singular, and he is the Imam (ع) and whoever the Imam appoints. There is a long argument concerning this, which we have abridged in this book for the sake of brevity.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said, concerning the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, ﴿وَمَنْ عَادَ فَيَنتَقِمُ اللَّهُ مِنْهُ﴾ [المائدة: ٩٥]: whoever kills game while in ihram is judged to give its equivalent; and if he returns and kills another, he is not judged again, but Allah takes retribution from him, for the expiation is only for the first. He (ع) said: whoever takes game while in ihram owes its equivalent in livestock as an offering; if he is in Hajj, he slaughters the offering at Mina, and if he is in Umrah, then at Makkah. If he finds no animal, he gives its price in charity; and if he finds nothing to give in charity, he fasts. He said: if a sacrificial camel became due upon him and he could not find one, he feeds sixty poor persons; and if he finds nothing to feed with, he fasts eighteen days. Whoever owes a cow and cannot find one feeds thirty poor persons; and if he cannot, he fasts nine days. Whoever owes a sheep and cannot find one gives charity to ten poor persons; and if he cannot, he fasts three days. He (ع) said: whoever finds neither an offering nor the means to give charity should begin fasting; and if he then finds the means, he may continue his fast if he wishes, and it suffices him.

He (ع) said, concerning the one in ihram who takes an ostrich or a wild ass: for each of them a sacrificial camel; for the wild cow, a domestic cow; and for the gazelle, the dove, and their like among the birds, for each of these a sheep. If he takes the eggs of an ostrich, the stallion is set upon a number of she-camels equal to the eggs, and whatever they bring forth he offers, while for whatever is not brought forth nothing is due from him, for the eggs likewise include what does not come to term. If he breaks an egg and finds chicks within it, he must set the stallion upon the she-camels until they conceive and their burden is plain, and whatever they bring forth of that he offers, while for whatever miscarries nothing is due, for the chicks likewise include those that die before hatching. If he takes chicks that have quickened and in which the spirits have arisen, he sets the stallion upon a like number of she-camels until they conceive and their young stir in their wombs, and whatever they bring forth after that he offers, while for whatever miscarries or dies nothing is due from him, for the chicks in the egg, after the spirits have arisen in them, may still die. This principle concerning the eggs that the one in ihram takes has been transmitted only through the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام).

For an Arab came to 'Umar (رض) in his day and said to him: O Commander of the Faithful, I set out for Hajj and entered ihram, then I passed by a nesting-place, that is, the place where the ostrich gathers its eggs until it secures them, and I took eggs there and roasted and ate them; what do you see for me in that? He said: Go to your companions, and whatever they judge for you I will confirm. He pointed to a group of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah (ص) who were seated in the mosque. The man went to them and asked, and they differed over him. He returned to 'Umar and said: There is a man here whom, when we differed over a matter, you would have decide it; send me to him. He said: And who is he? He said: 'Ali ibn Abi Talib. The man went to him and found him with al-Hasan and al-Husayn (عليهما السلام) before him, and told him his tale and asked him the answer. He said to him: Ask these two, gesturing toward al-Hasan and al-Husayn (عليهما السلام). The Arab said: What is this I meet from you, O Companions of Muhammad? Every time I ask one of you, he sends me on to another, until you have turned me back to the children. And he rose to depart. Some of those present said: These two are the sons of the Messenger of Allah (ص); ask them, O Arab, for with them you will find what you seek. So he asked them, and they said: You set a stallion of the camels upon young she-camels equal in number to the eggs you took, and whatever is brought forth of that you offer. The Arab said to 'Ali (ع): Is it so? He said: Yes, O Arab. The man departed. 'Umar saw him and sent for him and asked him what had passed, and he told him. 'Umar said: Return to him and say that the young she-camels may miscarry, that is, cast their young before birth, or may not conceive. He returned and said this, and they said to him: And likewise the eggs may be addled, that is, spoil from their brooding so that no chicks come of them. He went back to 'Umar and told him, and 'Umar said: For this we were commanded to ask them. In another account, 'Umar asked them of this himself.

The explanation regarding the chicks that are struck, counted as living and dead, comes from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (عليهما السلام) upon the same principle that came from 'Ali (ع). He (ع) said, concerning the one in ihram who takes a wild cow, that he owes a domestic cow; and if he cannot find one, he feeds thirty poor persons; and if he cannot, he fasts nine days. And they said: concerning the fawn that the one in ihram takes, a sheep; and if he cannot provide a sheep, he feeds ten poor persons; and if he cannot, he fasts three days. The like binds everyone upon whom a sheep becomes due as a recompense for game, and we shall mention what is owed in that regard.

Whoever owes a recompense but can find neither the equivalent animal, nor the means to feed, nor the ability to fast, nothing further is due from him. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, says, ﴿لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا﴾ [البقرة: ٢٨٦], and He says, ﴿لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا مَا آتَاهَا﴾ [الطلاق: ٧]. Whoever has neither in his possession nor within his power what he is charged with owes nothing until he finds it or is able for it; and if the one upon whom this fell then attains something of what is due, he fulfills it, and if he becomes able to fast, he fasts, as we have said before.

They (عليهم السلام) said, concerning the one in ihram who takes a hyena, a fox, or a hare, that a sheep is due for each one, or what stands in its place of feeding or fasting if he cannot provide the sheep, as we have described. They (عليهم السلام) said that this was laid upon him as a penalty, that he might shun hunting. And they (عليهم السلام) held that for the dove and its like, such as the wood-pigeon, the turtle-dove, the partridge, and whatever resembles them among the birds, there is due for each a sheep, or what stands in its place of feeding and fasting if it cannot be found. If he takes the egg of any of these, he does with the sheep as the one who takes the ostrich's eggs does with the camels, as has already been said.

They (عليهم السلام) said, concerning the one in ihram who takes a monitor lizard, a jerboa, or the like, that a young kid is due for each. They (عليهم السلام) said, concerning the one in ihram who takes game in the unconsecrated land, that the recompense is as already described; but if he takes it within the Sacred Precinct, both that recompense and the value of the game he took are due, the value given in charity, by way of severity and emphasis and magnifying of the Precinct, that he hold as trifling nothing of what Allah, Mighty and Majestic, and His Messenger (ص) have forbidden.

They (عليهم السلام) said, concerning a single game animal taken by a company of people in ihram, that each of them severally bears the recompense, for each has transgressed in it. And they (عليهم السلام) forbade the one in ihram to direct another to game, or to point it out, or to aid him in it. They (عليهم السلام) permitted the one in ihram who is in dire need to eat of game, and it suffices him, and he does not eat carrion while he can find game.

They (عليهم السلام) said: if the one in ihram casts at game and wounds it, or breaks its foreleg or hindleg, and it then flees and is lost to him so that he does not know what became of it, the full recompense is due from him. And if he takes it and it is sound and he releases it, there is due from him for the breaking of its foreleg or hindleg a quarter of the recompense, and for its wounding a portion of the recompense corresponding to the portion of the blood-money the wound would bear, given in charity.

If the one in ihram slaughters the game as his recompense, he does not eat of it, nor does he feed any other from it, neither one in the unconsecrated state nor one in ihram; and if he does, he must give the recompense a second time. They (عليهم السلام) commanded that it be buried so that it not be eaten; and if it is dug up and eaten after he has buried it, nothing is due from him, so long as he did not order that or point it out. And this falls upon the one who took it.

They (عليهم السلام) said, concerning the one in ihram who kills game by mistake or in ignorance, that the full recompense is due from him, as from one who did it deliberately; and this is well-nigh the consensus of the Muslims, most of them holding it. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, says, ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَقْتُلُوا الصَّيْدَ وَأَنتُمْ حُرُمٌ وَمَن قَتَلَهُ مِنكُم مُتَعَمِّدًا فَجَزَاءٌ مِّثْلُ مَا قَتَلَ مِنَ النَّعَمِ﴾ [المائدة: ٩٥], and the Friends of Allah (عليهم السلام) know best the interpretation of His Book, Mighty and Majestic. Nothing in it, nor upon the tongue of the Messenger of Allah (ص), has made plain the lifting of the recompense from the one in ihram who kills game by mistake. As for the saying of the Messenger of Allah (ص), "Allah has lifted from my community their mistakes, their forgetfulness, and what they are compelled to," this does not enter into this meaning; he (ص) meant only that Allah has lifted from them the sin of that which they did not intend. For he (ص) made blood-money obligatory for mistaken killing.

The penalty owed for wounds inflicted upon a sane person stands upon its principle, and this same principle governs the one in iḥrām who kills game. The penalty is not waived for the person in iḥrām who accidentally kills game, for there is nothing in either the Book or the Sunnah to remove it from the one who committed it. Its basis is the words of Allah the Almighty: "O you who believe, Allah will test you with something of the game that your hands and your spears reach" [Al-Māʾidah: 94]. The verse addresses those in iḥrām, and whatever a person in iḥrām takes, whether by his hand or his weapon, falls within the matter Allah the Almighty has here set before them. What is established from the Imams (عليهم السلام) is binding because Allah the Almighty has joined obedience to them with obedience to Him and to His Messenger (ص); and what is established from the Messenger of Allah (ص) is a proof for whoever accepts it, as is the consensus of the Ummah. The full argument on this point is long, and what has been cited here suffices for one who consents to accept the truth, God willing.

They said (عليهم السلام) that if a person in iḥrām catches a fish in the sacred precinct, he must release it; and likewise, if a person in iḥrām acquires game that was taken from the ḥaram, he must return it to it. If a person in iḥrām kills small birds such as larks, sparrows, swifts, and their like, he owes a mudd of food for each one. For a locust, for whatever resembles or approaches it such as wasps and bees and the like, a handful of food is owed if it is killed deliberately. But whatever spreads upon him unbidden, or whatever his mount tramples without his intending it, carries nothing upon him. The same applies to the creeping things of the earth, such as beetles and their like, and likewise ants, corn weevils, flies, mosquitoes, ticks, and lice.

They permitted (عليهم السلام) the person in iḥrām to kill whatever harms or endangers him: harmful flies, scorpions, snakes, birds of prey, and the four-footed vermin, the mouse and the biting dog, and whatever belongs to that same kind and runs its course and enters its category. There is nothing upon the person in iḥrām if he kills these. They permitted him to drive off the crow and the kite, and they made lawful for the person in iḥrām the catch of the sea, as Allah the Almighty has plainly permitted it in His Book, so that he may take it, eat of it, and provision himself from it. They said (عليهم السلام) concerning the birds of the water and the marshes: whatever of them lays its eggs on land belongs to the game of the land, and whatever lays its eggs in the water belongs to the catch of the sea. They said that nothing counts among the game of birds except what takes independently to flight; as for domestic fowl and the like that dwell among buildings and houses, such as the Sindī hen and its kind, these are not game at all. And they said (عليهم السلام) that whoever incurs a penalty for game slaughters it at Minā if he is a ḥajj pilgrim, or at Makkah if he is performing ʿUmrah.

As for the ḥaram, its sanctity, its entry, and what is permitted within it: Allah the Almighty said, "Have they not seen that We have made a safe sanctuary?" [Al-ʿAnkabūt: 67]. It is reported from the Messenger of Allah (ص) that he forbade the game of the ḥaram to be disturbed or its trees to be cut. On the day of the conquest of Makkah he (ص) stood with his back to the House, holding the doorpost, while the people of Makkah and others thronged before him, and he proclaimed: "There is no god but Allah, alone, with no partner. He fulfilled His promise, granted victory to His servant, and defeated the confederates alone. What do you say, O people of Makkah, and what do you expect?" They answered: "We say good, and we think well: a noble brother and the son of a noble brother, and you have power." He said: "I say as my brother Yūsuf said: 'No blame upon you today; may Allah forgive you, and He is the most merciful of the merciful.' Yet Allah the Almighty made Makkah sacred on the day He created the heavens and the earth, and it remains forbidden until the Day of Resurrection. Its game shall not be disturbed, its trees shall not be cut, its thorny shrubs shall not be uprooted, and its lost property shall not be taken except by one who will announce it." Al-ʿAbbās said, "O Messenger of Allah, save for idhkhir, for it serves the graves and the houses." He meant that they would lay idhkhir over their dead in the graves as a guard against the earth and place it beneath them, and set it upon the roofs of their houses, and the goldsmiths would burn it as fuel. So the Messenger of Allah (ص) said, "Save for idhkhir." He spoke much beside this at that place, and by these words the sanctity of the ḥaram is defined and preserved.

He (ص) commanded that none of the ḥaram's trees be struck down and that its game not be scared off in defiance of its sanctity. He permitted from it only what is truly needed, such as a staff for the shepherd or what makes his bucket serviceable, along with fruits, and what enters upon people in their dwellings among the trees, and what the beasts and camels eat and graze with their mouths, and what people plant themselves, as against what the ḥaram itself brings forth. Whoever takes anything of that must give in charity its value.

It is recommended that the one entering the ḥaram perform a ritual bath before he enters it. The one joining ʿUmrah to Ḥajj cuts off the talbiyah when he beholds the houses of Makkah, and after that he calls out the takbīr and the tahlīl. In Ḥajj the talbiyah is cut off when the sun declines on the Day of ʿArafah, and in the separate ʿUmrah when he enters the ḥaram. When the ḥajj pilgrim, the ʿumrah performer, the qārin, or the mutamattiʿ enters Makkah, he first secures his baggage and safeguards his belongings if he has a mount and gear, then goes out before occupying himself with anything and begins with the Sacred Mosque. He comes to it with tranquility and dignity, holding in his heart the exaltation of the House. Best for him is to walk to it barefoot, though there is no harm if he wears sandals or rides. It is recommended that he enter by the Gate of Banī Shayba, which is the Gate of the Iraqis, and that he supplicate with whatever he is able and finds ready.

Among what has come as supplication at that moment, from Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (صلوات الله عليه), he (عليه السلام) commanded: "When you enter Makkah and set down your baggage, begin with the Sacred Mosque; and when you reach it, stand at the gate facing the Stone and say: In the name of Allah, and by Allah, and to Allah, and as Allah wills, and upon the creed of the Messenger of Allah (ص). The finest of names belong to Allah, and praise be to Allah, and peace be upon the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon Muḥammad ibn ʿAbdillāh, peace be upon you, O Prophet, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings. Peace be upon the prophets of Allah and His messengers. Peace be upon Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl. Peace be upon the messengers and upon the righteous servants of Allah. O Allah, send blessings upon Muḥammad and the family of Muḥammad, and have mercy upon Muḥammad and the family of Muḥammad, and bless Muḥammad and the family of Muḥammad, as You blessed and had mercy upon and blessed Ibrāhīm and the family of Ibrāhīm among the worlds; surely You are Praiseworthy, Glorious. O Allah, send blessings upon Muḥammad, Your servant and Your Messenger, and send blessings upon Ibrāhīm, Your intimate friend, and upon Your prophets and messengers. Open for me the gates of Your mercy, employ me in Your obedience and in what pleases You, and preserve me with the safekeeping of faith so long as You keep me alive. Exalted be the praise of Your Face. Praise be to Allah who has made me among His delegation and among the visitors of His House. Upon every place that is come to and every one who is visited there is a due for whoever comes and visits; and You, O Allah, are the best who is come to and the best who is visited. So I ask You by Allah, O Most Merciful, by that You are Allah, there is no god but You alone, with no partner, and that Muḥammad is Your servant and Messenger, and that You are One, Eternal, who did not beget and was not begotten, and to whom none is equal: O Bountiful, O Majestic, O Tender, O Generous, make Your gift to me, and the first thing You grant me, my release from the Fire. O Allah, free my neck from the Fire." He says this three times, then: "And widen for me lawful, wholesome provision, and ward off from me the evil of the devils of jinn and men, and the evil of the corrupt among Arab and non-Arab."

Then draw near the House, raise your hands, and say: "O Allah, I ask You, in this my standing, at the beginning of my rites, that You accept my repentance, that You pass over my offense, and that You lift from me my burden. Praise be to Allah who has brought me to His Sacred House." Then advance to the corner and kiss the Black Stone if you are able and it is within your reach, or touch it, which is to lay your hands upon it; and if you cannot come to that, point toward it with your hand. Then take up the ṭawāf.

Concerning the ṭawāf, Allah the Almighty said, "And let them circumambulate the Ancient House" [Al-Ḥajj: 29], and He, Blessed and Exalted, said, "Purify My House for those who circumambulate, those who abide there, and those who bow and prostrate" [Al-Baqarah: 125]. Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī (صلوات الله عليه) was asked about the origin of the ṭawāf, and he said that when Allah the Almighty created Ādam (عليه السلام), He commanded the angels to prostrate to him and favored him with the names He taught him, and they held themselves great in their souls and said, "We did not think that Allah the Almighty would create a creature more honored in His sight than we." And when He told them He would settle him upon the earth, they said, "Will You place therein one who will spread corruption in it and shed blood?" and so they objected to Allah the Almighty in this. He, Blessed and Exalted, said to them, "Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and I know what you reveal," meaning this speech they uttered, "and what you were concealing" [Al-Baqarah: 33], meaning what they had said within themselves and held great concerning the affair of Ādam (عليه السلام). They came to know that they had fallen into offense, so they took refuge at the Throne and circled it seven times, seeking their Lord's pleasure, and He was pleased with them and said, "Descend and build for Me a house upon the earth, that whoever of My servants disobeys Me may circle it, so that I may be pleased with them as I have been pleased with you." So they built a house at the place of the House. The first to circle it was Ādam (عليه السلام), and so it remained until it was raised up in the time of the Flood. Then Allah the Almighty revealed to Ibrāhīm, and he built the House upon its foundation.

ʿAbbād ibn Bakr al-Baṣrī said to Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (صلوات الله عليه), "O son of the Messenger of Allah, we have received a report from the Messenger of Allah (ص) that we wish to lay before you: if it is true in your sight we will accept it, and if it is false we will leave it." He said, "What is it?" He said, "It has reached us that he (ص) said: Whoever rises after the first part of the night has passed, completes his ablution, touches the Stone, testifies that there is no god but Allah and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah, then circles seven times and prays two rakʿahs in which he recites, after the Opening of the Book, 'Say, O disbelievers' and 'Say, He is Allah, One,' in whichever part of the mosque he wishes, then stands between the Stone and the door and praises Allah the Almighty as He deserves and sends blessings upon me and upon the people of my House, his feet shall not stir until he is granted his request, eight gates of Paradise are opened for him to enter by whichever he wishes, and Allah the Almighty writes for him three thousand good deeds, effaces from him three thousand evil deeds, and raises him three thousand degrees." Abū ʿAbdillāh (عليه السلام) said, "All of that is true and sound, and it is six thousand."

He (عليه السلام) said: When the one performing ṭawāf begins at the House and kisses the Black Stone, or touches it, or points to it, he takes up the circling, going to his right and keeping the House upon his left, and circles it seven times, beginning at the Black Corner and ending at it. When he sets out from it and circles the House until he returns to it, that is one circuit, reckoned as such, and so on until he completes seven circuits, moving at a brisk pace for three and walking for four. The brisk pace is a swift manner of walking, and thus did the Messenger of Allah (ص) do, and he commanded his companions to do so, and they did. For it had reached him that Quraysh said the companions of Muḥammad had grown weak and the fever of Yathrib had worn them down, so he wished to show them the hardiness and strength of his companions. Yet if the one performing ṭawāf leaves off the brisk pace in these three circuits, or in some of them, there is nothing upon him; and there is no brisk pace upon women.

In every ṭawāf of the House, the one performing it kisses the Black Corner and the Yemeni Corner, or touches them if he is able, or points to them if he cannot. He circles from behind the Ḥijr and does not enter the Ḥijr; if out of ignorance he enters it, he comes out and resumes his ṭawāf from behind it. He does not touch the two corners that adjoin the Ḥijr. Whoever is unable to circle walking is carried and circled while borne; circling barefoot is better, though there is no harm in circling in sandals. Reciting and supplication are recommended during the ṭawāf, and there is no harm in permitted speech during it.

Among what has been narrated from Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (صلوات الله عليه) as supplication in the ṭawāf, he commanded that when you circle the House and come opposite the waterspout, you say, "O Allah, free me from the Fire"; and when you come opposite the Black Stone, you say, "O Allah, grant me good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and guard me from the torment of the Fire." And when you are in the seventh of your circuits and reach the Stone, you cling to the House, pressing your belly and face against it, and say: "Praise be to Allah who has honored you, ennobled you, and magnified you, and made you a place of return for people and a sanctuary. O Allah, the House is Your House and the servant is Your servant, and this is the standing-place of one who seeks refuge with You from the Fire. O Allah, I am poor before You, fearful of You, and seeking Your protection. Do not alter my body and do not change my name. O Allah, I am weak, so strengthen my weakness in Your good pleasure, and multiply my deed and accept it from me. O Allah, grant me good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and guard us from the torment of the Fire." He (عليه السلام) said that there are angels there who say "Amen" to the supplication of the believer.

The one performing ṭawāf should not circle except while pure and in a state of ablution. If he performs the obligatory ṭawāf without ablution, he repeats the ṭawāf; and if he performs a voluntary ṭawāf without ablution, he makes ablution and prays the two rakʿahs of ṭawāf, and there is nothing upon him. If he breaks his ablution or his nose bleeds during the ṭawāf, he makes ablution, or washes his nose of the blood, and then builds upon his ṭawāf if it is voluntary; and if it is obligatory and he has passed the half or more, he builds upon his ṭawāf, but if it is the half or less, he begins the ṭawāf anew.

The obligatory ṭawāf for the mutamattiʿ and the qārin is the ṭawāf of ʿUmrah, performed first when he enters Makkah, then the ṭawāf of Ḥajj when he returns from Minā, which is also the ṭawāf of ifāḍah. With each of these there is a saʿy between al-Ṣafā and al-Marwah, whose manner we shall describe. Then, after the ṭawāf of ifāḍah and its saʿy, comes the ṭawāf of the women, which is a circling of the House alone. This is the obligatory ṭawāf, each ṭawāf of it being seven circuits. The ṭawāf of farewell upon departing from Makkah is recommended and encouraged, and beyond these there is voluntary ṭawāf, in which there is merit: whoever wishes may circle as much as he wishes, by night or by day, save that he circle no fewer than seven at a time.

He ((ع)) said that there are angels who say "Amen" to the prayer of the believer.

One should never perform tawaf except in a state of ritual purity, having made ablution. If a person performs the obligatory tawaf without ablution, he must repeat the circumambulation. If he circumambulates voluntarily without ablution, then there are no two rak'ahs of tawaf for him to pray and nothing further is required of him. Should he experience a discharge or break his ablution during the tawaf, he performs ablution and washes the affected place, then continues. If the tawaf was voluntary, he builds upon what he has already completed, even if the interruption came at the very beginning. If it was the obligatory tawaf, and he had already passed the halfway point, he builds upon it; but if he had completed only half or less, he begins the tawaf anew.

The obligatory tawaf is of several kinds. There is the tawaf of 'Umrah, performed upon first entering Mecca; then the tawaf of Hajj, performed when one returns from Mina, which is the tawaf al-Ifadah. Each of these is joined by a sa'i between al-Safa and al-Marwah, which we shall describe in its proper place. After the tawaf al-Ifadah and its sa'i comes the tawaf of women, a circumambulation of the house on its own. This is the obligatory circumambulation, and each part of it consists of seven rounds. The farewell tawaf, performed upon departing from Mecca, is highly recommended and much encouraged. Beyond these, all other circumambulation is voluntary and carries great reward; whoever wishes may circumambulate as much as he wishes by night or by day, provided he does not perform less than a full week of seven rounds.

He (ع) said: The menstruating woman and the woman in a state of lochia complete all the rites once they have washed, cleansed, and purified themselves, except for the tawaf; this she does not perform until she is pure. The woman with irregular bleeding may circumambulate and perform all the rites, so long as she is not in a confirmed state of menstruation. The menstruating woman may perform the sa'i between al-Safa and al-Marwah once she is purified, and she makes up the tawaf and the sa'i afterward. Women should hasten their tawaf and their visit if they fear the onset of menstruation.

The Imams (عليهم السلام) granted a concession to anyone wearied during the tawaf or the sa'i: he may rest and then build upon the tawaf and sa'i he has already completed. If a prescribed prayer is called while a person is in the midst of tawaf, he interrupts his circumambulation at the point he has reached, prays, and then resumes, building upon what has passed. He should not perform the obligatory tawaf hastily and without reason, nor should he begin it when he judges that a prayer time is about to enter, or while the imam is praying and he has not yet completed his tawaf and sa'i. Rather, he delays the tawaf until he has prayed the obligatory prayer, and then he circumambulates.

The Imams also permitted the interruption of a voluntary tawaf for some occasion of righteousness that presents itself. They narrated of al-Husayn ibn 'Ali (ع) that he was circumambulating the house voluntarily when a man came to him with a need. He broke off his tawaf and went with the man. When he was questioned about this, he said: How could I not go with him, when the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وآله) said, "Whoever walks with his believing brother in his need, even if the need is not fulfilled, has an 'Umrah recorded for him for accompanying him; and if the need is fulfilled, a Hajj and an 'Umrah are recorded for him." I went with this man in his need, and it was fulfilled, so I gained a Hajj and an 'Umrah, and then I returned to my tawaf.

They said (عليهم السلام): Whoever is overcome by drowsiness during the obligatory tawaf or its sa'i and cannot complete it builds upon what he has done if he has passed the halfway point, and begins again if he has not. If he sets it aside until he is rested and then completes it himself, there is nothing against him. If doubt arises during the tawaf, he builds upon what is certain. And if a person adds to his rounds, performing eight, he joins to that eighth round six more so that he has completed two full weeks; the first week is his obligatory tawaf and the second is voluntary. He then prays four rak'ahs, two for each week, and performs the sa'i between al-Safa and al-Marwah for seven rounds. If he wishes, he may perform another seven voluntarily, and if he wishes, he may leave them. One should not deliberately join two weeks together without separating them by two rak'ahs, except in the case of forgetfulness we have described.

When the pilgrim has completed a week of tawaf around the house, he draws back to the Maqam of Ibrahim, placing it between himself and the house, and prays two rak'ahs. For Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said, "And take from the Maqam of Ibrahim a place of prayer" [Al-Baqarah: 125]. No one should pray these rak'ahs except from behind the Maqam, keeping it between himself and the house.

Whoever performs the obligatory tawaf after the 'Asr prayer prays its two rak'ahs at that time. But if his tawaf was voluntary, he delays the two rak'ahs until the disliked hour has passed; likewise, if he performs a voluntary tawaf after the Fajr prayer, he does not pray its two rak'ahs until the time when voluntary prayer becomes permissible, as we mentioned in the chapters on prayer. If, however, the tawaf was obligatory, he prays its two rak'ahs the moment he finishes, at whatever hour that may be. It is recommended that in the two rak'ahs of tawaf, after the opening of the Book, he recite Surat al-Kafirun in the first rak'ah and Surat al-Ikhlas in the second. All of this is the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt (صلوات الله عليهم).

They said (عليهم السلام): After completing and praying the two rak'ahs of tawaf, he returns to the Black Stone and touches it in reverence, then goes to Zamzam. He drinks of its water and pours some over himself if he is able, then goes out through the gate of al-Safa. He praises Allah, declares His oneness and His greatness, and supplicates with whatever comes readily to him, while he faces the house in the direction of the Black Corner.

It is related from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (صلوات الله عليه), traced back to the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وسلم), that upon al-Safa during the Farewell Pilgrimage he said: "There is no god but Allah alone, who has no partner. To Him belongs the dominion and to Him belongs all praise. He gives life and causes death, while He Himself is ever-living and does not die. In His hand is all good, and He is capable of all things. There is no god but Allah alone. He has fulfilled His promise, aided His servant, and defeated the confederates alone." He said this three times. And Abu 'Abdillah (ع) would say after it: "O Allah, I ask You for pardon and well-being in this world and the hereafter. I ask You, O Lord, to send blessings upon Muhammad and his pure household, to strengthen me in acting upon what You have taught me of Your right, and to spread over me some of Your provision. I ask You, O Lord, for good in this world and good in the hereafter, and I ask You to shield me from the torment of the Fire."

He (ع) said: When you have finished your supplication upon al-Safa, descend and walk at a measured, tranquil pace. When you reach the bottom of the valley at the point marked by the first beacon, which is the flag marking the start of the striving, quicken your pace until you have risen from the belly of the valley and reached the second beacon, likewise a flag marking the end of the striving. It is for this that this rite is called the sa'i between al-Safa and al-Marwah. The first to perform the sa'i was Ibrahim (ع). When he descended from al-Safa and reached the belly of the valley, Iblis, may Allah curse him, blocked his way, and Ibrahim hastened as if fleeing from him until he took hold of the far edge of the valley on the other side, whereupon the accursed one turned away from him, and he walked on.

He (ع) said: Among what is recommended to say during the sa'i is: "In the name of Allah; Allah is the greatest; and may Allah send blessings upon Muhammad and his family. O Allah, forgive, have mercy, and overlook what You know, for You are the Most Mighty, the Most Generous." When he leaves the place of quickened pace and comes to where he walks at his ordinary pace, he raises his hands toward the heavens and says: "O Lord of grace, bounty, favor, blessings, and generosity, my deed is weak, so multiply it for me and accept it from me, and send blessings upon Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You are worthy of and as they are worthy of from You."

He (ع) said: There is no quickened pace upon women; they walk throughout. The quickened pace is for the men. He then ascends al-Marwah and says what he said upon al-Safa, returns to al-Safa and stands upon it likewise, and returns again to al-Marwah, until he has completed seven rounds, beginning with al-Safa and ending with al-Marwah, striving in each round and supplicating as described above. If he begins with al-Marwah before al-Safa, he discards that round and begins the sa'i afresh, starting with al-Safa and ending with al-Marwah. For when the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وآله) ascended al-Safa, he recited the verse, "Indeed al-Safa and al-Marwah are among the rites of Allah" [Al-Baqarah: 158], and then said, "Begin with that which Allah began with." This is how whoever circumambulates the house should perform the sa'i, joining the sa'i to the tawaf, as the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وآله) did. If he separates the two for a valid reason, there is no harm, as when he is wearied and rests, or is pressed for time, or is presented with something he cannot decline. But he should not delay it without excuse.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (صلوات الله عليه) was asked whether the sa'i between al-Safa and al-Marwah is obligatory, and he said, "Yes." He was asked whether the shortening of prayer while travelling is likewise obligatory, and he said, "Yes." It was said to him: "But Allah, Mighty and Majestic, said, 'And when you journey through the land, there is no blame upon you if you shorten the prayer, if you fear that those who disbelieve may afflict you' [al-Nisa: 101], and He said of Hajj and 'Umrah that there is no blame upon one who walks between them." He answered that Allah, Mighty and Majestic, made mention of this in His Book, and the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وآله) made it clear by his word and his deed and his command. He is the one whom Allah charged with the clarification, for He said, "And We have revealed to you the Reminder, that you may make clear to the people what has been sent down to them" [al-Nahl: 44], and He made it obligatory upon the people to accept from him, for He said, "And whatever the Messenger gives you, take it, and whatever he forbids you, abstain from it" [al-Hashr: 7].

In the saying of Allah, "Indeed al-Safa and al-Marwah are among the rites of Allah, so whoever makes Hajj to the house or performs 'Umrah, there is no blame upon him for walking between them" [al-Baqarah: 158], there is proof that the sa'i between them is obligatory. For had its omission been a matter left to choice, He would have said, "there is no blame upon him if he does not walk between them." Rather, the words "there is no blame upon him for walking between them" came only after some had held that there was blame in it, so Allah made clear that it is among His rites which He has ordained, and that there is no sin upon one who performs it. For it is said that the Ansar used to enter the state of consecration for the idol Manat, which stood level with Qudayd, and so they were reluctant to walk between al-Safa and al-Marwah. Allah informed them that there was no blame upon them in it, and that the two are among His rites. In the same way, when the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وآله) commanded the shortening of prayer and the breaking of the fast while travelling, some of the Muslims found it weighty and burdensome to do, and Allah informed them that there was no blame upon them in it. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (صلوات الله عليه) said: Whoever deliberately abandons the sa'i must perform the Hajj again in the following year; and whoever forgets it appoints someone to make it up on his behalf, just as one who forgets the two rak'ahs of tawaf appoints someone to make them up.

Concerning the one who joins 'Umrah to Hajj in the manner of tamattu':

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (صلوات الله عليه) spoke of the one who performs tamattu', joining 'Umrah to Hajj, meaning whoever brings the two together in a single journey, enters ihram for both at once, and consecrates himself for them together, as we mentioned in the chapter on ihram. He (ع) said: When the one performing tamattu' has circumambulated the house and made the sa'i between al-Safa and al-Marwah, intending by them the tawaf and sa'i of the 'Umrah, he has fulfilled it. He should then release himself from ihram, taking something from his hair and his nails, and keeping the rest for the Hajj. So too the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وسلم) commanded in the Farewell Pilgrimage: those with him who had not driven a sacrificial offering released themselves from ihram once they had performed the tawaf and sa'i and became fully unbound. But he himself (صلى الله عليه وآله), having driven the offering, did not release himself, and he joined the 'Umrah to the Hajj, in keeping with the word of Allah, "And do not shave your heads until the offering reaches its place of sacrifice" [al-Baqarah: 196], meaning until the offering is slaughtered. It was at that time that Allah sent down the command of tamattu', and the Prophet said, "Had I foreseen from the start of my affair what I now perceive at its end, I would not have driven the offering, and I would have made it a tamattu'." He urged the people toward it, for they had not known this; they knew 'Umrah only as a rite performed on its own.

The Commander of the Believers, 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (صلوات الله عليه), arrived that day from Yemen, having driven an offering. He found Fatimah (عليها السلام), who was among those performing Hajj with the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وآله), wearing dyed garments and released from her ihram, and this was held against her. But the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وسلم) had commanded her to do so. He came to the Prophet and informed him of it, and the Prophet said, "Yes." Then he said, "And you, O 'Ali, for what did you enter ihram?" 'Ali said, "I said: O Allah, I enter ihram for that which Your Messenger has entered ihram." The Prophet said, "Then do not release yourself until you have sacrificed the offering; perform the tawaf and the sa'i for your 'Umrah, and remain as you are in your ihram until you are released from the Hajj." And 'Ali shared with him (صلى الله عليه وآله) in his offering; what he had brought from Yemen was combined with what the Messenger of Allah had driven, and he was made a partner in the whole of it.

Abu 'Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (صلوات الله عليه) was asked how the one performing tamattu' should deal with the shaving of the head. He said: He does not shave his head with the razor, but shortens it or takes from its edges, keeping some for his Hajj. It was asked: And if he does shave? He said: If he shaves and his hair has not grown back between then and the day of sacrifice, he owes a blood offering, that is, a sheep which he slaughters and whose meat he gives in charity. He said: The razor is passed over his head on the day of sacrifice whether the hair has grown back or not; and likewise the bald man, who has no hair upon his head, passes the razor over it in place of shaving.

The pilgrim who performs tamattu' owes his attention first to the ordering of his release. If he forgets to shorten his hair until he has already entered ihram for Hajj, and only then remembers, he should seek Allah's forgiveness, and nothing further is required of him. Once he has been released from his tamattu', he may travel between that release and his entry into ihram for Hajj as he wishes; but he should not perform any voluntary tawaf after the tawaf of tamattu' until he has dissolved his ihram and shortened his hair.

When he has completed his 'Umrah, he should neither wear the ihram garments again nor make himself resemble those still in ihram. The people of Makkah, likewise, should hold themselves in a manner befitting the days of Hajj, and if they do not perform Hajj they remain in their ordinary state. If the one in ihram comes to his wife before he has shortened his hair, he owes a camel; if he kisses her, he owes a sheep; and once he has shortened, he may come to her if he wishes and nothing is upon him, for what was forbidden to him during his ihram has become lawful upon his release from it. If the one performing tamattu' arrives in Makkah on the day of Tarwiyah, he performs the tawaf of his 'Umrah and the sa'i, walks between the two hills, and then enters ihram for Hajj on that same day.

As for the woman: permission is given to her, if she fears that menstruation will keep her from the tawaf and sa'i of Hajj, to perform them before she sets out for Hajj. And if, having joined 'Umrah to Hajj in tamattu', she menstruates before she can perform the tawaf of 'Umrah, she delays it until she becomes pure, and then performs it before the tawaf of Hajj.

He said: there is no tamattu' for the people of Makkah. Allah Almighty permitted it only for those who come from distant lands, and this is the meaning of His saying: ﴿ذَلِكَ لِمَنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ أَهْلُهُ حَاضِرِي الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ﴾ "That is for one whose family is not present at the Sacred Mosque." [al-Baqarah: 196]. This is because the people of Makkah may perform 'Umrah whenever they wish. And he said: whoever enters the Sacred Precinct and performs 'Umrah in one of the months of Hajj, then remains there until he performs Hajj, that man has performed tamattu'.

The one who performs tamattu', joining 'Umrah to Hajj, owes what Allah Almighty named: ﴿فَمَا اسْتَيْسَرَ مِنَ الْهَدْيِ﴾ "then whatever offering can be readily obtained." [al-Baqarah: 196], which is a sheep or something greater. He slaughters it during the days of Mina and distributes its meat in charity, eating none of it himself. If he cannot find a sheep, he takes a share in a camel, and the required portion of the camel will be explained in the section on the hady. If he can find neither, then he fasts, as Allah Almighty commanded, three days during the Hajj: one day before Tarwiyah, the day of Tarwiyah, and the day of 'Arafah; and seven days once he has returned to his family. If he fasts those seven on the road home, that suffices him. If he fasted neither the three days during the Hajj nor on the way back, whether out of ignorance or forgetfulness, then he lets them go and fasts ten days upon returning to his family. To fast them continuously is better, but if he separates them, that too suffices.

When the day of Tarwiyah comes, the one performing tamattu' enters ihram for Hajj from the Sacred Mosque, just as a pilgrim enters ihram from the miqat. The people of Makkah enter ihram from their own houses rather than from the miqat outside the city; and whoever resides in Makkah without being of its people enters ihram, likewise, from the place where he resides.

### Going out to Mina and the standing at 'Arafah

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said to one of those whom he was teaching the rites, among those performing tamattu': on the day of Tarwiyah, Allah willing, bathe, put on your garments, and enter the Sacred Mosque barefoot, with serenity and dignity. Perform, if you wish, seven rounds of voluntary tawaf upon your arrival, and pray the two rak'ahs of your tawaf. When the sun has passed its zenith, pray the obligatory prayer, then enter ihram for Hajj and do just as you did at Dhu al-Hulayfah. Then go out to Mina.

He said: the people should leave Makkah for Mina on the day of Tarwiyah, from the beginning of the day until its end. It was named the day of Tarwiyah because the people would water their animals on it, for there was no water in Mina at that time. It is the eighth day of Dhu al-Hijjah. When the people went out from Makkah toward Mina and 'Arafah, intending the Hajj, whoever wished among them rode and whoever wished walked; and for one who could find no mount, there is reward in walking.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: for one who is able, riding is better than walking, because the Messenger of Allah (may Allah bless him and his family) went out riding. Jabir ibn 'Abdullah (رض) said: "I looked out that day upon the company before the Messenger of Allah, people on foot and people mounted, as far as the eye could reach, and on his right and his left and behind him the like of that, between rider and walker." When they reached Kura' al-Ghamim, the pedestrians drew themselves up in a line, seeking the blessing of his supplication. As he passed by them they said, "O Messenger of Allah, the journey has grown long upon us and the walking has become hard for us." He said, "May Allah reward you," and then said, "If you were to help yourselves with a light quick pace, your bodies would grow lighter and you would cover the ground the sooner." They did so, and it eased them. So the rider is stronger for the standing at 'Arafah and for supplication than the man on foot, and for that reason riding is recommended for the one who finds a mount and is able.

He said: when the season is upon them, the imam should not pray the noon prayer on the day of Tarwiyah except at Mina, and he passes the night there, and the people pass the night with him. Then he goes out, and the people go out with him, from Mina to 'Arafah on the morning of the day of 'Arafah, after the sun has risen. He said: it is recommended to bathe on that day, and to break one's fast, for anyone who fears that fasting would weaken him for the standing and the supplication at 'Arafah. When they come to 'Arafah they descend there, and it is recommended to descend and to stand near the mountain. To descend and stand at the place of al-Idrak is forbidden; Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that those who descend beneath it and stand there have no valid Hajj. All the rest of 'Arafah is a valid standing-place.

He said: when the sun passes its zenith, the assembly gathers. The imam comes to the floor of the valley and addresses the people; then the muezzin calls the adhan and gives the iqamah, and the imam leads them in the noon prayer. Then the muezzins give the iqamah again, and he leads them in the afternoon prayer, the people praying with him, combining the two prayers with a single adhan and two iqamahs. So did the Messenger of Allah (may Allah bless him and his family). Then he stands, and the people stand with him, and they remain standing, their faces turned toward the sun, calling upon Allah Almighty, yearning toward Him, and asking Him for their needs of this world and the next, until the sun sets. Whoever wishes recites the Qur'an, and whoever wishes glorifies Allah, declares His oneness, exalts Him, and seeks His forgiveness.

It is narrated of Muhammad ibn 'Ali ibn al-Husayn (ع) that when he stood at 'Arafah he would begin Surah al-Baqarah, and after every ten verses he would glorify Allah, declare His oneness, exalt Him, seek forgiveness, and supplicate; then he would begin the next ten and do likewise, until he had completed the surah.

It is narrated of Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that he said: when you stand at the standing-place of 'Arafah, praise Allah and extol Him, and pray upon the Prophet Muhammad and his family, then say:

"O Allah, I ask You by Your might, by Your strength, by Your generosity, by Your bounty, by Your grace, and by all that comes from You, O most Hearing of those who hear, most Seeing of those who see, O Swift in reckoning, O Ruler of rulers, O Most Merciful of the merciful, that You send blessings upon Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, and that You do with me what is worthy of You." Then raise your hand and your face toward the sky and say: "O Allah, my need is such that if You grant it nothing You withhold will harm me, and if You withhold it nothing You grant will profit me. I ask You for the deliverance of my neck from the Fire. O Allah, I am Your servant, my forelock is in Your hand, and my term is within Your knowledge. I ask You to grant me success in what pleases You with me, and to make me one whose deed You are pleased with and whose striving You accept." Then say: "Praise be to Allah for His forbearance after His knowledge; praise be to Allah for His pardon after His power; praise be to Allah for His mercy that outstrips His wrath; and praise be to Allah with every praise that is His, for all His favors," until you conclude, "praise be to Allah in the measure that our Lord loves and is pleased with; praise be to Allah for His favors, which can neither be numbered nor ever repaid. O Allah, I ask You by Your beautiful names, by Your strength, Your power, and Your might, by all that Your knowledge encompasses, by the right of Your Messenger, and by Your Greatest Name, the most Great, by which whoever calls upon You, it is binding upon You to answer him and not to turn him back, that You grant him his request, that You forgive me all my sins and all that You know of me."

Then ask Allah for your needs of this world and the next, and say often: "O Allah, I ask You for the deliverance of my neck from the Fire." Repeat this supplication and others, whatever is made easy for you and opened to you, and multiply your exaltation, your declaration of His oneness, and your seeking of forgiveness, until the sun sets. When it sets, say: "O Allah, do not make this the last of my covenants with this standing-place, and open it to me forever as long as You keep me alive. Turn me back from it triumphant and successful, my supplication answered, my sin forgiven, returning with the best return of any of Your delegation who came to You and any of the pilgrims of Your Sacred House. Make me today among the most honored of Your delegation before You, and grant me the best of what You have granted any of them of good and blessing, of mercy and pleasure and well-being. Bless me in that to which I return, of family and wealth, of little and much, and bless them in me. O Allah, expand for us Your lawful and wholesome provision, and do not plot against me nor draw me on by degrees, and turn away from me the evil of the corrupt among jinn and mankind, and send blessings upon Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You are worthy and as they are worthy of You."

He (ع) said: whoever falls ill and loses consciousness is to be stood at the standing-place of 'Arafah. And he said: the standing at 'Arafah is not valid except in a state of purity. It is narrated from the Messenger of Allah (may Allah bless him and his family) that he said: "The gravest in sin of the people of 'Arafah is he who departs from it believing that he has not been forgiven." Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) explained: he means the one who despairs of Allah's mercy. And Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) said one day, having stood at 'Arafah: "Allah is a Lord of generosity. By Allah, not one who pours forth from this standing-place this evening, neither the righteous nor the corrupt, departs without a share from Allah. As for the righteous, he is answered for his Hereafter and his world; and as for the corrupt, he is given his portion and his lot of what he asked for of this world." And al-Husayn ibn 'Ali (ع) saw a man begging from the people on the day of 'Arafah, and said, rebuking him: "Woe to you. Do you ask of any but Allah on this day, when even those in the wombs are hoped for on this day?" And Abu Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) would not turn away any beggar on the day of 'Arafah. And Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) would increase greatly on that day in supplication, prayer, charity, and the seeking of forgiveness.

### The departure from 'Arafah to Muzdalifah

Allah Almighty said: "Then pour forth from where the people pour forth." Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: the Quraysh used to pour forth from Muzdalifah in the age of ignorance, saying, "We are more entitled to the House than the rest of the people." So Allah Almighty commanded them to pour forth from 'Arafat, from where the people pour forth; for before that they had not been standing at 'Arafat, but only at the Sacred Precinct, and they were commanded to stand at 'Arafat and to pour forth from it.

One does not depart from 'Arafah until the sun has set and its disc has disappeared. When that has happened, the people pour forth toward Muzdalifah, and they do not pray Maghrib until they have reached it, combining there the Maghrib and the later 'Isha prayer with the imam, with a single adhan and two iqamahs. They pass the night at Muzdalifah, the night of the Day of Sacrifice, and the best place is that which is near the Sacred Precinct. Whoever pours forth from 'Arafah before the sun sets owes a camel, which he slaughters, or else he gives its price in charity. And when he pours forth he should increase in the remembrance of Allah Almighty and in seeking forgiveness, for Allah Almighty says: ﴿ثُمَّ أَفِيضُوا مِنْ حَيْثُ أَفَاضَ النَّاسُ وَاسْتَغْفِرُوا اللَّهَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ﴾ "Then pour forth from where the people pour forth, and seek Allah's forgiveness; indeed Allah is Forgiving, Merciful." [al-Baqarah: 199].

He should be measured in his going. When the Messenger of Allah (may Allah bless him and his family) departed from 'Arafah in the Farewell Pilgrimage, riding upon his she-camel al-Qaswa, he drew in her rein until her head nearly touched his foot, and he was saying, gesturing with his right hand, "O people, tranquility, tranquility." And whenever he came upon a hill among the hills he would loosen her rein a little that she might climb, until he reached Muzdalifah, where he combined the two evening prayers.

Abu 'Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about praying the two evening prayers short of Muzdalifah, and he said, "No," even if a third of the night should pass; and whoever does so deliberately owes a sacrifice. And he said: to pass the night at Muzdalifah on the night of the Day of Sacrifice is of the Sunnah. Whoever deliberately does not pass the night there, knowing it to be of the Sunnah, owes a camel, unless he returns within what remains of the night and stands there. But whoever left it out of ignorance, and it has already passed, having stood at Muzdalifah even for a moment, owes nothing. It is recommended for the imam to stand in Muzdalifah at al-Quzah, which is the mountain upon which the standing is made; and all of Muzdalifah is a valid standing-place. The Messenger of Allah (may Allah bless him and his family) said: "All of 'Arafah is a standing-place, and all of Mina is a place of sacrifice." He also granted a dispensation to the women and the weak to pour forth from Muzdalifah by night, to cast the jamarat by night, and to pray Fajr at Mina; but the imam and the rest of the people do not depart from Muzdalifah until they have prayed Fajr and the daylight has fully broken.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: the Messenger of Allah (may Allah bless him and his family) stood at Muzdalifah until the sun shone upon Thabir, which is the first mountain in the world upon which the sun rises. When he saw it he said, "Shine, O Thabir; shine, O Thabir. I say as my brother Musa said: 'My Lord, expand for me my breast, and make my affair easy for me, and untie the knot from my tongue, that they may understand my speech; and appoint for me a helper from my family, 'Ali my brother; strengthen my back through him, and let him share in my affair.'"

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: when you stand at the Sacred Precinct, praise Allah and extol Him, and recite of the verses of the Qur'an what you are able, then pray upon the Prophet and his family, then supplicate for yourself as much as you are able. For the Messenger of Allah (may Allah bless him and his family), when he had prayed Fajr on the Day of Sacrifice at Muzdalifah, mounted al-Qaswa until he came to the Sacred Precinct, where he ascended and faced the qibla, and exalted Allah, declared His oneness, and proclaimed Him alone, and did not cease standing thus until the daylight had fully broken. Then he poured forth from Muzdalifah toward Mina until he came to Muhassir, the valley between Muzdalifah and Mina, where he quickened his pace a little and took the middle road that brings one out at the great jamrah.

From Muzdalifah the Messenger of Allah (ص) came down toward Minā, and when he reached Muḥassir, the valley that lies between Muzdalifah and Minā, he quickened his pace a little and took the middle road that brings the pilgrim out at the Great Jamrah. He went on until he reached the jamrah beside the tree and pelted it with seven pebbles. Then he came to Minā, offered his sacrifice, and shaved his head. Hastening through Muḥassir is a Sunnah, and one should hasten through it as the Messenger of Allah (ص) did. On that day the Jamrat al-ʿAqabah is thrown before the sacrifice; then one slaughters and shaves, and there is no harm in whichever of these he begins with.

Jaʿfar ibn Muhammad (ع) said: the pebbles for the jamarāt may be taken from Muzdalifah or from other places lying toward Minā. Whoever is at Minā should not carry pebbles from one jamrah to another; rather, the pebbles for all the jamarāt are taken from a single place. It is recommended that they be gathered up one by one, dark and speckled, about the size of a fingertip, and it is disliked to break them off from larger stones. It is recommended to perform the ritual washing before throwing them. The one who throws should keep Minā on his right and the House on his left when he means to throw, and should throw from the floor of the valley, not from above it. Abu Jaʿfar ibn ʿAli (ع) said: when you throw the jamarāt, pronounce the takbīr with each pebble you cast.

Jaʿfar ibn Muhammad (ع) said: whoever throws the Jamrat al-ʿAqabah on the Day of Sacrifice before slaughtering, then comes to Minā and slaughters and shaves, then proceeds to the House and circumambulates it seven times and performs the saʿy between al-Ṣafā and al-Marwah seven times as well, and this is the circumambulation of the Ḥajj, then returns and performs the circumambulation of the women, when he has done all of this, everything becomes lawful to him that iḥrām had forbidden to the muḥrim by reason of his consecration. He then returns to Minā and remains there through the days of Tashrīq, throwing the jamarāt each day after the sun has passed its zenith. Each jamrah is pelted with seven pebbles, beginning with the lesser, then the middle, then the greater. He has latitude to throw at any hour of the day, though it is preferable that he be in a state of purity. The Messenger of Allah (ص) used to walk to the jamarāt when he threw them.

ʿAli (ع) threw the jamarāt both on foot and mounted, and there is no fixed prescription in this. The Messenger of Allah (ص) granted the herdsmen a dispensation to throw the jamarāt by night, and whoever misses throwing them by day may throw them by night. Whoever places the pebbles of one jamrah upon another should repeat the throwing, beginning with the lesser jamrah and ending with the greater, which is the Jamrat al-ʿAqabah. The jamarāt are thrown on behalf of the sick, and whoever misses any part of the throwing makes it up when he remembers, or appoints someone to make it up for him. Whoever hastens his departure within two days leaves the pebbles that remain with him and does not throw them at the jamarāt after he has departed.

As for the sacrificial offering, Allah Almighty said: "They are the ones who disbelieved and turned you away from the Sacred Mosque, and the sacrificial offerings from reaching their place of slaughter" [al-Fatḥ: 25]. And He, Blessed and Exalted, said: "Do not shave your heads until the sacrificial offering reaches its place of slaughter" [al-Baqarah: 196]. The place of slaughter for the offering brought in a single Ḥajj, or in a Ḥajj joined to an ʿUmrah, is Minā; the place of slaughter for the offering brought in a single ʿUmrah is Mecca; and each is slaughtered in its own place. When the Messenger of Allah (ص) brought forward his offering he slaughtered it at the place of slaughter in Minā and said: "This is the place of sacrifice, and all of Minā is a place of sacrifice." So the people slaughtered at their own encampments. It is preferable that a man attend to the slaughter of his offering and his sacrificial animal himself, or slaughter it with his own hand; and if he is unable, let his hand be together with the hand of the one who slaughters; and if he is unable even for that, let him stand over it while the butcher acts in his stead. He should entrust this only to a Muslim.

The Messenger of Allah (ص) made ʿAli (ع) a partner in his offering. All the offerings that the Messenger of Allah (ص) brought, and those that ʿAli (ع) brought from Yemen, amounted to one hundred camels. The Messenger of Allah (ص) slaughtered sixty-three of them with his own hand, bound and standing, drawing near to him one after another, and he commanded ʿAli (ع), who slaughtered the rest. Then he ordered that a portion from each be placed in a pot and cooked; and the Messenger of Allah (ص) and ʿAli (ع) ate of that flesh and sipped of its broth, so that they might partake of every one of them, for Allah has said: "when their flanks fall, eat of them."

When ʿAli (ع) slaughtered his offering or sacrificed his devoted animal, he would say: "I have turned my face toward the One who created the heavens and the earth, as a sincere Muslim, and I am not among the polytheists. Truly my prayer, my devotion, my life, and my death are for Allah, the Lord of the worlds; no partner has He. Thus have I been commanded, and I am among the Muslims. O Allah, this is from You and to You. In the name of Allah." Then he took the knife to it. The naming of Allah suffices for that, and its omission is not permitted except for one who forgets; when he remembers, he names Allah. Whoever cannot find an offering to bring on his own should share with others in whatever he is able and can manage, and the more he does of this, the better.

The best of offerings is the she-camel, then the male camel, then the female of the cattle, for it was narrated from ʿAli (ع) that the female of the cattle is preferable to the male; and the name "badanah" applies to both camels and cattle. Then come the males of the cattle, then the ram of the sheep, which is better than the mūjā, which is the one whose testicles have been bound or crushed; and the mūjā is better than the muṣṭalim, which is the one whose testicles have been cut away; and this is better than the ewe; and the ewe is better than the ram of the goats; and the ram of the goats is better than the female of the goats. None of all these suffices except the mature animal or the thanī, save the sheep, of which the yearling suffices. Best of these is the mulḥ, whose whiteness is mingled with black in places, so that its muzzle is black, as are the sockets about its eyes, the extremities of its fore and hind legs, and the tip of its tail; and it was said that this is the description of the ram that was sent down to Ibrāhīm (ع).

Among offerings and sacrificial animals the plump is preferred, and the lean is disliked unless a man did not intend it, having bought it fat only for it to grow thin in his keeping. They forbade the animal whose horn is wholly broken; and if only the outer part is broken, it is called aqṣam. They directed that the eye and the ear be inspected carefully, meaning (عليهم السلام) that one look to this so that there be no defect. They forbade the animal whose udder teats, that is, the nipples of the udder, have been severed; and likewise the mangy, the aged, and the one whose ear has been cut off; and they disapproved of the muqābalah, whose ear has been cut at the front, and the mudābarah, whose ear has been cut at the back, and the sharqāʾ, whose ear has been split in two, and the kharqāʾ, in whose ear there is a round hole. As for a defect that befalls the animal after a man has bought it and dedicated it as an offering, nothing is required of him, and it suffices him. Its dedication is effected by marking it or garlanding it.

The marking of camels and cattle is that they be pierced in the hump on the right side with a piece of iron until their blood flows over it, so that the animal may be known as an offering should it stray. The ox is garlanded, and the sheep too when it is offered. The camel is draped with a covering cloth, which is given in charity when it is slaughtered. If the camel is disabled before it reaches its destination, it is slaughtered, a sandal is dipped in its blood and it is garlanded with it, and it is left so that whoever passes by of those for whom eating the offering is lawful may know that it is an offering and cut from it and eat, if he wishes. Of these offerings, what is voluntary its owner may eat from and feed to whomever he wishes; but what is obligatory, which is that due for a vow, or as recompense for game hunted, or for spoiling some part of the Ḥajj, of that he eats nothing. And the offering may not be sold, whether it is obligatory or otherwise.

Whoever's offering is disabled before it reaches its destination and who then finds no replacement bears nothing further; and if it strays, he buys another in a different place. If the offering he buys is the obligatory one, that is, if he has marked or garlanded it, he slaughters both together; if he has not dedicated it, then he has a choice in the matter. If a man's offering is misled together with the one who bought it, its owner may take it if he wishes, and it suffices for him but does not suffice for the one who slaughtered it; and he may claim its price, if he wishes, from the one who sold it to him. Whoever finds a stray offering should announce it, and if he finds no one who seeks it, he slaughters it on the last of the days of Tashrīq on behalf of its owner. Whoever has his offering slaughtered and then has part of it stolen, it suffices for him. They (عليهم السلام) made obligatory the presentation of the offering, which is that a man take it from Minā to ʿArafah and stand with it there, and at Muzdalifah, and at all the ritual stations, and then slaughter it. Whoever slaughters a voluntary offering may eat of it, give charity from it, and feed of it whatever he wishes; and there is no fixed time for this.

Abu ʿAbd Allah Jaʿfar ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about the words of Allah Almighty, "So eat of it and feed the poor and the wretched," and His saying, "eat of it and feed the contented and the beggar who importunes." He said: the poor man is the one who does not beg of people, and the wretched is in a harsher state than he and more sorely pressed. The contented is the one who is satisfied with what is given to him, who does not grow angry, nor cry out, nor twist his mouth in vexation. The beggar who importunes is the one who passes by presenting himself. Abu Jaʿfar Muhammad ibn ʿAli (ع) used to test the people in this so that the contented might be known from others: when a beggar came to a stop before him, he ordered that the head of the sacrificial animal be given to him. If he took it and passed on rejoicing, not vexed, he ordered that he be given of the meat as well; but if he was vexed and left it and went his way, he knew that he was not of the contented.

They disliked (عليهم السلام) that anything of the offerings be taken out of Minā, except a small thing to make use of, such as some of the hump for medicine, or something of the hide, the wool, the fur, the sinew, or the hair, for such as one has need of on a journey. They favored that the hides of the offerings and sacrificial animals be given in charity, though they permitted their use, and that the butcher be given some of them and of the covering cloth. They forbade the offering of the jallālah, which is the animal that feeds upon dung and filth, and the eating of its flesh, until it has been cleansed by fodder and confined for a known period; and likewise its milk is not to be drawn until it has been cleared: the she-camel and the camel forty days, cattle twenty days, sheep seven days, ducks five days, and chickens three days. They forbade riding the offering in a way that would harm or weaken it, and loading burdens upon it, and putting it to work in the same manner; but there is no harm in what neither weakens it nor injures it. Whoever forgets to slaughter at Minā slaughters at Mecca when he remembers. The days of Minā are three following the Day of Sacrifice. The day after the Day of Sacrifice is the first of them, and it is called the Day of Qarr, because one settles at Minā upon it and does not depart, nor leave Minā upon it except to visit the House and circumambulate. The second is the Day of the First Departure, as Allah Almighty said: "Whoever hastens within two days, there is no sin upon him," meaning the hastening of the departure from Minā. The third is the last of the two days of departure.

As for the shaving and the shortening, Allah Almighty said: "Allah has fulfilled His Messenger's vision in truth: you shall surely enter the Sacred Mosque, if Allah wills, in safety, with heads shaved and hair shortened" [al-Fatḥ: 27]. Shaving is what is done with the razor, and shortening is what is taken with scissors from the ends of the hair, leaving some of it. It has been narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that shaving is better than shortening, and that the Messenger of Allah (ص) shaved in the Farewell Pilgrimage, and some of the people shaved and some of them shortened. The Messenger of Allah (ص) said, "O Allah, have mercy upon those who shave." One who heard him said, "And those who shorten, O Messenger of Allah?" He said, "O Allah, have mercy upon those who shave and those who shorten." They ordered (عليهم السلام) that with the shaving one take also from the sides of the beard and the mustache and pare the nails, and that the razor reach to the two prominent bones beneath the temples level with the ears. We have mentioned that the bald man and the one whose head is close-shorn each passes the razor over his head. The woman, when she comes out of iḥrām, cuts something from the ends of her hair and does not shave her head or any part of it.

Whoever forgets to shave, or does not know of it, until he has departed from Minā should shave wherever he recalls or learns of it; and if he is near to Minā he returns to it and shaves there; and if he shaves after he has drawn far from it, he sends his hair to Minā and has it cast there, if he is able. They ordered (عليهم السلام) that whatever falls of the hair be buried. It has been narrated from ʿAli (ع) that he said, "Bury this hair, for everything that falls from the son of Adam while he is alive is dead matter that must be buried." ʿAli ibn al-Husayn (ع) used to bury his hair in a hollow at Minā when he shaved, saying at that time, "O Allah, grant me for every hair a light on the Day of Resurrection."

As for what the pilgrim does at Minā, Allah Almighty said: "Then let them complete their rites and fulfill their vows and circumambulate the Ancient House" [al-Ḥajj: 29]. It has been narrated from ʿAli (ع) that he said: the completing of the rites is the throwing, the shaving, the vows, the walking, and the sacrifices; and the circumambulation of the Ancient House is the Circumambulation of the Visit, which is the Circumambulation of the Overflowing, the Circumambulation of the Ḥajj, of which I have already spoken in the chapter on Circumambulation. It falls on the Day of Sacrifice: the people visit the House after they have shaved and slaughtered at Minā, then return to the House and circumambulate it, then return to Minā, having become free of everything from which their consecration had bound them, as I have mentioned; and it matters not with which he begins, whether with shaving or with slaughtering. It is recommended thereafter to hasten the Circumambulation of the Overflowing on the Day of Sacrifice for the one performing tamattuʿ, while they permitted the qārin and the one performing Ḥajj alone to delay it. There is no harm in throwing the jamarāt before shaving and slaughtering, or after them; but one does not make the visit before he has shaved, slaughtered, and thrown, unless he does so forgetfully or in ignorance, in which case nothing is required of him, and he shaves, slaughters, and throws when he turns back. And he does not spend the nights of Minā away from Minā; whoever spends a night away from it deliberately owes a blood offering for each night he spends away, and whoever does so in ignorance or forgetfulness owes nothing, and Allah forgives them.

It is related that the Messenger of Allah (ص) shortened his prayer in Mina and commanded that Allah the Almighty be remembered abundantly during the days spent there. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) commented on the words of Allah the Almighty, ﴿فَإِذَا قَضَيْتُم مَّنَاسِكَكُمْ فَاذْكُرُوا اللَّهَ كَذِكْرِكُمْ آبَاءَكُمْ أَوْ أَشَدَّ ذِكْرًا﴾ "When you have fulfilled your rituals, remember Allah as you remember your fathers, or with even greater remembrance" [Al-Baqarah: 200]. He said that when the polytheists would settle in Mina, they would boast of their fathers and recount the glories of their ancestors, and so Allah the Almighty commanded the Muslims to put in the place of that the remembrance of the Almighty.

They held it disapproved (عليهم السلام) to fast on the day of the sacrifice and the days of Tashreeq. They said that when the pilgrim throws the Jamarat al-'Aqaba al-Kubra on the day of sacrifice, he is released from everything that was forbidden to him in the state of ihram, except hunting, women, dwelling outside of Mina, and perfume. When he then performs the circumambulation of the House and the sa'y between al-Safa and al-Marwah, everything that was forbidden to him in ihram becomes permissible, except women; and once he performs the Tawaf al-Nisa', everything becomes permissible to him. They said that the takbir during the days of Mina is to be said after every obligatory prayer, the worshipper saying it after the taslim and before he rises: "Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar, la ilaha ill-Allah, wa Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar wa lillahi al-hamd. Allahu Akbar for what He has guided us to, and Allahu Akbar for what He has provided us of the grazing livestock." He may visit the House each day of Mina if he wishes and perform voluntary circumambulation as much as he wishes during the day, then return and spend the night in Mina.

Concerning the departure from Mina, Allah the Almighty said, ﴿وَاذْكُرُواْ اللَّهَ فِى أَيَّامٍ مَعْدُودَاتٍ فَمَن تَعَجَّلَ فِى يَوْمَيْنِ فَلاَ إِثْمَ عَلَيْهِ وَمَن تَأَخَّرَ فَلا إِثْمَ عَلَيْهِ لِمَنِ اتَّقَى﴾ "And remember Allah during the appointed days. Whoever hastens in two days, there is no sin upon him, and whoever delays, there is no sin upon him, for the one who fears Allah" [Al-Baqarah: 203]. Abu 'Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that this refers to the stay in Mina: if you wish to remain, you have done well, and if you wish to hasten your departure in two days, you may do so, as Allah the Almighty has said. Whoever hastens his departure on the second day of Tashreeq, which is the third day after the day of sacrifice, should not depart until he has prayed the noon prayer and thrown the jamarat; thereafter he may depart if he wishes, between then and sunset. If the sun sets upon him, he stays the night, and then belongs to the people of the third day and may depart whenever he wishes, from the beginning of the day to its end, after he has prayed Fajr and thrown the jamarat. It is mentioned that one should send his baggage ahead toward Makkah before the departure. It is recommended for whoever departs from Mina to descend at al-Muhassab, which is al-Bat-ha', and to tarry there a little before continuing on. So the Messenger of Allah (ص) did: when he came to al-Muhassab he descended, remained for an hour, and then set out again.

It was said that whoever is among the first to depart may remain in Makkah until the people have shaved. When the people move on to Makkah, whoever desires to enter the House should enter it if he is able, and whoever does not enter owes nothing. Abu 'Abd Allah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: If you are able to enter the House, enter it whenever you wish; but if you fear the crowd, do not endanger yourself. And he mentioned the account of a people who perished in the crush within it.

He said that it is recommended for whoever wishes to enter the Kaaba to perform a ritual bath. Upon entering, after praising Allah as He deserves and sending prayers upon the Prophet and his family, one says: "O Allah, You have said, and whoever enters it is safe, so make me safe from Your punishment, and cleanse my heart of arrogance, hypocrisy, ostentation, and doubt regarding Your religion, and secure me from my fear of the Fire on the Day of Resurrection. O Allah, whoever has arrayed himself, or equipped himself, or made ready, or prepared for a journey to some created being in hope of his gift and his bounty, to You, my Master, has been my arraying, my equipping, and my preparation, in hope of Your gift and Your bounty. So do not disappoint my hope today, and do not turn me away today in disappointment from Your House. You have made it sacred, and You are the most generous of those whose house is visited. O Most Great, there is no god but You."

He said that whoever wishes to pray voluntarily inside the House should do so, and it is recommended to pray between the two pillars, upon the red marble, for there is no spot on earth better than it, and upon it the Messenger of Allah (ص) prayed. He then rose and faced the back of the House in his prayer, then stood and faced each corner of the House, supplicating at it, before returning to the red marble to pray upon it. He said that one does not pray an obligatory prayer inside the Kaaba, nor is it permissible to pray upon its roof.

He said (ع) that whoever intends to depart from Makkah should make his final act with it a circumambulation of the House, performing seven rounds, then bidding it farewell and placing his hand between the Black Stone and the Door, saying: "O Allah, I am Your servant, and the son of Your servant, and the son of Your handmaid. You carried me upon Your mount and conveyed me through Your land until You brought me into Your sanctuary. O Allah, this is Your House, and I have come to it, and I have hoped, in the goodness of my thought of You, that You would forgive me my sins. So if You have forgiven me, then increase Your pleasure toward me and grant me nearness with You; and if You have not yet forgiven me, then from this hour forgive me, before I depart from Your House. This is the hour of my departure, if You have granted me leave, without turning away from You, nor from Your House, nor exchanging You or Your House for any other. So guard me on my right and on my left, behind me and before me, until You bring me to my family; and when You have brought me to them, do not forsake me, and take me unto Yourself upon that upon which You have taken Your righteous servants, for You are more worthy of that than Your creation and than I." Then he clings to the House and says: "O Allah, do not make this the last of my covenant with Your House." When he has gone out, he stands at the door of the mosque, turns his face toward the House, fills his eyes with the sight of it, raises his hands toward the sky, and says: "O Allah, send me back triumphant, prospering, shown mercy, and forgiven. O Allah, if You cause me to die, forgive me; and if You grant me life, provide me a Hajj in the coming year, and do not make this the last of my covenant with Your Sacred House."

He said (ع) that it is recommended for one who intends to leave Makkah to buy dates for dirhams, or whatever he is able, and to give them in charity, intending thereby to atone for whatever befell him during his ihram and his Hajj of neglect or shortcoming. And he said (ع) that one departing from Hajj should not hurry his travel as though he were turning away from what he dislikes toward what he loves; rather, let him depart with tranquility upon him. And Ali (ع) would not accept the testimony of one who races ahead of the pilgrims. One day he went out to al-Rahbah and saw a gathering. He asked what this was, and was told it was one who had raced ahead of the pilgrims. He said: "No welcome and no greeting. You have shortened the prayer and wearied the mount at noon."

As for the individual 'Umrah, Allah the Almighty said, ﴿وَأَتِمُّوا الْحَجَّ وَالْعُمْرَةَ لِلّهِ﴾ "And complete Hajj and 'Umrah for Allah" [Al-Baqarah: 196]. The Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام), and those of the reciters who agreed with them, read 'Umrah in the accusative, giving it the meaning of an obligation: complete the Hajj and the 'Umrah. They said that it is obligatory. Some of those who differed from them read "and 'Umrah for Allah" in the nominative, as though they cut off the speech after His words "and complete the Hajj," and began anew, so that the obligation fell upon what was for Allah the Almighty. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that 'Umrah is an obligation in the same rank as Hajj, because Allah the Almighty says, "Complete the Hajj and the 'Umrah." It was narrated from Ali (ع) that he said 'Umrah is obligatory. And it was narrated from al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that he said: 'Umrah to 'Umrah is an expiation for what lies between them. The Messenger of Allah (ص) said: An 'Umrah in the month of Ramadan equals a Hajj. For the individual 'Umrah, one who performs it alone enters ihram from the miqat, avoids what the one in ihram avoids, and forms the intention. When he comes to the House he circumambulates it seven times, performs the sa'y between al-Safa and al-Marwah, prays the two rak'ahs of the circumambulation, and is released; this is the 'Umrah. If he has driven a sacrificial animal, he slaughters it at Makkah. Al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: Perform 'Umrah in whichever of the months you wish, and the most excellent of that is an 'Umrah in the month of Rajab. If you perform 'Umrah in the month of Hajj and then depart, it is an individual 'Umrah; but if you then perform Hajj in that same year, you are one performing Qiran.

Concerning repulsion and siege, Allah the Almighty said, ﴿هُمُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا وَصَدُّوكُمْ عَنِ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ وَالْهَدْىَ مَعْكُوفًا أَن يَبْلُغَ مَحِلَّهُ﴾ "It is they who disbelieved and barred you from the Sacred Mosque, and the offering, prevented from reaching its place" [Al-Fath: 25]. And He, Blessed and Exalted, said, ﴿فَإِنْ أُحْصِرْتُمْ فَمَا اسْتَيْسَرَ مِنَ الْهَدْيِ﴾ "If you are prevented, then whatever offering is feasible" [Al-Baqarah: 196]. Al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that repulsion is prevention, and siege is illness. He said (ع) that the Messenger of Allah (ص) set out from Medina in the year of al-Hudaybiyyah with more than a thousand of his companions, intending 'Umrah. He entered ihram from Dhu al-Hulayfa, and they entered ihram, and he drove the sacrificial animals, collared them, and marked them. Word of this reached the Quraysh, and this was before the conquest of Makkah, and they gathered their forces against him and went out to bar his way. When he was near 'Asfan, their news came to him. He said: We have not come to fight anyone; we have come only as pilgrims of 'Umrah. If the Quraysh wish, I will make a truce with them for a term and leave the way open between me and the people; then, if they wish to enter into that which the people have entered, they may enter, and if they refuse, I will fight them until Allah judges between us, and He is the best of judges. The emissaries went back and forth between him and the Quraysh, and there was in that a long account, the end of which was that he (ص) made a truce with them for a term, on the terms that he would turn back that year and perform 'Umrah, if he wished, the following year, along with whoever wished to accompany him. He then slaughtered the sacrificial animals he had driven where he stood, and they cut their hair short, and he turned back, and the Muslims with him turned back.

This is the ruling of one who is turned back from the House, and what he must do when he has made the Hajj or the 'Umrah, or both together, binding upon himself, which is only when he has passed the miqat. As for one turned back before reaching the miqat, he simply turns away, whether he entered ihram or not; and if he had driven a sacrificial animal, he turns back if he wishes and does with it as he pleases, because he had not yet made it binding, since, as mentioned before, the offering is made binding by its marking and its collaring, and that is only after entering ihram from the miqat. Whoever enters ihram from short of the miqat is not in a valid ihram and has done wrong and departed from the Sunnah.

As for siege, it is illness, as al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) explained in the words of Allah the Almighty. He (ع) was asked about a man beset by illness who had with him an offering he had sent ahead with his companions, appointing them a set time: if he is in Hajj, the place of slaughtering the offering is on the Day of Sacrifice; and if he is in 'Umrah, let him reckon the measure of his companions' arrival in Makkah and the hour he has appointed for them, and when that hour comes, he cuts his hair short and is released. This, again, is only when he had entered ihram from the miqat and then fell ill afterward. But if he had not yet reached the miqat, he may turn back if he wishes or remain, because the obligation of Hajj had not yet become binding upon him. It was narrated that al-Husayn ibn Ali (ع) set out from Medina intending 'Umrah, and when he came to al-Suqya he fell ill, and news of him reached Ali (ع), who was in Medina. He came to him and said: My son, what do you complain of? He said: I complain of my head. So Ali (ع) called for a camel and slaughtered it on his behalf, shaved his head, and returned him to Medina; and when he recovered, he went out and performed 'Umrah. Likewise, whoever is besieged by illness after entering ihram for Hajj, or intending 'Umrah, or both together, is obliged to shed blood, as Allah the Almighty said, ﴿فَإِنْ أُحْصِرْتُمْ فَمَا اسْتَيْسَرَ مِنَ الْهَدْيِ﴾ "If you are prevented, then whatever offering is feasible" [Al-Baqarah: 196], and that is a sheep or more; then he turns back. When he recovers from his ailment, he goes out and makes up what he was besieged from of Hajj, or 'Umrah, or both together.

The one besieged by illness does not approach women until he completes what he was besieged from, while the one merely turned back may approach women and is not obliged to make up what he was turned back from. This is entirely the word of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام). Al-Sadiq Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about one who was besieged and then recovered, whether women became lawful for him. He said: No, not until he circumambulates the House. He was asked: Then why did women become lawful for the Messenger of Allah (ص) when he returned from al-Hudaybiyyah, though he had not circumambulated the House? He said: The two are not alike. For the one turned back, that is lawful; but for the one besieged, it is not.

Concerning Hajj on behalf of the incapacitated and the deceased, a woman from Khath'am came to the Messenger of Allah (ص) and said: O Messenger of Allah, my father is an old man, grown too feeble to move, and the obligation of Hajj has come upon him. Shall I perform Hajj on his behalf? The Messenger of Allah (ص) said: Yes, do so, for were there a debt upon your father which you discharged, would that not suffice for him? Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about the like of this and permitted it, citing the report of the Khath'ami woman. So the aged man and the aged woman who have despaired of being able to perform Hajj may each equip someone to perform Hajj on their behalf, and it suffices. But one who is able to make the journey is not discharged by another; he must go out and perform Hajj himself, whether he is able in ease or not, and even if he fears an enemy, because he is in the state of one who may hope for that fear to pass and for the way to Hajj to open, and there is no fixed time appointed for Hajj.

Hajj is an obligation once in a lifetime. As long as a person still hopes to be able to perform it, the obligation remains upon him; and if he finds a way to it at last and yet delays, he has wronged himself.

Hajj may be performed on behalf of one who has died, whether the deceased bequeathed it or his guardian volunteers to equip someone to perform it for him. If the deceased bequeathed that Hajj be performed on his behalf from the third of his estate, it is taken from that third. If he did not specify the third and had not yet performed his obligatory Hajj, it is taken from the whole of his estate, and any further bequests he made after that are paid from the third.

Whoever is engaged to perform Hajj on behalf of another, and finds that the wage exceeds his expenses, may keep the surplus, and the Hajj is credited both to him and to the one on whose behalf he performs it. One who has not yet performed Hajj for himself is only a hired man in this; if he has already performed his own Hajj, that is better. A man may perform Hajj on behalf of a man, and a woman on behalf of a woman. If a woman performs Hajj on behalf of a man, it suffices him when he can find no other, or when she is more capable than the men available to him and more exact in performing the rites. Whoever performs Hajj on behalf of another should strive to complete the Hajj in full and to discharge all its rites.

It was narrated of Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that, after his son Ismail (ع) died, he hired a man to perform Hajj on his behalf, instructed him in what to do during the Hajj, and stipulated its conditions upon him. Then he said to him: "Know that if you fulfil what we have stipulated of you, it will count as a Hajj for the one on whose behalf you have performed it, and you will have a reward for keeping the condition laid upon you and for the labour of your body." And the Messenger of Allah (ص) said that Allah, mighty and majestic, admits three into Paradise on account of a single Hajj: the one who bequeaths that Hajj be performed for him, the one who carries out his bequest after him, and the pilgrim who performs it on his behalf, if he strives and fulfils the duty upon him.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) said that there is no harm if one who performs Hajj for another for a wage should add, on his own behalf as a volunteer, whatever circumambulation or 'Umrah he wishes, once he has discharged what he was engaged to do. He also said (ع): whoever performs Hajj on behalf of another, let him say from the moment of his ihram, "اللهم إني أريد الحج عن فلان فتقبّل منه وأجرني على قضائي عنه" — O God, I intend the Hajj on behalf of so-and-so; accept it from him and reward me for discharging it on his behalf. Let him hold this intention and pronounce it at every station, at every sacred site and landmark, and in every act among the acts of Hajj, whether obligatory or recommended.

**Missed Hajj and 'Umrah**

Whoever performs the Hajj of tamattu', joining 'Umrah to Hajj, or the Hajj of qiran, and does not reach Mecca until a time when he fears that performing the circumambulation and the running of his 'Umrah would cause him to miss the standing, let him hasten instead to the standing so that his Hajj may be completed, make it a single Hajj, and perform the 'Umrah afresh afterward.

Whoever comes upon the people after they have left Mina and are standing at Arafat, and reaches the standing at Arafat before the sun sets, has attained the Hajj, even if he did not pass the night at Mina. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: likewise, if he reaches Arafat after the people have already poured forth from it, stands there through the night and remembers Allah, then comes to Jam' before the people pour forth from Muzdalifah, he has attained the Hajj. But if he does not reach Muzdalifah until after the people have poured forth from it and the sun has risen, he has missed the Hajj.

One who has missed the Hajj should convert his ihram into 'Umrah, and the Hajj remains due from him for a future occasion, unless at the time of his ihram he stipulated that his release would be wherever he is prevented, in which case no Hajj is due from him for the missed occasion. Even then, Hajj remains upon him if he is one who has not yet performed his obligatory Hajj though he was able to find the way. Likewise, if he does not catch the people until after the days of Hajj have passed, let him circumambulate the House and make it a single 'Umrah, if he had been performing qiran or tamattu'. But if he had entered ihram for Hajj alone, he owes no 'Umrah, because he neither entered ihram for 'Umrah nor intended it, unless he returns to the miqat and enters ihram for it there.

**The Vow of Hajj**

Allah, mighty and majestic, said: "عَيْنًا يَشْرَبُ بِهَا عِبَادُ اللَّهِ يُفَجِّرُونَهَا تَفْجِيرًا ۝ يُوفُونَ بِالنَّذْرِ وَيَخَافُونَ يَوْمًا كَانَ شَرُّهُ مُسْتَطِيرًا" — A fountain from which the servants of Allah drink, making it gush forth abundantly; they fulfil their vows and fear a Day whose evil is far-reaching [Al-Insan: 6-7]. We shall set out in the chapters on vows what of them is binding and what is not, God willing.

Whoever makes a binding vow to perform Hajj must fulfil it; and if he had not yet performed his obligatory Hajj, it suffices him in place of the Hajj of Islam. Abu Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that if he intends this and pronounces it, it is credited to him. Likewise, if he performs Hajj on behalf of another while he had also vowed to perform Hajj, that fulfils his vow and is credited to him.

It was narrated of Ali (ع) that he said: "Whoever vows to perform Hajj and is then unable to do so, let him send his sacrificial animal and enter ihram, remaining in the state of ihram among his household until it is sacrificed on his behalf." And Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said of one who makes a binding vow to walk to the Sacred House of Allah: let him walk; and when he grows weary and can no longer walk, let him ride and offer a sacrificial animal, for Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity. When he has given the full measure of his effort, nothing further is upon him. A binding vow is one made in obedience; but whoever vows to commit a sin has nothing binding upon him.
