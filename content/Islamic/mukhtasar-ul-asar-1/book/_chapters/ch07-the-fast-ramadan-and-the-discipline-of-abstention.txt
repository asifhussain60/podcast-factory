# The Fast: Ramadan and the Discipline of Abstention

Fasting in the month of Ramadan is a duty of its own, and it is fitting to set out what the fast is, what is desired in it, how it begins, what breaks it, and how it stands during travel and illness.

**On fasting and what is desired in it**

Allah Almighty said: "O you who believe, fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may become God-fearing, for an appointed number of days," continuing to His words, "so that you may complete the number and glorify Allah for having guided you, that perhaps you will be grateful." [al-Baqarah]

By this Allah Almighty made fasting the month of Ramadan an obligation, every year, upon every person of legal responsibility. I have mentioned in the chapters on prayer what boys are commanded regarding prayer and fasting, and in what circumstances they are commanded to it.

It is narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that when the month of Ramadan drew near he would supplicate, saying: "O Allah, the blessed month of Ramadan has come. If You bring us to it, help us through it and accept it from us." And when it entered he would say in his supplication: "O Allah, this is the month of Ramadan in which the Quran was sent down as guidance for the people, and as clear signs of guidance and the Criterion. O Allah, bless us in the month of Ramadan, help us through it, and accept it from us, for You are capable of all things."

It is narrated from his father (ع) that he said: "Do not say 'Ramadan' alone, but say as Allah Almighty said, 'the month of Ramadan.'" And from his forefathers, that the Messenger of Allah (ص) said: "Sha'ban is my month, and Ramadan is the month of Allah, and it is the springtime of the poor." By this he pointed to its excellence. All months belong to Allah Almighty, yet the month of Ramadan is attributed to Him in honor, and his saying "Sha'ban is my month" is likewise from the perspective of honoring it.

The Prophet (ص) used to fast. He ascended the pulpit and said: "O people, Jibril came to me and said, 'O Muhammad, whoever reaches the month of Ramadan and is not forgiven in it, then dies, may Allah keep him far away. Say Amin.'" So I said, "Amin." And he (ص) preached to the people on the last day of Sha'ban and said: "O people, a great and blessed month has cast its shade over you, a month in which one night is better than a thousand months. Whoever draws near to Allah in it through any quality of goodness is as one who performs an obligatory duty in another month, and whoever performs an obligatory duty in it is as one who performs seventy obligatory duties in another month. It is the month of patience, and the reward of patience is Paradise. It is the month of consolation, a month in which the sustenance of the believer is increased. Whoever provides a fasting person with what breaks his fast earns forgiveness of his sins and the freeing of his neck from the Fire, and he receives the like of that fasting person's reward, without anything being diminished from the fasting person's reward."

Some of them said, "O Messenger of Allah, not all of us find what to give a fasting person to break his fast with." He (ص) said that Allah Almighty grants this reward even to one who breaks a fasting person's fast with a draught of milk, or a date, or a drink of water. And whoever satisfies a fasting person, Allah will give him to drink from the Basin a drink after which he will never thirst until he enters Paradise. Its first part is mercy, its middle is forgiveness, and its last is freedom from the Fire. Whoever lightens the burden of the one his right hand possesses in it, Allah forgives him and frees him from the Fire. He said further: "So increase in it four qualities, two by which you will please your Lord, and two of which you can have no need to be without. As for the two by which you please your Lord, they are the testimony that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and that you seek His forgiveness. As for the two of which you cannot do without, they are that you ask Allah for Paradise and seek refuge in Him from the Fire."

He (ص) said: "For everything there is a zakat, and the zakat of bodies is fasting." And regarding the words of Allah Almighty, "And seek help through patience and prayer," he said: "Patience is fasting," and Allah gives the patient their reward without reckoning. He (ص) said to Ali (ع): "O Ali, fast in the heat, complete the ablution fully in the cold, that is, in winter, and teach yourself patience." He also said that the people who will have the most rest at the Standing are those who ate the least in this world, and the hungriest on the Day of Resurrection are those who were the most filled in this world. He (ص) said that Allah Almighty, and all His angels, send blessings upon those who fast, and he said: "I have not commanded My angels to supplicate for anyone without their supplication being answered."

He (ص) said: "The sleep of the fasting person is worship, and his breath is glorification." And he said that Allah Almighty says: "Fasting is for Me, and I Myself reward for it. The fasting person has two joys, a joy when he breaks his fast, and a joy when he meets his Lord. By Him in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, the odor of the mouth of the fasting believer is more fragrant to Allah than the scent of musk." And when he (ص) ate someone's food, he would say: "May the fasting break their fast with you, may the righteous eat your food, and may the angels send blessings upon you." This points to the excellence of providing for the breaking of the fast.

**On the beginning of the fast and entering it**

The Messenger of Allah (ص) said: "The pre-dawn meal is a blessing, so take it, even if one of you drinks only a mouthful of water, for Allah has angels who send blessings upon those who take the pre-dawn meal." The Commander of the Believers, Ali (ع), said: When Allah Almighty revealed, "Eat and drink until the white thread becomes distinct to you from the black thread," people took two threads, a white one and a black one, and kept eating and drinking through the night until the white thread became distinct from the black thread. So Allah Almighty revealed, "of dawn." He (ع) said: the dawn by which food is forbidden, and which is forbidden to the fasting person, is the dawn whose light spreads across the horizons of the east. The first dawn forbids nothing, and it is the one that rises slender and ascending on high.

He (ع) said: the sunnah is to hasten the breaking of the fast, meaning after the setting of the sun, and to delay the pre-dawn meal. He (ع) said that the Messenger of Allah (ص) forbade that the obligatory fast be observed except with a firm intention to fast. The Commander of the Believers (ع) said: Allah Almighty made the crescent moons markers for fasting, appointed times for the Hajj, and fixed terms for debts. That is among the blessings of Allah Almighty upon His creation. Whoever fasts upon something other than the sighting of the crescent, and then learns that that day was indeed of the month of Ramadan, must make it up, for he fasted only voluntarily. There is no harm in fasting voluntarily on a day about which there is doubt whether it belongs to the month of Ramadan, intending it as of Sha'ban. If he later learns it was of Ramadan, he makes it up, because he fasted it voluntarily. One does not fast in obligation upon a day of doubt, for Allah Almighty is not to be worshipped in any of His worship upon a basis of doubt.

**On what breaks the fast and what is required of one who breaks it**

Allah Almighty said: "It has been made lawful for you, on the night of the fast, to go in unto your wives," continuing to His words, "then complete the fast until the night." [al-Baqarah] The fasting person is thus forbidden food, drink, and intercourse during the daytime of the fast, and this is a matter of consensus among the Muslims. It has come from the Messenger of Allah (ص) that he made expiation obligatory upon whoever deliberately breaks the fast or has intercourse during the daytime of the month of Ramadan. The expiation for one who deliberately breaks the fast or has intercourse during the daytime of Ramadan is the freeing of a slave. If he does not find one, then fasting two consecutive months. If he is unable, then feeding sixty poor persons, together with making up the day on which he broke his fast. If he finds neither a slave to free nor anything to give in charity, and is unable to fast, then nothing is required of him, for Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity except with what He has given it.

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) concerning a man who dallies with his wife during the daytime of the month of Ramadan, or kisses her, or touches her, and emits: that he owes both the making-up and the expiation. If he does not emit, there is nothing upon him, though one who fears this for himself ought not to expose himself to it, but should keep his fast pure, guard it from what would corrupt it, and not expose it to what would break it. Whoever goes in unto his wife during the daytime of the month of Ramadan while she is asleep, or coerces her, or she is insane, or she is a child, owes the making-up and the expiation, if he is a responsible adult. There is nothing upon the woman when she is in one of the states we have mentioned.

They (عليهم السلام) said: whoever has intercourse in the night of the month of Ramadan should not deliberately sleep until he has performed the ritual bath. If he does so and rises in a state of major impurity, he owes the making-up of that day. Likewise if he has a nocturnal emission, wakes, and then sleeps again. But if his eyes overcome him, or he became impure and did not wake until dawn broke, he performs the ritual bath, completes his fast, and nothing is upon him. Whoever ate, drank, or had intercourse forgetfully during the daytime of the month of Ramadan should complete his fast, and there is no making-up upon him and no expiation, but he should leave that off the moment he remembers, spitting out whatever food or drink is in his mouth and rinsing it. If, after remembering, he deliberately swallows any of that, or persists in the intercourse, he owes the making-up and the expiation.

They said concerning one who deliberately vomits during the daytime of the month of Ramadan that he makes up a day in its place, on account of his laxity toward his fast. If anything of that vomit returns to his throat, he owes the making-up and the expiation. But if the vomit overcame him and he could not restrain it, nothing is upon him. Whoever rose in the last part of the night and ate or drank while inside his house, without going out and without looking to see whether dawn had risen, and dawn had in fact risen when he ate or drank, must complete his fast and make up a day. But if he had gone out, looked for the dawn, and did not see it risen, then returned and ate or drank while dawn had risen, without intending it, without laxity, and without neglecting to look, then nothing is upon him. Whoever is in doubt about the rising of the dawn and is among those who have knowledge of it may eat and drink until he is certain of its rising. But whoever is in doubt and has no knowledge of the dawn must refrain until the matter becomes certain to him, or until one who has knowledge of it and observes it confirms it. Whoever looks at the sun and judges that it has set, and so eats or drinks in the month of Ramadan, and then the sun appears to him again, should leave off food and drink until it sets, and nothing is upon him.

When the fasting person applies kohl and its taste reaches his throat, he owes the making-up of that day, if he did not know that its taste reaches his throat. But if he knows that its taste reaches his throat and does so deliberately, he owes the making-up and the expiation. And if he applies a kohl whose taste does not reach his throat, nothing is upon him. They permitted the tooth-stick for the fasting person, unless he uses it with a moist twig. For if he does that and its taste reaches his throat, he has broken his fast. So whoever does this deliberately, or exceeds that, owes the making-up and the expiation, and whoever did not intend it owes only the making-up. If he uses a dry twig, nothing is upon him. They also permitted the fasting person to chew gum and food, and to taste with his tongue, so long as nothing of it reaches his throat, just as he is permitted to rinse his mouth with water. As for cupping, if vomiting occurs to him from it, he makes up a day, and if he knows that cupping will bring this upon him, he ought not to expose himself to it unless he is compelled, in which case it is permitted. They also permitted the fasting person to smell perfume, oils, and pleasant scents, but he should not draw any of it in deeply, lest it reach his throat. Likewise he should not pour into his ear anything that would reach his throat.

**On fasting during travel**

Allah Almighty said: "O you who believe, fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may become God-fearing, for an appointed number of days, and whoever among you is sick or on a journey, then a number of other days." [al-Baqarah] Allah Almighty thus prescribed the fast of the month of Ramadan upon the resident who is of legal responsibility, and prescribed upon the traveler a number of other days. So it is the traveler's obligation to make up, while resident at home, the fast of whatever he traveled through of the month of Ramadan.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: The Messenger of Allah (ص) set out on the campaign of Mecca in the month of Ramadan, broke his fast, and commanded those with him to break their fast. It reached him that some of his companions had fasted, and he said: "Have you not in the Messenger of Allah a beautiful example?" Then he stopped on the road, at a place where any of them who wished could see him, took a cup of water, and drank it while they watched. When they saw this, they broke their fast. But a group persisted in their fasting, and the Messenger of Allah (ص) named them the disobedient.

Breaking the fast during the daytime of the month of Ramadan while traveling is therefore permitted, by the Book of Allah Almighty and the Sunnah of His Messenger (ص). Whoever refrains from food and drink while traveling is not counted as fasting, and must make up the number of days he refrained, as Allah Almighty has obligated upon him. And whoever does this, leaving food and drink while traveling without holding it to be a fast and without counting it as such, and then makes up those days while resident, nothing is upon him for having refrained from food and drink while traveling. It has come from many of the Imams (عليهم السلام) that they used to do this.

I have mentioned in the chapters on prayer which travel obligates the breaking of the fast and the shortening of the prayer, and wherever the shortening of the prayers is obligatory, the breaking of the fast is obligatory. It is disliked to travel deliberately in the month of Ramadan without need. When a traveler leaves his home during the month of Ramadan before the sun's decline from its zenith, he must make up that day, but if he leaves after the decline, he does not break his fast that day and does not make it up. Likewise, if he arrives home before the decline and has tasted nothing, he completes his fast for that day and does not make it up, and if he arrives after the decline, he must make it up.

**On breaking the fast for illnesses that arise**

Allah Almighty said: "O you who believe, fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may become God-fearing, for an appointed number of days, and whoever among you is sick or on a journey, then a number of other days." [al-Baqarah] I have set out in the chapter before this what follows from the apparent sense of these words of Allah Almighty for one who travels in the month of Ramadan, and the like of it is obligatory for one who is sick in it. Abu Abdillah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about the limit of illness at which the fasting person may break his fast, and he said that this is entrusted to the sick person, who is trusted concerning it: if he can bear the fast without harm to himself, let him fast.

**The Dispensation Granted to the Ill**

Allah Almighty said: *"Fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may fear God, for a fixed number of days… and whoever among you is sick or on a journey, then a number of other days."* [Al-Baqarah] As was clarified in the chapter preceding this one, it is not to be supposed that Allah intends by this only those who travel during the month of Ramadan, for the same obligation rests equally upon those who are ill during it.

Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about the degree of sickness that entitles a fasting person to break the fast. He said that this is entrusted to the sick person himself: if fasting harms him, there is no objection to his breaking it; and if it does not harm him, he should fast. Whatever harms a sick person through fasting is grounds for breaking it.

The Commander of the Believers, 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), ruled concerning the sick person who cannot fast during Ramadan that he must feed one poor person for every day, half a sa' of food. He specified that this applies to the sick person who may despair of recovering from his illness, such as one afflicted with leprosy or a settled, chronic ailment; and likewise the elderly man who is unable to fast must feed the poor, since he too may despair of ever being able to make up the missed days. But one who knows that he may recover from his illness and regain the ability to fast must make up the missed days once he is able.

As for the pregnant woman and the woman who is nursing, they are treated in the same manner as the sick: if either is unable to fast, she breaks her fast, even should her condition persist until another Ramadan arrives. If she is then able to fast, she fasts and also feeds the poor for the days she broke before that Ramadan; if she is still unable, she breaks that Ramadan's fast as well, making up the first missed days through feeding the poor and the second through fasting.

All of this is the position of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام). They held it beloved that whoever dies owing obligatory fasting should have it made up on his behalf. They also said that a woman who menstruates during Ramadan and breaks her fast is obligated to make up what she missed. Whoever owes make-up days for Ramadan may observe them as he wishes, consecutively or separately, for Allah has said only, *"then a number of other days."* They disliked making up the days of Ramadan during Dhu al-Hijjah, since that is a month of devotion, and they preferred that the missed days be made up as soon as possible after Ramadan itself. Nothing is incumbent upon one who delays the make-up, so long as it falls before the next Ramadan comes; but if the next Ramadan arrives before the make-up has been performed, then feeding the poor is required in addition, as has been mentioned.

**On Breaking the Fast**

Allah Almighty said: *"Then complete the fast until the night."* [Al-Baqarah: 187] The night begins with the setting of the disc, that is, the disappearance of the sun below the horizon at sunset with no barrier obstructing it. If a barrier does obstruct the view, then the sign of the disc's setting is the darkening of the eastern horizon and the advance of night from that direction, with the redness departing from it.

We have narrated from the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they loved to hasten the breaking of the fast and disliked delaying it. They instructed that a man break his fast after the Maghrib prayer as promptly as he is able, when he has food at hand, and that he not needlessly remain fasting. They said that if the food is present and the sun has set, he begins with the food before the prayer; but if no food is present, the fasting person prays and then breaks his fast. It is not fitting for him, when the food is set before him, to leave it and rise to pray; rather he eats, then rises and prays while the time still remains.

When the Messenger of Allah (ص) broke his fast, he would say: *"O Allah, for You we have fasted, and upon Your provision we have broken our fast, so accept from us. The thirst has gone, the veins are filled, and the reward remains, if Allah wills."*

They said (عليهم السلام) that if the crescent of Ramadan is sighted during the daytime, whether at the beginning of the day or at its end, the people should not break their fast until night, for that day is counted as a day of Ramadan; this is intended for the sighting at the end of the month. Likewise, if the crescent of the new month is seen during the daytime and had not been sighted the night before, then that day should not be fasted as part of Ramadan, since a day belongs to the month only when its crescent has been sighted on the night preceding it.

**On Laylat al-Qadr**

Allah Almighty said: *"Indeed, We sent it down on the Night of Power,"* to the end of the surah. [Al-Qadr] And He, Blessed and Exalted, said: *"Ha Mim. By the clear Book. Indeed, We sent it down on a blessed night. Indeed, We were warning. In it every wise matter is distinguished, a command from Us. Indeed, We were sending."* [Al-Dukhan]

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) said, concerning the words of Allah Almighty, *"The angels and the Spirit descend in it by the permission of their Lord,"* that the angels and the scribes descend to the lowest heaven by the permission of their Lord, and there they write down what is to be in the year of the affairs of men and what shall befall the servants. This matter has, with Allah, a station in which His will operates: He advances what He wills and delays what He wills and confirms what He wills, and with Him is the Mother of the Book. And Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said, concerning His words *"in it every wise matter is distinguished,"* that on that night the affairs of the year and all that is to occur in it are written.

The Messenger of Allah (ص) was asked about Laylat al-Qadr and said: *"Seek it in the last ten nights of Ramadan."* He also said that on Laylat al-Qadr the roll of pilgrims is written for each year, and in it every wise matter is distinguished. Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) gave a sign of the Night of Power: a wind blows on that night, and if the season is cold it grows warm, and if it is hot it grows cool. The Commander of the Believers (ع) related that the Messenger of Allah (ص) would awaken his household during the last ten nights of Ramadan, tighten his waistband, and pass the nights standing in prayer. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) explained that "tightening his waistband" means that he withdrew from his wives.

**On the Sunnah and Supererogatory Fasting, from Majlis al-Bayan**

Fasting is divided into four kinds: obligatory fasting, Sunnah fasting, forbidden fasting, and optional fasting.

The obligatory kind has seven divisions: the fast of the month of Ramadan every year; the fast of two consecutive months for one who deliberately breaks a fast on one of its days and cannot find a slave to free; the fast of two consecutive months for one who kills a believer by mistake when he cannot find a slave to free while paying the blood money to the family; the fast of two consecutive months in the expiation of zihar for one who cannot find a slave to free; the fast of three days in expiation for a violated oath for one who can find neither food, nor clothing, nor a slave to free; the fast of one who joins 'Umrah to Hajj in the tamattu' pilgrimage when he cannot find a sacrificial offering, which is ten days; and the fast undertaken by vow.

The Sunnah kind has twelve divisions: the fast of i'tikaf; the fast of Sha'ban; fasting a Thursday in every month; fasting the three white days; fasting Friday joined with the Thursday or the Saturday, without singling out Friday alone for the fast; the fast in the manner of Dawud (ع), which is one day of fasting followed by one day of breaking the fast, and which is the most rigorous form of fasting; the fast of the day of 'Arafah for one who is not a pilgrim; the fast of the boy when he reaches nine years of age; the fast that is the expiation for hunting; and the fast that is the expiation for shaving the head.

The forbidden kind has ten divisions: the first is the fast of Eid al-Fitr; the second is the fast of Eid al-Nahr; the third, fourth, and fifth are the fasts of the three days of Tashriq; the sixth is the fast of wisal, which is to join two or more days together without breaking the fast; the seventh is fasting in perpetuity; the eighth is fasting while menstruating; the ninth is fasting by a vow made in disobedience; and the tenth is fasting in doubt, since it is not permitted to fast except upon certainty.

The optional kind has twelve divisions, in each of which a person is given the choice: if he wishes he may fast, and if he wishes he may break his fast. These are: the fast of Rajab; the fast of some days of Shawwal after Eid al-Fitr, during which the reward of Ramadan is extended; the fast of nine days of Dhu al-Hijjah, which are the days of the ten; the fast of the day of 'Ashura' by refraining from food and drink without holding the belief that such a fast is superior, for that abstention resembles mourning more than devotion; the fast of the month of Muharram, for it is one of the sacred months; the fast of the sick person and the traveler, since upon them lies the making up of a number of other days, whether they fasted or broke; the fast of Monday; and the fast of permission, which includes that a woman may not fast voluntarily except with her husband's permission, a bondsman may not fast voluntarily except with his master's permission, and a guest may not fast voluntarily except with his host's permission.

Among the fasts, too, is the fast of withholding speech, of which Allah Almighty informed us in the account of Maryam, in His words: *"Eat and drink and be comforted; and if you see any human being, say: I have vowed to the Most Merciful a fast, so I will not speak this day to any human."* He commanded her to eat, to drink, and to speak, by His words *"so say: I have vowed,"* and this indicates that the speech she was forbidden was some speech and not all of it, just as it is permitted to speak within the prayer with the intimate supplication of Allah Almighty while all else is withheld from it, as I have mentioned in the chapters on prayer.

The Messenger of Allah (ص) did not bind himself to a prayer that was not prescribed; yet the Imams of his progeny (عليهم السلام) bound themselves to certain prayers and commanded their followers to observe them by way of encouragement and persuasion, not as a binding obligation that may not be left, and not as though it were like the obligatory prayer. In the same way, the Messenger of Allah (ص) bound himself, in the latter part of his life, to fasting two months in the year besides the month of Ramadan; and likewise the Imams of his progeny (عليهم السلام) bound themselves to that and urged their followers to it by way of exhortation and encouragement, not as something binding that may not be abandoned.

It has been narrated from the Imams of his progeny (عليهم السلام) that the Messenger of Allah (ص) would fast until it was said of him that he does not break his fast, and then break his fast until it was said of him that he does not fast. He used to fast Muharram, Rajab, and Sha'ban; he used to fast one day and break the fast the next; he used to fast Monday and Thursday; and he used to fast the white nights, which are the thirteenth of the month and the two days after it. These are called the white nights because the moon appears in them from the beginning of the night to its end, as though they were made white by its light. Then his practice came to this: that he would fast Sha'ban and three days in every month, namely a Thursday at the beginning of the month, which is its first Thursday, a Thursday at its end, which is its last Thursday, and a Wednesday in its middle, the Wednesday nearest to the midpoint from either side. Those two months are the like of the obligatory fast in reward, and upon this practice he (ص) was taken.

It has been narrated from Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that he would fast Rajab, Sha'ban, and Ramadan, joining them together. He (ع) said that Nuh (ع) boarded the Ark on the first day of Rajab and commanded those with him of the jinn and mankind to fast it. He said: whoever fasts a month of Rajab, the Fire is set from him the distance of a year's journey; if he fasts it two years, the Fire recedes from him the distance of two years; and so it continues, until when he fasts it seven, the seven gates of the Fire are shut against him; if he fasts it eight, the eight gates of Paradise are opened to him; if he fasts it ten, his request is granted; if he fasts it fifteen, it is said to him, "Begin your works anew"; and whoever adds more, Allah adds to him.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali (ع) was asked about the fast of the day of 'Ashura' and said that it was before the fast of Ramadan was made obligatory. Abu Abdullah Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said that the Ark came to rest upon Mount Judi on the day of 'Ashura', and so Nuh (ع) commanded those with him to fast that day. He said that it is the day on which Allah Almighty accepted the repentance of Adam and Hawwa'; the day on which the sea was parted for the Children of Israel and Pharaoh was drowned; the day on which Ibrahim (ع) was born; the day on which Allah Almighty accepted the repentance of the people of Yunus (ع); the day on which 'Isa ibn Maryam (عليهم السلام) was born; the day on which Allah Almighty honored al-Husayn (ع) with martyrdom; and the day on which the Qa'im from his progeny shall rise.

Imam al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah (ع) said that the day of 'Ashura' is a day that Allah, His Messenger, and His friends have magnified, for the merit and the proof it contains; while the enemies of Allah's friends, from the Umayyads and the progeny of Marwan, magnified it for what they attained in it in violating the sanctities of Allah and the religion of Allah and killing the sons of the Messenger of Allah (ص). So they set it in the place of gladness and gloating rather than the place of seeking forgiveness and righteous works, turning aside from the Sunnah of Allah and the Sunnah of His Prophet regarding it, and persisting in the things Allah has forbidden and in disobedience to Him.

It has come from the Imams (عليهم السلام) that they held it beloved to fast the day of 'Arafah, for whoever knows its virtue, except for one who is at the standing place and fears that the fast would weaken him for the standing. And they disliked singling out Friday for fasting, unless it is fasted together with another day, before it or after it.

Beyond the zakat that seals the fast, the fast itself carries its own fine rulings, and the Imams (عليهم السلام) drew careful lines around when a person fasts, when he refrains, and when fasting is not permitted at all.

'Ali (ع) said, "Whoever fasts on Friday, it is as if he fasted for the period between the two Fridays," meaning that a day of the week arrives and he joins it to another rather than singling Friday out for fasting by itself. He (ع) also said that he would not accept from anyone who had an obligatory fast still outstanding that he perform a supererogatory fast until the obligation upon him is first discharged. Yet a concession was granted in the other direction: whoever has begun a voluntary fast may break it, and no making-up is required of him, provided the sun has not yet passed its zenith. Should he break the voluntary fast after the sun has declined, he must then make up that day. And they forbade fasting on the day of al-Fitr, on the day of al-Adha, and on the three days of al-Tashreeq. They likewise forbade al-wasal in fasting, which is that the fasting person joins two days or more together without breaking his fast between them.

From fasting the teaching turns to i'tikaf, the retreat that fasting makes possible. I'tikaf is the act of remaining and residing within a mosque, the 'akif being the one who stays there. Of it Allah, mighty and majestic, said, "the one who remains there and the visitor are equal," and He, blessed and exalted, said, "And do not engage in relations with them while you are in retreat in the mosques" [al-Baqarah: 187] ﴿ وَلَا تُبَاشِرُوهُنَّ وَأَنْتُمْ عَاكِفُونَ فِى الْمَسَاجِدِ﴾, the reference being to women. The Messenger of Allah, (ع), used to observe i'tikaf in the last ten days of Ramadan, binding himself to the mosque by night and by day, and he used to say that i'tikaf in those ten days is equal in reward to two Hajj and two Umrahs. Through those nights he would remain standing in prayer, and each night he would bathe between the two evening prayers. He observed i'tikaf in the first ten days of Ramadan, then in the middle ten in the following year, and then in the last ten in the year after that.

'Ali (ع) said that whoever undertakes i'tikaf ought to keep to the mosque night and day and give himself over within it to prayer, to the recitation of the Quran, and to the remembrance of Allah, mighty and majestic. He should not occupy himself with conversation except in what cannot be avoided; he should neither sell nor buy, nor recite poetry, nor attend a funeral, nor visit the sick, nor enter a house, nor seclude himself with a woman, nor speak in coarse or whispered speech, nor lay a hand upon anyone. Whatever he holds back from speaking with people is better for him.

It was narrated from Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that he said, "There is no i'tikaf except with fasting, and no i'tikaf except in a mosque in which the congregational prayer is held. The one in i'tikaf does not pray in his house, does not receive women, does not sell and does not buy, and does not leave the mosque except for a need he cannot forgo, and once he leaves for it he does not sit until he returns." The same rulings hold for a woman in i'tikaf, except that should she begin to menstruate her i'tikaf is nullified and she departs from the mosque. And the least duration of i'tikaf is three days.
