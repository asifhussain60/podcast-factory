# Facing Death: The Rites of the Departed

The Book of Funerals

**Mentioning the Sick, Visiting the Ill, and Its Rewards**

We have narrated from the Messenger of Allah (ص) that Allah Almighty says: "If I afflict My servant with an illness and he is patient three times and does not complain, and Allah knows best his condition, I will replace his flesh with flesh better than his flesh and his blood with blood better than his blood. If I take him, I take him into My mercy; and if I heal him, I heal him with no sin upon him." And he (ص) said: fever is a purification from a Forgiving Lord. And he (ص) said: the groaning of the sick person, if he is patient, is written for him as good deeds; but if he panics, only panic is recorded for him and he has no reward for it.

The Commander of the Faithful, 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), said: the sick person remains in the custody of Allah so long as he does not complain to those who visit him, and his misdeeds are wiped away from him. And he (ع) said: when Allah Almighty afflicts a servant, He drops from him of his sins in proportion to his illness; and from the fever he bears there comes to him a reward beyond counting. And he (ع) said: there is no visiting the sick until after three days, and there is no visiting of women in illness. And he (ع) said: whoever visits a sick person seeking the face of Allah and fulfilling His appointment will be in the garden of Paradise so long as he sits with the sick person; and when he departs from him, Allah sends on that day seventy thousand angels who pray for him until nightfall, and if the visit was at night, then until morning. Visiting should therefore be hastened to the early part of the day.

Allah Almighty said: ﴿فِطْرَتَ اللَّهِ الَّتِي فَطَرَ النَّاسَ عَلَيْهَا﴾ [الروم: ٣٠], the natural disposition of Allah upon which He created mankind, meaning His fashioning of them so. And he (ع) said: it is from the fitrah that the dying person, when death draws near, be turned toward the Qiblah. We have narrated from the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they commanded this, and that he be laid upon his back and set so that his face and the sole of his foot are turned toward the Qiblah, if that is possible. If it is not possible, then he is placed upon his side with his face toward the Qiblah. They also commanded that the testimony be repeated aloud at his head, "There is no god but Allah, and that Muhammad is His servant and His messenger," and that the Verse of the Throne and the two verses after it be recited at his head, then: ﴿إِنَّ رَبَّكُمُ اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ﴾ [الأعراف: ٥٤] to the end of the verse, and the last third of Surah al-Baqarah: ﴿لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ وَإِن تُبْدُوا مَا فِي أَنفُسِكُمْ أَوْ تُخْفُوهُ﴾ [البقرة: ٢٨٤] to its end. And he is supplicated for, so it is said: if his appointed term has come, make his affair easy for him and draw his soul forth from him into contentment from You and good pleasure; and give him glad tidings, and forgive him, and have mercy upon him.

Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) said: when a believer among you dies and is prevented from speaking, the Messenger of Allah (ص) comes to him, together with 'Ali, Fatimah, al-Hasan, and al-Husayn. The Messenger of Allah (ص) says to him: as for what you used to fear, you have been made safe from it; and as for what you used to hope for, it is before you. Then a door to the Garden is opened for him, and he sees his dwelling within it, and it is said to him: if you wish to return to the world, you will be returned to it, and its gold and its silver are yours. He says: I have no need of the world nor of anything in it. And he recites the word of Allah Almighty: ﴿لَهُمُ الْبُشْرَىٰ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ﴾ [يونس: ٦٤].

The Messenger of Allah (ص) was asked about the believer, how death may be made hard upon him and how it may be made easy for him, and likewise about the disbeliever. He said: the believing servant has a station in the Garden that he cannot reach by any measure of affliction, so death comes to him before he has reached that degree, and death is made hard upon him so that he may reach it. And it may be that Allah, Blessed and Exalted, commands the Angel of Death to draw the believer's soul out from its gentlest side, so that people see that death has been made hard upon him, though it is in truth a lightening from Allah Almighty upon His servant. Or He may command him concerning the disbeliever, so that he pulls out his soul in one wrench, like the wrenching of a roasting-spit from wet wool, so that people see that it was made easy for him, though a great punishment has already befallen him.

And he (ع) said: death is a blessing for the believer. And he (ص) said: the believer is one who finds rest and one from whom others find rest. The one who finds rest has been relieved of the grief and hardship of this world and has come to the mercy of Allah; and as for the one from whom others find rest, that is the disbeliever, from whom the servants of Allah are relieved. And he (ع) said: increase the remembrance of the destroyer of pleasures. It was asked: O Messenger of Allah, what is the destroyer of pleasures? He said: death. And he (ع) said: are not the believers those who most remember death and most prepare themselves for it?

The Commander of the Faithful, 'Ali (ع), delivered a sermon and said: I have never seen a certainty of faith more resembling doubt than the state of this man. Every day he bids farewell to the dead and walks in their funeral procession to the graves, and yet he returns to the delusion of the world and does not cease from its desires and pleasures. Were there for the wretched son of Adam no sin to bring him low and no reckoning to be settled against him, the dead alone would summon him to caution, for death scatters what a man has gathered, disperses his assembly, and orphans his children, so that he ought to guard against the state he is in with the utmost guarding. Yet people have grown heedless of death with the heedlessness of a people upon whom it will never descend, and they have leaned upon the world and its desires with the leaning of a people who look for no reckoning and fear no punishment.

**Mentioning Grief at the Time of Death, and Condolence**

When Ibrahim, the son of the Messenger of Allah (ص), died, the Prophet ordered 'Ali (ع) to wash him, and the Messenger of Allah (ص) shrouded him. Then he said: O 'Ali, carry him. So he carried him until he brought him to al-Baqi', and the Messenger of Allah (ص) prayed over him. Then he said: O 'Ali, carry him. So he carried him until he brought him to the grave. Then he said: O 'Ali, go down into the grave. So he went down into it, and the Messenger of Allah (ص) lowered him to him. When he saw him laid in his place, he wept, and the people wept at his weeping, until the voices of the men rose above the voices of the women. Then the Messenger of Allah (ص) forbade them most sternly from wailing, and said: the eye sheds tears and the heart grieves, but do not say what angers the Lord. Indeed we are stricken by losing you, and indeed we sorrow over you, O Ibrahim.

He (ص) said: I do not forbid you to weep, but I forbid you from lamentation and wailing. This weeping is a mercy that Allah Almighty places in the heart of whom He wills among His creation, and Allah shows mercy to whom He wills, and Allah shows mercy only to the merciful among His servants. And he (ص) said: whoever is stricken by a calamity, let him remember his calamity in me, for it is greater than every calamity. And he (ع) said: patience is with the first shock, meaning at the moment of bereavement. And he (ع) said: whoever loses three of his children and bears their loss for the sake of Allah, they will be a shield for him from the Fire. It was said: and two, O Messenger of Allah? He said: and two.

When the Messenger of Allah (ص) was taken, his household heard one speaking whose words they heard but whose person they did not see. He said: peace be upon you, O people of the House, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings. ﴿كُلُّ نَفْسٍ ذَائِقَةُ الْمَوْتِ وَإِنَّمَا تُوَفَّوْنَ أُجُورَكُمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ فَمَن زُحْزِحَ عَنِ النَّارِ وَأُدْخِلَ الْجَنَّةَ فَقَدْ فَازَ وَمَا الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا إِلَّا مَتَاعُ الْغُرُورِ﴾ [آل عمران: ١٨٥]. Indeed in Allah is a consolation from every calamity and a replacement for everything that perishes. So in Allah place your hope, and Him alone worship, and know that the one truly afflicted is the one deprived of reward. And peace be upon you, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings.

The Messenger of Allah (ص) used to take from the women, when receiving their oath of allegiance, that they would not wail. And he (ص) said: three practices of the age of ignorance will not cease among the people until the Day of Resurrection: seeking rain by the stars, impugning lineages, and wailing over the dead. Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) enjoined at his death, saying: do not strike your cheeks for me, and do not tear your garments for me. And the Commander of the Faithful, 'Ali (ع), wrote to his judge over al-Ahwaz: beware of wailing over the dead in any land in which you hold authority.

**Mentioning the Washing of the Deceased**

The Messenger of Allah (ص) charged 'Ali (ع) to undertake his washing, and said to him: Jibra'il will be with you to turn me, and let al-Fadl ibn 'Abbas pass by so that he may lower his gaze and hand you the water, for none may see my nakedness but you. So 'Ali (ع) washed him, and he said: I was being helped with him, and I felt a hand turning him with me which I could not see. When I began to wash him, I wished to remove the shirt from him, but I was called to: do not remove the shirt from him. So he was washed in his shirt. And I wished to lay him face down, but I was called to: do not lay him face down. So I turned him upon his side.

He was washed three washings: a washing with water and cleansing herb, which is the soapwort with which the deceased is likewise washed, or with what stands in its place such as the lote-tree leaf and marshmallow and the like; a washing with water and camphor; and a washing with pure water. Upon this the deceased should be washed, according to the teaching of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام): three washings, a washing with water and lote-tree leaf, a washing with water and camphor, and the third and last with pure water. He is turned upon his side, and he is not laid upon his face, nor is he seated upright as the common people do with the dead when they prop the deceased up and set him sitting.

The washing of the deceased is like the washing from major ritual impurity. One begins by washing his hands, then his private parts, and the washer wraps a cloth around his hand when he washes the private parts. He wipes the belly gently at that point while pouring water over the private parts, so that whatever may be there comes out. He washes the face three times and the hands to the elbows, and wipes the head and the feet, but he does not put water into his mouth or nose. When he wipes his head, he wipes his ears. Then he pours the water over him and passes his hand over the rest of his body, and turns him so that the water reaches what is beneath him. He washes him unclothed, covering what is between his navel and his knees with a wrapper; and if he washes him in a garment, there is no harm in it. He cleanses whatever is upon him of smear and filth, and rubs the soles of his feet if there is dirt upon them with a shard or a stone, and removes any dirt that may be beneath the nails. He washes the head and the beard with lote-tree leaf and takes care in cleaning him and removing every smear and defilement from him; and if there is need to heat the water for his washing, it is heated. He is washed thus three times, as we have described.

The washer should conceal the nakedness of the deceased, be gentle with him, keep secret whatever he sees of him, and not mention anything he observes in him. The Messenger of Allah (ص) said: there is no Muslim man who washes his Muslim brother without finding him foul, without looking upon his nakedness, and without mentioning any evil of him, then walks in his procession, and prays over him, and sits until he is placed in his grave, but that he emerges fragrant, cleansed of his sins.

We have narrated from the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt of the Messenger of Allah (ص) that they commanded the washing of all Muslims, the people of disobedience and others alike, and that they permitted one in a state of major impurity and the menstruating woman to wash the dead. They said: a man washes men and a woman washes women; a woman may wash her husband and a man may wash his wife, when there is need, from behind a garment. If a woman dies among men, or a man among women, and there is no mahram among them and none is found to wash them, they are buried and not washed. The martyr is buried in the garments in which he was stricken and is not washed, and his weapon, leather, and fur are removed from him. This is when he dies on the field of battle; but if he is carried off and reaches his people and then dies, he is washed and shrouded. The drowned, the burned, and a limb of a person that is found, when it is such that a man could not live once it left him, are washed and buried. Whatever falls from the deceased is washed and placed with him; and if his limbs, or any part of them, become separated from his body, each is washed and each limb set in its place, and he is shrouded thus and buried. It is recommended to hasten the burial of the deceased, except the drowned and the one struck senseless and whoever is doubtful in his case, for one waits with him until the truth of his death is known. If a man dies while in major impurity, or a woman while menstruating or in childbed, the washing suffices for them, just as any other who dies in purity is washed.

**Mentioning the Embalming and the Shrouds**

We have narrated from the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that when the washer of the deceased has finished his washing, he should dry him with a cloth, take a length of cloth of the measure by which he may gird him, spread cotton upon him, and set embalming camphor upon it. He removes, with gentleness and modesty, the cloth with which the nakedness was covered, binds this cloth upon the loins, and places the cotton he has spread over the private parts and the seat, binding it well upon him. He clothes him in a shirt, if that is his shroud or one of his garments; and if that is not available, he leaves him so upon the washing-place.

Then the shrouds are spread out and their edges are set in order, and he lifts him and lays him within them. The embalming is placed on the places of prostration: upon his forehead and his nose, in the palms of his hands, and on the fronts of his knees and his feet. Then the folds of the shroud are drawn over him, and he is wound with a turban, the middle of which is set upon his head and returned beneath his chin, and its tail let down upon his chest with his shrouds. Of the embalming is placed upon his head, and in his hearing and his sight, and upon his nose and his beard and his chest. The embalming and whatever is of sweet scent are placed therein; but neither wars nor saffron is placed in the embalming of men, though it is placed in the embalming of women. The Commander of the Faithful, 'Ali (ع), placed in the embalming of the Messenger of Allah (ص) musk which he had charged him to place in it, and what remained over of it, the Messenger of Allah (ص) commanded 'Ali (ع) to place in his own embalming. Camphor is placed in the embalming.

The shrouds of the deceased and the place of his washing are perfumed with incense, but the censer is not carried behind his bier. One in a state of ritual consecration is not brought near any scent, just as scent is not brought near him while living. It is recommended that the number of the winding-sheets for the deceased be odd, three or five or seven, and there is no fixed limit set for that. A single garment suffices as a shroud, just as it suffices in the prayer. They disliked that a man be shrouded in pure silk, and they permitted shrouding in dyed garments. The shroud is taken from the principal of the estate the deceased has left.

A believer who has died is prepared for his final journey with care and dignity. We have narrated from the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that once the deceased has been shrouded he is to be placed upon a bier if one can be found, and carried upon the shoulders of men wherever this is possible. If no bier is available, he may be carried upon an animal or by whatever means can be found, and his shroud may be covered over with a garment. Over a woman's bier a coffin is set in the form of a dome and draped with a garment, and the deceased is tied down if there is any fear that the body might slip from the bier. The procession moves at a brisk and steady pace, neither hurried into disorder nor dragged slowly along as the Jews did. Whoever wishes to carry the bier begins by taking its front right side upon his right shoulder, then turns about it if he wishes, and the others take their turns in bearing it. Adorning the coffin is disliked. Walking ahead of the funeral is disliked, and walking behind it is what is commanded.

When the procession reaches the graveyard, the bier is set down crosswise with the deceased laid upon his back, his right side toward the qibla and his left toward the one who prays. The one who leads the prayer stands over the bier and pronounces five takbirs, raising his hands with each one, and gives the salutation to his right and his left as he does in the ritual prayer. In the first takbir he praises Allah and extols Him as He is worthy. In the second he prays upon the Prophet and his family. In the third he supplicates for the deceased, striving hard in that supplication if he knew him, saying: "O Allah, this is Your servant and the son of Your servant. He has departed from this world and come to You, and we know of him only good, and I have come as an intercessor for him." Then he prays for him with whatever supplication he is able. If he did not know the deceased he says: "O Allah, You know best his secret and his intention. If he was a doer of good, then increase him in his good and have mercy upon him; and if he was a sinner, then forgive him and pardon him." He labors in supplication and says: "Our Lord, You have encompassed all things in mercy and knowledge, so forgive those who repent and follow Your way," to the end of the verse, "and that is the great triumph." If he wishes he may say: "O Allah, You know this servant of ours better than we do, so gather him with those he loved, and give his charge to the one who had authority over him," and the like of this. And if the deceased is a child, he says: "O Allah, make him a forerunner for his parents, a reward and a treasure laid up in advance, and let them and us profit through him." One supplicates in a case such as this with whatever he is able. In the fourth takbir he supplicates for the believing men and women, and in the fifth he prays upon the Prophet (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) and his family, and then he withdraws. He keeps all of this quiet except the takbir, which he announces aloud; and if he prays over the deceased at night he raises his voice, as one raises it in the prayers of the night.

There is a second narration from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع). He said: the one praying over the deceased praises Allah and extols Him in the first takbir, prays upon the Prophet and his family in the second, supplicates for the Imam in the third and for the believers in the fourth, supplicates for the deceased in the fifth if he is one who is known, and then prays upon the Prophet and his family and gives the salutation. He said: in like manner the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) once pronounced only four takbirs over one whom he prayed over from among the hypocrites, who did not deserve to be supplicated for; they departed after the fourth takbir, did not supplicate for him, and left his corpse cast aside.

The prayer over the deceased may be offered at any hour of the day or night except at the moment of the sun's rising and its setting, and it is better that it be prayed by day. Funerals should not be prayed over at night unless one is compelled to it, for it has come from the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) that he said: "Bury your dead by day, for the angels of the day are more merciful than the angels of the night." If the ruler, or one whom the ruler has appointed over the congregational funeral prayer, is present, he has the better right to come forward and lead the prayer over the deceased than the guardian; and where there is no ruler, it is the nearest of the guardians, then the next nearest. The prayer is offered over the martyr, over those guilty of grave sins and transgressions, over the one put to death by a prescribed penalty or in retaliation, over the child born of adultery, and over a severed limb if it is known that its owner cannot live once it is parted from the body; but if that is not known, no prayer is offered over the limb apart from the rest of the body.

When several funerals are brought together, the leader may pray over them all at once if he wishes, ranging them in a single row if they are all men or all women, and standing in the middle of them; and even where there is only one, he stands at the chest of a man and at the head of a woman. If he prays over them one by one, that too is permitted. When funerals of men and women are gathered, the women are set nearest the qibla and the men beyond them; and likewise when there is a single man and a single woman, the woman is set nearest the qibla and the man beyond her. Whoever joins the leader after the takbir has begun enters the prayer with him, begins with whatever supplication he ought to begin with, and follows the leader's takbirs; and when the leader has finished, the latecomer makes up what he missed, then gives the salutation and withdraws, just as he does when he comes late to the ritual prayer. Whoever does not reach the leader before the salutation has missed the funeral prayer; he may supplicate for the deceased if he wishes, but he does not offer the prayer over him.

As for the burial itself, we have narrated from the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (عليهم السلام) that they commanded graves to be dug for the dead and forbade digging them deeper than three cubits. Then a niche, the lahd, is cut into the grave, or a trench, the darih, is dug, whichever is preferred. The lahd is a recess hollowed out at the side toward the qibla to receive the body where it will lie, while the ground behind it, toward the rear of the grave, is left, so that one who prepares the niche may stand upon it as he works. The darih is a hollow cut down the middle of the grave, with ledges before it and behind it. Whichever of these is done is permitted and good, and they allowed the niche to be lined when there is need for it.

When the deceased is brought to his grave and set down at its edge on the side toward the qibla, his guardians descend into the grave, or those whom they appoint to lower him. The one nearest to a man takes his place at the front, and the one nearest to a woman at her rear. They go down into the grave and come up again from the side of the deceased's feet and receive him, taking hold of him with their own hands. If it is a woman, a garment is spread over the grave to screen her while she is lowered, until she is hidden from view. They lay the body upon its right side, set evenly, not tilted onto its face nor onto its back, and they loosen the knots that were tied upon the shroud and supplicate for the deceased with whatever supplication is present with them. They set up the unbaked bricks over him, or planks, or palm branches if they wish; and if they heap the earth over him directly, that is good. They press the earth down over him from above, and those who went down to prepare the niche climb out. The earth taken from the grave is returned into it, and what remains is left mounded upon it, and water is sprinkled over it from above, and then the people withdraw.

If there is need to bury two, or a group, in a single grave, they are buried together, and the most excellent of them is placed nearest the qibla; and if there are men and women, the men are set in front of the women, with a little earth laid between each two to divide them. They regarded moving the dead from one place to another as disliked, and were severe in the matter. They directed that the grave be made square and level and not raised into a peaked mound, and they permitted that it be marked with a sign so that it may be recognized, and that it be plastered, whitened, and roofed over with a covering. They permitted the visiting of the grave from time to time across the length of the years, with mercy sought for the deceased and forgiveness asked for him, and they commanded whoever enters the burial ground to greet the people of the graves and supplicate for them all. They forbade stepping across the graves, laughing beside them, praying between them, and relieving oneself among them.

If a polytheist woman dies and there is in her womb a child fathered by a Muslim which has not yet come forth from her, her own people take charge of her and she is buried in their graveyards. If a woman dies and there is in her womb a living child that stirs, there is no harm in the midwife reaching in with her hand and delivering the child without cutting the mother open, if she is able; and if she cannot, she opens the belly and brings the child out. If no midwife can be found for this, then the man closest of kin to her may do it. But this is done only when the child's life is certain, that it has reached six months or more, at which age it is known to live if it is delivered; otherwise it is left until death overtakes it in the womb, and she is not to be buried while it still stirs within her. Likewise, if the child dies in the woman's womb and she is endangered by it, the midwife reaches in with her hand, severs it, and removes it, for a belly has been opened for other than a child and the mother has emerged alive; and to open the belly of the dead child is more fitting than to kill the living child in its mother's womb.

They held it proper that food be sent to the family of the deceased for as long as they remain in their grief, this being done for them by their neighbors and those closest to them, since grief so occupies them that they are kept from preparing food for themselves. The Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وعلى آله) commanded his household to prepare food for the family of Ja'far ibn Abi Talib (ع) when the news of his death reached them. Al-Nu'man said: "There has come upon them that which occupies them and turns them away from seeing to this for themselves."
