# Mukhtasar ul Asar

## The First Obligation: Seeking Knowledge

Process flow. The four things declared obligatory upon every person of reason and intellect. Ordered, each feeding the next.

Start: Knowledge arrives
  Step 1: Listen to knowledge
  Step 2: Preserve it
  Step 3: Act upon it
  Step 4: Spread it
End: The obligation is discharged

> "Four things are obligatory upon every person of reason and intellect among my community: to listen to knowledge, to preserve it, to act upon it, and to spread it." (The Messenger of God)

Timeline. The prophetic account gives a second, differently ordered ladder of the same discipline. The two sequences do not match, and the difference is the point.

Stage 1: Silence
Stage 2: Listening
Stage 3: Spreading it
Stage 4: Acting upon it

> "The first stage of knowledge is silence; the second is listening; the third is spreading it; and the fourth is acting upon it." (The Messenger of God)

Contrast pair. Two households, two inheritances, one law of transmission running in opposite directions. The believer bequeaths downward toward the Garden; the disobedient servant bequeaths downward toward the Fire.

Column A: The believer
- What is bequeathed: knowledge and righteous conduct
- Reach: every member, none lost
- Named group: young and old, servant and neighbor
- Destination: Paradise, all brought in together

Column B: The disobedient servant
- What is bequeathed: ignorance and evil conduct
- Reach: every member, none lost
- Named group: young and old, servant and neighbor
- Destination: the Fire, all brought in together

Shared row: The mechanism is identical. A man's household inherits his relation to knowledge, and it carries the whole house to a single end.

Annotated structure. The four things for which Ali held it a small price to wear out riding-beasts. Each is a discipline of the self before God.

Whole: The four aims worth any journey
- Aim 1: That the servant place his hope in none but his Lord
- Aim 2: That he fear nothing but his own sin
- Aim 3: That the ignorant not be ashamed to learn
- Aim 4: That the scholar, asked what he does not know, say "I do not know"
Reading: Two aims govern the heart before God (hope, fear); two govern the posture of the learner and the teacher (humility to learn, honesty to admit a limit).

## Purity: Water, Ablution, and the Body Made Ready

Process flow. The single rule that governs whether any water remains a purifier. Everything in the chapter reduces to this test, applied to dates, milk, or impurity alike.

Start: Water, with something cast into it
  Test: Does the added thing alter the water's color, taste, or smell so as to prevail over it?
  Branch A (No, it stays undiscernible): The ruling of water remains. Lawful to drink and to purify with.
  Branch B (Yes, it becomes evident and dominant): The name of water ceases. The ruling passes to whatever prevailed over it.
End (Branch B, impurity): The water is impure and takes the ruling of that impurity.
End (Branch B, drink such as dates or milk): It becomes a fermented drink or milk; purification with it is no longer permitted.

> "Water is a purifier; nothing renders it impure." (The Messenger of God)
> "Nothing renders water impure." (Ali)

Annotated structure. The verse of al-Ma'idah divides the four limbs of ablution into two actions. The division, washing against wiping, is the whole architecture of the rite.

Whole: The ablution commanded in al-Ma'idah 6, in fixed order
- Limb 1: The face, from hairline to chin, sideburn to sideburn. Action: WASH
- Limb 2: The hands, to the elbows. Action: WASH
- Limb 3: The head, front then back to the nape. Action: WIPE
- Limb 4: The feet, the tops, to the ankles. Action: WIPE
Reading: One begins with what God began with and never puts a later limb before an earlier one. Washing belongs to the face and arms; wiping to the head and feet. To wash the feet instead is permitted; to wipe over socks after impurity is not.

Contrast pair. What breaks the state of ablution against what leaves it intact. The line is drawn at the two outlets of the body.

Column A: Breaks ablution
- Source: only what issues from the two outlets, front and posterior
- Instances: urine, excrement, wind from the posterior
- Also: blood, discharge, pus, worms, moisture from either outlet
- Sleep: heavy sleep that severs awareness of what issues

Column B: Does not break ablution
- Touching: contact short of intercourse, a kiss, touching the male organ
- The body's other exits: blood, vomit, or discharge from elsewhere
- Consumption: food and drink
- Sleep: light dozing while still perceiving what happens

Shared row: Certainty governs doubt. One certain of purity who then doubts owes nothing; one certain of breaking who then doubts must purify again.

Comparison matrix. Three fluids of the male body, each with its own purification requirement. The distinctions turn on what the fluid is and whether urine has already cleared the tract.

Rows: al-madhi, al-wadhi, al-mani (semen by emission)
Columns: What it is | What it requires | Note
- al-madhi: the thin fluid issuing through desire | ablution only | no greater washing
- al-wadhi: the thin fluid following urine | ablution only | no greater washing
- al-mani: the gushing fluid of emission or intercourse | the greater washing (ghusl) | washing is obligatory once the two circumcised parts meet, whether or not emission occurs

Hierarchy. The occasions on which the greater washing becomes obligatory. Distinct triggers, one shared remedy.

- Ghusl becomes obligatory from:
  - Janabah (by emission, or by penetration once the glans disappears)
  - Menstruation, and postpartum bleeding, once it ends
  - A disbeliever's acceptance of Islam
  - Recovery from unconsciousness of a length one cannot account for
  - The washing of the dead when they die

## The Pillar of Religion: Standing in Prayer

Comparison matrix. The five daily prayers, each read out of the phrases of al-Rum and their fixed astronomical markers. The Book states the times in summary; the Sunnah fixes the marker.

Rows: Fajr, Zuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha
Columns: Quranic phrase (al-Rum 17-18) | First time marker
- Fajr: "when you reach the morning" | the true dawn spreading across the eastern horizon
- Zuhr: "when you reach the time of noon" | the sun's decline from the zenith
- Asr: "in the late afternoon" | when a thing's shadow equals, then doubles, its length
- Maghrib: "when you reach the evening" | the setting of the sun's disc, a single time only
- Isha: "when you reach the evening" and "after the Isha prayer" | when the twilight redness vanishes

> "The decline of the sun is its departure from the zenith, and that is the first time of Zuhr." (Ja'far ibn Muhammad)

Hierarchy. The multiplied reward of prayer, ranked by the place it is offered. A descending ladder from the Sacred Mosque down to the man praying alone in his house.

- Sacred Mosque of Makkah: 100,000 prayers
  - Mosque of Madinah: 10,000 prayers
    - Mosque of Jerusalem: 1,000 prayers
      - The great mosque, where the Friday prayer gathers: 100 prayers
        - The mosque of one's tribe: 25 prayers
          - The market mosque: 12 prayers
            - A man's prayer alone in his house: 1 prayer

Process flow. The descending congregation, from the camel-herder's question to the Messenger. At every step the household thins, yet the merit of congregation holds, down to the last man.

Start: A man with family, children, and servant-boys, he leads them all
  Servant-boys leave to graze: he leads family and children. Still a congregation.
  Children leave to track rain: he leads his family. Still a congregation.
  Household occupied with labor: he prays alone.
End: "The believer alone is a congregation."

Contrast pair. The call to prayer as the Ahl al-Bayt preserved it against the form that dropped a phrase. The dispute is over one line and the reason given for cutting it.

Column A: The adhan of the Ahl al-Bayt
- The phrase "Come to the best of deeds" retained in the call
- Given in the days of the Messenger, of Abu Bakr, and early in Umar's days
- Reason to keep: what God commanded and the Sunnah established is not cut away for a fear
- Remedy for the ignorant: teach them the truth they did not know

Column B: The call after the phrase was struck
- "Come to the best of deeds" removed from the adhan
- Struck in Umar's later days
- Reason given: "If the ignorant hear that prayer is the best of deeds, they will abandon jihad"
- Consequence feared here: most obligations would fall away if ignorance justified dropping them

Process flow. The order of the standing prayer itself, from consecration to the closing peace. The chapter's longest teaching, reduced to its transitions.

Start: Takbir of consecration, hands raised to the ears
  Opening supplication and Bismillah, then the Fatihah and a surah
  Bowing (ruku): "Glory be to my Lord, the Magnificent," three times
  Rising: "God hears the one who praises Him" / "Our Lord, to You be praise"
  First prostration (sujud): "Glory be to my Lord, the Most High," three times
  Sitting between the two prostrations
  Second prostration
  Rising to the next rak'ah, and repeating
  Sitting for the tashahhud, first and last
End: Two salutations, to the right and to the left

Annotated structure. The irreducible minimum of a prayer, the five bounds below which nothing remains. Whoever performs these and forgets the rest has still prayed.

Whole: The least by which a prayer is completed
- Bound 1: The opening takbir of consecration
- Bound 2: The standing, for whoever is able
- Bound 3: The bowing
- Bound 4: The prostration
- Bound 5: The sitting for the tashahhud
Reading: These five stand even under ignorance or forgetfulness. One who knows the remaining bounds may not drop any deliberately without voiding the prayer.

## Supplication and the Many Forms of Prayer

Contrast pair. The four doors, each opening its own answering door. God does not open the near door without opening the far one behind it. Read the columns as cause and guaranteed response.

Column A: The door the servant is given to open
- Door of supplication
- Door of seeking forgiveness
- Door of gratitude
- Door of deed

Column B: The door God opens in answer
- The door of response
- The door of pardon
- The door of increase
- The door of acceptance

Shared row: Each pairing is borne witness by the Book: "Call upon Me; I will answer you" [Ghafir 60]; "he will find God Forgiving, Merciful" [al-Nisa 110]; "If you are grateful, I will surely increase you" [Ibrahim 7].

Comparison matrix. The ninety-nine Names of God, counted where they fall across the surahs of the Book. Ali ibn al-Husayn taught that they are drawn from the Quran itself; this is the census, surah by surah.

Rows: A selection of the surahs bearing the Names
Columns: Count of Names | Sample Names named there
- The Opening of the Book: 5 | O Lord, O Most Merciful, O Ever Compassionate, O King
- Al-Baqarah: 26 | O Encompassing, O All-Powerful, O Wise, O Living, O Self-Subsisting
- Al 'Imran: 4 | O Ever-Standing, O Bestower, O Swift in Reckoning, O Aware
- Al-An'am: 5 | O Fashioner, O Subduer, O Able, O Subtle, O Ruler
- Hud: 7 | O Preserver, O Near, O Answerer, O Strong, O Loving
- Al-Hashr: 10 | O Holy, O Peace, O Overseer, O Creator, O Maker, O Fashioner
- Al-Hadid: 4 | O First, O Last, O Manifest, O Hidden
- Al-Ikhlas: 2 | O One, O Eternal Refuge
Total: ninety-nine Names in the Book, and with "By God" a hundred.

Process flow. The four intercessors, each request calling out its own witness. The servant petitions; heaven answers in kind; the chain returns the prayer upon the one who prayed.

Start: The servant petitions in the four forms
  "O God, I ask You for Paradise" → Paradise says: "Make him among my dwellers"
  "O God, deliver me from the Fire" → the Fire says: "Deliver him from me"
  "O God, wed me to the pure-eyed maidens" → they say: "Wed him to us"
  "O God, send prayers upon Muhammad and his family" → the angel at the Prophet's head reports it
End: God returns the blessing upon the one who sent it.

Contrast pair. What severs the prayer against what a servant may do within it and remain in prayer. The line separates speech-toward-people from speech-toward-God, and gross motion from slight.

Column A: Cuts off the prayer
- Ordinary speech to people
- Laughter aloud
- Turning the whole body from the qiblah
- Gross action, and deliberate finger-cracking as idle play
- Breaking of ablution

Column B: Does not cut off the prayer
- Supplication, glorification, takbir, recitation of the Quran
- A smile (though disliked)
- Returning a greeting within oneself or by a slight gesture
- Killing a snake, scorpion, or biting insect; scratching an unbearable itch
- Weeping from the fear of God (unforced)

Shared row: The better course in every permitted case is to leave it aside and turn wholly to the prayer.

## Facing Death: The Rites of the Departed

Process flow. The washing of the deceased, three washings in fixed order, mirroring the washing from major impurity. Each washing has its own additive.

Start: The body prepared, nakedness covered, belly gently pressed
  Washing 1: water with lote-tree leaf (the cleansing herb, soapwort)
  Washing 2: water with camphor
  Washing 3: pure water alone
End: The body dried, the washer keeping secret whatever he saw

> "There is no Muslim man who washes his Muslim brother without finding him foul, without mentioning any evil of him, then walks in his procession and prays over him, but that he emerges fragrant, cleansed of his sins." (The Messenger of God)

Comparison matrix. The five takbirs of the funeral prayer, and the two transmitted orderings of what is said within them. The frame is the same; the placement of the supplication for the deceased differs.

Rows: Takbir 1, Takbir 2, Takbir 3, Takbir 4, Takbir 5
Columns: First narration | Second narration (from Muhammad ibn Ali)
- Takbir 1: praise and extol God | praise and extol God
- Takbir 2: prayers upon the Prophet and his family | prayers upon the Prophet and his family
- Takbir 3: supplication for the deceased | supplication for the Imam
- Takbir 4: supplication for the believing men and women | supplication for the believers
- Takbir 5: prayers upon the Prophet, then the salutation | supplication for the deceased, then prayers and salutation

Note: over the hypocrite, the Messenger pronounced only four takbirs and did not supplicate.

Named-axis 2x2. Death for the believer against death for the disbeliever, and how each appears to onlookers against what is truly happening. The apparent ease or hardship inverts the reality.

Axes:
- Horizontal: The soul (Believer to Disbeliever)
- Vertical: The register (Appearance to onlookers, above; Hidden reality, below)

Quadrants:
- Believer + Appearance: death is made hard, drawn from the gentlest side, so people see hardship
- Believer + Reality: a lightening from God, raising him to a station in the Garden he had not reached
- Disbeliever + Appearance: the soul slips out in one wrench, so people see ease
- Disbeliever + Reality: a great punishment has already befallen him

Contrast pair. The two forms of the grave's inner chamber, either of which is permitted. The choice is between a side-recess and a central trench.

Column A: The lahd (niche)
- Form: a recess hollowed at the side toward the qiblah
- The body: laid within the recess
- The standing-place: the ground behind it, toward the rear, is left for the one preparing it

Column B: The darih (trench)
- Form: a hollow cut down the middle of the grave
- The body: laid in the central trench
- The standing-place: ledges left before it and behind it

Shared row: The grave is dug no deeper than three cubits. The body is laid on its right side toward the qiblah, the shroud-knots loosened.

## The Due of Wealth: Zakat

Annotated structure. The eight categories to whom the charity belongs, named in al-Tawbah 60, each defined. No single category may take the whole to the exclusion of the others.

Whole: "Charity is only for..." [al-Tawbah 60], eight rightful channels
- 1. The poor (al-faqir): the needy man who does not beg
- 2. The destitute (al-miskin): in greater hardship, the one who begs
- 3. Those who administer it: the collectors who receive it
- 4. Those whose hearts are to be reconciled: men of standing among their people, won toward obedience
- 5. The freeing of captives: manumission
- 6. Those in debt: people burdened with debts
- 7. In the cause of God: the struggle
- 8. The wayfarer: the stranger cut off from his means
Reading: The Imam is entrusted with setting each share in its place, for none but he knows the state of every class and the measure due at each season.

Comparison matrix. The zakat schedule of grazing camels, threshold by threshold. Below five, nothing; then sheep to twenty-four; then the graduated she-camels by age.

Rows: The rising count of camels
Columns: What becomes due
- 5 camels: 1 sheep
- 10 camels: 2 sheep
- 15 camels: 3 sheep
- 20 camels: 4 sheep
- 25 camels: a bint makhad (she-camel in her second year)
- 36 camels: a bint labun (she-camel in her third year)
- 46 camels: a hiqqah (she-camel in her fourth year, fit to bear)
- 61 camels: a jadha'a (she-camel in her fifth year)
- 76 camels: two bint labun
- 91 camels: two hiqqas
- 121 and beyond: for every 40, a bint labun; for every 50, a hiqqah

Timeline. The ages of the she-camel, the ladder of maturity that the zakat schedule climbs. The name marks the year the animal has entered and its readiness.

Year 2: bint makhad (mother has conceived again)
  → Year 3: ibn labun / bint labun (mother now giving milk)
    → Year 4: hiqqah (fit to bear and be covered)
      → Year 5: jadha'a (the full-grown offering)
Period band: the older the age owed, the larger the herd it answers for.

Contrast pair. The two precious metals, each with its own threshold and its own standing sum. Neither is joined to the other to reach a quorum.

Column A: Gold
- Nisab (threshold): 20 mithqals
- Held for: a full year in the owner's possession
- Due at threshold: half a mithqal
- On the excess: a quarter of a tenth

Column B: Silver
- Nisab (threshold): 200 dirhams
- Held for: a full year in the owner's possession
- Due at threshold: 5 dirhams
- On the excess: a quarter of a tenth

Shared row: No zakat until each reaches its own measure; the one is not joined to the other. A stratagem of spending or converting to escape the year's due does not lift the obligation.

## The Fast: Ramadan and the Discipline of Abstention

Hierarchy tree. The whole law of fasting sorted into four kinds, each branching into its numbered divisions. This is the chapter's own taxonomy, drawn from Majlis al-Bayan.

- Level 1: Fasting
  - Level 2: Obligatory (7 divisions)
    - Ramadan every year; two months for deliberate breaking; two months for killing a believer by mistake; two months for zihar; three days for a broken oath; ten days for tamattu' without an offering; the vow
  - Level 2: Sunnah (12 divisions)
    - I'tikaf; Sha'ban; a Thursday each month; the three white days; the fast of Dawud (a day on, a day off); the day of Arafah for the non-pilgrim; and others
  - Level 2: Forbidden (10 divisions)
    - Eid al-Fitr; Eid al-Nahr; the three days of Tashriq; wisal (joining days without breaking); perpetual fasting; fasting while menstruating; a vow of disobedience; fasting on a day of doubt
  - Level 2: Optional (12 divisions)
    - Rajab; days of Shawwal; nine days of Dhu al-Hijjah; Ashura by abstention; Muharram; Monday; and the fasts requiring permission (wife, bondsman, guest)

Process flow. The descending ladder of expiation for one who deliberately breaks a day of Ramadan. Each rung is reached only when the one above cannot be met.

Start: A day of Ramadan deliberately broken (or intercourse in its daytime)
  Rung 1: Free a slave
  If none is found → Rung 2: Fast two consecutive months
  If unable → Rung 3: Feed sixty poor persons
  If neither slave, nor charity, nor ability to fast → nothing is required
End: Together with all of this, the day itself is made up

> "God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity except with what He has given it."

Comparison matrix. Acts of the fasting person and what each incurs, the make-up alone or the make-up together with the full expiation. Intention and knowledge move a case up the scale.

Rows: The act during the daytime of Ramadan
Columns: What is owed
- Deliberate eating, drinking, or intercourse: make-up AND expiation
- Eating or drinking forgetfully: nothing (complete the fast)
- Dallying to emission, or coercing a sleeping wife: make-up AND expiation
- Deliberate vomiting: make-up (a day in its place)
- Vomit that overcame him: nothing
- Kohl whose taste reaches the throat, unknowingly: make-up only
- Kohl used deliberately knowing it reaches the throat: make-up AND expiation

Named-axis 2x2. The traveler and the zawal, the sun's decline as the hinge on which a day is fasted or made up. The rule turns on which side of noon he crosses his own threshold.

Axes:
- Horizontal: The movement (Leaving home, left; Returning home, right)
- Vertical: The timing relative to the sun's decline (Before decline, above; After decline, below)

Quadrants:
- Leaving + Before decline: he breaks the fast and must make up that day
- Leaving + After decline: he completes the fast and does not make it up
- Returning + Before decline (having tasted nothing): he completes the fast and does not make it up
- Returning + After decline: he must make it up

## The Journey to the House: Pilgrimage

Comparison matrix. The five stations of ihram fixed by the Messenger, each assigned to the people of a region. One must not pass a station without entering ihram, nor enter it before reaching one.

Rows: The people, by land
Columns: Their miqat (station of ihram)
- People of Madinah and their allies: al-Shajarah Mosque
- People of Syria and beyond: al-Juhfah
- People of Yemen: Yalamlam
- People of al-Ta'if: Qarn
- People of Najd: al-'Aqiq
Note: whoever's home lies nearer to Makkah than the stations enters ihram from his home.

Comparison matrix. The three forms of the pilgrimage, distinguished by whether an offering is driven and when ihram is released. Tamattu' is named the best and the form on which the Quran was revealed.

Rows: Tamattu', Qiran, Ifrad (Hajj alone)
Columns: Offering driven? | Release from ihram before Hajj? | For whom
- Tamattu' (Umrah joined to Hajj): no offering driven | yes, released after the Umrah's tawaf and sa'y, then re-enters ihram on the Day of Tarwiyah | those whose family is not present at the Sacred Mosque
- Qiran (Hajj joined to Umrah with an offering): offering driven | no, remains in ihram until the offering is sacrificed | the one who brings the hady
- Ifrad (Hajj alone): no offering required | enters ihram for Hajj only; no obligatory tawaf until return from Arafah | the people of the Haram, and others who choose it

> "Had I foreseen from my affair what I have now seen, I would not have driven the sacrificial animal, and I would have made it a tamattu'." (The Messenger of God)

Process flow. The rites of the Hajj across their days, from Tarwiyah to the departure from Mina. The spine of the chapter, reduced to its stations and transitions.

Start (8 Dhu al-Hijjah, Day of Tarwiyah): enter ihram for Hajj at the Sacred Mosque, go out to Mina, pass the night
  → (9th, Day of Arafah): stand at Arafah from the sun's decline until it sets, combining Zuhr and Asr
  → Pour forth to Muzdalifah after sunset: combine Maghrib and Isha, pass the night, stand at the Sacred Precinct
  → (10th, Day of Sacrifice) hasten through Muhassir to Mina: throw Jamrat al-Aqabah with seven pebbles
  → Slaughter the offering → shave the head
  → Go to the House: tawaf al-ifadah, sa'y between al-Safa and al-Marwah, then tawaf of the women
  → Return to Mina for the days of Tashriq: throw the three jamarat each day after the sun's decline
End: Depart from Mina, hastening in two days or delaying, for the one who fears God

Process flow. The progressive release from ihram, the prohibitions falling away in three stages. Each rite unlocks a further set of what was forbidden.

Start: Full ihram, all forbidden
  Stage 1, throw Jamrat al-Aqabah: everything becomes lawful EXCEPT hunting, women, perfume, and dwelling outside Mina
  Stage 2, perform tawaf al-ifadah and its sa'y: everything becomes lawful EXCEPT women
  Stage 3, perform tawaf al-nisa (the tawaf of the women): women become lawful
End: The pilgrim is wholly released from what his consecration had bound

## The Sword and the State: Jihad, Governance, and Justice

Process flow. The graded call that must precede the fighting, moving from the highest invitation down to battle only when each is refused. The sword is the last stage, not the first.

Start: An army or detachment dispatched, "in the name of God, upon the religion of the Messenger"
  Step 1: Invite them to bear witness that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His Messenger
  If they answer → they are brothers in religion; then invite them to migrate to the land of the emigrants
  If they decline migration → they are as the bedouin of the Muslims, under God's judgment, with no share in the spoils
  Step 2 (if they refuse Islam, being People of the Book): call them to pay the jizyah from a willing hand while subdued
  If they answer → accept it and hold your hand from them
End (if they refuse all): seek the help of God and fight them, harming no child, woman, or aged man who does not fight

Comparison matrix. The three enemies Ali was commanded to fight, each named, identified, and glossed. The single verse of al-Hujurat is worked out in history.

Rows: The three groups Ali named
Columns: Their name | Who they were | Meaning of the name
- The breakers of the covenant (al-nakithun): the people of Basra, Companions of the Camel | those who broke their pledge
- The deviators (al-qasitun): the people of Syria (Mu'awiya's party) | those who swerved from justice
- The renegades (al-mariqun): the Kharijites | those who "pass out of the religion as the arrow passes through the game"

> "I was commanded to fight the breakers of the covenant, the deviators, and the renegades; the Messenger of God commanded me to that." (Ali)

Comparison matrix. The jizyah levied on the free adult men of the covenanted people, ranked in three tiers by means. It is lifted from those who cannot bear it.

Rows: The three tiers of the dhimmi men
Columns: Who they are | Annual jizyah
- Highest tier: the people of ease and abundance | 48 dirhams
- Middle tier: those of middling means | 24 dirhams
- Lowest tier: those of the least means | 12 dirhams
Not levied upon: children, slaves, women, and one fallen into poverty and infirmity, "for God burdens no soul beyond its capacity" [al-Baqarah 286].

Annotated structure. The division of the spoils after a fighting victory, the whole apportioned in fixed shares. The fifth is lifted first; the rest is split between horse and foot.

Whole: The booty taken by fighting, divided
- First cut: the commander may take one choice thing for himself, and give from the whole for excellence of standing
- The fifth (khums): taken out for the Imam
- The remaining four-fifths: divided among those who fought with person and wealth
  - The horseman: two shares
  - The foot soldier: one share
Reading: What is taken without fighting, spurring neither horse nor camel, is fay' and belongs wholly to the Imam [al-Hashr 6-7], not to this division.
