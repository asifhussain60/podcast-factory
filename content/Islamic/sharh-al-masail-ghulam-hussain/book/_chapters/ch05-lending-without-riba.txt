# Lending Without Riba

God Most High says, and He is the Lord of charity, the One who grants that a loan made with the sincere intention of earning reward in the Hereafter is charity:

⟪ar:وَمَا آتَيْتُم مِّن رِّبًا لِّيَرْبُوَ فِي أَمْوَالِ النَّاسِ فَلَا يَرْبُو عِندَ اللَّهِ وَمَا آتَيْتُم مِّن زَكَاةٍ تُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَ اللَّهِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُضْعِفُونَ⟫

"Whatever you give as riba so that it may increase through other people's wealth does not increase with God; but whatever you give in zakah, seeking God's face, it is they who will receive manifold reward."

The one who gives a loan seeking the benefit of the Hereafter will have it multiplied for him in the next life. It is also said: whoever wishes to cultivate the harvest of the Hereafter, We shall increase it for him; and whoever wishes to cultivate the harvest of this world, We give him of it, though he will have no share in the reward of the Hereafter. It is said as well: whatever God accepts with goodwill, the reward of the Hereafter is doubled for him, meaning it is multiplied in the recompense of the next life.

Riba means increase. Not every increase, however, is forbidden, as for example increases in sales transactions and similar dealings. Rather, riba in the technical sense of Islamic law refers specifically to the increase that accrues on a loan when the lender's intention is to gain a worldly benefit, and when the principal is returned to him along with that increase. The blessings of God descend only upon the one whose intention in lending is sound.

A loan therefore falls into two categories: one where the lender's purpose is worldly benefit, and one where the lender's purpose is the reward of the Hereafter. Each category depends entirely on the intention of the one who lends, for actions are judged by intentions, and both categories fall within the domain of legally regulated transactions. A loan intended for worldly benefit, and extended on that basis, means that if the borrower is in a position to repay, the lender has the right to either reduce or excuse the repayment, or to increase it in terms of the time allowed and reduce the principal slightly or substantially, or to forgive the debt entirely, and there is reward in that.

As for a loan that is intended for the reward of the Hereafter, that reward is eighteen-fold for maintaining the bond of brotherhood with a fellow believer, and twenty-four-fold for maintaining the bond of kinship, the latter extending one's lifespan, as is mentioned in the chapter on the virtue of the Prophet's intercession. The owner of such a loan does not have the right to benefit from the principal in any way, whether small or great, or to impose any condition that burdens the principal. The reason is that God Most High has designated every loan made purely for His sake as an act of devotion. Every worldly benefit derived from it is therefore forbidden, and transactions were conducted among the Muslims for God's sake, that is, as a legitimate loan. Sometimes, even when the time for repayment arrives and the borrower is in hardship, the lender should cancel the arrangement for that period, accept the principal as agreed, and if the borrower is genuinely and visibly in hardship, grant him additional time and even remit his principal, as was done in the cases of 'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib and 'Uthman ibn 'Affan.

A Muslim should be guaranteed and conditional upon being free of betrayal, for the trustworthy person is like this. When the agreed time came, al-Maslarana said: if you take your right in full, you bring ruin upon these wretched children; if you take half and leave the other half for my children, I shall give you the following season's harvest at twice the other half, which would be satisfactory. When the agreed time came and they demanded repayment, the Muslim man felt no anxiety, and the matter reached the Messenger of God, (ع), and the following was revealed:

⟪ar:يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَذَرُوا مَا بَقِيَ مِنَ الرِّبَا إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ فَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا فَأْذَنُوا بِحَرْبٍ مِّنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ وَإِن تُبْتُمْ فَلَكُمْ رُءُوسُ أَمْوَالِكُمْ لَا تَظْلِمُونَ وَلَا تُظْلَمُونَ وَإِن كَانَ ذُو عُسْرَةٍ فَنَظِرَةٌ إِلَىٰ مَيْسَرَةٍ وَأَن تَصَدَّقُوا خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ إِن كُنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ⟫

"O you who believe, fear God and give up whatever riba remains outstanding, if you are truly believers. If you do not, then be warned of war from God and His Messenger. But if you repent, you shall have your principal, do no wrong and you will not be wronged. If the debtor is in hardship, grant him time until he is at ease; and if you remit it as charity, that is better for you, if only you knew."

The verse then says: O people of the Helpers, may God save your families and the benefit you have been receiving, for the one who is unable to fulfill that obligation, since what is morally intended is the lender's becoming a beloved of God and His Messenger. In the caliphate of God and His Messenger, if the dispute is abandoned and only the principal of the loan is returned, the lenders will not be wronging the borrower, nor will they be wronged by forgoing the increase. The one pressed for repayment who cannot pay the principal should be given the status of those granted respite. To remit the entire loan is better, and in it lies the secret of brotherhood. This question has been confused for most of the jurists. May God help us and them to grasp the intended meaning.

Both categories of loan were in use among the Arabs, Christians, and Jews and their associates before the advent of Islam, and after it in trading before the conquest of Mecca. Their transactions were in the grip of documented contracts, and they oppressed Muslims, particularly in the matter of available cash, giving them a paltry sum and taking from them enormous amounts in return, buying and selling at usurious rates until the situation became straitened for Muslims and they had no stratagem to escape it. The Prophet was then waiting for divine guidance in these matters, and God in His wisdom opened the conquest of Mecca. In the eighth year the strength of the Muslims increased and their capability in resisting exploitation grew. Then in the ninth year it was revealed:

⟪ar:إِنَّمَا الْمُشْرِكُونَ نَجَسٌ فَلَا يَقْرَبُوا الْمَسْجِدَ الْحَرَامَ بَعْدَ عَامِهِمْ هَٰذَا وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ عَيْلَةً فَسَوْفَ يُغْنِيكُمُ اللَّهُ مِن فَضْلِهِ إِن شَاءَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ⟫

"The polytheists are indeed impure, so let them not approach the Sacred Mosque after this year of theirs. If you fear poverty, God will enrich you from His bounty if He wills, God is All-Knowing, All-Wise."

'Ali (ع) accordingly conveyed from the Prophet the message of disavowal for the chapter of repentance, delivering the warning to the polytheists. Those who had passed through the Muslims to trade, taking grain and other goods at their doorstep and bringing money in from outside the faith, continued in exchange with the Muslims, from the nearest port to Mecca, until trade was regulated under Muslim authority and command. The Muslims were now capable of offering their money from that year onward and of dealing in both categories, the worldly and the other-worldly. So they dealt with Muslims according to the reward of the Hereafter, and with buyers and sellers according to worldly benefit. Those who dealt according to worldly benefit were to forgive the increase in cases of hardship and to stop pressing for loan principals in accordance with God's command, just as they had been strengthened by brotherhood and mutual generosity. Others were encouraged to account for their shares and transactions in a spirit of brotherhood, which in turn grew.

Dealings with non-Muslims were largely abandoned by those who prized their honor. The precedent of how polytheists treated the early community became known, and it was recognized that dealing with polytheists and others exposed the weak to a kind of helplessness. One should not draw water in the manner the mushrik walks, nor join with all of them indiscriminately. The current position is that such dealings are not forbidden by the Sharia outright. If the color of gold is not generally forbidden, scholars explain that in the face of clear evidence, or according to one's legal school, a thing may be forbidden to a person who does not hold that school's ruling. The polytheists are not under the governance of Islam, so charging them a fare is invalid, since there is no basis for it within their legal school, as the Shaykh stated. People other than the people of God are thereby lost to it.

The narration that has passed with a general benefit is that Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) said that a man should not partner with a dhimmi, nor count goods for him, nor deposit with him, nor cultivate affection for him, for this is not the highest conduct familiar to the Muslims. In the past this prohibition was understood.

As for the final point sworn to: gold for gold, silver for silver, barley for barley, and date for date, and a full financial measure means like for like; and whoever increases or takes more, both the one who takes and the one who gives in such a transaction are in sin. This exchange then became a practice that spread in certain regions. What exists today in the lands of Yemen is that people exchange things with one another in some circumstances, and when the road offers no alternative, people manage their needs, as in India and other countries, through the prevalence of exchange. For this reason, the well-known reply regarding interest was given in this context.

As for orphans: attend to them until they reach the age of marriage; if they are found to be of sound judgment, their property is handed over to them. It was said from Mawlana Jad, who said: if the orphan's guardian reads the Quran, is of discerning mind, and the messenger from him has paid him his money, then if the orphan lacks the voice or mental capacity to document his own affairs, the guardian has an inclination to act for him and spend on him with kindness, subject to the ruler. If the behavior of one who spends another's money in the wrong place is to be prohibited, woe to the workers of the institution who manage it and declare its necessities. This prohibition applies to an orphan who is not foolish; a profligate man, however, is called restrained.

As for a merchant who has debts that consume his wealth or increase beyond it: the trusted agent's knowledge is established against him, and the claim is established against him as well, so it must be pressed against him and he must receive his money, so that he may give. No portion of his own money will be cursed. The house in which he lived was a dwelling, rented out by a tenant who remained in it, then sworn by the mouth of the court. Their loans are treated as good money, and the wife's public account is held against her with her consent. If he finds something set aside especially, let him seek it, for in the eyes of the rulers it was cheap to grant or else to imprison him with the money. Then the ruler declared his bankruptcy famous, so that no one loses his money through lending to him. This declaration is called bankruptcy, and the one who is prohibited and made famous for it is bankrupt. If, however, the person was still dealing with people, even though his debts exceeded his assets, and no one had yet declared him bankrupt, then it is permissible for him to give to his children or others. But if it becomes clear to the judge that he did this in corrupt intent, the judge should invalidate those transactions.

The issue is not one of Sa'd al-Qadi and the ruler al-Muhsin al-Mughlis. It concerns the demand of the debt claim, but with a cautious view. The issue of a man who has a debt and whose assets are fruitful for men: the real-estate administration thereafter, and those who have not entered into justice; he is not the first of those to be deterred from the first hundred.

When the man used to deal with people, and they sought from him the benefit of their money because he was occupied buying and selling among them, but those to whom he was most indebted knew that he was prepared to supply their needs, he gave a debt after due consideration. The first of those who receive payment are those with the greatest claim. A bankrupt person is not prevented from marrying another wife and other such things, upon an offer of dirhams; riding an animal with a wife named Fullah is the Shaykh's position.
