# What Outlives You: Charity, Endowment, and Inheritance

Wealth passes out of a person's hands in three ways that reach beyond the span of his life: what he gives away while he lives, what he ties up so that it can never be given away again, and what he leaves behind for others to divide. Each has its own law, and each is treated in turn.

God Almighty said:

> مَثَلُ الَّذِينَ يُنْفِقُونَ أَمْوَالَهُمْ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ حَبَّةٍ أَنْبَتَتْ سَبْعَ سَنَابِلَ فِي كُلِّ سُنْبُلَةٍ مِائَةُ حَبَّةٍ وَاللَّهُ يُضَاعِفُ لِمَنْ يَشَاءُ وَاللَّهُ وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ

"The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of God is like a grain that sprouts seven ears, in every ear a hundred grains. And God multiplies for whom He wills, and God is Vast and Knowing."

And He said:

> الَّذِينَ يُنْفِقُونَ أَمْوَالَهُمْ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ لَا يُتْبِعُونَ مَا أَنْفَقُوا مَنًّا وَلَا أَذًى لَهُمْ أَجْرُهُمْ عِنْدَ رَبِّهِمْ وَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

"Those who spend their wealth in the way of God and then do not follow what they have spent with reminder of it or with harm, theirs is their reward with their Lord, and no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve."

The meaning is that the spending must be for the sake of God, unaccompanied by the reminder of the favor and unaccompanied by injury to the one who received it.

Concerning the same matter it is related:

> الْخَلْقُ عِيَالُ اللَّهِ، أَحَبُّهُمْ إِلَيْهِ أَنْفَعُهُمْ لِعِيَالِهِ وَأَوْسَعُهُمْ خُلُقًا، مَنْ سَعَى فِي مَعَاوَنَةِ أَخِيهِ كَانَ أَحَبَّ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ مِنْ أَنْ يَعْتَكِفَ شَهْرَيْنِ

"Creation is God's household; the most beloved of them to Him is the one most useful to His household and the most generous of them in character. Whoever exerts himself in the aid of his brother is more beloved to God than one who keeps a retreat of two months."

Ali (ع) said that the doer of good acts for the benefit of another without seeking anything in return, because he is certain that he does it only for the sake of God; therefore he asks acknowledgment from no one. And he said that the doer of good begins with his own household and his near kin and his brethren, then extends beyond them; and if anyone offers him a gift, he receives it graciously and does not refuse it, even if that thing is of no use to him. He places it where people may benefit from it, and compensates the giver with something, as God Almighty said:

> وَإِذَا حُيِّيتُمْ بِتَحِيَّةٍ فَحَيُّوا بِأَحْسَنَ مِنْهَا أَوْ مِثْلَهَا

"And when you are greeted with a greeting, greet with what is better than it, or return it."

Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) said:

> لَيْسَ شَيْءٌ أَفْضَلَ مِنَ الْخَيْرِ إِلَّا ثَوَابَهُ

"There is nothing more excellent than good, except its reward."

Preferring others over oneself is among the qualities of the people of faith. When God intends good for a servant, He joins to the will the power to act as well. The doer of good among the people of faith is counted among those who do good; the people of good works in this world are the people of good things in the Hereafter, and they are the foremost to the Garden. The doer of good is he who deals openly with his brethren and with goodness, aids them in their affairs, and benefits them.

Our master al-Qadi al-Nu'man said that whatever has come in the narrations concerning the merits of service and its reward is what was done for God and for the face of God, or in the benefit of the religion and its people, with no worldly purpose in it.

From Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) it is related that he said: nothing continues for a man after his death from reward except three things: a charity he set flowing during his lifetime, or a righteous child who prays for him, or a good practice he established, for it works on after his death.

God Almighty said:

> يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تُبْطِلُوا صَدَقَاتِكُمْ بِالْمَنِّ وَالْأَذَى كَالَّذِي يُنْفِقُ مَالَهُ رِئَاءَ النَّاسِ وَلَا يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ فَمَثَلُهُ كَمَثَلِ صَفْوَانٍ عَلَيْهِ تُرَابٌ فَأَصَابَهُ وَابِلٌ فَتَرَكَهُ صَلْدًا

"O you who believe, do not annul your charities by reminder of them and by injury, like one who spends his wealth to be seen of people and does not believe in God and the Last Day. His likeness is that of a smooth rock upon which is dust, and a heavy rain strikes it and leaves it bare."

The difference between goodness and charity is this: goodness is general, whether in word or in deed, in any matter whatever, whether for an hour or for a short or a long period. Charity is what is set for a known term, short or long, whether in lands, in property, in livestock, or in other things, and all of it is written down and made clear.

Charity is divided into two: the gift and the endowment. The gift is the removal of a thing from one man's ownership into the ownership of another, either for a known term or in perpetuity, and the giver has no right of return before the completion of the term. The endowment is the holding back of an estate, or of something a man was accustomed to give, so that after his death it is withheld from the people of the inheritance. The founder may benefit from what he has endowed so long as he lives, since he is the one who established it, provided it is of the class of real property rather than a thing exposed to loss, for in a perishable thing there is fear of diminution for those to whom it is endowed.

Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) said: whoever gives a gift in perpetuity, intending by it the face of God and the abode of the Hereafter and the joining of kinship, written, severed, and made plain, has no return to it. And whoever gives a gift for a known term, whether short or long, has the right of return after the completion of that term, if the thing still stands; but if it has perished, he has no such right.

He was asked about the lifelong grant, al-ʿumrā, and about the right of dwelling, al-suknā, and he said that people are held in that to their own stipulations. The dwelling-right, the lifelong grant, and al-ruqbā are of one rank, save that the condition distinguishes between them. The dwelling-right is that a man settles one man in his house, or several, whether women or men, for a known term, and permits him that without compensation. A grant of this kind is also called by the name of charity, even though it is not from the estate, as has come in the reports that the Messenger of God gave in charity from his wealth, making it an endowment, and used to spend from it upon his guests. The lifelong grant is that he settles him there for the whole of his life, or of his descendants also. Al-ruqbā is that he settles him there until one of the two dies; and when one of them dies, the ruling of al-ruqbā lapses with his death and the house returns to its people, either to the owner of the house or to whoever inherits from him.

As for what is related from the Messenger of God:

> إِنَّ الَّذِي يَتَصَدَّقُ ثُمَّ يَرْجِعُ كَالَّذِي يَقِيءُ ثُمَّ يَرْجِعُ فِي قَيْئِهِ

"The one who gives charity and then takes it back is like the one who vomits and then returns to his vomit."

That was when writing was not yet in common use and the giver did not make his intention plain, whether the charity was in perpetuity or for a specified time. So the writing is what makes it plain for him, that nothing be done contrary to his intention.

Al-Sadiq (ع) was asked about a man who gave in charity a share held in common, and he said it is valid. He was asked about charity before it is taken into possession, and he said: if the one given the charity accepted it, and it was accepted on his behalf if he was a child, it is valid, whether it was taken into possession or not; but if it was not accepted, it is nothing until it is accepted. He was asked about charity of undivided property, and he said it is valid, and it is taken into possession as such property is taken into possession.

Endowment is permissible in everything the founder wishes to dedicate, whether livestock, land, a house, jewelry, clothing, a sum of money, or a usufruct, so that the one endowed to may benefit from it, whether one person or many, whether from the people of the inheritance or from others, and whether the endowment is equal among them or with distinctions, whether they are men or women or mixed. The writing distinguishes all of that, so that nothing in it falls into doubt. If the founder stipulates in the whole of it or in part of it that, should he have need of something from it, he may take from it what he requires, that right is his; only such an endowment does not remain an endowment except during his lifetime, and after his death it is divided among the people of the inheritance.

The founder may administer the endowment himself, or appoint another over it, whether from his relatives or from others. The administrator may take the fee of administration for himself if he has need of it, just as he pays out to another a small fixed amount; there is no harm in that. The administrator may, when he sees that an animal has wasted away, or a garment has worn out, or jewelry or a house has fallen into ruin, and there are beneficiaries in it, act with their consent and exchange it for what brings them ease from the value of those things, without taking on debt against them. If the beneficiaries themselves contribute to repairing it as a charity from their own means, the administrator may accept it.

If the founder specified the channels of benefit from the endowed sum in a fixed amount and at a fixed time, and stipulated an order, that order is observed. If the benefit runs out while some of the channels remain unsatisfied, there is no harm in it; otherwise the benefit is divided among those channels equally or with distinctions, out of that benefit, according to the terms of the endowment.

Recorded here, for greater clarity in this matter, is the testament of our master Ali ibn Abi Talib (ع), as it is mentioned in the *Daʿāʾim*, in summary. This is what he enjoined and endowed and settled upon his property:

> This is what Ali ibn Abi Talib enjoined, seeking the face of God: what belongs to me at Yanbuʿ of property, and what is known to be mine of it and what lies around it, is charity, except that Riyāḥ and Abū Nayzar and Jubayr are free, no one has any way against them, and they are my freedmen who work in the property for five years, and in it is their maintenance and their sustenance and the sustenance of their families. And with that, what belongs to me in Wādī al-Qurā, a third of it, and the property of Fatima and its servants, is charity. And what belongs to me at Daymah and its people is charity, except that Zurayq has for him the like of what I have written for his companions. And what belongs to me at Udhayna and its people is charity. And what I have written of these my properties is a binding, irrevocable charity, so that, whether I live or die, it is spent in every expenditure by which the face of God is sought, in the way of God and for His face, and upon those of kinship from Banu Hashim and Banu Abd al-Muttalib, the near and the far. And in charge of it after that is al-Hasan ibn Ali, eating from it in the proper manner and spending it where God shows him, in what is lawful and made lawful, no blame upon him in it. If he wishes to make property of the charity serve in place of other property, he may do so, no blame upon him in it. If he wishes to sell a portion of the property and discharge a debt with it, he may do so if he wills, no blame upon him in it. And the children of Ali and their property revert to al-Hasan after him. And if al-Hasan ibn Ali has any dwelling other than the house of charity and it seems good to him to sell it, let him sell it if he wills, no blame upon him in it; and if he sells it, then its price is in three parts: a third he places in the way of God, and a third among Banu Hashim, and a third among Banu Abi Talib, placing it among them where God shows him. And if something befalls al-Hasan while al-Husayn is living, then it passes to al-Husayn ibn Ali, and al-Husayn ibn Ali does in it the like of what I commanded al-Hasan, and he has the like of what I have written for al-Hasan and upon him is the like of what is upon al-Hasan. And what belongs to the sons of Fatima of my charity is the like of what belongs to the sons of Ali. And I have appointed what I have appointed to the sons of Fatima seeking the face of God, and honoring the sanctity of Muhammad, and magnifying it and ennobling it and being well pleased with it. And if something befalls al-Hasan and al-Husayn, then the offspring of the survivor of the two shall look into that.

The remainder of the testament is as it is recorded.

God Almighty said:

> كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ إِنْ تَرَكَ خَيْرًا الْوَصِيَّةُ لِلْوَالِدَيْنِ وَالْأَقْرَبِينَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ حَقًّا عَلَى الْمُتَّقِينَ

"It is prescribed for you, when death approaches one of you and he leaves behind wealth, that he make a bequest for parents and near relatives in the proper manner, a duty upon the God-fearing."

And He said:

> يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا شَهَادَةُ بَيْنِكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ حِينَ الْوَصِيَّةِ اثْنَانِ ذَوَا عَدْلٍ مِنْكُمْ أَوْ آخَرَانِ مِنْ غَيْرِكُمْ

"O you who believe, testimony among you when death approaches one of you, at the time of bequest, shall be by two just men from among you, or two others from outside."

And it is related:

> لَيْسَ يَنْبَغِي لِلْمُسْلِمِ أَنْ يَبِيتَ لَيْلَةً إِلَّا وَوَصِيَّةٌ مَكْتُوبَةٌ عِنْدَ رَأْسِهِ

"It is not fitting for a Muslim to pass a night except that a written bequest is at his head."

And Abu Jaʿfar (ع) said: الْوَصِيَّةُ حَقٌّ عَلَى كُلِّ مُسْلِمٍ — "The bequest is a duty upon every Muslim."

The bequest is applied to the intentions a person wishes to have carried out after his death. For a person of full age, whether man or woman, is owner of his possessions and his wealth, to dispose of during his lifetime as he wishes: he may give to whom he wishes, or consume it, or let it go. But when death comes, everything he leaves is called the estate, and its owner has no more than the third; as for the two thirds, he has no power over them apart from the heirs. If the heirs consent, being of sound mind, then what he gave in his lifetime to whomever he wished, of whatever he wished, or according to the Sharia, is permissible. And if the heirs wish to divide the two thirds of the estate of the deceased among themselves by mutual agreement rather than by the division of the Sharia, that is theirs.

Everything he bequeathed in health of state or in the illness of his death, whether his senses were standing or not, or by a sign from him, is carried out from his estate to the extent that it may be carried out, even if he divides the whole of it among those who inherit from him or among others. If he acknowledges a loan to someone during the illness of his death, it is not acted upon until it is established; and the one to whom he acknowledged it is regarded, and the heirs may permit it.

It is related from the Prophet: إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ رَضِيَ بِالْخُمُسِ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ — "God, mighty and majestic, was content with the fifth from His servants." And he said: الْخُمُسُ اقْتِصَادٌ وَالثُّلُثُ حَيْفٌ — "The fifth is moderation and the third is excess," and that a bequest of a fourth is more beloved to him than a bequest of a third. Our master al-Qadi al-Nuʿman said that this is a matter of preference in what they related from him, and a bequest of a third is permissible.

Nothing removes the validity of the bequest except the loss of the testator's reason, or his apostasy, or squandering, or breach of trust. The executor of an executor is himself an executor. If the executor is permitted to trade with the property of the young child, that is his, and there is no liability upon him in it; if he stipulated for himself a portion of the profit, it is according to his stipulation. But if he traded when that was not made lawful to him in the bequest, he is liable for what is lost of the property, and the profit belongs to the orphan.

God Almighty said:

> يُوصِيكُمُ اللَّهُ فِي أَوْلَادِكُمْ لِلذَّكَرِ مِثْلُ حَظِّ الْأُنْثَيَيْنِ فَإِنْ كُنَّ نِسَاءً فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ فَلَهُنَّ ثُلُثَا مَا تَرَكَ وَإِنْ كَانَتْ وَاحِدَةً فَلَهَا النِّصْفُ وَلِأَبَوَيْهِ لِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ مِمَّا تَرَكَ إِنْ كَانَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ فَإِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَوَرِثَهُ أَبَوَاهُ فَلِأُمِّهِ الثُّلُثُ فَإِنْ كَانَ لَهُ إِخْوَةٌ فَلِأُمِّهِ السُّدُسُ مِنْ بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِي بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ آبَاؤُكُمْ وَأَبْنَاؤُكُمْ لَا تَدْرُونَ أَيُّهُمْ أَقْرَبُ لَكُمْ نَفْعًا فَرِيضَةً مِنَ اللَّهِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيمًا حَكِيمًا. وَلَكُمْ نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ أَزْوَاجُكُمْ إِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُنَّ وَلَدٌ فَإِنْ كَانَ لَهُنَّ وَلَدٌ فَلَكُمُ الرُّبُعُ مِمَّا تَرَكْنَ مِنْ بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِينَ بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ وَلَهُنَّ الرُّبُعُ مِمَّا تَرَكْتُمْ إِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ لَكُمْ وَلَدٌ فَإِنْ كَانَ لَكُمْ وَلَدٌ فَلَهُنَّ الثُّمُنُ مِمَّا تَرَكْتُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ تُوصُونَ بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ وَإِنْ كَانَ رَجُلٌ يُورَثُ كَلَالَةً أَوِ امْرَأَةٌ وَلَهُ أَخٌ أَوْ أُخْتٌ فَلِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ فَإِنْ كَانُوا أَكْثَرَ مِنْ ذَلِكَ فَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ فِي الثُّلُثِ مِنْ بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصَى بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ غَيْرَ مُضَارٍّ وَصِيَّةً مِنَ اللَّهِ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَلِيمٌ

"God enjoins you concerning your children: for the male the like of the portion of two females. If they are women, more than two, theirs is two thirds of what he left; and if she is one, hers is the half. And to his parents, to each one of them a sixth of what he left, if he had a child. If he had no child and his parents inherit from him, then to his mother a third; and if he had brothers, then to his mother a sixth, after a bequest he may have made or a debt. Your fathers and your sons, you do not know which of them is nearer to you in benefit. An ordinance from God; indeed God is Knowing, Wise. And yours is half of what your wives leave if they have no child; but if they have a child, then yours is a fourth of what they leave, after a bequest they may have made or a debt. And theirs is a fourth of what you leave if you have no child; but if you have a child, then theirs is an eighth of what you leave, after a bequest you may have made or a debt. And if a man or a woman is inherited from without direct heirs and he has a brother or a sister, then to each one of them a sixth; but if they are more than that, they are partners in the third, after a bequest made or a debt, without injury. A charge from God, and God is Knowing, Forbearing."

The order of settlement is therefore as follows. First there is taken out from the property of the deceased the cost of the shroud and the burial, and then the debt, if any debt lies upon him toward anyone. After that, what remains of his property is examined, and there is taken out from the third, which is his own share, the ransom of his fast and his pilgrimage if either remained upon him. Then his bequest is carried out, if he bequeathed anything of good works or otherwise, if the third suffices for all of that; and if it does not, there is paid from it what it covers, and beyond that nothing is due from him. If he had already discharged the ransom and the obligation and bequeathed nothing, then the third is not taken out at all, but the whole is divided among those who inherit from him.

Whoever is near to the deceased in the reckoning of the Sharia is his heir; the inheritance is his outright, without any levy taken from it. The heirs have been set out in five classes according to nearness and distance to the deceased. If relatives of the first class are found, there is no need to look to the classes beyond it; and likewise, if the second is found, there is no need to look to the third, and so on in order.

The classes are set out in tables. The first class comprises the children and the children of children, the father and the mother, and one of the two spouses. Where there are children of children, the son's son stands in the place of the son, and the son's daughter and the daughter's children stand in the place of the daughter; and where the mother is absent, her mother stands in her place. The second class comprises the father, the mother, and the brothers and sisters, with the shares of the spouse deducted first. The third class comprises the brothers and sisters and their children, whether of both parents, of the father, or of the mother, the male taking the portion of two females, and this division likewise made after the deduction of the share of the husband or the wife. The fourth class comprises the grandfather and grandmother together with the brothers, sisters, and their children, and here too the division follows the deduction of the share of the husband or the wife. The fifth class comprises the paternal uncle and the paternal aunt, the maternal uncle and the maternal aunt, together with the grandfather and grandmother; those of the father's side and those of the mother's side each take their prescribed portions, and in these tables also the share of the husband or the wife is taken out first. In every case the children of each group of brothers take the share of their father, and the children of each group of sisters take the share of their mother.

Finally there is set out the table of what is forbidden in marriage and in combining wives. Forbidden forever are the mother, the maternal aunt, the paternal aunt, the daughter of the brother, the daughter of the sister, and the sister. Forbidden likewise by suckling are the milk-mother, the milk-sister, the milk-aunt, the daughter of the milk-brother and of the milk-sister, and those of like standing, however far the line descends, for what is forbidden by lineage is forbidden by suckling. Forbidden also are the mother of the wife, and the daughters of the wife where the marriage has been consummated, and the wife of the father. These prohibitions are set out in the table, together with those which hold only under a condition, so that the lawful may be distinguished from the forbidden and no doubt remain.
