# Milk, Maintenance, and the Untying of a Marriage

On Breastfeeding

God Almighty said:

> وَإِنْ أَرَدْتُمْ أَنْ تَسْتَرْضِعُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا سَلَّمْتُمْ مَا آتَيْتُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

"If you wish to have your children breastfed, there is no blame on you, provided you pay what you have promised in a fair manner."

Parents may arrange for their children to be nursed by whoever is willing. A mother may say: "I will nurse him, my brother, for I am satisfied."

And God Almighty said:

> وَالْوَالِدَاتُ يُرْضِعْنَ أَوْلَادَهُنَّ حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ

"And mothers shall nurse their children two complete years, for whoever wishes to complete the nursing."

And He said:

> وَأُمَّهَاتُكُمُ اللَّاتِي أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ وَأَخَوَاتُكُمْ مِنَ الرَّضَاعَةِ

"And your mothers who have nursed you, and your sisters through nursing."

The hadith establishes that breastfeeding creates the same prohibitions as birth:

> يَحْرُمُ مِنَ الرَّضَاعَةِ مَا يَحْرُمُ مِنَ النَّسَبِ

"What is forbidden through nursing is what is forbidden through lineage."

The ruling of breastfeeding applies in a specific manner. The language of the verse means that if breastfeeding takes place with the consent of the one responsible for the newborn, whether wealthy or not, it carries legal consequences.

The relevant rulings are as follows:

(1) If a woman breastfeeds a child without the permission of the one responsible for him.

(2) If the permission is granted by him, the ruling falls on the one who pays for it, whether the milk reaches the child through his ears or nose.

(3) If the woman's milk has increased in her breast, causing her discomfort, and a milk vessel is lost, or what was placed in the child's mouth or given to a cousin is lost, within the two-year period, even with a single swallow, and the newborn inevitably swallows the milk.

In the first case, breastfeeding does not require the consent of the one responsible. In the second case, the breastfeeding is unintentional and therefore does not establish a prohibition. If the breastfeeding is done through a legitimate means, that is, through a proper nursing arrangement, then such breastfeeding does not establish prohibited degrees of kinship, even if the milk reaches the child by dripping through the nose or ear, or by tube in the case of injury to the throat, or by injection.

The second principle drawn from the verse is that the limit of permissible nursing is two full years from the day the child is born, calculated as complete months. All who breastfeed the child within this period fall under the relevant prohibition. If the woman has never given birth or is infertile, it sometimes happens that milk appears from her, even a small amount, but the condition for the prohibition to apply is that the one who nurses must not be born of unlawful relations. If breastfeeding is desired after the child's bones have developed further, this falls around a specific threshold, and such breastfeeding, when undertaken out of necessity, is permitted. The Imam, after a period of illness, is not considered to be nursing in a lawful manner under these circumstances.

Concerning the milk of the sire, Ali (ع) said: لَبَنُ الْفَحْلِ يُحَرِّمُ, "the milk of the sire creates prohibition." Milk-kinship prohibits marriage: those who share milk from a single woman become forbidden to one another as foster-siblings. If a man has multiple wives and a boy is nursed by one of them, the boy becomes the milk-son of that wife only, not of the other wives. Yet all of those co-wives are forbidden to one another's milk-children by virtue of the shared household bond. Even if the husband himself did not intend to establish milk-kinship, the suckling alone is sufficient to create the prohibition.

If a single woman nurses multiple children, whether they were brought to her from infancy or not, all of those children become milk-siblings to one another and are forbidden from marrying each other.

When a woman nurses a man's child, she becomes forbidden to him, as does her mother and her female ancestors, her daughters and her granddaughters. His mother may not be raised as a wife to her son's milk-brother, and her daughters are likewise forbidden in this way.

If the nursing woman is a slave, the prohibitions still apply fully: her daughters and the daughters of those above her in lineage are all forbidden, whether they came from the same husband or from another. However the milk-kinship arises, the woman who has nursed a man is forbidden to him.

A man's foster-daughters and his foster-granddaughters are all forbidden to him through nursing. Nursing does not deprive a daughter of the natural prohibition; she remains forbidden. So too are his foster-sister, his foster-nieces through brother or sister, and his foster-aunts on both sides.

Nursing also prohibits the combining of two foster-sisters in one marriage, and similarly prohibits combining a woman with her foster-aunt on either side, whether through nursing or through direct marriage.

A man who has nursed a son through a woman may also marry a different woman, one who was not nursed by that same woman, without any prohibition applying to her, because she is not a milk-sister but is otherwise unrelated to him. The foster-brother who was nursed is not guilty of anything regarding a woman who is not in this prohibited circle, nor is that woman his father's wife in any sense.

They may also marry the woman who nursed their son, in certain conditions, and similarly they may marry the daughter of a nurse or a wet-nurse, as long as those daughters did not themselves suckle the son or his children and no kinship was thereby established. If no Muslim woman is available, it may be permissible with a lawful non-Muslim woman under conditions, provided no harm results, for nursing does not override natural kinship, and nursing does not create prohibition in the absence of Islam's conditions being met.

On the question of whether milk-kinship is proven when the nursing occurs after the marriage contract but before consummation, the judge may confirm the prohibition between them. Complete nursing is defined as three consecutive days or ten separate sucklings. If friendship or intimacy had already been established before this was proven, the dowry remains owed and the retribution stands. After the first and second cases, the husband has no further claim, and they are to be separated. The testimony of a foster-brother is acceptable in this matter, and credible testimony is not to be rejected merely on suspicion.

On the Missing Person

Ali (ع) said that if the whereabouts of a missing man are known, he is not treated as missing but as absent. The distinction is that the absent person is known to be somewhere and has a recognized last place of residence, whereas the missing person's location is entirely unknown.

If a wife complains about lack of maintenance from a missing husband, the judge should first investigate whether he left any property. If property is found, it should be used to provide for her. If a guardian is appointed to administer it, that guardian may spend from it on her behalf. If all the property is exhausted and she remains without support, the judge writes to the place where the husband was last known to be, in order to inquire about him.

If the husband returns before the waiting period is complete and wishes to reclaim his wife, he may do so, and the time he was absent counts accordingly. If he returns after the period has elapsed, then her right in that matter stands. As for the missing man, he is treated among those who are present from the day he went missing with respect to his wife's maintenance. The wife of a man merely absent, however, has no automatic entitlement beyond what can be established.

On Maintenance

The husband's obligation is to provide for his family and to fulfill his duties toward them. God Almighty said:

> وَارْزُقُوهُمْ فِيهَا وَاكْسُوهُمْ وَقُولُوا لَهُمْ قَوْلًا مَعْرُوفًا

"And provide for them from it, and clothe them, and speak to them a kindly word."

And He said:

> وَعَلَى الْمُوسِعِ قَدَرُهُ وَعَلَى الْمُقْتِرِ قَدَرُهُ

"Upon the man of means according to his means, and upon the straitened according to his."

The hadith is sufficient for people to understand that neglecting those who depend on them is a grave matter:

> كَفَى بِالْمَرْءِ إِثْمًا أَنْ يُضَيِّعَ مَنْ يَعُولُ، وَأَنْ لَا يَشْبَعَ الرَّجُلُ وَيُجِيعَ أَهْلَهُ، الرِّفْقُ نِصْفُ الْعَيْشِ، مَنِ اقْتَصَدَ فِي مَعِيشَتِهِ رَزَقَهُ اللَّهُ وَمَنْ بَذَّرَ حَرَمَهُ اللَّهُ، إِذَا أَرَادَ اللَّهُ بِأَهْلِ بَيْتٍ خَيْرًا فَقَّهَهُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَرَزَقَهُمُ الرِّفْقَ فِي مَعِيشَتِهِمْ وَالْقَصْدَ فِي شَأْنِهِمْ

"It is enough sin for a man that he neglect those he supports, and that a man eat his fill while his family goes hungry. Gentleness is half of living. Whoever is moderate in his livelihood, God provides for him, and whoever squanders, God withholds from him. When God intends good for a household, He gives them understanding in religion, gentleness in their livelihood, and moderation in their affairs."

A man should be kind to his family, and the median standard of living is one that is neither wasteful nor miserly. It is from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) that concerning the words of God Almighty وَلَا تُبَذِّرْ تَبْذِيرًا, "and do not squander wastefully," he said: لَيْسَ فِي طَاعَةِ اللَّهِ تَبْذِيرٌ, "there is no squandering in obedience to God." From this principle it follows that when a man finds he cannot spend on what has passed and he is responsible for someone, whether his wife in Egypt or elsewhere, and if he finds nothing, there is a choice between the two parties: if she accepts the situation, she remains; if she does not, then standing without maintenance entitles her to seek relief.

It is also said that a woman of any age, whether young or old, or one who does not observe prayer, has a right to maintenance from her husband in accordance with her proximity to him. If the husband is a man of means who has left without his wife's permission and has been staying elsewhere at his own expense, then if there is rebellion, نشوز, on her part, she is not entitled to maintenance until she returns. As for the divorced woman, her maintenance is the husband's responsibility, except in the case of an irrevocable divorce, unless he fails to provide, and she remains silent about it against the husband until she gives birth. She is entitled to maintenance if she breastfeeds her child, or if she was divorced, as the Almighty said:

> وَلَا تُضَارَّ وَالِدَةٌ بِوَلَدِهَا وَلَا مَوْلُودٌ لَهُ بِوَلَدِهِ

"Let no mother be harmed on account of her child, nor a father on account of his child."

And God Almighty said:

> يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ إِذَا طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَطَلِّقُوهُنَّ لِعِدَّتِهِنَّ وَأَحْصُوا الْعِدَّةَ ... فَإِذَا بَلَغْنَ أَجَلَهُنَّ فَأَمْسِكُوهُنَّ بِمَعْرُوفٍ أَوْ فَارِقُوهُنَّ بِمَعْرُوفٍ وَأَشْهِدُوا ذَوَيْ عَدْلٍ مِنْكُمْ وَأَقِيمُوا الشَّهَادَةَ لِلَّهِ

"O Prophet, when you divorce women, divorce them at the beginning of their waiting periods, and count the waiting period... And when they reach their term, either retain them honorably or part from them honorably, and call to witness two just men among you, and establish the testimony for God."

A letter was written to Rufa'ah, judge of the matter in Egypt, concerning divorce: خُذْ حَذَرَكَ فِيهِ, "take care in it." This applies to those who have not taught the Muslims the way of marriage and its proper conduct, because people must follow their established ways.

On the Dissolution of a Marriage

Marriage is not merely a contract between two spouses. Rather, the husband has obligations, the dowry, maintenance, and good companionship, and the wife has obligations, chastity, affection, obedience, along with other terms of the covenant. When this covenant is broken and one of the parties inflicts serious harm by neglecting the duties assigned to the husband, the marriage contract may be dissolved.

Just as marriage is not generally contracted by the woman without a representative or agent, the dissolution too is only initiated by the representative of the aggrieved party, specifically by the one who complains on her behalf and seeks protection of the wife from harm. The judge must investigate whether the complaint is true, and if so, compel the husband to cease the harm or remain silent.

If the husband does not respond to the judge's intervention, the judge may proceed to issue a divorce on his behalf, though if news of his willingness reaches the court, and the situation on the wife's side allows it in the balance, the husband may be given her back into his care with renewed kindness toward her and the members of the household. After that, the husband has a choice: he may keep her on the condition he has described, or he may divorce her, and that divorce is revocable. The holy law in this matter takes careful account of the rights of the couple and the proper ordering of their lives.

Divorce was recognized in earlier revealed laws and among the prophets as well, and it is in accordance with the law of nature, for marriage touches the living relations of the couple under the purified covenants between them. If one party violates those covenants, the government of justice may deprive him of that bond, and this deprivation in the language of the Shari'ah is called divorce.

The benefit of divorce is sufficient for both parties, to spare each other further harm. It should not be said that divorce is a final cure for everything. Divorce falls on the one who wastes it through abuse, or on the one who seeks to remarry with humiliation. As the wise man said:

> اَتَيْتُ لِلْوَصْلِ لَا لِلْفَصْلِ

"Patience in the face of hardship is better than separation."

The bitterness of separation after intimacy is in Islam like the parable of nakedness after being clothed, something deeply disliked by the discerning.

According to the jurists, there are five forms of marital dissolution: طلاق العدة, the divorce of the waiting period; الخلع, khul'a; الإيلاء, ila'; الظهار, zihar; and اللعان, li'an. Each has a meaning that is clarified in its proper place, and there is also the divorce of the rebellious wife.

Regarding the divorce of the waiting period: when a husband wishes to divorce a woman, he should consider three things, that she is in a state of purity from menstruation, that he has not had relations with her after that purification, and the correct procedure, which is for him to record it, writing to the effect that "my wife, the daughter of so-and-so, is divorced by me today," and to call two men as witnesses. All of this is done in the presence of the proper authority.

If a husband dies during the days of the first or second waiting period, and the other party is still alive, even if the husband fell ill during those days and death came before the waiting period was complete, then the tool of that dissolution is complete. If the period was one of sitting in marriage, and the man divorces the woman first and then a second time, and she marries another man, and the waiting period of the second marriage ends, and she is released from that second marriage also, through completion or separation, she then returns to the first husband, who retains one more divorce with her, this being the second of his remaining. The divorce of a wife after she has returned to him from the second husband does not give him the furthest three full separations from her, except after consummation and the fulfilment of the three conditions already named.

Five categories of wives are dispensed from the conditions accordingly: the pregnant woman who has not yet reached full term, the one who has not been touched by her husband, the one who is not an adult, and the one for whom three months have passed from the divorce, so that the count of her waiting period is complete. The writing is not a substitute for verbal pronunciation; a sound and explicit utterance of the word of divorce is required, so that no doubt remains with the woman. Even if there is an intention to doubt her, a wink or a gesture, or the words of one intoxicated, does not constitute divorce.

On Khul'a

God Almighty said:

> وَلَا يَحِلُّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَأْخُذُوا مِمَّا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ شَيْئًا إِلَّا أَنْ يَخَافَا أَلَّا يُقِيمَا حُدُودَ اللَّهِ فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ أَلَّا يُقِيمَا حُدُودَ اللَّهِ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا فِيمَا افْتَدَتْ بِهِ ... وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ شِقَاقَ بَيْنِهِمَا فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا مِنْ أَهْلِهِ وَحَكَمًا مِنْ أَهْلِهَا إِنْ يُرِيدَا إِصْلَاحًا يُوَفِّقِ اللَّهُ بَيْنَهُمَا

"It is not permissible for you to take back anything of what you have given them, unless both fear that they cannot maintain the limits of God. If you fear that they cannot maintain the limits of God, there is no blame upon either of them for what she gives in ransom... And if you fear discord between them, appoint an arbitrator from his family and an arbitrator from her family. If they seek reconciliation, God will bring them together."

Ali (ع) said: الخلع طلاق, "khul'a is divorce." If union and agreement between the couple are not possible, and the woman says, "I cannot fulfill your rights as a returning wife, take this amount from me in exchange," then she is giving something, whether an unspecified amount or a known sum, in exchange for her release. This may be her dowry returned to him, or her mount for a period, or her service for a period of time, or something similar. If the husband agrees and says, "I accept your satisfaction," and the matter is brought before the authority with witnesses and a written document, recording that on such a date the husband is pleased and the wife has offered an amount acceptable to both, then if both accept, the khul'a is valid and neither will be judged from it unjustly. It befits the husband, if he is content with a sum, to be content with less than the dowry he gave her, or with whatever the two agreed upon.

Each may appoint an arbitrator from his own side, or they may agree upon one person. The arbitration that the arbitrators decide upon must be in agreement between the two spouses, and it is binding on them. This procedure was set down by the author in his legal compilation so that neither spouse transgresses the rights of the other. The one who calls them before a judge to agree between them, or who declares an explicit divorce, should have her observe her waiting period in her husband's house or elsewhere, because there are no courts that await them before they fight over the matter if they seek to break off entirely, and it is not permissible to marry another until the waiting period is completed. Should the two wish to marry each other again, whether during the waiting period or after it, that is open to them.

On the Waiting Period

God Almighty said:

> وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنْكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنْفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا، وَالْمُطَلَّقَاتُ يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنْفُسِهِنَّ ثَلَاثَةَ قُرُوءٍ، وَاللَّائِي يَئِسْنَ مِنَ الْمَحِيضِ مِنْ نِسَائِكُمْ إِنِ ارْتَبْتُمْ فَعِدَّتُهُنَّ ثَلَاثَةُ أَشْهُرٍ وَاللَّائِي لَمْ يَحِضْنَ، وَأُولَاتُ الْأَحْمَالِ أَجَلُهُنَّ أَنْ يَضَعْنَ حَمْلَهُنَّ وَلَوْ كَانَ بَعْدَ الطَّلَاقِ بِسَاعَةٍ

"Those of you who die and leave behind wives, they shall wait four months and ten days. And divorced women shall wait three menstrual cycles. As for those of your women who have despaired of menstruation, if you are in doubt, their waiting period is three months, and likewise those who have not menstruated. And those who are pregnant, their term is that they deliver, even if it be an hour after the divorce."

So the waiting period of divorce is three menstrual cycles, and three months for those past menstruation, and the waiting period of a woman whose husband has died is four months and ten days, whether she is an adult or a minor, whether the marriage was consummated or not.

The intention of the divorced woman, if it is possible, is to observe her waiting period in her husband's house, since he may take her back, and her maintenance and housing come from the property of the deceased husband in the case of death. In a first or second revocable divorce, the husband retains his option: at any time within the waiting period he may return to her if he wishes and she is content with that, ordering the writing of a document of return with the testimony of witnesses in the presence of the authority. If he wishes to reunite after the completion of the waiting period, that is not open to him except by a new marriage contract, and she remains with him thereafter under the counting of a first and then a second divorce.

As for the third divorce, the wife has no maintenance and no housing from the husband. He has no option to take her back, and there is no return between them. If reunion is desired after the third divorce, that is not open to them except that she marry another husband who consummates the marriage with her, and she is then released from him by divorce or by death. This is not for those who merely go through the motions of remarriage; the dissolution must be genuine, without manipulation or arrangement.

The woman whose marriage was not consummated has no waiting period, and she may marry at once, even on the day her first husband divorced her, and in her case there is no return. During the days of the waiting period no man may enter upon her except those forbidden to her in marriage, or a servant, or her daughter, or those below the age of puberty and their like. She is permitted the pilgrimage, necessary travel, and attendance with women for prayer in the virtuous nights and at the noble shrine.
