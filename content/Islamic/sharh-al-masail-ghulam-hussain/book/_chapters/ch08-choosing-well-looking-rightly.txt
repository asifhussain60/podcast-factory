# Choosing Well, Looking Rightly

Within that division of charge stands the truth that the real home is not the building. The rented house and its furnishings are not the purpose and not the aim. The real home is the man and the woman together, and they are the two who answer for it. The affairs of the figurative home are many, and they are not exhausted by walls and provisions. The bringing in of resources from outside belongs to the man, and such matters do not enter the domain of the woman except in what touches her charge; and in the same way there are affairs belonging to the woman which the man must leave to her, and he brings to her of expenditure according to his measure.

What is needed between them is affection, and out of the increase of love it is the mark of two sensible people that they consult one another in the matters attached to each. So the figurative home becomes a garden for them, and they are its dwellers, addressed by the words of God Almighty:

> يا آدم اسكن أنت وزوجك الجنة وكلا منها رغدًا حيث شئتما ولا تقربا هذه الشجرة فتكونا من الظالمين

"O Adam, dwell you and your wife in the Garden, and eat from it freely wherever you wish, and do not approach this tree, lest you become of the wrongdoers."

The tree here is the affairs attached to each one of them: that neither draw near to what belongs to the other, lest each become a wrongdoer against the other.

Since the true home is the two spouses, whoever marries off his children must look to them both. If they are found sensible in the management of the household means, he keeps them with him. And if they are sensible in what benefits them, and each of them stands firm in the duty binding upon him, he provides them a dwelling other than his own, so that they may live by their own soundness. If the two are weak, and their parents are the ones who bear the burden of them, then the parents must not withdraw from the affairs of their children. But two sensible parents, experienced in the affairs of this world, do not thrust themselves into the affairs of their children, for their children are now a real home of their own, and the parents are among those attached to it.

Concerning the choice itself, God Almighty said:

> الخبيثات للخبيثين والخبيثون للخبيثات والطيبات للطيبين والطيبون للطيبات

"Corrupt women are for corrupt men, and corrupt men for corrupt women; and good women are for good men, and good men for good women."

And there are the sayings: "Marry those who are equal to you, and marry among them, and choose well for your seed." "Beware of a marriage of ruin, for it is a marred creation." "Marry a woman who is dark and fertile rather than one fair and beautiful and barren, for I shall vie with the other nations through your numbers on the Day of Resurrection." "Take hold of the one who has religion." "Do not marry a woman for her wealth and her beauty, for her wealth and her beauty may be her ruin." "Among the happiness of the Muslim man is a righteous wife, a spacious dwelling, an easy mount, and a righteous child." "The best of your women is the chaste one." "Beware of marrying the foolish woman, for love of her is an affliction and her children are a loss." "Marry the fair-eyed, for in them there is blessing." "The virgin woman is sweeter of speech, quicker to learn, and firmer in affection and steadier in hope."

Our master the Judge al-Nu'man, may God cover him with mercy, said that the saying of the Messenger of God, "choose well for your seed," is a comprehensive word. Its meaning is that a believing man should marry only into a house in which there is purity, and should marry one born of lawful lineage, and should keep away from the people of debauchery and doubt. Since the wife is a necklace upon the husband, it is fitting that he choose his necklace well. Women, though they resemble one another in body and limb, differ greatly when their inward natures are examined and their characters compared. Between them lies a far distance in morals, in temperament, in good compliance, in pride, in refractoriness, and in other qualities besides. As the poet said:

> ليس النساء سواء
> إن الأصابع ليست بمستويات

"Women are not all alike, for even the fingers are not of one measure."

The point noted and prized in the saying of the Messenger of God, "choose well for your seed," is that the woman be fit to bear children, and be well mannered with the finest discipline and upbringing. If that is joined to purity and beauty, then that is more correct and more fitting. For this reason he loved for a man the woman who is fertile and affectionate, and said: "Five things are of happiness: a righteous wife, righteous children, choice companions, a man's provision in his own land, and the rope of Muhammad."

Marriage is not merely a legal contract between a man and a woman. Its intent is the continuance of lineage together with affection and good character, in the completeness of what each owes the other. With an eye to that intent it was said: "When one of you wishes to marry a woman, let him ask about her hair as he asks about her face, for the hair is one of the two beauties." And if it is possible for him to look at her, let him steal a glance without the look of evil intent. For sight is what falls first, and hearing follows; and where there is no such acquaintance, no bond and no sincerity arise between the two, but instead aversion in place of sincerity, and the house is afflicted with misery in place of happiness and with fire in place of the Garden.

From the content of that same saying, "choose well for your seed," it is drawn that a man and a woman should not marry until they have reached the age of discernment, right guidance, and understanding, so that each of them may choose for himself one with whom he can live. That is found only at the meeting point of some eighteen years of age. Marriage at these ages brings joys and sincerity between the two, and soundness for their children. Nor is marriage good where the one marrying is small. As for the narrations mentioned among the scholars concerning the marrying of a woman while young in years, these are particular to the places and times in which such a thing was a necessity, where the order of authority was not firm and its people were not secure from the overpowering of one people by another, as happened in the age of ignorance and as is still found in the outlying parts of the lands of the Arabs. For the wicked among them, whenever they hear of a girl approaching womanhood in a certain house, raid the people of that house and carry her off from her family, as is recorded in the books of that people and in their verses. In such places the marrying of a daughter, even while she is small, becomes obligatory, and it obliges the multiplying of those bound by ties of marriage; and marriage in such conditions is more fitting and more suitable.

But in settled countries, where affairs are ordered and the government holds firm over its people, there is no fear of the plundering of children, and children are under the guardianship of their households and there is no fear for the corruption of their morals. It is therefore not upon parents to marry their daughters before maturity and discernment, until judgment is settled and until the young strive in the earning of a livelihood so that they may support it in the maintenance of their spouses without need of others. Marriage in the state of smallness is not of benefit, in a place such as this, where by the praise of God we are in the land of India, secure for ourselves and our children.

With that security, the benefits of the community are beyond what can be stated. In whatever places joy or grief fall, and wherever the necessity of mutual help arises, its people rise in aid of one another, and they are then as the limbs of a single body, whether in the affairs of religion or the affairs of the world. Among its benefits is this: when a man wishes to marry one of the women, whether young or grown, her upbringing, her habits, and her rearing are not hidden from him in the choosing, as they would be hidden in a woman brought in from outside. So the selection for marriage becomes easy. And the righteous among the children do not act boldly before their parents and the guardians of their religion; rather they hold one another in regard. By the praise of God, whoever seeks a spouse finds among the daughters of the community those whose upbringing and whose companions have been among righteous women, so that they are of purity and soundness.

Necessary teaching should be broadened among them, so that they be knowing in the matters of household charge, and fulfil what is due from them of the rights of husbands and of sisters, and of the rights of children in good rearing and in the fixing of sound beliefs and correct knowledge in their hearts. This is most needed for girls before they enter into household charge. If such teaching were common among them all, each one of them would be fit to order the house, would be the best companion to her husband and a help to him in the affairs of his livelihood, and would be by her religion the first school for the children and their first training ground, before the schools outside.

Whoever among the men and women of the community seeks for himself or for his children a marriage from outside the community meets, by reason of the great mixing and the keeping of company in the passing affairs of this world, a state in which no long stretch of time passes before the matter turns to discord and mutual hatred. Beyond that, his standing is lowered within the community as well. This is so especially when a woman of the community marries a common man, and more so when a man marries from among the common people. And when such couples have children, no rest remains for them. The occurrences of these past twenty years, apart from what occurred before them, suffice as an example and a lesson for the believing men and women. Nor is the saying far from the truth: "Evil does not enter your house of itself unless you open the door to it." And the doors have been opened to it, meaning to evil, in some of the houses of the brethren. For the meeting of believing women with strangers from among the common people, even if those strangers are of good standing, is what has opened the ways for them toward marriage with them.

The keeping of women to the house, and the keeping to the veil, have been among the marks of the believers and among the traits of chastity and modesty, especially while they are unmarried, and are a stronger safeguard against what is disliked and shameful. Upon this the word of God Almighty gives evidence:

> يا أيها النبي قل لأزواجك وبناتك ونساء المؤمنين يدنين عليهن من جلابيبهن ذلك أدنى أن يعرفن فلا يؤذين

"O Prophet, say to your wives and your daughters and the women of the believers that they draw their outer garments close about them. That is more fitting, that they be known and not molested."

The believing women in the time of the Prophet were not distinguished before the common people from others, and there were those who harmed them with mockery. So the command came from God that they draw their outer garments close about them. The believing women of that age and after it kept to the veil, and the veil was among the marks of the people of faith. The veil in Islam became, by the frequency of its practice, a ruling among the rulings of Islam, and it was interpreted as a general ruling in the Quran.

When the Quran was found to bear a general ruling for the believing women and the Muslim women, those who paid no heed to the morals of the people of faith sought a way to permit the mixing of women with their friends and their associates among the common people, and found fault in the Quran concerning the veil. Yet it is a command of the Law, and among what distinguishes Islam from faith and their qualities.

It is related of Abu Ja'far (ع) that he was asked about a believing woman of understanding in a place where there was no one upon her religion: may she marry among them? He said: "She may not marry except one whose religion is known." And he was asked about a dissolute woman, whether a man may marry her. He said: "That is not fitting for him. The people of concealment and chastity are his choice." And it is related from the Messenger of God that a Muslim should not marry a woman who is not Muslim while he finds a Muslim woman. If a woman comes to know Islam, and it happens that she was married to a Muslim while she had before that been an associator, she has the choice: if she wishes she remains with him, and if she wishes she returns to her family. When the days of her waiting period are complete, she goes out from the contract. And if the believer divorces her and wishes to return to her before the days of her waiting period are completed, the choice of returning her is his.

God Almighty said:

> حرمت عليكم أمهاتكم وبناتكم وأخواتكم وعماتكم وخالاتكم وبنات الأخ وبنات الأخت وأمهاتكم اللاتي أرضعنكم وأخواتكم من الرضاعة وأمهات نسائكم وربائبكم اللاتي في حجوركم من نسائكم اللاتي دخلتم بهن فإن لم تكونوا دخلتم بهن فلا جناح عليكم وحلائل أبنائكم الذين من أصلابكم وأن تجمعوا بين الأختين إلا ما قد سلف إن الله كان غفورا رحيما والمحصنات من النساء إلا ما ملكت أيمانكم كتاب الله عليكم وأحل لكم ما وراء ذلكم

"Forbidden to you are your mothers, your daughters, your sisters, your paternal aunts, your maternal aunts, brother's daughters, sister's daughters, your mothers who nursed you, your sisters through nursing, the mothers of your wives, and your stepdaughters who are in your care, born of your wives with whom you have consummated marriage; but if you have not consummated marriage with them, then there is no blame upon you; and the wives of your sons who are from your own loins; and that you join together two sisters, except what has already passed. Indeed God is Forgiving and Merciful. And married women are forbidden, except those your right hands possess. This is the decree of God upon you. And lawful to you is what lies beyond that."

A question: if a man marries a woman while ignorant of the prohibition of joining two sisters, or does not know that this second woman he has married is the sister of the wife he already has, and then comes to know, separation between them is obligatory without divorce. If he has consummated the marriage, the dowry is due upon him and the waiting period is due upon her; and if he has not consummated it, nothing is due upon him. There is no objection to joining a maternal aunt's daughter with a paternal aunt's daughter, and no objection to joining the wife of a paternal uncle with his daughter by another woman, and no objection to marrying a daughter who is from other than his own parents. A man may not marry the sister of his wife when he has divorced his wife by a revocable divorce; but if the divorce was irrevocable, there is no objection to the marriage.

A question: four women are forbidden forever.

1. The woman divorced by the divorce that ends the marriage completely.
2. The woman a man marries after knowing her prohibition.
3. The woman a man marries during her waiting period after the death of her husband.
4. The woman he marries while in the state of consecration.

God Almighty said:

> ولا جناح عليكم فيما عرضتم به من خطبة النساء أو أكننتم في أنفسكم علم الله أنكم ستذكرونهن

"And there is no blame upon you in what you hint at of a proposal to women, or conceal within yourselves. God knows that you will be mindful of them."

And it is related that the Messenger of God forbade that a man propose over the proposal of his brother, meaning after mutual agreement has been reached.

The state of a woman with respect to men is of two kinds: married and unmarried. If she is married, her cases are three: either she is under a husband, or she is in the waiting period of divorce, whether irrevocable or revocable, or she is in the waiting period of one whose husband has died. If the woman is under a husband, or in the waiting period of a revocable divorce, no one may propose to her openly, nor hint at it. But if she is in the waiting period of an irrevocable divorce, or in the waiting period of one whose husband has died, then there is no objection to speech by which the woman comes to know his intent, though she may not be proposed to in the manner of open declaration. As for the unmarried woman, if she is not forbidden, proposing to her is permissible, and the proposal of one does not bar the proposal of another until she answers. Her answer must be an express acceptance; her weeping or her silence does not suffice as evidence of her consent.

Our master Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) said concerning the meaning of hinting: "It is not fitting for a man to propose to a woman in her waiting period; but the hinting that God has permitted is that he speak words by which the woman may come to know his intent."

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) entered upon Sakina bint Hanzala, whose husband, who was her cousin and had married her, had died. He greeted her and said: "How are you, daughter of Hanzala?" She said: "Well, may I be made your ransom." Then he said: "You have known my kinship to the Messenger of God, and my kinship to Ali (ع), and my standing in Islam, and my house among the Arabs." She said: "May God set you right, Abu Ja'far. Do you propose to me in my waiting period?" He said: "I did not do so. I only informed you of my kinship and my station. The Messenger of God entered upon Umm Salama, daughter of Abu Umayya ibn al-Mughira, when she had been widowed of Abu Salama, who was her cousin, and he did not cease to mention to her his station and his place with God until the mark of the mat appeared in his palm from the force of his leaning upon his hand. And that was not a proposal."

It is related from the Messenger of God that he said: "When one of you wishes to marry, there is no objection to his stealing a glance at the woman he intends to marry, if he finds the means." And God has commanded believing men to lower their gaze from believing women, and believing women likewise to lower their gaze from believing men. It is related from Ali (ع) that he said: the Messenger of God was asked about a man past whom a woman passes and he looks at her, and he said: "The first glance is for you, the second is against you, and the third glance is an arrow from the arrows of Iblis. Whoever leaves it for the sake of God, God will requite him with a faith whose sweetness he finds in his heart." And our master Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about a man past whom a woman passes and he looks after her, and he said: "Would he love that men should look at his own household? Accept for people what you accept for yourselves."

These narrations in which the permissibility of a woman's going out to the market is mentioned belong to places where there is a single people or a single tribe of the people of faith, as is the case now in some of the outlying parts of the lands of the Arabs, where the women are among their own quarters. And indeed, the woman who has no one to undertake her affairs has no remedy but to go out, and there is no objection to it. But in those places where there are many kinds of peoples, the noble among them and the base, it is not for the women of noble houses and the people of religion to go out from the house, even veiled, for that is held a fault among the people of religion.

It is related that a man came to the Messenger of God and said: "O Messenger of God, shall I ask permission of my sister when I wish to enter upon her?" He said: "Yes. Would it please you to see her uncovered?" The man said: "No." He said: "And my sister, O Messenger of God, uncovers her hair at times before me." He said: "No." The man said: "And why?" He said: "I fear for you that if something of her beauty appears to you, Satan may take hold of you." And he said: "When one of you kisses a woman of his close kin who has reached the age of menses, let him kiss between her eyes or upon her head, and let him refrain from her cheek and from her mouth."

It is related that he forbade that a virgin be given in marriage until she is consulted, and that there is no marriage except with a guardian and two witnesses. And Ali (ع) said: "The giving in marriage by the father and the grandfather is permitted for sons and daughters when they are small, and they have no option after they reach maturity."

Kinship is of two kinds. What comes by way of the father is called kinship of lineage, and the relatives connected through the father are bound to a man by the tie of blood. The one born into this kinship is not there by his own choice; it is necessary and unchosen. And what comes by way of marriage is called kinship of affinity, and the relatives connected through this tie are bound to the man and to the woman by the tie of marriage, and the two spouses stand in this kinship by their own choice, so that they are praised or blamed for it. Kinship by affinity is the kinship that a man and a woman are able to acquire for themselves.
