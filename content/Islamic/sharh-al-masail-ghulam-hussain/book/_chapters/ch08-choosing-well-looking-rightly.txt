# Choosing Well, Looking Rightly

The house that is real is not the building. The real home is the man and the woman together, for they are the ones responsible for it, and the rented dwelling with its furnishings is neither the purpose nor the aim. The affairs of the figurative home are many, and they are not confined to those things. The bringing in of resources from outside belongs to the man, and such matters do not enter the domain of the woman except in what is attached to her charge; and just as there are things attached to the woman which the man must supply for her out of what he has, so there are things she works at in which her manner and her gaze are what count, and modesty of gaze belongs to her.

What is needed between them is affection, and out of the increase of love between them it is fitting, if the two are people of sense, that they consult one another in the matters attached to each of them. Then the figurative home becomes a garden for them, and they are among its dwellers, and they are the ones addressed by His words:

> يا آدم اسكن انت وزوجك الجنة وكلا منها رغداً حيث شئتما ولا تقربا هذه الشجرة فتكونا من الظالمين

"O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in the Garden, and eat of it in ease wherever you both wish, and do not approach this tree, lest you become of the wrongdoers."

The tree here stands for the matters attached to each one of them: let neither of the two draw near what is attached to the other, lest they become of the wrongdoers, that is, lest each of the two wrong the other. Since the two spouses are the real home, whoever gives his children in marriage must look to them both. If he finds them people of sense in managing the affairs of the household, he keeps them with him. Otherwise, when the two are sensible in what benefits them, and each of them stands firm in the duty that is binding upon him, he should make for them a dwelling other than his own dwelling, so that they may live by their own soundness. If the two are weak, and the parents undertake the support of their children, then it is not for the parents to enter into the affairs of their sons and daughters, for the two sensible parents, experienced in the affairs of the world, do not intrude themselves into the affairs of their children, since their children are now the real home, and their parents are among those attached to them.

Concerning the choosing of a spouse there is the word of God:

> الخبيثات للخبيثين والخبيثون للخبيثات والطيّبات للطيّبين والطيّبون للطيّبات

"Corrupt women are for corrupt men, and corrupt men are for corrupt women, and good women are for good men, and good men are for good women."

And there are the reports:

> انكحوا الاكفاء وانكحوا اليهم واختاروا لنطفكم

"Marry those who are equals, and give in marriage to them, and choose well for your seed."

> عليك بذات الدين ولا تنكح المرأة لمالها وجمالها

"Take the one who has religion, and do not marry a woman for her wealth and her beauty."

> من سعادة المرء المسلم الزوجة الصالحة والمسكن الواسع والمركب الهنى والولد الصالح

"Among the happiness of the Muslim man is a righteous wife, a spacious dwelling, an easy mount, and a righteous child."

> وخير نساءكم العفيفة

"And the best of your women is the chaste one."

> اياكم وتزويج الحمقاء فان حبها بلاء وولدها ضياع

"Beware of marrying the foolish woman, for love of her is an affliction and her child is a loss."

And it is reported that the young woman married first is the sweetest of speech, the quickest to learn, and the firmest in affection.

Our master the Judge al-Nu'man, may God grant him mercy, said that the saying of the Prophet, (ع), اختاروا لنطفكم, "choose well for your seed," is a comprehensive word: that the believer should not marry except one in whom there is purity, one born of lawful union, and that he should shun the people of debauchery and doubt. For since the wife is a necklace for the husband, he ought to choose his necklace well. Women, though they resemble one another in body and limb, reveal, when their inward states and the differences of their characters are examined, that between them lies a far distance in morals and temperament, in good compliance, in pride and rebellion, and in other qualities besides. As the poet said:

> ليس النساء سواء بينهن كما
> إن الاصابع ليست بمستويات

"Women are not all alike among themselves, even as the fingers are not of one measure."

The point regarded and prized in the saying of the Messenger of God, (ع), اختاروا لنطفكم, is that the woman be fit for bearing children, well-mannered, and reared with the best discipline and upbringing. If that is joined to purity and beauty, then that is more correct and more fitting. For this reason he, (ع), loved the woman who bears children and shows affection, and he said:

> خمس من السعادة الزوجة الصالحة والاولاد الصلحاء والاصحاب الاخيار ورزق المرء فى بلده والحب لآل محمد

"Five things are of happiness: a righteous wife, righteous children, good companions, a man's provision in his own land, and love for the family of Muhammad."

Marriage is not merely a legal contract between a man and a woman. What is intended by it is the continuance of the lineage in what is right, and affection, and the completing of character on both sides. Since this is the aim, he, (ع), said that when one of them wishes to marry a woman, he should ask about her face, and even about her hair, for the hair is one of the two beauties; and if it is possible for him to look at her, let him steal a glance, not the glance of evil. It is the eye that first falls upon what the ear has heard; and where this is neglected, no bond and no sincerity arise between the two, but rather mutual aversion in place of sincerity, so that the house is tried with wretchedness in place of happiness, and with fire in place of the Garden.

From the content of the saying اختاروا لنطفكم it is also drawn that the man and the woman should not marry until maturity, right direction, and understanding have been reached in their ages, so that each of them may choose for himself the one with whom he is to live. That is not attained except at about eighteen years of age. Marriage at these ages brings about gladness and sincerity between the two and soundness for their children; nor is marriage commendable while the one marrying is young. As for the reports mentioned concerning the marrying of a woman while small in years, they belong to those situations in which necessity compelled it, where the order of the state is not firm and its people are not secure from the domination of one people over another, as was the case in the time of ignorance and as is still found in the outlying regions of Arab rule. The wicked among them, whenever they hear of a girl of marriageable age in some place, raid the household and carry her off from her people, as is recorded in the books of that people and in their poems. In such situations the marrying of a daughter, even while she is young, becomes necessary, and it multiplies those bound by ties of marriage; and likewise the marrying of a youth while he is still in his youth. In such conditions early marriage is better and more suitable.

But in the settled lands, where affairs are ordered and the government is firm over its people, and there is no fear of the plunder of children, and children are under the guardianship of their fathers and no fear of the corruption of their morals hangs over them, it is not upon the fathers to marry their daughters before maturity and right direction, so that their understanding may be settled and they may strive in earning a livelihood until they can support their spouses by it without need of another. Marriage in the state of youth is not fitting where such conditions hold, as it is with us, praise be to God, in the land of India, where we are secure for ourselves and our children.

Along with this, the benefits of the community are gained for us beyond what can be stated. Whatever occasions of joy and sorrow arise, and wherever the necessity of mutual help falls, they rise to help one another, and are then as limbs of one body, whether the matter be of religion or of the world. Among its benefits is that when one wishes to marry one of the women, whether young or grown, her upbringing, her habits, and her rearing are not hidden from him in the choosing, as they would be hidden in a woman coming from outside; so the selection for marriage is made easy. Moreover, the righteous among the children do not act boldly against their fathers and the guardians of their religion. And the daughters of the community, praise be to God, come with their upbringing and with their companionship among righteous women, so that in them are purity and soundness.

There is need also to widen the necessary teaching among them, that they may be knowing in the affairs of the household and may discharge what is due from them of the rights of husbands and of sisters, and of the rights of children in rearing them well and in fixing sound beliefs and true knowledge in their hearts, especially the girls, before they enter upon household life. If teaching were widespread among them generally, each one of them would set the order of the house right and be a good companion to her husband and a help to him in the affairs of his livelihood, and by her religion she would be the first school for the children, before the outside schools.

Whoever among the men of the community and its women seeks a marriage for himself or for his children from outside the community will find, because of the abundant mixing and the keeping company that arises between them in passing worldly matters, that no long stretch of time passes before the matter turns to discord and mutual hatred. Along with this his standing is lowered among the community as well. This holds especially when a woman of the community marries one of the common people, and more so when a man marries from among the common people; and when children come to such as these, no rest remains for the women. The events that have occurred in these twenty years, apart from what occurred before them, suffice as an example and a lesson for the believing men and the believing women. Nor is what has been said far from truth, that evil does not enter a house of itself unless the door is opened to it. Doors have been opened to it, that is, to evil, in some of the houses of the brethren; for the meeting of the women of the believers with strangers from among the common people, even when these are of their own kin, is what has widened the paths for them.

The keeping of the home and the keeping of modesty have been among the marks of the believing woman, and among the qualities of the people of chastity, especially while they are unmarried, and this is more guarded against what is disliked and shameful. Upon this rests His saying:

> يا ايها النبى قل لازواجك وبناتك ونساء المؤمنين يدنين عليهن من جلابيبهن ذلك ادنى ان يعرفن فلا يؤذين

"O Prophet, say to your wives and your daughters and the women of the believers that they draw their outer garments close about them; that is more fitting that they be known and not molested."

The women of the believers in the time of the Prophet were distinguished among the common people from others, who used to harm them with mockery; so the command came from God that they draw their outer garments close about them. The believing women of that age and after it kept the veil, and the veil was among the marks of the people of faith, so that the veil in Islam, through the abundance of its practice, became a ruling among the rulings of Islam, and it was interpreted as a general ruling in the Quran. Since the Quran laid down a general ruling for the believing women and the Muslim women, those who cared nothing for the morals of the people of faith found a way to permit the mingling of women with their friends and their associates among the common people, and they found fault in the Quran regarding the veil, which is a legislated command and a mark distinguishing Islam and faith and their qualities.

It is related of Abu Ja'far (ع) that he was asked about a believing woman of understanding, in a place where there was none of the people of her religion, whether she might marry from among them, and he said: she does not marry except one who is upon her religion. He was asked about the dissolute woman, whether a man might marry her, and he said: that is not fitting for him; the people of concealment and chastity are his choice. And it is from the Messenger of God, (ع), that a Muslim should not marry a woman who is not a Muslim while he finds a Muslim woman; and if a woman comes to Islam, and it happens that she is married to a Muslim while there is with him another woman who is an idolatress, then the choice is hers: if she wishes she remains with him, and if she wishes she returns to her people; and when the days of her waiting period are completed she leaves the bond. And if the believer divorces the idolatress and wishes to take her back before the days of her waiting period are finished, the choice of returning her is his.

Concerning those forbidden in marriage there is the verse:

> حرمت عليكم امهاتكم وبناتكم واخواتكم وعماتكم وخالاتكم وبنات الاخ وبنات الاخت وامهاتكم اللاتى ارضعنكم واخواتكم من الرضاعة وامهات نسائكم وربائبكم اللاتى فى حجوركم من نسائكم اللاتى دخلتم بهن فان لم تكونوا دخلتم بهن فلا جناح عليكم وحلائل ابنائكم الذين من اصلابكم وان تجمعوا بين الاختين الا ما قد سلف ان الله كان غفورا رحيما والمحصنات من النساء الا ما ملكت ايمانكم كتاب الله عليكم واحل لكم ما وراء ذلكم

"Forbidden to you are your mothers, your daughters, your sisters, your paternal aunts, your maternal aunts, the daughters of the brother and the daughters of the sister, your mothers who suckled you, your sisters through suckling, the mothers of your wives, your stepdaughters who are in your care, born of your wives with whom you have consummated marriage; but if you have not consummated marriage with them, then there is no blame upon you; and the wives of your sons who are of your own loins, and that you join two sisters together, except what has already passed. Indeed God is Forgiving, Merciful. And married women, except those your right hands possess: the decree of God upon you. And lawful to you is what is beyond that."

A question: one whose wife has a sister, and he marries the sister while ignorant of the prohibition of joining two sisters, or while not knowing that the second woman he has married is the sister of the wife he already has, and then comes to know. Separation between the two is required, without divorce. If he has consummated the marriage, the dowry is upon him and the waiting period is upon her; and if he has not consummated it, nothing is upon him. There is no harm in joining the daughter of the maternal aunt and the daughter of the paternal aunt; nor is there harm in joining the wife of the paternal uncle and his daughter by another woman; nor is there harm in marrying a daughter who is from other than her parents. A man does not marry the sister of his wife when he has divorced his wife with a revocable divorce; but when the divorce is irrevocable, there is no harm in the marriage.

A question: four women are forbidden forever. The woman divorced by the divorce of imprecation; the woman a man marries after knowing that she is forbidden to him; the woman a man marries after the death of her husband while she is in her waiting period; and the woman he marries while in the state of ihram.

Concerning the proposal there is the verse:

> ولا جناح عليكم فيما عرضتم به من خطبة النساء او اكننتم فى انفسكم علم الله انكم ستذكرونهن

"And there is no blame upon you in what you hint at of a proposal to women, or conceal within yourselves. God knows that you will remember them."

And the report: he, (ع), forbade a man to propose over the proposal of his brother, that is, after mutual agreement has been reached.

The condition of the woman with respect to men is of two kinds: married and unmarried. If she is married, her states are three: she is either under a husband, or in the waiting period of divorce, whether irrevocable or revocable, or in the waiting period of one whose husband has died. If the woman is under a husband, or in the waiting period of revocable divorce, it is not for anyone to propose to her openly or by hint. But if she is in the waiting period of irrevocable divorce or in the waiting period of one whose husband has died, then there is no harm in speaking words by which the woman may learn what is intended, though she is not to be proposed to by open declaration. As for the unmarried woman, when she is not forbidden, proposing to her is permitted, and once she has answered one man, she is not for another. The acceptance must be an open declaration; the weeping of a woman or her silence does not suffice as proof of her consent.

Our master Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) said, concerning the meaning of hinting: it is not fitting for a man to propose to a woman in her waiting period; but the hinting which God has made lawful is that he speak words by which the woman may learn what is intended.

Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali (ع) entered upon Sakina bint Handhala, whose husband, who was her paternal cousin and had married her, had died. He greeted her and said: How are you, O daughter of Handhala? She said: Well, may I be made your ransom. Then he said:

"You have known my kinship to the Messenger of God, (ع), and to Ali (ع), and my precedence in Islam and my house among the Arabs."

She said: May God forgive you, O Abu Ja'far, do you propose to me in my waiting period? He said:

"I have done no such thing. I have only informed you of my kinship and my station. The Messenger of God, (ع), entered upon Umm Salama the daughter of Abu Umayya ibn al-Mughira, and she was in mourning for Abu Salama, who was her cousin, and he, (ع), did not cease to mention to her his rank and his station with God until the marks of the mat were pressed into his palm from the weight of what he leaned upon his hand. That was not a proposal."

And it is related of the Messenger of God, (ع), that he said:

> إذا اراد احدكم ان يتزوج فلا بأس ان يختلس النظر الى المرأة التى يريد تزويجها إذا وجد مكنة

"When one of you wishes to marry, there is no harm in his stealing a glance at the woman he intends to marry, if he finds opportunity."

God has commanded the believing men to lower their gaze from the believing women, and the believing women likewise to lower their gaze from the believing men. It is related of Ali (ع) that he said: the Messenger of God, (ع), was asked about a man past whom a woman passes and he looks at her, and he said:

> اول نظرة لك والثانية عليك والنظرة الثالثة سهم من سهام ابليس من تركها لله عاقبه ايمانا يجد طعمه فى قلبه

"The first glance is for you, the second is against you, and the third glance is an arrow from the arrows of Satan. Whoever abandons it for the sake of God, God will requite him with a faith whose taste he will find in his heart."

Our master Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about a man past whom a woman passes and he looks after her, and he said: let a man consider whether he would be pleased that men should look at his own family; be pleased for people with what you are pleased for your own households.

These reports in which the going out of a woman to the market is permitted belong to places where there is a single people or a single tribe of the people of faith, as is now the case in some of the outlying parts of Arabia, where the women there are among their own quarters. And the woman who has none with her to undertake her affairs has no course but to go out, and there is no harm in it. But in places where there are many kinds of peoples, the noble among them and the base, it is not for the noble women and the women of religion to go out from the house even veiled, for that is held a fault among the people of religion.

It is related that a man came to the Messenger of God, (ع), and said: O Messenger of God, shall I ask permission of my sister when I wish to enter upon her? He said:

"Yes. Would it please you to see her uncovered?"

The man said: No. He said:

"Then ask permission."

The man said: O Messenger of God, my sister uncovers her hair at times in my presence. He said:

"No."

The man said: And why? He said:

"I fear for you, that if something of her beauty appears to you, Satan may unsettle you."

And he said:

> إذا قبل احدكم ذات محرم قد حاضت فليقبل بين عينيها او رأسها وليكف عن خدها وعن فيها

"When one of you kisses a woman forbidden to him in marriage who has reached the age of menstruation, let him kiss between her eyes or upon her head, and let him refrain from her cheek and her mouth."

Concerning the contracting of the marriage there is the verse:

> يا ايها النبى انا احللنا لك ازواجك

"O Prophet, We have made lawful to you your wives."

And the report: he, (ع), forbade that a woman be married until she is consulted, and there is no marriage except with a guardian and two witnesses. And Ali (ع) said: the giving in marriage by the father and the grandfather is permitted upon sons and daughters when they are young, and there is no option for them after reaching maturity.

Kinship is of two kinds, that of lineage and that of marriage. What is by relation to the father is called lineage, and the relatives related to his father have with him the tie of kinship by lineage, and the one born into this kinship has no choice in it and no praise or blame attaches to him for it. What is by marriage is called affinal kinship, and the relatives connected by this kinship have with the husband and the wife the tie of kinship by marriage; and the two spouses in this kinship are by choice, and they are praised and blamed in it. So the kinship of marriage is that which a man and a woman are able to obtain for themselves as relatives.
