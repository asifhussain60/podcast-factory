# The Contract: Forbidden Degrees, Guardians, and the Bride-Price

The Contract: Forbidden Degrees, Guardians, and the Bride-Price

On Lawful and Unlawful Women in Marriage

God Almighty said:

"يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِىُّ اِنَّا اَحْلَلْنَا لَكَ اَزْوَاجَكَ"

"O Prophet, We have made lawful for you your wives."

The hadith states that a man was swept away by his emotions and married his father's wife, with two witnesses present. It is transmitted that Salih said: a father and son may permissibly marry slave girls and daughters if the girls are young; upon reaching puberty, however, the girls have a choice in the matter.

Kinship falls into two categories: blood relation (نسبية, *nasab*) and affinity by marriage (صهارية, *musahara*). Blood relation on the father's side brings relatives into a close relationship with one another, for they are joined by lineage and are considered kin. One born into this kinship is bound on both sides by ties of blood. Where there is no such blood connection, the relationship is called *musahara*, affinity through marriage. Those who belong to this category of kinship are not strangers to the marriage bond. The relationship of *musahara*, like blood kinship, carries its own rulings regarding choice and permissibility.

When those under guardianship reach adulthood, as if they had come into the fullness of life, the authority of the guardian may appear to be coercive. Yet these two forms of guardianship remain in force until the ward reaches puberty. The furthest point from puberty is the age of *musahara*, and the age of maturity differs among them. No one denies guardianship in marriage except the father, where one can be found, or his duly appointed agent.

"فَاِنْ اَنْكَحَهَا غَيْرُ هَؤُلَاءِ الْاَرْبَعَةِ فَالنِّكَاحُ بَاطِلٌ"

"If she is given in marriage by other than these four, the marriage is void."

It is equally impermissible for these guardians to act on mere verbal consent alone. If an adult woman wishes to arrange her own marriage, she may appoint as her agent one of these four, or a genuine son, or a reliable relative, or her paternal brother to act in this matter, with two witnesses present. One of the guardians may also delegate authority to another among them who is living, allowing the one chosen to be appointed by the judge to act on her behalf. The matter should not be left unresolved, and no one else should be allowed to interfere. If the guardian refuses without valid cause, the judge steps in, and the appointed agent conducts the marriage on her behalf. This is the path narrated from the Imams of guidance (عليهم السلام). The judge, moreover, may serve as a witness to the contract of two men, and there is no objection if those who served as witnesses to the agency act also as witnesses to the marriage itself.

On Conditions Stipulated in the Marriage Contract

God Almighty said:

"وَلَا تَقُولُوا لِمَا تَصِفُ اَلْسِنَتُكُمُ الْكَذِبَ هَذَا حَلَالٌ وَهَذَا حَرَامٌ لِتَفْتَرُوا عَلَى اللهِ الْكَذِبَ"

"Do not say what your tongues describe, that this is lawful and this is forbidden, inventing lies against God."

Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) said:

"شَرْطُ اللهِ قَبْلَ شُرُوطِهِمْ"

"The condition of God comes before their conditions."

That is, any condition stipulated by the people is neither valid nor enforceable if it violates the Book or contradicts the Sunnah.

God has given the husband authority in certain matters: intercourse, divorce, maintenance by kindness, and the contracting of further marriages. If the time of the contract is divided between several wives, each receives her due share. The husband is obliged to fulfil the rights of each, whether his wife agrees with the arrangement or not, but all of this must be conducted with fairness and kindness. If either party is wronged, the wife may bring the matter before the judge, though the judge does not have the power to impose divorce through payment (*khul'*) or to remove the husband from the home. The husband must not strike his wife for anything that is not legally obligatory. God Almighty said:

"وَاللَّاتِى تَخَافُونَ نُشُوزَهُنَّ فَعِظُوهُنَّ وَاهْجُرُوهُنَّ فِى الْمَضَاجِعِ وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ فَاِنْ اَطَعْنَكُمْ فَلَا تَبْغُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ سَبِيلًا"

"As for those women from whom you fear *nushuz*, admonish them, then forsake them in bed, and then strike them; but if they obey you, seek no further means against them."

Maintenance, the provision of what is necessary, and divorce each have their own proper place. In any case, if a man is a Muslim, he is entitled to certain prerogatives, except if he acts in a state of intoxication or moral filth. As for *khul'* divorce, the couple must be given a choice. The matter comes before the arbitrator, or a dispute arises, as has occurred in various cases over the years. Whoever wished to proceed with marriage would do so, with the proper documentation recorded in a book designated for that purpose. A man must deal equitably with his wife in the matter of good treatment, fulfilling the right of *raj'ah*, providing maintenance, and observing the waiting period (محسب الزمان). If none of the aforementioned conditions are met and a directive is issued, that is, one that is on the side of the woman, then the man's right regarding the couple, if he wishes to reconcile them when the number exceeds thirteen, is that her divorce rests in the hand of the one who has usurped authority, so as to bring about reconciliation between them or to finalise the divorce. Both outcomes are subject to moderation.

If the man wishes to divorce his wife but the woman objects, she may ransom herself by returning her portion of the division to him, or some part of it, and they may agree on a settlement. Reconciliation through mutual agreement is permissible, as God Almighty said:

"اِنِ امْرَاَةٌ خَافَتْ نُشُوزًا اَوْ اِعْرَاضًا فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا اَنْ يُصْلِحَا بَيْنَهُمَا صُلْحًا وَالصُّلْحُ خَيْرٌ"

"If a woman fears ill-treatment or desertion from her husband, there is no blame on either of them if they come to a settlement between themselves, and settlement is better."

The essential addition is that they reconcile with one another on good terms. Seeking pain through divorce is blameworthy except where it is necessary to complete the separation. It is also established that a man may divorce his wife in order to marry another. As for defects that neither husband nor wife knew of before the marriage but that became apparent afterward, each of them has the right either to remain in the marriage or to separate. These defects are nine, and they are enumerated in the *Da'a'im*.

On the Bride-Price

God Almighty said:

"وَآتُوا النِّسَاءَ صَدُقَاتِهِنَّ نِحْلَةً"

"And give the women their bride-gifts as a free gift."

And the hadith:

"اِنَّ اللهَ غَافِرٌ كُلَّ ذَنْبٍ اِلَّا رَجُلًا اغْتَصَبَ امْرَاَةً مَهْرَهَا اَوْ اَجِيرًا اَجْرَتَهُ اَوْ رَجُلًا بَاعَ حُرًّا"

"God forgives every sin except that of a man who seizes a woman's bride-price, or a hired man's wage, or a man who sells a free person."

A man whose bride-price was provided by his people came forward. The Messenger of God (صلع) said:

"لَا تُغَالُوا فِى مُهُورِ النِّسَاءِ فَتَكُونَ عَدَاوَةٌ"

"Do not exaggerate in the matter of the bride-prices of women, lest enmity result."

It is also narrated that a man came to the Messenger of God (صلع) and said: "O Messenger of God, I wish to marry this woman." He asked him what he would give her as her bride-gift. The man said: "I have nothing." The Prophet looked to a ring on his hand and asked:

"اَهَذَا الْخَاتَمُ لَكَ"

"Is this ring yours?"

The man replied: "Yes." He said:

"فَتَزَوَّجْهَا عَلَيْهِ"

"Then marry her upon it."

And it is said that a marriage should not be contracted without a *mahr*. The Prophet (صلع) did not fix for any of his wives more than twelve and a half *uqiyyas*, which is the equivalent of roughly forty rupees, and upon that he married Fatima (رض). The *Da'a'im* states that there is no fixed limit upon the *mahr*; rather this was the *mahr* that the Messenger of God (صلع) set for his wives, as though he loved equality among them in the matter. The brotherhood settled upon forty rupees as the *mahr*, so that equality might obtain between the wealthy among the people and the poor among them. And what has come from the Commander of the Faithful is that consummation is not to take place without a *mahr*, consummation being what is meant by entering upon her, for the *mahr* is not among the conditions of the contract. It is permissible to contract a marriage without naming the *mahr* and without paying it in advance, though it is fitting that the customary observances of the *mahr* precede the marriage, named and specified.

"وَمَنْ فَوَّضَ اَمْرَ الْمَهْرِ اِلَى الزَّوْجَةِ قَبْلَ الْعَقْدِ فَكَذَلِكَ بَعْدَ الدُّخُولِ لِاَنَّهُ قَدْ وَجَبَ الْاَدَاءُ عَلَيْهِ بَعْدَ الدُّخُولِ"

"Whoever delegates the matter of the bride-price to the wife before the contract, it is likewise after consummation, for payment has become obligatory upon him after consummation."

The husband does not simply determine the amount himself. Rather, the judge considers the woman's status among her peers, her circumstances and wealth, and calculates accordingly. If her state is elevated, a comparable *mahr* is set for her, equal to the *mahr* of the wives of the Prophet, which is forty rupees. If she is below that, he gives her less, in proportion to her station. If the setting of the *mahr* is assigned to the husband, she may accept whatever he gives her. As for the husband who does not have much to offer, he should give her something before he enters upon her, a *dirham* or whatever else it may be. The *mahr* is the right of the wife; neither the parents nor the guardian nor the agent nor anyone else has any share in it, unless she herself makes a gift of it to one of them, and that is hers to do. Likewise everything that reaches her by way of gifts at the time of the marriage, whether from the husband or from the relatives, is hers and is her right, unless it is made clear that what was given her was only for use, in which case she must return that thing and nothing besides.

On Mutual Rights in Marriage

God Almighty said:

"وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ"

"And live with them in kindness."

And He said:

"هُنَّ لِبَاسٌ لَكُمْ وَاَنْتُمْ لِبَاسٌ لَهُنَّ"

"They are a garment for you and you are a garment for them."

A hadith recommended by the Messenger of God (صلع) has been transmitted, a matter of which he continued to speak to the people until his tongue faltered and his speech grew weary. He said:

"الصَّلَاةَ الصَّلَاةَ وَمَا مَلَكَتْ اَيْمَانُكُمْ لَا تُكَلِّفُوهُمْ مَا لَا يُطِيقُونَ، اللهَ اللهَ فِى النِّسَاءِ فَاِنَّهُنَّ عَوَانٍ فِى اَيْدِيكُمْ اَخَذْتُمُوهُنَّ بِاَمَانَةِ اللهِ وَاسْتَحْلَلْتُمْ فُرُوجَهُنَّ بِكَلِمَةِ اللهِ"

"Prayer, prayer, and those whom your right hands possess: do not burden them beyond their capacity. Fear God, fear God regarding women, for they are captives in your hands. You took them by the trust of God and made lawful their private parts by the word of God."

The original purpose of marriage is the continuation of offspring and mutual assistance in the well-being of this life and the next. God Almighty made this clear in His words:

"وَخَلَقَ لَكُمْ مِنْ اَنْفُسِكُمْ اَزْوَاجًا لِتَسْكُنُوا اِلَيْهَا وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَكُمْ مَوَدَّةً وَرَحْمَةً"

"And He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy."

And He said:

"وَالَّذِينَ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا هَبْ لَنَا مِنْ اَزْوَاجِنَا وَذُرِّيَّاتِنَا قُرَّةَ اَعْيُنٍ وَاجْعَلْنَا لِلْمُتَّقِينَ اِمَامًا"

"And those who say: Our Lord, grant us from among our spouses and offspring comfort to our eyes, and make us leaders of the God-fearing."

It falls to the believers and to the righteous among the servants to make their married life like a garden beneath which springs flow, delightful in its meadows and its greenness to the eye, each of them seeking the good of the other. The Quran has made this plain in His words:

"وَلَهُنَّ مِثْلُ الَّذِى عَلَيْهِنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ"

"And they have rights similar to those due from them, in kindness."

And His words:

"وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ"

"And live with them in kindness."

And His words:

"وَلَا تُضَارُّوهُنَّ لِتُضَيِّقُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ"

"And do not harm them so as to straiten them."

The Prophet (صلع) said:

"خَيْرُكُمْ خَيْرُكُمْ لِاَهْلِهِ"

"The best of you is the best of you to his family."

It is fitting that the one who marries hold a wedding feast on the day of his marriage or on the day after it, once or twice, according to his means, so that the matter be made known. The like of this holds at the time of the *aqiqa*, at circumcision, and upon the arrival of a traveller from a journey, so that the benefits of travel are made plain to those who remain settled in their homelands.

It is not permissible for a woman to wear the dress of a man, nor for a man to wear the dress of a woman, nor is either to be compelled to jewellery or garments beyond what capacity allows.

A woman asked the Prophet (صلع), saying: "What is the right of the husband over the wife?" He said:

"لَا تَتَصَدَّقْ مِنْ مَالِهِ اِلَّا بِاِذْنِهِ وَلَا تَمْنَعْهُ نَفْسَهَا وَلَا تَصُومْ تَطَوُّعًا اِلَّا بِاِذْنِهِ وَلَا تَخْرُجْ مِنْ بَيْتِهِ اِلَّا بِاِذْنِهِ"

"She is not to give in charity from his wealth except by his leave, nor to refuse him herself, nor to fast voluntarily except by his leave, nor to leave his house except by his leave."

She said: "Then who among people has the greatest right over a man?" He said: "His mother." She said: "Then who among people has the greatest right over a woman?" He said: "Her husband."
