# Clean and Unclean: Meat, the Knife, and the Hunt

What has been made forbidden is set down in the verse:

«حُرِّمَتْ عَلَيْكُمُ الْمَيْتَةُ وَالدَّمُ وَلَحْمُ الْخِنْزِيرِ وَمَا أُهِلَّ لِغَيْرِ اللهِ بِهِ وَالْمُنْخَنِقَةُ وَالْمَوْقُوذَةُ وَالْمُتَرَدِّيَةُ وَالنَّطِيحَةُ وَمَا أَكَلَ السَّبُعُ إِلَّا مَا ذَكَّيْتُمْ وَمَا ذُبِحَ عَلَى النُّصُبِ»

"What has been made forbidden to you are carrion, blood, the flesh of swine, and that which has been slaughtered for other than Allah, as well as that which has been strangled, beaten to death, killed by a fall, gored, or partly devoured by a wild beast, unless you are able to slaughter it properly, and that which has been slaughtered on altars."

From Mawla Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) it is narrated that he was asked about something and said that it is not forbidden outright, but it is not permissible for one who is already full to eat it. He explained that foods fall into categories: the first category includes all grains, such as wheat and barley and the like; the second is the group of all pulses and plants, and everything in this class is not a natural food for human beings but may provide benefit and energy; and if there were no harm in it, it would not be forbidden to eat. The condition for permissibility is that it be used medicinally, or that it be less harmful than animal flesh. Also permitted is the flesh of camels, sheep, and similarly the meat of certain wild animals.

Among those are animals that have neither tusks nor claws, such as deer and rabbits. Among birds, those without a crop are permitted. From sea creatures, everything that has no scales is forbidden to eat. Regarding eggs: if both sides of the egg are distinguishable, one side indicating an animal that may be eaten and the other an animal that may not, then the egg may be eaten; otherwise it may not. Insects of the earth, such as snakes, scorpions, and the like, are forbidden to eat.

As for the food of the People of the Book, there is a difference of opinion. The flesh of polytheists, Jews, Christians, and Magians is forbidden. If one is driven by severe hunger, one may eat only enough to stave off death.

The consensus of the Imams (عليهم السلام) permits eating glands, the spleen, the liver, and similarly the turtle and crab after they have been removed from their shells. The horse, mule, and donkey, however, are subject to restrictions. As for the lamb, if it is a matter of necessity during a shortage of food, the donkey and mule may be eaten but one may not ride them afterward. If one is in dire need and fears for one's life and is compelled to eat carrion or a prohibited thing, one may eat until satisfied; but if the necessity persists, one must assess what is strictly needed to sustain oneself.

As for thirst for wine, it is doubly harmful until it is quenched, and there is no recourse but to avoid returning to it. If something falls into oil that has thereby become impure, the ruling follows the state of the oil. If a creature that bleeds dies in it and the oil is liquid, the oil is spoiled; if the oil is solid, then what surrounds the dead thing is spoiled, but the rest may be eaten. As for what is cooked in a defiled vessel, the one in that situation has a choice: either to eat what was cooked or to leave it. The first gift goes to the one who makes use of it. Muslims may eat what is sold in the markets by way of meat, whether or not it comes from a slaughterer whose background is unknown, provided it is sold in the general Muslim marketplace.

As for water, the verse says:

«وَأَنْزَلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً طَهُورًا»

"And We have sent down from the sky pure water."

This includes rainwater, river water, spring water, and well water; all of it is permissible to drink. Fruits and their juices are permissible to consume unless they have been boiled to the point that the liquid has changed and become intoxicating; if one drinks it and becomes intoxicated, then whatever causes intoxication is forbidden by the Shari'a. As for cheese, much of it is produced and some of it is sold in the markets, and mention has been made of how it is made lawfully.

Concerning ritual slaughter, and whose slaughter may be eaten, the verse says:

«فَكُلُوا مِمَّا ذُكِرَ اسْمُ اللهِ عَلَيْهِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ بِآيَاتِهِ مُؤْمِنِينَ»

"So eat of that over which the name of Allah has been mentioned, if you are believers in His signs."

The Messenger of Allah (ص) spoke regarding the animal and the patience required in slaughtering beasts: even if a limb is left hanging after the cut, the slaughter is considered fully lawful; but if the limb is severed and cut before life has fully departed, it is forbidden to eat it.

Ali (ع) said that when one of them slaughters an animal, he should turn it toward the qibla and lay it on the ground, and then say:

«بِسْمِ اللهِ وَاللهُ أَكْبَرُ»

"In the name of Allah, and Allah is the Greatest,"

and cut across the throat with a sharp blade; he should not press down upon it until it has died. The blood should flow, once it is known that the animal has died from the cut, and one waits until it grows still and dies; only then is the head severed and the animal skinned.

As for forgetting to mention the name of Allah, or mentioning it in a manner that falls short of proper intention, or cutting off the animal's head outright without the proper method, or slaughtering without facing the qibla, there is no harm in eating it if this was done out of ignorance or forgetfulness. But whoever does so deliberately, in opposition to the rulings of Allah, his slaughter is not eaten. If cattle, buffalo, or similar animals become agitated and uncontrollable at the time of slaughter, so that the throat cut cannot be performed, or fall into a pit from which they cannot be brought out and the throat cannot be reached, then one may strike and stab the animal with a spear or sword wherever one is able to reach it, and it is permissible to eat it.

The fetus of an animal whose mother has been lawfully slaughtered belongs to the one who slaughtered it and is lawful. It is also permissible for a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian to slaughter if they mention the name of Allah. But whoever omits the name of Allah, whether from among them or from among the Muslims, and was accused of doing so deliberately, the animal he slaughtered is not permissible to eat.

Whoever apostatizes from the religion of Islam, whether he becomes a Jew or a Christian or joins any other sect, his slaughter is not permissible. Permission is granted for the slaughter of the blind man, the woman, and the boy before he reaches adulthood, if they do it properly. Likewise the mute, if he understands and forms the intention of the name of Allah. There is no harm in purchasing and eating what is sold in Muslim markets, whether meat, eggs, milk, or clarified butter, and no need to investigate the slaughterer. Fish and locusts taken from the sea and land are lawful to eat, whether the one who caught them was a Muslim, a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian. As for what polytheists catch and bring alive, it may be eaten.

Concerning hunting, and what is lawful of the catch and what is forbidden of it, the verse says:

«أُحِلَّ لَكُمْ صَيْدُ الْبَحْرِ وَطَعَامُهُ مَتَاعًا لَكُمْ وَلِلسَّيَّارَةِ وَحُرِّمَ عَلَيْكُمْ صَيْدُ الْبَرِّ مَا دُمْتُمْ حُرُمًا وَإِذَا حَلَلْتُمْ فَاصْطَادُوا»

"Lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food, a provision for you and for travellers; but forbidden to you is the game of the land so long as you are in a state of ihram. And when you have come out of ihram, then hunt."

Concerning the bird, the report is that when it has been held and then flies away and another takes it, it is lawful for the one who takes it.

There is no harm in eating what trained hunting animals seize, such as dogs, cheetahs, falcons, hawks, and other beasts of the land and birds of prey, provided the name of Allah Almighty was mentioned when they were sent out, and provided it is known that the animal died from the effect of the hunter's strike. If it is caught while still alive, it is slaughtered and then eaten. If the name was forgotten at the moment the dogs or other animals were released, there is no harm in eating what they killed. As for the dog of a Magian, if a Muslim, a Jew, or a Christian has trained it and takes it and releases it, then what it seizes of the game is lawful to eat.

If a man is in a position where he cannot reach the game to slaughter it, and kills it with a sword, or strikes it with an arrow or a spear and wounds it in any part of its body, there is no harm in eating it. The flesh of the wild ass is lawful, and its slaughter is distinct from that of the domesticated donkey. If game is shot and struck and then disappears, and afterward is found fallen from a height or from an elevated place and dead, its flesh is eaten if it is known that it died from the shot; otherwise it is not eaten. What is struck by stone or pellet is not eaten unless one is able to slaughter it with something having an iron edge before it dies. There is no harm in eating what a polytheist or a Magian has caught of fish and locusts, provided they are brought alive to a Muslim, a Jew, or a Christian, and die in the hands of those mentioned.

Concerning dress and adornment, the verse says:

«وَأَمَّا بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثْ»

"And as for the favour of your Lord, proclaim it."

And the hadith:

«لِيُنْفِقْ كُلُّ ذِي سَعَةٍ مِنْ سَعَتِهِ. مَنِ اتَّخَذَ شَعْرًا فَلْيُحْسِنْ إِلَيْهِ، وَمَنِ اتَّخَذَ زَوْجًا فَلْيُكْرِمْهُ، وَمَنِ اتَّخَذَ نَعْلًا فَلْيَسْتَجِدْهُ، وَمَنِ اتَّخَذَ دَابَّةً فَلْيُسْمِنْهَا، وَمَنِ اتَّخَذَ ثَوْبًا فَلْيُنَظِّفْهُ. كَرَامَةُ الثَّوْبِ طَيُّهُ، كَرَامَةُ الْبَيْتِ كَنْسُهُ»

"Let every man of means spend from his means. Whoever keeps hair, let him treat it well; whoever takes a spouse, let him honour that spouse; whoever takes a sandal, let him make it good; whoever keeps a beast, let him feed it well; whoever takes a garment, let him keep it clean. The honouring of a garment is its folding; the honouring of a house is its sweeping."

Mawlana Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about a man, and the questioner said: "O son of the Messenger of Allah, is it counted as extravagance for a man to take many garments, adorning himself with them and preserving some of them by the use of others?" He said that this is not extravagance.

A man said to him: "The dearest of people to me is one who wears coarse cloth, eats food without seasoning, and humbles himself, so that the mark of reverence is seen upon him." He said:

«وَيْحَكَ! إِنَّمَا الْخُشُوعُ فِي الْقَلْبِ لَا فِي اللِّبَاسِ. وَمَا عَلِمْتَ أَنَّ نَبِيَّنَا يُوسُفَ نَبِىَّ ابْنَ النَّبِىِّ كَانَ يَلِيسُ أَقْبِيَةَ الدِّيبَاجِ مُزَوَّرَةً بِالذَّهَبِ وَيَجْلِسُ مَجْلِسَ إِلَى فِرْعَوْنَ يُحَلْدِيبِنُ النَّاسَ وَاِنَّمَاكَانَ النَّاسُ يَحْتَاجُونَ إِلَى عَدْلِهِ وَقِسْطِهِ»

"Woe to you! Reverence is in the heart, not in the clothing. Did you not know that our Prophet Yusuf, a prophet and the son of a prophet, used to wear robes of brocade embroidered with gold, and would sit in the assembly of Pharaoh and judge among the people? The people had need only of his justice and his equity."

It is recorded that Sufyan al-Thawri once visited Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) and saw fine garments upon him. Sufyan said: "O son of the Messenger of Allah, you narrate to us that Ali (ع) used to wear coarse clothing, yet you wear finer than that." He said: "Woe to you, Sufyan! Ali (ع) lived in a time of straitness, and Allah has expanded His provision upon us; and it is recommended for one upon whom Allah has expanded His provision that the mark of it be seen upon him."

Imam Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) mentioned what is lawful of clothing in a summary word, saying that everything the earth brings forth may be worn, and prayer performed in it, without harm. Every animal whose flesh is lawful, its hide may be worn without harm if it has been lawfully slaughtered, and likewise its wool, its hair, and its fur. If it has not been lawfully slaughtered, there is no harm in wearing it outside the time of prayer, but prayer is not performed in it. The Messenger of Allah (ص) loved white garments of cotton, and used to say:

«اِلْبَسُوا الْبِيضَ وَكَفِّنُوا فِيهَا مَوْتَاكُمْ»

"Wear white, and shroud your dead in it."

If a man wears silk and prays in it without intending display or pride before his fellows, there is no harm in it. It is reported of Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that he was seen seated upon a carpet bearing figures, and when this was mentioned to him he said that the Sunnah permits it.

Jewelry stands in place of adornment for a woman's neck, hand, and foot, and it is not fitting for her to strip herself of ornament, whether it be of gold or of silver. But a woman is forbidden to strike the ground with her foot so that the sound of her anklets is heard. For men, adornment with gold ornament is not permitted, though there is no harm in a small amount for young girls and for women.
