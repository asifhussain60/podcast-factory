# Clean and Unclean: Meat, the Knife, and the Hunt

### What is Lawful and What is Forbidden of Food

God Almighty said:

> حرمت عليكم الميتة والدم ولحم الخنزير وما أهل لغير الله به والمنخنقة والموقوذة والمتردية والنطيحة وما أكل السبع إلا ما ذكيتم وما ذبح على النصب

"Forbidden to you are carrion, blood, the flesh of swine, that over which a name other than God's has been invoked, the strangled, the beaten to death, that killed by a fall, that gored to death, and that which a wild beast has partly devoured, unless you are able to slaughter it properly; and that which has been slaughtered upon altars."

From Mawla Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) it is related that he set out what is lawful and what is forbidden in a summary saying. He said that what is lawful to eat of what the earth brings forth falls into three classes of food:

1. The class of grains in all their kinds, such as wheat and barley and the rest;
2. The class of fruits, all of them;
3. The class of vegetables and growing plants.

Whatever of these holds nourishment for man, and benefit, and strength, is lawful to eat; and whatever of them holds harm is forbidden to eat, except in the case of treatment by way of medicine.

As for the flesh of animals that is lawful: the cow, the camel, the sheep, and likewise what comes from them. Of the flesh of wild animals, everything that has neither fang nor claw, such as the gazelle and the hare that have cloven hooves. Of the flesh of birds, everything that has a crop. Of the game of the sea, everything that has scales. What falls outside these classes is forbidden to eat. As for eggs, the egg whose two ends differ is lawful to eat; the egg whose two ends are alike belongs to the eggs of what is not eaten. Of the creeping things of the earth, such as the snake and the scorpion and their like, eating them is forbidden.

A dispensation is given concerning the food of the People of the Book and of others among the sects, where the food contains nothing slaughtered by an idolater. As for the slaughter of the Jews, the Christians, and the Magians, eating it is lawful when they have slaughtered according to the conditions of slaughter.

The Imams (عليهم السلام) held it disagreeable to eat the glands, the marrow of the spine, the spleen, and the gall bladder, and likewise the tortoise, the crab, and what comes out of shells. As for the horse, the mule, and the donkey, they are for riding and for carrying; yet the horse, if it is slaughtered out of necessity, is lawful to eat, unlike the donkey and the mule, for those two are forbidden.

If a man is in need and fears for his life and is compelled to eat carrion or a forbidden thing, he eats once, until he is satisfied. If he is compelled by thirst to something that will settle it and finds only wine, he drinks once, until he is no longer thirsty, and it is not for him to return to that until he is compelled to it a second time.

If there falls into a condiment or into oil one of the creeping things of the earth, such as flies, or what has no flowing blood, there is no harm in eating it once the thing has been removed. If an animal that has blood dies in it, such as the mouse and its like, then if the substance is liquid it is spoiled, and if it is solid what lies around the dead thing is spoiled and the rest is eaten. Such is the dispensation of the Shari'a. But if a man finds something of this distasteful, the choice is his: he may eat if he wishes, or he may hold back from it, or he may give it to one who will use it and eat it among the Muslims.

Concerning drink, God Almighty said:

> وأنزلنا من السماء ماء طهورا

"And We sent down from the sky pure water."

This includes the water of the sky, of rivers, of springs, and of wells. Likewise the milk of every animal whose flesh is eaten, and the juice of fruits, whether boiled or unboiled, so long as it has not altered and does not intoxicate, is lawful to drink. If it intoxicates, then it has become wine, whose drinking is forbidden in the Shari'a.

### How the Slaughter is to be Performed, and Whose Slaughter is Eaten

God Almighty said:

> فكلوا مما ذكر اسم الله عليه إن كنتم بآياته مؤمنين

"So eat of that over which the name of God has been mentioned, if you are believers in His signs."

It is related in the hadith that the Prophet forbade cruelty to animals and forbade the maiming of beasts. If a limb remains hanging after it has been cut through, and the slaughter follows, the animal is wholly lawful; but if a limb is broken and severed before the slaughter, eating it is forbidden.

Ali (ع) said:

> إذا ذبح أحدكم البهيمة فعليه أن يستقبل بها القبلة ويضعها على الأرض ثم يقول بسم الله والله أكبر

"When one of you slaughters a beast, let him turn it toward the qibla and lay it upon the ground, then say: In the name of God, and God is the Greatest."

Then he cuts the head of the windpipe with a sharp blade, and does not sever the spinal cord until the animal has died, and lets the blood flow, so that he knows it has died by the slaughter. He waits until it grows still and dies, and only after that does he cut off its head and strip off its hide.

If a man forgets the mention of the name, or mentions God over it with glorification and declaration of His unity, or forgets that also, or slaughters without facing the qibla, or the knife runs ahead and cuts off the head, or he cuts below the place of slaughter, there is no harm in eating it, so long as this came from ignorance or forgetfulness. If he abandons that deliberately, in opposition to the rulings of God, his slaughter is not eaten.

If cattle, buffalo, or other animals bolt at the time of slaughter and resist, so that a man is unable to slaughter them, or if the animal falls into a well and cannot be brought out and its throat cannot be reached, then he strikes or stabs it with a spear or a sword wherever he is able, and it dies, and eating it is permitted.

The Prophet held it disagreeable to slaughter a milk-giving animal, or one bearing young, for one who finds another.

The slaughter of the Jew, the Christian, and the Magian is permitted when they mention the name over it. Whoever among them, or among the people of Islam, abandons the mention deliberately, and is under suspicion of abandoning it, his slaughter is not lawful to eat. And whoever apostatizes from the religion of Islam, whether he became a Jew or a Christian or joined one of the sects, his slaughter is not permitted.

A dispensation is given for the slaughter performed by a blind man, a woman, and a boy before he reaches maturity, when they do it well; and likewise the mute, when he understands and forms in his mind the mention of the name and gestures toward it.

There is no harm in buying and eating what is sold in the markets of the Muslims of meat, eggs, milk, and clarified butter, without investigating the one who slaughtered. What is hunted of the creatures of the sea, of fish and of locusts, is lawful to eat, whether the one who took them was a Muslim, a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian; and what idolaters hunt and bring alive is eaten.

### Hunting, and What is Lawful and Forbidden in It

God Almighty said:

> أحل لكم صيد البحر وطعامه متاعا لكم وللسيارة وحرم عليكم صيد البحر ما دمتم حرما وإذا حللتم فاصطادوا

"Lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food, a provision for you and for travellers; and the game of the sea is forbidden to you so long as you remain in the state of ihram; and when you have left that state, then hunt."

It is related that the game of God's sanctuary is held disagreeable; and if the game takes flight and departs, it may be taken. The Prophet said that a bird, when it is held and then flies and is taken again, is lawful for the one who takes it.

There is no harm in eating what the trained birds and beasts of prey seize: dogs, hunting leopards, falcons, hawks, and others of the beasts of the earth and the birds, when the name of God Almighty has been mentioned at the moment of sending them out, and when it is known that the animal died from the stroke of the hunting beast. If it is reached while still alive, it is slaughtered and eaten. If the mention of the name was forgotten at the sending out of the dogs and the rest, there is no harm in eating what they have killed. As for the dog of a Magian, when a Muslim, a Jew, or a Christian trains it, takes it, and sends it out, it is permitted to eat what it seizes of the game.

If a man is in such a position that he cannot reach the game to slaughter it, and he kills it with the sword, or strikes it with an arrow or a spear and wounds it in any part of its body, there is no harm in eating it. The flesh of the wild ass is lawful, and its slaughter is unlike that of the domestic donkey.

If a man shoots at game and strikes it, and the animal disappears and then falls from a height or from an elevated place and dies, its flesh is eaten if he knows that it died from the shot; otherwise it is not eaten. What is struck with a stone or a pellet is not eaten, unless its slaughter is reached with something having an edge of iron before it dies.

There is no harm in eating what an idolater or a Magian hunts of fish and locusts, when he brings them alive to a Muslim, a Jew, or a Christian, and they die in the hands of those named.

### Dress and Ornament

God Almighty said:

> وَأَمَّا بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ تُحِلُّ

"And as for the favour of your Lord, by it is the lawful made lawful for you."

And in the hadith:

> لِيُنْفِقْ كُلُّ ذِي سَعَةٍ مِنْ سَعَتِهِ

"Let every man of means spend out of his means."

Whoever keeps hair, let him tend it well; whoever takes a wife, let him honour her; whoever takes a sandal, let him keep it in good repair; whoever takes a riding beast, let him give it its full measure; whoever takes a garment, let him keep it clean. The honouring of a garment is its cleanliness; the honouring of a house is its sweeping.

Mawlana Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) was asked about a man, and the questioner said: "O son of the Messenger of God, is it counted as extravagance that a man should keep many garments, adorning himself with them and preserving some of them by means of others?" He said: "No, that is not extravagance."

A man said to him that the dearest of people to him was one who wore coarse cloth and ate food without seasoning and made himself humble, so that the mark of humility was seen upon him. He said:

> ويحك إنما الخشوع في القلب لا في اللباس، وَمَا عَلِمْتَ اَنَّ نَبِيَّنَا يُوسُفَ نَبِىَّ ابْنَ النَّبِىِّ كَانَ يَلْبَسُ أَقْبِيَةَ الدِّيبَاجِ مُزَرَّرَةً بِالذَّهَبِ وَيَجْلِسُ مَجْلِسَ فِرْعَوْنَ يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَ النَّاسِ وَإِنَّمَا كَانَ النَّاسُ يَحْتَاجُونَ إِلَى عَدْلِهِ وَقِسْطِهِ

"Woe to you! Reverence is in the heart, not in the dress. Did you not know that our prophet Yusuf was a prophet, the son of a prophet, who used to wear robes of brocade buttoned with gold, and sit in the council of Pharaoh judging between the people? For the people had need only of his justice and his equity."

Sufyan al-Thawri entered upon Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) and saw fine garments upon him, and said: "O son of the Messenger of God, you relate to us that Ali (ع) used to wear coarse garments, and you wear better than that." He said:

> ويحك يا سفيان إن عليا كان في زمان ضيق وإن الله قد وسع علينا ويستحب لمن وسع الله عليه أن يرى أثر ذلك عليه

"Woe to you, Sufyan. Ali lived in a time of straitness, and God has widened His provision upon us; and it is fitting for one upon whom God has widened His provision that the mark of it be seen upon him."

Imam Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) set out what is lawful of dress in a summary saying. He said that everything the earth brings forth may be worn without harm, and prayer performed in it. Every animal whose flesh is lawful, there is no harm in wearing its hide when it has been slaughtered according to the rite, nor its wool, its hair, or its fur when it has been so slaughtered. If it has not been slaughtered according to the rite, there is no harm in wearing it outside the time of prayer, but prayer is not performed in it.

The Messenger of God loved white garments of cotton, and he used to say:

> البسوا البياض وكفنوا فيه موتاكم

"Wear white, and shroud your dead in it."

If a man wears silk and prays in it, not intending by it display and boasting before his fellows, there is no harm in it. And it is related of Ja'far ibn Muhammad (ع) that he was seen sitting upon a carpet bearing figures, and when this was mentioned to him he said that the Sunnah is that it be trodden upon.

For a woman, the neck, the hand, and the foot are the places of ornament, and it is not fitting for her to strip herself of ornament, whether her ornament be of gold or of silver. A woman is forbidden to strike the ground with her foot so that the sound of her anklet is heard. For men, adorning oneself with the ornament of gold is forbidden, though there is no harm in it for young girls and for women.
