# The Dignity of Earning Your Bread

God Most High says:

> **فانتشروا في الأرض وابتغوا من فضل الله**
>
> "Travel the earth and seek of God's bounty."

The Messenger of God (ص) said:

> **التاجر الصدوق يحشر يوم القيامة مع الصديقين والشهداء**
>
> "The truthful merchant will be raised on the Day of Resurrection with the truthful ones and the martyrs."

And he said:

> **من طلب الرزق حلالاً وتعففاً عن المسألة وسعياً على عياله وتعطفاً على جاره لقي الله ووجهه كالبدر**
>
> "Whoever seeks provision by lawful means, keeping himself above begging, striving for his dependants and showing kindness to his neighbour, will meet God with a face like the full moon."

He also said: Trade, and be forthright in seeking. And he said:

> **لأن يأخذ أحدكم حبله فيحتطب على ظهره خير من أن يأتي رجلاً أعطاه الله من فضله فيسأله أعطاه أو منعه**
>
> "That one of you should take his rope and gather firewood upon his back is better than that he come to a man whom God has given of His bounty and beg from him, whether that man gives him or refuses him."

It is related of him that during the expedition of Tabūk he passed a strong young man driving fat camels. Some of those present said: O Messenger of God, if only the strength of this one, his vigour and his substance were spent in the way of God, that would be better. The Messenger of God (ص) heard them and said: "What is this beast of yours, and for what do you labour upon it?" The young man said: I have a wife and children, so I earn upon it, and I spend upon my dependants, and I keep them from having to ask of people, and I discharge a debt that lies upon me. He said: "Perhaps it is otherwise than that?" He said: No. When the young man turned away, the Messenger of God (ص) said: "If he is truthful, he has a reward like the reward of the fighter, and the reward of the pilgrim, and the reward of the one who performs the lesser pilgrimage."

And he (ص) said:

> **إذا أعسر أحدكم فليخرج من بلده ويضرب في الأرض يبتغي من فضل الله ولا يغم نفسه وأهله**
>
> "When one of you falls into hardship, let him go out from his town and strike through the earth seeking of God's bounty, and let him not bring grief upon himself and his household."

It is related of him also that he said:

> **سبعة يظلهم الله يوم لا ظل إلا ظله رجل خرج ضارباً في الأرض يطلب من فضل الله ما يكف به نفسه ويعود به على عياله**
>
> "Seven whom God shades on a day when there is no shade but His shade: among them a man who goes out striking through the earth, seeking of God's bounty what will suffice him and what he may return with upon his dependants."

And he said:

> **إن الله يحب من العبد أن يكون سهل البيع وسهل الشراء وسهل القضاء وسهل الاقتضاء**
>
> "God loves of a servant that he be easy in selling, easy in buying, easy in settling what he owes, and easy in claiming what is owed to him."

He would describe trade and hold it dear, and he would counsel people toward it, because in it lies much good and much blessing. Once the Messenger of God (ص) passed by the traders, who in those days were called brokers, and said to them:

> **ألا إني لا أسميكم السماسرة ولكني أسميكم التجار والتاجر هو الفاجر في النار**
>
> "I do not name you brokers; rather I name you merchants. And the merchant who deals corruptly is in the Fire."

At this they shut their doors and held back from trading. The Messenger of God (ص) came out and said: "What ails the people?" It was said: O Messenger of God, they heard what you said yesterday, and they have held back. He said: "And I say it again today: except whoever takes his right and gives what is due."

In this same sense there is a narration that a man said to the Commander of the Believers (ع): O Commander of the Believers, I wish to trade. He said: "Have you gained understanding in the religion of God?" The man said: Something of that. He said: "Woe to you! Understanding first, then trade."

And he (ص) used to say:

> **إني لأبغض الرجل يكون كسلاناً في أمر دنياه لأنه إذا كان كسلاناً في أمر دنياه فهو عن أمر آخرته أكسل**
>
> "I dislike the man who is lazy in the affair of his world, for if he is lazy in the affair of his world, he is lazier still concerning the affair of his hereafter."

Among his sayings is this:

> **ما من غدوة أحدكم في سبيل الله بأعظم من غدوته يطلب فيها لولده وعياله ما يصلحهم**
>
> "No morning setting-out of one of you in the way of God is greater than his setting-out to seek for his child and his dependants what will make them well."

It is related that a man asked Imam Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (ع) to pray to God for him that He grant him provision. He said: "I will not pray for you. Seek, as you have been commanded." Then he said: "It behoves the Muslim to seek provision until the heat of the sun reaches him."

He counselled one of his companions, saying:

> **لا تكن دواراً في الأسواق ولا تكن شراء دقائق الأشياء بنفسك فإنه لا ينبغي للمرء المسلم من أهل الدين والحسب أن يلي شراء دقائق الأشياء بنفسه**
>
> "Do not be one who circles about in the markets, nor one who buys the trifling things himself; for it does not befit the Muslim of religion and standing to attend himself to the purchase of trifling things."

One day he looked at a man of the Companions carrying a bunch of herbs in his hand, and said that it is disliked for a man of nobility to carry the meanest of things, lest anyone become emboldened against him.
