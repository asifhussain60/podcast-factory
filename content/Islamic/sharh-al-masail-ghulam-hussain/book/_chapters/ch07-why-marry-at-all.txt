# Why Marry at All

And among the signs of God is marriage itself. God Almighty said:

> ﴿وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ أَنْ خَلَقَ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا لِتَسْكُنُوا إِلَيْهَا وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَكُمْ مَوَدَّةً وَرَحْمَةً إِنَّ فِي ذَلِكَ لَآيَاتٍ لِقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ﴾

"And among His signs is that He created for you from yourselves spouses, that you may dwell in tranquility with them, and He placed between you affection and mercy. Indeed in that are signs for a people who give thought."

And the hadith:

> «مَنْ أَحَبَّ أَنْ يَلْقَى اللهَ طَاهِرًا مُطَهَّرًا فَلْيَسْتَعْفِفْ بِزَوْجَتِهِ، وَمَنْ أَحَبَّ أَنْ يَكُونَ عَلَى سُنَّتِي فَإِنَّ مِنْ سُنَّتِي النِّكَاحَ»

"Whoever loves to meet God pure and purified, let him keep himself chaste through his wife; and whoever loves to be upon my Sunnah, then of my Sunnah is marriage."

It is narrated from 'Ali (ع) that he said none of the companions of the Messenger of God (صلع) married but that the Messenger of God said to him that he had completed his religion and his faith. And it is said that among the manners of the prophets are cleanliness, the trimming of hair, the use of perfume, and frequent recourse to spouses, meaning women; for the more the servant increases in faith, the more he increases in love for his wife.

He (صلع) said that whoever abandons marriage from fear of the burden of a household has thought ill of his Lord, for God has promised His servants and said:

> ﴿إِنْ يَكُونُوا فُقَرَاءَ يُغْنِهِمُ اللهُ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ وَاللهُ وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ﴾

"If they are poor, God will enrich them from His bounty, and God is Vast and Knowing."

And he used to say:

> «لَا رَهْبَانِيَّةَ فِي الْإِسْلَامِ»

"There is no monasticism in Islam."

He forbade celibacy for men and for women alike, and he said:

> «تَنَاكَحُوا تَكَاثَرُوا فَإِنِّي أُبَاهِي بِكُمُ الْأُمَمَ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ»

"Marry and increase, for I shall boast of you before the nations on the Day of Resurrection."

God Almighty, having deposited in the human being manifold interests, faculties and drives such as hunger, thirst, sleep and wakefulness and others, likewise deposited in him the desire for marriage, which appears in him at the time of puberty. And just as the Messenger of God (صلع) set down a law for every one of these drives, so too did he set for this drive its regulating limits, so that a person might be kept from resembling the beasts and might travel upon the road of the prophets. For the human being was created social by nature, and cannot manage the whole of his affairs without the help of another in them. The nearest of those who assist him is his wife. When he goes out from his house to earn his sustenance and to store away a portion of it against a time when he cannot go out, whether through illness or weakness or some other cause, and when he seeks to guard his children, he must have someone who keeps his house and his furnishings. For this no one is suited except the woman. The mother is the helper in the man's living, in the ordering of his household, in the sponsorship of his children, their maintenance and their upbringing. And as the soundness of the house is not achieved without the woman, so the soundness of the woman is not achieved without the man.

These two, the husband and the wife, are the house in truth, whether they dwell beneath a tree in the desert or in some lofty dwelling among the buildings. The house in which they live is called a house only by figure of speech. The true house is the school of feeling and of upbringing, the place where human character is acquired, where children receive their first nurture, and where the greatest part of their formation comes to them from their mother. In this true house the children obtain the highest foundations of humanity and the lessons of upbringing, and what they attain in the season of childhood accompanies them ever after. It falls, therefore, upon a man to choose from the community the best woman for this school, so that she may be fit for the teaching of children; and none is fit for that except one sound in her body, sound in her soul, and correct in her knowledge. If a woman is without a husband, or a man without a wife, then neither of them has a household in truth, even though they dwell in lofty chambers.

What follows from this is that the house is with the woman and the woman is with the house. The aim of the religious Muslim in marriage is nothing other than the obtaining of children and chastity against what is forbidden, as the Qur'an indicates in His words:

> ﴿مُحْصِنِينَ غَيْرَ مُسَافِحِينَ﴾

"Taking them in chaste wedlock, not in licentiousness."

That is, the aim of marriage is not the mere discharge of appetite as an animal discharges it, but entry into the fortress of devotion and chastity. For the word مُحْصِنِين is derived from الإحصان, the verbal noun of الحصن, and الحصن in the language is the fortress. What is meant is that one who marries with a good intention has fortified and protected himself from bodily diseases and from deficiency. The unmarried man, though he be free in purpose from whisperings and the dangers of appetite, is not free from bodily diseases such as ill temper, coarseness of nature, derangement, constriction and torpor and the like. Rather, there increase through marriage the good qualities of character: gravity, tranquility, forbearance, devotion, patience, gentleness and others.

Here a question arises. Since the appetite for marriage was placed in the human being by nature, as the other natural drives were placed in him, he is compelled from within himself toward its satisfaction, as is seen in the state of the corrupt and dissolute man, who finds in himself a compulsion after committing the vile act despite its being forbidden. What, then, is intended by the encouragement to marry that comes from the Lawgiver in this chapter, as it is mentioned in the Da'a'im?

The encouragement in this chapter is built upon two causes, one religious and one worldly.

The religious cause is the encouragement of those who travel the road of the Hereafter and who practice renunciation in this world. When they are absorbed in longing for the Hereafter, they renounce worldly attachments, and for this reason they are seen not to marry at all, since marriage is the first step upon the stairway of worldly attachments. It is narrated from 'Ali (ع) that he said: 'Uthman ibn Maz'un came to the Messenger of God (صلع) and said, "O Messenger of God, the promptings of my soul have overcome me, and I would do nothing until I consult you." The Messenger of God (صلع) said, "With what has your soul prompted you, O 'Uthman?" He said, "I wished to wander the earth." He said, "Do not wander in it, for the wandering of my community is to the mosques." He said, "Then I resolved to forbid meat to myself." The Messenger of God (صلع) said to him, "Do not do so, for I desire it, and by God, were I to ask God to feed it to me every day He would do so." He said, "And I resolved to castrate myself." He said, "O 'Uthman, he is not of us who does that to himself. The castration of my community is fasting." He said, "And I resolved to forbid myself Khawla my wife." He said, "Do not do so, O 'Uthman," and he mentioned the benefits of living with a wife which are set down in the Da'a'im. Then the Messenger of God (صلع) placed his hand upon his breast and said, "O 'Uthman, do not turn away from my Sunnah, for whoever turns away from my Sunnah, the angels shall turn away from him on the Day of Resurrection and shall turn his face from my Pool."

In this same meaning there came a narration from Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع). He was asked about a man in whom the fear of God had so entered that he abandoned women and wholesome food and would not raise his head to the sky, out of magnification of God Almighty. He said, "As for your words concerning the abandonment of women, you know what the Messenger of God had of them. As for your words concerning the abandonment of wholesome food, the Messenger of God (صلع) loved meat and honey. As for your words that the fear of God so entered him that he cannot raise his head to the sky, humility is in the heart. And who was more fearful of God than the Messenger of God (صلع)? Yet he did not do this. Then he recited the words of God, mighty and majestic:

> ﴿لَقَدْ كَانَ لَكُمْ فِي رَسُولِ اللهِ أُسْوَةٌ حَسَنَةٌ لِمَنْ كَانَ يَرْجُو اللهَ وَالْيَوْمَ الْآخِرَ﴾

"Indeed there is for you in the Messenger of God a fair example, for whoever hopes in God and the Last Day."

The encouragement from the Lawgiver for the worldly cause is the encouragement of the destitute, the indigent and the poor, when they do not marry because of the narrowness of provision and livelihood. For this reason it was said that whoever does not find a dower should give even a little of it. Upon this there are narrations from Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) concerning the smallness of the dower: that if a believer finds nothing, then let her dower be a dirham, or a garment, or some small thing; and if he cannot find any of these, then let him teach her a sura of the Qur'an. And concerning the burden of household and the straitness of livelihood, God Almighty said:

> ﴿وَأَنْكِحُوا الْأَيَامَى مِنْكُمْ وَالصَّالِحِينَ مِنْ عِبَادِكُمْ وَإِمَائِكُمْ إِنْ يَكُونُوا فُقَرَاءَ يُغْنِهِمُ اللهُ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ وَاللهُ وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ﴾

"And marry off the single among you and the righteous among your male and female servants. If they are poor, God will enrich them from His bounty, and God is Vast and Knowing."

Whoever does not find a wife to marry, and awaits ease from God at its time, upon each of the two, the man and the woman, is to live in chastity and devotion until each finds what is hoped for, and there is no blame in that. There is no doubt that the destitute person, alone, whether man or woman, cannot repel poverty from himself without the help of another in his earning, especially in the Arabian Peninsula, where there is no livelihood except agriculture, and where in the years of drought the houses are left without food and without provision. There a man must labor with himself and his dependents in cultivation from the time of sowing to the season of harvest, and their affair does not stand there with one woman, but with two women or up to four; the more so when hostilities fall out among them by reason of the multitude of tribes and the difference of their creeds,

and the enmity that runs among them from generation to generation, so that they reckon the killing of a human being as the killing of a single animal, there being no true government over the subjects and their rights; and the more so when war appears and the men of both parties are killed, and there remains for the widow and the orphan none to guard them from those who overpower and plunder them. They are content, then, to enter under the protection of men and to preserve themselves by marriage, even with one who already has a wife before them. Likewise, because the land of the Arabs, by its nearness to the equator, brings upon its people intense heat, the women there lose their beauty and their bloom before the men do, and grow weak before them. And when a woman is with a man, the days of her menstruation, the days of her pregnancy and delivery, and the days of her nursing require that he keep away from her; or there arises some cause among the causes to be mentioned in the chapter that follows. So it is not possible for the village man to endure with one wife, whether he seeks chastity or seeks rest for himself in the earning of provision in a state of destitution. From the time of the Prophet (صلع) to this hour, the necessity that calls for plurality of wives has remained standing among them.

Plurality of wives, viewed against the causes just mentioned, is not confined to war alone. There are many other causes which occur among peoples other than the Arabs as well, of which one may be mentioned. When strife arises between the great powers and war is kindled between them, men perish on both sides. Taking the reckoning from the beginning of this recent twentieth century, there is the war that fell out between the English and the Boer people in South Africa, in which souls of men and leaders were lost; and Japan, in whose war five hundred thousand men were lost from the two sides; and many wars besides these between small states and great, for differing purposes among them. By their consequence there falls a scarcity of men in those places, without escape.

For clarification the condition of two or three of these states may be set down, such as the English, the French and the Germans, turning attention to the pages of their own reckoning of men and women, that it may be known. In the reckoning of the French, the women exceeding the men were four hundred and twenty-three thousand four hundred and nine. In the reckoning of the Germans, after the point of equality between men and women among them, the women exceed by eight hundred and eighty-seven thousand six hundred and forty-eight,

and there is none among the men to marry them, since each man already has a woman equal to him in the count. In the reckoning of the English, the number of women exceeding men was one hundred and eighty-two thousand six hundred and nineteen. Let those who forbid plurality of wives be asked concerning the women of these three states alone, apart from others, whose number reaches twenty-four hundred thousand: what is the arrangement they have set for them, or the law they have codified for them? For a law must accord with nature. Either they say that these women do not desire what other women desire, or they permit them fornication. Do they not look at the children of the bathhouses in those kingdoms, to what number they reach every year, by the thousands? By these vile acts they have ruined the houses whose owners are afflicted with immorality toward those who are not their wives. Yet with all the shortness of their sight in this chapter, the opponents censure Islam and say that the Qur'an gives license to the riding of appetites, in that it permits plurality of wives.

Had they looked with the eye of fairness at the noble verse which they supposed grants Muslims license for plurality, they would have seen that it is not as they supposed, but is of the highest degrees of eloquence, in that it forbids plurality with the very words of licensing. For had it granted license for plurality, it would have stopped at His words:

> ﴿فَانْكِحُوا مَا طَابَ لَكُمْ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ مَثْنَى وَثُلَاثَ وَرُبَاعَ﴾

"Marry such women as seem good to you, two, three or four."

But it closed the opening after this with His words:

> ﴿فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ أَلَّا تَعْدِلُوا فَوَاحِدَةً﴾

"And if you fear you will not deal justly, then one."

And after that:

> ﴿وَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعُوا أَنْ تَعْدِلُوا بَيْنَ النِّسَاءِ وَلَوْ حَرَصْتُمْ فَلَا تَمِيلُوا كُلَّ الْمَيْلِ فَتَذَرُوهَا كَالْمُعَلَّقَةِ وَإِنْ تُصْلِحُوا وَتَتَّقُوا فَإِنَّ اللهَ كَانَ غَفُورًا رَحِيمًا﴾

"And you will never be able to deal justly between women, however much you desire it; so do not incline with total inclination, leaving her as one suspended. And if you make amends and fear God, then God is Ever-Forgiving, Most Merciful."

Surat al-Nisa'.

No fair-minded person who looks into the meaning of this verse and its intent can fail to know whether the license the noble verse gave is an approval and an encouragement toward plurality, or a treatment and a remedy. It is plain that the license in this chapter is nothing but a remedy and a treatment. And it is known that the use of medicine is proper only for the one who is ill, so that diseases may not increase, but rather be checked by the treatment. Such a license in treatment is not addressed to the healthy, and they are not commanded to burden themselves with it where there is no necessity. So the one who marries beyond one without necessity is like one who uses medicine in a state of health and wearies himself thereby. This is explained by the words of our master al-Qadi al-Nu'man, may God sanctify his soul, in the Da'a'im, where he said: the merit in taking many women belongs to one who is able to undertake their maintenance, and is given such strength of capacity as will suffice them, and is able to leave off inclining toward one against another, and not to leave any of them as one suspended, as God has forbidden. So if a man has several wives, he must observe the right of each one, and satisfy them, and act well toward each one of them. If it is possible for him to house them in one house, there is no harm in it for him, though it is difficult; otherwise he should follow the Messenger of God (صلع) and house them separately.

The counsel: a man whose wife has died should not remain after her death dwelling alone without a wife, but should marry a second time and not hesitate in it, for in this is the good of the people and the community and the increase of affection among them. And if either of the two has children, that should be no reason to hesitate. Upon a man's kin and companions is that they should have regard for the widows of the people and consider marrying them off, looking to what is suitable for each one of them; for the sitting of a woman without the cover of a husband is more shameful and more grievous than the sitting of a man without a wife.

The Messenger of God (صلع) already had with him two wives, 'A'isha and Sawda, when he proposed to a Muslim woman after her husband had died, that she might be under his care. She answered that she was aged and had dependents and was subject to jealousy, and asked how she should marry a second time. He (صلع) said that he was older than she, and that if she had dependents, they would live with the dependents of the Messenger of God (صلع); and as for what she said of jealousy, he would supplicate God Almighty to lift the jealousy from her. So it was in the second marriage. And when she entered the chamber in the house of the Messenger of God and speech was renewed, she excused herself and said, "I said what I said, O Messenger of God, before the marriage, and there was nothing in it from me except that my state might be known beforehand."

What has gone before makes plain that the true house is the man and the woman together, and that a house of stone and timber is a house only by figure of speech. There is no doubt that the carrying out of the affairs of the figurative house belongs to the hand of the woman, since it is attached to her, just as the bringing in of expenditure from outside is attached to the man. And as the woman has no entry into the affairs attached to the man, so the man has no entry into the affairs attached to the woman. Rather it is upon him to bring to her of expenditure according to the measure of his capacity, and it is upon the woman to act in what is under her charge according to her own judgment.
