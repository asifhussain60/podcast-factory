# Why Marry at All

ذِكْرُ النِّكَاحِ وَالرَّغَائِبِ

The matter opens with the word of God:

> «وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ أَنْ خَلَقَ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا لِتَسْكُنُوا إِلَيْهَا وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَكُمْ مَوَدَّةً وَرَحْمَةً إِنَّ فِي ذَلِكَ لَآيَاتٍ لِقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ»

"And among His signs is that He created for you from yourselves spouses, that you may dwell in tranquility with them, and He placed between you affection and mercy. Indeed in that are signs for a people who give thought."

And the hadith:

> «مَنْ أَحَبَّ أَنْ يَلْقَى اللهَ طَاهِرًا مُطَهَّرًا فَلْيَسْتَعْفِفْ بِزَوْجَتِهِ، وَمَنْ أَحَبَّ أَنْ يَكُونَ عَلَى فِطْرَتِي فَلْيَسْتَنَّ بِسُنَّتِي فَإِنَّ مِنْ سُنَّتِي النِّكَاحَ»

"Whoever loves to meet God pure and purified, let him keep himself chaste through his wife; and whoever loves to be upon my nature, let him follow my Sunnah, for marriage is of my Sunnah."

It was narrated from Ali (ع) that he said: there was none among the companions of the Messenger of God who married but that the Messenger of God said to him, "He has completed his religion and his faith." And he used to say:

> «مِنْ أَخْلَاقِ الْأَنْبِيَاءِ التَّنَظُّفُ وَالْحَلْقُ وَالتَّطَيُّبُ وَكَثْرَةُ الطَّرُوقَةِ»

"Among the manners of the prophets are cleanliness, shaving, the use of perfume, and frequency of الطَّرُوقَة," which is to say, of wives. The more faith increases in a servant, the more his love for his wife increases. And he said:

> «مَنْ تَرَكَ النِّكَاحَ مَخَافَةَ الْعَيْلَةِ فَقَدْ أَسَاءَ الظَّنَّ بِرَبِّهِ»

"Whoever abandons marriage from fear of poverty has thought ill of his Lord," for God has promised His servants, saying: «إِنْ يَكُونُوا فُقَرَاءَ يُغْنِهِمُ اللهُ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ وَاللهُ وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ», "If they are poor, God will enrich them from His bounty, and God is Vast and Knowing." And he used to say, «لَا رَهْبَانِيَّةَ فِي الْإِسْلَامِ», "There is no monasticism in Islam," forbidding celibacy to men and to women alike, and saying:

> «تَنَاكَحُوا تَكَاثَرُوا فَإِنِّي أُبَاهِي بِكُمُ الْأُمَمَ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ»

"Marry and multiply, for I shall boast of you before the nations on the Day of Resurrection."

God has placed in the human being many faculties and drives which serve his welfare, such as hunger and thirst, sleep and wakefulness, and others besides. Among these He deposited the desire for marriage, which appears in him at the age of puberty. And just as the Messenger of God set down a law for each of these drives, so he set down regulations for this one, so that man might be kept from resembling the beasts and might walk upon the road of the prophets. Man was created civil by nature, and it is not possible for him to attend to all his dealings without the help of another in them. The nearest of helpers to him is his wife. When he leaves his house to earn his sustenance, and to store what he earns against a time when he cannot go out, whether through illness or weakness or some other cause, and to guard his children, he must have someone who guards his house and his furnishings. For this no one is fit but the woman.

She is the helper in his living, in the ordering of his house, in the care of his children and his dependants and their upbringing. As the house is not sound without the woman, so the woman is not sound without the house, and these two, the pair, are the house in truth, whether they dwell beneath a tree in the desert or in the high place of a dwelling. The building in which they live is called a house only by figure of speech. The true house is the school of feeling and of upbringing, the place where the character of humanity is gathered, where the children receive their first training and the earliest knowledge of their affairs, above all from their mother. In this true house the children obtain the highest foundations of humanity and the lessons of nurture, which they carry with them from the time of childhood into their dealings afterward. Therefore a man must choose from among the people the one best suited for this school, so that she may be fit for the teaching of children; and none is fit for it save one who is sound in body, sound in soul, and sound in what she knows. If a woman is without a husband, or a man without a wife, neither of them possesses a house in truth, however lofty the dwelling they imagine. What is plain from this is that the house is with the woman and the woman is with the house.

The intent of the religious Muslim in marriage is nothing but the obtaining of children and chastity against what is forbidden, as the Quran indicates in His word «مُحْصِنِينَ غَيْرَ مُسَافِحِينَ». The aim of marriage is entry into the fortress of devotion and chastity, not the mere discharge of appetite as the animals do; for the word مُحْصِنِين derives from الإحصان, the verbal noun of الحصن, and الحصن in the language is the fortress. The meaning is that whoever marries with the intention of good has, as it were, fortified and protected himself from bodily diseases and from defects of soul. The unmarried man, even if he were free of whisperings and the peril of appetite, is not free of bodily ailments such as ill temper, coarseness of nature, madness, constriction, and torpor. Rather, by marriage there increase in him fine qualities: gravity, calm, forbearance, devotion, patience, gentleness, and the like.

Here a question arises. Since the desire for marriage was deposited in man by nature, as the other natural drives were, he is compelled from within himself to satisfy it, as is seen in the state of the corrupt and sinful man who finds in himself an urge to commit the shameful act though it be forbidden. What, then, is meant by the encouragement to marriage that comes from the Lawgiver in this chapter, as it is set down in the Da'a'im? The encouragement rests upon two causes, the one religious and the other worldly.

The religious cause is the encouragement of those who travel the road of the Hereafter and renounce this world. Being absorbed in longing for the Hereafter, they turn ascetic toward worldly attachments, and it is seen that some of them do not marry at all, since marriage is the first step upon the stairs of worldly attachment. It was narrated from Ali (ع) that he said:

> حَضَرَ عُثْمَانُ بْنُ مَظْعُونٍ إِلَى رَسُولِ اللهِ فَقَالَ: يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ قَدْ غَلَبَنِي حَدِيثُ النَّفْسِ وَإِنِّي لَمْ أُحْدِثْ شَيْئًا حَتَّى أَسْتَأْمِرَكَ. قَالَ: بِمَا حَدَّثَتْكَ نَفْسُكَ يَا عُثْمَانُ؟ قَالَ: أَرَدْتُ أَنْ أَسِيحَ فِي الْأَرْضِ. قَالَ: لَا تَسِحْ فِيهَا، فَإِنَّ سِيَاحَةَ أُمَّتِي الْمَسَاجِدُ. قَالَ: فَهَمَمْتُ أَنْ أُحَرِّمَ عَلَى نَفْسِي اللَّحْمَ. فَقَالَ لَهُ رَسُولُ اللهِ: لَا تَفْعَلْ، فَإِنِّي أَشْتَهِيهِ، وَاللهِ لَوْ سَأَلْتُ اللهَ أَنْ يُطْعِمَنِيهِ كُلَّ يَوْمٍ لَفَعَلَ. قَالَ: وَهَمَمْتُ أَنْ أَجُبَّ نَفْسِي. قَالَ: يَا عُثْمَانُ، لَيْسَ مِنَّا مَنْ فَعَلَ ذَلِكَ بِنَفْسِهِ وَلَا بِأَحَدٍ، إِنَّ وِجَاءَ أُمَّتِي الصِّيَامُ. قَالَ: وَهَمَمْتُ أَنْ أُحَرِّمَ خَوْلَةَ زَوْجَتِي عَلَى نَفْسِي. قَالَ: لَا تَفْعَلْ يَا عُثْمَانُ.

Then he mentioned the benefits of living with a wife, as recorded in the Da'a'im, and the Messenger of God placed his hand upon his breast and said:

> «يَا عُثْمَانُ لَا تَرْغَبْ عَنْ سُنَّتِي، فَإِنَّهُ مَنْ رَغِبَ عَنْ سُنَّتِي أَعْرَضَتْ عَنْهُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ وَصَرَفَتْ وَجْهَهُ عَنْ حَوْضِي»

"O 'Uthman, do not turn away from my Sunnah, for whoever turns away from my Sunnah, the angels will turn away from him on the Day of Resurrection and will turn his face from my Pool."

In this same sense a narration has come from Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع). He was asked about a man whom fear of God had so entered that he left women and good food, and could not raise his head to the sky out of reverence for God Most High. He said:

> «أَمَّا قَوْلُكَ فِي تَرْكِ النِّسَاءِ فَقَدْ عَلِمْتَ مَا كَانَ لِرَسُولِ اللهِ مِنْهُنَّ، وَأَمَّا قَوْلُكَ فِي تَرْكِ الطَّعَامِ الطَّيِّبِ فَقَدْ كَانَ رَسُولُ اللهِ يُحِبُّ اللَّحْمَ وَالْعَسَلَ، وَأَمَّا قَوْلُكَ إِنَّهُ دَخَلَهُ الْخَوْفُ مِنَ اللهِ حَتَّى لَا يَسْتَطِيعَ أَنْ يَرْفَعَ رَأْسَهُ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ، فَإِنَّمَا الْخُشُوعُ فِي الْقَلْبِ، وَمَنْ ذَا يَكُونُ أَخْوَفَ لِلهِ مِنْ رَسُولِ اللهِ؟ وَمَا كَانَ يَفْعَلُ هَذَا»

Then he recited the word of God, mighty and majestic: «لَقَدْ كَانَ لَكُمْ فِي رَسُولِ اللهِ أُسْوَةٌ حَسَنَةٌ لِمَنْ كَانَ يَرْجُو اللهَ وَالْيَوْمَ الْآخِرَ», "Indeed there is for you in the Messenger of God a goodly example, for whoever hopes in God and the Last Day."

The encouragement from the Lawgiver for the worldly cause is directed to the destitute, the poor, and those of no means, who do not marry because of the narrowness of provision and livelihood. For this reason it was said that if they do not find the like of a dowry, then a little of it suffices. Upon this, narrations have come from Mawlana Ja'far al-Sadiq (ع) concerning the smallness of the dowry: if a believer finds nothing, then a dirham, or a garment, or some small thing; and if he finds none of these things, then let him teach her a sura of the Quran. And in the matter of poverty and the straitness of living, God Most High said:

> «وَأَنْكِحُوا الْأَيَامَى مِنْكُمْ وَالصَّالِحِينَ مِنْ عِبَادِكُمْ وَإِمَائِكُمْ إِنْ يَكُونُوا فُقَرَاءَ يُغْنِهِمُ اللهُ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ وَاللهُ وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ»

"And marry off the single among you and the righteous among your male and female slaves. If they are poor, God will enrich them from His bounty, and God is Vast and Knowing." Whoever does not find a wife to marry, and waits upon God for ease in its time, let each of the two live in chastity and devotion until he finds what he hopes for, and there is no harm in that. There is no doubt that the destitute person, man or woman, who wards off need alone is not without the help of others in his earnings, especially in the Arabian Peninsula, where there is no means of livelihood there but agriculture. In years of drought their houses are without food and without provision, so a man must labour, he and his dependants, in cultivation from the time of sowing to the time of harvest. Their affair is not settled there by one woman, but by two women up to four; and the more so when quarrels fall out among them by reason of the multitude of the tribes and the difference of their ways.

Enmity persists among them generation after generation, so that they reckon the killing of a man as the killing of a single animal, there being no true governance over the subjects and their rights. This is most of all when war appears and the men of both parties are killed, and there remains for the widow and the orphan none to guard them against those who overpower them and plunder them. So they consent to enter under the protection of men and to guard themselves by marriage, even with one who already has a wife. So too because the land of the Arabs, by its nearness to the equator, brings excessive heat upon its people, and their women therefore lose their beauty and their strength before their men do. When a woman is with a man, the days of her menstruation, the days of her pregnancy, her delivery and her nursing prevent nearness to her; or there is some other cause of those which will be mentioned in the chapter to come. So it is not possible for the man of the village to endure with one wife alone, whether for chastity or for ease of soul in earning provision in a state of destitution. From the time of the Prophet until this hour, the necessity calling for plurality of wives has remained present for its men.

Those who forbid plurality of wives should look to the causes mentioned above. Nor are these causes confined to war, for there are other causes besides which occur among non-Arabs as well, of which one may be mentioned. When strife arises between the great powers and war is kindled between them, the men perish on both sides. Reckoning from the beginning of this recent twentieth century: such as the war which fell out between the English and the Boer people in South Africa, in which multitudes of men and of leaders were lost; and the war of Japan, in which five hundred thousand men were lost from the two sides; and the like of this in wars between small states and great, for various ends among them. By reason of these there falls, inevitably, a scarcity of men in those places. For clarification, the state of two or three of the powers may be mentioned, such as the English, the French, and the Germans, and the eye turned to the sheets of their reckoning of men and women. In the reckoning of the French there were four hundred thousand and twenty-three thousand four hundred and nine women in excess of the men. In the reckoning of Germany, in a certain year, after the equalizing of men and women among them, the women exceeded by eight hundred thousand and eighty-seven thousand six hundred and forty-eight, and there was none among the men to marry them, since each of the men had a woman with him already in the count. And in the reckoning of the English, in a certain year, the number of women in excess of the men was one hundred and eighty-two thousand six hundred and nineteen.

Those who forbid plurality of wives may be asked concerning the women of these three states alone, and no others, whose number reaches twenty-four hundred thousand: what is the ruling they have laid down for them, or the law they have codified? A law must accord with nature. Either they must say that these women do not desire what other women desire, or they must permit them fornication. Do they not look to the children of the bath-houses in those kingdoms, to what number they reach every year? By these shameful deeds houses are ruined, whose owners are afflicted by immorality with other than their spouses. Yet with all the shortness of sight of the opponents in this matter, they reproach Islam, saying that the Quran licenses the riding of appetites, since it permits plurality of wives.

Had they looked with the eye of fairness at the noble verse which they suppose to license Muslims in plurality, they would have seen that it is not as they suppose, but is among the highest degrees of eloquence, in that it forbids plurality with the very words of permission. Had it truly licensed plurality it would have left it unqualified in His word «مِنَ النِّسَاءِ مَثْنَى وَثُلَاثَ وَرُبَاعَ»; but the door of enjoyment is closed by what comes after it, which is «فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ أَلَّا تَعْدِلُوا فَوَاحِدَةً», and after that «وَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعُوا أَنْ تَعْدِلُوا بَيْنَ النِّسَاءِ وَلَوْ حَرَصْتُمْ فَلَا تَمِيلُوا كُلَّ الْمَيْلِ فَتَذَرُوهَا كَالْمُعَلَّقَةِ وَإِنْ تُصْلِحُوا وَتَتَّقُوا فَإِنَّ اللهَ كَانَ غَفُورًا رَحِيمًا», from Surat al-Nisa'.

Whoever of the fair-minded looks into the sense of this verse and its intent knows that the license the noble verse gives is not a commendation and an encouragement to plurality, but a treatment and a remedy. It is plain that the license in this matter is nothing but a medicine and a cure, and it is known that the use of medicine is fitting only for one who is ill, so that his sickness may not increase but be checked by the treatment. Such a license in treatment is not for the healthy, who are not commanded to burden themselves with what necessity does not require. The one who marries beyond one without necessity is like the one who uses medicine in a state of health and wearies himself by it. This is explained by the word of our master al-Qadi al-Nu'man, may God sanctify his soul, in the Da'a'im, where he said:

> إِنَّمَا الْفَضْلُ فِي الْإِكْثَارِ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ لِمَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ الْقِيَامَ بِمَعَاشِهِنَّ وَأُوتِيَ مِنَ الْقُوَّةِ عَلَى الْبَاءَةِ مَا يُحْصِنُهُنَّ وَقَدَرَ عَلَى تَرْكِ الْمَيْلِ بَيْنَهُنَّ وَأَنْ لَا يَذَرَ بَعْضَهُنَّ مُعَلَّقَاتٍ كَمَا نَهَى اللهُ عَنْ ذَلِكَ

So if a man has more than one wife, it is upon him to observe the right of each one, and to satisfy them, and to do well by each single one of them. If it is possible for him to house them in one house, there is no harm in it for him, though it is difficult; and if not, then it is upon him to follow the Messenger of God and to house them separately.

An admonition. A man whose wife has died should not sit alone thereafter without a companion in his household, but should marry a second time and not hold back from it, for in it is the welfare of the people and the community and the increase of affection among them. If either of the two has children, that is no reason to hold back. Upon those who are his kin and his companions it falls to look to the widows of the people and to consider marrying them to those who suit each one of them; for the sitting of a woman without the covering of a husband is a greater blemish than the sitting of a man without a wife.

The Messenger of God had two wives with him, 'A'isha and Sawda, when he proposed to a Muslim woman after her husband had died a martyr. She came forward with an excuse, saying that she had dependants with her, and that she was given to jealousy, and that she had married a second time. He said: "I am older than you; and if you have dependants with you, what is it to you to live with the dependants of the Messenger of God? As for the jealousy you have spoken of, I shall call upon God Most High that He lift the jealousy from you." When she entered the chamber in the house of the Messenger of God and the talk was renewed, she excused herself and said, "I said what I said before the marriage, O Messenger of God, only that my condition might be known beforehand."

What has been said before makes it clear that the true house is the man and the woman together, and that the house of stone and timber is a house only by figure of speech. So it is fitting that the carrying out of the affairs of the figurative house be in the hand of the woman, since it is attached to her, just as the bringing in of expenditure from outside is attached to the man. As the woman has no entry into the affairs attached to the man, so the man has no entry into the affairs attached to the woman. It is upon him to bring her the expenditure according to the measure of his capacity, and to leave to her what falls under her supervision; and it is upon the woman to spend what is under her supervision.
