# At the Table: Eating, Feeding, and Being Fed

Among the courtesies of food, the Book opens the matter with the command to eat what is wholesome and to act righteously:

«كلوا من طيبات ما رزقناكم واعملوا صالحاً»

"Eat of the good things We have provided you, and act righteously."

When food is set before people, the name of God is pronounced at its beginning and praise is given at its end, and food is not to be wasted. The Messenger of God (ص) ate as a servant eats. He did not eat reclining, and he would say:

«إنما أنا عبد آكل كما يأكل العبد وأجلس كما يجلس العبد»

"I am only a servant; I eat as a servant eats and I sit as a servant sits."

He disliked eating while lying on the side, or stretched out on the back, or in the manner of tyrants who eat at their ease. He ate with his right hand and began with water. He did not eat from the crown of the dish, nor from the middle of the food, but from what lay nearest him. He made the morsel small and chewed it well, and he did not reach for a second morsel before the first was swallowed. He did not fault food: what pleased him he ate, and what did not he left. He forbade breathing into hot food, and waited instead until it grew cool enough to swallow. He did not drink water in the midst of the meal unless a morsel caught in his throat or he sneezed. When the meal was finished he cleaned his fingers and washed his hand.

Such was the practice of the Messenger of God (ص) in eating and drinking: humility before God and reverence for His provision. The people of religion ought therefore to follow the acts of the Prophet and his courtesies in their living, without display of pride or vanity. One seated on the ground may set his food upon a cloth if he is able. One seated upon a bedding may set the food a little apart from where he sits. One accustomed to sitting on a chair may spread the table upon something raised, so that his honouring of the provision is visible.

It is narrated from our master Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (ع) that he said:

«لا يأكل الرجل بشماله ولا يشرب بها ولا يناول بها»

"A man is not to eat with his left hand, nor to drink with it, nor to hand anything with it."

When water is drunk, «بسم الله» is said, and at the end «الحمد لله». One looks into the vessel first, drinks the water pure and clean, and pauses within his drinking for three breaths. If no vessel is found, he drinks from his hands, sipping the water; and milk he takes in a draught. The food itself must be lawful in itself and clean, and one should not intend by eating it indulgence and luxury, but rather strength for righteous deeds.

Concerning the merit of feeding others, God Most High says:

«وَيُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَى حُبِّهِ مِسْكِينًا وَيَتِيمًا وَأَسِيرًا ۝ إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ اللَّهِ لَا نُرِيدُ مِنْكُمْ جَزَاءً وَلَا مَشْكُورًا»

"And they give food, despite their love for it, to the poor, the orphan and the captive: 'We feed you only for the sake of God; we desire from you neither reward nor thanks.'"

And in the hadith:

«إن في الجنة غرفاً يرى ظاهرها من باطنها وباطنها من ظاهرها لمن ألان الكلام وأطعم الطعام وصلى بالليل والناس نيام»

"In Paradise there are chambers whose outside is seen from within and whose inside is seen from without, for whoever speaks gently, feeds others, and prays by night while the people sleep."

Likewise:

«خيركم من أطعم الطعام»

"The best of you is he who feeds others."

And:

«من أطعم الطعام حتى يشبعه وسقاه حتى يرويه أبعده الله من النار»

"Whoever feeds a man until he is satisfied and gives him drink until his thirst is quenched, God will remove him far from the Fire."

It is narrated from the Commander of the Believers (ع) that the Messenger of God (ص) was brought seven captives and said to him:

«يا علي قم فاضرب أعناقهم»

"O ʿAlī, rise and strike their necks."

Then Jibrīl descended upon him and said:

«يا محمد اضرب أعناق هؤلاء الستة وحلّ عن هذا الواحد»

"O Muḥammad, strike the necks of these six and release this one."

The Messenger of God (ص) said to him:

«يا جبريل وما حاله»

"O Jibrīl, what is his case?"

He said:

«هو سخي الكف يطعم الطعام»

"He is open of hand; he feeds others."

The Messenger of God (ص) said:

«أعنك أو عن ربك»

"Is this from you or from your Lord?"

He said:

«بل عن ربك يا محمد»

"Rather from your Lord, O Muḥammad."

And it is narrated likewise from ʿAlī (ع) that he said:

«إذا دخل أخوك المؤمن فأطعمه من أطيب ما في بيتك وإن كان صائماً فادهنه»

"When your believing brother enters upon you, feed him from the best of what is in your house; and if he is fasting, anoint him."

It is narrated from Imam Muḥammad al-Bāqir (ع) that he said:

«إطعام المؤمن يعدل عتق رقبة»

"Feeding a believer is equal to freeing a slave."

And:

«أحب الأعمال إلى الله إدخال السرور على المؤمن يشبعه أو بقضاء دينه»

"The most beloved of deeds to God is bringing joy to a believer, by satisfying his hunger or by settling his debt."

Hospitality belongs to the character of the believer and to the noble virtues, and the entertaining of a guest is among them.

«وحد الضيافة ثلاثة أيام فما كان فوق ذلك فهو صدقة»

"The limit of hospitality is three days; whatever is beyond that is charity."

Generosity is among the noblest of the qualities of the prophets. It falls to the one visited to bring near to his brother whatever is easily at hand, even if it be no more than a draught of water. Whoever is able to bring near to his brother what he has and refuses will remain under God's displeasure a day and a night; and whoever despises what his brother brings near to him will remain under such displeasure a day and a night. ʿAlī (ع) would attend an invitation and say that it is a right upon the one invited to answer it, and that whoever comes to a meal without being invited has come to what is not lawful for him. And it is reported from the Messenger of God (ص) that he said:

«من أكل الطعام لم يدع إليه فإنما يأكل في جوفه شعلة نار»

"Whoever eats food to which he was not invited eats only a flame of fire into his belly."

It is forbidden that a man should feed another along with himself from food to which he alone was invited, unless he is given leave in that. And it is reported of al-Ḥusayn ibn ʿAlī (ع) that he saw a man who had been invited to food say to the one who invited him, "Excuse me." Al-Ḥusayn (ع) said:

«قم فليس في الدعوة عفو إن كنت مفطراً فكل وإن كنت صائماً فبارك»

"Rise, for there is no excusing in an invitation. If you are not fasting, then eat; and if you are fasting, then pray for blessing."

Our master Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (ع) said:

«إذا دخل أحدكم على أخيه وهو صائم فسأله أن يفطر فليفطر إلا أن يكون صيام ذلك قضاءً أو نذراً وقد زالت الشمس»

"When one of you enters upon his brother while fasting and is asked to break the fast, let him break it, unless that fast is one of compensation or of a vow and the sun has already passed the meridian."

And he said:

«إذا قال لك أخوك كل فكل ولا تلجئه إلى أن يقسم عليك فإنما يريد كرامتك»

"When your brother says to you, 'Eat,' then eat, and do not oblige him to swear an oath upon you, for he desires only to honour you."

And he said:

«إذا أتاك أخوك فقدم إليه ما تيسر عندك وإن دعوته فتكلف له ما أمكنك»

"When your brother comes to you, set before him what is readily at hand; and if you have invited him, take pains for him as much as you are able."

He (ع) said to one of his companions who was eating with him:

«إنما تعرف مودة الرجل لأخيه بجودة أكله من طعامه وإنه ليعجبني الرجل يأكل من طعامي فيجيد الأكل يسرني بذلك»

"A man's affection for his brother is known by the heartiness of his eating from his food; and it pleases me when a man eats of my food and eats it well, for that gladdens me."

And he used to say:

«إذا اجتمعتم عند الطعام مع الأهل والأولاد لا تسكتوا ولكن تكلموا بالمعروف وتحدثوا بحكايات الصالحين»

"When you gather at food with family and children, do not sit in silence, but speak of what is good and relate the accounts of the righteous."

For such a gathering over food with one's family is not counted with God among the affairs of this world alone.

As to the manners of the guest, it is not of the Sunnah to enter a house to which one has not been invited at the time of eating, for that is an intrusion. In the report:

«من مشى إلى طعام لم يدع إليه مشى فاسقاً وأكل حراماً»

"Whoever walks to food to which he was not invited walks as a transgressor and eats what is forbidden."

Yet if a man's entry happens to coincide with a meal, he should hold back until they have finished; and if they call upon him to share with them, he should consider the matter. If he knows they call him sincerely, wishing his company, let him share with them. If he perceives that it is only speech and bashfulness on their part, he should not.

Entering the houses of those named in the Book is set out plainly, as God Most High says:

«وَلا عَلَى أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَنْ تَأْكُلُوا مِنْ بُيُوتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ آبَائِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أُمَّهَاتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ إِخْوَانِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أَخَوَاتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أَعْمَامِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ عَمَّاتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أَخْوَالِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ خَالاتِكُمْ أَوْ مَا مَلَكْتُمْ مَفَاتِحَهُ أَوْ صَدِيقِكُمْ لَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ أَنْ تَأْكُلُوا جَمِيعاً أَوْ أَشْتَاتاً»

"Nor is there blame upon yourselves if you eat from your own houses, or the houses of your fathers, or the houses of your mothers, or the houses of your brothers, or the houses of your sisters, or the houses of your paternal uncles, or the houses of your paternal aunts, or the houses of your maternal uncles, or the houses of your maternal aunts, or those whose keys you possess, or of your friend. There is no blame upon you whether you eat together or separately."

The Messenger of God (ص) entered the house of Barīra and ate food that was there while she was absent, and that food had been given to her as charity. When she came and was asked about the food, she answered that the charity had reached its rightful place; and he had known of her gladness in that.

The companions of al-Ḥasan (ع) would enter his house and eat what they found there without asking leave, and al-Ḥasan (ع) would enter and see this and be gladdened by it, saying that this is how they used to act with their friends. It is also narrated of al-Ḥasan (ع) that one day he was passing through the market, and he took from one basket once and from another once, and ate. Hishām said to him, "What has come over you, O Abū Saʿīd, you of scrupulous restraint, that you eat a man's goods without his leave?" He said to him, "Recite to me the verse of eating." So he recited until he reached the words of God Most High, «أو صديقكم», and he grew aware and said, "Then who is the friend?" He (ع) said:

«من استراحت إليه النفس واطمأن إليه القلب»

"He in whom the soul finds rest and toward whom the heart is at peace."

Such is the substance of many reports and of the habits of the righteous.

If the guest is given a choice among foods, let him choose, and let him not regard the bond between them as a thing to be measured. It is among the noble qualities of a man that when he is invited to two places he should not prefer between the poor and the rich, nor between the far and the near, but should answer the first invitation. He should answer the invitation of the needy and the stranger, since such a one may not be able to arrange hospitality a second time. He should decline to answer if the one inviting is an oppressor, or corrupt, or given to evil. He should not intend by his acceptance the satisfying of his appetite, but the bringing of joy to the heart of the one who invited him. He should not enter the house without leave. He should not crowd the place upon those gathered there, but should sit where dignity and composure are possible. He should not fix his gaze upon the place from which the food is brought. And when he sits among people, it is fitting that he single out with kindness and good wishes the one who is nearest to him.

As to the manners of the host, the Messenger of God (ص) said:

«لا خير فيمن لا يضيف»

"There is no good in one who offers no hospitality."

And he (ص) said:

«أكرم الضيف»

"Honour the guest."

The Messenger of God (ص) passed by a woman who had many camels and sheep, and she did not offer him hospitality; and he passed by a woman who had only a few small sheep, and she slaughtered for him. He (ص) said:

«انظروا إليها إنما هذه الأخلاق بيد الله فمن شاء أن يمنحه خلقاً حسناً فعل»

"Look at her. These qualities of character are in the hand of God; whomever He wishes to grant a goodly character, He does so."

The host of a gathering should not intend by his invitation display or boasting, but the gladdening of the hearts of the brethren, and the following of the Sunnah of the Messenger of God (ص) in feeding others and bringing joy to the hearts of believers. He should give regard in his invitation especially to the people of merit and knowledge and to those chosen for their hearts, even if they are of little means, rather than to the wealthy. He should clean the house in which he invites his guests, and he should be attentive to it, for that is part of honouring them. He should observe the times he has appointed for those he has invited, so as not to weary them with waiting. If some of those invited are late beyond the appointed hour, it is his right to bring the food to those present and not to wait for the rest; but if only a short while has passed, there is no harm in waiting for them. And of the completeness of honouring a guest:

«طلاقة الوجه وطيب الحديث وإظهار التقصير في الخدمة والوداع إلى باب الدار حين الانصراف»

"A cheerful face, pleasant speech, acknowledgement of one's shortcoming in service, and accompanying the guest to the door of the house at his departure."

What is lawful to eat, and what is not forbidden of food, is mentioned in its own place.
