# At the Table: Eating, Feeding, and Being Fed

Food is a matter of religion before it is a matter of appetite, and the conduct of the Prophet at his meals was itself a teaching. He ate as a servant eats, and he said of himself:

> أنا عبد آكل كما يأكل العبد وأجلس كما يجلس العبد

"I am a servant: I eat as the servant eats, and I sit as the servant sits."

He disliked eating while reclining, and he disliked eating lying back or stretched out in the manner of tyrants. He would sit upon the ground, drawing up one leg and settling upon the other, and in that posture he took his food.

He ate with his right hand, and began with water. He did not eat from the crown of the dish nor from the middle of the food, but from what lay nearest him. He made the morsel small and chewed it well, and did not reach for the next morsel until the first was swallowed. He never faulted a food: if it pleased him he ate it, and if it did not, he left it. He forbade haste and gulping at the table, and would remain patient until the whole of the mouthful was swallowed. He did not drink water in the midst of the meal unless a morsel had caught in his throat, or he had sneezed.

When the food was finished he would clean his fingers and wash his hand. This was the manner of the Messenger of God in eating and drinking: humility before God and honour shown to His provision. The people of religion ought therefore to imitate the acts of the Messenger and his courtesies in their own living, without display of greatness and without vanity.

If a man is seated upon the ground, he may set his food upon a cloth if he is able. If he is upon a bed, he may set it apart from where he sits. If it is his custom to sit upon a chair, he may spread the table upon something raised above him, so that his honouring of the sustenance is made plain.

It is narrated from Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (ع) that he said:

> لا يأكل الرجل بشماله ولا يشرب بها ولا يناول بها

"A man should not eat with his left hand, nor drink with it, nor hand anything with it."

When drinking water he should say بسم الله, and when he sets the vessel down, الحمد لله. He should look into the vessel first, and drink water that is pure and clean, pausing over it in three breaths. If he finds no vessel, he may drink from his hands, sipping the water; and milk he may take in a draught. The food itself must be lawful and clean, and he should not intend by eating it mere pleasure and indulgence, but rather strength for righteous deeds.

On the merit of feeding others.

God Most High says:

> وَيُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَى حُبِّهِ مِسْكِينًا وَيَتِيمًا وَأَسِيرًا إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ اللَّهِ لَا نُرِيدُ مِنْكُمْ جَزَاءً وَلَا شُكُورًا

"And they give food, in spite of their love for it, to the poor, the orphan, and the captive, saying: 'We feed you only for the sake of God; we desire from you neither reward nor thanks.'"

And in the hadith:

> إن في الجنة غرفا يرى ظاهرها من باطنها وباطنها من ظاهرها لمن ألان الكلام وأطعم الطعام وصلى بالليل والناس نيام

"In Paradise there are chambers whose outside is seen from within and whose inside is seen from without, prepared for whoever speaks gently, feeds food, and prays by night while the people sleep."

And it is said: the best of you are those who feed food. Whoever feeds a hungry man until he is satisfied, and gives him drink until he is quenched, God removes him far from the Fire.

It is narrated from the Commander of the Believers (ع) that the Messenger of God was brought seven captives, and he said: "O ʿAlī, rise and strike their necks." Then Jibrīl descended and said: "O Muḥammad, strike the necks of six of these, and release this one." The Messenger of God said: "O Jibrīl, what is his state?" He said: "He is open-handed, generous with food." He said: "Is this from you or from your Lord?" He said: "Rather from your Lord, O Muḥammad."

Also from ʿAlī (ع) that he said:

> إذا دخل أخوك المؤمن فأطعمه من أطيب ما في بيتك وإن كان صائما فادهنه

"When your believing brother comes in, feed him from the best of what is in your house; and if he is fasting, anoint him."

And it is narrated from Imam Muḥammad al-Bāqir (ع) that he said: feeding a believer is equal to freeing a slave; and the most beloved of deeds to God is bringing gladness to a believer, whether by satisfying his hunger or by settling his debt.

Hospitality belongs to the character of the believer and to the noble virtues. Honouring the guest is among them, and the limit of hospitality is three days; what is given beyond that is charity. Among the noblest of the manners of the prophets is generosity of food. It is upon the one visited that he offer to his brother whatever is easy for him, even if it be only a draught of water. Whoever disdains to offer his brother what is easy for him remains in the displeasure of God by day and by night; and whoever belittles what his brother offers him likewise remains so.

ʿAlī (ع) would attend the invitation and say: it is a right upon whoever is invited that he answer it. But whoever comes to it uninvited has come to what does not befit him. And the Messenger of God said:

> من أكل الطعام لم يدع إليه فإنما يأكل في جوفه شعلة نار

"Whoever eats food to which he was not invited eats only a flame of fire in his belly."

He also forbade that a man feed another from food to which he himself had been invited, unless he is given leave in that.

It is related of al-Ḥusayn ibn ʿAlī (ع) that he saw a man invited to a meal who said to the one inviting him: "Excuse me." Al-Ḥusayn (ع) said: "Rise and answer the invitation: if you are not fasting, then eat; and if you are fasting, then bless them."

Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad (ع) said: when one of you enters upon his brother while fasting, and his brother asks him to break the fast, let him break it, unless the fast be a make-up fast or a vow, or unless the sun has already passed its meridian. And he said: when your brother says to you "eat," then eat, and do not oblige him to swear upon you, for he desires only to honour you. And he said: when your brother comes to you, offer him what is easy for you; and if you invite him, then take trouble for him as far as you are able.

He said to one of his companions who was eating with him: a man's affection for his brother is known by the goodness of his eating from his food. It pleases me that a man should eat of my food and eat well; that gladdens me. And he used to say: when you gather over food with family and children, do not sit in silence, but speak of what is good and relate the stories of the righteous.

On the manners of the guest.

It is not of the Sunnah to enter a house uninvited at the time of eating, for that is an intrusion. In the report, whoever walks toward food to which he was not invited walks as a transgressor and eats what is unlawful. Yet if a man enters and finds them at their food, he should hold back until they finish. If they call him to share with them, he should consider: if he knows that they call him sincerely, wishing his company, let him share with them; but if he perceives that it is only politeness and embarrassment on their part, he should decline.

As for entering the houses of those whose closeness is established, God Most High has said:

> وَلَا عَلَى أَنفُسِكُمْ أَن تَأْكُلُوا مِن بُيُوتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ آبَائِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أُمَّهَاتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ إِخْوَانِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أَخَوَاتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أَعْمَامِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ عَمَّاتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أَخْوَالِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ خَالَاتِكُمْ أَوْ مَا مَلَكْتُم مَّفَاتِحَهُ أَوْ صَدِيقِكُمْ لَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ أَن تَأْكُلُوا جَمِيعًا أَوْ أَشْتَاتًا

"Nor is there blame upon yourselves that you eat from your own houses, or the houses of your fathers, or the houses of your mothers, or the houses of your brothers, or the houses of your sisters, or the houses of your paternal uncles, or the houses of your paternal aunts, or the houses of your maternal uncles, or the houses of your maternal aunts, or those whose keys you possess, or of your friend. There is no blame upon you whether you eat together or separately."

The Messenger of God entered the house of Barīra in her absence and ate food that was there, and the food had come to her as charity. When she returned and was asked about it, she answered that the charity had reached its place, and he had eaten knowing this, and she rejoiced in it.

The companions of al-Ḥasan (ع) used to eat in his house what they found there without asking leave, and al-Ḥasan (ع) would enter and see this and be gladdened by it, and would say: thus we used to do with our friends.

It is narrated of al-Ḥasan (ع) that one day he passed through the market, and he took once from this basket and once from that, and ate. Hishām said to him: "What has come over you, O Abū Saʿīd, in your scrupulousness, that you eat a man's goods without his leave?" He said: "Recite to me the verse of eating." So he recited until he reached the words of God Most High, أو صديقكم. Then he was alerted and said: "And who is the friend?" He (ع) said: "The one to whom the soul finds rest and in whom the heart is at ease." Many narrations and the practice of the righteous run in this way.

The guest, if given a choice among the foods, should choose, and should not reckon it a breach of the bond between them. It is of the noble character of a man that, when he is pressed by invitations to two places, he not prefer between the poor and the rich, nor between the far and the near. Rather he answers the first invitation. He should answer the invitation of the poor and the stranger, since such a one may not be able to take the trouble of hospitality a second time. He refrains from answering when the one inviting is a tyrant, a transgressor, or a man of evil.

He should not intend by his acceptance the satisfying of his appetite, but the bringing of gladness to the heart of the one who invited him.

He should not enter the house without permission.

He should not crowd the place upon those gathered there, but should sit wherever tranquillity and dignity are possible for him.

He should not fix his gaze upon the place from which the food is brought out.

When he sits among people, he should give particular attention to those near him in courtesy and well-being.

On the manners of the host.

The Messenger of God said: "لا خير فيمن لا يضيف" — "There is no good in one who does not offer hospitality." And he said: take trouble for the guest.

The Messenger of God passed by a woman who had abundant flocks and herds, and she did not extend hospitality to him; then he passed by a woman who had only a few small ewes, and she slaughtered for him. He said: "Look at her. These characters are in the hand of God: whoever He wishes to grant a good character, He grants it."

The host should not intend by his invitation display and boasting. He should intend by it the gladdening of the hearts of the brethren, and following the Sunnah of the Messenger of God in feeding food and bringing joy to the hearts of the believers. He should have regard in his invitation especially for the people of merit and knowledge and for the chosen among hearts, even if they be weak rather than wealthy. He should clean the house in which he invites his guests, and should attend to whatever belongs to the honouring of guests. He should observe the appointed times of those he has invited, so that he does not weary them with waiting. If some of those invited are late beyond the appointed hour, it is the right of those present that the food be set before them and that the rest not be awaited; but if only a short while passes in waiting for the remainder, there is no harm in it.

And of the completeness of honouring:

> وَطِيبُ الْحَدِيثِ وَإِظْهَارُ التَّقْصِيرِ فِي الْخِدْمَةِ وَالْوَدَاعُ إِلَى بَابِ الدَّارِ حِينَ الْانْصِرَافِ

a cheerful face, pleasant speech, the acknowledgement of one's own shortcoming in service, and the farewell taken at the door of the house at the time of departure.
