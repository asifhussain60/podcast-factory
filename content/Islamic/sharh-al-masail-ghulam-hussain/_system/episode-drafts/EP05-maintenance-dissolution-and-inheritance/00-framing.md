# Maintenance Dissolution And Inheritance

## Opening directive
Two hosts, unnamed: a male scholar leads, a female seeker questions; never speak their names. Open with a brief warm welcome — name the book and its author, a practical treatise of rulings called *Explaining the Issues* — then preview in one sentence: a husband vanishes, and his wife's claim on his property does not pause. Target a 50 to 60 minute in-depth conversation. Then say, word for word: "With respect to his wife's maintenance, the missing man is treated as being among those who are present, from the day he went missing." Say once that this part of the treatise is damaged in transmission, worst at the end.

## Name discipline

- The Prophet → "the Prophet"; at first mention only, add "peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family."
- Father of Imams → that title alone, never paired with a personal name; at first mention only, add "peace be upon him."
- Household figures → the fifth Imam / the sixth Imam / his elder grandson / his younger grandson — never rotated.

## Three-part focus
Walk the source's own order. Three movements, each landing once.
1. Crisis — what a household is owed. A wife complains; the judge feeds her from the missing man's property before looking for him. Poverty is no defence either. The standard is neither wasteful nor miserly, covers teaching as well as food, and holds for a woman who does not pray. Land the stakes before any resolution.
2. The pivot — dissolution under a judge, not in private. Say the sentence word for word again here. A divorce must be spoken: writing and two witnesses are required too, and a wink is nothing — from the same author who insisted a loan be documented. Waiting periods are counted; a wife may buy her release; the third divorce is closed against a staged remarriage, a refusal that is the author's own argument.
3. Stakes — wealth leaving a person's hands. Gift, endowment, will: only a third was ever his to direct, and the endowment severs the bond of return in his lifetime. Say plainly where the inheritance grid breaks down; give the recoverable principles, enumerate nothing more.

## Host dynamic
The scholar leads; the seeker challenges at least three times and concedes once. Qualify doctrine by school ("the classical Ismaili reading"); the source gives no chapter-and-verse, so supply none. Define the waiting period before using it.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — a man who is gone cannot be made to pay."; "That sounds like wordplay — a written loan binds but a written divorce does not?" Steelman each objection first.
Each host lets the other finish: no interjections, never "yeah" / "mm-hmm" — begin with substance.

## Tone constraints
Use only these three images, all from the source, and no others:
Analogy 1: nakedness after being clothed — the bitterness of separation after intimacy.
Analogy 2: the grain that sprouts seven ears, each ear a hundred grains — wealth spent in the way of God.
Analogy 3: wages that keep arriving after a man's death, while good works continue in his name.

## Landing
Close on the pattern governing the whole: the obligation outlives the will of the man who made it — his absence, his refusal, his death. Just before that turn, repeat word for word the sentence from the opening. Then end on a question tied to action — which duty you carry would still stand if you stopped showing up for it? Leave it open. Do not recap; nothing follows the question, and nothing beyond this chapter is announced.
