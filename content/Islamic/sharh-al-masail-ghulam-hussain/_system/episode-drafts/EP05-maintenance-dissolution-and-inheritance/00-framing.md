# Maintenance Dissolution And Inheritance

## Opening directive
A male scholar leads; a female seeker questions. Open with a warm welcome naming the treatise *Explaining the Issues* and its author, Ghulam Hussain; preview in one sentence: what a wife is owed when her husband simply stops existing. Then say, word for word: "With respect to his wife's maintenance, the missing man is treated as being among those who are present, from the day he went missing."

## Three-part focus
Source order; each lands once. Biographical or historical background only where it bears on a ruling, once.
1. The crisis — a household is owed and the man who owes it is gone. Absence is no defence: the judge feeds her from his property before searching. The standard is median, neither wasteful nor miserly, and covers teaching as well as food. It holds for a woman who does not pray; it lapses in nushuz until she returns. Poverty she may consent to, or refuse.
2. The pivot — a marriage comes apart under a judge, not in private. Say the spine sentence verbatim here. Divorce must be spoken; writing with two witnesses is required too; a wink is nothing. Steelman his defence of divorce as recognized in earlier revealed laws before the seeker presses it; then patience in hardship beats separation. The third divorce closes, and a staged remarriage does not open it: present the refusal as his.
3. The stakes — only a third of a man's wealth was ever his to direct. Shroud, then debts, then the third; two-thirds are the heirs' before anything is written. The gift can be reclaimed; the endowment cannot. Say once that transmission breaks down in the inheritance grid; state what survives, reconstruct nothing.

## Host dynamic

Friction: "I don't buy that yet — why does a vanished man still owe her money?"; "That sounds like wordplay — a written loan binds, a written divorce does not?"
Once, a host raises a passage the other never led toward — they sound separately prepared.
Steelman before critique. Let each host finish a thought — no interjections, no filler. Say where the text is damaged.

## Tone constraints
Only these three images, all from the source:
Analogy 1: the grain sprouting seven ears of a hundred grains.
Analogy 2: nakedness after being clothed, the bitterness of parting.
Analogy 3: the giver of charity delivered and set free.
Short sentences, thinking out loud. Pause once to define the waiting period. The source gives no chapter-and-verse; supply none.

## Landing
Say the spine sentence verbatim a third time, then turn outward: obligations outlive the will of whoever took them on. End on a question — which claim on you would still stand if you walked away this week? No tidy resolution, no recap, no preview.
