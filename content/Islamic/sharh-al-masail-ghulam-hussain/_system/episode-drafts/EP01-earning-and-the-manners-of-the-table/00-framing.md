# Earning And The Manners Of The Table

## Opening directive
Two hosts, unnamed: a male scholar leads, a female seeker questions; never speak their names. Open with a brief warm welcome — name the book and its author, a practical treatise of rulings called *Explaining the Issues* — then preview: it opens at the market stall and the dinner table, not the prayer mat. No cold open. Target a 50 to 60 minute conversation. Then say, word for word: "Whoever earns lawfully and spares his children from having to ask will meet God with a face like the full moon."

## Name discipline
One fixed English label per figure; never speak Arabic names or book titles.
- The Prophet → "the Prophet"; at first mention only, add "peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family."
- Commander of the Believers → that title alone, never paired with a personal name; at first mention only, add "peace be upon him."
- Household figures → the fifth Imam / the sixth Imam / the Prophet's grandson — fixed, never rotated.
- Also: the author; the visiting ascetic; the early teacher; the early transmitter; Gabriel; Joseph; Pharaoh.

## Pronunciation
Say each term ONCE. Never say the original spelling and the English form back-to-back.
- ihram: ihraam
- Sunnah: sunnah

## Three-part focus
Walk the source's own order. Three movements, each landing once.
1. Crisis and dignity — earning sits inside religion, not beside it: trade and keep away from laziness; better the rope and the load than asking a man of wealth, whether he gives or withholds; learn the rulings first, then trade. The reward attaches to lawful means and to sparing dependants from asking — not to wealth or success.
2. The pivot — the table as discipline: eating on the earth as a servant eats, from what is in front of you, small mouthfuls, never faulting the food; giving food while it is still wanted and refusing even thanks; the guest's right of three days; the host's intention. Say the sentence word for word again here. The discipline breaks by the way it is kept — performed to be seen, it inverts.
3. Boundary and stakes — the rulings, plainly: what may be eaten, the gentle slaughter with a sharp blade and no pressing down, hunting, then dress. Necessity relieves fully, then tightens; intention and competence qualify a slaughterer, not status.

## Host dynamic
The scholar leads; the seeker supplies the friction — genuine pushback at least three times, one real concession. Qualify doctrine by school ("the classical Ismaili reading"); the source gives no chapter-and-verse, so supply none.
Sample friction: "I don't buy that yet — these sayings sound like an ordinary endorsement of hard work."; "That sounds like wordplay — fine clothing becomes proper because provision expanded?" Steelman each objection before answering.
Each host lets the other finish a thought: no interjections, no talking over, never "yeah" / "right" / "mm-hmm" — begin with substance.

## Tone constraints
Use only these three images, all from the source, and no others:
Analogy 1: the rope and the load carried on the back — the man who earns rather than asks.
Analogy 2: the palace whose outer face is seen from within and inner from without — a life with no hidden side.
Analogy 3: Joseph in Pharaoh's council on gilded couches — fine surroundings and service to the weak at once.
No editorialising on the rulings.

## Landing
Close on the pattern governing the whole: the thing is permitted; what is regulated is the announcement of it — the merchant's honesty, the host's intention, a woman's hidden ornaments. Just before that turn, repeat word for word the sentence from the opening. Then end on a question tied to action — which half of your week, the earning or the way you eat it, would not survive these rulings? Leave it open. Do not recap; nothing follows the question.

## Do not (forbidden vocabulary — do not speak)
Forbidden: Twitter, X, social media, algorithm, YouTube, TikTok, "deep dive", "the 21st century".
Forbidden: "wow", "right?", "exactly", "no way", "that's so interesting".
Forbidden: "Firstly", "Secondly", "Furthermore", "In conclusion", "To summarize", "Lastly".
No modern framings, no cross-episode references, no "today we'll discuss", no pre-announcing what comes later.
R-RECURRING-THESIS: repeat the spine thesis verbatim three times — at opening, pivot, and close.
