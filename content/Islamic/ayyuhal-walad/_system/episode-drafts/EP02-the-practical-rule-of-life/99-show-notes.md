# Show notes — EP02

**Title:** The Practical Rule of Life

**Blurb:** The second and closing chapter of *Ayyuhal Walad* (AY-yu-hal WA-lad — "O Beloved Son") receives the practical regimen the opening chapter held in reserve. The chapter opens at the threshold the first chapter held open — Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA — sha-FEEQ al-BAL-khee) putting his question to his disciple Hatim Ism (RA — HAA-tim ISM): *what eight benefits did you distil from thirty-three years of my company?* The eight are walked one by one — observation of the world, Quranic anchor, decision of the will, ratification by the master. The disciple's eight observations recover the doctrinal spine of the *Deen* (DEEN — the path to God): righteous deeds as the only beloved who descends into the grave; war against the *Nafs* (NAFS) until it finds peace in the worship of Allah; wealth as a deposit with Allah; *Taqwa* (TAQ-wa — pious reverence) as the sole coordinate of honor in Allah's sight; contentment with Allah's distribution of livelihoods; *Shaytan* as the sole enemy and *Sirat al-Mustaqeem* (si-RAAT al-mus-ta-QEEM — the straight path) as what opens once the enmity is correctly targeted; sustenance as the responsibility of Allah; complete *Tawakkul* (ta-WAK-kul — reliance) on Allah in every matter. Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) verdicts that these eight are what the *Tawrat* (taw-RAAT — Torah), *Injil* (in-JEEL — Gospel of Prophet Isa, peace of Allah be upon him), *Zabur* (za-BOOR — Psalms of Prophet Dawud, peace of Allah be upon him), and Holy Quran all teach. The Imam then resumes the seven conditions of the seeker — the fifth (a *Shaykh al-Kamil* — SHAYKH al-KAA-mil, with the qualities and chain back to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, plus the outward and inward etiquettes of the disciple toward him), the sixth (guarding the *Nafs* by abandoning the company of the ill-charactered), and the seventh (humility and contentment) — and gives the doctrinal definitions of *Tasawwuf* (ta-SAW-wuf — obedience to the *Shari'ah* joined with selfless service to creation), servitude (obedience, contentment with the decree, abandonment of one's claims), *Tawakkul*, and *Ikhlas* (ikh-LAAS — the dissolution of *Riya* — ri-YAA — by regarding all creation as powerless pebbles and stones). With the counsel of Khidr (KHI-dr) and the saying of Shaykh Zun Noon Misri (RA — zun-NOON mis-REE), the Imam pivots to the eight closing admonitions — four restraints (do not debate except with the patient who can be healed; do not preach for performance or applause; do not greet, look at, or keep the company of the affluent and rulers; do not accept gifts from rulers) and four actions (the inverted-servant test for the relation with Allah; the loved-for-self test for the relation with the slaves of Allah; study only what you would study in the week before death; do not stockpile more than one year's provision). The chapter and the book close with the long supplication the Imam writes for the disciple to recite after every *Salat* (sa-LAAT — obligatory prayer), and with the closing seal: *This book comes to an end with the help of Al-Malik, the Absolute Sovereign, and Al-Wahhab, the Giver of all.*
