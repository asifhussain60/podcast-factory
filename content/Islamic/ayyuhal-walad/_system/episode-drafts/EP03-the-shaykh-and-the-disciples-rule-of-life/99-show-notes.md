# Show notes — EP03

**Title:** The Shaykh and the Disciple's Rule of Life

**Blurb:** The practical rule of life Imam al-Ghazali hands his disciple. Three braided strands. First, the eight benefits Hatim ibn Ism brought back to his master Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi from long inward observation of the people of the world — each one anchored in a Quranic verse, each one the same shape: *I looked, I weighed against the Book, I changed what I did.* The only beloved who descends into the grave is one's own righteous deeds; the Nafs must be disciplined under Quran 79:40–41; wealth must be sent ahead under Quran 16:96; Taqwa is the only true nobility under Quran 49:13; contentment with the assigned portion under Quran 43:32; Shaytan alone is the enemy under Quran 35:6 and the covenant verse; sustenance is Allah's responsibility under Quran 11:6; Tawakkul on the One who suffices under Quran 65:3. Shaykh Shafeeq's verdict on the eight is that whoever acts on them has acted on all four sacred Books — Tawrat, Injil, Zabur, Quran. Second, the figure at the center of the seeker's life — the Shaykh al-Kamil, the perfected guide whose Bay'a chains unbroken back to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ; the full catalog of his qualifications; the bright line that he cannot command anything forbidden by the Shari'ah; the inner etiquette of no doubt in the heart — with the honest-withdrawal clause when the inward and outward cannot agree. Third, the inner vocabulary of the Path — Ghazali's brief, precise definitions of Tasawwuf (Shari'ah + good toward creation), servitude (obey, accept, dissolve the *I want*), Tawakkul (certitude in what is promised), and Ikhlas (deeds for Allah alone, with the metaphysical cure for Riya). The chapter closes with Ghazali's instruction that the rest of the answers come through practice rather than further questioning — three Quranic verses on not hastening, the verb *travel* from Quran on those who refuse to set out, and Shaykh Zun Noon Misri's line that the path is the soul actually offered, not the beautiful sayings of the Sufis. The eight admonitions belong to the next episode.
