INTRODUCTION OF THE BOOK

It has been learned that one of the students of Imam Ghazali (Rahmatullahi Alayhe [RA — May Allah shower His mercy upon him]), who had acquired a great deal of knowledge and spiritual benefit from him over a number of years, found a question weighing upon his heart. He reflected that he had spent many years learning from Imam Ghazali (RA), studying various branches of religious knowledge and making great sacrifices toward that end. Yet he still did not know which of those branches of knowledge would truly benefit him, illuminate his grave, and be of help to him on the Day of Judgment — nor which branches would be of no benefit to him on that Day, so that he might refrain from them. For the Holy Prophet Muhammad (May the Peace and Blessings of Allah be upon him) said:

*"A'auzu Billahi Min 'Ilm-il La Yanfa'au."*
"I seek Allah's refuge from knowledge that is of no benefit."

This disciple of Imam Ghazali (RA) pondered these matters for several days and then wrote a letter to Imam Ghazali seeking an answer to his dilemma along with responses to certain other questions. He also asked Imam Ghazali (RA) for personal counsel and for a supplication he could recite at all times. He wrote in his letter that although Imam Ghazali (RA) had already authored numerous books on these subjects — such as *Ihya al-Ulum ad-Din* (Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God), *Kimiya as-Sa'ada* (Alchemy of Ultimate Success and Happiness), *Jawahir ul-Quran* (Priceless Gems of the Holy Quran), *Arba'een* (Forty Steps), and *Minhaj ul-'Abideen ila Jannatu Rabbul 'Alamin* (The Best Way for the Worshippers of God Leading Them to the Paradise of the Lord of All Universes), and others — this humble individual was in need of something he could continually study and consistently act upon. In reply to his letter, Imam Ghazali (RA) sent him the following counsel.

IMAM GHAZALI'S RESPONSE TO THE LETTER

In the Name of Allah (God), the Most Compassionate and the Most Merciful.

All praises belong to Allah alone, the Lord of all the universes, and blessings and peace of Allah be upon His Messenger Muhammad and upon all of his descendants and followers.

My dear beloved son and true friend!

May Allah, the Exalted, grant you a long life in His worship and obedience, and may He enable you to walk on the path of His beloveds. You should know that the fountain of all good counsel is the holy existence of the Prophet Muhammad (Peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), for the foundation of all counsel rests upon his Ahadith (traditions) and Sunnah (outward character and conduct, as well as internal states). Any counsel contrary to his Ahadith and Sunnah will be of no benefit. Countless accounts of advice have been written and conveyed in the light of the Prophet's Ahadith and Sunnah. If you have received guidance from them, then you have no need of any counsel from me. However, if you have not received benefit from the counsels of the Prophet Muhammad (Peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), then tell me — in all these years, what knowledge have you truly acquired from me?

My dear beloved son! Among the counsels of the Holy Prophet Muhammad (Peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) is the following:

*"'Alamatu I'radil Allahi 'Anil 'Abdi Ishtighaluhu Bima La Ya'nehe, Wa Inn Imra'un Zahabat Sa'atuhu Min 'Umrihi Fi Ghayri Ma Yukhlaq Lahu Lahariyyun Ayyatula 'Alayhe Hasratun."*
"A servant's preoccupation with useless matters is a sign that Allah has withdrawn His special favor from him or her. And if even a single moment of a servant's life passes in pursuit of something other than that for which Allah created him or her, then that is a matter of profound sorrow."

The Holy Prophet Muhammad (Peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) has furthermore said:

*"Mun Jaawazal Arba'eena Sinatu Wa Lam Yughlab Khayruhu 'Ala Sharrihi Fal Yatajahhaza Ilan Nar."*
"An individual whose condition, even after forty years of age, is such that the good within him or her does not prevail over the evil, should prepare to go to Hell."

My dear beloved son! It is very easy to give this advice to all the people of the world, yet very difficult to act upon it oneself. Those in whose hearts the pleasures of the world and the desires of the lower self hold dominion find such advice and guidance bitter. Their hearts are ensnared in worldly desires and pleasures. Particularly deserving of mention here is the individual who acquires wisdom, philosophy, and worldly learning, and then imagines that this knowledge will be his or her means of salvation in the Hereafter — while also bringing fame and prestige in this world. Such a person thinks it is not necessary to act upon this knowledge and that he or she is exempt from doing so. This mistaken belief belongs to those who study philosophy alone. All Glory and Honor to Allah the Great. Such a person does not even realize that the knowledge acquired and left unacted upon will itself become a cause of punishment on the Day of Judgment. Does this person not know that the Holy Prophet Muhammad (Peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said:

<!-- PAGE 5 -->

*"Inna Ashaddan Nasi 'Azaban Yawmal Qiyamati 'Alimun Lam Yanfa'hul Allahu Bi 'Ilmihi."*
"On the Day of Judgment, the most severe torment will be visited upon the scholar whom Allah did not benefit through his or her knowledge — that is, one who did not act upon what was acquired."

It has been narrated in the accounts of the intimate friends of Allah that a certain saint saw Shaykh Junaid Baghdadi (RA) in a dream after his death and asked him:

"O Abu al-Qasim! Tell me of your state after death!"

Shaykh Junaid Baghdadi (RA) replied:

"All discourses and spiritual signs (Isharaat) turned out to be of no avail. But certain cycles (Rak'at) of prayer I had performed during the Tahajjud (the third portion of the night) were of benefit."

My dear beloved son! Do not deprive yourself of good deeds, do not neglect the knowledge of outward matters (Shari'ah), and do not be without inner excellences and noble qualities of the heart. Be firmly convinced that knowledge alone, without righteous deeds, will not benefit you on the Day of Judgment. Consider the following illustration: a person is passing through a jungle or desert carrying ten sharp swords, fine bows, and various other weapons, and possessing full knowledge of the art of war and the use of those weapons — and then suddenly a lion confronts him. Tell me, can that person be saved without actually using those weapons? You know very well that he cannot. Likewise, understand that if someone knows a hundred thousand points of jurisprudence (Fiqh) but does not act on them, that knowledge will be of no benefit.

A second example is that of a sick person who knows that his or her cure lies in such and such medicines, yet does not take them. Will the knowledge of those medicines and their healing properties actually cure the illness? You can see quite clearly that merely possessing the knowledge of a remedy cannot cure one's spiritual or inner diseases. As a poet has said: even if you purchase two thousand pounds of wine, you will not become intoxicated until you drink from it.

Acquiring great quantities of knowledge, continually browsing through many books, yet not acting upon that knowledge will be of no benefit to you unless and until you make yourself deserving of Allah's mercy through righteous deeds. Until then, the Grace of Allah will remain distant from you. Hear what the Quran says in this regard:

*"Wa Anna Laysa Lil Insani Illa Ma Sa'a."*
"Human beings will attain only what they have striven for."

This establishes that human beings can achieve success only through genuine effort and striving. My dear beloved son! I am aware that you may have read that this verse was abrogated. Let us grant that it was — but then what do you say about the following two verses:

*"Faman Ya'mal Mithqala Zarratin Khayran Yarahu, Wa Man Ya'mal Mithqala Zarratin Sharran Yarahu."*
"And so, whoever shall have done an atom's weight of good shall see it, and whoever shall have done an atom's weight of evil shall see it." (Quran, 99:7–8)

<!-- PAGE 6 -->

*"Faman Kana Yarjul Liqa'a Rabbihi Fal Ya'mal 'Amalan Salihan."*
"Hence, whoever looks forward with hope and awe to meeting his or her Lord on the Day of Judgment, let him or her perform righteous deeds, and let him or her not ascribe unto anyone or anything a share in the worship due to the Lord." (Quran, 18:110)

*"Innal Ladhina Amanu Wa 'Amilus Salihati Kanat Lahum Jannatul Firdawsi Nuzulan Khalidina Fiha."*
"Verily, those who believe in Allah and perform righteous deeds — for them the highest Paradise shall be a welcome abode in which they will dwell forever."

And at another place the Exalted Allah has said:

*"Illa Man Taba Wa Amana Wa 'Amila 'Amalan Salihan."*
"Except for those who repented from their sins and wrongdoings, and believed, and performed righteous deeds."

And what do you say about the following holy Ahadith — the traditions of the Prophet Muhammad (May the peace and blessings of Allah be upon him)?

*"Buniyal Islamu 'Ala Khams: Shahadatu An La Ilaha IllAllahu Wa Anna Muhammadan Rasulullahi, Wa Iqamus Salati, Wa Ita'iz Zakati, Wa Sawmu Shahri Ramadana, Wa Hijjul Bayti Manistataa'a Ilayhi Sabila."*
"Islam is built upon five things: to testify that there is none worthy of worship other than Allah and that Muhammad (May the peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) is His Messenger; to establish regular prayers (Salat); to pay the Zakat (obligatory charity); to fast during the holy month of Ramadan; and to perform Hajj (pilgrimage to the House of God) for those who possess the means to do so."

*"Al Imanu Iqraarun Bil Lisani Wa Tasdiqun Bil Janani Wa 'Amalun Bil Arkani."*
"Iman (faith) is to testify with the tongue, to affirm with the heart, and to act upon the teachings of Islam."

After this explanation, should a thought arise in your heart that I am saying a servant of God will enter Paradise not by Allah's Grace but solely by his or her own deeds — know that you have not understood my meaning. On the contrary, what I am saying is that a servant will enter Paradise because of Allah's Favor, Generosity, and Blessings. However, unless that servant makes himself or herself deserving of Allah's Mercy through worship and obedience, he or she will not be in a position to receive that Mercy and Favor. I do not merely assert this on my own — rather, Allah, the Exalted, Himself says:

*"Inna Rahmata Allahi Qaribun Minal Muhsinin."*
"Verily, the Mercy of Allah is close to the doers of good."

How, then, will a servant enter Paradise without the Mercy of Allah, the Exalted? I repeat this important point: how will a servant enter Paradise without Allah's Mercy? Yet if someone argues that one will enter Paradise on account of faith (Iman) alone, there are many formidable valleys — that is, obstacles — that lie ahead. Among them, the first and most daunting is to carry one's faith safely to the end.

My dear beloved son! Be firmly convinced that without effort, you will not receive its reward. There was a man among the Children of Israel who worshipped Allah with great devotion. Allah sent an angel to him as a test, and the angel said:

<!-- PAGE 7 -->

"Allah, the Holy, says that you have been tormenting yourself to no purpose, for your worship is not accepted by Him and you will go to Hell."

Upon hearing this message from the angel, the righteous man replied:

"Allah, the Exalted, created us for His worship and servitude, and our duty is to serve Him — no matter what. He is the Owner of all things and all people, including myself, and He is the All-Powerful."

The angel then returned to the Presence of Allah, the Exalted, and said:

"O my Lord! You are the Knower of the secrets of this universe, and You also know what Your worshipping servant has replied."

Upon this, Allah, the Exalted, said:

"Since this servant of Ours does not turn his face away from Our worship and servitude, We too will not turn Our Face away from him."

*"Ishhadu Ya Mala'ikati Anni Qad Ghafartu Lahu."*
"O My angels! Bear witness, all of you, that I have forgiven him."

My dear beloved son! Hear what the Messenger of Allah, Muhammad (Peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), says:

*"Hasibu Qabla An Tuhasabu Wa Zinu Qabla An Tuzanu."*
"Take account of yourselves before you are taken to account on the Day of Judgment, and weigh your deeds — that is, critically examine and correct the shortcomings in your deeds — before your deeds are weighed and judged, and reward or punishment is decreed."

Ali (May Allah honor his countenance) said:

*"Man Zanna Annahu Bidunl Juhdi Yasilu Fahuwa Mutmanniun, Wa Man Zanna Annahu Bibazlil Juhdi Yasilu Fahuwa Mut'ibun."*
"A person who thinks he or she will reach Paradise without righteous striving is self-deluded, and a person who thinks he or she will reach Paradise through striving alone is merely exhausting himself or herself."

Shaykh Hasan al-Basri (May Allah shower His Mercy upon him) says:

*"Talabul Jannati Bila 'Amalin Zambun Minaz Zunub."*
"To desire Paradise without good deeds is itself a sin among the sins."

Another intimate of Allah says:

*"Al Haqiqatu Terkul Mulahazatil 'Amali La Terkul 'Amal."*
"The reality is that one should act without admiring one's own actions — but one should never abandon the performance of good deeds, even if there is a risk of self-admiration."

<!-- PAGE 8 -->

Our master of both worlds, the Prophet Muhammad (May the peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), expresses this truth in a manner far more eloquent, clear, pure, and beautiful than any of the above sayings:

*"Al Kayyisu Man Dana Nafsahu Wa 'Amila Lima Ba'dal Mawt, Wal Ahmaqu Man Ittaba'a Nafsahu Hawaha Wa Tamanna 'Alal Allahi."* In another narration: *"Wa Tamanna 'Alal Allahil Maghfira."*
"Intelligent is the one who subdues his or her nafs (lower self, with its evil desires and inclinations) and performs righteous deeds for the life to come. Mindless — that is, foolish — is the one who surrenders his or her nafs to pleasures, greed, and vain desires, and yet supposes that Allah is with him or her." In some traditions the wording is: "and hopes for Allah's forgiveness."

My dear beloved son! You have stayed awake at night rehearsing and memorizing knowledge, and you have sacrificed sleep in its study. If your intention in all of this was to gain worldly benefits and worldly prestige and status, then:

*"Fawaylun Laka Thumma Waylun Lak."*
"Woe unto you, and again woe unto you."


However, if your intentions were to establish and strengthen within yourself — and then within others — the Path to God (*Deen*) brought by Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), to attain moral uplift and spiritual civilization, and to eliminate evil from your soul, then:

"*Fatuba Laka Thumma Tuba Laka.*"
"For you is happiness and blessings, and again for you is happiness and blessings."

"*Sahrul 'Uyuna Bighairi Wajhika Sana ay'un*
*Wa Baka uhunna Bighairi Faqdika Batilun*"
"O my Lord! The wakefulness of the eyes, except for beholding the beauty of Your Countenance, is useless. And unless it is for the sorrow of separation from You, the weeping of eyes over anything or anyone else is gravely wrong."

A prophetic tradition (*hadith*) says:

"*'Ishu Ma Shay'ta Fa Innaka Mayyeytu Wahbib Ma Shay'ta Fa Innaka Mufariqatun Wa'mil Ma Shay'ta Fa Innaka Tujza Bihi.*"
"O human being! Spend your life however you wish, but know that one day you will die. Love whoever you wish, but know that one day you will be separated from them. Perform deeds however you wish, but know that one day you will surely receive their just recompense — good or bad, according to what you have done."

What benefit have you derived for yourself, and what have you truly gained from studying the various fields of knowledge — theology (*Ilmul Kalaam*), ethics (*Ilmul Ikhlaq*), medicine (*Ilmut Tib*), astrology (*Nujoom*), linguistics, poetry, the science of warfare, and the like? What have you obtained beyond a wasted life and worldly renown? I have read in the Gospel (*Injil*) of Prophet Isa (Jesus, peace of Allah be upon him) that between the moment a corpse is placed in the casket and carried to the grave, Allah the Exalted addresses it with forty questions by His Power. In the first of these, Allah the Exalted asks:

<!-- PAGE 9 -->
"*'Abdi Qad Tahherta Munzaral Khalqi Sineena Fahul Tahherta Munzari Sa'atun.*"
"O My slave! For many years you adorned and beautified yourself with outward knowledge to display before My mortal creation. But for My sake alone, did you ever purify your heart from heedlessness and evil — even for a single moment?"

[Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said in an authentic tradition: "Allah does not look at your faces or your bodies, but He looks at your hearts." Therefore, it is of the utmost importance to purify the heart, which is forever within Allah's sight.]

My dear beloved son! Every day the Voice of Allah resonates within your heart:

"*'Abdi Ma Tasna'u Bighairi Wa Anta Majfufum Bikhairi.*"
"O My slave! Why do you worship for show and ostentation, when both good and difficult circumstances are in My Hands alone? Worship Me with sincere intention and devotion — not to impress any of My mortal creation, who cannot benefit or harm you in the slightest except by My Will."

My dear beloved son! Knowledge without action is madness, and action without knowledge is worthless. The knowledge that does not keep you away from sins today, and does not kindle within you the desire to obey Allah, will not save you from Hellfire tomorrow. If you do not perform righteous deeds today and do not make amends for the time you have wasted, then on the Day of Judgment you will cry out:

"*Ferje'yna Na'mal Saaliha.*"
"Send us back, O our Lord, so that we may perform righteous deeds!"

And it will be said to you:

"O heedless one! That is precisely where you have come from."

My dear beloved son! Summon courage within yourself and stir your body to strive upon the Path of God. Make every effort to perform pious deeds, for you are journeying toward the grave. Those who preceded you in the grave are waiting for you at every moment, watching for when you will join them. Beware — do not approach them empty-handed, without the fruit of good deeds. Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (may Allah be pleased with him) said:

"*Hazihil Ajsaadu Qafasut Tuyouri Awo Astabalud Dawab.*"
"These bodies are either cages of birds or stables of animals."

Reflect, then, on which of the two you inhabit. If you are among the birds, then when you hear "*Irji'I ila Rabbika!*" — "Return to your Lord!" — you will be liberated and will soar to the loftiest heights.

"*Ihtuzza 'Arshur Rahmaani Limauti Sa'adibne Muadh.*"
"The Throne of the Most Compassionate shook at the death of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh."

But God forbid that you should be among the animals, of whom it has been said:

<!-- PAGE 10 -->
"*Uulaaika Kal An'aami Bal Hum Adallu.*"
"They are like cattle — nay, even more astray."

Then be certain that you will travel carrying your burdens from the place of the *zawiya* (spiritual retreat) straight to the place of *hawiya* (the abyss of Hell) — that is, from this world directly into the Fire. Once Shaykh Hasan al-Basri (may Allah shower His mercy upon him) was handed a cold, sweet drink. The moment he took it in his hand, he let out a deep sigh and fell unconscious. When he regained consciousness, people asked him what had come over him. He replied:

"*Zakartu Umniyyata Ahlin Naari Heena Yaquluna Le Ahlil Jannati Un Uufidu 'Aleyna Minal Ma'i.*"
"I remembered the plea of the inhabitants of Hell when they will beseech the inhabitants of Paradise to pour upon them even a little water."

O beloved! If knowledge without action had been sufficient for you and deeds were truly unnecessary, then why would Allah the Exalted call out at the time before dawn:

"*Hul Min Ta ibin Hul Min Sa ilin Hul Min Mustaghfirin.*"
"Is there anyone who repents from sins? Is there anyone who asks of Me? Is there anyone who seeks My forgiveness?"

Were knowledge alone enough, this proclamation of Allah the Exalted would be rendered meaningless. In truth, this divine call at dawn is directed toward those of whom it is said:

"*Kanu Qaleelum Minal Laili Ma Yahja'oon.*"
"There are only a few servants of Allah who sleep but little in the last part of the night — that blessed hour of nearness to Allah."

Once a group of Companions were praising 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar (may Allah be pleased with them both) in the presence of the Messenger of Allah (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), upon which the Messenger of Allah said:

"*Nay'mur Rajula Huwa Lau Kana Yusalli Bil Laili.*"
"He is a good man. If only he were to pray the Tahajjud — those optional prayers in the blessed final hours before dawn, which are indispensable for spiritual progress on the Path of Allah."

On another occasion, the Messenger of Allah (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) said to a Companion:

"*La Takthirin Nauma Bil Laili Fainna Kathratan Naumi Bil Laili Tad'oo Sahibahu Faqirai Yaumal Qiyamati.*"
"Do not sleep overmuch at night, for excessive sleep at night leaves its keeper empty-handed on the Day of Judgment — having squandered the opportunity of drawing close to Allah through remembrance, prayer, and devotion, especially in those final blessed hours."

"*Waminal Laili Fatahajjad Bihi Nafilatal Laka.*"
"And in the last part of the night, perform the Tahajjud prayers for the sake of Allah — it shall be of great benefit to you." (Quran)

<!-- PAGE 11 -->
In the above verse, this is a direct commandment from Allah to His Prophet (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him).

"*Wa Bil Ashaarihum Yustaghfiroon.*"
"And those true servants of Allah, in the time before dawn, plead earnestly for Allah's forgiveness." (Quran)

In this verse, there is an expression of divine gratitude toward such servants.

"*Wul Mustaghfireena Bil Ashaari.*"
"And those true servants of Allah seek Allah's forgiveness at the time before dawn." (Quran)

In this verse, there is an honorable mention of such servants of Allah.

The Messenger of Allah (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), master of both worlds, said:

"Allah the Exalted is especially pleased by three sounds: the crowing of the rooster that rouses people for the Tahajjud prayers; the recitation of the Holy Quran; and the voices of those who, in the last portion of the night, plead to Allah the Exalted for His forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance."

Shaykh Sufyan al-Thawri (may Allah shower His mercy upon him), one of the intimate friends of Allah, said:

"*Inna Allaha Ta'ala Khalaqa Reehun Tahabbu Waqtal Ashaari Tahmilul Azkaara Wal Istighfaara Ilal Malikil Jabbari.*"
"Allah the Exalted has created a wind that blows in the last part of the night. At that hour, the voices of those servants of Allah who engage in His remembrance and seek His forgiveness are carried by this wind and presented to the Court of Allah the Exalted."

Shaykh Sufyan al-Thawri (may Allah shower His mercy upon him) has also said:

"When the night begins, an angel beneath the Throne of Allah proclaims that all worshippers should rise. Those whom Allah has granted *tawfiq* — divine enablement and good fortune — arise and begin to pray. In the middle of the night, a second angel proclaims that all obedient and devoted servants of Allah should rise, and they do so, continuing in prayer until the time of *sahr* — the last blessed portion of the night. When *sahr* arrives, a third angel proclaims that all who seek Allah's forgiveness should rise, and they rise and seek His forgiveness. When the time of dawn draws near, a fourth angel calls out: 'O heedless ones! Arise — the day has broken!' And these people rise from their beds as the dead will rise from their graves on the Day of Resurrection."

My dear beloved son! It is recorded in the testament of Luqman (peace of Allah be upon him) that he counseled his son, saying:

"*Ya Buneyya La Takununnad Deeku Akyas Minka Fainnahu Yunaadi Bil Ashaari Wa Anta Na'imun.*"
"My son! Do not let the rooster prove more intelligent than you. Let it not be that he rises in the last part of the night to proclaim the hour of remembrance

<!-- PAGE 12 -->
and glorifies his Lord, while you remain sunk in the deep sleep of heedlessness, carelessness, and negligence."

This truth is made vivid in the following verses of poetry:

"*Laqad Hatafet Fi Junhil Laili Hamamatun*
*'Ala Funanin Wahnun Wa Inni Na'imun*

*Kazabtu Wa Baitil Allahi Lau Kuntu 'Aashiqan*
*Lama Sabaqatni Bil Buqa'il Hamaaimun*

*Wa Az'amu Anni Haa'imun Zu Sababatin*
*Lahu Bi Wa La Abki Wa Tabkil Bahaaimun.*"

"In the depths of the night, the dove perched upon a branch calls out to her Lord,
While I lie fast asleep.

By the Lord of the House of Allah — the Ka'ba! I am a liar!
Had I been a true lover of Allah, the doves would not have surpassed me in weeping from the pain of separation from my Beloved.

Yet I claim to be a wandering lover consumed by longing,
While my eyes remain dry — and the very animals weep in their love for their Creator."

My dear beloved son! Our purpose here is that you should truly understand what obedience and worship are. Obedience and worship mean following the *Shari'ah* — the Divine Law — of Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), whether the commandments call for performing righteous deeds or for restraining oneself from evil, whether they pertain to his words or his example. In short, whatever you do or refrain from doing, whatever you say or leave unsaid — all of it must be in full accordance with the teachings of Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him). If you speak, speak according to his teachings. If you remain silent, do so according to his guidance. If you act or abstain from acting, do so in conformity with his example. If you do something believing it to be an act of worship, yet you do not do it in obedience to the command of Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), then it will not be counted as worship — it will be counted as a sin, even if it takes the outward form of prayer or fasting. Do you not know that if someone fasts on either of the two days of Eid, or on the days of Hajj (pilgrimage), they commit a sin? Though it is fasting in form, it is not done in accordance with the commandment of Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), and so the person is guilty of wrongdoing. Likewise, if someone performs prayers at a forbidden time — such as after the Asr (late afternoon) prayer before sunset, or after the Fajr (dawn) prayer before the sun has risen sufficiently — or performs prayers at an unlawfully occupied place, that person is called *athim* (a sinner) and *fasiq* (a transgressor). Though it outwardly resembles worship, it is not correct because it was not done in accordance with the guidance of Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him). Conversely, when a husband and wife come together in lawful intimacy, it is not a sin, though it may appear otherwise to the uninformed — for it is done within the guidance of Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) and is therefore *halal* (permissible). It is thus established that worship is another name for obedience — that is, worship means obedience.

<!-- PAGE 13 -->
In the same way, prayers and fasting are counted as acts of worship only when they are performed in accordance with the Divine guidelines. Therefore, my dear beloved son, let all your words and deeds conform to the commandments of Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him). Whatever actions you perform and whatever conversations you hold must be in accordance with the *Shari'ah* — the Divine Law — because any knowledge or action of God's creation that falls outside the *Shari'ah* is plainly misguided and draws human beings away from the truth. This is why Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) abrogated all prior religious dispensations.

Therefore, do nothing without the guidance of Prophet Muhammad (may peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), and be certain that you cannot advance upon the Path of Allah the Exalted through accumulated knowledge alone. This Path must be traveled through earnest effort and *mujahadah* — intense inner spiritual struggle and disciplined exercise of the soul — and the pleasures and demands of the lower self must be severed with the sword of *mujahadah*. These desires of the lower self cannot be uprooted through immorality or through the deceptions of false Sufis — those who claim the station of Sufism and mimic its outward language and appearance while remaining hollow and devious within. REALITY OF TASAWWUF (IHSAN, SUFISM, SPIRITUAL STRUGGLE)

You have asked: what is Tasawwuf? Tasawwuf is defined by two qualities. The first is that the servant should be obedient to Allah — that is, he or she should be acting in accordance with the Shari'ah, the outward laws of Islam encompassing obligations, commandments, and prohibitions. The second is that the seeker should be compassionate and a doer of good toward all of Allah's creation, which is not possible without first purifying one's soul of all evil qualities and then adorning it with all noble ones. One who perseveres in acting upon the Shari'ah and who genuinely desires the betterment and true success of all humanity is rightly called a Sufi — and this is the correct application of the term, as opposed to the distorted usages so widespread today. Loyalty and obedience to Allah means that the seeker sacrifices personal happiness for the sake of Allah. Sincerity toward all people means that the seeker does not maintain relationships for personal motives, but is selfless and performs selfless service, free from self-interest. Instead, the seeker dedicates himself or herself to the welfare of all people — though the condition is that this welfare must be in accordance with the Shari'ah, the Divine law.

1. PRINCIPLES OF DEBATE

First: as far as possible, do not engage in religious debates with everyone, and do not argue with anyone on any matter — religious or otherwise — for there are many hidden harms within such disputes, and the harms far outweigh any benefits. The second sign is that you consider it preferable to discuss the matter in private, away from an audience. However, if you are in a dispute and you are certain that you are correct and your opponent is arguing merely for the sake of arguing, then be on guard — do not continue the argument; end the discussion at that point. But if the disease worsens and its causes cannot be identified, then a more expert physician must be sought — one who can determine which ailments are curable and which are not. To expend effort trying to cure an incurable disease is nothing but a waste of time. Now understand the following:

CATEGORIES OF PATIENTS: The ignorant, who are likened to patients, fall into four categories. Of these four, only one category is curable. The remaining three categories are beyond cure.


The first kind of patient is one who asks questions out of jealousy or raises objections from envy. Jealousy is a lethal disease that has no cure. Whatever answer you give, no matter how sound, such a person will regard you as an enemy, and the fire of envy within them will only burn more fiercely. It is therefore better not to respond to this person at all. A poet has said it well:

*"Kullul 'Adawati Qad Turja Izalatuha*
*Illa 'Adawata Mun 'Aadaka Min Hasadin."*

"Every kind of animosity can be remedied — except the animosity that is born of jealousy."

The remedy, then, is to leave such a jealous person to remain afflicted with their disease:

*"Fa 'AaRid 'Um Mun Tawalla 'Un Zikrina Walam Yurid Illal Hayatad Dunya."*

"So turn away from the one who, out of jealousy, turns his face from Our remembrance and desires nothing but the pleasures of the life of this world."

The second kind of patient is one whose disease arises from a lack of intelligence and foolishness. This disease is likewise incurable. Prophet 'Isa (Jesus, peace be upon him) said that he was — with the permission of God — not incapable of restoring the dead to life, yet he was incapable of curing the fool and the ignorant. The ignorant fool is the one who has spent only a brief time acquiring knowledge, has not even begun to grasp intellectual and transmitted religious learning, and yet raises objections against scholars who have devoted their entire lives to those very disciplines. Such a person does not even perceive that their objection causes distress — to themselves, to others like them, and to scholars. Nor do they perceive that their objection is of no benefit whatsoever to themselves. The depth of knowledge and insight possessed by a great scholar is entirely beyond their comprehension, and beyond the comprehension of those who resemble them. That incomprehension itself is their foolishness. One should keep away from this kind of person and offer them no response.

**Advice according to one's comprehension.** The third kind of patient is one who, owing to restlessness and impatience, cannot absorb the teachings of the spiritually elevated, and who relies instead on their own deficient understanding, interpreting everything only according to their personal interest. This person is simple-minded, and their intellect is incapable of grasping the truth.

<!-- PAGE 25 -->

This kind of person should not be given a response either, for the Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) has said:

*"Nahnu Ma'aashirul Anbiyaee Umirna Un Natakallamun Nasa 'Ala Qadre 'Uuqulihim."*

"We, the community of prophets, have been commanded by Allah to speak to people according to the capacity of their minds."

**The individual capable of benefiting from advice.** The fourth kind of patient is one who is genuinely seeking the right path of guidance (Sirat al-Mustaqeem) — obedient, sharp, and intelligent — and who is free from anger, the following of base desires of the lower self (Nafs), jealousy, and the craving for wealth and fame. Whatever question this person asks, or whatever objection they raise, arises not from jealousy, fault-finding, or the desire to test, but from an earnest search for truth and correct guidance. This is the patient who can be cured. To answer the questions of such a person is therefore not merely permissible — it is obligatory (Wajib).

**2. The Reality of Preaching**

You are admonished to refrain from preaching and making speeches, for great calamities and harms lie within it. If you believe that whatever you preach you have already acted upon yourself, then bear in mind that Allah, the Exalted and the Truth, said to Prophet 'Isa (Jesus, peace be upon him):

*"Ya Ibna Maryama A'azee Nafsaka Fa In At-ta'azta Fa Izin Nasa Fustahyee Minni."*

"O Son of Mary! First admonish your own soul (Nafs); then, if it accepts your counsel, admonish others — otherwise have shame before Me."

If circumstances arise that compel you to preach, then guard against two things. First, in your sermon avoid colorful narrations, theatrical gestures, secretive language, flowery and embellished speech, pleasing poetry, any talk that transgresses the bounds of the Shari'ah (Divine Law), and the fabrications of false claimants to Sufism. Allah, the Exalted, does not love those who are pretentious and showy. Indeed, when a preacher's speech goes beyond the boundaries of mere pretense — when his sole aim is to impress others with seemingly eloquent language — know that his inner being is corrupt and his heart is heedless of God's remembrance. For the purpose of a sermon is not to display one's abilities; it is to speak of the punishment of the Hereafter. It is to describe one's own shortcomings in servitude to Allah and to express deep regret over wasted time and futile endeavors. It is to speak of the difficult stages of the life to come that stand as obstacles on the path ahead. It is to speak of how to pass through this world with one's faith (Iman) intact, of the awe-inspiring appearance of the Angel of Death (Malik ul-Maut) at the moment of dying, of the questioning in the grave by Munkar and Nakir — the two angels who appear after burial — and of the stations of the Day of Judgment. It is to speak of the fearsome spectacle of accountability on the plain of Hashr, where all of humankind will be assembled after resurrection; of the weighing of deeds in the Mizan — the mighty scale created by Almighty Allah for that purpose; of the crossing of the bridge called Sirat — a bridge over Hell, leading to Paradise, thinner than a hair and sharper than a sword, over which every human being will be made to walk; and of the other terrors of that Day. The preacher should set all these fearful realities before the people and make them aware of them. Beyond this, the preacher should bring to the minds of the gathering their own inner and outer faults and shortcomings, so that fear of the punishment of the Hereafter is kindled in their hearts, so that they grieve as deeply as possible over time lost in vain pursuits and strive to make up for it,

<!-- PAGE 26 -->

and so that they shed tears over the hours spent without the worship and remembrance of God. All these are the things that a sermon or religious talk should convey. Consider the example of a person whose house is struck by a flood — water reaching the very door, threatening within seconds to engulf the house and drown everyone inside. In that moment, the owner of the house will cry out urgently: *"Al-Hazr! Al-Hazr!"* — "Beware! Beware! Run! The floodwater is here!" At such a moment of imminent catastrophe, no one would describe the flood in ornate sentences, with secretive language, embellished prose, or decorated poetry. A sermon should be precisely like this — rooted in genuine fear of one's own reckoning, and conveying that same urgency to others with the gravity that an impending disaster demands.

Second, while delivering sermons, one must never allow the thought to enter one's heart that after listening, people will shout praises, fall into ecstasy, tear their clothes, raise a clamor in the gathering, and declare that it was a wonderful sermon given by such-and-such a person. Such thoughts are pure ostentation (Riya), and to be pleased by them is proof of one's lack of wisdom. Your true intention should be to summon God's creation from this world toward the next; from sin toward servitude of God; from greed toward detachment of the heart from worldly attachment and the adoption of contentment; from miserliness toward generosity; from ostentation (Riya) toward sincerity (Ikhlas — doing everything solely for the countenance of Allah); from arrogance toward humility; from heedlessness of Allah's remembrance toward God-consciousness; and from pride toward piety. Kindle in their hearts love for the life of the Hereafter so that they incline toward it. In this way, loosen their hearts' attachment to the world so that they come to regard that attachment as an enemy standing between them and their true purpose. Likewise, do not keep people in a state of false security about Allah's generosity and mercy by distorting the truth. Instead, cultivate in them piety (fear of Allah) and God-consciousness. Observe what lies in their hearts that contradicts Allah's approval, and what they are inclined toward that contradicts the Shari'ah of the Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him). Keep watch over their morality and deeds so that their wrongful conduct comes to an end and is replaced by noble character and righteous action. For those who are already overwhelmed by fear of Allah, instill in them such hope that when they rise from your gathering, certain noble inner qualities have been awakened within them and their outward demeanor has also been transformed. Those who were neglectful in worship should leave inclined toward it, with a deep and settled desire for the servitude of God; and those who were bold and audacious in committing sins should leave with the fear of Allah kindled in their hearts. A sermon that is not of this character — where the preacher does not address these matters — becomes a source of ruin for both the preacher and the audience. Such a person is a Shaytan (Satan) who, having become a slave of his own degraded lower self, imagines that the pulpit is a stage on which to display his abilities and harvest the fame and grandeur of the world. This Satan misguides God's creation from the path of true guidance, sheds their blood, and plunges them into eternal destruction. God's creation should keep far away from such a person. Such people spread a corruption (Fasad) in religion that even Satan himself does not spread. Whoever has the authority to remove such a preacher from the pulpit (Minbar), it is obligatory (Wajib) upon him to do so deliberately and firmly, and to prevent

<!-- PAGE 27 -->

such people from giving sermons, so that they are not able to distort the meaning of commanding the right and forbidding the evil ('Amr bil Ma'roof Wa Nahi 'Anil Munkar).

**3. Staying Away from the Affluent and Rulers**

The third admonition is that you should not even greet any king, any person of wealth, or any ruler. Stay away from their company and gatherings, and beyond that, do not so much as lift your eyes to look at them. For in looking upon them and keeping their company lie many hidden calamities. But if you ever happen to find yourself in their company by chance, refrain from praising them. As a hadith states:

*"Fa InnAllaha Yaghdib Iza Mudihal Faasiqu Waz Zalimu Wa Iza Madaha Wa Mun Da'aa Li Zalimin Bitoolil Baqaaii Faqad Ahabba Aye Ya'siyAllahu Fil Arde."*

"Verily, Allah, the Exalted, is angered when any transgressor (Fasiq) or oppressor (Zalim) is praised; and whoever prays for a long life for an oppressor has, in effect, expressed a desire that sin be perpetuated upon the earth."

**4. Not Accepting Gifts from Rulers**

The fourth admonition is that you should not accept any gifts from rulers, even if you are certain that what they offer comes from lawful (Halal) sources. The reason is that harboring any desire for their possessions causes great damage and corruption in the Deen — the path to God. The favors and rewards that come from rulers give rise to oppression, transgression, and sin, all of which damage the Deen. The minimum corruption that flows from this is that you will begin to love the oppressors, and when a person loves another, they pray for that person's long life. If an oppressor lives long, oppression endures longer, and there is greater turmoil and corruption in the world — and what could be worse than that? Beware! Beware! Shaytan (Satan) will try to misguide you by planting this thought in your heart: "First take the money from these rulers, then distribute it among the poor, bring them comfort, and fulfill their needs." Beware — never accept this kind of counsel from any Shaytan, whether from among the Jinn — an invisible creation of God who, when inclined toward evil, become Satan's instruments for inciting harm among human beings — or from among humankind. Do not be ensnared by their machinations and deceived by their plots, for Satan has shed the blood of many through this very strategy and continues to do so. There are so many calamities embedded within this that we have explained at length in our book *Ihya' 'Ulum ad-Din* (Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God). You may search for them there.

**Four Matters Worthy of Our Actions**

My dear beloved son! Refrain from the four things mentioned above. But there are also four things that you must actively attend to, and it will be fitting that you take full care of them.

**1. The Method of Developing an Intimate Relationship with Allah**

The first is that every matter between you and Allah should be conducted in such a way that, if your own servant were to do the same for you, you would not be saddened — rather, you would approve of it, commend it, and feel no anger over it. Likewise, whatever you would dislike your servant doing, realize that if you fall short in your own servitude to your Lord, your Creator will equally dislike it. The deeper truth to be grasped here is this: your servant was not created by you — you merely employed him. But you yourself were created, nurtured, and owned by your Real Creator and Lord.

<!-- PAGE 28 -->

You are His slave in the fullest sense, and therefore your obligation not to displease your Lord — in thought, word, or deed — is immeasurably greater.

**2. The Method of Relating to the Slaves of Allah**

The second is that every matter between you and other slaves of Allah should be conducted in such a way that, if they were to do the same to you, you would be pleased and not aggrieved. As it has been said in a sacred hadith (a tradition of the Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, in which Allah speaks directly):

*"Fala Yakmalu Eemanu 'Abdi Hatta Yuhibba Lisaa-irin Nasi Ma Yuhibba Li Nafsihi."*

"My slave's faith (Iman) is never complete until he loves for all human beings what he loves for himself."


3. AN INSISTENT ADVICE TO STUDY AND LEARN

The third thing is that if you want to increase your knowledge and wish to study scholarly works, then imagine that your lifetime will end in no more than a week. In that situation, consider carefully what kind of knowledge will truly benefit you, and devote yourself only to that. If you knew with certainty that your life would last no more than a week, you would never spend those final days studying books on religious debate and argumentation, lexicography, linguistics and grammar, poetry, poetic collections, or the art of composition. You would recognize that such knowledge could offer you no benefit. Instead, you would devote that entire week to contemplation of the heart and to recognizing the faults within your Nafs — your own self — so that you might make amends in whatever time remained. You would turn your face away from the world and its preoccupations, purify your heart of all evil qualities, adorn it with the love of Allah and with noble inner virtues, and give yourself wholly to worship and servitude before Him. It is even possible that during any day or night of that final week you would not visit anyone at all, even knowing that death might not come on that particular day or night.

My dear beloved son! Listen to one more thing: remember it, grasp its reality, reflect upon it deeply, and act upon it — for by doing so you will surely earn your salvation. If you were told that the king would visit your home next week, you would spend the entire week consumed with a single concern: that the king's gaze should not fall upon anything unseemly. You would clean and adorn everything — your body, your clothing, the walls and floors of your house. You would leave nothing untended. Now reflect and understand for yourself. What more must I explain by way of this hint? You are intelligent — a hint ought to suffice. This is why the Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, said:

*"InnAllaha La Yunzuru Ila Suwarikum Wa La Ila 'Aamalikum Wa Lakin Yunzuru Ila Qulubikum Wa Niyyatikum."*

"Verily, Allah does not look at your faces or your outward actions, but He looks at your hearts and your intentions."

<!-- page 29 -->

When the gaze of the Supreme Ruler — Ahkamul Hakimeen — rests upon your heart, why do you not purify it? If you desire to acquire knowledge of the matters of the heart, study my book *Ihya al-Uloom ad-Deen* — "Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God," also known as "Revival of Religious Learnings" — along with my other works, for such knowledge is personally obligatory, Fard al-'ayn, upon every Muslim. Other forms of knowledge carry the status of communal obligation, Fard Kifaya — an obligation upon the whole community, discharged when at least one group within it fulfills it. Yet even that knowledge should be acquired only to the extent necessary for obeying the commandments of Allah, the Exalted. If Allah, the Exalted, grants you the Tawfeeq — the capability and inner motivation — then you must necessarily pursue this knowledge.

4. NOT STOCKPILING FOOD

The fourth thing is that you should not accumulate more than one year's provision for your family from the goods of this world. The Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, set aside one year's worth of provisions for certain of his wives and said:

*"Allahumma Aj'al Quta Aale Muhammadin Kafafun."*

"O my Allah! Make this provision sufficient for the family of Muhammad, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him."

However, the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, did not set aside provisions for all of his wives, the Azwaj al-Mutahharat. He reserved a year's supply only for those among the Azwaj al-Mutahharat whose Tawakkul — reliance upon Allah — was weaker. For those among the Ummahatil Mu'mineen, the Mothers of the Believers, whose certainty in Allah was perfect and whose Tawakkul was strong, the Prophet, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, did not set aside even a single day's provisions. Among them was 'Aisha Siddiqa, may Allah be pleased with her, and others like her among the Mothers of the Believers.
