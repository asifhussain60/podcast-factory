For those whose hearts are already at rest, like Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, not even a single day is hoarded. The provision is left entirely to the unfolding of divine will. It demonstrates that the outer rule is always in service of the inner reality. The goal is a heart free from the anxiety of provision. If you need a year's supply to achieve that peace of mind, take it, but take no more. If you can achieve that peace with nothing in the cupboard, knowing God will provide tomorrow, you have reached the higher station. The disciple has now received the full diagnosis. But how does a human being actually sustain this immense discipline? The standard is so high, the audit is so rigorous. A person would break under the weight of this without divine help. The disciple asks the master for a supplication, a constant prayer to carry him through. The master gives him a vital overview of how to source his prayer life. He tells the disciple not to invent his own prayers from his own limited vocabulary. But to seek out supplications in the established traditions. Specifically, he directs him to the six most authenticated collections of the prophet's traditions. He also instructs him to seek the supplications that have reached us through the al-al-Bayt, the family of the prophet. The disciple's internal prayer life must be sourced from the profound established canon, not from his own fleeting emotions. But within that vast field of tradition, the master hands him one specific, meticulously crafted supplication. This is to be recited after every single obligatory prayer. When we look at the architecture of this prayer, it is built in three massive blocks. Let us walk through how it is constructed. The first block is a breathtaking request for absolute abundance. Listen to the vastness of what the disciple is told to ask for. my Allah, I seek from You the fullness and perfection of Your blessings. I seek the permanence of inward and outward purity. I seek the all-encompassing breadth of Your mercy. I seek the attainment of health. I seek increase in my sustenance. I seek happiness in my life. I seek true and lasting success in this life. I seek the completion of noble qualities. And it continues, asking for the most abundant reward, the sweetest plenitude, the most plentiful favor. He asks for the best of all deeds, the most beneficial of all knowledge, and the most wholesome of all sustenance. It is not a timid prayer. He is asking for the absolute pinnacle of everything, purity, mercy, health, knowledge. He is asking the Creator to open the treasuries of the heavens, but immediately the second block shifts the tone entirely. Having asked for ultimate abundance, The disciple now asks for intense correction and protection. Oh my Allah, be on our side, grant us benefit, and do not burden us, protect us from harm. The disciple asks for salvation at death, for the reformation of his deeds, and for safety in the days and nights. He asks God to extend mercy into his home and possessions. And crucially he prays. Cover our sins and faults with the veil of your forgiveness. Do us the favor of correcting all our shortcomings inward and outward and grant us confidence and reliance upon you. Correcting all our shortcomings inward and outward. That phrase perfectly mirrors the eight contours we just studied. He is asking God to do the work of purification that he cannot complete on his own. And then the final block of the prayer is a desperate plea for deliverance. It is a cry for liberation from the ultimate consequences of failure. Oh my Allah, grant us steadfastness and perseverance our religion and path to God. Save us from deeds in this world that would become a source of shame and humiliation on the day of judgment. Lighten the burden of our sins. Grant us the life of the pious. Be sufficient for us in all our affairs and protect us from the evil of the evildoers. Liberate us and our forebears from the fire of hell, from debt and from oppression. The progression is perfect. Abundance, correction, deliverance. And then, at the very peak of the supplication, the master has the disciple invoke God through 11 divine names. These are not random selections. Each one is a specific face of the divine mercy and majesty under which his entire letter has been written. The disciple invokes them in a solemn ascending rhythm.
