## Where this chapter picks up

The first chapter held open at a precise threshold. Stephen, the early master from Balkh, had asked his disciple Henry what he had drawn out of thirty-three years of his company; Henry had answered that he had distilled eight benefits and was certain that on those eight benefits his salvation rested in this life and the next. The chapter closed at that doorway. This chapter walks the eight one by one.

The architecture is no accident. The opening chapter diagnosed the disease — *knowledge without action is madness, and action without knowledge is worthless* — through the witness of the Prophet, the post-mortem report of Jonathan on the cycles of the last-third night vigil, the Quranic chain on striving, the architecture of submission and faith, the hinge on intention, and Samuel's lemma of sufficiency. The diagnosis was complete. The cure begins here.

## The first benefit — the only beloved who descends into the grave

Henry speaks. *O my teacher!* he begins — the form of address that will be the spine of the dialogue. *The first benefit is that I looked at the people of this world and saw that everyone has a beloved.* Some have a spouse, a child, wealth, friends, rank. But Henry looked closely at the *limit* of those loves. The wife and the child accompany the lover until illness; some friends until death; some accompany the body until burial. *But after burial, all the loved ones return.* None descend into the grave to offer company in the *darkness, solitude, narrowness, and terror* of that place.

Who, then, is the only true beloved? One who accompanies a person into the grave, who dwells with the lover there, who offers company, who illuminates the grave, and who is a partner on the Day of Judgment and through all the stations one passes through after burial until that Day. He arrives at the single beloved who survives the burial. *One's own righteous deeds.*

The shape of the benefit is the shape that all eight will share. Observation of the world; identification of a hidden limit; a Quranic or Prophetic anchor; a decision of the will. *After reaching this awareness and determination,* Henry says, *I made my righteous deeds my beloved, so that they will accompany me into my grave when I die, will offer me their company there, will become an illuminated candle within my grave, will be my partner through every stage of the life hereafter, and will never separate from me.*

Stephen ratifies. *Bravo, O Henry! You have said a most excellent thing.* The ratification — short, exact, without ornament — sets the rhythm. Each benefit is told, each is ratified, the next is requested. The eight will not be argued; they will be *witnessed* and *received*.

## The second benefit — and a verdict on the lower self

*O my teacher!* Henry continues. *The second benefit is that I glanced at the people of the world and saw that everyone was running after the pleasures and desires of the lower self — the inward self that incites one toward evil — and that everyone was subservient to its desires.* The diagnosis is precise. The world is full of people who are not enjoying their pleasures freely; they are being *driven* by their pleasures. The lower self is in command and they are in service. Henry reflects on this until a verse from the Quran arrives in answer:

> But as for the one who has stood in awe of his Sustainer's Presence and restrained his inner self from base and evil desires, Paradise will be the place of rest.
> — Quran, chapter on those who pull out the souls, verses 40 and 41

The verse names two operations in sequence — awe of the Lord's Presence and restraint of the lower self — and joins them to a single result. *I developed the certitude,* Henry says, *that the Wise Quran is correct and that the Book of God is the truth. I then took a firm stand against my lower self and became resolved to oppose it. I disciplined it to such a degree that I refused to fulfil any of its desires, until at last it began to find peace and satisfaction in the worship of God, the Exalted.*

The last clause is the pivot. The discipline of the lower self does not end in permanent conflict; it ends in *the lower self itself becoming pleased with the worship of God*. The benefit is not the war; it is the peace that the war produces.

The same teaching is carried through our tradition in the form of a saying preserved from the Father of Imams, the first Imam in the canonical lineage:

> The lower self is like a mount: if you neglect it, it carries you to ruin; if you discipline it, it carries you to the goal.
> — The Father of Imams, in the classical compilations of his sermons and aphorisms

The image is the same image Henry arrives at by observation. The lower self is not to be killed; it is to be *trained until it carries you to where you intend to go*.

Stephen ratifies again. *May God, the Exalted, grant His blessings in your good deeds. Now narrate the third benefit.*

## The third benefit — possessions deposited with God

*O my teacher!* Henry continues. *The third benefit is that when I glanced at the people I saw that every person, through great pain and hard labour, was busy accumulating the possessions — money, wealth, and goods — of this mortal world, and was greatly pleased to have amassed much of it.* The observation has a precise shape. People are not merely acquiring; they are *taking pleasure* in the act of acquiring. The accumulation is the goal, not the means.

The Quranic verdict on this picture arrives:

> All that is with you is bound to come to an end, whereas all that which is with God is everlasting.
> — Quran, chapter on the bee, verse 96

The verse names a contrast that is not psychological but ontological. *What is with you* ends; *what is with God* does not end. The two classes are distinguished by their location, not by their type. The same coin, possessed by the servant, perishes; given in the Way of God, does not perish. *Then whatever material possessions I had accumulated in this world, I spent them entirely in the Way of God by distributing them among pious indigents and the needy, so that those possessions would be deposited with God, the Exalted, as my trust, and would become a source of immense good and ransom — that is, salvation — in the life hereafter.*

The image is the image of a deposit. The lover of wealth is not asked to *love it less* but to *redirect the love to the place where its object will survive*. Stephen ratifies. *O Henry! May God grant you His reward for your spending in His Way. You have said an excellent thing and done an excellent thing.*

## The fourth benefit — what counts as honor in God's sight

*O my teacher!* Henry continues. *The fourth benefit is that I looked at the people of the world and saw that some of them believe grandeur and honour belong to the nation or tribe that is large and powerful, and so they take pride in their tribe. Some believe that grandeur comes from an abundance of wealth, material possessions, family, and relatives, and so they boast of their riches and children. Some believe that their grandeur and honour lie in displaying anger, in beating, killing, murder, and pillage. Some believe that their grandeur lies in wasteful spending, and so they regard such extravagance as a source of honour and boast of it.*

The catalogue names the four registers in which the world locates honor — tribe, wealth, violence, conspicuous waste — and shows each as a *misreading* of where honor actually lies. The Quranic verdict overturns all four:

> Verily, the noblest of you in the sight of God is the one who is most deeply conscious — that is, most reverent — of Him.
> — Quran, chapter on the inner chambers, verse 13

The verse names a single coordinate of nobility and locates it in a single place — the sight of God. *This alone is correct; this alone is the truth,* Henry says. *The thoughts of the people of the world are false conjectures and nothing more. Therefore I adopted pious reverence — consciousness, awe, and reverence of God — so that I might be counted among those of the highest rank in the sight of God, the Exalted.*

The benefit is not the dismissal of honor but its *relocation* — from the eye of the tribe to the sight of the Lord. Stephen ratifies. *O Henry! May God be pleased with you. You have said a most excellent thing.*

## The fifth benefit — the distribution is already made

*O my teacher!* Henry continues. *The fifth benefit is that I looked at the people who were complaining against one another. I found that they were doing this on account of envy, jealousy, and enmity, and that the root cause of all of it was competition over greatness, grandeur, material possessions, wealth, and knowledge.* The named grievances are the *surface* of social conflict. Underneath is competition; underneath competition is envy.

The Quranic verdict cuts the root:

> It is We who distribute their means of livelihood — their sustenance — among them in the life of this world.
> — Quran, chapter on the gold ornaments, verse 32

The verse names the agent, the object, and the finality of the distribution. *I then reflected,* Henry says, *that God, the Blessed and Exalted, has appointed all possessions and ranks from the very beginning of creation, and that no one has any control or say in this matter. Therefore I ceased to compete with or harbour jealousy toward anyone, and I remained content with God's distribution of worldly bounties and with His appointed destiny, living in peace with all of creation by surrendering every jealousy and enmity against the creatures of God.* To envy another for what one does not have is to quarrel with the Distributor. The peace is not earned by changing the world but by recognizing its Owner.

The same teaching is preserved in another saying of the Father of Imams, where contentment is named as the inward correlate of the recognition Henry has reached:

> Contentment is a wealth that does not run out, and envy is a fire that consumes its own kindling before it reaches another.
> — The Father of Imams, in the classical compilations of his aphorisms

Stephen ratifies. *O Henry! You speak the truth and you are doing what is right.*

## The sixth benefit — Satan alone as the enemy

*O my teacher!* Henry continues. *The sixth benefit is that when I glanced at the people I saw that every person holds enmity toward someone else for some reason.* Enmity attaches itself to almost every life. The Quranic verdict redirects it:

> Verily, Satan is your enemy, so treat him as your enemy.
> — Quran, chapter on the originator, verse 6

The verse does not *forbid* enmity; it *targets* enmity. The enemy is Satan. *I developed the certitude that God's words are the truth, and that I should hold no enmity toward any person. From that time forward, I regarded Satan alone as my enemy and refused to obey any of his promptings. Instead, I cultivated obedience to God, followed the commandments of God, the Exalted, and devoted myself entirely to His worship and servitude.* The benefit is sealed with the Quranic naming of the path that opens once the enmity is correctly targeted:

> Did I not make this covenant with you, O Children of Adam — that you should never obey Satan, for he is your avowed and open enemy — and that you should worship Me alone? This is the straight path.
> — Quran, chapter on those who pull out the cords, verses 60 and 61

This is the straight path of guidance in which there is no crookedness: withdraw enmity from where it does not belong, attach it where it does, and the path opens. Stephen ratifies. *O Henry! You have done an excellent thing and said an excellent thing.*

## The seventh benefit — sustenance is the responsibility of God

*O my teacher!* Henry continues. *The seventh benefit is that I looked at the people and saw that every person is preoccupied with his or her livelihood and the search for sustenance, consumed by intense effort in that pursuit. In this race, many do not even distinguish between what is permissible and what is forbidden, and instead degrade themselves by pursuing doubtful and forbidden earnings.* The pursuit itself is not the problem; its *intensity* collapses the distinctions on which the path to God rests. The Quranic verdict removes the foundation of the rush:

> And there is no living creature on earth whose sustenance is not the responsibility of God.
> — Quran, chapter on the prophet Hud, verse 6

The verse is exhaustive — *no living creature on earth* — and locative — *the responsibility of God*. The creature's task is not to *manufacture* provision but to *receive* it. *I then developed the certitude,* Henry says, *that the Wise Quran is true and correct, and that I am also among those living creatures of God present upon the earth. Therefore I devoted myself to the worship of God and held firm to the certitude that He would bring my sustenance to me, for He has promised to provide it.* The benefit is not the abandonment of work but its *order* — worship first; sustenance through God's appointed channels; no transgression of the permissible line under the rush of provision-seeking. Stephen ratifies. *You have done an excellent thing and said an excellent thing.*

## The eighth benefit — complete reliance on God

*O my teacher!* Henry continues. *The eighth benefit is that when I looked at the people I found that every person's reliance and trust rests on something or someone. Some place their trust in their material possessions, and some rely upon other people.* The first seven benefits each named a particular operation; the eighth is the summary operation — on whom does the heart *finally* rest?

Two verses arrive in answer:

> And for everyone who places his or her trust in God, He alone is sufficient.
> — Quran, chapter on divorce, verse 3

> And He is sufficient for me, and He is the best of Helpers.
> — Quran, chapter on the family of Imran, verse 173

The first verse states the principle; the second is the servant's response. *Therefore,* Henry says, *I adopted complete reliance upon God in every matter.* The seven prior benefits each named an object of misplaced reliance and corrected it to its proper object. The eighth states the correction in its pure form: *He alone is sufficient.*

## The four heavenly Books

Stephen hears the eight benefits and gives the closing verdict. *O Henry! May God grant you His inner enablement — that is, may He enable you to perform righteous deeds and to walk firmly upon His Path. You have conveyed most excellent things. I have seen in the Torah revealed to Moses, in the Gospel revealed to Jesus, in the Psalms revealed to David, and in the praiseworthy differentiator between right and wrong — the Quran — that all four of these Books of God speak of these very eight benefits. Whoever acts upon these eight principles is as one who has acted upon all four holy Books.*

The eight are not Henry's discovery; they are the eight registers of guidance the four heavenly Books teach. A disciple who acts on these eight has acted on every Book that has come from God. You now know from these traditions that you do not need a great quantity of knowledge.

## The fifth condition — a perfected spiritual guide

I now return to the seven conditions I had begun to enumerate before the Henry narrative. The first four conditions had been walked in the opening chapter (pious reverence, sincere repentance, the settling of the rights of creatures, and sufficient knowledge of the Divine Law). The fifth condition now follows. The fifth condition that is obligatory upon you, the seeker on the Path of God, is that you should have a perfected spiritual guide who leads you, removes evil qualities and morals from within you, and replaces them with noble ones.

The work of the guide is like that of a farmer tending a crop: when he sees unnecessary weeds that have sprung up among the grain, he pulls them out and throws them away. Then he waters the crop and nourishes it so that it grows well and develops good quality. Evil qualities are uprooted; noble qualities are watered; the crop is you.

At every stage of the journey, the traveler on the Path of God has no remedy or solution except through a true and perfected spiritual guide. For God sent the Prophet Muhammad to His servants so that he would serve as an illuminated guide on the Way of God and bring people to that Way. When the Messenger of God departed from this world, he appointed his deputies and successors in his place, so that they would serve as proofs of the Way of God until the Day of Judgment. Your guide is an illuminated proof and deputy of the Prophet — a link in a chain that goes back to the Messenger himself.

## The qualities of a true guide

Not every scholar can be a perfected spiritual guide. For this work of guiding people spiritually — purifying their souls and helping them advance on the Way of God — only a person possessing certain specific qualities is truly qualified. I will briefly enumerate some of these qualities here, so that no foolish or misguided person may falsely claim to be a guide, for such impostors are many, and their sole purpose is to deceive people for material gain, causing great harm to this vital dimension of the faith and leading many astray.

The true guide must have turned away from love of the world, love of honor, and love of worldly rank; must have taken a solemn covenant with a perfected spiritual guide, who in turn has taken that covenant with his own perfected spiritual guide, and so on, such that this spiritual chain extends all the way back to the Messenger; must have undergone all manner of spiritual struggles and disciplines; must have obeyed every commandment of the Prophet; must eat little, sleep little, pray abundantly, fast frequently, and give generously in the Way of God; must possess all praiseworthy moral qualities — patience, gratitude, certitude, generosity, contentment, trustworthiness, tolerance, humility, truthfulness, modesty, dignity, tranquility; must have received a light from among the lights of the Messenger; and must have had all evil qualities — miserliness, jealousy, enmity, worldly expectation, anger, and rebellion — purged from within.

A guide whose life cannot be measured against this catalogue is a guide by claim only. Following such a guide is not the correct way; following one who measures against the catalogue is. I work this same test at greater length in my major work *Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God*, in the volume on the manners of the disciple, where the same catalogue is unfolded into a full taxonomy of false claimants and true guides.

## The etiquettes of the disciple — outward and inward

Such spiritual guides are exceedingly difficult to find. But if you have been granted this wealth and blessing — that you have found such a guide, and that guide has admitted you among his disciples — then it is necessary for you to maintain both outward and inward respect, honor, and proper etiquette toward your spiritual guide.

The outward etiquettes. You should not argue with your guide, and if an argument arises and you think your guide has overlooked something, you should not raise an objection. You should not occupy the place of prayer permanently; rather, when the time for prayer arrives, you should go to the place of prayer, perform the prayer, and then roll up your prayer mat and leave. You should not perform lengthy supererogatory prayers in front of your guide. Whatever the perfected guide commands, you should obey to the best of your ability.

The doctrinal safeguard: a true spiritual guide cannot and will not command a disciple to do anything forbidden by the Divine Law. False impostors who lack the aforementioned qualities, by contrast, may command their disciples to do things that are forbidden. The catalogue of qualities is both the *test* by which the true guide is recognized and the *guarantee* that he will not command what is forbidden.

The inward etiquettes. Whatever you hear outwardly from your guide, you should not entertain even the slightest doubt in your heart regarding his words or deeds — otherwise you will be counted among the hypocrites. If you cannot maintain this inner sincerity, then you should withdraw from the company of your spiritual guide until your inward state comes into harmony with your outward. The remedy is not to force the inward state but to *withdraw* until inward matches outward. The guide is not lost; the relationship is paused.

## The sixth and seventh conditions — guarding against the lower self; humility and contentment

Now I will complete the seven conditions. The sixth condition is that you guard against the deceptions of the lower self: an inward, subtle presence invisible to the outward eye yet clearly perceptible to one possessing inner sight, which commands a person to evil by whispering into the heart. This is only possible if you abandon the gatherings of the ill-charactered and the ignorant. In this way, the dominion of Satan over your heart will come to an end, and all Satanic influences will be uprooted entirely. Satan operates across two registers — among humankind or from among the invisible creation of God made from fire — and works through the company you keep; the discipline is to refuse that company.

The seventh condition is that in every circumstance, you should prefer humility and freedom from greed over wealth, and should embrace contentment and satisfaction with the Will of God. These seven conditions are essential for every traveler on the Path of God, and for every seeker of truth.

## The reality of the inward purification science

Now I turn to the doctrinal definitions you requested. The first is the inward purification science — what later tradition will call *the science of the heart*. The inward purification science is defined by two qualities. The first is that the servant should be obedient to God — that is, acting in accordance with the Divine Law, the outward laws of the faith encompassing obligations, commandments, and prohibitions. The second is that the seeker should be compassionate and a doer of good toward all of God's creation, which is not possible without first purifying one's soul of all evil qualities and then adorning it with all noble ones.

The inward purification science is not *only* obedience to the Divine Law nor *only* compassion toward creation; it is the joining of the two, and the joining cannot occur without the purification that precedes it. One who perseveres in acting upon the Divine Law and who genuinely desires the betterment and true success of all humanity is rightly called a master of the inward sciences — and this is the correct application of the term, as opposed to the distorted usages so widespread today. The label is not a costume; it is the *consequence* of a particular kind of life.

Loyalty and obedience to God means that you sacrifice personal happiness for the sake of God. Sincerity toward all people means that you do not maintain relationships for personal motives, but are selfless and perform selfless service, free from self-interest. Instead, you dedicate yourself to the welfare of all people — though the condition is that this welfare must be in accordance with the Divine Law. Service is bounded by the Divine Law; service in violation of it is not service.

## The reality of servitude

The second definition is the nature of servitude — what it means to be a *slave* of God. Servitude consists of three things. First is obedience to the commandments of the Divine Law. Second is contentment with the decree and destiny appointed by God. Third is the abandonment of your own desires and claims, and the acceptance of and happiness with the Will and Power of God. The triple moves from outward act to inward reception to inward transformation.

## The reality of reliance on God

The third definition is reliance on God. Reliance on God means holding firm belief and certitude in what God has promised. In other words, you believe with conviction that whatever God has decreed for you, you will surely receive — and if the entire world were to conspire to prevent it, it could not be prevented. But for that which God has not written in your destiny, no amount of effort by you or by the entire world combined will ever bring it to you.

Reliance on God is the inward face of the outward decision Henry made when he stopped competing for sustenance. The decree is fixed; the world is powerless against it. The servant who has reliance on God lives in the precise space the decree defines.

## The reality of sincerity

The fourth definition is sincerity to God. Sincerity means that all of your deeds are exclusively for the sake of God and His pleasure. Whatever you do should not be for show or ostentation — performance done for the gaze of others. When you perform good deeds, your heart should not lean toward impressing people or seeking their praise. Your heart should neither be gladdened by people's admiration nor grieved by their criticism.

Showing off is born from the praises and honors of people. The cure for the spiritual disease of showing off is to regard the entire universe as subject to the Power and Control of God, and to consider all created things as equivalent to pebbles and stones — understanding that stones possess no power to bring you either grief or comfort. If you truly regard all creation in this light, you will be freed from showing off. As long as you believe that mortal created beings have the power to cause you grief or bring you comfort, showing off will never depart from your heart. The cure is not to suppress the showing off but to dissolve the foundation that makes it possible.

## The transition — and the counsel of the wise companion of Moses

Your remaining questions are such that some have been addressed in books I have written, which you may consult, while others are of a nature that writing their answers is not permissible. So take what is written here and steadily put it into practice — and through that practice, those matters which you do not yet understand will become clear to you.

The Quran itself names patience as the right posture of the questioner:

> Had those people exercised patience — instead of clamoring outside your house — until you came out of your house yourself, it would have been better for them.
> — Quran, chapter on the inner chambers, verse 5

And the wise companion of Moses gives the counsel I now direct to you:

> If you wish to follow me, then do not question me about anything until I myself bring it up and explain it to you.
> — Quran, chapter on the cave, verse 70

And God Himself names the time-rule for understanding:

> We will soon show you Our signs, so do not seek to hasten this matter.
> — Quran, chapter on the prophets, verse 37

You are not refused; you are *paced*. The teaching will come at its proper time, through practice, and the matters that are *not permissible to write* will become clear through that practice, not through further questioning. Do not ask before the right time arrives. When you reach that spiritually elevated stage, you will see it yourself. Therefore, hold firm to this certainty: unless you set out toward that destination, you will neither arrive there nor be able to see what awaits.

Have you not traveled the earth so that you might see?
— Quran, chapter on the family of Imran, verse 137

An early Egyptian master of the inward sciences said it best:

> If you have the courage to exert your utmost with your soul in this way, then come and step onto this path — otherwise do not merely occupy yourself with the beautiful sayings of the inner-sciences tradition.
> — An early Egyptian master of the inward sciences, in the canonical biographies of the inner-sciences tradition

The vocabulary of the inner sciences is not their substance; the substance is *the exertion of the soul*. If you have the courage to exert your soul, you are welcome on the path. If you have only the appetite for the vocabulary, you should leave the vocabulary alone.

## Eight admonitions — four things to refrain from

My dear beloved son! I will now bring my explanation to a close with eight admonitions. Of these eight admonitions, there are four things you must do and four things you must not do — so that your knowledge does not turn against you on the Day of Judgment. Let us begin with the four things you should not do.

### The first admonition — the principle of debate

As far as possible, do not engage in religious debates with everyone, and do not argue with anyone on any matter — religious or otherwise — for there are many hidden harms within such disputes, and the harms far outweigh any benefits. Debate and argument are the fountainhead of all manner of evils: ostentation and show, jealousy, arrogance, resentment, enmity, pride, and egotism.

There is one exception. If an argument arises between you and another person over some issue, and your genuine desire is for the truth to prevail, then your intention to engage in that discussion may be considered sound. The two signs of right intention: whether the truth emerges from your own tongue or from the tongue of your opponent, it makes no difference to you; and you consider it preferable to discuss the matter in private, away from an audience. These two signs test for sincerity. But if you are in a dispute and you are certain that you are correct and your opponent is arguing merely for the sake of arguing, then be on guard — do not continue the argument; end the discussion at that point. Otherwise, grudges and resentments will take root and no benefit whatsoever will come of it.

Think of it this way. Asking questions about religious and spiritual matters is like describing the diseases of the heart and their causes before a physician of the heart. Ignorant people are like patients afflicted with sickness of the heart, while scholars are like physicians and healers. Imperfect scholars are incapable of curing these diseases; a perfected scholar can cure them.

But there are four kinds of patient, and only one is curable. The first is one who asks questions out of jealousy or raises objections from envy. Jealousy is a lethal disease that has no cure. Whatever answer you give, no matter how sound, such a person will regard you as an enemy. Do not respond. As the poet said:

> Every kind of animosity can be remedied — except the animosity that is born of jealousy.
> — Classical Arabic poetry, preserved in the literary collections of the early scholarly tradition

The Quran seals the rule:

> So turn away from the one who, out of jealousy, turns his face from Our remembrance and desires nothing but the pleasures of the life of this world.
> — Quran, chapter on the star, verse 29

The second kind is one whose disease arises from a lack of intelligence and foolishness. This disease is likewise incurable. Jesus said that he was — with the permission of God — not incapable of restoring the dead to life, yet he was incapable of curing the fool and the ignorant. The ignorant fool is the one who has spent only a brief time acquiring knowledge, has not even begun to grasp intellectual and transmitted religious learning, and yet raises objections against scholars who have devoted their entire lives to those very disciplines. The signature of the fool is not ignorance itself but the *objecting* posture from inside ignorance.

The third kind is one who, owing to restlessness and impatience, cannot absorb the teachings of the spiritually elevated, and who relies instead on their own deficient understanding, interpreting everything only according to their personal interest. The Prophet names the rule for this:

> We, the community of prophets, have been commanded by God to speak to people according to the capacity of their minds.
> — The Prophet, in the canonical hadith collections

You do not unburden yourself of *what you know*; you give the student *what the student's mind can carry*.

The fourth kind is the one who can be cured. This is the patient who is genuinely seeking the right path of guidance — obedient, sharp, and intelligent — and who is free from anger, the following of base desires of the lower self, jealousy, and the craving for wealth and fame. Whatever question this person asks, or whatever objection they raise, arises not from jealousy, fault-finding, or the desire to test, but from an earnest search for truth and correct guidance. This is the patient who can be cured. To answer the questions of such a person is therefore not merely permissible — it is obligatory.

Your task is to recognize the kind of patient you are sitting with and to spend your time only on the one who can be healed. Spending time on the other three is not generosity; it is a failure of triage.

### The second admonition — the reality of preaching

The second restraint is on preaching. You are admonished to refrain from preaching and making speeches, for great calamities and harms lie within it. If you believe that whatever you preach you have already acted upon yourself, then bear in mind that God said to Jesus:

> O Son of Mary! First admonish your own self; then, if it accepts your counsel, admonish others — otherwise have shame before Me.
> — A sacred tradition in which God speaks directly, addressed to Jesus, preserved in the early collections

The right to preach is *earned* by having admonished your own lower self first. If circumstances arise that compel you to preach, then guard against two things.

First, against rhetorical performance. In your sermon avoid colorful narrations, theatrical gestures, secretive language, flowery and embellished speech, pleasing poetry, any talk that transgresses the bounds of the Divine Law, and the fabrications of false claimants to the inner sciences. God does not love those who are pretentious and showy. The purpose of a sermon is not to display your abilities; it is to speak of the punishment of the Hereafter. It is to speak of your own shortcomings, your regret over wasted time, the difficult stages of the life to come, how to keep your faith intact, the appearance of the Angel of Death, the questioning in the grave by the two questioning angels, the plain of assembly on the Day of Resurrection, the weighing of deeds in the great scale, and the bridge over Hell, thinner than a hair and sharper than a sword. A sermon is not a performance; it is a warning.

Consider this: a person's house is struck by a flood — water reaching the very door, threatening within seconds to engulf the house and drown everyone inside. In that moment, the owner of the house will cry out urgently: *Beware! Beware! Run! The floodwater is here!* At such a moment of imminent catastrophe, no one would describe the flood in ornate sentences. A sermon should be precisely like this — rooted in genuine fear of your own reckoning, and conveying that same urgency to others.

Second, against the desire for applause. While delivering sermons, you must never allow the thought to enter your heart that after listening, people will shout praises, fall into ecstasy, tear their clothes, raise a clamor in the gathering, and declare that it was a wonderful sermon. Such thoughts are pure ostentation. Your true intention should be to summon God's creation from this world toward the next; from sin toward servitude of God; from greed toward detachment; from miserliness toward generosity; from ostentation toward sincerity; from arrogance toward humility; from heedlessness toward God-consciousness; and from pride toward piety. The sermon's work is a series of *transfers* — the listener is moved from a deficient register to a richer one. The sermon that fails to perform these transfers is the sermon Satan preaches. Such people spread a corruption in religion that even Satan himself does not spread. Whoever has the authority to remove such a preacher from the pulpit, it is obligatory upon him to do so, so that they cannot distort the meaning of commanding the right and forbidding the evil.

### The third admonition — staying away from the affluent and rulers

The third restraint is on the company of the affluent and rulers. You should not even greet any king, any person of wealth, or any ruler. Stay away from their company and gatherings, and beyond that, do not so much as lift your eyes to look at them. For in looking upon them and keeping their company lie many hidden calamities. But if you ever happen to find yourself in their company by chance, refrain from praising them. The Prophet said:

> Verily, God, the Exalted, is angered when any transgressor or oppressor is praised; and whoever prays for a long life for an oppressor has, in effect, expressed a desire that sin be perpetuated upon the earth.
> — The Prophet, in the canonical hadith collections

To praise the transgressor is to anger God; to pray for his long life is to pray that his transgression continue upon the earth.

### The fourth admonition — not accepting gifts from rulers

The fourth restraint is on accepting gifts from rulers. You should not accept any gifts from rulers, even if you are certain that what they offer comes from lawful sources. The reason is that harboring any desire for their possessions causes great damage and corruption in the path to God. The favors and rewards that come from rulers give rise to oppression, transgression, and sin, all of which damage the path. The minimum harm is this: you will begin to love the oppressors, and when a person loves another, they pray for that person's long life. If an oppressor lives long, oppression endures longer, and there is greater turmoil and corruption in the world — and what could be worse than that?

Beware! Satan will try to misguide you by planting this thought in your heart: "First take the money from these rulers, then distribute it among the poor, bring them comfort, and fulfill their needs." Beware — never accept this kind of counsel from any Satan, whether from among humankind or from among the invisible creation of God. He has shed the blood of many through this very strategy. There are so many calamities embedded within this that I have explained at length in my book *Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God*. You may search for them there.

## The four things to do

Refrain from the four things mentioned above. But there are also four things that you must actively attend to, and it will be fitting that you take full care of them.

### The first — the method of developing an intimate relationship with God

The first is that every matter between you and God should be conducted in such a way that, if your own servant were to do the same for you, you would not be saddened — rather, you would approve of it, commend it, and feel no anger over it. Likewise, whatever you would dislike your servant doing, realize that if you fall short in your own servitude to your Lord, your Creator will equally dislike it.

The deeper truth to be grasped here is this: your servant was not created by you — you merely employed him. But you yourself were created, nurtured, and owned by your Real Creator and Lord. You are His slave in the fullest sense, and therefore your obligation not to displease your Lord — in thought, word, or deed — is immeasurably greater.

### The second — the method of relating to the slaves of God

The second is that every matter between you and other slaves of God should be conducted in such a way that, if they were to do the same to you, you would be pleased and not aggrieved. The rule is the parallel of the first rule, now for horizontal relations. As it has been said in a sacred tradition in which God speaks directly:

> My slave's faith is never complete until he loves for all human beings what he loves for himself.
> — A sacred tradition, in the canonical collections

The completion of faith is conditional on a precise inward movement — *to love for all human beings what he loves for himself.* You now have, in two rules, the entire architecture of the two relations that compose a servant's life: the vertical with the Lord, the horizontal with the Lord's creation.

### The third — the rule of studying as if you had only a week to live

The third thing is that if you want to increase your knowledge and wish to study scholarly works, then imagine that your lifetime will end in no more than a week. In that situation, consider carefully what kind of knowledge will truly benefit you, and devote yourself only to that. If you knew with certainty that your life would last no more than a week, you would never spend those final days studying books on religious debate and argumentation, lexicography, linguistics and grammar, poetry, poetic collections, or the art of composition. You would recognize that such knowledge could offer you no benefit. Instead, you would devote that entire week to contemplation of the heart and to recognizing the faults within your own self — so that you might make amends in whatever time remained.

This is my most economical answer to your original question. *Which* knowledge benefits on the Day of Judgment? The knowledge you would study *if you knew you had a week to live*. Everything you would *not* study is, by that test, knowledge you do not need.

Imagine you were told that the king would visit your home next week. You would spend the entire week consumed with a single concern: that the king's gaze should not fall upon anything unseemly. You would clean and adorn everything — your body, your clothing, the walls and floors of your house. You would leave nothing untended. The Prophet said:

> Verily, God does not look at your faces or your outward actions, but He looks at your hearts and your intentions.
> — The Prophet, in the canonical hadith collections

When the gaze of the Supreme Ruler rests upon your heart, why do you not purify it? If you desire to acquire knowledge of the matters of the heart, study my book *Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God* along with my other works, for such knowledge is personally obligatory upon every follower of the faith. Other forms of knowledge carry the status of communal obligation — an obligation upon the whole community, discharged when at least one group within it fulfills it. Yet even that knowledge should be acquired only to the extent necessary for obeying the commandments of God. Your life is to be filled, first and almost only, with the personally obligatory.

### The fourth — not stockpiling food

The fourth thing is that you should not accumulate more than one year's provision for your family from the goods of this world. The Prophet himself set aside one year's worth of provisions for certain of his wives and said:

> O my God! Make this provision sufficient for the family of Muhammad.
> — The Prophet, in the canonical hadith collections

He did not set aside provisions for all of his wives. He reserved a year's supply only for those whose reliance upon God was weaker. For those among the Mothers of the Believers whose certainty in God was perfect, the Prophet did not set aside even a single day's provisions. Among them was the youngest of the Mothers of the Believers. One year is the *maximum* for a household whose reliance on God is not yet perfect; the *minimum* — for those whose reliance is perfect — is *not a single day*. Your task is to live truthfully between the two.

## A supplication that may always be recited

My dear beloved son! In this book I have answered all of your questions. Now gather your resolve, act upon everything within it, and do not forget me in your supplications. You also asked for a supplication that you might recite at all times. Seek out supplications in the books of the prophetic traditions, particularly in the six canonical hadith collections, and commit them to memory. Similarly, many supplications have reached us through the family of the Prophet; seek them out there as well. I will give you one particular supplication to recite after every obligatory prayer:

> O my God! I seek from You the fullness and perfection of Your blessings; I seek the permanence of inward and outward purity; I seek the all-encompassing breadth of Your mercy; I seek the attainment of health; I seek increase in my sustenance; I seek happiness in my life; I seek true and lasting success in this life; I seek the completion of noble qualities; I seek from Your rewards the most abundant reward; I seek from Your plenitude the sweetest plenitude; I seek from Your favors the most plentiful favor; I seek from all deeds the deed that is best; I seek from all knowledge the knowledge that is most beneficial; and I seek from all sustenance the sustenance that is most wholesome.

> O my God! Be on our side — grant us benefit — and do not burden us — protect us from harm. O my God! Grant us salvation at our death; reform our deeds; grant us safety in Your days and nights; extend Your mercy to us in our homes and in our possessions; cover our sins and faults with the veil of Your forgiveness; do us the favor of correcting all our shortcomings, inward and outward; and grant us confidence and reliance upon You.

> O my God! Grant us steadfastness and perseverance in our path. Save us from deeds in this world that would become a source of shame and humiliation on the Day of Judgment. Lighten the burden of our sins; grant us the life of the pious; be sufficient for us in all our affairs; and protect us from the evil of the evildoers. Liberate us and our forebears from the Fire of Hell, from debt, and from oppression. O You the Victorious One! O You the Forgiving One! O You the Generous One! O You the Coverer of Faults! O You the Forbearing One! O You the Compeller! O You the Magnificent One! O You Who Subdues all things! O God! O God! O God! O You the Compassionate One in this world! O You the Merciful One in the life hereafter! O You the Greatest of the Merciful! You are the Granter of boundless mercy. And may the mercy and blessings of God descend always upon Muhammad, the finest of all creation from eternity to eternity, and upon his descendants, and upon his companions. All praise belongs solely to God, the Lord of all the Universes.

This book comes to an end with the help of the Absolute Sovereign, and the Giver of all.
