# The Disciple's Letter and the Reply

My dear beloved son and true friend, may God grant you a long life in His service, and may He guide you along the path of those He loves.

You should know that the source of all good counsel is the life of the Prophet himself. Every piece of advice that matters rests on his traditions, on his conduct. Any counsel that contradicts them is a waste of your time. Countless words of advice have been written in the light of his life. If you have already found guidance in them, you have no need for any counsel from me.

But if you haven't—and this is the question I must ask you—then tell me: in all these years you have spent with me, what knowledge have you truly acquired?

## The Prophet's two warnings — preoccupation and the forty-year threshold

My son, the Prophet warned us with a sign we can all see. He said, “A person’s preoccupation with useless matters is a sign that God has withdrawn His special favor from them.” Think about that. Every moment you spend on something other than what you were created for is a cause for profound sorrow. And if you have passed the age of forty, he gave a second warning: “An individual whose good does not prevail over their evil after forty years should prepare to go to Hell.”

It is easy to give this advice. It is another thing entirely to live it. For the person whose heart is captured by the pleasures of this world, these words taste bitter. And the hardest case of all—the one I fear for you—is the person who gathers wisdom, philosophy, and worldly learning, and then imagines this knowledge is a golden ticket. He thinks it will save him in the next life while bringing him fame and status in this one. He doesn't even realize that the very knowledge he has collected, if he doesn't act on it, will stand as a witness against him. The Prophet was clear: “On the Day of Judgment, the most severe torment will be for the scholar whom God did not benefit through his knowledge.” The scholar who did not act on what he knew.

## Jonathan in the dream — what availed at the end

Listen. One of the saints saw the great master Jonathan in a dream after he had died. “Tell me,” the saint asked, “what happened to you?”

Jonathan’s reply should stop your heart. He said, “All my discourses, all my spiritual signs, all the beautiful words—they turned out to be useless. But those few cycles of prayer I performed in the deep of the night, when the world was silent—those were of benefit.”

Do you see? This was Jonathan, a master of spiritual discourse. And he tells us from beyond the grave that the words did not save him. The *prayers* saved him. The action.

So do not deprive yourself of good deeds. Do not neglect the clear commands of the Divine Law. And do not leave your heart empty of noble qualities. You must be absolutely convinced of this: knowledge alone, without righteous deeds, will not help you on the Day of Judgment.

## The warrior with the sharp swords; the sick man with the medicine

Imagine a warrior, a master of combat, walking through the desert. He carries ten sharp swords, the finest bows, every weapon you can imagine. He knows exactly how to use them. And then a lion attacks. What good is his knowledge if he doesn't draw a sword? Will his expertise save him? Of course not. In the same way, if you memorize a hundred thousand points of law but act on none of them, that knowledge will be of no benefit to you.

Or imagine a sick man. He has a precise diagnosis and a cabinet full of the exact medicines that will cure him. He knows their properties, their dosages, everything. But he never takes them. Will his knowledge make him well? It’s obvious, isn’t it? Just knowing the remedy for your spiritual diseases cannot cure you. A poet once said, you can buy two thousand pounds of wine, but you won’t get drunk until you actually drink it.

## The Quranic anchor — and what to do with abrogation

God says in His book: “Human beings will attain only what they have striven for.”

Now, I know what you might be thinking. You’ve read the commentaries, and you’ll tell me that this verse was abrogated, that its ruling was superseded. Fine. Let’s grant that for the sake of argument. But what will you do with these verses?

“Whoever has done an atom’s weight of good shall see it, and whoever has done an atom’s weight of evil shall see it.”

And: “Whoever hopes to meet their Lord, let them perform righteous deeds, and let them not associate anyone in the worship of their Lord.”

And: “Those who believe and perform righteous deeds—for them the highest Paradise shall be a welcome abode.”

And: “Except for those who repented, and believed, and performed righteous deeds.”

The evidence is overwhelming. The Prophet himself defined our path through action. He said submission is built on five things: testifying to God and His Messenger, establishing the prayer, paying the charity, fasting the holy month, and making the pilgrimage if you are able. And he said faith is not just words or feelings; it is “to testify with the tongue, to affirm with the heart, and to act upon the teachings.”

Now, don’t misunderstand me. I am not saying you enter Paradise by the price of your own deeds. That is a terrible error. You enter Paradise by God’s Grace, His Favor, His Mercy. But your deeds are how you qualify for that Mercy. They are what bring you close enough to receive it. As God Himself says, “Verily, the Mercy of God is close to the doers of good.”

And if someone then argues, “Fine, then faith alone is enough,” I say to them: even that path is not so simple. To carry your faith safely to the end of your life is a monumental task, a work in itself.

## The Israelite and the angel — a story about why we work even if the work seems refused

There is a story from the Children of Israel that settles this. A man was worshipping God with tremendous devotion, year after year. To test him, God sent an angel who said, “God says that you are tormenting yourself for nothing. Your worship is not accepted, and you are destined for Hell.”

The man heard this, and his reply was perfect. He said, “God created us to worship Him. Our duty is to serve Him, no matter what. He is the Owner of everything, including me, and He can do as He wills.”

The angel returned to God and reported the man’s words. And God, the Exalted, said, “Since this servant of Ours does not turn his face from Our worship, We too will not turn Our Face from him.” And then, to all the angels, He declared: “Bear witness, all of you, that I have forgiven him.”

The man was saved not because he argued, but because his worship was unconditional. He was going to do the work whether it was accepted or not. The work is not the price you pay for the result. The work is what makes you into the kind of person upon whom the result can be bestowed.

## The chorus of the saints — the Father of Imams, Samuel, Jonathan, and the Prophet's eloquence

The great masters all saw this same truth. The Father of Imams said, “A person who thinks they will reach Paradise without striving is self-deluded, and a person who thinks they will reach it through striving alone is merely exhausting themself.” He saw both errors. You cannot have the reward without the work, but you also cannot imagine the work itself is what earns the reward. It is a balance: you strive in order to become worthy of the Mercy you depend on.

Samuel of Basra put it even more bluntly: “To desire Paradise without good deeds is itself a sin.”

And another of the friends of God gave this precise advice: “One should act without admiring one’s own actions—but one should never abandon the performance of good deeds, even if there is a risk of self-admiration.” The danger of pride is real, but the answer isn’t to stop acting. The answer is to act, but to drop your admiration for the act.

But no one said it better than the Prophet himself. He gave us the final word on the matter: “Intelligent is the one who subdues his lower self and performs righteous deeds for the life to come. Mindless—foolish—is the one who surrenders to his own desires and yet supposes that God is with him.” In some versions, it ends: “…and hopes for God’s forgiveness.” That is the definition of a fool: to live for your own pleasure and still expect God to save you.

## Woe or blessing — the formula at the hinge

My son, think of all those nights you’ve stayed awake, memorizing, rehearsing, sacrificing your sleep for the sake of knowledge. I know you have done this. Now you must ask yourself *why*.

If your intention was to gain some worldly benefit, some prestige, some advantage over your peers, then I must say to you: *Woe unto you, and again woe unto you.*

But if your intention was to revive the path of the Prophet, to discipline your own soul, to uproot the evil within yourself and help others do the same, then: *For you is happiness and blessings, and again for you is happiness and blessings.*

The same act—studying through the night—can fall on either side of this divide. Your intention is the hinge. As a poet, a true lover, once prayed:

*O my Lord! The wakefulness of my eyes is useless, unless it is to see Your Face. And the weeping of my eyes is a crime, unless it is from the sorrow of being apart from You.*

Every act is judged by what it is for. The Prophet gave us the frame for our whole lives in three commands. He said: “Live however you wish, but know that one day you will die. Love whoever you wish, but know that one day you will be separated from them. Do whatever you wish, but know that one day you will be repaid for it.”

You are free to choose your actions. You are not free to choose their consequences.

## The questions at the casket — and the address of the heart

So I ask you again: what have you really gained from all your studies in theology, ethics, medicine, poetry? What have you truly gotten from it all, beyond a wasted life and a bit of worldly renown?

I read once in the Gospel of Jesus that in the moment after a body is placed in its casket, before it is carried to the grave, God Himself addresses the dead person with forty questions. The very first one is this:

“O My slave! For years you polished your outward appearance for the sake of other people. But for My sake alone, did you ever purify your heart, even for a single moment?”

That is the first question. Not about the books you read or the arguments you mastered. It is about the heart. Did you ever, for even one moment, work on your heart for Him alone? The Prophet told us, “God does not look at your faces or your bodies, but He looks at your hearts.” The heart is what He sees. That is where the work must be done.

And every day, if you listen, you can hear God addressing that heart: “O My slave! Why do you perform for an audience? Both ease and hardship are in My hands alone. Worship Me with a sincere heart, not to impress My creation, who cannot help you or harm you in the slightest except by My Will.”

## The thesis at its sharpest — and the cry from the Fire

Let me state it as clearly as I can. Knowledge without action is madness. And action without knowledge is worthless.

The knowledge that does not keep you from sin today, that does not ignite in you a desire to obey God—that same knowledge will not save you from the Fire tomorrow.

On that Day, you will hear the people in the Fire crying out, “Send us back, O our Lord, so that we may perform righteous deeds!”

And the answer they will receive is devastatingly simple: “O heedless one! That is precisely where you have just come from.”

The place they beg to return to is the very life they wasted.

So find your courage. Stir your body to action. You are on a journey to the grave, and the ones who went before you are waiting. They are watching for you to arrive. Do not show up empty-handed.

The first Caliph said that our bodies are either “cages of birds or stables of animals.” When the cage of the bird opens at death, the soul hears the call, “Return to your Lord!” and soars to the highest heavens. When the stable of the animal is emptied, it is just a beast being hauled from one place to the abyss of the next. Reflect on which of these you are building. When Saul of Medina died, the Prophet said the very Throne of God shook. But of the other kind, God says in the Quran, “They are like cattle—nay, even more astray.” God forbid you should be one of them.

## Samuel's sigh — the cold drink and the pleading in the Fire

One day, someone handed the great master Samuel of Basra a cup of cold, sweet water. As soon as he took it, he let out a deep sigh and collapsed, unconscious. When he came to, they asked him what had happened.

He said, “I remembered the plea of the people in Hell, when they will beg the people of Paradise to pour down on them even a little water.”

A simple cup of water in his hand became a portal. It opened a vision of that future moment so terrifying that it knocked him to the ground. This is what it means to be awake.

## The night and its four announcements — and the dove that weeps while the lover sleeps

If knowledge alone were enough for you, why would God call out in the final hours before dawn, “Is there anyone who repents? Is there anyone who asks of Me? Is there anyone who seeks My forgiveness?”

That call is not for the sleepers. It is for those of whom God has said, “They sleep but little in the last part of the night,” and “In the time before dawn, they plead earnestly for God’s forgiveness.” He commanded the Prophet himself, “And in the last part of the night, perform the night vigil prayers… it shall be of great benefit to you.”

Once, the Companions were praising the son of the second Caliph, and the Prophet said, “He is a good man. If only he were to pray the night vigil.” Do you hear that? A good man, but not yet complete. The night vigil was missing. He also warned us, “Do not sleep overmuch at night, for it leaves its keeper empty-handed on the Day of Judgment.”

Theodore, one of the early masters, said that a special wind blows in the last part of the night, carrying the voices of those who are awake and praying directly to the Court of God. He said the night has four announcements. When it begins, an angel calls for the worshippers to rise. In the middle of the night, a second angel calls for the devoted to rise. In the final third, a third angel calls for those seeking forgiveness to rise. And just before dawn, a fourth angel cries out, “O heedless ones! Arise!” And those people stumble out of bed like the dead rising from their graves.

The wise sage in the Quran told his son, “My son! Do not let the rooster be more intelligent than you. He rises in the last part of the night to praise his Lord, while you remain sunk in sleep.”

A poet felt this same shame and wrote:

*In the deep of the night, a dove on a branch cries out to her Lord, while I lie fast asleep.
I swear I am a liar! If I were a true lover, the doves would not have surpassed me in weeping.
I claim to be consumed with longing, yet my eyes are dry—while the very animals weep in love for their Creator.*

## Worship is obedience — and the Divine Law is the form

My son, you need to understand what worship actually is. It is obedience. It is following the Divine Law that came through the Prophet, in what you do and what you refrain from doing, in your words and in your silence.

Everything must be measured against his teaching. If you do something that looks like worship—prayer, fasting—but you do it in a way that contradicts his command, it is not worship. It is sin. If you fast on one of the festival days, you are sinning. If you pray in a forbidden time, after the afternoon prayer or just after dawn, you are sinning. The outward form is not enough.

Conversely, some things that might seem worldly, like a husband and wife being intimate, are acts of obedience when done within the law. So you see, worship is another name for obedience. The form of the act does not decide its value. Its conformity to the divine command does.

## Inner struggle — the sword that severs the desires of the lower self

You will not advance on this path through accumulated knowledge alone. The path must be traveled. The desires of your lower self must be fought. You must sever them with the sword of inner struggle.

And let me be clear what I mean by struggle. I do not mean indulging your desires until you are sick of them. That is not struggle; that is surrender. And I do not mean the deceptions of those false guides who mimic the language of the inner path but are hollow inside. A true traveler is one who has actually purified his soul, who has uprooted the evil from its depths. Be warned: a tongue that speaks beautiful words while the heart remains a slave to its appetites is a sign of the deepest misfortune.

Unless you cut away the desires of your lower self with the sword of truth and struggle, the light of intimate knowledge of God will not dawn in your heart.

## What cannot be written — love and the inner taste

You asked me about certain spiritual matters. Some of these cannot be described in writing. When you reach that station, you will know them for yourself. The lesson of love is not taught in a book; it is born from within.

How can I describe an inner taste to you? Can I explain sweetness to someone who has never tasted sugar? Can I put the reality of bitterness into words? These things must be experienced. If you reach that destination, you will understand. If not, words can only give you a faint shadow of the reality. I have written about some of these things in my other books, like the *Revival*, but here I will only give you what is essential.

## The four conditions for the seeker

You asked what is necessary for a person walking this path. There are four conditions.

First, a conviction and a reverent fear of God so deep that you never fall into innovation—and by innovation, I mean anything that contradicts the established principles of the Quran and the Prophet’s way.

Second, a true and complete repentance, made with such sincerity that you never return to the humiliation of sin.

Third, you must satisfy the rights of all people, to the point that even your enemies have no claim against you. Settle your accounts with creation before you try to settle your account with the Creator.

Fourth, you must acquire enough knowledge of the Divine Law to obey God’s commands. Learn what is obligatory for you to know in order to act correctly. You do not need more than that. Learning every detail of every science is a communal obligation, not a personal one. You only need to learn enough to obey.

## Marcus — four thousand traditions, four hundred masters, one practice

The great master Marcus once said something that should teach you everything you need to know about this. He said:

“I served four hundred spiritual masters. From them, I heard four thousand traditions of the Prophet. But I acted upon only one of them, and left the rest aside. When I reflected on that one tradition, I found my salvation in it. It contained all the knowledge of those who came before and all who will come after.”

This was the tradition he chose:

“Work for this world as much as you will live in it. Work for the Hereafter as much as you wish to live there. Work for God as much as you are in need of Him. And work to avoid the Fire as much as you can bear its pain.”

That is the entire map. Your time in this world is short; your work for it should be proportional. Your time in the Hereafter is eternal; your work for it should be vast. Your need for God is absolute; your work for Him should be total. Your ability to bear the Fire is zero; your work to escape it should be unceasing.

You see? You do not need a vast quantity of knowledge. You need knowledge that leads to action.

## The threshold — the master from Balkh and the disciple of thirty-three years

Now, pay close attention to this story.

There was a great master from Balkh named Stephen. One of his disciples, Henry, stayed in his company for many years, learning from him, until he himself became a master.

One day, Stephen asked him, “Henry, how long have you been with me?”

“Thirty-three years,” Henry replied.

“And in all that time,” Stephen asked, “what benefit have you derived from me?”

Henry said, “I have obtained eight benefits from you.”

When Stephen heard this, he recited the verse we say in times of loss: “To God we belong and to Him shall we return.” He said, “I have spent my entire life teaching you, and you have gained only eight benefits?”

Henry replied, “My teacher, that is the truth of it. I do not need anything more. These eight benefits are enough for me, for I am certain that through them I will find salvation in this life and the next.”

Stephen said to him, “Very well—tell me, what are those eight benefits?”
