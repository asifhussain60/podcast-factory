# Eight Lessons from Thirty-Three Years

Consider now, my dear son, a tradition told of the seekers and disciples, those who pursue spiritual elevation and enlightenment by purifying the soul of every evil desire and inclination under the guidance of a master who has himself fully attained that purity and that high station before Allah through the training, guidance, and nearness of his own guide. Among the disciples of Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi was one such seeker, Hatim bin Ism, who through Shaykh Shafeeq's training and guidance later became a great master in his own right. One day Shaykh Shafeeq said to him:

"O Hatim! For how long have you kept my company, in my very nearness, listening to what I have taught?"

Hatim replied: "Thirty-three years."

Shaykh Shafeeq asked: "In all that long stretch of time, what benefit have you drawn from me?"

Hatim answered: "I have obtained eight benefits from you."

At this Shaykh Shafeeq said: "To Allah we belong, and to Him we shall return." Then he said: "O Hatim! I have spent my entire life in teaching and training, and you have gained only eight benefits from me?"

Hatim replied: "O my teacher! If you ask me for the truth, then the truth is exactly as I have said. I need nothing more, for these eight benefits of knowledge are sufficient for me, and I am certain that through them I will attain salvation and success in this life and in the life to come."

Shaykh Shafeeq said: "Very well. Tell me, then, what are those eight benefits?"

These, then, are the benefits as Hatim bin Ism (رض) related them.

"O my teacher! The first benefit is that I looked upon the people of this world and saw that every one of them has a beloved. Yet the beloveds of worldly people are such that some accompany their lovers only until the illness that brings on death, some until death itself, and some as far as the edge of the grave. Once the burial is done, all of them turn back and leave; not one descends into the grave to keep the dead company in its darkness, its loneliness, its narrowness, and its terror. I reflected on this and said to myself: the only true beloved is one that goes down with a person into the grave, that dwells there beside him, that offers him company, that lights up the grave, and that remains his companion on the Day of Judgment and through all the stations one passes after burial until that Day. And I saw that the only beloved possessing these qualities is a person's own righteous deeds. Having come to this awareness and this resolve, I took my righteous deeds for my beloved, so that they will go down with me into my grave when I die, will keep me company there, will become a shining lamp within my grave, will be my companion through every stage of the life to come, and will never part from me."

Shaykh Shafeeq said: "Well done, O Hatim! You have said a most excellent thing. Now relate the second benefit."

"O my teacher! The second benefit is that I glanced at the people of the world and saw that everyone runs after the pleasures and cravings of the Nafs, the lower self that incites toward evil, and that everyone is a servant to its desires. Seeing this, I reflected on this holy verse:

> وَأَمَّا مَنْ خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ وَنَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَى * فَإِنَّ الْجَنَّةَ هِيَ الْمَأْوَى

"But as for the one who has stood in awe of his Sustainer's Presence and restrained his inner self from base and evil desires, Paradise will be the place of rest." (Quran, Chapter 79, Verses 40 to 41)

"I gained the certitude that the Wise Quran is true and that the Book of Allah is the truth. So I set myself firmly against my Nafs and resolved to oppose it. I disciplined it so thoroughly that I refused to satisfy any of its cravings, until at last it found its peace and its contentment in the worship of Allah, the Exalted."

Shaykh Shafeeq said: "May Allah, the Exalted, bless you in your good deeds. Now relate the third benefit."

"O my teacher! The third benefit is that when I looked at the people I saw that every one of them, through great pain and hard labour, was busy heaping up the goods of this passing world, its money, wealth, and possessions, and rejoiced greatly at having amassed so much of it. But when I reflected on this holy verse of the generous Quran:

> مَا عِنْدَكُمْ يَنْفَدُ وَمَا عِنْدَ اللهِ بَاقٍ

"All that is with you is bound to come to an end, whereas all that is with Allah is everlasting." (Quran, Chapter 16, Verse 96)

"Then whatever material goods I had gathered in this world I spent entirely in the Way of Allah, distributing them among the pious poor and the needy, so that they would be laid up with Allah, the Exalted, as my trust, and would become a source of immense good and of ransom, that is, of salvation, in the life to come."

Shaykh Shafeeq said: "O Hatim! May Allah reward you for your spending in His Way. You have said an excellent thing and done an excellent thing. Now relate the fourth benefit."

"O my teacher! The fourth benefit is that I looked at the people of the world and saw that some of them suppose grandeur and honour to lie in belonging to a large and powerful nation or tribe, and so they take pride in their tribe. Others suppose that grandeur comes from abundance of wealth, possessions, family, and kin, and so they boast of their riches and their children. Others reckon their grandeur and honour to lie in violence, in beating, killing, murder, and plunder, and they take pride in that. And others hold that their grandeur lies in wasteful spending, counting such extravagance a badge of honour and boasting of it. But I reflected on this verse, in which Allah, the Exalted, says:

> إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِنْدَ اللهِ أَتْقَاكُمْ

"Verily, the noblest of you in the sight of Allah is the one most deeply conscious of Him, most in awe and fear of Him." (Quran, Chapter 49, Verse 13)

"This alone is correct; this alone is the truth. The notions of the people of the world are false conjectures and nothing more. So I chose Taqwa, that reverent consciousness, awe, and fear of Allah, that I might be counted among those of the highest rank in the sight of Allah, the Exalted."

Shaykh Shafeeq said: "O Hatim! May Allah be pleased with you. You have said a most excellent thing. Now relate the fifth benefit."

"O my teacher! The fifth benefit is that I looked at people finding fault with one another and speaking ill of one another. I found that they did so out of envy, jealousy, and enmity, and that the root of it all was rivalry over greatness, grandeur, possessions, wealth, and knowledge. I reflected on this verse of the Holy Quran:

> نَحْنُ قَسَمْنَا بَيْنَهُمْ مَعِيشَتَهُمْ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا

"It is We who apportion among them their means of livelihood, their sustenance, in the life of this world." (Quran, Chapter 43, Verse 32)

"I saw then that Allah, the Blessed and Exalted, has fixed all possessions and all ranks from the very beginning of creation, and that no one has any hand or say in the matter. So I ceased to compete with anyone or to envy anyone, and I rested content with Allah's apportioning of worldly bounty and with His decree, living at peace with all of creation by surrendering every jealousy and enmity toward His creatures."

Shaykh Shafeeq said: "O Hatim! You speak the truth and you do what is right. Now relate the sixth benefit."

"O my teacher! The sixth benefit is that when I looked at people I saw that every one of them holds enmity toward some other for one reason or another. I then reflected on this verse:

> إِنَّ الشَّيْطَانَ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ فَاتَّخِذُوهُ عَدُوًّا

"Verily, Shaytan is your enemy, so treat him as your enemy." (Quran, Chapter 35, Verse 6)

"After that I gained the certitude that the words of Allah are the truth, and that I should hold no enmity toward any person. From that time onward I took Shaytan alone for my enemy and refused to obey any of his promptings. Instead I cultivated obedience to Allah, followed the commandments of Allah, the Exalted, and gave myself wholly to His worship and service."

Shaykh Shafeeq said: "O Hatim! You have done an excellent thing and said an excellent thing. Now relate the seventh benefit."

"O my teacher! The seventh benefit is that I looked at people and saw that every one of them is consumed by the search for livelihood and sustenance, wearing himself out in that pursuit. In this race many no longer distinguish between the Halal, what is permitted, and the Haram, what is forbidden, and they debase themselves by chasing after doubtful and forbidden earnings. I then reflected on this verse:

> وَمَا مِنْ دَابَّةٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا عَلَى اللهِ رِزْقُهَا

"And there is no creature that moves upon the earth whose sustenance is not the responsibility of Allah." (Quran, Chapter 11, Verse 6)

"I gained the certitude that the Wise Quran is true and correct, and that I too am among those moving creatures of God upon the earth. So I gave myself to the worship of Allah and held firmly to the certitude that He would bring my sustenance to me, for He has promised to provide it."

Shaykh Shafeeq said: "You have done an excellent thing and said an excellent thing. Now relate the eighth benefit."

"O my teacher! The eighth benefit is that when I looked at people I found that everyone's reliance and trust rests upon something or someone: some place their trust in their possessions, and some lean upon other people. I then pondered this holy verse, in which Allah, the Blessed and Exalted, says:

> وَمَنْ يَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى اللهِ فَهُوَ حَسْبُهُ إِنَّ اللهَ بَالِغُ أَمْرِهِ قَدْ جَعَلَ اللهُ لِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدْرًا

"And whoever places his trust in Allah, He alone is sufficient for him. Verily, Allah brings His command to pass; indeed, Allah has set a measure for all things." (Quran, Chapter 65, Verse 3)

"So I placed my trust in Allah, and He is sufficient for me, فَهُوَ حَسْبِي وَنِعْمَ الْوَكِيلُ, He is sufficient for me and the best of trustees. From then on I took to complete reliance upon Allah in every matter."

When Shaykh Shafeeq heard these eight benefits, he said: "O Hatim! May Allah grant you His Tawfeeq, that is, may He enable you to perform righteous deeds and to walk firmly upon His Path. You have conveyed most excellent things. I have looked into the Tawrat, the Injil, the Zabur, and the praiseworthy Criterion between right and wrong, the Holy Quran, and I have found that all four of these Books of God turn upon these very eight benefits. Whoever acts upon these eight principles is as one who has acted upon all four holy Books."
