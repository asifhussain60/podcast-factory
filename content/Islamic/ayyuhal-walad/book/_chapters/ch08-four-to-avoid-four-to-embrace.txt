# Four to Avoid, Four to Embrace

My dear son, I will now bring my explanation to a close with eight pieces of counsel. Accept them from me, so that your knowledge does not turn against you on the Day of Judgment. Of these eight, there are four things you must do and four things you must not do. Let me begin with the four you must leave.

The first is that, as far as you are able, you should not dispute with anyone over any question, for there are many hidden harms in such disputes, and their sin is greater than their benefit. Debate is the fountainhead of every blameworthy trait: ostentation, envy, arrogance, resentment, enmity, self-display, and their like. Yes, if a question arises between you and another person or group, and your desire in it is that the truth should appear and not be lost, then investigation is permitted. But that intention has two marks. The first is that it makes no difference to you whether the truth is disclosed upon your own tongue or upon the tongue of another. The second is that you would rather examine the matter in private than before an audience.

Now hear a benefit I set down for you here. Know that to ask about difficult matters is to present the disease of the heart before the physician, and the answer given is the physician's effort to cure that disease. Know that the ignorant are the sick whose hearts are diseased, and the scholars are the physicians. The deficient scholar cannot heal well, and even the perfected scholar does not treat every patient. He treats only the one in whom he hopes for acceptance of the cure and for reform. When the malady is chronic or barren and will not accept treatment, the physician's skill lies in saying, "This one will not accept a cure, so do not busy yourself with treating him," for to do so is to waste one's life.

Then know that the disease of ignorance is of four kinds. One accepts the cure; the remaining three do not.

As for the ones that do not accept the cure: the first is the person whose question and objection spring from his envy and his hatred. However finely, however clearly, however eloquently you answer him, it adds nothing to him but more hatred, enmity, and envy. The path, then, is not to busy yourself with answering him. As it has been said:

> كلّ العداوة قد ترجى إزالتها * إلاّ عداوة من عاداك عن حسد

"Every enmity may be hoped to be removed, save the enmity of one who bears you enmity out of envy."

You ought to turn away from him and leave him to his disease. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> فَاَعْرِضْ عَنْ مَنْ تَوَلَّى عَنْ ذِكْرِنَا وَلَمْ يُرِدْ اِلاَّ الْحَيَوَةَ الدُّنَا (النجم: ٢٩)

"So turn away from him who turns his back on Our remembrance and desires nothing but the life of this world."

The envier, by everything he says and does, kindles a fire in the standing crop of his own works, for the Prophet (ع) said:

> الحسد يأكل الحسنات كما تأكل النّار الحطب

"Envy devours good deeds as fire devours firewood."

The second is the one whose malady is foolishness, and this too does not accept the cure. As 'Isa (ع) said:

> إنّي ما عجزت عن إحياء الموتى وقد عجزت عن معالجة الأحمق

"I was not powerless to bring the dead to life, yet I was powerless to treat the fool."

This is a man who busies himself with seeking knowledge for a little while, learns some small portion of the rational and religious sciences, and then out of his foolishness questions and objects against the great scholar who has spent his life in those sciences. This fool does not know, and imagines that what is obscure to him is obscure to the great scholar also. Since he cannot grasp even this much, his question springs from foolishness, and you should not busy yourself answering him.

The third is one who is seeking guidance, who carries whatever he does not understand of the words of the great back to the shortcoming of his own understanding, and whose question is asked in order to benefit; yet he is dull and does not perceive realities. It is not fitting to busy yourself answering him either, for the Messenger of Allah (ع) said:

> نحن معاشر الأنبياء أمرنا أن نكلّم النّاس على قدر عقولهم

"We, the company of the prophets, have been commanded to speak to people according to the measure of their minds."

As for the disease that does accept the cure: it is the one who is seeking guidance, intelligent and understanding, who is not overpowered by envy, anger, love of appetite, of rank, or of wealth, and who is a seeker of the straight path, whose question and objection do not spring from envy, obstinacy, or a wish to test. This one accepts the cure, so you may busy yourself answering his question. Indeed, it is obligatory upon you to answer him.

The second thing you must leave is that you guard against becoming a preacher and an admonisher, for there is much affliction in it, unless you first act upon what you say and then admonish others by it. Reflect on what was said to 'Isa (ع):

> يا ابن مريم عظ نفسك فإن اتعظت فعظ النّاس وإلا فاستح من ربّك

"O son of Maryam, admonish your own soul; then, if it takes the admonition, admonish the people, and if not, be ashamed before your Lord."

And if you are tried with this work, then beware of two traits. The first is affectation in speech, with ornate phrases, allusions, extravagant claims, verses, and poetry, for Allah, the Exalted, detests the affected, and affectation that oversteps the bound points to the ruin of the inward and the heedlessness of the heart. The meaning of admonition is that the servant should recall the fire of the Hereafter and his own shortcoming in the service of his Creator; that he should reflect upon his past life, which he wasted in what did not concern him; and that he should reflect upon the obstacles before him: the possibility that his faith may not be sound at the end, his state under the seizing hand of the Angel of Death, whether he will be able to answer Munkar and Nakir, his condition at the Resurrection and its stations, and whether he will cross the Bridge in safety or fall into the abyss. Let the remembrance of these things remain in his heart until it drives him from his rest. This boiling of these fires and this wailing over these calamities is what is called admonition. Its aim is to make the people know these things, to alert them to their negligence and their excess, and to open their eyes to the defects of their own souls, so that the heat of these fires may touch those present in the gathering and these calamities may grieve them, that they may make up their past life as far as they can and grieve over the days that passed without the obedience of Allah, the Exalted. This whole matter, upon this path, is what is called preaching.

It is as if you saw that a flood had rushed upon a man's house while he and his household were inside it, so you cry out, "Beware, beware! Flee from the flood!"

> الحذر الحذر، فرّوا من السّيل

Would your heart, in such a state, wish to inform the master of the house of his danger with laboured phrases, subtleties, and allusions? Not in the least. Such, then, is the condition of the preacher, and he ought to shun all of that.

The second trait is that your concern in your preaching should not be that the people should cry out in your gathering, or display ecstasy, or tear their garments, so that it may be said, "What an excellent gathering this was!" for all of that is inclination toward this world, and it is born of heedlessness. Rather, your resolve and your concern should be to call the people from this world to the Hereafter, from disobedience to obedience, from greed to abstinence, from miserliness to generosity, from doubt to certainty, from heedlessness to wakefulness, and from delusion to God-fearing. You should make the Hereafter beloved to them and this world hateful, and teach them the knowledge of worship and abstinence. Do not deceive them with the generosity of Allah, the Exalted, and His mercy, for the prevailing tendency in their natures is to swerve from the way of the Law, to strive after what does not please Allah, and to be content with base traits of character. So cast dread into their hearts, alarm them, and warn them of the terrors that face them, that perhaps the qualities of their inward may change and the dealings of their outward be transformed, and that they may show eagerness and desire for obedience and a turning away from disobedience.

This is the path of preaching and sincere counsel. Every sermon that is not thus is a bane upon the one who speaks and the one who hears. Indeed, it has been said that such a speaker is a ghoul and a devil who leads the creation away from the path and destroys them, so that they are obliged to flee from him, for the corruption that this speaker works in their religion, not even the devil can work its like. And whoever has the hand and the power is obliged to bring him down from the pulpits of admonition and to restrain him from what he has undertaken, for this is part of enjoining the right and forbidding the wrong.

The third thing you must leave is that you should not mix with commanders and rulers, nor even look upon them, for to see them, to sit with them, and to mingle with them is a great affliction. And if you are tried with that, then keep clear of praising them and lauding them, for Allah, the Exalted, is angered when the corrupt and the oppressor are praised. It has been said:

> إنّ الله تعالى يغضب إذا مُدح الفاسق والظّالم، ومن دعا لطول بقائهم فقد أحبّ أن يُعصى الله في أرضه

"Allah, the Exalted, is angered when the corrupt and the oppressor are praised; and whoever prays for the length of their stay has loved that Allah be disobeyed in His earth."

The fourth thing you must leave is that you should not accept anything of the gifts and presents of the rulers, even if you know them to be from what is lawful, for coveting from them corrupts the religion, since from it is born flattery, the guarding of their side, and agreement with them in their injustice, and all of this is corruption in the religion. The least of its harm is that, when you accept their gifts and profit from their world, you come to love them; and whoever loves someone necessarily loves the length of his life and his continuance; and in loving the continuance of the oppressor there is a desire for oppression upon the servants of Allah, the Exalted, and a desire for the ruin of the world. What thing, then, could be more harmful than this to the religion and the final end?

Beware, beware, that the seduction of the devils deceive you, or the saying of some people to you that it is better and more fitting for you to take the dinar and the dirham from them and distribute them among the poor and the destitute, since they would only spend it in corruption and disobedience, and your spending upon the weak among the people is better than their spending. For that accursed one has cut the throats of many people with this very whispering. We have mentioned this at length in the Revival of the Sciences of Religion (Ihya' 'Ulum ad-Din), so seek it there.

Now for the four things it is fitting that you should do.

The first is that you should make your dealing with Allah, the Exalted, such that, were your own servant to deal with you by it, you would be content with it from him, would not be vexed with him, and would not be angered. And whatever you would not accept for yourself from your hired servant, do not accept it either for Allah, the Exalted, who is your true Master.

The second is that whatever you do with people, make it such as you would be pleased to receive from them, for the faith of a servant is not complete until he loves for all people what he loves for himself. As it has been said:

> لا يكمل إيمان عبد حتّى يحبّ لسائر النّاس ما يحبّ لنفسه

"A servant's faith is not complete until he loves for all people what he loves for himself."

The third is that when you read knowledge or study it, your knowledge should be such as reforms your heart and purifies your soul. Consider it as though you knew that your life would remain no longer than a week; then of necessity you would not busy yourself in it with the science of jurisprudence, ethics, principles, theology, and the like, for you would know that these sciences would not avail you. Rather, you would busy yourself with watching over the heart, with knowing the qualities of the soul, with turning away from the attachments of this world, with purifying your soul of blameworthy traits, and with the love of Allah, the Exalted, His worship, and the adorning of yourself with beautiful qualities. And let no day and night pass over a servant but that it is possible his death may be within it.

My dear son, hear from me another word and reflect upon it, that you may find deliverance. If you were told that the sultan would come to visit you after a week, I know that in that span you would busy yourself with nothing but putting right whatever you knew the sultan's gaze would fall upon: the garments, the body, the house, the furnishings, and the rest. Now consider what I am pointing you toward, for you are a man of understanding, and a single word suffices the discerning. The Messenger of Allah (ع) said:

> إنّ الله لا ينظر إلى صوركم ولا إلى أعمالكم ولكن ينظر إلى قلوبكم ونيّاتكم

"Allah does not look at your forms nor at your deeds, but He looks at your hearts and your intentions."

If you desire the knowledge of the states of the heart, then look into the Ihya' and the others of my writings, for this knowledge is an individual obligation, while other knowledge is a communal obligation, except for the measure by which the ordinances of Allah, the Exalted, are discharged. And He it is who gives you the ability to attain it.

The fourth is that you should not gather of this world more than a year's sufficiency, as the Messenger of Allah (ع) would set that aside for some of his chambers, and said:

> أللّهمّ اجعل قوت آل محمّد كفافا

"O Allah, make the provision of the family of Muhammad just enough."

He did not set it aside for all his chambers; rather he set it aside for the one in whose heart he knew there was a weakness. As for her who was a possessor of certainty, he would set aside for her no more than the provision of a day, or half of it.
