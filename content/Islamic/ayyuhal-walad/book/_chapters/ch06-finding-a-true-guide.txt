# Finding a True Guide

My dear beloved son, you now know from these traditions that you have no need of a great quantity of knowledge. Let me return, then, to my explanation, and set out for you the inward conditions required of the student and seeker upon the Path of God.

The fifth condition binding upon the seeker on the Path of Allah, the Exalted, is that he must have a Shaykh al-Kamil, a perfected spiritual guide, who leads him, draws the evil qualities and morals out of him, and sets noble ones in their place. This spiritual training is like the work of a farmer tending his crop. When he sees useless weeds that have sprung up among the grain, he pulls them out and casts them away, and then he waters the crop and nourishes it so that it grows well and comes to good quality. In the same manner, at every stage of the journey the traveler on the Path of Allah has no remedy and no solution save through a Murshid al-Kamil, a true and perfected spiritual guide. For Allah, the Exalted, sent the Prophet Muhammad (صلى الله عليه وسلم) to His servants so that he might be an illumined guide upon the Way of Allah and bring the people to that Way. When the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وسلم) departed from this world, he appointed his deputies and successors in his place, so that they might stand as proofs of the Way of Allah, the Exalted, until the Day of Judgment.

The seeker of God, therefore, must have a perfected spiritual guide, a Shaykh al-Kamil, who, for the sake of walking the Path of Allah, stands as an illumined proof and deputy of the Prophet Muhammad (صلى الله عليه وسلم).

The heir of the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وسلم) whom one takes as his Shaykh must be a scholar, yet not every scholar is fit to be a Shaykh al-Kamil. For this work of guiding people spiritually, purifying their souls and helping them advance upon the Way of Allah, only a person possessing certain particular qualities is truly qualified. I set down some of these qualities briefly here, so that no foolish or misguided person may falsely claim to be a Shaykh. Such impostors are many, and their sole aim is to deceive people for material gain, working great harm to this vital dimension of Islam and leading many astray.

I say that a man is fit to be a Shaykh only when, having turned away from love of the world, love of honor, and love of worldly rank, he has taken Bay'a, a solemn covenant with Allah made upon the hand of a Shaykh, pledging to turn away from evil inwardly and outwardly, to excel upon the Path of God by obeying His commandments and keeping away from His prohibitions, and to strive to purify the soul in pursuit of God's nearness and approval, with a perfected spiritual guide, who in his turn has taken Bay'a with his own perfected spiritual guide, and so on, so that this spiritual chain reaches all the way back to the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وسلم). Such a man must have passed through every manner of Riazat and Mujahada, spiritual struggles and disciplines, and must have obeyed every commandment of the Prophet Muhammad (صلى الله عليه وسلم). He must eat little, sleep little, pray abundantly, performing the five obligatory daily prayers as well as the supererogatory prayers, fast often, and give generously in charity and in the Way of God. Within his character, inward and outward alike, there must dwell every praiseworthy moral quality: patience, gratitude, certitude in Allah, generosity, contentment, trustworthiness, tolerance, seriousness of purpose, humility, obedience to Allah, truthfulness, modesty and reserve, dignity, tranquility, and all virtues of their kind. He must have received a light from among the lights of the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وسلم), so that every evil quality, miserliness, jealousy, enmity, worldly expectation, anger, and rebellion, has been purged from within him, and he must rely upon no one for knowledge except for the knowledge we receive from the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وسلم).

These qualities, among the marks that distinguish the perfected spiritual guides who lead others upon the Path of God, are what render a man worthy to be a deputy and heir of the Messenger of Allah (صلى الله عليه وسلم). To follow such guides is the correct way.

Such spiritual guides are exceedingly hard to find. But if a man has been granted this wealth and blessing, that he has found such a guide, and that the guide has admitted him among his disciples, his Mureeds, then it is necessary for that disciple to keep both outward and inward respect, honor, and proper etiquette toward his spiritual guide.

The outward etiquettes toward one's spiritual guide are these. The disciple should not argue with the guide, and if a dispute arises and the disciple imagines that the guide has overlooked something, he should not raise an objection. He should not remain stationed at the place of prayer at all times; rather, when the time for prayer arrives, he should go to the place of prayer, perform the prayer, and then roll up his prayer mat and depart. He should not perform lengthy supererogatory prayers before the Shaykh after each obligatory prayer. Whatever the perfected guide commands, the disciple should obey to the utmost of his ability. And it is of the greatest importance to understand that a true spiritual guide can never and will never command his disciple to do anything forbidden by the Shari'ah, the law of Allah. The false impostors, who lack the qualities I have named, may by contrast command their disciples to do things that are Haram.

The inward etiquettes toward one's spiritual guide are these. Whatever the disciple hears outwardly from the Murshid, he should not entertain even the least doubt in his heart concerning the Shaykh's words or deeds, lest he be counted among the hypocrites. And if he cannot maintain this inner sincerity, then he should withdraw from the company of his spiritual guide until his inward state comes into harmony with his outward.

The sixth condition is that the disciple, the seeker of truth, guards himself against the deceptions of the Nafs, the lower self, an inward and subtle presence invisible to the outward eye yet plainly perceptible to one who possesses inner sight, which commands a man to evil by whispering into his heart. This is possible only if the seeker abandons the gatherings of the ill-charactered and the ignorant. In this way the dominion of Shaytan over the heart is brought to an end, and every Satanic influence is uprooted entirely. This Satanic company may come from among mankind or from among the Jinn, an unseen creation of God made of fire, to whose kind Shaytan belongs and from among whom he recruits his followers.

The seventh condition is that in every circumstance the seeker should prefer humility and freedom from greed over wealth, and should embrace contentment and satisfaction with the Will of Allah. These seven conditions are essential for every traveler upon the Path of Allah, the Exalted, and for every seeker of truth.
