# Worship Is Obedience, Nothing Less

My dear beloved son! My purpose here is that you should truly understand what obedience and worship are. Obedience and worship mean following the *Shari'ah*, the Divine Law, of the Prophet Muhammad (ع), whether the commandments call for performing righteous deeds or for restraining oneself from evil, and whether they pertain to his words or to his example. In short, whatever you do or refrain from doing, whatever you say or leave unsaid, all of it must be in full accordance with his teaching. If you speak, speak according to his teaching. If you remain silent, remain silent according to his guidance. If you act or abstain from acting, do so in conformity with his example.

Understand this well: if you do something believing it to be an act of worship, yet you do not do it in obedience to his command, it will not be counted as worship at all. It will be counted as a sin, even when it wears the outward form of prayer or fasting. Do you not know that a person who fasts on either of the two days of Eid, or on the days of Hajj, commits a sin? It is fasting in its outward shape, but it is not done in accordance with the command, and so the one who does it is a wrongdoer. In the same way, whoever performs prayers at a forbidden time, such as after the Asr prayer before sunset or after the Fajr prayer before the sun has risen far enough, or performs prayers upon unlawfully occupied ground, is called *athim*, a sinner, and *fasiq*, a transgressor. Though the act resembles worship, it is not sound, because it was not done according to the guidance of the Prophet (ع). Yet when a husband and wife come together in lawful intimacy, this is no sin, however it may appear to the uninformed, for it falls within his guidance and is therefore *halal*, permissible. So it is established that worship is only another name for obedience. Worship means obedience.

In the very same way, prayer and fasting are counted as worship only when they are offered according to the Divine guidelines. Therefore, my dear beloved son, let all your words and all your deeds conform to the commandments of the Prophet (ع). Whatever you do and whatever you speak must accord with the *Shari'ah*, for any knowledge or action of God's creation that falls outside the *Shari'ah* is plain misguidance, and it draws a human being away from the truth. This is why the Prophet (ع) abrogated all the religious dispensations that came before him.

So do nothing without his guidance, and be certain of this: you cannot advance upon the Path of Allah the Exalted through accumulated knowledge alone. This Path is traveled by earnest effort and *mujahadah*, the intense inner struggle and disciplined exercise of the soul. The pleasures and demands of the lower self must be severed with the sword of *mujahadah*. These desires cannot be uprooted through immorality, nor through the deceptions of false Sufis, those who claim the station of Sufism and mimic its outward language and appearance while remaining hollow and devious within. The true Sufis are those who have attained an intimate nearness to God through the emergence of purified souls, having driven evil out of the deepest recesses of their being by genuine *mujahadah*. Allah the Exalted takes no delight in subtleties of philosophy spun amid a life engulfed in sin and darkness. A tongue that utters words of profound wisdom and beauty while the heart stays enslaved to heedlessness and the appetites of the lower self is a mark of the gravest misfortune.

Unless you sever the desires and inclinations of the lower self with the sword of truth and *mujahadah*, the light of *ma'rifah*, that deep and intimate knowledge and conscious relationship with Allah, will never dawn within your heart.

My dear beloved son! You have asked me about certain spiritual matters, and some among them cannot be fully set down in writing or in speech. When you reach that high station of enlightenment, you will come to know them of yourself. The lesson of love is not taught; it is born and learned from within. If you reach that state, knowing these things will be like passing from one spiritual station into another. For *Ishq*, intense love, is another name for love and for the fervent longing to seek the Beloved, which is *Zauq*. Love and *Zauq* can be conveyed neither in speech nor in writing, and their true essence cannot be laid before another. Just as sweetness and sourness and bitterness cannot be adequately described by anyone who tries to put them into words, so it is here. Know, then, that if you arrive at this destination of high station, you will come to know all of this by yourself; but if you cannot reach it, the truth can be described to you only so far as writing and speech allow.

My dear beloved son! Some of your questions are of this first kind. As for those that can be answered in writing and speech, I have already addressed them in my book *Ahya al-Uloom ad-Deen*, the Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God, and in other works, in detail, which you may read. Even so, I will explain them here in a concise manner, God willing.

Secondly, you have asked what is required of those who walk upon the Path of Allah the Exalted, the sincere seekers of Him. Four things are obligatory upon such a seeker.

The first condition is that you hold a conviction and fear of Allah so firm that you never fall into innovation, *bid'a*, which is any matter contrary to the established principles of the Quran and the Sunnah. The Sunnah encompasses the teachings, actions, outward conduct, and inner states of the Prophet Muhammad (ع), such as contentment, patience, gratitude, certitude in God, intimacy with Him, *ma'rifah*, the deep and intimate knowledge and recognition of God, intense love of God, and the like.

The second condition is that you make a true and complete repentance, *Taubatun Nasuh*, of such a kind that you will never return to the humiliation of sins.

The third condition is that you keep even your enemies satisfied, so that none of God's creation holds any unfulfilled claim against you.

The fourth condition is that you acquire enough knowledge of the *Shari'ah* to be able to obey the commandments of Allah the Exalted that are obligatory upon you. It is not required to learn more of the *Shari'ah* than that, and of the other branches of knowledge you should learn only so much as is obligatory.

Know that it has been narrated in the traditions of the intimate friends of Allah, the saints, that Shibli (رض) said:

"I served four hundred spiritual masters and guides, and out of the four thousand traditions of the Prophet Muhammad (ع) that they narrated to me, I adopted and acted upon only one, and I left the rest aside. For when I deliberated upon this single hadith, I found my salvation within it. I found in this one hadith the knowledge of all who came first and all who came last:

> إعمل لدنياك بقدر مقامك فيها، واعمل لآخرتك بقدر بقائك فيها، واعمل لله بقدر حاجتك إليه، واعمل للنّار بقدر صبرك عليها

'Work for this world as much as you will live in it, and work for the life hereafter as much as you wish to live there, and work for Allah the Exalted as much as you are in need of Him, and work toward avoiding the Fire as much as you can bear its pain.' "

The Prophet (ع) spoke these words to one of his companions.

My dear beloved son! It is plain from this hadith that you do not need a vast store of knowledge, for studying and acquiring knowledge beyond one's personal obligation is *Fard Kifayah*, a communal obligation discharged once a sufficient number within the Muslim community fulfil it; but if no one discharges it, the whole community bears the sin of that neglect. Give your closest attention to what follows, so that you may attain a high degree of certitude.
