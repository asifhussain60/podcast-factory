# Sufism, Servitude, Trust, Sincerity

You have asked me what Tasawwuf is. Know that Tasawwuf rests upon two qualities. The first is that the servant should be obedient to Allah, acting in accordance with the Shari'ah, the outward law of Islam with its obligations, its commands, and its prohibitions. The second is that the seeker should be compassionate and a doer of good toward all of Allah's creation, and this is not possible until one has first purified the soul of every evil quality and then adorned it with every noble one. Whoever perseveres in acting upon the Shari'ah and genuinely desires the betterment and true welfare of all people is rightly called a Sufi, and this is the correct meaning of the word, unlike the distorted uses of it so common today. Loyalty and obedience to Allah means that you sacrifice your own share of happiness for the sake of the command of Allah. Sincerity toward all people means that you do not carry others toward what you yourself want, but carry yourself toward what they want, provided they do not go against the Shari'ah. So the seeker keeps no relationship for a private motive, but is selfless in service, dedicating himself to the good of all, on the condition that this good remains within the Divine law.

You have asked me what servitude is. Servitude consists of three things.

The first is guarding the command of the Shari'ah.

The second is contentment with the decree and destiny appointed by Allah, the Exalted, and with the portion He apportions.

The third is the abandonment of your own pleasure in seeking the pleasure of Allah, the Exalted, being content and glad with His Will and His Power.

You have asked me what reliance upon Allah is, what Tawakkul is. Know that Tawakkul means that you make your belief firm in what Allah, the Exalted, has promised. That is, you believe with certainty that whatever He has decreed for you will reach you without fail, even if everyone in the world should strive to turn it away from you, and that whatever He has not written for you will never reach you, even if the whole world should come to your aid.

You have asked me what sincerity is, what Ikhlas is. Know that Ikhlas means that all your deeds are for Allah, the Exalted, alone, that your heart takes no ease in the praises of people and pays no heed to their blame. Whatever you do should not be for show, for Riya. Your heart should neither be gladdened by people's admiration nor grieved by their criticism. Know that Riya is born from magnifying the creation. Its cure is that you see all people as subject to the Power of Allah, and reckon them like inert stones that have no power to bring you either comfort or hardship, so that you may be freed from displaying yourself to them. As long as you reckon them to possess power and will, Riya will not depart from you.

My dear son, the rest of your questions are of two kinds. Some of them I have already set down in books I have written, so seek their answers there; and some are such that to write their answers is not permitted. Act upon what you know, and through that acting there will be unveiled to you what you do not yet know.

My dear son, from this day onward, do not ask me about what is difficult for you except through the tongue of the heart, for Allah, the Exalted, has said:

> (وَلَوْ اَلُهُمْ صَبَرُوا حَتَّى تَخْرُجَ اِلَيْهِمْ لَكَانَ خَيْرًا لَهُمْ * الحجرات: ٥)

"Had those people been patient until you yourself came out to them, it would have been better for them." (Quran, Chapter 49, Verse 5)

And accept the counsel of Khidr (ع) when he said:

> (فَلاَ تَسْئَلْنِي عَنْ شَيْءٍ حَتَّى أُحْدِثَ لَكَ مِنْهُ ذِكْرًا * الكهف: ٧٠)

"Do not question me about anything until I myself bring it up to you and explain it." (Quran, Chapter 18, Verse 70)

Do not be hasty until the time of a thing arrives; then it will be unveiled to you and you will see it, for Allah, the Exalted, has said:

> (سَأُرِيكُّمْ آيَاتٍ فَلاَ تَسْتَعْجِلوُنِ * الأنبياء: ٣٧)

"I will show you My signs, so do not seek to hasten them." (Quran, Chapter 21, Verse 37)

So do not ask me before the time. And be certain that you will not arrive except by traveling the road, for Allah, the Exalted, has said:

> (آَوَ لَمْ يَسِيرُوا فِيِ اْلاَرْضِ فَيَنْظُرُوا * فاطر: ٤٤)

"Have they not traveled through the earth and seen?" (Quran, Chapter 35, Verse 44)

My dear son, by Allah, if you travel you will see wonders at every stage, so give of your very soul, for the head of this whole matter is the giving of the soul. As Zun Noon al-Misri (may Allah have mercy on him) said to one of his students:

> «إن قدرت على بذل الرّوح فتعال، و إلاّ فلا تشتغل بترّهات الصّوفّيّة.»

"If you are able to give of your soul, then come; and if not, do not busy yourself with the idle talk of the Sufis."
