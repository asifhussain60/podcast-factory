# The Shaykh (الشيخ) and the Disciple's Rule of Life

*The working rule of life for a seeker on the Path of Allah rests on three pillars that cannot be substituted for one another: a set of inward principles anchored in the Quran and tested by actual practice — exemplified in the eight benefits Hatim ibn Ism brought back from his long looking at the people of the world; a* Shaykh al-Kamil (شيخ الكامل) *(perfected spiritual guide) at the center of the seeker's life, whose chain of guidance traces back to the Messenger of Allah and whose authority is bounded absolutely by the* Shari'ah (شريعة)*; and a small, exact inner vocabulary —* Tasawwuf (تصوف)*, servitude,* Tawakkul (توكل) *(reliance on God), and* Ikhlas (إخلاص) *(sincerity) — each defined in one sentence, because the inner life is not a long curriculum but four named realities the seeker either lives or does not. The remaining questions of the disciple are not refused but reassigned: from the page to the practice, since some answers open only through walking.*

## The eight benefits Hatim brought back

Hatim ibn Ism, may Allah be pleased with him, was a disciple of the great Khurasanian master Shaykh (الشيخ) Shafeeq al-Balkhi, may Allah shower His mercy upon him. The setting is the master's house. Hatim has been away. Now he comes back with what he has gathered — not from books, but from his long looking at the people of the world: how they live, what they chase, what eats their hearts. He reports eight benefits. After each, his master responds. Ghazali walks all eight, and the structure matters: every single one is the same shape — *I looked at the people and saw X; then I contemplated this verse from the Quran; therefore I did Y*. The Quran is the corrective lens through which the diseases of the people of the world become visible, and the *therefore* clause is the act the disciple actually performs in response. This is what walking with a master looks like — not a higher theory, but a long discipline of *looking, weighing against the Book, and changing what one does*.

**First benefit — the only beloved who descends into the grave.** *I looked at the people of this world and saw that everyone has a beloved. Yet these beloveds — some accompany their lovers only until the illness that brings death, some until death itself, and some until the grave. After burial, all the loved ones return. None descend into the grave to dwell with the deceased, to offer companionship in the darkness, solitude, narrowness, and terror of that place.* The only beloved possessing the qualities required — accompanying the lover into the grave, illuminating it, becoming a partner on the Day of Judgment and through every station after burial — is one's own righteous deeds. *After reaching this awareness and determination, I made my righteous deeds my beloved.* Shaykh (الشيخ) Shafeeq says: *Bravo, O Hatim. You have said a most excellent thing.*

**Second benefit — refusing the Nafs (نفس).** *I glanced at the people and saw that everyone was running after the pleasures and desires of the* Nafs (نفس) *(the lower self that incites one toward evil) and was subservient to its desires.* Hatim reflects on the verse:

*Wa Amma Mun Khafa Maqama Rabbihi Wa Nahun Nafsa 'Anil Hawa (وأما من خاف مقام ربه) Fa Innal Jannata Heyal Mawa.* *— "But as for the one who has stood in awe of his Sustainer's Presence and restrained his inner self from base and evil desires, Paradise will be the place of rest."* (Quran 79:40–41)

*I developed the certitude that the Wise Quran is correct. I then took a firm stand against my Nafs (نفس) and was resolved to oppose it. I disciplined it to such a degree that I refused to fulfill any of its desires, until at last it began to find peace and satisfaction in the worship of Allah, the Exalted.*

**Third benefit — sending wealth ahead.** *I saw that every person, through great pain and hard labor, was busy accumulating the possessions of this mortal world — money, wealth, goods — and was greatly pleased to have amassed much of it.* He weighs this against the verse:

*Ma 'Indakum Yanfadu Wa Ma 'IndAllahi Baaqin (ما عندكم ينفد).* *— "All that is with you is bound to come to an end, whereas all that which is with Allah is everlasting."* (Quran 16:96)

*Therefore whatever material possessions I had accumulated in this world, I spent entirely in the Way of Allah by distributing them among pious indigents and the needy, so that those possessions would be deposited with Allah as my trust, and would become a source of immense good and ransom — salvation — in the life hereafter.*

**Fourth benefit — Taqwa (تقوى) is the only true nobility.** *I looked at the people and saw that some believe grandeur and honor belong to the nation or tribe that is large and powerful; some believe it comes from abundance of wealth, possessions, family, and relatives; some believe it lies in displaying anger, in beating and killing and pillage; some believe it lies in wasteful spending — and so they take pride and boast in their respective fictions.* Against all four, Hatim sets the verse:

*Inna Akramakum 'IndAllahi Atqakum (إن أكرمكم عند الله أتقاكم).* *— "Verily, the noblest of you in the sight of Allah is the one who is most deeply conscious — most fearful — of Him."* (Quran 49:13)

*This alone is correct; this alone is the truth. The thoughts of the people of the world are false conjectures. Therefore I adopted* Taqwa (تقوى) *(pious reverence, consciousness, awe, and fear of Allah), so that I might be counted among those of the highest rank in the sight of Allah.*

**Fifth benefit — content with the assigned portion.** *I looked at the people complaining against one another. I found that they were doing this on account of envy, jealousy, and enmity, and that the root cause was competition over greatness, grandeur, possessions, wealth, and knowledge.* Against this Hatim sets the verse:

*Nahnu Qasumna Baeynahum Ma'eeshatahum Fil Hayatid Dunya (نحن قسمنا بينهم معيشتهم).* *— "It is We who distribute their means of livelihood — their sustenance — among them in the life of this world."* (Quran 43:32)

*I reflected that Allah has appointed all possessions and ranks from the beginning of creation; no one has any control or say in this matter. Therefore I ceased to compete with or harbor jealousy toward anyone, and I remained content with Allah's distribution and with His appointed destiny, living in peace with all of creation by surrendering every jealousy and enmity against the creatures of God.*

**Sixth benefit — the one true enemy.** *I saw that every person holds enmity toward someone else for some reason.* Hatim reflects on the verse:

*Innash Shaytana Lakum 'Aduwwun Fettakhizuhu 'Aduwwa (إن الشيطان لكم عدو).* *— "Verily, Shaytan (شيطان) is your enemy, so treat him as your enemy."* (Quran 35:6)

*From that time forward, I regarded Satan alone as my enemy and refused to obey any of his promptings. Instead, I cultivated obedience to Allah, followed His commandments, and devoted myself entirely to His worship and servitude. This is the* Sirat al-Mustaqeem (صراط المستقيم) *(the straight path of guidance in which there is no crookedness)*, as Allah declares: *Alam A'had Ilaeykum Ya Bani Aadama Al La Ta'budush Shaytana (ألم أعهد إليكم يا بني آدم) — Did I not make this covenant with you, O Children of Adam: that you should never obey Shaytan (شيطان), for he is your avowed enemy, and that you should worship Me alone? This is the straight path.*

**Seventh benefit — sustenance is on Allah.** *I saw that every person is preoccupied with his or her livelihood and the search for sustenance, consumed by intense effort in that pursuit. In this race, many do not even distinguish between the* Halal (حلال) *(what is permissible) and the* Haram (الحرام) *(what is forbidden), and instead degrade themselves by pursuing doubtful and forbidden earnings.* Against this, the verse:

*Wa Ma Min Daabbatin Fil Arde Illa 'AlalAllahi Rizquha.* *— "And there is no living creature on earth whose sustenance is not the responsibility of Allah."* (Quran 11:6)

*I am also among those living creatures of God present upon the earth. Therefore I devoted myself to the worship of Allah and held firm to the certitude that He would bring my sustenance to me, for He has promised to provide it.*

**Eighth benefit — reliance on the One who suffices.** *I found that every person's reliance and trust rests on something or someone. Some place their trust in their material possessions; some rely upon other people.* Hatim then weighs the verse:

*Wa Maey Yatawakkal 'AlalAllahi Fahuwa Hasbuhu.* *— "And for everyone who places his or her trust in Allah, He alone is sufficient."* (Quran 65:3) — *And He is sufficient for me, and He is the best of Helpers.*

*Therefore I adopted complete reliance upon Allah in every matter.*

When the master Shafeeq al-Balkhi heard the eight, he said: *O Hatim! May Allah grant you His* Tawfeeq (التوفيق) *(divine enablement to perform righteous deeds and to walk firmly upon His Path). I have seen in the* Tawrat (التوراة) *(the Torah revealed to Prophet Moses), the* Injil (إنجيل) *(the Gospel of Prophet Jesus), the* Zabur (الزبور) *(the Psalms of Prophet David), and in the praiseworthy differentiator between right and wrong — the Holy Quran — that all four of these Books of God speak of these very eight benefits. Whoever acts upon these eight principles is as one who has acted upon all four holy Books.*

Ghazali then turns to the disciple in his own voice. *My dear beloved son! You now know from these traditions that you do not need a great quantity of knowledge.* The eight benefits are not a curriculum; they are the report of a man who looked, weighed, and *changed what he did*. Four sacred Books on one side of the balance; these eight performed acts on the other. The balance holds.

## The fifth condition — the perfected guide

Now Ghazali resumes the catalog of inward conditions for the seeker (the first four have already been laid down; the fifth, sixth, and seventh are gathered here). The fifth condition is that the seeker must have a *Shaykh al-Kamil (شيخ الكامل)* — a perfected spiritual guide — who leads the seeker, removes evil qualities and morals from within, and replaces them with noble ones.

The figure Ghazali reaches for is the farmer. When the farmer sees unnecessary weeds that have sprung up among the grain, he pulls them out and throws them away. Then he waters the crop and nourishes it so that it grows well. In the same way, at every stage of the journey, the traveler on the Path of Allah has no remedy except through a *Murshid al-Kamil (مرشد الكامل)* (a true and perfected spiritual guide). The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was himself the illuminated guide; when he departed from this world, he appointed his deputies and successors in his place, so that they would serve as proofs of the Way of Allah until the Day of Judgment.

This is the doctrinal frame: the chain of guidance is unbroken. The seeker is not asked to invent the path. The seeker is asked to find one in whom the path still lives.

## The qualities of a true Shaykh (الشيخ)

Ghazali warns the disciple sharply: every Shaykh (الشيخ) is a scholar, but not every scholar is a Shaykh al-Kamil (شيخ الكامل). The work — *purifying souls and helping them advance on the Way of Allah* — requires a person possessing specific qualities. *No foolish or misguided person may falsely claim to be a Shaykh (الشيخ), for such impostors are many, and their sole purpose is to deceive people for material gain, causing great harm to this vital dimension of Islam.* The list is not pious decoration. It is the test.

Only that person can be a Shaykh (الشيخ) who has turned away from love of the world, love of honor, and love of worldly rank; who has taken *Bay'a (البيعة)* (a solemn covenant with Allah made on the hand of a Shaykh (الشيخ), pledging to turn away from evil inwardly and outwardly, to obey His commandments, keep away from His prohibitions, and strive to purify the soul in pursuit of God's nearness) with a perfected spiritual guide, who in turn has taken Bay'a (البيعة) with *his* perfected spiritual guide — and so on, such that this spiritual chain extends all the way back to the Messenger of Allah.

He must have undergone all manner of *Riazat (الرياضة)* (spiritual disciplines) and *Mujahada (المجاهدة)* (inner struggles) and have obeyed every commandment of the Prophet. He must eat little, sleep little, pray abundantly — performing the five obligatory daily prayers as well as supererogatory prayers — fast frequently, and give generously in charity and in the Way of God. In his character, inward and outward, must reside all praiseworthy moral qualities: patience, gratitude, certitude in Allah, generosity, contentment, trustworthiness, tolerance, seriousness of purpose, humility, obedience to Allah, truthfulness, modesty, dignity, tranquility, and all similar virtues. He must have received a light from among the lights of the Messenger of Allah, such that all evil qualities — miserliness, jealousy, enmity, worldly expectation, anger, and rebellion — have been purged from within, and he must not rely on anyone for knowledge except the knowledge that comes from the Messenger of Allah.

The list is the working specification. Where these marks are present, the person is worthy to be a deputy and heir of the Messenger of Allah. Where they are absent, the title is a lie.

## Obedience to the guide — outer and inner etiquette

If the disciple — the *Mureed (مريد)* — has been granted the wealth of finding such a guide and being admitted among his disciples, then it becomes obligatory to maintain both outward and inward respect.

**Outer etiquette.** The disciple does not argue with the guide; if an argument arises and the disciple thinks the guide has overlooked something, he does not raise an objection. The disciple does not remain stationed at the place of prayer at all times; rather, when the time for prayer arrives, he goes to the place of prayer, performs the prayer, and then rolls up the prayer mat and leaves. The disciple does not perform lengthy supererogatory prayers in front of the Shaykh (الشيخ) after each obligatory prayer. Whatever the perfected guide commands, the disciple obeys to the best of his ability — *with one absolute boundary: a true spiritual guide cannot and will not command a disciple to do anything forbidden by the* Shari'ah (شريعة) *(the law of Allah). False impostors who lack these qualities, by contrast, may command their disciples to do things that are Haram (الحرام).* The boundary is not a guideline; it is the diagnostic that separates the master from the impostor.

**Inner etiquette.** Whatever the disciple hears outwardly from the *Murshid*, he must not entertain even the slightest doubt in the heart regarding the Shaykh's words or deeds — otherwise the disciple will be counted among the hypocrites. *If the disciple cannot maintain this inner sincerity, then he should withdraw from the company of the spiritual guide until the inward state comes into harmony with the outward.* Inward and outward must agree. To be in the master's company while doubting him in the heart is not respect; it is hypocrisy, and the right response is honest withdrawal.

## The sixth and seventh conditions

The sixth condition is that the disciple guards against the deceptions of the *Nafs (نفس)* — the lower self, an inward subtle presence invisible to the outward eye yet clearly perceptible to one possessing inner sight, which commands a person to evil by whispering into the heart. This is only possible if the seeker abandons the gatherings of the ill-charactered and the ignorant. In this way, the dominion of Shaytan (شيطان) over the heart comes to an end, and all Satanic influences are uprooted. Ghazali notes that this Satanic company may come from among humankind or from among the *Jinn (الجن)* (jinn (الجن) — an invisible creation of God made from fire, to whose kind Satan belongs and from whom he recruits his followers). The remedy is concrete and social, not abstract: *change the room you sit in.*

The seventh condition is that in every circumstance, the seeker should prefer humility and freedom from greed over wealth, and should embrace contentment and satisfaction with the Will of Allah. *These seven conditions are essential for every traveler on the Path of Allah and for every seeker of truth.* The full catalog is now on the table.

## The inner vocabulary of the Path

Ghazali now answers four questions the disciple had asked. He answers each with one verbatim definition. The brevity is the point: the inner life is not a long curriculum; it is four named realities, and the seeker either lives them or does not.

**Reality of Tasawwuf (تصوف).** *You have asked: what is* Tasawwuf (تصوف) *(what is broadly called Sufism, the inner science of purifying the soul)? Tasawwuf (تصوف) is defined by two qualities. The first is that the servant should be obedient to Allah — acting in accordance with the Shari'ah (شريعة), the outward laws of Islam encompassing obligations, commandments, and prohibitions. The second is that the seeker should be compassionate and a doer of good toward all of Allah's creation, which is not possible without first purifying one's soul of all evil qualities and then adorning it with all noble ones. One who perseveres in acting upon the Shari'ah (شريعة) and who genuinely desires the betterment and true success of all humanity is rightly called a* Sufi (صوفي) *— and this is the correct application of the term, as opposed to the distorted usages so widespread today. Loyalty and obedience to Allah means the seeker sacrifices personal happiness for the sake of Allah. Sincerity toward all people means the seeker does not maintain relationships for personal motives, but is selfless and performs selfless service, free from self-interest — though the condition is that this welfare must be in accordance with the Shari'ah (شريعة).* Two qualities; one outward, one toward others. No mystique. No private signs. The Shari'ah (شريعة) holds the perimeter and good toward the creation fills the interior.

**Reality of servitude.** *Servitude consists of three things. First is obedience to the commandments of the Shari'ah (شريعة). Second is contentment with the decree and destiny appointed by Allah. Third is the abandonment of one's own desires and claims, and the acceptance of — and happiness with — the Will and Power of Allah.* Three again: do what He has commanded; accept what He has decreed; let the *I want* dissolve into the *He wills*.

**Reality of Tawakkul (توكل).** *You have also asked: what is reliance on God —* Tawakkul (توكل)*? Tawakkul (توكل) means holding firm belief and certitude in what Allah has promised. In other words, one believes with conviction that whatever Allah has decreed for you, you will surely receive — and if the entire world were to conspire to prevent it, it could not be prevented. But for that which Allah has not written in your destiny, no amount of effort by you or by the entire world combined will ever bring it to you.* Tawakkul (توكل) is not a feeling; it is a settled conviction about what is and is not possible. The decree is the perimeter; effort lives inside it.

**Reality of Ikhlas (إخلاص).** *You have also asked: what is sincerity to God —* Ikhlas (إخلاص) *? Ikhlas (إخلاص) means that all of your deeds are exclusively for the sake of Allah and His pleasure. Whatever you do should not be for show or ostentation —* Riya (الرياء) *. When you perform good deeds, your heart should not lean toward impressing people or seeking their praise. Your heart should neither be gladdened by people's admiration nor grieved by their criticism. Riya (الرياء) is born from the praises and honors of people. The cure for the spiritual disease of Riya (الرياء) is to regard the entire universe as subject to the Power and Control of Allah, and to consider all created things as equivalent to pebbles and stones — understanding that stones possess no power to bring you either grief or comfort. If you truly regard all creation in this light, you will be freed from Riya (الرياء). As long as you believe that mortal created beings have the power to cause you grief or bring you comfort, Riya (الرياء) will never depart from your heart.* The cure has the same precision as a physician's prescription. The disease is metaphysical (a wrong estimate of where power lives); the cure is metaphysical (re-estimate). Until that estimate is corrected, the symptom returns.

## "Do not seek the answer except through the tongue of your heart"

The chapter closes with one of the gentlest and most stringent passages in the letter. *My dear beloved son! Your remaining questions are such that some have been addressed in books I have written, which you may consult, while others are of a nature that writing their answers is not permissible. So take what is written here and steadily put it into practice — and through that practice, those matters which you do not yet understand will become clear to you.*

The answer is not refused. The answer is *reassigned* — from the page to the practice. Some answers are written elsewhere; some answers refuse the page entirely; the way to know which is which, and the way to receive the latter, is to act on what is already in hand.

Ghazali then stacks three Quranic verses on the same point.

*Wa Lau Annahum Sabaru Hatta Takhruja Ilaeyhim Lakana Khairan Lahum.* — *"Had those people exercised patience — instead of clamoring outside your house — until you, O Prophet, came out yourself, it would have been better for them."* (Quran 49:5)

Accept the counsel of *Khidr (الخضر)* (the wise traveler who taught Prophet Moses in the well-known Quranic story), peace be upon him:

*Fala Tas-alni 'Un Shaeyin Hatta Uhdithu Laka Minhu Zikra.* — *"If you wish to follow me, O Moses, then do not question me about anything until I myself bring it up and explain it to you."* (Quran 18:70)

*Sa Urikum Ayate Fala Tusta'jiloon.* — *"We — Allah — will soon show you Our signs, so do not seek to hasten this matter."*

The three together draw a single line. Do not clamor; do not interrogate ahead of the right moment; do not demand the signs before the time the signs are given. *Do not ask before the right time arrives. When you reach that spiritually elevated stage, you will see it yourself. Therefore, hold firm to this certainty: unless you set out toward that destination, you will neither arrive there nor be able to see what awaits.*

*Awalum Yaseeru Fil Ardi Fayanzuru.* — *"Have they not traveled the earth so that they might see?"*

The verb is *travel*. Not read about traveling, not ask after travelers, not dispute the geography. *Travel.* And then, the soft hammer at the end: *By Allah! If you illuminate your heart — purifying it by driving out the darkness of evil — you will surely behold wondrous things. Strive with your utmost at every step along the way. Without that striving, the purpose will not be achieved.*

Ghazali seals the threshold with a saying of Shaykh (الشيخ) Zun Noon Misri (زن نون مصري) (a great early Egyptian master, may Allah shower His mercy upon him), spoken to one of his own students:

*In Qadarta 'Ala Bazlir Ruhi Fata'ala Wa Illa Tashtaghil Bi Nazahatis Sufiyyati Wal Qaal.* — *"If you have the courage to exert your utmost with your soul in this way, then come and step onto this path — otherwise do not merely occupy yourself with the beautiful sayings of the Sufis."*

That line is the exact echo of Junaid's dream — *beautiful sayings without a soul actually offered are nothing* — restated here as an instruction to the living. Beautiful sayings, by themselves, are nothing. The path is the soul actually offered. If the disciple cannot offer that, the right response is not to refine the sayings — it is to stop pretending the sayings are the path.

The disciple now has the working rule of life in his hand. Eight inward principles, distilled by Hatim from long looking; the master at the center, with his qualifications, his right to obedience, and the bright line of the Shari'ah (شريعة) past which his commands cannot pass; the inner conditions of Nafs-discipline, humble company, and contentment; the four named realities of Tasawwuf (تصوف), servitude, Tawakkul (توكل), and Ikhlas (إخلاص); and the closing instruction that the rest of the answers will come through practice, not through asking. *My dear beloved son! I will now bring my explanation to a close with eight admonitions.* The eight admonitions — and the supplication the master will hand his disciple to carry — are what remains of the letter.
