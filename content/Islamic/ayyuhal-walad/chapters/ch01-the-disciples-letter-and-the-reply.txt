# The Disciple's Letter and the Reply

*This chapter covers the opening of Imam al-Ghazali's (Rahmatullahi Alayhe — rah-ma-tul-LAH-hi a-LAY-hi — may Allah shower His mercy upon him) letter known as* Ayyuhal Walad *(AY-yu-hal WA-lad — "O Beloved Son"), preserved in the three-volume collection* Majmu'a Rasail Imam Ghazali *(maj-MOO-ah ra-SAH-il — "The Collected Letters of Imam Ghazali"). The book begins with the framing narrative of a long-time disciple who, after many years of study under the Imam, found a question weighing on his heart — which of the many sciences he had been taught would actually benefit him on the Day of Judgment, illuminate his grave, and save him from the Fire. He wrote a letter to his master asking for a counsel he could continually study and consistently act upon, together with a supplication he could recite at all times. Imam Ghazali (RA — Rahmatullahi Alayhe) replies. The reply opens with the Imam's most piercing thesis — that the foundation of all good counsel is the Ahadith (a-HA-deeth — traditions) and Sunnah (SUN-nah — outward conduct and inner states) of the Holy Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), and that any counsel contrary to them is of no benefit. It then drives, layer by layer, into the diagnosis at the heart of the whole letter: knowledge without action is madness, and action without knowledge is worthless. The Imam works the diagnosis through the Prophet's warnings, the Quran's verdicts, the saints' confessions, the vignettes of Junaid Baghdadi (ju-NAYD bagh-DAA-dee) seen in a dream and Hasan al-Basri (HA-san al-BAS-ree) collapsing over a sweet drink, the Injil's (in-JEEL — the Gospel of Prophet Isa) forty questions at the casket, the four-angel announcement at the four watches of the night, Luqman's (LUQ-maan) testament to his son on the rooster, the four conditions for the seeker, and Shibli's (SHIB-lee) confession that of four thousand Ahadith he had heard from four hundred masters, he had adopted only one — the saying that work for the world should match the time you will live in it, work for the Hereafter the time you will live there, work for Allah the measure of your need of Him, and work against the Fire the measure of what you can bear of its pain. The chapter closes at the threshold of the long Hatim-Ism narrative — the eight benefits a disciple distilled out of thirty-three years of his master's company — which the next chapter will receive.*

## Where this chapter picks up

This is the opening of the book. *Ayyuhal Walad* (AY-yu-hal WA-lad — "O Beloved Son") is one of the shortest and most widely transmitted of the works of Imam al-Ghazali (RA), preserved in the three-volume collection *Majmu'a Rasail Imam Ghazali* (maj-MOO-ah ra-SAH-il — "The Collected Letters") that gathers the shorter treatises he composed late in his life. Unlike the *Ihya al-Ulum ad-Din* (ih-YAH al-oo-LOOM ad-DEEN — *Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God*), which fills four large volumes, *Ayyuhal Walad* is a single sustained letter — perhaps fifty pages in print — written to one of the Imam's own disciples in answer to a question the disciple had carried unspoken for years.

The frame the editor sets at the head of the book gives the reason for the letter. The disciple had spent many years studying with Imam Ghazali (RA), acquiring great quantities of knowledge in many branches — theology, jurisprudence, the sciences of the heart, and the rest — and making great sacrifices for the sake of that study. And yet he found himself, after all of it, unable to answer a simple question: of all that he had learned, *which* branches would actually benefit him on the Day of Judgment, illuminate his grave, and stand as a witness for him before Allah, and which would not — so that he might leave them aside. For the Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him) had said:

> A'auzu Billahi Min 'Ilm-il La Yanfa'au.
> I seek Allah's refuge from knowledge that is of no benefit.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (a-OO-zu bil-LAH-hi min IL-mil la yan-FA-oo)

After pondering this question for several days, the disciple wrote a letter to Imam Ghazali (RA). He set out the matters that had been weighing on him; he asked for personal counsel; and he asked for a single supplication that he might recite at all times. He noted in the letter that the Imam had already authored many books on these subjects — the *Ihya al-Ulum ad-Din* (ih-YAH al-oo-LOOM ad-DEEN — *Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God*), the *Kimiya as-Sa'ada* (kee-mee-YAH as-sa-AH-dah — *Alchemy of Ultimate Success and Happiness*), the *Jawahir ul-Quran* (ja-WAH-hir ul-qur-AAN — *Priceless Gems of the Holy Quran*), the *Arba'een* (ar-ba-EEN — *Forty Steps*), and the *Minhaj ul-'Abideen* (min-HAAJ ul-aa-bi-DEEN — *The Best Way for the Worshippers of God Leading Them to the Paradise of the Lord of All Universes*) — but that he was looking for something he could continually study and consistently act upon. *Ayyuhal Walad* is the reply.

The chapter we walk now is the first long stretch of that reply. It runs from the Imam's opening salutation — *My dear beloved son and true friend!* — through his sustained working of a single thesis: that knowledge without action is madness, and action without knowledge is worthless. The Imam states the thesis early; works it through the Prophet's warnings, the saints' confessions, the Quranic verdicts, and a series of vignettes that hold the same picture from many sides; and ends the long opening at the threshold of a narrative whose actual unfolding is held for the next chapter — the story of Hatim Ism (HAA-tim ISM), the disciple of Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (sha-FEEQ al-BAL-khee), who claimed to have distilled the entirety of thirty-three years of his master's teaching into *eight benefits*. The cliffhanger is deliberate. The diagnosis of the disease comes here; the practical regimen of the cure begins there.

## The Imam's opening counsel — and the first piercing question

The Imam opens his reply in the manner of every classical letter:

> In the Name of Allah, the Most Compassionate and the Most Merciful. All praises belong to Allah alone, the Lord of all the universes, and blessings and peace of Allah be upon His Messenger Muhammad and upon all of his descendants and followers.

He addresses the disciple — *My dear beloved son and true friend!* — with the warmth that the title of the book will preserve as its name across the centuries. And then he sets the doctrinal frame on which the whole letter will run. May Allah grant you a long life in His worship, the Imam writes; may He enable you to walk on the path of His beloveds. *You should know that the fountain of all good counsel is the holy existence of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, for the foundation of all counsel rests upon his Ahadith and Sunnah. Any counsel contrary to his Ahadith and Sunnah will be of no benefit.*

It is a precise opening. The Imam is not claiming the authority of his own pen. He is locating the source of all counsel in the Prophet ﷺ. Countless accounts of advice, he continues, have been written and conveyed in the light of the Prophet's Ahadith and Sunnah. If you have received guidance from those, then you have no need of any counsel from me. And then he turns the question back onto the disciple in the form that breaks open the entire letter: *if you have not received benefit from the counsels of the Prophet ﷺ, then tell me — in all these years, what knowledge have you truly acquired from me?*

The cut is sharp. The disciple has come with a question about knowledge, and the master has answered, before any of the substantive teaching, with a counter-question: if the Prophet's ﷺ counsels have not already saved you, what would *mine* save you with? It is the question that opens up the rest of the book — because the answer the disciple needs is not a new body of knowledge but a different *relation* to knowledge he already has.

## The Prophet's two warnings — preoccupation and the forty-year threshold

Having set the frame, the Imam quotes the Prophet ﷺ on the two registers that will run through everything that follows. The first is the warning on *preoccupation with the useless*:

> 'Alamatu I'radil Allahi 'Anil 'Abdi Ishtighaluhu Bima La Ya'nehe, Wa Inn Imra'un Zahabat Sa'atuhu Min 'Umrihi Fi Ghayri Ma Yukhlaq Lahu Lahariyyun Ayyatula 'Alayhe Hasratun.
> A servant's preoccupation with useless matters is a sign that Allah has withdrawn His special favor from him or her. And if even a single moment of a servant's life passes in pursuit of something other than that for which Allah created him or her, then that is a matter of profound sorrow.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (a-LAA-ma-tu i-RAA-dil al-LAH-hi a-nil AB-di ish-ti-GHAA-lu-hu bi-ma la yaa-NEE-hi)

The second is the warning attached to the fortieth year of life:

> Mun Jaawazal Arba'eena Sinatu Wa Lam Yughlab Khayruhu 'Ala Sharrihi Fal Yatajahhaza Ilan Nar.
> An individual whose condition, even after forty years of age, is such that the good within him or her does not prevail over the evil, should prepare to go to Hell.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (mun jaa-wa-ZAL ar-ba-EE-na si-NA-tu)

The Imam pauses on the two warnings before pressing on. *My dear beloved son! It is very easy to give this advice to all the people of the world, yet very difficult to act upon it oneself.* Those in whose hearts the pleasures of the world and the desires of the lower self hold dominion find such advice bitter. The hardest case, he notes — the case that the rest of the letter will keep returning to — is the case of *the man who acquires wisdom, philosophy, and worldly learning, and then imagines that this knowledge will be his means of salvation in the Hereafter while also bringing fame and prestige in this world.* Such a person, the Imam writes, thinks he is exempt from acting on what he has learned. He does not even realize that the knowledge acquired and left unacted upon will itself become a cause of punishment on the Day of Judgment. The Prophet ﷺ has said:

> Inna Ashaddan Nasi 'Azaban Yawmal Qiyamati 'Alimun Lam Yanfa'hul Allahu Bi 'Ilmihi.
> On the Day of Judgment, the most severe torment will be visited upon the scholar whom Allah did not benefit through his or her knowledge — that is, one who did not act upon what was acquired.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (in-na ASH-sha-dan NAA-si a-ZAA-ban)

The hadith is the Imam's first and sharpest cut at the disciple's diagnosis. The harshest punishment on the Day of Judgment is not for the unlearned, but for the scholar who studied and did not act. The disciple, in writing his letter, has placed himself on exactly this knife-edge. The whole book turns on what he does with it.

## Junaid Baghdadi in the dream — what availed at the end

The Imam turns now from the Prophet's ﷺ word to the witness of one of the masters of the inner sciences, Shaykh Junaid Baghdadi (RA — ju-NAYD bagh-DAA-dee), the early master of the path who was kunya'd Abu al-Qasim. *It has been narrated in the accounts of the intimate friends of Allah,* the Imam writes, *that a certain saint saw Shaykh Junaid Baghdadi (RA) in a dream after his death and asked him: "O Abu al-Qasim! Tell me of your state after death."*

Shaykh Junaid (RA) gave a reply that the Imam holds up as one of the chapter's anchors. *All discourses and spiritual signs (Isharaat — i-sha-RAAT) turned out to be of no avail. But certain cycles (Rak'at — RAK-at) of prayer I had performed during the Tahajjud (ta-HAJ-jud — the third portion of the night) were of benefit.*

The vignette is doing exact work for the Imam's argument. Junaid Baghdadi (RA) was not a man short on *Isharaat* — he was one of the masters of them; his discourses, preserved in fragments by his students, were treated as touchstones of the inner sciences for centuries after his death. And yet what Junaid (RA) himself reports from beyond death is that *the discourses did not avail him*; what availed him was *the Rak'at of Tahajjud he had actually performed*. The hierarchy is severe. Knowledge of the path did not save the master of knowledge of the path. The *cycles he had actually prayed in the last third of the night* did.

The Imam draws the rule out. *My dear beloved son! Do not deprive yourself of good deeds, do not neglect the knowledge of outward matters — Shari'ah (sha-REE-ah — the Divine Law) — and do not be without inner excellences and noble qualities of the heart. Be firmly convinced that knowledge alone, without righteous deeds, will not benefit you on the Day of Judgment.*

## The warrior with the sharp swords; the sick man with the medicine

To press the rule, the Imam offers two parables. The first is the warrior. Consider, he writes, a person passing through a jungle or a desert carrying ten sharp swords, fine bows, and various other weapons, and possessing full knowledge of the art of war and the use of those weapons — and then a lion confronts him. Can that person be saved without actually using the weapons? Of course not. *Likewise, understand that if someone knows a hundred thousand points of jurisprudence (Fiqh — FIQH) but does not act on them, that knowledge will be of no benefit.*

The second is the sick man. Consider a sick person who knows that his cure lies in such and such medicines, yet does not take them. Will the knowledge of those medicines and their healing properties actually cure the illness? You can see quite clearly that merely possessing the knowledge of a remedy cannot cure one's spiritual or inner diseases. As a poet has said, the Imam adds — *even if you purchase two thousand pounds of wine, you will not become intoxicated until you drink from it.*

The two parables hold the same line at two different ends of life. In the desert, with the lion bearing down, swords-on-the-shoulder do not save the warrior; only the sword *raised and used* saves him. In sickness, the prescription written on the page does not heal the patient; only the medicine *taken into the body* heals him. The intermediate condition — possessing the knowledge, not doing the act — is in the warrior's case death, and in the sick man's case continued sickness. In neither case is the *knowledge itself* a fault. The fault is in stopping at it.

## The Quranic anchor — and what to do with abrogation

The Imam now turns to the Quran. He begins with the verse that the disciple may himself have learned and may know to have a complicated history in the tradition:

> Wa Anna Laysa Lil Insani Illa Ma Sa'a.
> Human beings will attain only what they have striven for.
> — Quran (wa AN-na LAY-sa lil in-SAA-ni il-la ma SA-a)

The Imam handles the disciple's anticipated objection directly. *I am aware,* he writes, *that you may have read that this verse was abrogated. Let us grant that it was — but then what do you say about the following two verses?* He produces a small chain of further verses that the abrogation question cannot reach:

> Faman Ya'mal Mithqala Zarratin Khayran Yarahu, Wa Man Ya'mal Mithqala Zarratin Sharran Yarahu.
> And so, whoever shall have done an atom's weight of good shall see it, and whoever shall have done an atom's weight of evil shall see it.
> — Quran 99:7–8 (fa-man YA-mal MITH-qa-la zar-RA-tin)

> Faman Kana Yarjul Liqa'a Rabbihi Fal Ya'mal 'Amalan Salihan.
> Hence, whoever looks forward with hope and awe to meeting his or her Lord on the Day of Judgment, let him or her perform righteous deeds, and let him or her not ascribe unto anyone or anything a share in the worship due to the Lord.
> — Quran 18:110 (fa-man KAA-na YAR-ju li-QAA-a RAB-bi-hi)

> Innal Ladhina Amanu Wa 'Amilus Salihati Kanat Lahum Jannatul Firdawsi Nuzulan Khalidina Fiha.
> Verily, those who believe in Allah and perform righteous deeds — for them the highest Paradise shall be a welcome abode in which they will dwell forever.
> — Quran (in-nal la-DHEE-na aa-MA-nu wa a-MI-lus saa-li-HAA-ti)

> Illa Man Taba Wa Amana Wa 'Amila 'Amalan Salihan.
> Except for those who repented from their sins and wrongdoings, and believed, and performed righteous deeds.
> — Quran (il-la man TAA-ba wa aa-MA-na wa a-MI-la a-MA-lan SAA-li-han)

And then the two anchoring Ahadith on the architecture of Islam and Iman (ee-MAAN — faith) themselves:

> Buniyal Islamu 'Ala Khams: Shahadatu An La Ilaha IllAllahu Wa Anna Muhammadan Rasulullahi, Wa Iqamus Salati, Wa Ita'iz Zakati, Wa Sawmu Shahri Ramadana, Wa Hijjul Bayti Manistataa'a Ilayhi Sabila.
> Islam is built upon five things: to testify that there is none worthy of worship other than Allah and that Muhammad ﷺ is His Messenger; to establish regular prayers (Salat — sa-LAAT); to pay the Zakat (za-KAAT — obligatory charity); to fast during the holy month of Ramadan (ra-ma-DAAN); and to perform Hajj (HAJJ — pilgrimage to the House of God) for those who possess the means to do so.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (bu-ni-YAL is-LAA-mu a-la KHAMS)

> Al Imanu Iqraarun Bil Lisani Wa Tasdiqun Bil Janani Wa 'Amalun Bil Arkani.
> Iman (faith) is to testify with the tongue, to affirm with the heart, and to act upon the teachings of Islam.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (al ee-MAA-nu iq-RAA-run bil li-SAA-ni)

The Imam pauses at this point to head off the most likely misreading of his own argument. *After this explanation,* he writes, *should a thought arise in your heart that I am saying a servant of God will enter Paradise not by Allah's Grace but solely by his or her own deeds — know that you have not understood my meaning.* The correction is precise. A servant enters Paradise *by Allah's Favor, Generosity, and Blessings*. The deeds are not the *price* of Paradise; they are the *qualification* by which the servant comes within reach of the Mercy. And the proof of that, the Imam writes, is not his own assertion. It is Allah's own word:

> Inna Rahmata Allahi Qaribun Minal Muhsinin.
> Verily, the Mercy of Allah is close to the doers of good.
> — Quran (in-na rah-MA-tal al-LAH-hi QA-ri-bun mi-nal muh-si-NEEN)

*How, then,* the Imam asks, *will a servant enter Paradise without the Mercy of Allah, the Exalted? I repeat this important point: how will a servant enter Paradise without Allah's Mercy?* And he heads off the second misreading just as sharply. If someone argues, *fine — then we enter Paradise on the strength of Iman alone* — the Imam responds that even that path is hedged with what he calls *formidable valleys*, the first and most daunting of which is *to carry one's faith safely to the end*. Iman is not a deposit one makes once and recalls later in full. Iman is a thing one carries — and that carrying is itself the work.

## The Israelite and the angel — a story about why we work even if the work seems refused

The Imam now turns to a story from the people of Israel that he uses to settle a particular doubt — the doubt that says, *if Allah is the one who saves and the one who decrees, then what is the point of my striving?* The story is brief, and the Imam tells it directly.

There was a man among the Children of Israel who worshipped Allah with great devotion. Allah, in His wisdom, sent an angel to him as a test. The angel said:

> Allah, the Holy, says that you have been tormenting yourself to no purpose, for your worship is not accepted by Him and you will go to Hell.

The worshipper heard the message; and his reply did not turn on whether the report was true. *Allah, the Exalted, created us for His worship and servitude,* he said, *and our duty is to serve Him — no matter what. He is the Owner of all things and all people, including myself, and He is the All-Powerful.*

The angel returned to the Presence of Allah and reported what the servant had said. And Allah, the Exalted, said:

> Since this servant of Ours does not turn his face away from Our worship and servitude, We too will not turn Our Face away from him.

And then the verdict was sealed:

> Ishhadu Ya Mala'ikati Anni Qad Ghafartu Lahu.
> O My angels! Bear witness, all of you, that I have forgiven him.
> — In the canonical accounts of the people of Israel (ish-HA-du ya ma-LAA-i-ka-ti)

The point of the vignette is the reply of the worshipper. The worshipper did not say, *I have rights against Allah*. He said, *worship is my duty regardless of the outcome*. The acceptance was given to the man who had already decided he would worship whether or not it was accepted. The story is the Imam's correction of the most refined form of the disciple's question — the form that says, *if the result is in His hands anyway, why must I work?* The answer is that the work is not the price of the result. The work is what makes the worker into the kind of worker on whom the result can fall.

## The chorus of the saints — Ali, Hasan al-Basri, Junaid, and the Prophet's eloquence

The Imam now gathers a chorus of voices, each of whom names the same balance from a different angle. He begins with the Father of Imams (may Allah honor his countenance):

> Man Zanna Annahu Bidunl Juhdi Yasilu Fahuwa Mutmanniun, Wa Man Zanna Annahu Bibazlil Juhdi Yasilu Fahuwa Mut'ibun.
> A person who thinks he or she will reach Paradise without righteous striving is self-deluded, and a person who thinks he or she will reach Paradise through striving alone is merely exhausting himself or herself.
> — The Father of Imams (may Allah honor his countenance), in the canonical collections of his sayings (man ZAN-na an-NA-hu bi-DU-nil JUH-di ya-SI-lu)

The aphorism is calibrated. It names *both* errors at once. One who imagines reward without striving has miscounted; one who imagines reward by striving alone has miscounted in the opposite direction. The middle is the position the whole letter is calling the disciple toward — striving *as the qualification by which the Mercy is received*.

The Imam then quotes Shaykh Hasan al-Basri (RA — HA-san al-BAS-ree), the great early master of Basra:

> Talabul Jannati Bila 'Amalin Zambun Minaz Zunub.
> To desire Paradise without good deeds is itself a sin among the sins.
> — Shaykh Hasan al-Basri (RA) (ta-LA-bul jan-NA-ti bi-la a-MA-lin)

And then another saying of one of the intimate friends of Allah:

> Al Haqiqatu Terkul Mulahazatil 'Amali La Terkul 'Amal.
> The reality is that one should act without admiring one's own actions — but one should never abandon the performance of good deeds, even if there is a risk of self-admiration.
> — One of the intimate friends of Allah (al ha-QEE-qa-tu TER-kul mu-la-ha-ZA-til)

The saying is precise on a fine spiritual hazard. The danger of self-admiration in the performance of one's deeds is real; but the response to that danger is not to *abandon* the deeds — that would be a worse error — but to *abandon the admiration of them*. Drop the regard; keep the act.

And then the Imam ends the chorus by going back to the Prophet ﷺ, whose word, he says, expresses the same truth more eloquently than any of the saints' formulations:

> Al Kayyisu Man Dana Nafsahu Wa 'Amila Lima Ba'dal Mawt, Wal Ahmaqu Man Ittaba'a Nafsahu Hawaha Wa Tamanna 'Alal Allahi.
> In another narration: Wa Tamanna 'Alal Allahil Maghfira.
> Intelligent is the one who subdues his or her nafs (NAFS — lower self, with its evil desires and inclinations) and performs righteous deeds for the life to come. Mindless — that is, foolish — is the one who surrenders his or her nafs to pleasures, greed, and vain desires, and yet supposes that Allah is with him or her. In some traditions: and hopes for Allah's forgiveness.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (al KAY-yi-su man DAA-na naf-SA-hu)

The hadith is the Prophet's ﷺ verdict on the disciple's exact case — the case of the man who has acquired knowledge, has not subdued his Nafs, and yet imagines that knowledge has placed him under Allah's special care. The Prophet ﷺ calls that hope by its name: *foolishness*.

## Woe or blessing — the formula at the hinge

The Imam now puts the disciple at the hinge directly. *My dear beloved son!* he writes. *You have stayed awake at night rehearsing and memorizing knowledge, and you have sacrificed sleep in its study.* The factual record is given to the disciple as he himself would have given it — long nights of memorizing and rehearsing. The question is the *intention* under the work. If the intention was worldly benefit and worldly prestige and status, then:

> Fawaylun Laka Thumma Waylun Lak.
> Woe unto you, and again woe unto you.
> — Quranic register (fa-WAY-lun LA-ka thum-ma WAY-lun LAK)

But if the intention was *to establish and strengthen within yourself — and then within others — the Path to God (Deen — DEEN) brought by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, to attain moral uplift and spiritual civilization, and to eliminate evil from your soul,* then:

> Fatuba Laka Thumma Tuba Laka.
> For you is happiness and blessings, and again for you is happiness and blessings.
> — Quranic register (fa-TOO-ba LA-ka thum-ma TOO-ba LA-ka)

The same act, the same long nights of study, can fall on either side of the hinge. What decides which side it falls on is not the act; it is the intention under the act.

The Imam supports the verdict with two short pieces of poetry from the early Arabic tradition. The first is the prayer of a lover:

> Sahrul 'Uyuna Bighairi Wajhika Sana ay'un
> Wa Baka uhunna Bighairi Faqdika Batilun
> O my Lord! The wakefulness of the eyes, except for beholding the beauty of Your Countenance, is useless. And unless it is for the sorrow of separation from You, the weeping of eyes over anything or anyone else is gravely wrong.

The wakefulness of the eyes is only honored when its object is Allah; the weeping of the eyes is only honored when its sorrow is separation from Him. The lover's complaint is the perfect form of the Imam's question — every act, including the act of staying awake to study, is rated by *what it is for*.

And then a Hadith Qudsi-register saying that names the same rule in three commands the Prophet ﷺ delivered as a single block:

> 'Ishu Ma Shay'ta Fa Innaka Mayyeytu Wahbib Ma Shay'ta Fa Innaka Mufariqatun Wa'mil Ma Shay'ta Fa Innaka Tujza Bihi.
> O human being! Spend your life however you wish, but know that one day you will die. Love whoever you wish, but know that one day you will be separated from them. Perform deeds however you wish, but know that one day you will surely receive their just recompense — good or bad, according to what you have done.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (i-SHU ma SHAY-ta)

The three lines hold a single shape: *live as you will — but you will die; love as you will — but you will part; act as you will — but you will be repaid.* The acts are *yours to choose* and *not yours to keep*. The Imam is bringing the disciple back to the only honest position: choose accordingly.

## The questions at the casket — and the address of the heart

The Imam now widens the camera. *What benefit have you derived for yourself,* he asks the disciple, *and what have you truly gained from studying the various fields of knowledge — theology (Ilmul Kalaam — il-mul ka-LAAM), ethics (Ilmul Ikhlaq — il-mul ikh-LAAQ), medicine (Ilmut Tib — il-mut TIBB), astrology (Nujoom — nu-JOOM), linguistics, poetry, the science of warfare, and the like? What have you obtained beyond a wasted life and worldly renown?*

He goes to a passage he says he has read in the Injil (in-JEEL — the Gospel of Prophet Isa — Jesus, peace of Allah be upon him). The passage describes the moment between the placing of a corpse in the casket and the carrying of it to the grave. In that interval, the Imam reports from the Injil, Allah the Exalted addresses the corpse with *forty questions* by His Power. The first of these is the question on which the Imam's argument turns:

> 'Abdi Qad Tahherta Munzaral Khalqi Sineena Fahul Tahherta Munzari Sa'atun.
> O My slave! For many years you adorned and beautified yourself with outward knowledge to display before My mortal creation. But for My sake alone, did you ever purify your heart from heedlessness and evil — even for a single moment?
> — The Voice of Allah at the casket, as recorded in the Injil of Prophet Isa, peace of Allah be upon him (AB-di qad tah-HAR-ta)

The Imam pauses on the form of the question. Allah is not asking, in that first of the forty, about books read or arguments mastered. He is asking about *the heart purified — for His sake alone — even for a single moment*. The Imam reaches back into the Prophet's ﷺ own hadith to underline the same axis. The Prophet ﷺ said in an authentic tradition: *Allah does not look at your faces or your bodies, but He looks at your hearts.* Therefore, the Imam writes, it is of the utmost importance to purify the heart, which is forever within Allah's sight.

And then he names the address of Allah to the heart that — the Imam says — sounds every day from within:

> 'Abdi Ma Tasna'u Bighairi Wa Anta Majfufum Bikhairi.
> O My slave! Why do you worship for show and ostentation, when both good and difficult circumstances are in My Hands alone? Worship Me with sincere intention and devotion — not to impress any of My mortal creation, who cannot benefit or harm you in the slightest except by My Will.
> — The Voice of Allah within the heart of His servant (AB-di ma tas-NA-u bi-GHAY-ri)

## The thesis at its sharpest — and the cry from the Fire

The Imam now states the thesis at its sharpest pitch. *Knowledge without action is madness, and action without knowledge is worthless.* The two halves are held in absolute balance. *The knowledge that does not keep you away from sins today, and does not kindle within you the desire to obey Allah, will not save you from Hellfire tomorrow.*

And then he gives the cry of the regretful from the Fire on the Day of Judgment:

> Ferje'yna Na'mal Saaliha.
> Send us back, O our Lord, so that we may perform righteous deeds!
> — Quranic register (fer-ji-NAA na-MAL saa-LI-ha)

The reply that meets that cry is brutally simple. *O heedless one! That is precisely where you have come from.* The place to which the cry asks to return is the very place the cryer wasted. The cry will not be granted because the time has already been given.

The Imam draws the immediate practical conclusion. *Summon courage within yourself and stir your body to strive upon the Path of God. Make every effort to perform pious deeds, for you are journeying toward the grave. Those who preceded you in the grave are waiting for you at every moment, watching for when you will join them. Beware — do not approach them empty-handed, without the fruit of good deeds.*

Then he produces the famous formula of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (a-BOO BAKR as-SID-deeq — may Allah be pleased with him):

> Hazihil Ajsaadu Qafasut Tuyouri Awo Astabalud Dawab.
> These bodies are either cages of birds or stables of animals.
> — Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (may Allah be pleased with him) (ha-ZI-hil aj-SAA-du qa-FA-sut tu-YOO-ri)

The image is exact. The body is either the *cage of a bird* — and when the cage is opened at death, the bird inside answers the call *Irji'I ila Rabbika* (ir-JI-ee i-la RAB-bi-ka — *Return to your Lord!*) and flies to the loftiest heights — or it is *the stable of an animal*, whose burdens at death travel *from the place of the zawiya (ZAA-wi-ya — spiritual retreat) straight to the place of hawiya (HAA-wi-ya — the abyss of Hell)*. The two destinies are not symmetrical; the bird soars, the beast is hauled. *Reflect, then, on which of the two you inhabit.* The first kind of body is described in the moment of the death of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh (SA-ad ib-ne MU-aadh), at which the Throne of Allah itself trembled:

> Ihtuzza 'Arshur Rahmaani Limauti Sa'adibne Muadh.
> The Throne of the Most Compassionate shook at the death of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (ih-TUZ-za AR-shur rah-MAA-ni)

The second is described in the verse that the Imam writes (*God forbid*) that the disciple should fall into:

> Uulaaika Kal An'aami Bal Hum Adallu.
> They are like cattle — nay, even more astray.
> — Quran (oo-LAA-i-ka kal an-AA-mi)

## Hasan al-Basri's sigh — the cold drink and the pleading in the Fire

The Imam adds one more vignette to the chapter's gallery of confessions. Once, he writes, Shaykh Hasan al-Basri (RA) was handed a cold, sweet drink. The moment he took the cup in his hand, he let out a deep sigh and fell unconscious. When he regained consciousness, the people around him asked what had come over him. He replied:

> Zakartu Umniyyata Ahlin Naari Heena Yaquluna Le Ahlil Jannati Un Uufidu 'Aleyna Minal Ma'i.
> I remembered the plea of the inhabitants of Hell when they will beseech the inhabitants of Paradise to pour upon them even a little water.
> — Shaykh Hasan al-Basri (RA) (za-KAR-tu um-niy-YA-ta AH-lin NAA-ri)

The detail is the cold sweet drink in his hand. The man had not been delivered a sermon; he had been handed a refreshment. And the sight of the refreshment cracked him open onto the future moment in which a drop of water will be the most precious thing the inhabitants of Paradise can give and the inhabitants of the Fire can beg for. The everyday object — the cup — became, in the saint's perception, the door to a vision that knocked him to the floor.

## The night and its four announcements — and the dove that weeps while the lover sleeps

The Imam now turns to the night and the call of the dawn. *O beloved!* he writes. *If knowledge without action had been sufficient for you and deeds were truly unnecessary, then why would Allah the Exalted call out at the time before dawn:*

> Hul Min Ta ibin Hul Min Sa ilin Hul Min Mustaghfirin.
> Is there anyone who repents from sins? Is there anyone who asks of Me? Is there anyone who seeks My forgiveness?
> — The Voice of Allah at dawn, in the canonical hadith collections (hul min TAA-i-bin)

If knowledge alone were enough, the proclamation would be meaningless. The proclamation is directed at those of whom Allah has said:

> Kanu Qaleelum Minal Laili Ma Yahja'oon.
> There are only a few servants of Allah who sleep but little in the last part of the night — that blessed hour of nearness to Allah.
> — Quran (kaa-nu qa-LEE-lum mi-nal LAY-li)

And at those of whom He has said:

> Wa Bil Ashaarihum Yustaghfiroon.
> And those true servants of Allah, in the time before dawn, plead earnestly for Allah's forgiveness.
> — Quran (wa bil ash-AA-ri-hum yus-tagh-fi-ROON)

> Wul Mustaghfireena Bil Ashaari.
> And those true servants of Allah seek Allah's forgiveness at the time before dawn.
> — Quran (wul mus-tagh-fi-REE-na bil ash-AA-ri)

And then the explicit commandment to the Prophet ﷺ himself:

> Waminal Laili Fatahajjad Bihi Nafilatal Laka.
> And in the last part of the night, perform the Tahajjud prayers for the sake of Allah — it shall be of great benefit to you.
> — Quran (wa-mi-nal LAY-li fa-ta-HAJ-jad)

Once a group of Companions, the Imam recalls, were praising 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar (may Allah be pleased with them both) in the presence of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, upon which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> Nay'mur Rajula Huwa Lau Kana Yusalli Bil Laili.
> He is a good man. If only he were to pray the Tahajjud — those optional prayers in the blessed final hours before dawn, which are indispensable for spiritual progress on the Path of Allah.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (nay-MUR ra-JU-la)

The verdict from the Prophet ﷺ is precise: *he is a good man — IF only he were to pray the night vigil*. The good is not yet complete without the night.

And the Prophet ﷺ said on another occasion:

> La Takthirin Nauma Bil Laili Fainna Kathratan Naumi Bil Laili Tad'oo Sahibahu Faqirai Yaumal Qiyamati.
> Do not sleep overmuch at night, for excessive sleep at night leaves its keeper empty-handed on the Day of Judgment — having squandered the opportunity of drawing close to Allah through remembrance, prayer, and devotion, especially in those final blessed hours.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (la tak-THI-rin NAW-ma bil LAY-li)

The Imam adds a saying from Shaykh Sufyan al-Thawri (RA — suf-YAAN ath-THAW-ree), one of the early masters of the inner sciences, on the wind that carries the voices of the wakeful:

> Inna Allaha Ta'ala Khalaqa Reehun Tahabbu Waqtal Ashaari Tahmilul Azkaara Wal Istighfaara Ilal Malikil Jabbari.
> Allah the Exalted has created a wind that blows in the last part of the night. At that hour, the voices of those servants of Allah who engage in His remembrance and seek His forgiveness are carried by this wind and presented to the Court of Allah the Exalted.
> — Shaykh Sufyan al-Thawri (RA) (in-na al-LAH-ha ta-AA-la KHA-la-qa REE-hun)

And then the architecture of the four announcements at the four watches of the night, also from Shaykh Sufyan al-Thawri (RA). When the night begins, an angel beneath the Throne of Allah proclaims that all worshippers should rise; those whom Allah has granted *tawfiq* (taw-FEEQ — divine enablement and good fortune) arise and begin to pray. In the middle of the night, a second angel proclaims that all obedient and devoted servants of Allah should rise; and they do, continuing in prayer until the time of *sahr* (SAHR — the last blessed portion of the night). When *sahr* arrives, a third angel proclaims that all who seek Allah's forgiveness should rise; they rise and seek His forgiveness. And when the time of dawn draws near, a fourth angel calls out: *O heedless ones! Arise — the day has broken!* And these people rise from their beds as the dead will rise from their graves on the Day of Resurrection.

The Imam then quotes the testament of Luqman (peace of Allah be upon him), recorded in the Quran's surah of his name, in which Luqman counseled his son:

> Ya Buneyya La Takununnad Deeku Akyas Minka Fainnahu Yunaadi Bil Ashaari Wa Anta Na'imun.
> My son! Do not let the rooster prove more intelligent than you. Let it not be that he rises in the last part of the night to proclaim the hour of remembrance and glorifies his Lord, while you remain sunk in the deep sleep of heedlessness, carelessness, and negligence.
> — Luqman (peace of Allah be upon him), in his testament to his son (ya bu-NAY-ya la ta-ku-NUN-nad DEE-ka)

And he closes the night-stretch on a piece of Arabic poetry that names the same shame:

> Laqad Hatafet Fi Junhil Laili Hamamatun
> 'Ala Funanin Wahnun Wa Inni Na'imun
> Kazabtu Wa Baitil Allahi Lau Kuntu 'Aashiqan
> Lama Sabaqatni Bil Buqa'il Hamaaimun
> Wa Az'amu Anni Haa'imun Zu Sababatin
> Lahu Bi Wa La Abki Wa Tabkil Bahaaimun.
>
> In the depths of the night, the dove perched upon a branch calls out to her Lord, while I lie fast asleep.
> By the Lord of the House of Allah — the Ka'ba! I am a liar! Had I been a true lover of Allah, the doves would not have surpassed me in weeping from the pain of separation from my Beloved.
> Yet I claim to be a wandering lover consumed by longing, while my eyes remain dry — and the very animals weep in their love for their Creator.

The poem holds the same charge as Luqman's testament: the dove and the rooster have answered the call of the night; the human being who claims to love the Lord more than they do has not. *Even the animals weep.* The would-be lover sleeps.

## Worship is obedience — and the Shari'ah is the form

The Imam now lays the formal definition. *My dear beloved son! Our purpose here is that you should truly understand what obedience and worship are. Obedience and worship mean following the Shari'ah (sha-REE-ah — the Divine Law) of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, whether the commandments call for performing righteous deeds or for restraining oneself from evil, whether they pertain to his words or his example.*

He gives the rule in its starkest form. Whatever you do or refrain from doing, whatever you say or leave unsaid — all of it must be in full accordance with the teachings of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. *If you speak, speak according to his teachings. If you remain silent, do so according to his guidance. If you act or abstain from acting, do so in conformity with his example.*

The corollary is sharp. If you do something believing it to be an act of worship, yet you do not do it in obedience to the command of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, *it will not be counted as worship — it will be counted as a sin, even if it takes the outward form of prayer or fasting*. The Imam offers concrete cases. If someone fasts on either of the two days of Eid, or on the days of Hajj, they commit a sin — though it is fasting in form, it was not done in accordance with the commandment of the Prophet ﷺ. Likewise, if someone performs prayers at a forbidden time — such as after the Asr (ASR — late afternoon) prayer before sunset, or after the Fajr (FAJ-r — dawn) prayer before the sun has risen sufficiently — or in an unlawfully occupied place, that person is called *athim* (aa-THEEM — a sinner) and *fasiq* (FAA-siq — a transgressor). The outward form is worship; the absence of conformity makes it sin.

Conversely, when a husband and wife come together in lawful intimacy, it is not a sin, though it may appear otherwise to the uninformed — for it is done within the guidance of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and is therefore *halal* (ha-LAAL — permissible). *It is thus established that worship is another name for obedience — that is, worship means obedience.* The form does not decide the verdict; the conformity does.

## Mujahadah — the sword that severs the desires of the lower self

Therefore, the Imam writes, do nothing without the guidance of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, and *be certain that you cannot advance upon the Path of Allah the Exalted through accumulated knowledge alone*. The path must be traveled by *mujahadah* (mu-JAA-ha-dah — intense inner spiritual struggle and disciplined exercise of the soul), and the pleasures and demands of the lower self must be severed with the *sword of mujahadah*.

The Imam pauses here to name a dangerous counterfeit. The desires of the lower self are not severed *through immorality* — that is, by indulging them on the theory that indulgence will exhaust them — nor *through the deceptions of false Sufis* (SOO-fees), the Imam writes, those who claim the station of *Tasawwuf* (ta-SAW-wuf — the spiritual struggle of the inner path) and *mimic its outward language and appearance while remaining hollow and devious within*. True Sufis, the Imam adds in a parenthesis the disciple is meant to underline, are those *who have attained an intimate relationship with God through the emergence of purified souls, having eradicated evil from the deepest recesses of their being through genuine mujahadah*. The vocabulary of the inner path does not by itself confer the inner path. A tongue that speaks words of profound wisdom and beauty while the heart remains enslaved to heedlessness and the appetites of the lower self — *this is a mark of the gravest misfortune*.

And the Imam gives the rule that closes this stretch. Unless you sever the desires and inclinations of the lower self with the *sword of truth and mujahadah*, the light of *ma'rifah* (Ma'arifah — ma-aa-ri-FAH — deep, intimate knowledge, awareness, and conscious relationship with Allah) will not dawn within your heart.

## What cannot be written — Ishq and Zauq

The Imam now answers a strand of the disciple's letter that had asked about certain spiritual matters directly. *You have asked me about some spiritual issues,* he writes, *among which certain matters cannot be fully described in writing or speech.* When you reach the high station of spiritual enlightenment, you will come to know them yourself. *The lesson of love is not taught — it is born and learned from within.*

The reasoning is exact. *Ishq* (ISHQ — intense love) is another name for love and for the fervent desire to seek what one loves — *Zauq* (ZAWQ — the taste, the inner relish). Love and Zauq can neither be conveyed in speech nor in writing; their true essence cannot be presented. Just as sweetness, sourness, and bitterness cannot be adequately described by anyone who attempts to put them into words, so too is it here. *If you reach this destination of high spiritual station, you will come to know all of this by yourself.* If you cannot reach that destination, the truth can be described in writing and speech *to some extent*.

Some of the disciple's questions, the Imam adds, have already been answered at length in the *Ihya al-Ulum ad-Din* and in his other books; he refers the disciple back to those, and promises that in this letter he will only summarize, *inshallah* (in-SHAA-al-laah — God-willing).

## The four conditions for the seeker

Then the Imam begins the practical regimen. The disciple had also asked what is essential for those walking on the Path of Allah, the Exalted — that is, for the sincere seekers of Allah. The Imam gives four conditions.

The first condition is *the conviction of fear of Allah* — *Taqwa* (TAQ-wa — pious reverence, consciousness, awe, and fear of Allah) — to such a degree that the seeker does not become involved in *bid'a* (BID-ah — innovation), defined as any matter contrary to the established principles of the Quran and Sunnah. The Sunnah, the Imam glosses, encompasses the teachings, actions, outward conduct, and inner states of the Prophet ﷺ — including contentment, patience, gratitude, certitude in God, intimate relationship with God, Ma'arifah, intense love of God, and so forth.

The second condition is *Taubatun Nasuh* (taw-BA-tun na-SOOH — true and complete repentance) — repentance made in such a way that the seeker will *never return to the humiliation of sins*.

The third condition is that *even one's enemies should be kept satisfied* to such an extent that none of God's creation holds any unfulfilled obligation against the seeker. The rights of the creatures are settled before the rights of the Creator are pursued.

The fourth condition is that the seeker should acquire enough knowledge of the *Shari'ah* to follow and obey the commandments of Allah that are obligatory upon him or her. *It is not an obligation to learn more from the knowledge of Shari'ah than that.* From other branches of knowledge, one should learn only to the extent that is obligatory. The Imam is precise here in a way the disciple, given his complaint, is meant to feel directly. The condition is *sufficiency*, not *exhaustiveness*. The seeker is not obligated to read every book in every science. The seeker is obligated to know enough of the law to *obey* it.

## Shibli — four thousand Ahadith, four hundred masters, one practice

To press the point of *sufficiency*, the Imam reaches back to one of the most cited masters of the early sciences of the heart, Shibli (RA — SHIB-lee), and reports his startling confession:

> I served 400 spiritual masters and guides, and I adopted — that is, acted upon — only one out of the 4,000 Ahadith of the Prophet ﷺ that they narrated, and I left the rest of the Ahadith aside. This is because when I deliberated on this one hadith, I found my salvation within it. I found in this one hadith the knowledge of all who came first and all who came last.
> — Shibli (RA), in the traditions of the intimate friends of Allah

The hadith Shibli adopted is the rule that fits the whole of life onto a single calibration:

> Aymal Lid Dunya Be Qadre Maqamika Feeha Wa'mal Le Akhirateka Be Qadre Baqaa'ika Feeha Wa'mal Lillahi Be Qadre Hajateka Ilaeyhe Wa'mal Lin Naari Be Qadre Sabrika Alaeyha.
> Work for this world as much as you will live in it, and work for the life hereafter as much as you wish to live there, and work for Allah, the Exalted, as much as you are in need of Him, and work toward avoiding the Fire as much as you can bear its pain and sufferings.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (ay-MAL lid DUN-ya be QAD-re ma-QAA-mi-ka FEE-ha)

The rule is four lines that hold the whole of a life. *Work for the world to the measure of your time in it.* The world is short; the work for it is small. *Work for the Hereafter to the measure of your time there.* The Hereafter is endless; the work for it is great. *Work for Allah to the measure of your need of Him.* Need of Him is total; work for Him is total. *Work to escape the Fire to the measure of what you can bear of its pain.* You can bear nothing of it; work to escape it must therefore be unceasing.

The Imam's commentary is exact. *It is evident from this hadith,* he writes, *that you do not need a vast quantity of knowledge.* Studying and acquiring knowledge beyond one's personal obligation is *Fard Kifayah* (FARD ki-FAA-yah — a communal obligation discharged when a sufficient number of people within the Muslim community fulfil it). If no one within the community discharges it, the whole community bears the sin of the neglect. But the individual seeker is not obligated to discharge it singly.

The Imam closes the long opening on a note that prepares the next chapter directly. *Pay special attention to the following tradition,* he writes, *so that you may attain a high level of certitude.*

## The threshold — the master of Balkh and the disciple of thirty-three years

The Imam begins the next vignette in the chapter's closing breath. *It is said that among the students and mureeds (mu-REEDs — disciples who seek spiritual elevation and enlightenment through the purification of the soul from all evil desires and tendencies, under the guidance of a spiritual master who has himself completely attained that purification and high station before Allah through the training, guidance, and proximity of another spiritual guide) of Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (sha-FEEQ al-BAL-khee — may Allah shower His mercy upon him) was a student and disciple named Hatim Ism (HAA-tim ISM — may Allah shower His mercy upon him), who in turn later became a great spiritual master through the training and guidance of Shaykh Shafeeq.*

One day Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA), having taught Hatim for many years, asked him: *O Hatim! For how long have you been in my company — my spiritual proximity — and have been listening to what I have been teaching?*

Hatim (RA) replied: *Thirty-three years!*

Shaykh Shafeeq (RA) asked: *In that long period of time, what benefit have you derived from me?*

Hatim (RA) responded: *I have obtained eight benefits from you.*

Shaykh Shafeeq (RA), the Imam reports, said:

> Inna Lillahi Wa Inna Ilaeyhe Raji'oon.
> To Allah we belong and to Him shall we return.
> — Quranic supplication recited at times of loss and grief (in-na lil-LAH-hi wa in-na i-LAY-hi raa-ji-OON)

And then the master said to his disciple: *O Hatim! I have spent my entire life in teaching and training, and you have gained only eight benefits from me!*

Hatim (RA) replied: *O my teacher! If you ask me for the truth, then the reality is exactly what I have just told you. I do not need anything more, and these eight benefits from knowledge are quite sufficient, for I am certain that I will attain salvation and success in this life and in the life hereafter through them.*

Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA), the Imam says, said to him: *Very well — tell me, what are those eight benefits?*

And here the chapter holds. The recitation of the eight benefits — what Hatim distilled out of thirty-three years of his master's company — is the labor of the next chapter. The Imam has spent the long opening of his letter setting up the diagnosis: knowledge without action is madness; the highest knowledge of the path will not avail at the end if it is not joined to the action that the path demands; and the action that the path demands is conformity to the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ, severance of the desires of the lower self by *mujahadah*, and the four conditions of *Taqwa*, *Taubatun Nasuh*, the settlement of the rights of creatures, and the sufficient knowledge of the Shari'ah. Shibli's one-hadith-out-of-four-thousand has set the *form* of sufficiency. Hatim's eight-benefits-out-of-thirty-three-years is about to set the *content*.

The disciple, reading his master's letter, has been brought to the door. The next chapter opens it.
