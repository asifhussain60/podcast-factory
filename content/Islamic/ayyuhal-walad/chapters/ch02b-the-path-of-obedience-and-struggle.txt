# The Path of Obedience and Struggle

*Worship is not a separate department of life that can be performed in any way one chooses — it is another name for obedience to the* Shari'ah *of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, so that the same act is salvation when it sits inside the Prophetic frame and sin when it falls outside, regardless of its outward form. The Path of Allah cannot be traveled by accumulated knowledge alone; it requires* mujahadah *(inner spiritual struggle and disciplined exercise of the soul), with the desires of the lower self severed by its sword, sustained by the dawn watch when the heedless sleep and the rooster, the dove, and the saints answer the four proclamations of the night. The sincere seeker walks this Path under four conditions: fear of Allah that prevents drift into innovation; true repentance that closes the door on return; right dealing with others that leaves no debt outstanding; and an exact discipline of study that takes only what action requires.*

## Where this chapter picks up

The diagnosis is closed. The disciple has been shown — through hadith, parable, Quranic verse, and the dream of Junaid — that knowledge unaccompanied by action will not save him, and indeed that the most severe judgment falls precisely on the scholar who did not act. Now Ghazali must say what action is, and what Path the disciple must walk to learn it. He begins not with a definition, but with a scene — a sip of cold water in the hand of a great saint.

## The sigh of Hasan al-Basri

A cold, sweet drink is handed to Shaykh Hasan al-Basri. The moment he takes the cup in his hand, he lets out a deep sigh and falls unconscious. When he regains himself, the people around him ask what has come over him. He replies: *Zakartu Umniyyata Ahlin Naari Heena Yaquluna Le Ahlil Jannati Un Uufidu 'Aleyna Minal Ma'i* — "I remembered the plea of the inhabitants of Hell when they will beseech the inhabitants of Paradise to pour upon them even a little water."

The cold cup has not been refused. It has been *received* — and in the receiving, Hasan al-Basri has felt the whole weight of what is being asked of the world in that single sip. Somewhere, in a region the cup will never reach, others will be begging across an unbridgeable gulf for a fraction of what is in his hand. The reflex of the saint is not refusal; it is the unbearable consciousness of the contrast.

Ghazali does not draw a moral from the story. The story IS the moral — and what it locates is exactly the place from which the next teaching has to come. The diagnosis is intellectual; the cure begins in the body, in the throat that feels the cold water, in the chest that involuntarily heaves at what the comfort calls to mind. The Path is not first an idea. It is first a wakefulness of the soul that pays attention.

## The dawn watch — the four angels and the rooster

Ghazali turns immediately to the dawn watch. *If knowledge without action had been sufficient for you and deeds were truly unnecessary, then why would Allah the Exalted call out at the time before dawn:* *Hul Min Ta ibin Hul Min Sa ilin Hul Min Mustaghfirin* — "Is there anyone who repents from sins? Is there anyone who asks of Me? Is there anyone who seeks My forgiveness?" The pre-dawn proclamation is not a literary flourish in the religion; it is a standing summons. If knowledge alone could save, the summons would have no addressee.

The Quran itself names the answerers. *Kanu Qaleelum Minal Laili Ma Yahja'oon* — "There are only a few servants of Allah who sleep but little in the last part of the night — that blessed hour of nearness to Allah." Ghazali stacks two more witnesses behind the verse. When the Companions praised 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar to the Prophet, the Prophet's reply was the small reservation: *Nay'mur Rajula Huwa Lau Kana Yusalli Bil Laili* — "He is a good man. If only he were to pray the *Tahajjud* — those optional prayers in the blessed final hours before dawn, which are indispensable for spiritual progress on the Path of Allah." Even of a praised man, the Prophet marks the absence of the night-prayer as the missing piece. And to another Companion he says: *La Takthirin Nauma Bil Laili Fainna Kathratan Naumi Bil Laili Tad'oo Sahibahu Faqirai Yaumal Qiyamati* — "Do not sleep overmuch at night, for excessive sleep at night leaves its keeper empty-handed on the Day of Judgment."

Then the Quranic command itself, addressed by Allah directly to the Prophet: *Waminal Laili Fatahajjad Bihi Nafilatal Laka* — "And in the last part of the night, perform the *Tahajjud* prayers for the sake of Allah — it shall be of great benefit to you." And two further verses on the same hour: *Wa Bil Ashaarihum Yustaghfiroon* — "And those true servants of Allah, in the time before dawn, plead earnestly for Allah's forgiveness" — and *Wul Mustaghfireena Bil Ashaari* — "And those true servants of Allah seek Allah's forgiveness at the time before dawn." A divine gratitude in the first; an honored mention in the second.

The Messenger of Allah then enumerates the three sounds that especially please Allah: *the crowing of the rooster that rouses people for the* Tahajjud *prayers; the recitation of the Holy Quran; and the voices of those who, in the last portion of the night, plead to Allah for His forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance.* The rooster has standing in this list. Its crow has a purpose; its purpose is named; the purpose is that human beings should rise to meet what the rooster has already risen for.

Shaykh Sufyan al-Thawri carries the dawn watch one further turn. *Allah the Exalted has created a wind that blows in the last part of the night. At that hour, the voices of those servants of Allah who engage in His remembrance and seek His forgiveness are carried by this wind and presented to the Court of Allah the Exalted.* And then his cosmological scene — the four proclamations of the night. When the night begins, an angel beneath the Throne proclaims that *all worshippers* should rise; those granted *tawfiq* (divine enablement) arise. In the middle of the night, a second angel proclaims that *all obedient and devoted servants of Allah* should rise; they continue in prayer until the *sahr* (the last blessed portion of the night). At *sahr*, a third angel proclaims that *all who seek Allah's forgiveness* should rise; they rise and seek forgiveness. When dawn draws near, a fourth angel calls: *O heedless ones! Arise — the day has broken!* And these last rise from their beds *as the dead will rise from their graves on the Day of Resurrection.*

Four proclamations; four hours of the night; four sets of human responders, narrowing from the broadest call (all worshippers) to the most specific (the seekers of forgiveness at the hour of nearness), and then a fourth call addressed to those who heard none of the first three. The whole architecture is structured by what the soul did when each call was issued. The night is not a passive interval; it is a Court continuously convening, and the heedless are those who, even when finally summoned by name, rise only with the unconsciousness of corpses.

## Luqman to his son — the rooster as teacher

Ghazali then takes the dawn watch home, into the genre the whole letter belongs to — a father's counsel to his son. *It is recorded in the testament of Luqman (peace of Allah be upon him) that he counseled his son, saying:* *Ya Buneyya La Takununnad Deeku Akyas Minka Fainnahu Yunaadi Bil Ashaari Wa Anta Na'imun* — "My son! Do not let the rooster prove more intelligent than you. Let it not be that he rises in the last part of the night to proclaim the hour of remembrance and glorifies his Lord, while you remain sunk in the deep sleep of heedlessness, carelessness, and negligence."

The line lands with the force of the genre. Luqman is the proverbial wise father; the disciple is being addressed by Ghazali as his own beloved son; the rooster is the smallest, least dignified bird in the courtyard. And yet the rooster has answered the dawn proclamation. To be out-answered by the rooster is to occupy a place in the order of creation lower than the creature for whom the proclamation is not even addressed.

Ghazali seals the teaching with verses of poetry. *In the depths of the night, the dove perched upon a branch calls out to her Lord, while I lie fast asleep. By the Lord of the House of Allah — the Ka'ba! I am a liar! Had I been a true lover of Allah, the doves would not have surpassed me in weeping from the pain of separation from my Beloved. Yet I claim to be a wandering lover consumed by longing, while my eyes remain dry — and the very animals weep in their love for their Creator.* The bird again — this time the dove on the branch in the depths of the night. The lover who claims love and yet sleeps through the hour of the beloved's nearness is not a lover; he is, by his own self-indictment in the poem, *a liar*.

## The pivot — worship IS obedience

Now Ghazali takes the second great step of the letter. He has shown the disciple the dawn watch. He must now show him *what* a watch IS — and the answer is structural. *Our purpose here is that you should truly understand what obedience and worship are. Obedience and worship mean following the* Shari'ah *(the Divine Law) of Prophet Muhammad, whether the commandments call for performing righteous deeds or for restraining oneself from evil, whether they pertain to his words or his example.*

The principle is sweeping. *Whatever you do or refrain from doing, whatever you say or leave unsaid — all of it must be in full accordance with the teachings of Prophet Muhammad.* Worship is not a separate spiritual department to be added to ordinary life. It is the form that ordinary life takes when ordinary life is lived in conformity with the Prophetic example. Speech under his guidance; silence under his guidance; act under his guidance; abstention under his guidance.

The edge-cases that follow are where the principle bites. *If you do something believing it to be an act of worship, yet you do not do it in obedience to the command of Prophet Muhammad, then it will not be counted as worship — it will be counted as a sin, even if it takes the outward form of prayer or fasting.* The example: *if someone fasts on either of the two days of Eid, or on the days of Hajj, they commit a sin.* The fast is the fast; the abstention is real; the discomfort is real. Yet on those days the Prophetic command runs the other way, and therefore the *form* of worship is the *substance* of disobedience. Likewise prayer at a forbidden time — after the Asr before sunset, after the Fajr before the sun has risen sufficiently — or prayer on an unlawfully occupied place: outwardly worship, inwardly transgression. The performer earns the names *athim* (sinner) and *fasiq* (transgressor). *Though it outwardly resembles worship, it is not correct because it was not done in accordance with the guidance of Prophet Muhammad.*

And then the reverse edge-case, which is doctrinally as important as the first: *when a husband and wife come together in lawful intimacy, it is not a sin, though it may appear otherwise to the uninformed — for it is done within the guidance of Prophet Muhammad and is therefore* halal *(permissible).* What outwardly looks like worldly indulgence is, when it falls within the Prophetic frame, an act of obedience indistinguishable in standing from the prayer. *It is thus established that worship is another name for obedience.*

This is the secret of the whole moral architecture of the letter. There is no neutral act in the life of the believer. Every act is either obedience or transgression — and the test is not the outward form, but whether the act sits inside the Prophetic frame. The disciple who has spent years learning the sciences must now learn this: the same prayer that is salvation at one hour can be sin at another; the same fast that is praise on one day can be disobedience on the next; the same union that the uninformed would judge as appetite can be the very obedience that earns the reward. The frame is everything.

Ghazali generalizes the rule once more for emphasis: *let all your words and deeds conform to the commandments of Prophet Muhammad. Whatever actions you perform and whatever conversations you hold must be in accordance with the* Shari'ah, *because any knowledge or action of God's creation that falls outside the* Shari'ah *is plainly misguided and draws human beings away from the truth.* And the historical seal: *This is why Prophet Muhammad abrogated all prior religious dispensations.* The Prophetic frame is not one frame among others. It is the one in which the previous frames culminate and from which the deviations away from it depart.

## The sword of mujahadah

The principle of obedience is now in place. Ghazali turns to its inner condition. *Do nothing without the guidance of Prophet Muhammad, and be certain that you cannot advance upon the Path of Allah through accumulated knowledge alone. This Path must be traveled through earnest effort and* mujahadah *(intense inner spiritual struggle and disciplined exercise of the soul) — and the pleasures and demands of the lower self must be severed with the sword of* mujahadah.

The image is military. The *nafs* (the lower self with its evil desires and inclinations) is not to be coaxed and certainly not to be indulged; it is to be *severed*, with the sword of struggle. And Ghazali adds the warning the rest of the tradition has had to keep repeating in every century: *These desires of the lower self cannot be uprooted through immorality or through the deceptions of false* Sufis *— those who claim the station of* Sufism *and mimic its outward language and appearance while remaining hollow and devious within.* The parenthesis is doctrinally exact: *True Sufis are those who have attained an intimate relationship with God through the emergence of purified souls, having eradicated evil from the deepest recesses of their being through genuine* mujahadah.

The polemic is precise. The danger is not Sufism; the danger is the simulation of Sufism — the appropriation of its vocabulary and outward posture by figures whose actual inner life remains untouched by the *mujahadah* without which the vocabulary is empty. Ghazali names the symptom: *Allah the Exalted does not delight in the subtleties of philosophy spun amid a life engulfed in sin and moral darkness. A tongue that speaks words of profound wisdom and beauty while the heart remains enslaved to heedlessness and the appetites of the lower self — this is a mark of the gravest misfortune.*

And the seal: *Unless you sever the desires and inclinations of the lower self with the sword of truth and* mujahadah, *the light of* ma'rifah *(that deep, intimate knowledge, awareness, and conscious relationship with Allah) will not dawn within your heart.*

The chapter is almost complete. The diagnosis was *knowledge without action will not save you*; the cure is *obedience under the Prophetic frame, sustained by the* mujahadah *that severs the lower self.* The *light of ma'rifah* — the intimate knowledge that the disciple had been seeking through all the books — does not enter as the prize for accumulated learning. It enters when the soul has cleared the room for it.

## The four conditions of the sincere seeker

Ghazali now turns at last to the disciple's specific questions. Some of them, he notes with care, *cannot be fully described in writing or speech* — the lessons of *Ishq* (intense love) and *Zauq* (the fervent desire to seek what one loves) cannot be conveyed by the tongue any more than sweetness or bitterness can. *When you reach that high station of spiritual enlightenment, you will come to know them yourself. The lesson of love is not taught — it is born and learned from within.* Some questions have already been answered at length in *Ihya al-Ulum ad-Din* (Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God); they will be touched here only concisely.

But the second of the disciple's questions — *what is essential for those walking on the Path of Allah?* — Ghazali answers directly, in four conditions:

*The first condition* is that the seeker should possess the *conviction of fear of Allah* to such a degree that he never becomes involved in *bid'a* (innovation; any matter contrary to the established principles of the Quran and Sunnah, which includes the teachings, actions, outward conduct, and inner states of the Prophet — contentment, patience, gratitude, certitude in God, intimate relationship with God, *Ma'arifah*, intense love of God).

*The second condition* is that *true and complete repentance* (*Taubatun Nasuh*) must be made in such a way that the seeker will never return to the humiliation of sins.

*The third condition* is that even one's enemies should be kept satisfied to such an extent that no one among God's creation holds any unfulfilled obligation against him.

*The fourth condition* is that the seeker should acquire enough knowledge of *Shari'ah* to be able to follow and obey the commandments of Allah obligatory upon him — and from other branches of knowledge, only what is itself obligatory. Beyond personal obligation, study and acquisition of knowledge is *Fard Kifayah* (a communal obligation discharged when a sufficient number within the community fulfill it; but if no one within the community discharges it, the entire community bears the sin of the neglect).

The four conditions are the discipline of the chapter applied as a rule of life: fear of God that prevents drift into innovation; repentance that closes the door on return; right relating to others that leaves no debt outstanding; and an exact discipline of study that takes only what action requires and leaves the rest to those whose vocation it is.

Ghazali drives the fourth condition home with the saying of Shibli: *I served 400 spiritual masters and guides and I adopted — that is, acted upon — only one out of the 4,000* ahadith *of the Prophet Muhammad that they narrated, and I left the rest of the* ahadith *aside. This is because when I deliberated on this one hadith, I found my salvation within it. I found in this one hadith the knowledge of all who came first and all who came last:* *'Aymal Lid Dunya Be Qadre Maqamika Feeha Wa'mal Le Akhirateka Be Qadre Baqaa'ika Feeha Wa'mal Lillahi Be Qadre Hajateka Ilaeyhe Wa'mal Lin Naari Be Qadre Sabrika Alaeyha* — "Work for this world as much as you will live in it, and work for the life hereafter as much as you wish to live there, and work for Allah as much as you are in need of Him, and work toward avoiding the Fire as much as you can bear its pain and sufferings."

Four clauses; four proportions; one hadith carrying *the knowledge of all who came first and all who came last*. Ghazali draws the lesson the rest of the book will work out: *you do not need a vast quantity of knowledge.* You need the small set of teachings whose acting-out reorders the entire shape of a life.

These principles take their living form in the disciple's daily practice — in the eight benefits drawn by Shaykh Hatim ibn Ism over thirty-three years in the company of his master Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi, and in the qualities of a true Shaykh, the etiquette of obedience to him, and the inner vocabulary of the Path (*Tasawwuf*; *servitude*; *Tawakkul*; *Ikhlas*).

For now, the principle is in place. The Path is traveled by *obedience under the Prophetic frame*, with the *sword of* mujahadah *severing the lower self*, in a life ordered by the four conditions of the sincere seeker. The dawn watch is its hour; the rooster is its first teacher; the Shari'ah is its rule. *The light of ma'rifah will dawn in the heart* — but only in the heart that has cleared the room for it.
