# The Practical Rule of Life

*This chapter receives the practical regimen the opening chapter held in reserve. Imam al-Ghazali (Rahmatullahi Alayhe — rah-ma-tul-LAH-hi a-LAY-hi — may Allah shower His mercy upon him) answers the disciple's question in the shape the opening chapter forced: not as new knowledge, but as a structure of living. The chapter opens at the threshold the first chapter held open — Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (sha-FEEQ al-BAL-khee) and his disciple Hatim Ism (HAA-tim ISM) — and walks Hatim's eight benefits one by one. The eight together compose a single discipline: take the deeds for your friend in the grave; war against the* Nafs *(NAFS — the lower self); spend wealth into the next world; replace the world's standards of honor with* Taqwa *(TAQ-wa — pious reverence); refuse envy and rest in Allah's distribution; recognise Satan alone as your enemy; rely on Allah for your sustenance; place all reliance on Allah alone. Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) verdicts that these eight are what the* Tawrat *(taw-RAAT — Torah), the* Injil *(in-JEEL — Gospel of Prophet Isa, peace of Allah be upon him), the* Zabur *(za-BOOR — Psalms), and the Holy Quran all teach. The Imam then resumes the conditions of the seeker (the fifth — a* Shaykh al-Kamil*; the sixth — guarding against the* Nafs*; the seventh — humility and contentment), walks the qualities of the true guide and the etiquettes of the disciple, and gives the doctrinal definitions of* Tasawwuf*, servitude,* Tawakkul*, and* Ikhlas*. He closes with eight admonitions — four restraints (debate, preaching, the company of the affluent and rulers, accepting gifts from rulers) and four actions (the discipline of one's relation with Allah, the discipline of one's relation with His slaves, studying as if one had only a week to live, and not stockpiling more than a year's provision) — and a long supplication to be recited after every* Salat *(sa-LAAT — obligatory prayer).*

## Where this chapter picks up

The first chapter held open at a precise threshold. Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA — sha-FEEQ al-BAL-khee), the great early master of Balkh, had asked his disciple Hatim Ism (RA — HAA-tim ISM) what he had drawn out of thirty-three years of his company; Hatim had answered that he had distilled eight benefits and was certain that on those eight benefits his salvation rested in this life and the next. The chapter closed at that doorway. This chapter walks the eight one by one.

The architecture is no accident. The opening chapter diagnosed the disease — *knowledge without action is madness, and action without knowledge is worthless* — through the witness of the Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him), the post-mortem report of Shaykh Junaid Baghdadi (ju-NAYD bagh-DAA-dee) on the *Rak'at* (RAK-at — cycles) of *Tahajjud* (ta-HAJ-jud — the last-third night vigil), the Quranic chain on striving, the architecture of Islam and *Iman* (ee-MAAN — faith), the *Fawaylun-Fatuba* hinge on intention, and the *Shibli* (SHIB-lee) lemma of sufficiency. The diagnosis was complete. The cure begins here.

## The first benefit — the only beloved who descends into the grave

Hatim (RA) speaks. *O my teacher!* he begins — the form of address that will be the spine of the dialogue. *The first benefit is that I looked at the people of this world and saw that everyone has a beloved.* Some have a spouse, a child, wealth, friends, rank. But Hatim (RA) looked closely at the *limit* of those loves. The wife and the child accompany the lover until illness; some friends until death; some accompany the body until burial. *But after burial, all the loved ones return.* None descend into the grave to offer company in the *darkness, solitude, narrowness, and terror* of that place.

*Who, then, is the only true beloved? One who accompanies a person into the grave, who dwells with the lover there, who offers company, who illuminates the grave, and who is a partner on the Day of Judgment and through all the stations one passes through after burial until that Day.* He arrives at the single beloved who survives the burial. *One's own righteous deeds.*

The shape of the benefit is the shape that all eight will share. Observation of the world; identification of a hidden limit; a Quranic or Prophetic anchor; a decision of the will. *After reaching this awareness and determination,* Hatim (RA) says, *I made my righteous deeds my beloved, so that they will accompany me into my grave when I die, will offer me their company there, will become an illuminated candle within my grave, will be my partner through every stage of the life hereafter, and will never separate from me.*

Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) ratifies. *Bravo, O Hatim! You have said a most excellent thing.* The ratification — short, exact, without ornament — sets the rhythm. Each benefit is told, each is ratified, the next is requested. The eight will not be argued; they will be *witnessed* and *received*.

## The second benefit — and a verdict on the lower self

*O my teacher!* Hatim (RA) continues. *The second benefit is that I glanced at the people of the world and saw that everyone was running after the pleasures and desires of the Nafs — the lower self that incites one toward evil — and that everyone was subservient to its desires.* The diagnosis is precise. The world is full of people who are not enjoying their pleasures freely; they are being *driven* by their pleasures. The *Nafs* is in command and they are in service. Hatim (RA) reflects on this until a verse from the Holy Quran arrives in answer:

> Wa Amma Mun Khafa Maqama Rabbihi Wa Nahun Nafsa 'Anil Hawa Fa Innal Jannata Heyal Mawa.
> But as for the one who has stood in awe of his Sustainer's Presence and restrained his inner self from base and evil desires, Paradise will be the place of rest.
> — Quran 79:40–41 (wa AM-ma mun KHA-fa ma-QAA-ma RAB-bi-hi wa NA-hun NAF-sa a-nil HA-wa)

The verse names two operations in sequence — awe of the Lord's Presence and restraint of the *Nafs* — and joins them to a single result. *I developed the certitude,* Hatim (RA) says, *that the Wise Quran is correct and that the Book of Allah is the truth. I then took a firm stand against my Nafs and became resolved to oppose it. I disciplined it to such a degree that I refused to fulfil any of its desires, until at last it began to find peace and satisfaction in the worship of Allah, the Exalted.*

The last clause is the pivot. The discipline of the *Nafs* does not end in permanent conflict; it ends in *the Nafs itself becoming pleased with the worship of Allah*. The benefit is not the war; it is the peace that the war produces.

Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) ratifies again. *May Allah, the Exalted, grant His blessings in your good deeds. Now narrate the third benefit.*

## The third benefit — possessions deposited with Allah

*O my teacher!* Hatim (RA) continues. *The third benefit is that when I glanced at the people I saw that every person, through great pain and hard labour, was busy accumulating the possessions — money, wealth, and goods — of this mortal world, and was greatly pleased to have amassed much of it.* The observation has a precise shape. People are not merely acquiring; they are *taking pleasure* in the act of acquiring. The accumulation is the goal, not the means.

The Quranic verdict on this picture arrives:

> Ma 'Indakum Yanfadu Wa Ma 'IndAllahi Baaqin.
> All that is with you is bound to come to an end, whereas all that which is with Allah is everlasting.
> — Quran 16:96 (ma in-DA-kum yan-FA-du wa ma in-dal-LAH-hi BAA-qin)

The verse names a contrast that is not psychological but ontological. *What is with you* ends; *what is with Allah* does not end. The two classes are distinguished by their location, not by their type. The same coin, possessed by the servant, perishes; given in the Way of Allah, does not perish. *Then whatever material possessions I had accumulated in this world, I spent them entirely in the Way of Allah by distributing them among pious indigents and the needy, so that those possessions would be deposited with Allah, the Exalted, as my trust, and would become a source of immense good and ransom — that is, salvation — in the life hereafter.*

The image is the image of a deposit. The lover of wealth is not asked to *love it less* but to *redirect the love to the place where its object will survive*. Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) ratifies. *O Hatim! May Allah grant you His reward for your spending in His Way. You have said an excellent thing and done an excellent thing.*

## The fourth benefit — what counts as honor in Allah's sight

*O my teacher!* Hatim (RA) continues. *The fourth benefit is that I looked at the people of the world and saw that some of them believe grandeur and honour belong to the nation or tribe that is large and powerful, and so they take pride in their tribe. Some believe that grandeur comes from an abundance of wealth, material possessions, family, and relatives, and so they boast of their riches and children. Some believe that their grandeur and honour lie in displaying anger, in beating, killing, murder, and pillage. Some believe that their grandeur lies in wasteful spending, and so they regard such extravagance as a source of honour and boast of it.*

The catalogue names the four registers in which the world locates honor — tribe, wealth, violence, conspicuous waste — and shows each as a *misreading* of where honor actually lies. The Quranic verdict overturns all four:

> Inna Akramakum 'IndAllahi Atqakum.
> Verily, the noblest of you in the sight of Allah is the one who is most deeply conscious — that is, most fearful — of Him.
> — Quran 49:13 (in-na ak-RA-ma-kum in-dal-LAH-hi AT-qa-kum)

The verse names a single coordinate of nobility and locates it in a single place — the sight of Allah. *This alone is correct; this alone is the truth,* Hatim (RA) says. *The thoughts of the people of the world are false conjectures and nothing more. Therefore I adopted Taqwa — pious reverence, consciousness, awe, and fear of Allah — so that I might be counted among those of the highest rank in the sight of Allah, the Exalted.*

The benefit is not the dismissal of honor but its *relocation* — from the eye of the tribe to the sight of the Lord. Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) ratifies. *O Hatim! May Allah be pleased with you. You have said a most excellent thing.*

## The fifth benefit — the distribution is already made

*O my teacher!* Hatim (RA) continues. *The fifth benefit is that I looked at the people who were complaining against one another. I found that they were doing this on account of envy, jealousy, and enmity, and that the root cause of all of it was competition over greatness, grandeur, material possessions, wealth, and knowledge.* The named grievances are the *surface* of social conflict. Underneath is competition; underneath competition is envy.

The Quranic verdict cuts the root:

> Nahnu Qasumna Baeynahum Ma'eeshatahum Fil Hayatid Dunya.
> It is We who distribute their means of livelihood — their sustenance — among them in the life of this world.
> — Quran 43:32 (nah-nu qa-SUM-na bay-NA-hum ma-ee-sha-TA-hum fil ha-YAA-tid DUN-ya)

The verse names the agent, the object, and the finality of the distribution. *I then reflected,* Hatim (RA) says, *that Allah, the Blessed and Exalted, has appointed all possessions and ranks from the very beginning of creation, and that no one has any control or say in this matter. Therefore I ceased to compete with or harbour jealousy toward anyone, and I remained content with Allah's distribution of worldly bounties and with His appointed destiny, living in peace with all of creation by surrendering every jealousy and enmity against the creatures of God.* To envy another for what one does not have is to quarrel with the Distributor. The peace is not earned by changing the world but by recognizing its Owner. Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) ratifies. *O Hatim! You speak the truth and you are doing what is right.*

## The sixth benefit — Satan alone as the enemy

*O my teacher!* Hatim (RA) continues. *The sixth benefit is that when I glanced at the people I saw that every person holds enmity toward someone else for some reason.* Enmity attaches itself to almost every life. The Quranic verdict redirects it:

> Innash Shaytana Lakum 'Aduwwun Fettakhizuhu 'Aduwwa.
> Verily, Shaytan is your enemy, so treat him as your enemy.
> — Quran 35:6 (in-nash shay-TAA-na la-kum a-DUW-wun fat-ta-KHI-zu-hu a-DUW-wa)

The verse does not *forbid* enmity; it *targets* enmity. The enemy is *Shaytan*. *I developed the certitude that Allah's words are the truth, and that I should hold no enmity toward any person. From that time forward, I regarded Satan alone as my enemy and refused to obey any of his promptings. Instead, I cultivated obedience to Allah, followed the commandments of Allah, the Exalted, and devoted myself entirely to His worship and servitude.* The benefit is sealed with the Quranic naming of the path that opens once the enmity is correctly targeted:

> Alam A'had Ilaeykum Ya Bani Aadama Al La Ta'budush Shaytana Innahu Lakum 'Aduwwum Mubeen Wa Ane'buduni Haza Siratum Mustaqeem.
> Did I not make this covenant with you, O Children of Adam — that you should never obey Shaytan, for he is your avowed and open enemy — and that you should worship Me alone? This is the straight path.
> — Quran (a-lam AA-had i-LAY-kum ya ba-NI AA-da-ma)

This is the *Sirat al-Mustaqeem* (si-RAAT al-mus-ta-QEEM — the straight path of guidance in which there is no crookedness): withdraw enmity from where it does not belong, attach it where it does, and the path opens. Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) ratifies. *O Hatim! You have done an excellent thing and said an excellent thing.*

## The seventh benefit — sustenance is the responsibility of Allah

*O my teacher!* Hatim (RA) continues. *The seventh benefit is that I looked at the people and saw that every person is preoccupied with his or her livelihood and the search for sustenance, consumed by intense effort in that pursuit. In this race, many do not even distinguish between the* Halal *(ha-LAAL — what is permissible) and the* Haram *(ha-RAAM — what is forbidden), and instead degrade themselves by pursuing doubtful and forbidden earnings.* The pursuit itself is not the problem; its *intensity* collapses the distinctions on which the *Deen* (DEEN — the path to God) rests. The Quranic verdict removes the foundation of the rush:

> Wa Ma Min Daabbatin Fil Arde Illa 'AlalAllahi Rizquha.
> And there is no living creature on earth whose sustenance is not the responsibility of Allah.
> — Quran 11:6 (wa ma min DAAB-ba-tin fil AR-di il-la a-lal-LAH-hi RIZ-qu-ha)

The verse is exhaustive — *no living creature on earth* — and locative — *the responsibility of Allah*. The creature's task is not to *manufacture* provision but to *receive* it. *I then developed the certitude,* Hatim (RA) says, *that the Wise Quran is true and correct, and that I am also among those living creatures of God present upon the earth. Therefore I devoted myself to the worship of Allah and held firm to the certitude that He would bring my sustenance to me, for He has promised to provide it.* The benefit is not the abandonment of work but its *order* — worship first; sustenance through Allah's appointed channels; no transgression of the *Halal* line under the rush of provision-seeking. Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) ratifies. *You have done an excellent thing and said an excellent thing.*

## The eighth benefit — complete reliance on Allah

*O my teacher!* Hatim (RA) continues. *The eighth benefit is that when I looked at the people I found that every person's reliance and trust rests on something or someone. Some place their trust in their material possessions, and some rely upon other people.* The first seven benefits each named a particular operation; the eighth is the summary operation — on whom does the heart *finally* rest?

Two verses arrive in answer:

> Wa Maey Yatawakkal 'AlalAllahi Fahuwa Hasbuhu.
> And for everyone who places his or her trust in Allah, He alone is sufficient.
> — Quran 65:3 (wa ma-yee ya-ta-WAK-kal a-lal-LAH-hi fa-hu-wa HAS-bu-hu)

> Wa Huwa Husbi Wa Nai'mal Wakeel.
> And He is sufficient for me, and He is the best of Helpers.
> — Quran (wa HU-wa HUS-bi wa NA-ee-mal wa-KEEL)

The first verse states the principle; the second is the servant's response. *Therefore,* Hatim (RA) says, *I adopted complete reliance upon Allah in every matter.* The seven prior benefits each named an object of misplaced reliance and corrected it to its proper object. The eighth states the correction in its pure form: *He alone is sufficient.*

## The four heavenly Books

Shaykh Shafeeq al-Balkhi (RA) hears the eight benefits and gives the closing verdict. *O Hatim! May Allah grant you His Tawfeeq* (taw-FEEQ — the capability and inner motivation to act on what one knows) *— that is, may He enable you to perform righteous deeds and to walk firmly upon His Path. You have conveyed most excellent things. I have seen in the Tawrat* (taw-RAAT — the Torah, revealed to Prophet Musa, peace of Allah be upon him)*, the Injil* (in-JEEL — the Gospel revealed to Prophet Isa, peace of Allah be upon him)*, the Zabur* (za-BOOR — the Psalms revealed to Prophet Dawud, peace of Allah be upon him)*, and in the praiseworthy differentiator between right and wrong — the Holy Quran — that all four of these Books of God speak of these very eight benefits. Whoever acts upon these eight principles is as one who has acted upon all four holy Books.*

The eight are not Hatim's discovery; they are the eight registers of guidance the four heavenly Books teach. A disciple who acts on these eight has acted on every Book that has come from Allah. *You now know from these traditions,* the Imam adds, *that you do not need a great quantity of knowledge.*

## The fifth condition — a perfected spiritual guide

The Imam now returns to the seven conditions he had begun to enumerate before the Hatim narrative. The first four conditions had been walked in the opening chapter (*Taqwa*, *Taubatun Nasuh* — taw-BA-tun na-SOOH — sincere repentance, the settling of the rights of creatures, and sufficient knowledge of the *Shari'ah*). The fifth condition now follows. *The fifth condition that is obligatory upon the seeker on the Path of Allah, the Exalted, is that he or she should have a Shaykh al-Kamil* (SHAYKH al-KAA-mil — a perfected spiritual guide) *who leads the seeker, removes evil qualities and morals from within, and replaces them with noble ones.*

The Imam gives the work of the guide as an image. *The example of this spiritual training is like that of a farmer tending a crop: when he sees unnecessary weeds that have sprung up among the grain, he pulls them out and throws them away. Then he waters the crop and nourishes it so that it grows well and develops good quality.* Evil qualities are uprooted; noble qualities are watered; the crop is the disciple.

*At every stage of the journey, the traveler on the Path of Allah has no remedy or solution except through a Murshid al-Kamil* (mur-SHID al-KAA-mil — a true and perfected spiritual guide). *For Allah, the Exalted, sent Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to His servants so that he would serve as an illuminated guide on the Way of Allah and bring people to that Way. When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ departed from this world, he appointed his deputies and successors in his place, so that they would serve as proofs of the Way of Allah, the Exalted, until the Day of Judgment.* The guide of the seeker, the Imam writes, is *an illuminated proof and deputy of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ* — a link in a chain that goes back to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ himself.

## The qualities of a true Shaykh

The Imam then enumerates the qualities of the true *Shaykh*. *Not every scholar can be a Shaykh al-Kamil,* he warns. *For this work of guiding people spiritually — purifying their souls and helping them advance on the Way of Allah — only a person possessing certain specific qualities is truly qualified. We briefly enumerate some of these qualities here, so that no foolish or misguided person may falsely claim to be a Shaykh, for such impostors are many, and their sole purpose is to deceive people for material gain, causing great harm to this vital dimension of Islam and leading many astray.*

The catalogue is dense. The true *Shaykh* must have *turned away from love of the world, love of honor, and love of worldly rank*; must have *taken Bay'a* (bay-AH — a solemn covenant with Allah made on the hand of a *Shaykh*, pledging to turn away from evil inwardly and outwardly, to obey His commandments, and to purify the soul in pursuit of His nearness) *with a perfected spiritual guide, who in turn has taken Bay'a with his own perfected spiritual guide, and so on, such that this spiritual chain extends all the way back to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ*; must have *undergone all manner of Riazat and Mujahada* (ri-YAA-zat wa mu-JAA-ha-dah — spiritual struggles and disciplines); must have *obeyed every commandment of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ*; must *eat little, sleep little, pray abundantly, fast frequently, and give generously in the Way of God*; must possess *all praiseworthy moral qualities* — patience, gratitude, certitude, generosity, contentment, trustworthiness, tolerance, humility, truthfulness, modesty, dignity, tranquility; must have received *a light from among the lights of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ*; and must have had *all evil qualities — miserliness, jealousy, enmity, worldly expectation, anger, and rebellion — purged from within.*

A *Shaykh* whose life cannot be measured against the catalogue is a *Shaykh* by claim only. Following such a guide is not the correct way; following one who measures against the catalogue is.

## The etiquettes of the disciple — outward and inward

*Such spiritual guides are exceedingly difficult to find,* the Imam concedes. *But if someone has been granted this wealth and blessing — that he or she has found such a guide, and that guide has admitted him or her among his disciples (Mureeds — mu-REEDS) — then it is necessary for that disciple to maintain both outward and inward respect, honor, and proper etiquette toward the spiritual guide.*

The outward etiquettes. *The disciple should not argue with the guide, and if an argument arises and the disciple thinks the guide has overlooked something, he or she should not raise an objection.* The disciple should not occupy the place of prayer permanently; *rather, when the time for prayer arrives, he or she should go to the place of prayer, perform the prayer, and then roll up the prayer mat and leave.* The disciple should not perform lengthy supererogatory prayers in front of the *Shaykh*. *Whatever the perfected guide commands, the disciple should obey to the best of his or her ability.*

The doctrinal safeguard: *a true spiritual guide cannot and will not command a disciple to do anything forbidden by the Shari'ah — the law of Allah. False impostors who lack the aforementioned qualities, by contrast, may command their disciples to do things that are Haram.* The catalogue of qualities is both the *test* by which the true *Shaykh* is recognized and the *guarantee* that he will not command the *Haram*.

The inward etiquettes. *Whatever the disciple hears outwardly from the Murshid, he or she should not entertain even the slightest doubt in the heart regarding the Shaykh's words or deeds — otherwise the disciple will be counted among the hypocrites. If the disciple cannot maintain this inner sincerity, then he or she should withdraw from the company of the spiritual guide until the inward state comes into harmony with the outward.* The remedy is not to force the inward state but to *withdraw* until inward matches outward. The guide is not lost; the relationship is paused.

## The sixth and seventh conditions — guarding against the Nafs; humility and contentment

The Imam now completes the seven conditions. *The sixth condition is that the disciple guards against the deceptions of the Nafs: the lower self, an inward, subtle presence invisible to the outward eye yet clearly perceptible to one possessing inner sight, which commands a person to evil by whispering into the heart. This is only possible if the seeker abandons the gatherings of the ill-charactered and the ignorant. In this way, the dominion of Shaytan over the heart will come to an end, and all Satanic influences will be uprooted entirely.* Satan operates across two registers — *among humankind or from among the Jinn* (JINN — the invisible creation of God made from fire) — and works through the company one keeps; the discipline is to refuse that company.

*The seventh condition is that in every circumstance, the seeker should prefer humility and freedom from greed over wealth, and should embrace contentment and satisfaction with the Will of Allah. These seven conditions are essential for every traveler on the Path of Allah, the Exalted, and for every seeker of truth.*

## The Reality of Tasawwuf

The Imam now turns to the doctrinal definitions the disciple had requested. The first is *Tasawwuf* (ta-SAW-wuf — the inner science of purification, often rendered into English as Sufism). *Tasawwuf is defined by two qualities. The first is that the servant should be obedient to Allah — that is, he or she should be acting in accordance with the Shari'ah, the outward laws of Islam encompassing obligations, commandments, and prohibitions. The second is that the seeker should be compassionate and a doer of good toward all of Allah's creation, which is not possible without first purifying one's soul of all evil qualities and then adorning it with all noble ones.*

*Tasawwuf* is not *only* obedience to the *Shari'ah* nor *only* compassion toward creation; it is the joining of the two, and the joining cannot occur without the purification that precedes it. *One who perseveres in acting upon the Shari'ah and who genuinely desires the betterment and true success of all humanity is rightly called a Sufi* (SOO-fee) *— and this is the correct application of the term, as opposed to the distorted usages so widespread today.* The label *Sufi* is not a costume; it is the *consequence* of a particular kind of life.

*Loyalty and obedience to Allah means that the seeker sacrifices personal happiness for the sake of Allah. Sincerity toward all people means that the seeker does not maintain relationships for personal motives, but is selfless and performs selfless service, free from self-interest. Instead, the seeker dedicates himself or herself to the welfare of all people — though the condition is that this welfare must be in accordance with the Shari'ah, the Divine law.* Service is bounded by the *Shari'ah*; service in violation of it is not service.

## The Reality of Servitude

The second definition is the nature of servitude — what it means to be a *slave* (*'abd*) of Allah. *Servitude consists of three things. First is obedience to the commandments of the Shari'ah. Second is contentment with the decree and destiny appointed by Allah, the Exalted. Third is the abandonment of one's own desires and claims, and the acceptance of and happiness with the Will and Power of Allah, the Exalted.* The triple moves from outward act to inward reception to inward transformation.

## The Reality of Tawakkul

The third definition is *Tawakkul* (ta-WAK-kul — reliance on Allah). *Tawakkul means holding firm belief and certitude in what Allah, the Exalted, has promised. In other words, one believes with conviction that whatever Allah has decreed for you, you will surely receive — and if the entire world were to conspire to prevent it, it could not be prevented. But for that which Allah has not written in your destiny, no amount of effort by you or by the entire world combined will ever bring it to you.*

*Tawakkul* is the inward face of the outward decision Hatim (RA) made when he stopped competing for sustenance. The decree is fixed; the world is powerless against it. The servant who has *Tawakkul* lives in the precise space the decree defines.

## The Reality of Ikhlas

The fourth definition is *Ikhlas* (ikh-LAAS — sincerity to Allah). *Ikhlas means that all of your deeds are exclusively for the sake of Allah and His pleasure. Whatever you do should not be for show or ostentation — Riya* (ri-YAA — performance done for the gaze of others). *When you perform good deeds, your heart should not lean toward impressing people or seeking their praise. Your heart should neither be gladdened by people's admiration nor grieved by their criticism.*

*Riya is born from the praises and honors of people. The cure for the spiritual disease of Riya is to regard the entire universe as subject to the Power and Control of Allah, the Exalted, and to consider all created things as equivalent to pebbles and stones — understanding that stones possess no power to bring you either grief or comfort. If you truly regard all creation in this light, you will be freed from Riya. As long as you believe that mortal created beings have the power to cause you grief or bring you comfort, Riya will never depart from your heart.* The cure is not to suppress the *Riya* but to dissolve the foundation that makes it possible.

## The transition — and the counsel of Khidr

The Imam then closes the doctrinal questions with a clause that defines the form of the rest of the letter. *Your remaining questions are such that some have been addressed in books I have written, which you may consult, while others are of a nature that writing their answers is not permissible. So take what is written here and steadily put it into practice — and through that practice, those matters which you do not yet understand will become clear to you.*

He follows the clause with three Quranic anchors and a verdict in the voice of Khidr (KHI-dr — the wise companion of Prophet Musa, peace of Allah be upon him, in the Quran's *Surat al-Kahf*). The first names patience as the right posture of the questioner:

> Wa Lau Annahum Sabaru Hatta Takhruja Ilaeyhim Lakana Khairan Lahum.
> Had those people exercised patience — instead of clamoring outside your house — until you, O Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, came out of your house yourself, it would have been better for them.
> — Quran 49:5 (wa LAU an-NA-hum sa-BA-ru hat-ta takh-RU-ja i-LAY-him)

The second is the counsel of Khidr to Musa, peace of Allah be upon him, which the Imam now directs to the disciple:

> Fala Tas-alni 'Un Shaeyin Hatta Uhdithu Laka Minhu Zikra.
> If you wish to follow me, O Moses (peace be upon him), then do not question me about anything until I myself bring it up and explain it to you.
> — Quran 18:70 (fa-la tas-AL-ni un SHAY-in hat-ta uh-DI-thu LA-ka MIN-hu ZIK-ra)

The third names the time-rule for understanding:

> Sa Urikum Ayate Fala Tusta'jiloon.
> We — that is, Allah — will soon show you Our signs, so do not seek to hasten this matter.
> — Quran (sa u-REE-kum AA-ya-tee fa-la tus-tA-ji-LOON)

The disciple is not refused; he is *paced*. The teaching will come at its proper time, through practice, and the matters that are *not permissible to write* will become clear through that practice, not through further questioning. *Do not ask before the right time arrives. When you reach that spiritually elevated stage, you will see it yourself. Therefore, hold firm to this certainty: unless you set out toward that destination, you will neither arrive there nor be able to see what awaits.*

He closes the transition with one more Quranic verse:

> Awalum Yaseeru Fil Ardi Fayanzuru.
> Have they not traveled the earth so that they might see?
> — Quran (a-wa-lum ya-SI-ru fil AR-di fa-yan-ZU-ru)

And a saying of Shaykh Zun Noon Misri (RA — zun-NOON mis-REE), the early master of the inner sciences:

> In Qadarta 'Ala Bazlir Ruhi Fata'ala Wa Illa Tashtaghil Bi Nazahatis Sufiyyati Wal Qaal.
> If you have the courage to exert your utmost with your soul in this way, then come and step onto this path — otherwise do not merely occupy yourself with the beautiful sayings of the Sufis.
> — Shaykh Zun Noon Misri (RA), in the canonical biographies of the inner-sciences tradition (in qa-DAR-ta a-la baz-LIR ROO-hi)

The vocabulary of the inner sciences is not their substance; the substance is *the exertion of the soul*. If the disciple has the courage to exert his soul, he is welcome on the path. If he has only the appetite for the vocabulary, he should leave the vocabulary alone.

## Eight admonitions — four things to refrain from

*My dear beloved son!* the Imam writes. *I will now bring my explanation to a close with eight admonitions. Of these eight admonitions, there are four things you must do and four things you must not do — so that your knowledge does not turn against you on the Day of Judgment. Let us begin with the four things you should not do.*

The architecture closes the letter in the same form as the eight benefits opened the chapter. Eight items, divided into two registers — what to *not* do (four) and what to *do* (four). The disciple now has a complete account of the two halves of the work — restraint and action — each in a list of four.

### The first admonition — the principle of debate

*As far as possible, do not engage in religious debates with everyone, and do not argue with anyone on any matter — religious or otherwise — for there are many hidden harms within such disputes, and the harms far outweigh any benefits. Debate and argument are the fountainhead of all manner of evils: ostentation and show — Riya — jealousy, arrogance, resentment, enmity, pride, and egotism.*

The Imam names the exception precisely. *If an argument arises between you and another person over some issue, and your genuine desire is for the truth to prevail, then your intention to engage in that discussion may be considered sound.* The two signs of right intention: *whether the truth emerges from your own tongue or from the tongue of your opponent, it makes no difference to you*; and *you consider it preferable to discuss the matter in private, away from an audience.* The two signs test for *Ikhlas* in dispute. *If you are in a dispute and you are certain that you are correct and your opponent is arguing merely for the sake of arguing, then be on guard — do not continue the argument; end the discussion at that point. Otherwise, grudges and resentments will take root and no benefit whatsoever will come of it.*

The Imam opens a deeper image. *Asking questions about religious and spiritual matters is like describing the diseases of the heart and their causes before a physician of the heart. Ignorant people are like patients afflicted with sickness of the heart, while scholars are like physicians and healers. Imperfect scholars are incapable of curing these diseases; a perfected scholar — a Shaykh al-Kamil — can cure them.*

The Imam then enumerates the four kinds of patient — only one of whom is curable. The first is *one who asks questions out of jealousy or raises objections from envy. Jealousy is a lethal disease that has no cure. Whatever answer you give, no matter how sound, such a person will regard you as an enemy.* The remedy: *do not respond.* The poet's couplet seals the rule:

> Kullul 'Adawati Qad Turja Izalatuha Illa 'Adawata Mun 'Aadaka Min Hasadin.
> Every kind of animosity can be remedied — except the animosity that is born of jealousy.
> — Classical Arabic poetry (KUL-lul a-da-WAA-ti qad TUR-ja i-za-LA-tu-ha)

The Quranic verdict on the jealous turner-away follows:

> Fa 'AaRid 'Um Mun Tawalla 'Un Zikrina Walam Yurid Illal Hayatad Dunya.
> So turn away from the one who, out of jealousy, turns his face from Our remembrance and desires nothing but the pleasures of the life of this world.
> — Quran (fa AA-rid um mun ta-WAL-la un ZIK-ri-na)

The second kind is *one whose disease arises from a lack of intelligence and foolishness. This disease is likewise incurable. Prophet Isa (Jesus, peace of Allah be upon him) said that he was — with the permission of God — not incapable of restoring the dead to life, yet he was incapable of curing the fool and the ignorant. The ignorant fool is the one who has spent only a brief time acquiring knowledge, has not even begun to grasp intellectual and transmitted religious learning, and yet raises objections against scholars who have devoted their entire lives to those very disciplines.* The signature of the fool is not ignorance itself but the *objecting* posture from inside ignorance.

The third kind is *one who, owing to restlessness and impatience, cannot absorb the teachings of the spiritually elevated, and who relies instead on their own deficient understanding, interpreting everything only according to their personal interest.* The Prophet ﷺ names the rule:

> Nahnu Ma'aashirul Anbiyaee Umirna Un Natakallamun Nasa 'Ala Qadre 'Uuqulihim.
> We, the community of prophets, have been commanded by Allah to speak to people according to the capacity of their minds.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (NAH-nu ma-AA-shi-rul an-bi-YAA-i u-MIR-na)

The teacher does not unburden himself of *what he knows*; the teacher gives the student *what the student's mind can carry*.

The fourth kind is the one who can be cured. *The fourth kind of patient is one who is genuinely seeking the right path of guidance — Sirat al-Mustaqeem — obedient, sharp, and intelligent — and who is free from anger, the following of base desires of the lower self (Nafs), jealousy, and the craving for wealth and fame. Whatever question this person asks, or whatever objection they raise, arises not from jealousy, fault-finding, or the desire to test, but from an earnest search for truth and correct guidance. This is the patient who can be cured. To answer the questions of such a person is therefore not merely permissible — it is obligatory* (Wajib — WAA-jib).

The taxonomy commands the disciple to *recognize the kind of patient he is sitting with* and to spend his time only on the one who can be healed. Spending time on the other three is not generosity; it is failure of triage.

### The second admonition — the reality of preaching

The second restraint is on preaching. *You are admonished to refrain from preaching and making speeches, for great calamities and harms lie within it.* The Imam locates the danger precisely. *If you believe that whatever you preach you have already acted upon yourself, then bear in mind that Allah, the Exalted and the Truth, said to Prophet Isa (peace of Allah be upon him):*

> Ya Ibna Maryama A'azee Nafsaka Fa In At-ta'azta Fa Izin Nasa Fustahyee Minni.
> O Son of Mary! First admonish your own soul (Nafs); then, if it accepts your counsel, admonish others — otherwise have shame before Me.
> — Hadith Qudsi register, addressed to Prophet Isa, peace of Allah be upon him (ya ib-na MAR-ya-ma a-AA-zee NAF-sa-ka)

The right to preach is *earned* by having admonished one's own *Nafs* first. *If circumstances arise that compel you to preach, then guard against two things.*

First, against rhetorical performance. *In your sermon avoid colorful narrations, theatrical gestures, secretive language, flowery and embellished speech, pleasing poetry, any talk that transgresses the bounds of the Shari'ah, and the fabrications of false claimants to Sufism. Allah, the Exalted, does not love those who are pretentious and showy. The purpose of a sermon is not to display one's abilities; it is to speak of the punishment of the Hereafter.* The sermon should speak of one's own shortcomings, regret over wasted time, the difficult stages of the life to come, how to keep *Iman* (ee-MAAN — faith) intact, the appearance of the Angel of Death (*Malik ul-Maut*), the questioning in the grave by *Munkar and Nakir* (MUN-kar wa na-KEER — the two angels who appear after burial), the plain of *Hashr* (HASHR — the assembly of all humankind after resurrection), the weighing of deeds in the *Mizan* (mi-ZAAN — the mighty scale), and the bridge called *Sirat* (si-RAAT — a bridge over Hell, thinner than a hair and sharper than a sword). The sermon is not a performance; it is a warning.

The parable: *Consider the example of a person whose house is struck by a flood — water reaching the very door, threatening within seconds to engulf the house and drown everyone inside. In that moment, the owner of the house will cry out urgently: Al-Hazr! Al-Hazr! — Beware! Beware! Run! The floodwater is here! At such a moment of imminent catastrophe, no one would describe the flood in ornate sentences. A sermon should be precisely like this — rooted in genuine fear of one's own reckoning, and conveying that same urgency to others.*

Second, against the desire for applause. *While delivering sermons, one must never allow the thought to enter one's heart that after listening, people will shout praises, fall into ecstasy, tear their clothes, raise a clamor in the gathering, and declare that it was a wonderful sermon. Such thoughts are pure ostentation — Riya. Your true intention should be to summon God's creation from this world toward the next; from sin toward servitude of God; from greed toward detachment; from miserliness toward generosity; from ostentation toward sincerity — Ikhlas; from arrogance toward humility; from heedlessness toward God-consciousness; and from pride toward piety.* The sermon's work is a series of *transfers* — the listener is moved from a deficient register to a richer one. The sermon that fails to perform these transfers is the sermon *Shaytan* preaches. *Such people spread a corruption — Fasad — in religion that even Satan himself does not spread. Whoever has the authority to remove such a preacher from the pulpit — Minbar — it is obligatory upon him to do so, so that they cannot distort the meaning of commanding the right and forbidding the evil — 'Amr bil Ma'roof Wa Nahi 'Anil Munkar* (amr bil ma-ROOF wa NAH-yi a-nil MUN-kar).

### The third admonition — staying away from the affluent and rulers

The third restraint is on the company of the affluent and rulers. *You should not even greet any king, any person of wealth, or any ruler. Stay away from their company and gatherings, and beyond that, do not so much as lift your eyes to look at them. For in looking upon them and keeping their company lie many hidden calamities. But if you ever happen to find yourself in their company by chance, refrain from praising them.* The hadith anchors the restraint:

> Fa InnAllaha Yaghdib Iza Mudihal Faasiqu Waz Zalimu Wa Iza Madaha Wa Mun Da'aa Li Zalimin Bitoolil Baqaaii Faqad Ahabba Aye Ya'siyAllahu Fil Arde.
> Verily, Allah, the Exalted, is angered when any transgressor (Fasiq) or oppressor (Zalim) is praised; and whoever prays for a long life for an oppressor has, in effect, expressed a desire that sin be perpetuated upon the earth.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (fa-in-nal-LAA-ha YAGH-dib i-za MU-di-hal FAA-si-qu waz ZAA-li-mu)

To praise the transgressor is to anger Allah; to pray for his long life is to pray that his transgression continue upon the earth.

### The fourth admonition — not accepting gifts from rulers

The fourth restraint is on accepting gifts from rulers. *You should not accept any gifts from rulers, even if you are certain that what they offer comes from lawful — Halal — sources. The reason is that harboring any desire for their possessions causes great damage and corruption in the Deen — the path to God. The favors and rewards that come from rulers give rise to oppression, transgression, and sin, all of which damage the Deen.* The Imam names the minimum harm with precision. *The minimum corruption that flows from this is that you will begin to love the oppressors, and when a person loves another, they pray for that person's long life. If an oppressor lives long, oppression endures longer, and there is greater turmoil and corruption in the world — and what could be worse than that?*

*Beware! Beware! Shaytan will try to misguide you by planting this thought in your heart: "First take the money from these rulers, then distribute it among the poor, bring them comfort, and fulfill their needs." Beware — never accept this kind of counsel from any Shaytan, whether from among the Jinn or from among humankind. Satan has shed the blood of many through this very strategy. There are so many calamities embedded within this that we have explained at length in our book Ihya' 'Ulum ad-Din* (ih-YAH oo-LOOM ad-DEEN — *Revival of the Knowledge of the Path to God*). *You may search for them there.*

## The four things to do

The Imam now turns to the four things the disciple must *do*. *Refrain from the four things mentioned above. But there are also four things that you must actively attend to, and it will be fitting that you take full care of them.*

### The first — the method of developing an intimate relationship with Allah

*The first is that every matter between you and Allah should be conducted in such a way that, if your own servant were to do the same for you, you would not be saddened — rather, you would approve of it, commend it, and feel no anger over it. Likewise, whatever you would dislike your servant doing, realize that if you fall short in your own servitude to your Lord, your Creator will equally dislike it.*

The disciple measures his relation with Allah by an inverted analogue — *take your own servant's possible conduct toward you, and ask: would I approve?* *The deeper truth to be grasped here is this: your servant was not created by you — you merely employed him. But you yourself were created, nurtured, and owned by your Real Creator and Lord. You are His slave in the fullest sense, and therefore your obligation not to displease your Lord — in thought, word, or deed — is immeasurably greater.*

### The second — the method of relating to the slaves of Allah

*The second is that every matter between you and other slaves of Allah should be conducted in such a way that, if they were to do the same to you, you would be pleased and not aggrieved.* The rule is the parallel of the first rule, now for horizontal relations. *As it has been said in a sacred hadith — a tradition of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in which Allah speaks directly:*

> Fala Yakmalu Eemanu 'Abdi Hatta Yuhibba Lisaa-irin Nasi Ma Yuhibba Li Nafsihi.
> My slave's faith (Iman) is never complete until he loves for all human beings what he loves for himself.
> — Hadith Qudsi, in the canonical collections (fa-la YAK-mu-lu ee-MAA-nu AB-di hat-ta yu-HIB-ba li-SAA-i-rin NAA-si)

The completion of *Iman* is conditional on a precise inward movement — *to love for all human beings what he loves for himself.* The disciple now has, in two rules, the entire architecture of the two relations that compose a servant's life: the vertical with the Lord, the horizontal with the Lord's creation.

### The third — the rule of studying as if you had only a week to live

*The third thing is that if you want to increase your knowledge and wish to study scholarly works, then imagine that your lifetime will end in no more than a week. In that situation, consider carefully what kind of knowledge will truly benefit you, and devote yourself only to that. If you knew with certainty that your life would last no more than a week, you would never spend those final days studying books on religious debate and argumentation, lexicography, linguistics and grammar, poetry, poetic collections, or the art of composition. You would recognize that such knowledge could offer you no benefit. Instead, you would devote that entire week to contemplation of the heart and to recognizing the faults within your Nafs — your own self — so that you might make amends in whatever time remained.*

This is the Imam's most economical answer to the disciple's original question. *Which* knowledge benefits on the Day of Judgment? The knowledge you would study *if you knew you had a week to live*. Everything you would *not* study is, by that test, knowledge you do not need.

The second image — the visit of the king. *If you were told that the king would visit your home next week, you would spend the entire week consumed with a single concern: that the king's gaze should not fall upon anything unseemly. You would clean and adorn everything — your body, your clothing, the walls and floors of your house. You would leave nothing untended.* The hadith of the Prophet ﷺ on the gaze of Allah seals the image:

> InnAllaha La Yunzuru Ila Suwarikum Wa La Ila 'Aamalikum Wa Lakin Yunzuru Ila Qulubikum Wa Niyyatikum.
> Verily, Allah does not look at your faces or your outward actions, but He looks at your hearts and your intentions.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (in-nal-LAA-ha LA YUN-zu-ru i-la SU-wa-ri-kum)

*When the gaze of the Supreme Ruler — Ahkamul Hakimeen — rests upon your heart, why do you not purify it? If you desire to acquire knowledge of the matters of the heart, study my book Ihya al-Uloom ad-Deen along with my other works, for such knowledge is personally obligatory — Fard al-'ayn (FARD al-AYN) — upon every Muslim. Other forms of knowledge carry the status of communal obligation — Fard Kifaya (FARD ki-FAA-yah) — an obligation upon the whole community, discharged when at least one group within it fulfills it. Yet even that knowledge should be acquired only to the extent necessary for obeying the commandments of Allah, the Exalted.* The disciple's life is to be filled, first and almost only, with the *Fard al-'ayn*.

### The fourth — not stockpiling food

*The fourth thing is that you should not accumulate more than one year's provision for your family from the goods of this world.* The rule is calibrated against the conduct of the Prophet ﷺ himself. *The Prophet ﷺ set aside one year's worth of provisions for certain of his wives and said:*

> Allahumma Aj'al Quta Aale Muhammadin Kafafun.
> O my Allah! Make this provision sufficient for the family of Muhammad ﷺ.
> — The Holy Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in the canonical hadith collections (al-LAA-hum-ma AJ-al QU-ta AA-le mu-HAM-ma-din ka-FAA-fun)

*The Prophet ﷺ did not set aside provisions for all of his wives, the Azwaj al-Mutahharat. He reserved a year's supply only for those whose Tawakkul — reliance upon Allah — was weaker. For those among the Ummahatil Mu'mineen, the Mothers of the Believers, whose certainty in Allah was perfect, the Prophet ﷺ did not set aside even a single day's provisions. Among them was 'Aisha Siddiqa (may Allah be pleased with her).* One year is the *maximum* for a household whose *Tawakkul* is not yet perfect; the *minimum* — for those whose *Tawakkul* is perfect — is *not a single day*. The disciple's task is to live truthfully between the two.

## A supplication that may always be recited

*My dear beloved son! In this book I have answered all of your questions. Now gather your resolve, act upon everything within it, and do not forget me in your supplications, your Du'a.* The Imam then closes the second of the disciple's two original requests — the request for a supplication that he might recite at all times. *Seek out supplications in the books of Ahadith — the traditions of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — particularly in the Sahah Sitta (si-HAAH sit-TA — the six most authenticated collections of his traditions), and commit them to memory. Similarly, many supplications have reached us through the Ahl al-Bayt, the family of the Prophet ﷺ; seek them out there as well.* The Imam then gives one particular supplication for the disciple to recite after every *Salat* (sa-LAAT — obligatory prayer):

> O my Allah! I seek from You the fullness and perfection of Your blessings; I seek the permanence of inward and outward purity; I seek the all-encompassing breadth of Your mercy; I seek the attainment of health; I seek increase in my sustenance; I seek happiness in my life; I seek true and lasting success in this life; I seek the completion of noble qualities; I seek from Your rewards the most abundant reward; I seek from Your plenitude the sweetest plenitude; I seek from Your favors the most plentiful favor; I seek from all deeds the deed that is best; I seek from all knowledge the knowledge that is most beneficial; and I seek from all sustenance the sustenance that is most wholesome.

The supplication continues, broadening from request to plea for protection:

> O my Allah! Be on our side — grant us benefit — and do not burden us — protect us from harm. O my Allah! Grant us salvation at our death; reform our deeds; grant us safety in Your days and nights; extend Your mercy to us in our homes and in our possessions; cover our sins and faults with the veil of Your forgiveness; do us the favor of correcting all our shortcomings, inward and outward; and grant us confidence and reliance upon You.

And closes on the Divine names:

> O my Allah! Grant us steadfastness and perseverance in our Deen — our religion and path to God. Save us from deeds in this world that would become a source of shame and humiliation on the Day of Judgment. Lighten the burden of our sins; grant us the life of the pious; be sufficient for us in all our affairs; and protect us from the evil of the evildoers. Liberate us and our forebears from the Fire of Hell, from debt, and from oppression. O You the Victorious One, Ya Aziz! O You the Forgiving One, Ya Ghaffar! O You the Generous One, Ya Kareem! O You the Coverer of Faults, Ya Sattar! O You the Forbearing One, Ya Haleem! O You the Compeller, Ya Jabbar! O You the Magnificent One, Ya Azeem! O You Who Subdues all things, Ya Qahhar! O Allah! O Allah! O Allah! O You the Compassionate One in this world, Ya Rahman! O You the Merciful One in the life hereafter, Ya Raheem! O You the Greatest of the Merciful, Ya Arhamu ar-Rahimeen! You are the Granter of boundless mercy. And may the mercy and blessings of Allah descend always upon Muhammad ﷺ, the finest of all creation from eternity to eternity, and upon his descendants, and upon his companions. All praise belongs solely to Allah, the Lord of all the Universes, Rabb ul-'Alameen.

The supplication is the disciple's daily companion. Its three movements gather the three registers the letter has walked — fullness of gift, protection from harm, and the Divine names (*Aziz*, *Ghaffar*, *Kareem*, *Sattar*, *Haleem*, *Jabbar*, *Azeem*, *Qahhar*, *Rahman*, *Raheem*, *Arhamu ar-Rahimeen*) that hold the whole architecture.

*This book comes to an end with the help of Al-Malik, the Absolute Sovereign, and Al-Wahhab, the Giver of all.*

The disciple, who came in the opening chapter with a question about knowledge, leaves the closing chapter with a *practical rule of life* — eight benefits to live by, seven conditions to satisfy, four definitions to hold, eight admonitions (four restraints, four actions) to discipline the days, and one supplication to recite after every prayer until the grave receives him.
